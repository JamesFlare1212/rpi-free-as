## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Students will learn the importance to place emphasis on the experiment set-up, including pre-laboratory activities, note taking, data analysis and reporting of results.
- 2\. Students will show competence with chemical laboratory techniques including mass measurements, volume measurements, solution preparation, dilution, titration, instrumentation methods and synthesis methods. Spectroscopy methods will be used; these include Infrared and Nuclear Magnetic Resonance. Separation methods of thin layer chromatography and gas chromatography will be used.
- 3\. Students will show competence at the handling and processing of laboratory (experimental) data.
- 4\. Students will be able to search for answers about chemistry or chemical systems in the chemical literature.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

#### Quizzes (25 pts.)

A quiz will be given following the completion of each experiment. Each quiz will be made up of several questions to test your understanding of the techniques and concepts connected with the experiment that you had performed. The source material for the questions will be the recitation slides, the assigned reading for the experiment listed on the Organic Chemistry Laboratory schedule and your Organic Chemistry textbook. Students must show up to laboratory on time or they will forfeit their opportunity to take the quiz. Make-ups will not be given. All work must be submitted in ink.

**Pre-labs** (20 pts.) **\*\*See Appendix II, IV and V (p. 912-923) in the Lehman text.**

You should buy a lab notebook that is capable of creating carbonless copies of what you write. If you make a mistake in your notebook, draw a single line through the mistake and continue writing. Upon arrival at Walker Labs, please go into the lab and submit your prelab assignment to the teaching assistant. Then proceed to the classroom to take the quiz. The TA’s will review your pre-lab while you are taking the quiz and unlock your equipment drawer. The pre-lab requires students to prepare in advance and know precisely what he/she will be doing after entering the lab. *Since this is a safety issue, a student will not be allowed into the lab without turning in an acceptable pre-lab*. All work must be submitted in ink.

The typical pre-lab should be at least two pages in order for you to provide enough detail of how the experiment will be prosecuted. The following should be incorporated:

(a) Your name, experiment number & title, followed (if appropriate) with a balanced (set of) equation(s) using appropriate structures.

(b) Complete table of reagents – including molecular weights – amounts - # moles used, densities, mp/bp, etc. This should suffice for you to set up a limiting reaction calculation … which reagent is used in excess?

(c) Detailed procedure that you will follow. Flow charts often are an excellent way to organize your plan, especially when sequential isolation or purification steps are involved. Sketches of your glassware also are useful in visualizing (organizing) steps in a synthesis sequence. Also indicate the physical techniques and spectroscopic procedures that will be used in identifying a material and determining its purity. What else? Your objective is to tersely incorporate all operational information that you will need to run the experiment. Textbooks will not be allowed in the laboratory.

**This pre-lab must be complete in order to enter the lab.** We retain the carbon copy; the original remains in the lab notebook.

**Lab Notebook/ Lab Notes (Data and Obs) \*\* See Appendix II (p. 912) of the Lehman text. (10 pts)**

Please check the lab white board for additional instructions or procedure changes before you begin the experiment. Lab notes additionally will be recorded in your notebook (1-2 pages) as you do the experiment. All work must be submitted in ink.

1. Make sure that the top of each page has your name, experiment number & title.
2. The following information must be recorded as you make observations on:

All amounts of reactants actually used and products isolated,

All reaction times,

All color, temperature and consistency changes, as well as melting point (mp) and boiling point (bp) values recorded,

All extraction layers formed and removed (a flowchart is generally more useful),

All qualitative techniques used to identify your product and establish its purity.

3. You will find it convenient to document some of your results with sketches. Examples include drawings of developed TLC plates (Rf values denoted) and of a chromatography during one or more stages of its development.
4. Specifically note all deviations from your research plan (pre-lab report) in these lab notes. (Do not alter what you originally wrote in your copy of the pre-lab report.) Also note any unforeseen problems that you encountered and what you did to resolve them, or at least to minimize any adverse impact.
5. Tear out the copy of these notes and turn it in to a TA before you leave the lab. They will file it; you keep the original.

**Post-lab Report** (20 pts.) **\*\*See Appendix III and IV (p. 913-922) of the Lehman text.**

Post-labs are due the next lab meeting after you finish the experiment. For some labs we will allocate extra time if you (as a group) need a little more time during the next lab period for weighing a product, determining a mp, or recording a spectrum. This post-lab report (2-3 pages) should be typewritten. Make sure that the top of each page has your name, experiment number & title. All work must be submitted in ink.

The post-lab report that you submit should include:

(a) An introduction to the experiment including the experimental problem to be solved, how you will attempt to solve the scientific problem including specific techniques, instrumentation, measurements, etc… and a brief prediction of the outcome. Chemical mechanisms may be appropriate to clearly show the experimental reaction.

(b) the percent yield or percent recovery of a product, showing all calculations. (Look at Appendices II-IV in your textbook for help.)

(c) identification of an unknown. Clearly show your reasoning.

(d) append copies of NMR & IR spectra (fully interpreted) and of gas chromatograms (fully labeled) used to establish identity and/or purity.

(d) Assigned post lab questions. Post lab questions will be posted if they may apply.

Grading rests primarily on the post-lab report plus appended data (if any), although this may be reviewed in the context of the other information present in your laboratory notes. For example, both your conclusions and how you arrived at them are important grading criteria, whereas the yield and purity of your product will already have been graded as a separate entry, Product Yield and Purity. The actual point allocation for the 25 point contribution of your post-lab report will vary for each lab experiment.

**Product Yield and Purity** (15 pts.)

Your product should be turned in at the end of the laboratory period. You must label the vial with your name, product name, boiling point/melting point, product mass, percent yield or recovery, and date. If you lost your sample or used your entire product for some purpose, you must turn in a note explaining the absence of sample for some credit.

**General Lab Technique** (10 pts.)

Your performance in the lab including attention to safety and cleanliness will be evaluated. The laboratories are scheduled for three hours and it is expected that students can complete them in the allotted time. The need for additional time is usually indicative of poor laboratory technique and preparation.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:**

The relationship between students and faculty is based upon trust and the continued maintenance of this trust is necessary for education to be successful. Students need to trust faculty to make appropriate judgments about the content and structure of the course. Faculty members need to trust that the work turned in by students represents their own effort. Violation of this trust undermines the educational process. For example, as professionals, you may be responsible in designing products (aircraft, the computers that run them, or the runways on which they land) where lives are at stake. As a result, there is no tolerance for any breach of academic integrity such as cheating, plagiarizing, or inappropriate sharing of laboratories or quizzes.

**Anyone caught cheating or plagiarizing or any breach of Academic Integrity will receive an “F” in the course.**

Cheating can include sharing answers, as well as stealing answers. Plagiarism means copying words from someone’s work, even if you “change the sentence a bit.” If you share your laboratory report you are as guilty as the person copying it. If you do use material from an appropriate source, make sure you reference it properly in your reports. The Rensselaer Student Handbook and the Dean of Students Office Webpage (http://doso.rpi.edu/update.do?artcenterkey=676) defines various forms of academic dishonesty. Please familiarize yourself with these and ask us if you have questions about what constitutes academic dishonesty.
