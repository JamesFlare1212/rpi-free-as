**Course Description**

This course introduces Network Science and reviews current research in this field. Classes will interchangeably present chapters from the textbook and related current research. The emphasis will be on the mathematical background of network science: graphs and networks, random networks, and various types of scale-free networks; network properties such as assortativity, mobility, and robustness; social networks and communities; and dynamics of processes on networks.

**Prerequisites**

CSCI-2300; a 4000 level algorithms-based CSCI (e.g., 4020, 4050, 4260, 4800), or MATH (4100, 4150, 4200, 4210, 4800) course; junior or senior level standing; some familiarity with probability theory, linear algebra, and calculus; or permission of the instructor.

**Course Content**

- Mathematical background of network science: graphs and networks.
- Random networks and their properties.
- Scale-free networks, small world networks, and Barabási-Albert model.
- Mobility and networks
- Network robustness
- Social networks and communities
- Assortativity of networks
- Dynamic processes

**Student Learning Outcomes**

Upon completion of this course, all students will be able to:

1. Apply fundamental network science ideas to create models and understand the dynamics of networked systems;
2. Compare, contrast, and describe the similarities and differences of different kinds of networks and processes modeled on networks;
3. Critique the strengths and weaknesses of each of the models and types of networks based on them and these network types of performance in diverse network science applications;
4. Understand the principles of applying network science to disciplinary science and design and set up basic models for some specific applications.
5. Read, analyze, and critique published literature in the field of network science and social networks;
6. Assess the novelty of network science research and its relation to the state of the art in the field.

**Course Assessment Measures** Students' performance will be measured using three different methods:  
(i) After selecting a current research paper, each student will present the selected paper, including its content and evaluation of its scientific results, and  
(ii) contributions to in-class discussions.  
(iii) Independent and novel mini-project using different data or methods on the presentation topic.

The first two methods are the same as the undergraduate methods (i) and (ii), while the third method assesses students’ ability to apply network science to novel problems.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The students' performance will be measured using three different methods:  
(i) selection of a current research paper for presentation and formulating a plan of an experiment to test the results and methods of the paper,  
(iii) presentation on the selected paper, including slides content, and evaluation of the test experiment with the results and method of the paper,  
(iv) contributions to in-class discussions.  
The presentation and experiment plans will measure the student’s ability to apply concepts of network science to networks analysis.  
The presentation slides and the experiments evaluating paper results and methods will measure student’s ability to prepare summary material based on fundamental scientific concepts and basic research.  
The presentation will measure student’s ability to communicate the concepts and notions related to the research projects in which the student is involved.  
The discussion in class will measure the student’s skills to critically evaluate and objectively assess the presentations of other students.
