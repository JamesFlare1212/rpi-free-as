## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides an opportunity to explore **state-of-the-art research in machine learning** by identifying and analyzing the most active and impactful topics in the field. Each week, we will focus on a current research area, review key papers, and engage in discussions to critically evaluate the methods and results. Students will take an active role by reviewing and presenting selected works, which will strengthen both technical understanding and communication skills. The seminar will also feature **invited talks from leading researchers** across different areas of machine learning.

Beyond reviewing existing research, the seminar emphasizes how to **identify open problems, formulate original ideas, and clearly articulate them in writing**. By the end of the course, you will have gained practical experience in navigating the research process, from exploring cutting-edge topics to shaping your own research directions, while deepening your understanding of advanced machine learning.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The goal of the course is to enable students to approach research, criticize the existing literature, develop new research ideas, present their research ideas, and learn from the experts in the field.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Providing constructive criticism on others’ research and identifying open problems
- Effective presentation skills
- Collaborating in teams on developing new ideas
- Formulating and writing research ideas

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students are assessed based on the following four items:

- Assignment:  
  Students are divided into multiple groups. Each group is responsible for one research paper. Each group has one week to read a research paper and submit their review by 8:00 am on the class day. The assignment includes the feedback provided on research papers. Students may collaborate on reading the papers but should provide the feedback independently. The feedback includes summary of the paper, strengths, weaknesses, and future research directions.
- Class presentation:  
  During the class, a student presents the paper assigned to him/her as an assignment for review. Students are expected to clearly introduce the problem so that everyone with different research experience can follow, highlight strengths and weaknesses of the paper followed by future research directions. Presentations are evaluated based on understanding of the paper and clarity.
- Class participation:  
  Students are evaluated based on attendance, and participating in the discussion during and after the students’ presentation, as well as the invited talks. The discussion includes asking questions, answering questions and sharing thoughts and notes.
- Project:  
  Students form a group of 3-5. Each group completes a project on a topic of their choice which can be: 1) original research paper; 2) in-depth literature survey with strengths and weaknesses of each paper and future research directions; 3) implementation of a theoretical concept or an experimental work. The topic can be related to students' research (this is encouraged). At the end of semester, students present their project and submit a (5-page maximum) report in the form of a research paper. Also, a preliminary version of the project (introduction and literature review) may be presented during the mid-term.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

\* Assignment: 20%  
\* Class presentation: 20%  
\* Class participation: 20%  
\* Project: 40%  
\* Assignment: students provide feedback on a research paper including summary of the paper (5%), strengths of the methodology and experiments (25%), weaknesses of the methodology and experiments (35%), future directions extending the methodology and experiments (35%)  
\* Class presentation: students present a research paper, 4-5 times during the semester depending on the number of groups. The presentation is graded based on the clarity (providing background, motivation, and introducing the methodology) standing at 30% and understanding of the paper (understanding the proposed methodology, understanding experiments, answering questions in the class, being critical, providing future directions) standing at 70%.  
\* Class participation: This includes attending the class and participating in the discussions during the class includes asking questions, answering questions and sharing thoughts and notes. Points awarded per contribution: 1–20 (out of 100). Evaluation is based on the depth and quality of the question or comment:

- 1 = minimal/low depth
- 20 = highly challenging/deep insight

\* Project: This project is conducted in groups of 3-5 students. Each group is responsible for researching a topic of their choice. The research should demonstrate some level of novelty, even if minor, but it does not necessarily need to result in a publishable paper. The work must be original and not previously presented. This could be in the form of an original research paper, in-depth literature survey with strengths and weaknesses of each paper and future research directions, or implementation of a theoretical concept or an experimental work. There will be both mid-term and final presentations, along with a final 5-page report in a paper format. The mid-term presentation will introduce and motivate the research problem with literature review and will be graded on clarity, contributing 20% of the project grade. The final presentation, together with the report, will account for the remaining 80% of the project grade. The final presentation will be assessed based on the clarity and understanding of the proposed methodology. The report will be evaluated on writing quality, the novelty of the method, relevance of the experiments, and the potential for the report to be developed into a research paper suitable for submission to a peer-reviewed conference or journal.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Using an artificial intelligence (AI) machine to summarize and review a paper without carefully reading the paper and having enough knowledge about the paper is considered cheating. The same is true for the project, and using any AI tools to help with writing the report is considered cheating. A grade of zero will be given on the first assignment where cheating and/or plagiarism are detected. If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of F for the course. A grade of zero will be given on the project report if cheating and/or plagiarism are detected.
