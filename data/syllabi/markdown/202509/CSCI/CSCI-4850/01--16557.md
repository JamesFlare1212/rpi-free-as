## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Cybersecurity is one of the fastest growing fields in computer science. Modern Binary Exploitation will focus on teaching practical offensive security skills in binary exploitation and reverse engineering. Through a combination of interactive lectures, hands-on labs, and guest speakers from industry, the course will offer students a rare opportunity to explore involved subjects in a rapidly evolving field.

The course will start off by covering basic x86 reverse engineering, vulnerability analysis, and classical forms of Linux-based userland binary exploitation. It will then transition into defenses found on modern systems ( Canaries, DEP, ASLR, RELRO, Fortify Source, etc.) and the techniques used to defeat them. Time permitting, the course will also cover other subjects in exploitation including kernel-land and Windows based exploitation, as well as static analysis of binaries.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Possess the skills necessary to carry out vulnerability analysis of binary applications.
- Understand executable formats, program control flow at the assembly level, and other low level concepts.
- Understand classic and contemporary vulnerabilities and exploitation techniques.
- Apply both source code auditing and binary reverse engineering to the vulnerability discovery process.
- Be capable of exploiting vulnerabilities found in real world software as defined by MITRE’s Critical Vulnerabilities and Exposures (CVE) system.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- Labs: Typically, each lecture will be accompanied by a lab that will solidify understanding of the topic of the lecture. Each lab will contain at least 3 problems, a C-level, a B-level, and an A-level problem.
- Projects: There will be three projects over the course of the semester. In these projects students will be exploiting known vulnerabilities in a realistic piece of software. The projects resemble lab problems, however, they are lengthier and combine multiple topics.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

- Labs: 60%, 9 chapters and labs equally weighted at 6.67% of the final grade

- Labs will typically consist of 3 problems, a C-, B-, and A-level problems with each problem harder than the previous. Lab grading works as follows.

- Students who complete only the C problem will receive 75% (C)
- Students who complete both the C and B problems will receive 85% (B)
- Students who complete all three lab problems will receive 100% (A)

- If a student does not complete the C-level problem before the end of the associated lab period, they will receive 5 percentage point reduction. For example, if a student fails to complete the C-level problem during the lab period, but then goes on to successfully complete the C- and B-level problems for the week, the grade received for that week’s lab will be 80%.
- Note that one cannot access a B-level problem in WarGames before completing the C-level problem first. Similarly, one cannot access an A-level problem before completing the corresponding B-level one.
- In order to receive full credit for a problem the student must submit their exploit code, the flag, and may be asked to explain their work upon submission to be checked off. Please leave comments and other supporting materials in your code.
- Lab problems will be introduced by the end of lecture typically once a week. The C-level problem will be due by the end of the corresponding lab period, typically Thursday. All other problems become homework, and are due at 11:59 pm EDT/EST on Monday the following week. (See calendar.)
- The calendar specifies suggested deadlines for lab submission. No submissions will be be accepted past the end of the semester.

- Term Projects: 40%, three projects equally weighted at 5%, 15%, and 20% correspondingly

- Project specific grading breakdowns will be given when they are assigned.
- Projects submitted late will receive a reduction of 2% per each calendar day (full or partial) late. Projects may be submitted up to 5 days late for the total reduction of 10%.
