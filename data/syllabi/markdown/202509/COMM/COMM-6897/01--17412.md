## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Social media impacts our everyday lives; it affects how we develop our sense of identity, how we communicate with others and develop relationships, and how we find information about topics, products, pop culture, and politics. Simultaneously, social media reciprocally reflects existing social dynamics. This course explores those dynamics and that reciprocal relationship. Specifically, rather than a “how-to” course, this course explores theoretical approaches to social media, including identity formation, community formation, surveillance, and digital labor.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Define key terms, issues, and debates in social and cultural analyses of social media and compare and contrast different frameworks to understand social media as media products and independently pose further questions through reading responses and academic essays
- • Analyze and add to scholarly conversations about how social media systems and infrastructures mutually shape people’s practices to understand the ways society, culture, and technology influence each other through reading responses and longer academic essays with academic sources located independently.
- • Apply social and cultural frameworks and theories about social media to real-world, contemporary examples and add to those scholarly conversations by communicating their analyses effectively through longer academic essays that have clear claims and evidence, with academic sources located independently.
- • Define and investigate key challenges posed by social media platforms and challenges social media companies face through reading responses and longer academic essays with academic sources located independently.
- • Imagine and propose their own solutions to the challenges posed by social media platforms or challenges social media companies face, considering crucial social and cultural complexities discussed in class.
- • Independently investigate a social media topic of their choice by locating relevant academic sources and lead one 30-minute lesson in which they critically engage undergraduate students on the topic.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Assignment Descriptions**

**Initial Reflection (5 points, 0.5%).**

The initial reflection will be completed in-class on the first day of classes. If you register for the class late, you will need to complete the initial reflection within the first two days of joining the class. It will not be graded on grammar or correctness. If you complete the assignment fully, you will receive full points.

**Reading Responses (20 Due x 7.5 points=150 points/15% complete/incomplete**).

These are out-of-class activities in which you will respond to two readings for that class period. You will respond to the 4000-level reading for that class period as well as a reading labelled “6000-level.” You will turn in 1) an annotation for each reading, 2) a paragraph where you attempt to put the sources into conversation with each other, and 3) three substantive discussion questions about the readings. They are due ***before*** class because we will engage with your answers in class. These are not graded on whether your answer is perfectly correct, but I am looking to see that you genuinely engaged with the readings. If you complete all parts of the responses, with a relevant, well-written answer, you will receive full points. These do not need to be grammatically perfect, but should be well-written enough for me to understand, the answer.

**Library Module: Scholarship as conversation module (5 points, 0.5%)**

To understand how academic discourse works, you will complete a short (15-minute) module developed by the Liz King at RPI’s library. The module is called “Scholarship as Conversation Tutorial” (https://guides.lib.rpi.edu/InfoLit/cocurricular). Click on the link and watch through the videos. There is a quiz at the end. You will receive a Percipio Completion Certificate when you finish. Please download the certificate and upload it to Blackboard to receive credit.

**APA citation quiz. (5 points, 0.5%)**

There will also be an APA citation quiz for you in Blackboard this day. This is meant for you to practice APA citation, which will be required on your papers.

**Peer Reviews** **(2 total x 50 points each=100 points/10%).**

For peer reviews, you will bring in nearly complete drafts of your upcoming assignments. These are meant for you to practice getting feedback from actual readers (your peers) and myself. These are also meant for you to practice putting yourself in the position of the reader. Answer the peer review questions for your peers and write 3-5 things you will change for the final draft. **You must be in class to receive points for peer review, unless you discuss an alternative with me first.**

**Midterm Paper (250 points, 25%).**

Your **main task** is to write a 2,500-word paper in which you (1) select a case study from social media that exemplifies at least one (at ***most*** two) of the topics we covered in this section of the course about people’s practices (see below for examples and restrictions). (2) Then, apply the theories that we talked about in class to your case study. To connect your case study to what we talked about in class, use **two scholarly sources** from our class. In addition to the two articles from this class, as a 6000-level student, find at least **two** **relevant academic sources from outside of this class**, for a total of **four** academic sources (*at least*). Broadly, you are answering: **How does this case study add to existing scholarly conversations about people’s practices on social media? Why does this matter?** To support your argument, you will need to use **primary** **evidence**.

**Final Paper (250 points, 25%).**

Your **main task** is to write a 2,500-word paper in which you (1) identify a “gap” in the literature that is not easily solvable. (2) Then, you will offer a case study that demonstrates this issue. You will explain how this case study demonstrates this issue with primary evidence. In other words, how does this case study complicate existing literature? (3) Finally, you will pose a solution to the issue or “gap” that you identified. Whether that is a concrete technical solution, a new theoretical framework, etc. is up to you, although the solution should clearly link back to the gap in the literature. In addition to the two articles from this class, as a 6000-level student, find at least **two** **relevant academic sources from outside of this class**, for a total of **four** academic sources (*at least*). To support your argument, you will need to use **primary** **evidence**.

**Final Reflection (5 points, 0.5%).**

The final reflection will be completed in-class as your final exam on the last day of classes. The portal to turn in the in-class activities will open during class time and close by the end of class time. **You must be in class to receive points for in-class activities**. It will not be graded on grammar or correctness. If you complete the assignment fully, you will receive full points.

**Research Colloquium Presentation (90 points, 9%).**

The research colloquium presentation is an opportunity for you to share with your peers what you researched for your final paper. The presentation will be 5-10 minutes long and cover the basics of your final paper. You will also turn in a PowerPoint **before class** with this presentation and be able to answer questions from your audience about your research. You will also need to ask at least two of your peers’ questions. You should exhibit basic presentation skills, including speaking loud enough, organizing your speech, and basic slide design. You will sign up for one of two days to present, to give everyone enough time to present. However, everyone’s PowerPoints will be due on the ***first*** day of presentations by class time to ensure that the people who have later presentation days are not unfairly privileged.

**Teach a Lesson (140 points, 14%).**

As 6000-level students, you will also practice teaching 4000-level students. Your **main task** is to create and lead a 45-minute lesson about one of the other articles that you read as 6000-level students. For this assignment, you will be graded on three elements. First, you will be graded on (1) your lesson plan. (2) Next, you will be graded on your lesson execution. (3) Finally, you will turn in a brief reflection about your lesson. You lesson plan and your reflection will both be due by **11:59 PM the day *after* you teach your lesson.**

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance Policy**

I take attendance each class period. You are graded on participation, including in-class activities. As we do a lot of in-class work to prepare for upcoming assignments, it is imperative that you attend class. This will ensure you keep up with the materials and that you receive participation points. You cannot receive participation points if you are absent, unless there is a documented health-related issue. If you show up for class more than **5 minutes** late, or if you leave more than **5 minutes** early, you will only receive half credit. **You can miss up to two classes this semester, no questions asked, with no penalty to your grade.** After two absences, you will lose points. **On the days that you are absent, you will still be responsible for what we do in class, so I highly suggest that you** use these days judiciously in case there is an emergency (family illness, car accident, etc.). **If you foresee any reasons that you will miss class, please let me know as soon as possible so that we can make other arrangements to get you the course materials. If you delay communicating to me that you will miss class, that makes it more difficult for me to make alternative arrangements to get you the course materials for which you are responsible.** If you are absent for COVID, you **MUST** report your positive test result to student health services, stay home for five days, and wear a mask for five days.

### Other Course Policies:

**Grading Reporting, Missed Work, and Late Work Policy**

Grades will be reported via Blackboard, and most assignments will have a portion submitted in learning management system. Some work will be completed in class (see assignment prompts and schedule below for specifics). All major assignments are due by the announced date and time except in the event of a medical or personal emergency (which includes COVID-19 related issues). Coursework is due by class time uncles otherwise noted (for in-class activities).

Each assignment portal has one attempt possible (with a few exceptions). When you turn in your assignments, it is your responsibility to make sure the formatting is correct and was not altered if you downloaded your paper from a cloud service, like Google docs. I will not open the assignment portal again for formatting that you did not double check.

**For late work for major assignments, 10% (one letter grade) will be deducted for each 24-hour period as soon as the deadline has passed. I will continue to deduct 10% from the assignment for every 24-hour period until a grade of F is reached.** Some minor assignments cannot be made up since things like peer review require you to have turned in the assignment on time. Missed readings responses also cannot be made up since you need to have done the reading to participate in class. In general, I do not give make-up assignments, unless a student must miss a course deadline due to them representing the university in an authorized event.

**Classroom Conduct**

In general, be respectful and kind to each other. It is important to establish a safe and positive learning environment for everyone. Appropriate course conduct entails creating a positive learning experience for all students; therefore, sexist, racist, prejudicial, homophobic, or other derogatory remarks ***will not be tolerated***. If you use this language in class, you will be asked to leave. You will also receive a 0 for any assignment that violates this rule. Please talk to me if you have questions about a specific assignment.

Questions, comments, and observations are helpful if shared in class, since others likely have similar concerns. If you do want to reach me individually, our official mode of communication is through **email**. **Please do not try to contact me through WebEx, as this class uses email.** In general, you can expect me to answer a short e-mail within 48 hours, except between 5 pm on Friday and 9 am on Monday or as otherwise noted. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity Statement**

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs.

RPI’s Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities states:

“If found responsible for committing academic dishonesty, a student may be subject to one or both types of penalties: **an academic (grade) penalty administered by the professor and/or disciplinary action through the Rensselaer judicial process described in this handbook**.

Academic dishonesty is a violation of the Grounds for Disciplinary Action as described in this handbook. A student may be subject to any of the following types of disciplinary action should disciplinary action be pursued by the instructor: disciplinary warning; disciplinary probation; disciplinary suspension, expulsion and/or alternative actions as agreed on by the student and hearing officer. It should be noted that no student who allegedly commits academic dishonesty will be able to drop or change the grade option for the course in question and is not eligible to request an F examination for the course. The definitions and examples presented below are a sampling of types of academic dishonesty and are not to be construed as an exhaustive or exclusive list. The academic integrity policy applies to all students, undergraduate and graduate, and to scholarly pursuits and research. Additionally, attempts to commit academic dishonesty or to assist in the commission or attempt of such an act are also violations of this policy.”

You can read more about the judicial process here: https://www.rpi.edu/dept/doso/resources/policies/Fillable2015-2016Version2AcademicIntegrityBrochure.pdf

For this class, I differentiate between misuse of sources and intentional plagiarism. The extent of the infraction will determine my response. For example, minor misuse of sources may result in you having to rewrite the paper and take a late penalty. Papers that do not reflect your work may result in an F in the class and disciplinary action through the RPI judicial process.

For clarity, all your papers should have (1) a works cited page at the end of a paper ***and*** (2) in-text citations. Materials that you **must cite in-text** include: (1) Quotations, paraphrases, and summaries of words, ideas, and opinions of others, (2) Visual and statistical material and computer programming code derived from others (3) Facts not widely known and arguable claims (4) Help from others.

For this class, should use **APA** format to cite your paper. You can read more about how to use both through the OWL Purdue. If you have any other questions about citations, I suggest you take your paper to COMM+D (see below).

**Artificial Intelligence Statement**

Artificial intelligence (AI) is often used in the writing process, and may have a place in your other classes. However, for this class, you ***may not*** simply copy and paste language from ChatGPT or other LLMs into your assignments. All assignments must accurately reflect the readings and answer the questions. This includes using AI to generate answers to assignments, exams, or projects, or using AI to complete any other course-related tasks. Using AI in this way undermines your ability to develop critical thinking, writing, or research skills that are essential for this course and your academic success. Since AI hallucinates, you are responsible for fact checking statements all statements in your assignments. If they are not backed up by sources, I will assume they are AI. **If statements are false, there will be a penalty, up to and including a 0 for the assignment**. Likewise, since AI hallucinates information, if you grossly misrepresent a source in your papers, you will lose points. So, make sure to accurately characterize sources.

Failure to follow these guidelines may result in academic integrity violations, up to and including failing the assignment and failing the course. If you use ChatGPT, I will report the incident to the Dean of Students’ Office, so that there is a record of the violation. If you are in doubt as to whether you are using AI language models appropriately in this course, I encourage you to discuss your situation with me.
