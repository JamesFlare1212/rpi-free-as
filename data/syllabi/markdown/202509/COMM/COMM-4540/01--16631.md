## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will give you tools to critically examine the barrage of images we encounter daily and understand how these images affect us emotionally as individuals, how they influence the way we think and understand our reality, and in turn, how we conduct our lives together in the world. We’ll examine a broad range of visual media from photographs, art, television, and film to social media platforms and new media technologies (e.g., YouTube, TikTok and VR). We’ll also spend some time examining how “visuality” is experienced and expressed for those who are blind or visually disabled, and how visual media plays a role in witnessing traumatic events and their political and cultural implications.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Foundational course readings providing an overview of Visual Culture as a scholarly field will be assigned from the latest edition of this text: Lisa Cartwright and Marita Sturken *Practices of Looking: An Introduction to Visual Culture.* Please procure it via your preferred book store or source. PDFs of the first two week’s chapters from this book will be provided on the LMS. In addition, there will be individual readings from both scholarly journals and popular media also posted to the LMS. If readings are listed as “Recommended” they may be discussed in the lecture but are not required reading. If readings are listed as “Optional” they are not required but may be used in assignments where appropriate.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Students will be able to analyze visual images in terms of their cultural meaning and affective impact.
- 2\. Students will be able to discuss and to write about how we create and are created by the visual media environment that we inhabit.
- 3\. Students will learn key concepts in the study of visual culture and be able to produce their own visually based project engaging these concepts.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Assessments and Evaluation for COMM 4540:**

- **Class Participation (10%)**

Course Participation includes participation in class discussions, exercises, and assignments.

- **Weekly Short Example Responses (15% – complete 10 total over the course of the semester at 1.5% each, about 150 words)**

You will find and briefly describe an example, whether historical or contemporary, and explain how concepts from the readings and/or lectures for the week help illuminate the example, or how the example supports, complicates, or refutes the concepts. *Beginning Week 2 and ending Week 14. I will count the 10 highest (so you may skip two weeks if you choose). Response assignments will close for the given week on Friday at 11:59pm.*

- **One Close Reading of a Film or Video Clip (10% – 1.5-2 pages double spaced)**

You will use the film|minutes video|graphic workstation to do a close reading of a film or video clip of your choice (file must be mov, mp4, or avi). The workstation plays segments of the video file in minute loops, allowing you to take notes in a text editor. Further details will be provided in advance of the assignment, which is due the end of Week 4.

- **One Longer Example Response (10% ­– two opportunities, 1.5-2 pages double spaced)**

For this Longer Example Response, you are to select a concept from either Week 5 or Week 6 and apply it to an example of a visual practice or “text” of your choosing.

The goal of the assignment is to give you the opportunity to practice connecting the concepts to examples in progressively longer and more-focused written arguments.

- **Two Papers (40% total, 20% each – 4-6 pages double spaced)**

Prompts and further details will be provided. You will have the option to propose your own topic (with instructor approval).

- **One Group Project including presentation (15%)** For this project, you will collaborate in groups of 3-4 to produce a visual text or series of texts highlighting an issue, offering a critique or parody, or presenting a visual representation commenting on lived experiences. Further details will be provided later in the semester.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Because class discussion contributes significantly to the educational goals and outcomes of this course, attendance is a requirement. Four unexcused absences without justification (such as illness, a funeral, athletic event, job interview) after the first week of classes may result in a reduction of the final grade by a full letter. Further additional unexcused absences may result in further letter grade reductions. If you are approaching four unexcused absences, or are experiencing any difficulties with attendance, please don’t hesitate to speak with me. Whatever the reason, you are responsible for talking to a classmate about the work done on the day you miss, for checking the LMS, and for obtaining information regarding any announcements or assignments made in class.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
