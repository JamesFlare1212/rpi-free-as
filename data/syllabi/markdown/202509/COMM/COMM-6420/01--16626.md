## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- LEARNING OUTCOMES: Students in 4420 and students in 6420 These goals translate into more specific learning outcomes. You’ll be expected to: • Analyze technological artifacts. Identify specific ways technological mediation impacts knowledge, needs, emotions, values, and experiences that are both individual and social-cultural in origin. • Engage clients. Learn to more deeply see and listen to your future client’s needs more deeply while guiding them to more successful outcomes. • Explain your ideas in ways that reflect a thorough understanding of the subject and your audience’s needs, knowledge, values, etc. by anticipating and answering the audience’s likely questions and elaborating appropriately (i.e. to consider things your audience takes as “givens”). • Explain appropriate technology, not only in your design processes but in the kinds of questions you answer or avoid, and the kinds of information you choose to include and exclude. • Practice and hone your speaking and listening communication skills in both one-on-one, small group, and large group settings. • Reflect on how you want technology to function. • Begin to establish a toolkit of “practices” that you can take with you for designing your own content and analyzing the work of others. • Create a portfolio level final project with team members. Additional learning outcomes for students in 6420 • Work as team leaders mirroring strong organization culture of clear communication and how to encourage inspired collaboration rather than compliance in group divisions of labor. • Articulate mastery over the ways in which your own research goals fit within the broader HCI knowledge base. • Develop a better understanding of HCI ethics and how these ethical constraints inform a global understanding of technological development. • Develop professional presentation skills while participating in a structured Q&A session. • Apply professional class developed communication skills as point of contact for client facing aspects of the UX project. • Complete readings at the graduate level articulating the theory involved.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**ASSESSMENTS Students in 4420**

The deliverables of this class are as follows.

1. **Students in 4420** Reading Reflections 35% (350): Over the course of the semester, we will be reading medium articles that explore contemporary issues in HCI and UX. To facilitate conversation in class I am requiring that you submit your reading reflections at the end of class in hard copy. Each reflection should be about one to two paragraphs long. You need to complete 7 (50pts each) hand them in as you finish them. You can use the articles I picked or select your own medium articles about HCI or UX. If you do pick your own include the author and title of the article as part of your report. Please print and bring to class. They should be your own work AI generated content will result in a zero.

2. **Students in 4420** Research Proposal 20% (200): This is an individual plan outlining how you plan to contribute to UX Study. Your proposal should include an overview of method, timeline, technical requirement, and constraints. The proposal should be concise somewhere around 5 double spaced pages and show and understanding of the methods discussed in class.

3. **Students in 6420 and** Research Proposal 20% (200): This is an individual plan outlining how you plan to contribute to UX Study. Your proposal should include an overview of method, timeline, technical requirement, and constraints. The proposal should be somewhere around 10 double space pages in proposal format showing understanding of the methods discussed in class. Included in the proposal should be an additional timeline and the ways in which you will distinguish your work from expectations of the 4000-level course. These tasks could include organizational tasks, check-ins, application of advanced research methods, advanced client contact, or prototyping. Grading will reflect these tasks being associated with and providing an understanding of the application and integration of your area of study or research interest at the 6000 level expected rigor.

4. **Students in 4420 and Students in 6420** User Experience Study 35% (350) (Group Project):

5. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

*The* *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* and *The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement*define various forms of academic dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade *must represent your own work*. **If you plagiarize once,** you will receive an **F on the assignment.** If you plagiarize again, you will **fail the entire course** and may be reported to the *Dean of Students* as appropriate. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask me for clarification. The basics: when using information from another source **(including anonymous online sources**) always cite the original text or URL and distinguish it from your own commentary. If you directly cut and paste another person’s language, you need to use it as a **quotation**. *We will talk specifically about citation styles and how to make sure that you are giving “credit” when it is required of you in spirit of scholarly collaboration.* In regard to using AI (e.g. ChatGPT or other Open AI resources): While it may be tempting to use this resource, please note that there is no “check” to ensure the information that you are receiving is correct. Researchers are finding that ChatGPT can provide misinformation putting your grade at risk. Using this resource is NOT considered *your own work* and **will** be deemed as an instance of academic dishonesty if discovered. Easy ways we can tell you used AI writing programs: 1. You are unable to explain the concepts in your own words 2. You are using vocabulary that is uncommon 3. There is a dramatic difference between your in-class writing skills and your assignments. Rather than try to game the system why not take this as an opportunity to grow as a writer.
