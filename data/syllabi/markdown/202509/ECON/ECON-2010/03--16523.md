## Course Goals

### Goals:

By the end of the semester students will be able to:

1. Use the mathematical tools of algebra and calculus to determine the optimal allocation of resources for a given economic agent’s preferences and constraints.
2. Analyze how market structures (perfect competition, monopoly, oligopoly), given agent’s optimizing behavior, affect resource allocation decisions, equilibrium market prices, economic efficiency, and the effectiveness of policy.
3. Apply microeconomic concepts to evaluate real-world policy issues including taxation, government spending, regulation, externalities, and price controls.
4. Critique the limits of economic reasoning and modeling, including identifying what separates economic models from the real-world choice situations that they are designed to represent.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- To be able to explain to others why economists use mathematics and formal modeling to analyze situations of constrained choice. To be able to use the mathematical tools of algebra and calculus to determine the optimal allocation of resources for a given economic agent’s preferences and constraints (where the agent is either a consumer or producer). To be prepared, in subsequent classes, to approach academic articles in economics and explain the article’s mathematical model and/or implied theoretical concepts to others. To be able to document the limits of economic reasoning and modeling, and clearly explain to others the choices that might separate economic models from the real-world choice situations that they supposedly represent.
