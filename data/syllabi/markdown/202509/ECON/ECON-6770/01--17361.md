## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This first course in a two-course sequence analyzes micro- and meso-economics of innovation. Innovation is the direct cause of most of economic growth, and is essential to address societal problems such as climate change. At the micro level, we examine where innovations come from and what incentivizes innovation, company R&D and roles of small and large businesses, diffusion of innovations into use, and science. At the meso level, we examine industries, and the dynamics of science and industry. We consider theoretical models and empirical analysis, and their interrelationships; data sources on R&D, innovation, and technology; and appropriate policy. We ask questions and look at how to answer those questions through our own research.  
(Macroeconomic growth and international trade are mainly left to the second course.)

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

You must do all assigned readings, make notes on the readings, and practice to remember key points and methods. In the reading list in this syllabus, readings marked R* should be the primary focus of your attention, and should be studied closely. Readings marked R are also required, and you should pick up for each its main point, conclusions, and method(s). Other readings are optional, and I suggest you glance at them.  
We will discuss the readings in class. I will normally ask each student to prepare a *5-minute* discussion of one of the readings, stating its main contribution, what makes it influential, and flaws or shortcomings that can be addressed in follow-on research. (I am grateful to Kevin A. Bryan for this discussion format.) You should figure out how to make these points succinctly. That will leave most of the class time for other discussion.  
You need to retain knowledge of the required readings, including findings and methods. For some readings, I will give you questions, handouts with my notes including answers to questions, and/or self-study quizzes. Please use the questions, handouts, and self-study quizzes to help you learn and test your knowledge. Ask similar questions to yourself in case of readings for which I do not give you questions. For all required readings, regardless what materials I give you, keep your own notes. I suggest a single journal or file of notes for the entire course; you can use a word processor file, copy each reference, and type your notes beneath the reference.  
I will give you short quizzes in class as we go. Quizzes cover current and previous course content. Use the self-study quizzes, my questions and notes, and your own notes to prepare for in-class quizzes. Keep up to date with the self-study quizzes, and periodically review self-study quizzes and notes that you already studied so that you retain and improve your knowledge. Sometimes you will solve mathematical problems related to the readings. Solving and proving mathematical results builds core methods skills essential for economics. We all have different backgrounds and will have to adjust as we go. If it’s not easy to accomplish your learning goals, don’t worry, but do discuss your challenges in class and in office hours so that we can work on your reading approaches, thinking and analysis, and mathematical problem solving.

You will develop a paper on an innovation-related topic. The paper may be a group effort, if individual people’s contributions are clear and if everyone develops substantial research skills and seeks to verify or at minimum understand other people’s parts too. I encourage finding a topic that connects with your potential research and that could potentially become a publication and perhaps part of your dissertation, if you choose to develop the paper further. At minimum a paper should have a specific research plan with research question and motivation, key context citing relevant literature, methods, and potential interpretation. (For potential interpretation, either interpret your results, or explain alternative interpretations that might result from the study and how you would know that different interpretations seem to be the truth.) Cite literature in all sections as appropriate. Data or modeling proposed should be feasible. To the extent data can be obtained during the semester, please make a strong effort to complete initial data analyses and reporting. Your paper is due by the last day of classes of the semester. Let’s work together to work on the paper as we go, with some writing completed on your research paper or progress toward a paper at least every 2-3 weeks.
