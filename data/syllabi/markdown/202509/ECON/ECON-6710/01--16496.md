## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course aims to offer an introduction to microeconomic theory. It explores contemporary issues in economic analysis including consumer and producer behavior, interaction of firms and households, competitive equilibrium, general equilibrium theory, welfare theorems, externalities, uncertainty, and the implications for economic policy.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

**Required** Jehle and P. Reny: Advanced Microeconomic Theory, 3rd edition. Pearson, 2011 (**JR**).

**Optional** Mas-Collell, M. Whinston, and J. Green: Microeconomic Theory. Oxford University Press, 1995 (**MCW**).

We will also have readings from peer-reviewed articles. All assigned readings will be announced in lecture prior to when you are responsible for having read them.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- By the end of this course, the typical student should be able to (i) Derive and explain core results about decision-making by consumers and firms; (ii) Show proficiency in the expected utility model and its use in the economic analysis; and (iii)\&nbsp;Understand the general equilibrium analysis and policy implications

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Grading strategy**.

Exams (80% of your grade)

There will be two exams over the course of the semester. The midterm exam (exam #1) and final exam (exam #2) are not cumulative and will be in-class. The midterm will be made of the material covered from the beginning of the term until October 21, 2025. The final exam will be made of material covered after October 21, 2025. Consulting the course material or other resources will not be allowed during both exams (both exams are closed-book and closed-notes).

Problem sets (20%)

For each problem set, figuring out **the solution approach** is an important part of the course. You are responsible for the choice of the adequate solution approach. Although working in small groups is encouraged, solutions must be submitted individually. It is not necessary to type up the solutions.

Problem sets will be posted on the course website. Late work will be accepted but will be penalized following the rule

Grade *=* Problem Set Grade (1 - 0.5*D*), where *D* stands for the number of days late the problem set is handed in. When calculating your course grade, your lowest problem set grade will be replaced with your third-highest one.

## Course Calendar

### Calendar Details:

The course will consist, generally, of the following topics. Students must read the assigned chapters or papers before lecture. Important: Class content and readings are subject to change, contingent on mitigating circumstances and the progress of the class. Depending on the pace of lectures and in-class exercises, the order and timing of the following may change. Any changes will be announced in class and via LMS and updates to the syllabus will be posted to LMS as necessary.

**S1, 08/28 - 09/19: Consumer theory**

- Preference and utility
- Utility maximization
- Duality in consumption
- Properties of consumer demand
- *Revealed preference

**S2, 09/23 - 10/03: Theory of the firm**

- Production
- Cost
- Duality in production
- Profit maximization and supply

**S3, 10/07 - 10/24: Partial equilibrium**

- Perfect competition
- Imperfect competition
- Market efficiency

**S4, 10/28 - 11/14: Choice under uncertainty**

- Preferences over lotteries
- Expected utility
- Money lotteries and risk preferences

**S5, 11/18 - 12/09: General equilibrium**

- Pure exchange economies
- Existence and efficiency of competitive equilibrium
- Economies with production
- *Contingent commodities
- *Forward market and spot markets

*=time permitting

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own.
