## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • demonstrate in-depth knowledge of the history of behavioral economics and its application;
- • compare and contrast the approaches of behavioral economics and standard economics to modern issues of human behavior;
- • construct a meaningful new research question in the field of behavioral economics; and
- • design an empirical method of assessing said research question

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

You will be assessed on the above learning outcomes using a combination of quizzes, written exercises, and a research project. Specifically, you’re going to be asked to complete the following:

- Participation, Quizzes, and Miscellaneous Items: you are required to participate in all course discussions.
  - o **Quizzes:** each Tuesday, there will be a Quiz on the video lecture material covered for that week’s topic.
  - o **Discussions**: you can satisfy this part of your grade by contributing meaningfully to “in-class” discussion on CE and Paper Days. For CE days, this includes i) asking follow-up questions on video lecture material and ii) meaningfully participating in the communication exercise for that day (e.g. asking questions, contributing responses, engaging in group discussion). For Paper days, this includes i) signaling that you have read your assigned working paper, ii) asking engaging questions of your peers who read a different working paper, and iii) engaging in the student-led discussion for that day.
- Paper Discussions: you, along with 1 – 2 other students, will be responsible for leading a discussion of papers once throughout the semester. You are responsible for i) finding two papers on the module topic, ii) submit these papers to me for approval, iii) read your paper on your assigned day, and iv) lead discussion of your paper on your Paper day. I will lead discussion on Reference Dependence to give you a model of what this should look like.
- Research Project: since this course is Communication Intensive, there are several things required of you in this vein. To satisfy these requirements, you’ll be working in small groups (alone if you are registered for the 6000-level course). You will pick a topic and write several items, most due at the end of the semester, but present your work-in-progress along the way. We’ll talk more about this at a later date.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
