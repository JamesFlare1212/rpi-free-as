## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

## This course provides an introduction to experimental methods in economics. It teaches you how to test economic theories, as well as introducing you to the experimental methods that are used to investigate questions that may not be possible to study using existing data sets. Students will be introduced to state-of-the-art research methodology in experimental economics. Although not required, it is highly suggested that students take a statistics or econometrics course before taking this course.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Identify and understand the elements of experimental design
- Analyze and critique an experimental design
- Analyze human motivation and behavior across different economic paradigms
- Evaluate existing work on selected topics in behavioral and experimental economics
- Replicate a published experimental paper by running the experiment and analyzing the data, and then produce a replication report

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

## Course Assessment Measures

***Class Participation:***

- I will determine your grade based on your active class participation and general classroom citizenship. I expect you to show up on time and attend class, participate in and contribute to in-class discussions (by asking questions, providing answers or comments etc), do activities on Moblab, and read the materials that will be assigned. **In general, you are expected to join all lectures and be an active participant.**
- I will take your special circumstances into consideration. If your circumstances change, make sure to communicate this to me as soon as you can. I will keep track of your active engagement weekly.
- Please bring your laptops. However, you are expected to have all your electronics put away until they are needed for class. If you need your electronic device to take notes etc., you must obtain my prior approval.

***Leading Paper Discussion:***

- During each of the paper discussion days, we will discuss two papers in class. Each paper discussion will be led by 1-3 students (i.e. leaders) (depending on the class size). These papers will be chosen from a list of papers (provided by me) by discussion leaders.
- Discussion leaders will read their assigned paper very carefully (including the online appendices and experimental instructions, if available) and lead a discussion of the paper in class. They will come to class prepared with slides and a list of questions that you want to ask to class that will guide the discussion of the paper.
- In addition to their assigned paper, leaders are responsible to read the introduction of the other paper to be discussed and participate in the discussion.

***Grades:*** Your grade will be determined based on i) how well you understood the paper, and ii) your ability to foster an active and engaging discussion environment.

***Discussion Participation:***

- During each of the paper discussion days, we will discuss two papers in class. Half of the class will be assigned to one paper and the other half will be assigned to the other one. You will be responsible for reading your assigned paper very carefully. Additionally, you are also expected to read the introduction section of the other paper.
- Whether it is your assigned paper or not, all students are expected to participate in the discussion of both papers. Students are expected take an active role by asking leaders questions, sharing their thoughts, and making comments.

***Grades:*** Your grade will be determined by the quality of your participation in discussing of both papers.

***Quiz:***

- At the **end** of **each** paper discussion, we will randomly determine whether there will be a quiz (with a 50% chance). Each quiz will be about 5 min long and contain one or two questions.
- No make-up quiz will be given without a university-approved and documented excuse.

***Exams:***

- There will be two essay type closed book exams. Exam dates are indicated on the course calendar.
- No make-up exams will be given without a university-approved and documented excuse. If you know in advance that you have a conflict with a possible exam time, please see me as soon as possible so that we can work out an alternative.

***Research Project (******ECON 4360 Students******):***

You will be put in a group, and each group will be responsible for conducting one replication experiment. All replication experiments will be run using google forms or a similar online platform. More detailed instructions to come, but here are the main parts.

1. *Group Formation.* I will provide a list of potential papers for students to choose from. Each student will examine these papers and submit their preferences to me. Based on your preferences, I will put you in groups. However, you are also free to form your own group of four! There will be a total of 5 groups.
2. *Read the paper carefully*. You will read your assigned paper very carefully and understand their research in detail. You are expected to understand the motivation, research questions, and every detail about their experimental design. You should start thinking about how to implement it on an online platform while reading it.
3. *Group Project Meeting with Prof*. See course calendar for the exact date. Before this meeting, you must have read your paper very carefully, already discussed your plan with your group members, and have started working on your experimental materials. During the meeting, you will explain your paper to me in detail and we will discuss your replication plans. Schedule meetings with me if further discussions are needed.
4. *Prepare Experimental Materials*. This means that you will have to prepare all the materials that you will use to run your experiment (e.g. google forms). You will add me as a collaborator on your google form and share the screenshots (from the participants’ view not the experimenter’s) in a word document. I will go over your materials and provide feedback. You will revise your materials if needed. You will receive my feedback only twice, so make it count!
5. *Get ready for the experiment*. Incorporate my feedback on your experimental materials and update them. Schedule meetings with me if further discussions are needed.
6. *Conduct the experiment*. Your group will conduct the entire experiment. You will explain the instructions to the class, run the experiment, keep records, collect the data etc.
7. *Organize your data and analyze it*. Organize your data and start analyzing it. You should start working on your final project write-up and presentation slides. Meet with your TA or me if you need assistance.
8. *Present the replication findings in class*. Present a quick summary of the original paper, their motivation and research question. Then present your own data and compare your findings to theirs. In presenting your data, you will provide summary tables and graphs, calculate useful statistics, perform statistical tests, and discuss how your data compares to the original study. I will provide a template for this presentation.

*Evaluation*. At the end of your presentation, your classmates will evaluate your research. I will incorporate these evaluations to determine your grade.

9. *Final Project Write up*. You will submit a replication report (along with your experimental materials and your raw data). I will provide a template for this. This is the final product of your hard work!

*Evaluation*. We will have an in-group evaluation. This means that each group member will evaluate one another. I will incorporate these evaluations to determine your grade from the group project.

**Research Project Grades (ECON 4360 Students):**

Students will earn numerical grades using the following weighting:

*Meeting deadlines and overall group preparedness (10%)*

You are expected to meet all deadlines. You will be penalized for each deadline you miss.

*Quality of Experimental Materials (25%)*

The quality of your experimental materials submitted during the first round and how closely you follow my instructions to revise them during the second round will help me determine your grade.

*Presentation (30%)*

The quality of your presentation and how closely you follow my instructions will help me determine your grade.

*Final Project Write up (35%)*

The overall quality of your replication project write-up, data analysis and presentation, how well you follow my instructions etc. will help me determine your grade.

***Based on my own observations as well as the feedback provided by your group members, I will revise your final grade. Thus, it is possible that not all group members will receive the same grade.***
