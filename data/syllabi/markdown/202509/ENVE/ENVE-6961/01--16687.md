## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The graduate-level course will investigate theories and principles that are used for the design and operation of photochemical reactors. Different ultraviolet (UV) emitting sources and UV radiation measurement techniques will be presented. Subsequent lectures will focus on numerical and experimental methods used for characterizing and validating photochemical reactors. Finally, different applications of UV radiation will be discussed including advanced oxidation processes (AOPs), water/wastewater disinfection, and the inactivation of airborne pathogens such as COVID-19. Students will read and reflect on the latest scientific papers on the topics of UV disinfection and photochemical reactions.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students will learn the fundamental principles of photochemistry and photochemical processes that are commonly used. These fundamental principles will be used as a foundation to explore the behavior of photochemical reactors and methods used to evaluate and predict their behavior, including analytical and numerical methods.

Applications of UV radiation, including:

- Disinfection of water and wastewater,
- Disinfection of air and surfaces.
- Chemical transformations by direct photolysis, and
- Photooxidation and photoreduction processes.

The focus of this course will be on fundamental concepts, so as to prompt their application in a wide range of settings, encourage questions and discussion, and promote creativity. Students will develop their critical thinking and communication skills from the evaluation of essential research publications and formal presentations.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Perform kinetics calculations for photochemical reactions based on electromagnetic energy and photons for monochromatic and polychromatic radiation sources.
- Describe the process of UV radiation generation from different sources and radiometric measurements.
- Apply fluence field and segregated flow models to predict the dynamic behavior of photochemical reactors.
- Describe the applications of UV-based photochemical reactors (e.g., photolysis, UV/AOPs) for microbial inactivation and chemical degradation, such as COVID-19 and NDMA, and explain the associated limitations.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* (found at www.rpi.edu/dept/doso/handbook.html) defines various forms of academic dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Students should familiarize themselves with this portion of the *Handbook* and should note that the penalties for academic dishonesty can be quite severe.

In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work, although students are encouraged to work with each other by collaborating on assignments (see item 2 under *Homework Policy* above). Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy (e.g., one student copying work done by another student or a student copying from a previously developed solution) will result in a grade of zero for that particular assignment. Also, cheating on an exam will result in a grade of zero for that exam and, in accordance with the *Handbook*, notification to the Dean of Students Office of the incident of academic dishonesty.
