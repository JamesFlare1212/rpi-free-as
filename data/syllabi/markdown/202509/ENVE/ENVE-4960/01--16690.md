## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Principles of environmental engineering are used to design effective engineered systems that promote public health and environmental quality. In this course, students will be guided to identify a real-world problem. Appropriate parameters will be obtained and used for the design of a theoretical innovative solution. The system will be tested and optimized with bench-scale experiments. Final deliverables include written reports, software products, and/or a prototype, depending on the nature of the work. Students will be required to submit their work to regional and national competitions.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course allows students to work collaboratively to plan, design, and build engineering solutions to one or more tasks assigned by WERC (Waste Management Education-Research Conference).

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Learn how to identify important design variables for solving environmental problems
- Synthesize and apply engineering principles to formulate a systematic solution to design problems
- Use bench-scale experiments to elucidate and optimize the proposed design solution.
- Prepare a technical report with project information and design solutions

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* (found at [www.rpi.edu/dept/doso/handbook.html](http://www.rpi.edu/dept/doso/handbook.html)) defines various forms of academic dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Students should familiarize themselves with this portion of the *Handbook* and should note that the penalties for academic dishonesty can be quite severe.

In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work, although students are encouraged to work with each other by collaborating on assignments. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy (e.g., one student copying work done by another student or a student copying from a previously developed solution) will result in a grade of zero for that particular assignment. Also, cheating on an exam will result in a grade of zero for that exam and, in accordance with the *Handbook*, notification to the Dean of Students Office of the incident of academic dishonesty.
