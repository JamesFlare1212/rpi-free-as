## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Welcome! This course’s content is divided into three chunks. The first is the basics: philosophical methodology, core concepts in ethics, and normative ethical theories. The second chunk is specific topics in bioethics (abortion, eugenics, vaccine ethics, right to die, anti-natalism, disability and disease, etc.). The third chunk of content is student generated: in groups, research and discuss a topic of shared concern or interest. Create an engaging, useful in-class presentation on that topic.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

- Students should develop an explicit (rather than tacit) understanding of the ethical principles, frameworks, practices, and underlying assumptions that guide decision-making in medicine, public health, and the life sciences.
- Students should be able to develop and defend positions in bioethics. While some questions are more abstract, many arise in everyday clinical, research, and policy contexts; students ought to leave the course better prepared to reason effectively about these dilemmas.
- Develop the ability to communicate clear and effective ethical analysis and argumentation—both orally and in writing (e.g., case discussions, philosophical debates).
- Distinguish and evaluate competing bioethical arguments, including how they use facts, values, stakeholder perspectives, and empirical evidence.
- Be able to plan and work collaboratively with others on challenging, interdisciplinary case studies and projects. Be able to give and receive feedback on communication practices.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grades will be determined as follows. There are two tracks (or “pathways”) through which you can earn a grade in this course.

The **first track** involves writing an essay instead of completing any of the take-home exams. This essay will count as 50% of your final grade. The paper is 7-15 pages in length. Students will be responsible for proposing a paper topic and getting it approved. There is a draft process for this paper. It is your responsibility to propose a topic within the first few classes (before the first exam is assigned) and meet with me at least three times: once to approve the topic and general structure of the paper, a second time to review progress on developing the argumentation and writing, and a third time to review a full first draft. You will then receive comments for editing a final submission.

NOTE: This track will be, on average, about *twice* as much work as the second track. This option still might be worth it for some students for the following reason. (1) You have a deep and narrow interest in one specific issue that you want to develop expertise in. (2) You are trying to develop a writing sample for a job or graduate work. (3) You are developing arguments in preparation for a capstone project related to Bioethics. (4) You are a philosophy major and already have competence in independent philosophical research and argumentation.

The **second track** involves completing two cooperative take-home exams instead of writing the individual essay. Each take home exam will be worth 25% of your final grade. Here is what take-home exams look like. The exams are open-book/note/interest, with no time-limit. There will be 7-10 questions or so, and you pick 5 questions to answer. As a rough heuristic, an answer should be about 1.5-2 pages in length (double-spaced), so roughly 10 pages of writing in total. You will be assigned a partner when the exam is made available. Over multiple meetings, you and your partner will submit one exam. Estimated time to complete work each exam: about 7-8 hours.

NOTE: This track is for students who desire a basic mastery in the subject matter of the course or are taking the course simply as a HASS credit, of communication intensive credit. This track may be desirable for students who have an especially heavy workload, are working a job in addition to school, dealing with excess extra stress or anxiety, etc.

Optionally, you are welcome to complete both take-home exams, and the paper (and I’ll grade you based on which track would have returned the highest grade), but I would **strongly advise** against this. You’ll be overworked, and my intuition tells me that this would be **unlikely to significantly improve** your grade (instead, just devote more effort and attention to a smaller number of assignments, right?).

Each student will participate in a group presentation project worth 40%, which involves groups of 2-4 students working on a 25-30 minute presentation. I will be setting aside multiple class periods specifically to work on this project with your group members, although you will need to meet with group members and work on the project outside of class time as well.  
All students will be asked to submit a small number (think between eight to twelve short quizzes on the assigned reading and lecture content. These quizzes in total will make up 10% of your final grade. Quizzes will be completed in-class. They are designed to be completed quickly and to be low stakes (I’ll drop your two lowest quiz grades). This is what letter grades mean:

A 93-100; A- 90-92; B+ 87-89; B 83-86; B- 80-82; C+ 77-79; C 73-76; C- 70-72; D+ 67-69; D 60-66; F 0-59

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is mandatory. Each unexcused absence beyond the second will result in the subtraction of three percentage points for the final course grade for each day that you are absent from class. If for example, you have a final grade of 90% but have three unexcused absences, then your final grade will be lowered to 87% because of that one unexcused absence beyond the second. Of course, not all absences are unexcused. An absence is excused if (a) you let me know you will be out of class beforehand and we plan accordingly or, if that is not possible, as soon as you can afterward and (b) you are absent for a reason that is either documented or can be documented. I reserve the right to request documentation. For example: Court date, death in the family, etc. “Woke up late,” or “just wasn’t feeling it today,” or no documentation would be an unexcused absence. I am willing to be extremely flexible and reasonable about what counts as unexcused provided you email me ahead of time.
