## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**About This Class**

Design as a practice and profession dates to the “outbreak” of the Industrial Revolution, when mass production definitively separated the manufactured object from the direct hand of the artisan, or roughly from the time RPI was founded in 1824. Over the following 200 years, according to the International Council of Design (ICOD), designers not only designed physical objects, physical spaces, and digital environments using various technical and corporate methodologies, but in service to the imperatives of industry, “became agents of ‘growth’ through ever-expanding consumption by generating stereotypes of ‘success’ and objects of ‘desire.’” Today, issues of sustainability, environmental degradation, cultural imperialism, mental health, accessibility, and the “Disneyification” of the planet (among many other issues) have brought into question the central tenet of design, specifically that designers are critical for making a world that is ever more humane, livable, and enjoyable.

In this class we will explore the history of design, the history of that history (its “still in development” historiography), key figures in this history, various corporate design processes, and the politics that are the inevitable result of industrial design processes and ideologies interacting with the cultures in which they interface and are an evolving part.

We will also touch on a few fundamentals of design, including typography, logo design, and interface design; major art and design movements, including Dada, streamlining, modernism, the automobile-created landscape, and Googie; and the major cultural forces, technologies and events that have driven design, including civil rights, mass media, wars, the development of new materials, computing, disability awareness, etc.

The course will be very interactive and participatory. As the parameters of the topic are nearly infinite, students will be encouraged to raise questions and express enthusiasms as they relate to the politics of design and their other academic pursuits.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

*In this class, you will learn how to:*

- Better see the designed world that surrounds you and how it is influencing or attempting to influence your behaviors and beliefs
- Contextualize and participate in contemporary cultural debates through the lens of design history
- Gain a nuanced awareness of the possibilities of practice and politics of design in building more inclusive, sustainable, and rich human environments

## Course Content

### Content Details:

- Designers & design ideologies
- Design process & politics
- Branding, advertising, and status
- Art movements; art vs. design
- Architecture, urban planning, and historic preservation
- Political cartoons
- Design as a handmaiden of capitalism vs design as the savior of the world
- Control vs the carnival

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- (1) Understand the social, cultural and political origins of the designs we find around us;
- (2) Interpret ways in which political ideas are represented in design;
- (3) Discuss how design affects people's orientation toward power, authority, community, and freedom;
- (4) Learn how an understanding of the significance of design might influence future attempts to build humane, sustainable, democratic structures to aid human activity;

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Time Investment**

There is no textbook for *Politics of Design,* but there will be many reading assignments, as well as video and podcast reviews. **NOTE: It will take time to view the assigned media.** And as you will find, many of the media assets assigned will need to be viewed multiple times in order to extract the information available within them. You must give yourself time to read and watch, and then re-read and re-watch with a critical eye, assigned media.

**Presentations**

There will be very few traditional writing assignments in *Politics of Design.* Instead, the class will be largely built around short live PowerPoint presentations based on readings, videos, podcasts, and other assets plus original research. Students will be regularly assigned the task of creating a short PPT (you can use any tool you feel comfortable with) which they will upload to the LMS. Most PPT assignments will be simple “cut-and-paste” affairs. On each PPT due date, 1-3 students will be selected at random to present their homework PPT assignments. No original copy required, and images can be taken right form the web or your photo app (though casual citations will be required). The Photo Essay and the Term Project presentations will be more formal academic assignments, 5 to 7 minutes, with properly formatted citations.

**Meetings**:

We will meet twice a week for a seminar style discussion. Attendance will be taken. Everyone should come to class fully prepared to talk about the assigned readings, videos, podcasts, or other meida for that week and their own ideas about them.

**Communication Intensive:**

Regular, *active participation is required* and is an important part of your grade. I will call on people directly, more or less at random. As a CI course you will develop and practice key communication skills to carry into the future. The modes of communication in this class will be expressed through writing, speaking, visually and technologically.
