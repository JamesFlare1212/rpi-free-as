## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

We will not use a textbook in the course but journal articles and excerpts from books.  
All assigned readings are selected primary and secondary sources which will be available on the class LMS site as we go along.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- By the end of this course, students will be able to: - Better understand the relations between war, culture, and society. - Appreciate the wide range of cultural perspectives on war. - Identify ways in which war engages with society. - Relate class material to contemporary issues. - Gain a basic understanding of some key concepts and theories in social sciences and the ability to apply them to present-day debates.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

## 1. Readings

Students must think about questions coming from the readings and email them to the instructor before each class.

## 2. Class Presentations

Each student will conduct some original research on a theme of her/his choice related to course material.

## 3. Exams:

There will be three in-class exams scheduled during the class period.

The three exams correspond to the major sections of the class. The last exam covers mostly the last section but may include questions on the whole class.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grading is based on a total of 100 points (100%).

\- Exams: 75 points (25 points each).

\- Presentation: 20 points.

\- Participation / Attendance / questions on readings: 5 points

A single poor performance will weight lower than other assignments.

Students may appeal grades after the assignment at the end of the class or during office hours.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. Students and teachers will act in honesty. The Rensselaer Handbook defines various forms of academic dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Students should familiarize themselves with this portion of the Rensselaer Handbook and should note that the penalties for plagiarism (copying sentences verbatim from a published source in a term paper without referencing) and other forms of cheating can be quite harsh.

Cheating on an exam. Anyone cheating on an exam will be given a zero for that exam. Two cheating episodes will result in an F in the course and may be reported for academic discipline. No notes, crib sheets or other written materials are allowed during exams. Looking at other student’s work is not allowed.

Do not write anything on an exam paper returned to you after grading by the Instructor. If an exam paper has new markings, this will disqualify you from any consideration of re-grading. Anyone submitting an altered exam for re-grading will be given an F in the corresponding exam.
