## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Studio B is intended to promote designing for the future. In this class, we’ll use this science fictional framework to organize our collective work, considering how people have cast the future and its dangers and how people, today, talk about technology, design, and artificiality and nature. These are ongoing concerns in Science and Technology Studies (and across the social sciences and humanities) and students will engage with a variety of readings that stimulate creative approaches to design and its role in making specific futures. As foundations for this work, students will be introduced to a variety of key texts in the social sciences, the humanities, social theory, and science fiction; the aim is to think across these varied texts about categories like the “artificial” and the “natural” and their place in human societies.

One of the primary goals of this course is to aid students in telling narratives through objects. Scholars often argue that objects have narratives embedded in them (e.g., this is how this object should be used, this is where this object comes from) and shape how they are interacted with through the narratives that users bring to them (e.g., this is how you use this object). How can we shape these narratives as designers, either to uphold specific ideas about objects, their origins, uses, and futures, or subvert these narratives in productive ways?

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to analyze historical exhibitions for their content as design objects;
- Students will develop proficiency in community-based design methods employing qualitative methods;
- Students will work collaboratively to develop individual and shared object-based narratives.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Analyze an Object (5 in-class at 1 point each, 5 out of class at 1 point each, 1 out of class at 5 points)**—See Appendix A for full details. Over the course of the semester, we will watch several films and TV show episodes in class as the basis for discussing a specific futuristic object. In addition, several short stories will be read, which will provide the basis for out of class assignments. During Week 6, this assignment is associated with a novel that you will read and prepare an object-focused report on. Only readings labeled with [Analyze an Object eligible] are eligible for this assignment.

**Historical Exhibition Reports (2 at 10 points each)**—See Appendix B for full details. You will visit a historical museum and describe an exhibit of your choice from a design perspective. What does it include? What kind of story does it seek to tell? How does it fit in the context of the museum? Who is its audience? These reports will include photographs that you take as well as a written critique of 3-4 pages, all to be presented in class.

**Constituency Reports (2 at 10 points each)**—See Appendices C & G for full details. You will conduct a series of interviews with members of the potential constituency of the Museum of Unnatural History. What do people want in a museum? In specific exhibits? How do they understand key ideas that are motivating our project? How can these insights be used to shape your design practice? You will write up a summary analysis (4-5 pages) and present your findings in class.

**Pitch an Exhibit & Revised Pitch (10 points and 15 points)**—See Appendix D for full details. Based on your exhibition r eports and the first constituency report, you will prepare a pitch for an individually produced exhibit and present it to the class for feedback. Your exhibit will include at least one item and one narrative description of the item and its historical and social context. Based on your Pitch an Exhibit, students will develop collaborations that lead to their revised pitch (See Appendix E).

**Design Fiction (15 points)**—See Appendix F for full details. Working with your exhibit group, you will write a design fiction that situates your exhibit historically and socially. This will be done in reference to the readings in class to date. You will present a draft of your design fiction to the class for feedback, revise it, and then use it as the basis of your second constituency report and your final exhibit.

**Waystation (10 points)**—This is intended as a first critique and you should have your object and exhibit fully prototyped and drafted. You will present these materials in class and receive feedback from the class. Incomplete prototypes and exhibition content drafts will be ineligible for full points for this assignment.

**Final Synthesis (10 points)**—See Appendix H for full details. In 6-8 pages and with reference to course content, you will provide a reflection on your object, exhibition, and independent and collaborative work from the semester. This is an opportunity to demonstrate your facility with course content and how your object and exhibit engage with theoretical and methodological approaches in Science and Technology Studies.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
