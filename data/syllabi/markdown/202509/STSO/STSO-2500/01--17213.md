## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course surveys major political, social, cultural and economic developments in American history. Although core course material focuses on the domestic history of the United States, course lectures and activities will place special emphasis on the international context of United States history.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- ● Identify major political, social, cultural and economic developments in American history.
- ● Describe historical methods and the ways in which historians approach study of the past.
- ● Discuss key scholarly debates related to American history.
- ● Explain how international circumstances have influenced the history of the United States

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Class attendance and participation**

Attendance **and participation** in online class meetings and site visits are required. Participation means posing thoughtful questions and responding to the questions of others in ways that demonstrate completion of and reflection upon course readings and meetings.

**In-class reading presentations**

Over the course of the semester, each student will play the role of discussion leader for one day of class. On the assigned day, the discussion leader will begin discussion of **at least one** of the assigned readings for that day by providing a five-minute summary of the reading or readings. The student will then pose at least three questions related to the reading, which we will answer during class discussion.

Presentation schedules will be coordinated during the first week of the semester. **Except in the case of official excused absences, students who fail to attend class or present on their assigned day will not be able to make up the assignment**.

**Identification quizzes**

On five occasions throughout the semester, you will complete an in-class identification quiz. In each quiz, you will be presented with 6 terms related to the course. You must identify each one by writing 1-2 sentences explaining what the term means and why it is relevant to the course. Quizzes will be graded on a pass/fail basis.

The quizzes cannot be made up, even for excused absences; however, you may miss one quiz over the course of the semester without penalty.

**Comprehensive essay**

As a course wrap-up assignment, you will complete a short essay in which you summarize key themes from the course. Additional details are available on LMS. The essay is due by the deadline indicated on LMS.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
