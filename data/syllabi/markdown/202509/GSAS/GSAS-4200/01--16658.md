### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

This course explores methods for 3D computer lighting, rendering, and camera authoring. We will discuss strong foundational theory from film in regard to lighting and cinematography. The course will focus on producing high fidelity rendered images and video for animation, games, and digital art.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand the basics of cinematography.
- Compare the mechanics and use cases of traditional and real-time render engines.
- Produce original still and animated works for portfolio use.
- Deploy AOV (arbitrary output variable) data.
- Utilize 3D compositing to parse and assemble render data.
- Discuss completed projects in a critique format.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

This course is primarily project based. This means that you will be working across multiple class sessions on a project. 70% of your final grade will be the average grade of these projects. The remainder will be based on in-class participation. Therefore, while a large percentage of your grade is based on the outcome of your work, attendance and participation is critical.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

GRADING CRITERIA

- Criteria for project grading will include, but is not limited to, technical proficiency, creativity, craftsmanship, and presentation.
- Completed projects are due at the beginning of class on the stated date and time.
- Assignments not completed on due date/time will be lowered one letter grade.
- Participation includes being prepared to work on projects in class, participating in discussions and critiques, and being open to constructive suggestions from the instructor and classmates.
- Students have the option to redo or revise any project for an improved grade.

GRADING PERCENTAGE BREAKDOWN

- Participation and Improvement 30%
- Projects 70%

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

While deciding who the "author" is in artistic production can be a thorny process, in this class, all work must be made WHOLLY by you. If there are any exceptions to this rule, I will inform you in advance. This means: * If a model is required for a project, **you and you alone** will be doing that modeling. * If a rig needs to be constructed, **you and you alone** will be the one constructing it. * If a script needs to be written, **you and you alone** will write that script. The first infraction against this policy will result in failure of the project. The second infraction will result in failure of the course and a report to the Office of the Dean.
