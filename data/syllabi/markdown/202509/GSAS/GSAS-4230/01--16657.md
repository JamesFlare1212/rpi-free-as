## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Game Animation is a course that focuses on animation techniques, workflows, and software specific to the needs of video games. While principles from other animation pipelines will still be helpful and apply, this course specifically looks at the needs of game animators, both 2D and 3D.

Game animation is often about thinking in loops, and in responsive animation workflows. Animations often need to be bespoke and completely ad hoc to the controls. to allow for players to input whatever they wish. This means game animators spend alot of their time generating lots of transition, looping, and edge case animations so they can pull animations to respond to the player's actions in any situation. These are often managed through state machines in the engine, and require extensive animation suites in the DCC.

Students will be working with all types of character rigs in Maya, creature rigs, and other custom assets to prepare them for technical implementation in Unreal Engine 5. They will also be managing some technical animation polish in the engine as well, to better understand how things like player control and environment interaction function.

Games are often character and combat centric, so the subject matter of this course will often focus on these parameters, but will not be exclusive.

Students should already feel comfortable in DCC's like Maya, with basic rigging, driven keys, morph targets, animation layers, constraints, and basic engine usage.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. **2D Pixel Suite -** Students will be putting together a suite of animations for a single 2D pixel character.
   1. ![](https://img.itch.zone/aW1hZ2UvMzE2MDk5MS8xODg3NzcyMC5naWY=/original/nRsADN.gif)
2. **Combat** **Chain/Combo**- Students will be putting together a chain or combo of 3D combat animations on a single character.
3. **Constraints** - Students will be utilizing constraints to have a 3D character interact with their environment/props.
4. **State Machine** - Students will be assembling a state machine with blend spaces and other technical animation tools in Unreal Engine.
   1. ![](https://lms.rpi.edu/bbcswebdav/pid-897465-dt-content-rid-16308697_1/xid-16308697_1)
5. **Environment Animation/Sequencing/Cinemetography** - Students will be assembling a short film focusing on cinematography and sequencing in Unreal.
   1. ![](https://bunny-wp-pullzone-j4tpretgnf.b-cdn.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ntrfoge4n5re1-ezgif.com-optimize.gif)
6. **Implementation/Simulation** - Students will be setting up animations to be functional with an existing system, while also implementing extra animation like simulation, trails, etc.
   1. ![](https://img.edc-cdn.net/imgproxy/G3gKyo0WOByWh_42krF7-_dECebPAPO-aH0-LVGYJQE/filename:run1.gif/resizing_type:fit/width:0/height:0/aHR0cHM6Ly9kMWl2N2RiNDR5aGd4bi5jbG91ZGZyb250Lm5ldC9pbWFnZXMvZmI3NGQ5OWMtMjYzZC00NThjLWE2MjItN2QyYmE4ZWMyMDYwL3J1bjEuZ2lm)
7. **MOCAP** - Time permitting students will record a short motion capture animation, clean, and parent it in Maya.
   1. ![OptiTrack Slimx 120 Motion Capture Camera - Robotic Gizmos](https://www.roboticgizmos.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/09/OptiTracks-Motive-3D-motion-capture-software.gif)
8. **Final Project** - The final project is open for interpretation and will be negotiated with the instructor depending on scope, but students are encouraged to revisit one of the assignments in the semester and expand upon it.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Create character animations for 3D and 2D game pipelines
- Analyze effective game animation principles and styles
- Implement animations into game engines in an industry standard environment
- Create narrative, technical, MOCAP, and camera cinematography animation

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

##### Grading

Grading standards for this class are subjectively mid range. Animation is not as saturated in the industry as highly competitive fields such as concept art or narrative design, but it is not as desperately underrepresented as a field such as technical art or engine programming. Grading Standards will reflect this.

| A | A's denote work that is at industry standards or slightly below. Work is on time, named correctly, contains all the elements the assignment denotes, and elements are worked at or beyond reference styles. |
| --- | --- |
| B | B's denote work that contains all elements in the assignment, but may not be worked to a level that is ready for industry implementation. The work may only be a short amount of time and work away from being ready. |
| C | C's denote work that is missing elements required for the assignment, or elements are clearly not rendered to an industry standard. It would require significant time and effort to raise the quality of the work, or possibly starting all over. |
| D | D's denote work that are not only missing elements from the assignment, is possibly late, not named correctly, but the execution of the work barely moves past the rough layout stage. It does not resemble the style guides provided by the student. |
| F | F's denote work missing major elements of the assignment, are submitted late, not named correctly, and does not demonstrate any of the practiced techniques or learning outcomes in class. |

4. State Machine -10%

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

##### Policies

**Late Work** *-* There is no late work in this class. Assignments are extensive and take time to complete. Getting behind only means you will have less time to complete the next assignment, etc. Part of the work is being able to complete it in the time given, which is already generous. Submit early and often, all assignments are open for unlimited submissions. Do not be tempted by midnight submissions, assume something will go wrong.

**Professionalism** **-** This is your attendence, preperation, and participation in the class. Have the necessary software installed, ask questions, engage with your classmates, show up on time.

**Purposeful Ambiguity** - Creative fields can be frustrating when feedback is not specific, but it is an unfortunate part of the industry. You must acclimate yourself to working with limited feedback at times. The instructor will always step in with more directed feedback or paintover work when progress has stalled, but be prepared to deal with times where you must creatively work through ambiguity.

**Sensitive Topics** - Like with any creative/artistic course there may be a discussion of senstive topics. These topics will always be dicussed neutrally, and from an academic perspective. The instructor will always do their best to handle them elegantly and use them as learning opportunities.

**AI & Plagiarism -** AI usage is considered plagiarism and will be treated like any other plagarism case and will be reported to the University for disciplinary action.
