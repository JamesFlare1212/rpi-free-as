## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Experimental Game Design is an upper level intensive studio focusing on the creation of innovative, workable game prototypes using various media. The class will prompt students with three different thematic, aesthetic, social, or mechanical parameters to help students become agile with the practice of prototyping: working quickly to create an experimental and playable game that effectively proves or disproves a design concept or the exploration of a unique concept. Students' games are analyzed as cultural artifacts and by their concept, function, style.

This analysis is applied to the creation of a mega project which demonstrates depth and quality of ideation, innovation, and interaction. It is student driven in subject, design, and development. The project must be fully functional and be accompanied by a detailed advanced game design document.

A sequel project follows that amplifies or perfects previous work or delves into another experimental game project.

Students work in trans-disciplinary collaborative teams whose members learn by thinking, experimenting, creating, making, resonating, and reiterating. Students present their work in the studio in a series of formal and informal critiques that help to guide and expand their iterative process.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Create an archeological, socio-cultural and ethical overview of one own history of game and toy preferences as both a child and as an adult
- identify current cultural issues from class discussions and create a short workable\&nbsp;game project that evolves out of the theme of “games respond to the big questions of our time” \<b>\&nbsp;\</b>
- create a short workable game project about the issue of “Connectivity”
- create an original large-scale, innovative, fully functional game project that goes beyond conventional style gaming paradigms and expresses depth of creative goals, sensitivity to social issues, and exhibits quality of interaction.
- successfully articulate informed ideas relating to the representation of gender, race, and behavior in games and simulations and an increased awareness of games as cultural and aesthetic artifacts as demonstrated in class discussions and critiques and in short written reaction papers to relevant readings and events
- create a comprehensive game design document, poster, project overview statement and game trailer
