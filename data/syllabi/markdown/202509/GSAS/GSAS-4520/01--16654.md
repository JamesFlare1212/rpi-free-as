## Course Goals

### Goals:

- Students will learn the basics of using multiple game development engines.
- Students will create 2D and/or 3D games in interdisciplinary teams.
- Students will apply game design principles in developing games in response to creative prompts.
- Students will effectively utilize version control and project management software in game development.
- Students will develop collaboration skills with an emphasis on the remote-team context.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Course Description**

In this intensive studio production course, students work in interdisciplinary teams to create short digital games using multiple game engine technologies and a variety of creative design prompts. Developing video games takes a myriad of talents: art, programming, design, and most importantly, the ability to work in a team and communicate with others.

The course will be broken into 2 projects based on game engine, with the first 6 week project in Unity, and the 2nd 8 week project in Unreal. The projects will be themed around a selected game genre from below:

- Platformer
- Roguelite
- Card Game

Students will have a chance to pitch a game in any subject, pitch jointly, or abstain and select from the pitches which game they would like to work on. Teams will be assembled by the professors for both projects based on project needs.

The first project will be 2D, and the 2nd project will be 3D. Students should begin familiarizing themselves with Unreal’s blueprints, and other editors early, so they can be ready to begin development when the midway point switch happens. The 2nd project will require controller input as the primary control mechanism. There will be some crash courses in programming in Unreal in class though.

Students will dictate what type of role they would like to take on for the project:

- Art
- Programming
- Audio
- Design
- Narrative
- Management

Some roles like Audio or Narrative may be asked to work on multiple projects.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Grading**

The majority of your grade for each project will be assessed at various points and in a 3 part package including:

- A personal statement of your work (gifs, code, music, etc.)
- A summary of your logged time (Notion)
- An anonymous assessment from your team on your performance

15% of your grade will also be assed under “Professionalism” which is comprised of attendance, punctuality, participation, tutorials, post mortems, and presentation.

Cancellation and team recomposition does not affect your grade.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Grading Criteria**

- Collaboration/communication
  - How well did you work together as a team?
  - Assessed via in-class discussion, Perforce, Notion
- Work quality (in your discipline)
  - How much time and effort was put in?
  - Assessed via playing the game and Perforce
- Technical accuracy
  - Does the game play cleanly and well?
  - Did the team use version control correctly?

*While this is often a direct responsibility of programming, everyone on the team is held responsible for this.

- Assessed via playing the game and Perforce

- Efficiency
  - How much progress was made during the sprint?
  - Was progress consistent and substantial?
  - Assessed via playing the game, Perforce, Basecamp

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Trust: Student-Teacher relationships are built on trust. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. And, teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts, which violate this trust, undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

Plagiarism: All work produced in this course must be original and created by the student. Incidents of academic dishonesty or attempted academic dishonesty will result in grade penalties and may be referred to the Dean of Students. While the creation of digital artwork may utilize prior works for inspiration, reference, and source material, assignments in this class are expected to represent the student’s originality and technical ability through the creation of substantially new works. When in doubt, ask your instructor.

AI: As Artificial Intelligence Creation methods become more prevalent, students will be able to utilize them in industry congruent workflows. As AI still can not be copyrighted and is not prevalently used in final products, AI will not be allowed to demonstrate final art capabilities.

AI can be used for proof of concept, art direction, and conceptualization. And all usages should be cited in the design document. Prompts, software, and appearances in pitches, documents, mood boards, etc.

Premade Assets: Existing asset libraries such as cubebrush, epic games store, artstation, etc. should be used for quick ideation, greyboxing, proof of concepts, prototypes, etc. Generally these assets should be replaced over time, and especially if recognizable. Keep a record of these assets in your design document as they are implemented. \*No premade assets for the first project.\*

Purposeful Ambiguity: This course is treated as a production course. And often the guidelines will be vague, abstract, or not specific.

- This is done to mimic an industry level setting where developers must be comfortable working with loose direction from their Creative and Art directors.
- It may be frustrating at first, but it is necessary to become a more unique and professional developer.
  - The instructors will always be available to help when necessary.

Discussion/production of sensitive topics: As this is a creative field it is possible that there will be production of subject matter that some may deem offensive, inappropriate, etc. The instructors will make every effort to keep the discussion and production of such material safe, productive, rich, and uncensored.

- Art often challenges our operating assumptions, and a university setting makes sure to do that as often as possible.
