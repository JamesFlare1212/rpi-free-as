## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

# Course Description

In this seminar we will build an understanding of games as technologies of collective imagination, looking at how they can alter our perception of the world around us, direct our imagination towards better alternatives, and guide us towards practices that shape reality. We'll apply our insights to developing games that apply imaginative overlays to local real-world spaces.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

# Readings

Each week the seminar will discuss a set of texts, which will often be academic writing but may include fiction, poetry, games, or other media considered by the seminar to in constellation with the emergent themes of the seminar.

The reading program presented below is intended as an initial plan that sets the general pace and direction of the seminar, but each week the seminar membership will review the program and discuss any changes that might be made to keep the texts in line with the emergent goals and themes of the seminar. No changes will be permitted less than two weeks in advance, to allow seminar members to plan ahead.

A selection of other likely possibilities for readings is included below, but new seminar members may propose readings not mentioned here. Changes to the readings will be made by majority vote. Any member of the seminar may veto a change, which can be overridden by a 2/3 majority.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

## Seminar Participation

Seminar participation includes attending seminar sessions, engaging in discussion, and any other expectations developed by the seminar members (e.g. filling particular roles or shared duties developed together). One unexcused absence will not affect this grade, but any absences beyond that should be discussed with the instructor and/or seminar.

## Commonplace Book / Slipbox

Seminar members will develop a regular practice for recording fleeting notes, permanent notes, notable passages, seminar notes, and literature notes.

## Seminar Papers

Students will write two 1000-word papers that synthesizes themes and concepts emerging in the seminar and explores them through a ludic case study of the student’s choosing. The case study can be a hypothetical or imagined game that does not yet exist.

Seminar papers are due the week before they will be discussed. All seminar members will read the papers, which will provide the basis for discussion the week after they are submitted.

## Final Project

Students will develop a creative project in a medium of their choice, accompanied by a 3,000 word paper that positions the project as a case study of the themes explored in the seminar. This paper may incorporate revised text from the seminar papers.

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

This seminar is structured as a collaborative membership body, with the institutionally assigned instructor serving as the chair of the seminar. Other officers may be created as necessary. The creation of officers or any other changes to the seminar will be made by amendment to this syllabus. Unless otherwise specified, amendments require a two-thirds majority vote. The chair of the seminar may veto an amendment. The membership may appeal a veto by seeking mediation through the GEM Graduate Program Director or their appointed designee.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
