## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

“This course surveys 5000 years of game history, from ancient Sumer/Sumerian to the latest next-generation consoles and MMOGs. In parallel with this historical tour, several major theories will be examined about the nature of play and the nature of games. Along the way, it will also look at how games and play influence the cultures they are found in, and how culture in turn influences how people structure their leisure time will also be considered.”

## Course Log

You will keep a log of the course in a composition book. The course log is comparable to a lab notebook in a laboratory science course, but the content includes **all** aspects of the course

- A running list of required entries will be kept in the shared course files.
- You will submit your log on each exam date except for the final exam
- Each course log submission is weighted as equal to 2 homework assignments

- Additional entries for extra credit:
  - 5 extra entries add 1% to final grade
  - A list of suggested entry types will be kept in the shared course files
  - This can only be done once per log submission

### Course Log Specifications

A main purpose of the course log is to explore possibility within structure. As such, meeting the following specifications is essential to your log grade.

**Each specification not met will reduce the log grade by 5%.**

- Notebook is actually a composition book
- Name and Course on the Cover
- The first page says "Table of Contents" at the top
- The first 5 pages of are reserved for the table of contents
- The table of contents is up to date with a title for each entry and page numbers in a column on the far right
- The bottom right of every odd numbered page has a page number (if you have used the page)
- New entries only begin on right-handed (i.e., odd-numbered) pages
- New entries have a title at the top

# Quizzes and Examinations

## Quizzes

Each Friday we will have a short quiz in class, based on our work in that week. The format of the quizzes will vary, but will be a mixture of factual information recall, reflection, and analysis. If memorization of facts, definitions, or the like, is expected, you will be given advanced notice.

## Examinations

At the end of each segment of the course we will have a written examination. The emphasis in each examination will be on practical application of concepts and techniques practiced in the course. In some cases this may involve responding to a new case study or drawing on data gathered in your course log. The class session before each exam will be a review of relevant concepts and techniques.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).
