## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will demonstrate knowledge of fiction writing in general and the short story in particular by utilizing appropriate terminology to analyze and discuss published fiction.
- Students will demonstrate craft-based reading strategies through annotations, short analytical responses, and discussions.
- Students will establish a consistent creative practice by regularly producing short creative writings and craft analyses.
- Students will demonstrate engagement in the writing process through drafting, peer critique, and revision.
- Students will practice interpersonal skills through participation in critique sessions.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Reading. In this course, you will learn to read like a writer, which is somewhat different than the  
reading you may be used to in English classes. The expectation is that as you engage with these  
texts you are “looking behind the curtain” to determine how the artistic effects were achieved and  
preparing to discuss your theories with others. Reading should be an active endeavor, and your  
preparedness will be evaluated bi-weekly under through class participation.  
Short Exercises (SEs). These are short creative writing prompts designed to give you practice in  
one particular skill. There is no minimum length so long as the prompt is thoughtfully addressed.  
Maximum length is one double-spaced page. Short exercises should be posted in your unit SE  
journal by the date they appear on the schedule. They will be evaluated for completion at the  
end of each unit.  
Craft Essay Exams (3): Once per unit, you will be given an in-class essay exam in which you  
showcase your ability to read and analyze the craft of fiction. See rubric below.  
CRAFT Talks (3): (Close Reading and Analysis of Fiction Tools)  
In this assignment, you will select one element of craft (such as point of view, dialogue, structure,  
or characterization) from a story we’ve read and give a short, focused presentation of how it  
works. Think of this as somewhere between a comment and a formal presentation: you’re  
highlighting one tool, showing how it showing how it functions in the text, and sparking conversation.  
Drafting Portfolios (3): You will write: one work of flash fiction (500-1000 words), one short  
story (1200- 2000 words), and 2-4 micro fictions (100-499 words). For full credit, drafts must be  
polished, free of errors, and reflect your attempts at engaging the craft elements we will be  
studying in class. Drafts should be submitted as a PDF (preferred) or MS Word Doc (not as  
Pages or RTF). Drafts should be double-spaced in 11- or 12-point font with 1-inch margins and  
no extra white space between paragraphs. A physical copy of your work must be submitted to  
peers. A drafting portfolio will be submitted digitally to me (template provided). You are solely  
responsible for ensuring your submission is uploaded legibly in the proper format before the due  
date and that all links are working. Submissions that are illegible, do not include evidence of  
drafting, or are otherwise unreadable will be considered unsubmitted.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

This is a small seminar class, the success of which is entirely dependent on active student  
presence and participation. I do not routinely distinguish between excused and unexcused  
absences. Instead, everyone has two absences to use for illness, self-care, or other obligations  
(e.g. interviews, medical appointments) without penalty. Otherwise, I expect that you will come  
to class every class (school athletes see me at the beginning of the term). Each absence beyond  
the second one will drop your grade by ten points (1%). If you miss more than five classes, you  
will receive no credit for the course even if you are otherwise passing. Note that three tardies of  
half hour or more accrue to one absence. Attendance deficits are calculated into your participation  
grade.

### Other Course Policies:

Due to the nature of workshop, late workshop submissions cannot be accepted. Do not miss  
workshop submission deadlines. For all other assignments, late work is eligible for no higher  
than a “C” grade. However, I do allow a twenty-four-hour grace period only on the following  
assignments before they are considered late: short exercises, revision plans. Per institutional  
policy, late work cannot be submitted after the last day of class for a better grade.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own.
