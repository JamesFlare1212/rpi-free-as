## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Cybersecurity is one of the fastest growing fields in computer science. Modern Binary Exploitation will focus on teaching practical offensive security skills in binary exploitation and reverse engineering. Through a combination of interactive lectures, hands-on labs, and guest speakers from industry, the course will offer students a rare opportunity to explore involved subjects in a rapidly evolving field.

The course will start off by covering basic x86 reverse engineering, vulnerability analysis, and classical forms of Linux-based userland binary exploitation. It will then transition into defenses found on modern systems (Canaries, DEP, ASLR, RELRO, Fortify Source, etc.) and the techniques used to defeat them. Time permitting, the course will also cover other subjects in exploitation including kernel-land and Windows based exploitation, as well as static analysis of binaries.

As a culminating event, students will compete in the National Cyber League, a CTF tournament, against students from across the country.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Possess the skills necessary to carry out vulnerability analysis of binary applications.
- Understand executable formats, program control flow at the assembly level, and other low level concepts.
- Understand classic and contemporary vulnerabilities and exploitation techniques.
- Apply both source code auditing and binary reverse engineering to the vulnerability discovery process.
- Be capable of exploiting vulnerabilities found in real world software as defined by MITRE’s Critical Vulnerabilities and Exposures (CVE) system.
- Perform real-world hands-on cybersecurity tasks in a virtual environment through national competitions.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- Labs: Typically, each lecture will be accompanied by a lab on the Wargames platform that will solidify understanding of the topic of the lecture. Each lab will contain at least 3 problems, a Level 1, 2, and 3 problem. Students may work in groups of up to four for labs.

- Term Projects: There will be two projects over the course of the semester: one around midterm time and the other as a final experience for the Wargames platform. Students may work in groups of up to four for projects.
- National Cyber League (NCL): Students will compete in a nationwide cybersecurity CTF tournament. There are two events in the NCL: an individual event and a team event. Students must participate in both events.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Labs: 50%, 10 labs equally weighted at 5%, of the final grade

- Labs will typically consist of 3 problems, a C, B, and A-level problem with each problem harder than the last. Lab grading works as follows.

- Students who complete only the Level 1 problem will receive 2 points
- Students who complete both the Level 1 and 2 problems will receive 3 points
- Students who complete all three lab problems will receive 5 points

- If a student does not complete the Level 1 problem before the end of the associated lecture period, they will receive a letter grade reduction for the lab. For example, if a student fails to complete the Level 1 problem during the lecture period, but then goes on to successfully complete the Level 1 and 2 problems for the week, the grade received for that week’s lab will be a B- (i.e., A- minus one letter grade).

- In order to receive full credit for a problem the student must submit their exploit code, the flag, and may be asked to explain their work upon submission to be checked off. Please leave comments and other supporting materials in your code.

- Lab problems will be introduced no later than the start of Thursday class each week. The Level 1 problem will be due in person by the end of the Thursday class for the week. All other problems become homework. It is highly advised that Level 2 problems be submitted no later than one week from their original assigned date.

- Students may collaborate on labs in teams of up to four students. Collaborations (or lack thereof) must be noted in comments in code turned in, including but not limited to the names of your collaborators, explanations of who worked on what, and an explanation in your own words the techniques and exploit concepts used. Failure to include this information when collaborating will result in half credit (-50% reduction) for the week’s labs. All students are responsible for turning in their own personal labs in order to receive any credit, even if they worked collaboratively. If you worked alone for a lab, you must note that in your submission.

- All work MUST be submitted no later than the last day of classes (December 10, 2024). Anything submitted later than that will not be accepted.

Term Projects: 25%, two projects: midterm (Mission 1) weighted at 10% and final (Mission 2) weighted at 15%

- Project specific grading breakdowns will be given when they are assigned.

- Project checkpoints exist to keep you on pace with projects, failing to make checkpoints will result in a -5% reduction to the term project grade.

- All work MUST be submitted no later than the last day of classes (December 10, 2024). Anything submitted later than that will not be accepted.

NCL: 25%, two events: individual event weighted at 12.5% and team event weighted at 12.5%

- All the rules of the NCL are in effect: https://nationalcyberleague.org/competition/rules

- Teams must be comprised entirely of students at RPI. There are RPI students playing in the NCL who are not in MBE, and you are welcome to be on a team with them. Teams are 7 maximum.

- Bonuses may be won for exceptional performance in the NCL. Bonuses are cumulative:

- Challenge Coin (top 500 in individual game) earns 1 free lab
- Individual trophy (top 3 in individual game) earns 1 free lab
- Team trophy (top 5 in team game) earns 1 free lab
- If 75 or more Challenge Coins are won, everyone earns 1 free lab
- If two or more trophies are won in an event, everyone earns 1 free lab

- Students are welcome, but are not required, to attend Rensselaer Cybersecurity Collaboratory weekly CTF meetings, Wednesdays at 6 PM in Lally 104, for additional training for the NCL.

- In order to earn full credit for each event, students must complete at least 65% of the challenges in each event. There will be a 10% reduction in grade for the event for each 5% increment below the minimum 65% not completed.

- NCL individual game is October 25-27; team game is November 8-10.

There will be servers dedicated to hosting the problems to be completed by students for both the lab and projects. The submitted exploits/solutions can be developed in any practical manner, but ultimately must work on the course servers to receive credit.

Grades and course progress will be made available to students throughout the semester. A grade can only be appealed within 5 days of the grade being made available to students.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

The labs and interactive exercises used in lecture will be hosted in a variety of different series on an internal “Wargames” server meant purely for teaching security concepts in a safe and educational environment. Students enrolled in the course will have access to the server and are expected to use it as an aide to the learning process. Attacking parts of the server infrastructure beyond the scope of what is assigned or attempts to disrupt services for the course or other students is not allowed and will be grounds for immediate failure of the course and will be reported as an academic dishonesty violation.

All NCL rules are in effect for the NCL events: https://nationalcyberleague.org/competition/rules

Some of the techniques learned in this course can be used for malicious purposes. These techniques are taught because they are necessary for a complete understanding of information security. Using such techniques for malicious purposes may be a crime, depending on context. Using such techniques against any RPI machines, including but not limited to other students’ machines, the shared course server, or any RPI computers, are grounds for immediate failure of the course and will be reported as an academic dishonesty violation.
