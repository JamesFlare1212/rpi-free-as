**COURSE DESCRIPTION**

This course provides a forum for interdisciplinary graduate discourse and community at Rensselaer. Students will curate a series of presentations by guest speakers, faculty, and graduate students. Related readings and writing assignments will be based on colloquium presentations. Arts Ph.D. students are required to take the colloquium each semester until the semester they take their qualifying examination and enroll for dissertation credits.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The primary goal for the class will be to get everyone speaking with each other and looking at work related to their respective practices. We will be engaging with each other and with people from within and outside of RPI. During class, this will take the form of discussions, studio visits and/or studio time. To that end, the curriculum will change and respond to our speakers and in-class conversations, as well as your needs in the studio.

“Studio practice” is used loosely here and can refer to a wide variety of activities, not all of which need to be centered on physically working in one’s own private space. How this is defined will vary by individual and be the basis for some discussion.

The class has a small budget for some guest speakers for the coming year, courtesy of iEar Presents! Each of us will use this funding to select one outside speaker to give a lecture (either remotely or in-person) and (if they visit campus in-person) visit studios and/or lead discussion. Through this curation, we'll all have the opportunity to get familiar with each other's work as well as our respective discursive backgrounds.

Thematically speaking, there will be some wiggle room within a preliminary framework. Our starting point this semester will be on small, self-forming communities. For example, the artists/critical voices that I have already scheduled to visit are all people who were responsible for spearheading an artistic “scene” of some kind. One will be discussing 90's Los Angeles (Lane Relyea), another the 2000's and artist-run independent spaces (Michelle Grabner). Finally, 2010's Lower East Side underground performance (Pieter Schoolwerth). All of these people have very strong opinions about the arc of these worlds and will be reflecting on how their historical trajectories can help us to understand the formation of more current contexts for emerging artistic discourse. In other words, what still works, what doesn’t, why?

And finally, it is my hope that in doing all of this, **OUR** small quasi-institutional community can know itself better and build from this self-awareness.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Establish a framework for the one's area of studio practice
- Form a coherent understanding of the discursive breadth of one's colleagues/classmates.
- Gain a wider sense of both the history and the current state of one's field.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students will be assessed based on the progress of three elements:  
Studio work: this is the main component of the course. As a complement to Research Methods, Colloquium is designed to provide space and time for production. While not every session will build in studio time, all other activities will revolve around this.  
Curatorial work: Each student will be expected to curate one outside speaker to present their work and (potentially) do studio visits with our class. Students are expected to perform the bulk of the administrative work surrounding these visits and lead discussion either during or following their visit.  
Participation: Students are expected to participate actively in discussions as well as engage with the visiting speakers.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grading percentage breakdown:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

In this class, all scholarly assignments must be completed WHOLLY by you. If there are any exceptions to this rule, I will inform you in advance. This means that in discussion, writing, administration, and participation, **you and you alone** will be executing the work.  
For studio work, deciding who the "author" is in artistic production can be a much thornier process. If one is engaging in work that has a complex relationship with authorship, I need to be aware of this relationship prior to any assessment of the work. Even if my awareness of this relationship compromises a performative element within the work, I STILL need to be made aware of it prior to assessment of the work. The first infraction against this policy will result in failure of the project. The second infraction will result in failure of the course and a report to the Office of the Dean.
