## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This seminar builds on the skills and knowledge students have developed in the Music & Sound courses, expanding them with new outlooks and techniques to aid students in developing their unique artistic voice. Throughout the semester, students will be exposed wide swath of contemporary compositional approaches, practices, and philosophical outlooks through listening, class discussion, readings, analytic presentations, creative projects, and guest lectures. Students will also be exposed to the fundamentals of orchestration. Particular attention will be paid to the interface between technology and artistic production. Each student will also produce a composition of significant weight to be performed at the end of the semester. At certain points during the semester, class meetings will be supplanted by individual meetings focused on the development of the final project.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Gain a broad knowledge of diverse contemporary compositional practices.
- Gain awareness of a variety of music notation strategies.
- Expand the student's abilities to develop musical ideas.
- Gain practical knowledge of the interfaces between technology and creative practice.
- Gain a basic knowledge of traditional and contemporary orchestration practices.
- Have experience developing their own unique creative voice.
- Gain practical experience working with and communicating your ideas to a musical ensemble.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Attendance / Class Participation  
This course relies heavily on in-class discussion of a great variety of artistic and practical issues. Attendance in mandatory. Students are expented to participate in all class discussions, voice their opinions, and be respectful of differences.

Composition Exercises  
A series of composition exercises, focused on developing specific techniques, strategies, and harmonic considerations will be assigned over the course of the semester.

Final Project  
The core of the course is the Final Project - a composition of significant weight to be performed/presented at the end of the semester. A small ensemble of professional musicians will be available for the students to write for in whole or in part. Students may also choose to create an electronic work, sound installation, or other kind of composition-oriented project.

**Advanced students** who are repeating the course have the following requirements:

- You will only be expected to attend certain designated meetings with the entire class.
- Private lessons will be scheduled weekly through the semester. These are mandatory, and treated the same as attendance in any class would be.
- Your focus will be on a single, large-scale composition project, developed with the instructor.
- Additional assignments in analysis or composition may be given as determined by the instructor.
- You will be required to give a presentation on your work to the entire class.

**Grading:**

- Participation/Attendance : 50%
- Final Project: 50%
