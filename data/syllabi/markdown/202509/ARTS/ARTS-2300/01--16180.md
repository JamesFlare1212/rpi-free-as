### Additional Course Description :

## Course Description

## The Rensselaer Orchestra brings together musicians from across the campus community to prepare and present programs which explore the connections between traditional symphonic repertoire, overlooked historical works, and contemporary compositions. To fully examine the social and aesthetic context of musical works, the orchestra also seeks to create opportunities for multimedia and technological collaboration. Performers on any traditional orchestral instrument are welcome to audition. In addition to its regular performances at the EMPAC Concert Hall, the orchestra also performs with the winner of the Rensselaer Concerto Competition and collaborates with faculty and student composers.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Student Learning Outcomes Students will meaningfully engage in the production, cultural expression, and study of the creative arts. Students will demonstrate their knowledge through the production of an artistic artifact and/or performance, and through analysis of historical and/or theoretical frameworks. Orchestra members will contribute to the ensemble as a whole and progress as individual musicians through study and practice of the following major areas: 1. Rehearsal Etiquette Students will demonstrate their understanding of rehearsal etiquette and protocol by: • Arriving to all rehearsals on time with music, their instrument, and a pencil • Purchasing and using a tuner and a metronome for rehearsal and at home practice • Taking instruction from the conductor, section leaders, and concertmaster • Playing with appropriate bowings, articulations, dynamics, rhythms, and pitches and making changes as necessary throughout the rehearsal process. • Playing with correct posture and form throughout the rehearsal • Asking music-related questions directed towards the appropriate person to clarify understanding • Notating all musical markings given in your music with pencil • Being mindful and aware of what to practice at home for the next rehearsal and taking practice parts home • Waiting respectfully as the director addresses questions throughout the ensemble • Actively listening to the other instruments in the orchestra while playing and adjusting accordingly • Turning off and refraining from using cell phones 2. Demonstration of Musical Skills • Theory • Reading and interpreting musical notation • Playing correct pitches and rhythms • Musical vocabulary • Ensemble skills • Understanding of historical and cultural context • Expression • Performance practice • Articulation • Dynamic contrast Students will demonstrate their informed understanding of these areas through active participation in rehearsals and concerts. 3. Performance Etiquette Students will demonstrate their understanding of appropriate performance etiquette by: • Arriving to all performance call times on time with music, instrument, and proper concert attire • Playing with all the musical elements as rehearsed including bowings, articulation, phrasing, intonation, and dynamics • Being engaged with individual sections, ensemble, and the conductor at all times • Observing protocol discussed for bows and acknowledgements following the performance

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

## Course Assessment Measures

**Homework**

Individual practice is essential to the success and progress of the orchestra. You are expected to practice at least 4-5 hours per week individually outside of orchestra. Daily practice builds confidence and familiarizes you with your part and your instrument. It is best not to overload on a practice session on just one day; rather, practice every day. Frequency beats duration.

**Preparedness Review**

Before the December Concert, all students in Orchestra are required to submit a brief preparedness review. This review will be an excerpt or excerpts from the concert repertoire, and will be assessed based on accuracy of pitch, rhythm, articulation, dynamics, bowings (if applicable), and other concrete musical information. The preparedness review video will be due Nov. 7th. Excerpts will be announced one month prior. Completion of a preparedness review is required to participate in the Holiday Concert. If a student does not pass the preparedness review, their final grade in the course will be dropped by one full letter grade and will be asked to retest. If the retest is failed, the final grade will be dropped again by a full letter grade and the student will not be permitted to participate in the Holiday Concert.

**Starred Passages**

In rehearsal, if after three attempts at a passage it does not substantially improve, the passage will be starred. When a passage is starred, it will be the first thing rehearsed at the next rehearsal. The demonstrating substantial improvement of the starred passage will be integrally tied to the musician’s preparation/improvement grade for that day.

**Seating**

Seating will be assigned by the instructor. For strings, principal positions will also be assigned and will require extra time/work (bowings, articulations, calling sectionals when needed).

**Respectful Communication**

Creating a positive learning environment for all is critical to the ensemble’s growth and learning. Distracting communication of any sort will not be permitted. Talking in class is only condoned so long as it is music/course-related discussion (e.g., resolving a bowing question) that is done quietly and without disrupting rehearsal. If there is disruptive communication that is not course-related and/or significantly distracts from the flow of rehearsal, the individual(s) will be asked to leave class immediately without warning and their grade will be lowered one-half letter grade per dismissal (e.g., from A to A-). Using a cellphone/mobile device in rehearsal is considered disrespectful to the rehearsal and learning environment and the student will immediately be asked to leave.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

## Attendance Policy

Excellent ensemble performance occurs when individual members are responsible for detailed preparation with the full group. Orchestra rehearsals are meant for putting the pieces together, refining, polishing, getting used to playing as a cohesive ensemble, to hearing and listening to everything around you as well as to reading the conductor, NOT a time to be learning notes and rhythms. Attendance, punctuality, and full participation are mandatory for rehearsals and performances, except in the following excused cases:

1. Emergencies: family emergency or hospitalization.

2. Serious illness: if you display symptoms of and/or test positive for COVID-19, please stay home. Please make sure you notify the instructor and teaching assistant via e-mail in advance of class.

For each unexcused absence, your grade will be docked half a letter grade.

Lateness will not be permitted. Class begins at the class start time, and late-comers will be marked late immediately following the start of class unless they otherwise notify the professor and teaching assistant.

If you miss a dress rehearsal or concert for unexcused reasons, your final grade will be docked 25%. For every two times that you arrive late or miss part of a rehearsal for unexcused reasons, your final grade will be docked by half a letter grade. Your grade presumes that you’re in rehearsal and participating fully, so any lack of participation (no instrument, not notating rehearsal markings, etc.) will penalize your grade, as will any behavior that conflicts with our rehearsal etiquette standards.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
