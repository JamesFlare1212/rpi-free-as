## Course Goals

### Goals:

Through active engagement and completion of course activities, you will be able to:

- understand the fundamental concepts of acoustics and auditory perception
- articulate ways in which acoustics and auditory perception interact to form the basis for a variety of musical traditions and practices
- gain fluency in critical listening skills and have a language for articulating what you hear
- understand and analyze musical notation and express musical concepts effectively on your own and with others
- gain an understanding of the variety of roles musical notation can be used to accomplish musical goals
- learn to comfortably interact with others in musical contexts, both spontaneously and with direction.
- gain an appreciation for the great variety of contemporary musical practices, including the acoustic, electronic, and electroacoustic realms
- get familiar with the fundamentals of Western musical practices so that you may progress on to Music and Sound I

## Course Content

### Content Details:

This course is the first part of a sequence aimed at cultivating theory, creative, and musicianship skills that will enhance students’ ability to understand and create music in a diverse range of styles and media. Each unit is focused around a general technical principle which is explicated and explored through written work, analysis, ear-training, improvisation, performance, and creative practice. Topics include the physics of sound, pitch and rhythmic notation, meters, scales, key signatures, solfege, intervals, chords, and melodic structure and introductory keyboard skills. Students will also engage in expanded listening exercises, helping them to develop and articulate their own approaches and responses to a variety of musical and sonic experiences drawn from contemporary music and sound art.

- All course texts and materials will be provided by the instructor. Students are required to have a pencil and blank manuscript paper to use every class.
- Notated creative projects should be completed using notation software. The best notation programs are Sibelius and Dorico, but free programs like MuseScore (https://musescore.org/en) are also perfectly acceptable.
- A simple digital audio program will be necessary for some of the creative projects. A reasonably good free option is Audacity (https://www.audacityteam.org/).
- We will use pianos and MIDI keyboards in class. In order to make use of the keyboards, it is necessary to have some kind of program that can take MIDI input and produce sound. A free program that can be used for this purpose is bitKlavier (https://bitklavier.com/)
- For extra musicianship practice, students can use programs like Musicca(musicca.com) and Earmaster (https://www.earmaster.com/).

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Class Participation**

This is a hands-on, experiential course. Therefore, students are expected to participate in all class activities, including discussions, singing, rhythm exercises, improvisation, and other forms of in-class interaction. Making mistakes, struggling with the material, creative approaches, and taking risks activities is strongly encouraged and supported. Students will be graded on effort, integrity of interaction, and improvement over the course of the semester.

**Weekly Homework Assignments**

Homework assignments will include listening & reading with written responses, analysis, and worksheets. Homework will usually be assigned on Friday, and due the following Friday at the beginning of class.

**- Written Assignments:** The nature of written assignments will vary, and may consist of theory questions, melodic transcriptions, etc. They will be relatively easy if you have done the drills. They will typically be due Fridays at the beginning of class of the week following their availability. Assignments may be completed by hand, or using properly labeled staff paper or notation software. All writing MUST be legible: *illegible answers the will be marked incorrect!* Assignments done using hand-drawn staff lines will not be accepted.

**Sound Journals**

The majority of your written response takes place in your Sound Journal. This is a weekly writing of 350-500 words. Each week, you will be given a question, idea, sound file, or other item, which you will use as the source of your journal entry. Journal entries will include subjective responses, aural analysis, memory reflection, and other orientations. Sound Journal entries are to be submitted through the LMS site.

**Aural Drills**

The aural drills portion of the homework will be conducted through different software. New musicianship modules (rhythm, interval identification, singing, and dictation) will be posted each week. If you have successfully completed and passed all of the tasks, you will receive full credit for your weekly musicianship grade. This will take approximately 45min each week.

**Creative Collaborative Projects**

Each unit will culminate in a collaborative composition/improvisation project with a small group of your classmates. Projects may include an in-class performance or presentation. The **Final project** will consist of two parts: a short take-home exam covering course materials and a creative project. The creative project may be a short composition, a harmonic or theoretical proposition, a piece of software, a radio show, or another proposal. The creative project must receive the approval of the instructor.

Class Participation: 20%

Weekly Homework Assignments (Sound journals | Aural Drills | Written) : 50%

Creative Collaborative Projects (5): 15%

Final Project: 15%

All work is expected to be handed in or finished on the assigned due date at the beginning of class. Late work will be reduced by 1/3 of a grade (e.g. B to B-) for each day after the due date.
