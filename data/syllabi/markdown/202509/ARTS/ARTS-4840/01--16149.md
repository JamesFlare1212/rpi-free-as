## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This two-semester course is for senior Electronic Arts majors and is the core creative forum for the development and presentation of the Thesis Project. Creative Seminar is designed to guide the senior student through a professional process of conceptualization, research, writing, and production, leading to the Thesis Project presented in the Spring Exhibition. This course is the first part of a two-course sequence, with the second part offered Spring, 2025 with instructor Ken Ragsdale.

The work may involve and integrate diverse media, including animation, digital and new media, 3D and 2D graphics, video, music, installation, sculpture and performance. In Creative Seminar I, students are expected to conceptualize, articulate, commit to and move forward on a path that leads to the realization of a unique project that will communicate their vision to a defined audience. Writing, research and production are inextricably entwined in art practice. Students are therefore expected to develop and articulate a conceptual position in relation to what has come before as well as important connections that are made in the content of the work.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Interpret, compare and critique a variety of forms of contemporary art.
- Develop a daily artistic process.
- Develop and articulate a personal philosophy toward contemporary art and art- making.
- Learn and demonstrate practical skills of the working artist.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students are expected to commit to the project idea and to develop their work progressively over the course of the semester, both in execution of production and writing. In this first semester students will:

- Develop the conceptual basis for the senior project through research, prototyping, writing and discussion
- Develop and accomplish one’s own disciplined, production process
- Produce and present a Proof of Concept prototype that provides a “snapshot” of the project, demonstrating its technical feasibility, conceptual integrity and progress.
- Write a Project Proposal and implementation plan
- Write an artist statement
- Develop a Research Question and preliminary outline and bibliography for the written thesis
- Develop a “family tree” of artistic influences
- Develop a daily studio practice

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grading will be composed of three major components: Did you make your benchmarks? Did you accomplish the Weekend Make/Reading? Did you contribute to discussion? A heavy grade weight will be given to the final semester presentation.

**Benchmarks**:

Students will set weekly benchmarks for themselves with the help of the instructor on Tuesdays. You will want to set benchmarks that are both achievable and challenging/ambitious. Benchmark progress will be reviewed Fridays. A typical benchmark period will be 3-6 hours of worktime outside of class between Tuesday and Friday, split between making art, researching art and artists, viewing relevant film and video, or even playing artistic games. Some weeks your benchmark will be set only by the instructor. Some weeks you may spend most of your research time researching other artists. Some weeks you may spend all your time making your art. How you “bank” your research time will be up to you and the instructor. There should be no week where you aren’t making art.

**Weekend Make/Reading:**

Assigned at the end of class Friday for review on Tuesday. If assigned a “make,” you will be expected to produce a short studio art study that will be a foundation skill for the practicing artist. If assigned reading, you will be expected to be able to come to class on Tuesday able to discuss relevant topics OR write short responses. The Weekend Make/Read should take 1-2 hours of time outside of class.

**Discussion:**

Are you able to reflect on relevant topics in class discussion? Are you putting in the work to constructively critique artworks produced by classmates to help them make better work?

**Based on these three components, you will be assigned weekly points, with a maximum of 100 points per week of class.** Weekly point rubric:

- 100 points: I made all my goals, and made some surprising discoveries, exceeded

expectations. I fulfilled the Weekly Make/Reading, and I participated in all relevant class discussions.

- 90 points: I made a lot of progress, didn’t make every goal. I completed the

weekly Make/Reading pretty well, and participated in most class discussions.

- 80 points: I made some decent progress. I did some of the weekly Make/Reading, and I talked a bit in class.
- 70 points: I made something. I said something
- No points: no effort, no discussion

All grade appeals must be brought to the instructor during office hours or at a scheduled time convenient to both parties.

**There will be 12 weeks of 100 point graded assignments.**

**Your final semester presentation will be given a grade weight of 400 points. The total point count for the semester will be 1600 possible points.**

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic Dishonesty

*The Rensselaer Handbook* defines various forms of academic dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Students are responsible for familiarizing themselves with these policies. Students should note that the penalty for plagiarism and other forms of cheating is a grade of “F” for the course.

In this class “cheating” consists of plagiarism or representing the work of others as your own. This can include the deliberate or unintentional omission of acknowledgments or references. Cheating also consists of copying images, audio, video, or any creative assets generated by others (inlcuding AI) and submitting that work as your own.
