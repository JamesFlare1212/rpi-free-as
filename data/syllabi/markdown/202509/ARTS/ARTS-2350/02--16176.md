## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Welcome to Arts 2350_01 and 02: Chamber Music Ensemble. This is an instrumental class that will be coached, rehearsed, and will perform regularly. It focuses on the collaborative experience of playing music in small ensembles such as string quartets, woodwind quintets, trios, etc., as well as in more unusual combinations that might be required to prepare 21st-century repertoire. One can participate as an individual or as part of a pre-formed ensemble. You need to be able to read, practice, and perform a musical score at an intermediate or advanced level, so an audition with the instructor is a prerequisite. The class is based on an interpretive context and practice of all musical concepts taught in other musical classrooms and private lessons, such as notation, structure, dynamics, expressive techniques, etc. The aim is an analytical and performative engagement with musical texts, their historically grounded expressive codes for the different repertoires, and their specificities for different instruments.

By the end of the class, you should be able to depict theoretical, technical, expressive, and historical principles within musical scores and learn to apply collaborative techniques and strategies to allow for their renditions. These skills will serve you in developing your musical practice and performance skills and will be useful in all future music classes you will take. You will also be able to apply collaborative and cooperative strategies within the frame of non-verbal communication, such as gestures and instrumental play techniques, that can be applied to out-of-classroom situations.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Through active engagement and completion of course activities, you will be able to:

- understand and analyze musical notation and express musical concepts effectively on your own and with others
- develop collaborative strategies and techniques for synchronization in group music playing

- explain musical concepts to non-music people
- develop a short repertoire to illustrate new performance and musical skills

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Coursework and Grading Scheme**

This course is worth 1 unit of credit, which means you may be asked to complete up to 4 hours of work per week, including class time. It is a class based on performance, so personal daily practice is necessary for the course completion.

The table below summarizes the kinds of work you will be asked to do and their contributions to your course grade.

| Category | % of course letter grade | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attendance and participation in class and section meetings | 60% | - 2 absence ‘passes’, no questions asked - Up to 3 can be made up with alternative work - Excused absences are permitted by a letter from a medical doctor or the Dean of Students’ office only - Arrival time >15 mins late counts as an absence |
| Engagement, personal practice and rehearsal participation | 20% | - Learning the score, parts, and full, as soon as possible, is highly recommended as it will allow for putting all parts together during class - Eligible for 2 late ‘passes’ on personal work |
| Final Project | 20% | - Recording or participation in a live concert performance - The recording must be submitted on time |

This is a music-reading-intensive and playing-intensive seminar. On a weekly basis, you will attend an ensemble coaching and up to three hours of ensemble or section rehearsal time. Learning the complete score is required, and supplemental reading and listening sessions will be added accordingly. Students taking the course will complete a recording up to 12’ of music performed by their ensemble. Students who choose and are selected to participate in the end-of-class concert(s) will have to have an additional sound check and rehearsal time in the venue. The final concert performances are video and audio recorded, and they will be accessible to the students.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

## **Course Policies**

**Presence and Participation**  
Thinking through difficult performative and musical issues is only possible, most productive, and most fun when done out loud in the company of others! This is why attendance and participation are a large part of your course grade (80%).

You get 2 ‘passes’ to be absent from class, no questions asked.

For an additional 3 classes beyond that, you can make up an absence by:

- Watching or listening to a recording, if there is one, and
- Responding to the recording and/or the material for the last class by submitting a 500-word statement on how you would’ve reacted and contributed.

Being 15 minutes or more late to a class counts as an absence.

Even if an absence is excused, any work due in that class/group is subject to the policies below.

Students who anticipate persistent challenges to participating in class or presenting work on time should share this with the course instructor as soon as possible.

### Other Course Policies:

**The Honor Code**

Class meetings, group and ensemble meetings in this course will be highly interactive; you will hear plenty of great ideas from your peers and especially from your assigned group. However, you will be evaluated in this course as an individual and are expected to display academic, intellectual, and creative integrity. Please review the [RPI's student rights and responsibilities](https://rpi.app.box.com/s/p1b39vybm914b5cao4csrgl84bfrrh2l), and when in doub,t contact your instructor.

**Extended Absences**

It is possible that one or more of us in the class will get sick, will need to give care to someone sick, or, for other reasons, will require an extended absence during the quarter.

- If a group member requires an extended absence, students in that group will be redistributed among other groups as fits students’ schedules.
- If a student requires an extended absence before more than 70% of coursework is completed, there might be opportunities to Withdraw from the course or develop a schedule for making up and submitting coursework later in the quarter. Students in this situation should talk to the instructor as soon as possible.
- If a student requires an extended absence after at least 70% of the coursework, students may request an Incomplete. Students in this situation should talk to their instructor to discuss options.

**Final Project Policy**

There are two forms of final project presentation: either a recording or participation in a concert with your assigned group. If you foresee or encounter extenuating circumstances that would not allow you to complete either of the two final project forms, please contact me as soon as possible.
