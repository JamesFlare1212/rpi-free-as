## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Intermediate Video is a hands-on intensive course that teaches the language, aesthetics, and techniques of video production. Students will develop and produce several short video projects. Emphasis will be on the acquisition of technical production skills in visualizing, scripting, aesthetics, shooting, sound design, and editing. We will create video artworks using production strategies that range from traditional to experimental. We will discuss diverse strategies for field and studio video production and will go over pre-production, production, and post-production.

Throughout the semester, in support of the student productions in the class, we will view a range of works that reveal the aesthetic, conceptual, and methodological shifts in the way videos are constructed and perceived. We will watch videos, from the classics of film and video art to recent work, to identify and examine thematic issues central to video production, and aesthetics. This structure will allow us to examine a myriad of production styles and methods as we work on our independent projects throughout the semester.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The term "Basic Video Production" covers a lot of territory. It encompasses pre-production planning, camerawork, lighting, sound, and editing - a host of disciplines. There are a lot of techniques, tips, tricks, and terms that need to be mastered before one can manipulate production variables to communicate a clear visual message.

There will be readings that you will be expected to read before the appointed class. A substantial portion of in-class time will be spent reviewing and discussing this material as well as topics not covered in the assigned readings and viewings. Whenever possible, examples of professional works will be shown to reinforce the subject matter. Although screening titles are listed, we may not get to all of the examples or may substitute for other titles where appropriate, as new work and forms are always being created.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- By the end of the course students should be able to set-up and operate equipment used in video production and post-production including cameras, tripods, microphones, lights, digital editing, and rendering playable formats. (note: we will adjust tools for online instruction)
- By the end of the course students should be able to manipulate this equipment to create coherent sequences that "communicate", while meeting the fundamental aesthetic criteria covered.
- By the end of the course students should be able to perform the preproduction and postproduction steps necessary to create a video project.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Hands-on exercises & projects**

There are projects aimed at giving you a chance to use what we're learning in class. See the weekly outline and google classroom for due dates.

**Production Projects #1-3**

**Project 1:Client video**

We will be making videos for TRIP.

**Project 2:** ***REMAKE*** **a five-minute scene of a movie (we can discuss your selection) shot-for-shot**

Choose a five-minute scene (minimum) from any movie/media (can be a composite scene, meaning with different cuts/locations, etc.) and remake it, shot-for-shot. First storyboard the 5 minutes or write down each of the shots in the 5 minutes.

*For either choice you must use either chroma-keying or "masking" technique from Premiere or add animation on top of your footage via the Photoshop animation tool. You are free to do both, and, also make your own music if you'd like. However, for this final, you can use pre-existing tracks/music if they are copy left.

**Project 3: Music video**

Students will create their own music video, analyze others, and do a presentation on a video of your choice.

**Treatment/proposal for your final project.** You can start fresh or further develop one of your other projects for your final project. Your treatment/proposal should include: (1) a synopsis of the work (50 words); (2) A description of the style/aesthetics you will employ (50-100 words); (3) a breakdown of all the elements, characters, etc. you will use; (4) a production schedule; and (5) an indication of your intended audience and plans for distributing your work in the world. We will present these in class and accept feedback.

You are expected to bring rushes of this project to class, to be able to show your progress with it within the class critique structure. You are expected to screen your final project in class for final presentation and critique, as part of the conclusion of your work for this course.
