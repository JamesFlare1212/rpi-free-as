## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

## ***Course Description***

Wind Symphony is a laboratory ensemble in which students encounter and perform music from diverse styles, genres and historical periods written for symphonic winds and wind band. Students in the Wind Symphonywill perform in concert once per semester, and may participate in special activities, events, projects, and collaborations with featured guest artists. The Rensselaer Wind Symphony is open to all RPI students on an auditioned basis.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Student Learning Outcomes Students will meaningfully engage in the production, cultural expression, and study of the creative arts. Students will demonstrate their knowledge through the production of an artistic artifact and/or performance, and through analysis of historical and/or theoretical frameworks. Wind Symphony members will contribute to the ensemble as a whole and progress as individual musicians through study and practice of the following major areas: 1. Rehearsal Etiquette Students will demonstrate their understanding of rehearsal etiquette and protocol by: • Arriving to all rehearsals on time with music, their instrument, and a pencil • Taking instruction from the conductor, section leaders • Playing with appropriate articulations, dynamics, rhythms, and pitches and making changes as necessary throughout the rehearsal process. • Playing with correct posture and form throughout the rehearsal • Asking music-related questions directed towards the appropriate person to clarify understanding • Notating all musical markings given in your music with pencil • Being mindful and aware of what to practice at home for the next rehearsal and taking practice parts home • Waiting respectfully as the director addresses questions throughout the ensemble • Actively listening to the others and adjusting accordingly • Turning off and refraining from using cell phones 2. Demonstration of Musical Skills • Theory • Reading and interpreting musical notation • Playing correct pitches and rhythms • Musical vocabulary • Ensemble skills • Understanding of historical and cultural context • Expression • Performance practice • Articulation • Dynamic contrast Students will demonstrate their informed understanding of these areas through active participation in rehearsals and concerts. 3. Performance Etiquette Students will demonstrate their understanding of appropriate performance etiquette by: • Arriving to all performance call times on time with music, instrument, and proper concert attire • Playing with all the musical elements as rehearsed including articulation, phrasing, intonation, and dynamics • Being engaged with individual sections, ensemble, and the conductor at all times • Observing protocol discussed for bows and acknowledgements following the performance

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

## Course Assessment Measures

**Homework**

Individual practice is essential to the success and progress of the ensemble. You are expected to practice at least 4-5 hours per week individually outside of rehearsal. Daily practice builds confidence and familiarizes you with your part and your voice. It is best not to overload on a practice session on just one day; rather, practice every day. Frequency beats duration.

**Starred Passages**

In rehearsal, if after three attempts at a passage it does not substantially improve, the passage will be starred. When a passage is starred, it will be the first thing rehearsed at the next rehearsal. The demonstrating substantial improvement of the starred passage will be integrally tied to the musician’s preparation/improvement grade for that day.

**Seating**

Seating will be assigned by me and may change as needed throughout the semester. Section leaders may take be assigned extra time/work (e.g., leading sectionals).

**Respectful Communication**

Creating a positive learning environment for all is critical to the ensemble’s growth and learning. Distracting communication of any sort will not be permitted. Talking in class is only condoned so long as it is music/course-related discussion (e.g., resolving a pronunciation question) that is done quietly and without disrupting rehearsal. If there is disruptive communication that is not course-related and/or significantly distracts from the flow of rehearsal, the individual(s) will be asked to leave class immediately without warning and their grade will be lowered one-half letter grade per dismissal (e.g., from A to A-). Using a cellphone/mobile device in rehearsal is considered disrespectful to the rehearsal and learning environment and the student will be immediately asked to leave.
