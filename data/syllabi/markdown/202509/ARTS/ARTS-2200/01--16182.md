## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Through close observation of a limited number of subjects, the student will explore the possibilities of visual translation using a variety of media and techniques.

Over the term, each successive drawing project will provide the student the opportunity to develop and control a particular set of media, technique, and perspective, which reveals a unique and personal approach to image making.

Participation in critiques, discussions, and lectures will enable the student to become fluent with the vocabulary and language of drawing, and focus their preferred methods and developed skill-sets into appropriate and actionable processes.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

In this course, the student will have the opportunity to explore many avenues of material approaches to the same situations, which will broaden their understanding of the many options for emotive description and narrative possibilities within a limited field of their observed world.

This course will encourage and facilitate the ambitious student to understand how to create a complex visual space, construct fully developed and realized drawings, and gain a greater awareness of the critical decision-making process, insofar as it relates to meaning and visual dialogue.

The student will also be expected to research and become familiar with the work of contemporary artists and the relationship of this work to their own, and to current critical ideas and theory relating to the field of art.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Construct unique and fully developed drawings, which express their ideas and concepts in the most effective manner.
- Evaluate and analyze the work of their peers with relevant critical feedback.
- Apply the criticism of their own work by constructively improving their future projects.
- Relate their personal histories to the history of art-making, and combine this knowledge of the general artistic conversation with their individual skill-sets.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

All grades are based on the following:

- Attendance
- Quality of work produced
- Presentation of completed work and work in progress
- Timely completion of projects
- Understanding of concepts discussed CLEARLY demonstrated in work
- Interest and participation in discussions and critiques
- Serious and lively approach to work
- Motivated problem solving both in class and outside of class.

Students’ final grades are based upon the completion of the final portfolio at the end of the term. However, ongoing assessments for individual projects and current progress and standing can be discussed at any time upon request.

**Attendance, participation in critiques, and presentation of final portfolio is required.** Regular attendance is mandatory unless special circumstances have been presented and discussed at the beginning of the term. Under most circumstances, **regular absences which affect the quality of the learning experience and the work produced will negatively affect the final grade.**

Lecture and demonstration times are generally scheduled at the beginning of class, so arriving for studio promptly at the beginning of the scheduled class-time is critical. In case of late arrival, students should be prepared to gather any information they may have missed from classmates, as lectures are not given at the individual's convenience. Excessive lateness will negatively affect the final grade.

**Being unprepared to work during scheduled class times, and without the necessary tools, is the same as being absent and will be recorded as such.**

Course evaluation is made at the end of the semester through a portfolio review. The portfolio consists of all work done in and out of class during the semester.

**Do not discard any work**. A portfolio review can be arranged at any point during the semester. Submission of portfolio will take place during the last day of class. **Failure to submit a complete portfolio will result in failure of the course.**

You must complete all major assignments. **If you do not submit all assigned projects, you will not pass the class**. Projects presented in an unfinished state **will not** be critiqued or graded.

**Late Work**

You should submit your work on time. Due dates and times will be announced in class (another reason for regular attendance). Late work will only be accepted after consultation with the professor prior to the day in which the work is due, or an official excuse is presented.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**FINAL GRADE 50% for projects 1-5 / 50% for final 5-part series**

**WORK** (Points are per project) Possible total of **7 points**

- Is the student mentally present, ready to work, and committed to contributing to the day’s conversation? **+1 point**
- Is the student turning in finished drawings by the due date? **+2 points**
- Is the student making well crafted, complex, and conceptually layered drawings that fulfill their stated goals? **+2 points**
- Does the student search for higher ground, and push to expand their knowledge and skill-set to produce work of increasing quality and significance? **+2 points**

**PARTICIPATION** (Points are per project) Possible total of **3 points**

- When critiquing the work of others, does the student show interest in the artist’s sources and interpretations, and make comments related to their presentation, which help to amplify their meanings and inform the artist of possible alternative choices for realizing their intent? **+1 point**
- Does the student present their work using intelligent and applicable references to their stated intent and to their awareness of the relationship of their work to related examples from the greater history of art making? **+1 point**
- After receiving constructive criticism, does the student respond in a positively considered manner to improve their work? **+1 point**

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

*The Rensselaer Handbook* defines various forms of academic dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Students are responsible for familiarizing themselves with these policies. Students should note that the penalty for plagiarism and other forms of cheating is a grade of “F” for the course.

In this class “cheating” consists of plagiarism or representing the work of others as your own through deliberate omission of acknowledgments or references. Cheating also consists of copying graphics, created by others and submitting that work as your own.

It is always acceptable (and encouraged) to discuss your assignments with others in the class and solicit feedback to improve your work.

Violations of academic integrity as listed in course policies will result in failure of the course.
