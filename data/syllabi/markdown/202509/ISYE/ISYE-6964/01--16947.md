## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course introduces methods for automating the process of decision-making. Starting with the concept of probabilistic reasoning, this course will provide a foundation for a sequential decision-making framework, which can be applied in various domains including autonomous driving, exploration, and robotics. The focus will specifically be on decision making problems under state uncertainty and finally multi-agent systems. Topics include: Bayesian networks, Markov decision process, partially observable Markov decision processes, multi-agent reasoning.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Apply probabilistic reasoning about uncertainty in problems inspired by real-world situations
- Identify key concepts in sequential decision making under state uncertainty
- Formulate problems inspired by real-world situations using sequential decision problem formulation
- Implement and apply fundamental decision-making algorithms to solve problems driven by real-world conditions
- Write about a decision-making problem, approaches for solutions, and analysis results in a detailed way as a research paper
- Critique a decision-making problem, and solution approaches

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Course deliverables will consist of homework assignments, midterm exams, final project (with a written paper) and final presentation.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is not graded, but students should try to attend the lectures unless they have a good reason not to. Attendance can be collected with a sign-up sheet or an online questionnaire, which will be only used to keep track of attendance percentages over weeks.

### Other Course Policies:

**Late Submissions**  
Homework submissions are at 5 PM on the posted date. Because there might be unexpected events (e.g., work or family commitments) there is 0% penalty for 48 hours (no questions asked) for  
late homework assignment submissions. After this period, the solutions will be posted and students will get 0 credits. Please note that this rule only applies to homework assignments, and you  
cannot get bonus points if you submit late. Final presentations, reports, and papers should be submitted on the posted due date, after which students would get 0 credits. If you get  
sick or have another excuse that prevents you from submitting your work, communicate this to the instructor before the posted due date (not the late submission due date) so the necessary extensions can be granted (unless it is an emergency). This applies to all assignments.  
**Grade Appeals**  
Requests for regrade must be made (in writing) within 72 hours of the grade being communicated to the student. Regrade request for the final reports/projects need to be made within 4 hours of the grade being communicated to the student due to tight final grade schedule. Any concerns regarding overall course performance must be communicated to the instructor as soon as possible. Final grades will not be modified once published in SIS unless there is a demonstrable error made by the instructor (i.e., a calculation error).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Specifically, in this course, midterm exam submission must be performed individually and must represent the student's own work. If there is any collaboration during homework assignments, it needs to be indicated during the submission. Projects can be performed in groups. In cases where external help was received or given in projects, a note should be included to indicate the collaboration. Usage of materials (books, internet sites, etc.) outside the listed resources should be cited. Evidence of academic dishonesty will result in a zero for the associate assignment and a report to the Dean of Students for possible additional actions.  
**Note on Generative AI:** Students may use generative AI tools (such as ChatGPT) as part of their research and preparation for assignments, or as a text editor, but the text and the code that is submitted must be written by the student. Essentially, using such tools should be treated as a collaboration with a classmate, with direct copying prohibited. Students are only allowed to use these tools when collaboration is allowed. Hence, students are not allowed to use such tools for midterms. Students should also be aware that the outputs of these tools are not always reliable or accurate. Any use of such tools and the extent of the use should be acknowledged and cited properly. Violations of this policy will be treated as academic misconduct. If you have any questions about this policy or if you are unsure whether a particular use of AI is acceptable, please do not hesitate to ask for clarification.
