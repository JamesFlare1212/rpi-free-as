## Course Content

### Content Details:

**(Tentative) Course Content**

- Introduction
  - o What is game theory? What is a game? Elements of a game.
  - o Motivation
- Static games of complete information
  - o Basic theory
  - o Applications
    - Cournot model
    - Bertrand model
    - Final-offer arbitration
    - The tragedy of the commons
  - o Mixed strategies
- Dynamic games of complete information
  - o Perfect information
    - Theory of backward induction
    - Stackelberg model
    - Sequential bargaining
  - o 2-stage games of complete but imperfect information
    - Bank runs
    - Tariffs and imperfect international competition
    - Tournaments
  - o Repeated games
  - o Dynamic games of complete but imperfect information
- Supply chain coordination
- Static games of incomplete information
- Dynamic games of incomplete information

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Identify the type and elements of a game.
- Formulate different situations as a game.
- Develop ability to solve for the outcome of a game.
- Understand different supply chain coordination models and apply them.
- 6000-level students: Develop ability to critique game theoretical models and suggest improvements.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

This course will have 4-5 assignments and 3 exams.

### Criteria Details:

**Grading**

Assignments: 20%

Exam 1: 25% (Monday, October 6)

Exam 2: 25% (Thursday, November 6)

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
