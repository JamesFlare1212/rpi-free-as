### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Quizzes 50% -** Multiple choice questions covering several chapters in the text and any supplemental material posted on the slides. To be taken in class on the day indicated in the syllabus.

**Research Proposal 25% -** The student will write a proposal to conduct research on a psychology topic of their choosing. The proposal should be between 6 and 9 pages long. The research proposal should include three sections (see rubric on Blackboard):

- **Introduction** – The scientific and societal rationale for studying the problem
- **Methods** – The method for how you would go about conducting the research including materials, population sampling, and techniques for gathering data.
- **Implications** – A description of the potential outcomes of the research if you were to conduct it and what that would mean for psychological science and for society.

**Policy Makers Assignment 20% -** Students will be put into teams of 3-5 people where they will create policies for implementing the teaching, research, and practice of psychology in a hypothetical world society. Across a series of assignments, they will determine a budget, and other resource applications, and describe how these will be administered to enable the members of their community to flourish.

**Attendance 5%** - Successful course completion is *directly correlated* with class attendance. Students who attend every class are much more likely to earn the grade they desire. Attendance is required and each class day comprises approximately 0.2% of the course grade.

**Extra credit (TBD%) -** Cognitive science professors periodically offer research experiments in which students of this course may participate for bonus points. You have the option of participating in one of these experiments and receiving extra credit depending on the duration and nature of the experiment. More information to come.

**Instructor’s AI policy:** As an instructor, I believe that AI should not be used without first understanding the fundamentals of a concept or topic. Once you know “how” to do something, assistance with AI is a fair use of the resources available to you. However, if you would like to use AI as a starting point for an assignment, such as outlining, brainstorming, and planning, you will need to disclose which AI software you used and how it was used for the assignment.

Please cite the AI-generated material in-text (i.e., APA style in-text citations). You will also need to demonstrate that you have created sufficient content above and beyond (i.e., at least 65%) what the AI generated. Please note that AI doesn’t know any better and hallucinates, or fabricates, information, such as citations for research articles that don’t exist.

It is your responsibility to verify that the content generated by AI is true. You can do this by providing links to the research articles generated by AI in your references section. AI-generated text and references must be credited appropriately. Authors and/or creators, and not the AI tool(s), are accountable for their work, its originality, accuracy, and integrity.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.
