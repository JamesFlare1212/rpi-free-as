## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course reviews the influence of lighting conditions on people's visual capabilities, perceptions, moods, and alertness, with understanding derived from the structure and operating characteristics of the human visual system. A review of the human factors issues related to applications such as office lighting, retail lighting, emergency lighting, outdoor lighting, security lighting.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Textbook: Boyce, P.R., 2014. *Human Factors in Lighting*, 3rd Edition, London: CRC Press.

Additional readings will be maintained on reserve in the Lighting Research Center's Resource Collection Library, or distributed to students in class.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

At the conclusion of the course, students in LGHT-6845 will be able to:

1. Explain how the visual system works
2. Analyze the visual requirements of various visual tasks, and make and critique lighting recommendations
3. Recognize and predict lighting conditions likely to cause discomfort, generate specific impressions, and/or modify behavior
4. Explain the basic physiology of the human circadian system
5. Specify the stimulus presented to the visual system under various conditions
6. Estimate the effects of different lighting conditions on people with different viewing capabilities
7. Explain how light intensity, light spectrum, duration, timing and light exposure history affects the human circadian system

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

For the students in LGHT-6845, student assessment will be based upon a review of field assignments, critique assignments, laboratory assignments, quizzes and exams, and the degree to which the student participates in class discussions.

In the critique assignment, students will review and critique a published recommendation for lighting in a specific application and suggest improvements. In the field assignment, teams will measure and assess lighting conditions in an office, classroom, home or other space for visual performance and comfort and prepare individually written reports on the findings. In the laboratory assignment, teams will carry out a simple human factors experiment and report on the results and implications for lighting practice. In the examinations, students will demonstrate their knowledge of course concepts by providing short answers to a broad range of questions. Class participation includes attending all class sessions and active involvement in team assignments. In addition to these, student assessment will include a written and oral report for a final class project. The objective of the final class project for students in LGHT-6845 is to provide practical experience in analyzing the relevant human factors aspects of a realistic lighting application. The final class project will require students to review and synthesize information from class and the literature to analyze a set of conditions in a lighting application and propose alternatives to improve visual and/or non-visual aspects of the application to the benefit of the end user in the space. The final class project will be assigned during the first half of the semester with a due date on or near the last day of classes.
