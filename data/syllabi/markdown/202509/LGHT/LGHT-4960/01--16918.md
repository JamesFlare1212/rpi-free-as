## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides an overview of lighting software commonly used to estimate light levels in general illumination applications indoors (e.g., office, classrooms) and out (e.g., parks, streets and roadways). The main objective of the course is to understand the general capabilities and weaknesses of commercial lighting software to produce photometrically accurate results.

## Course Text(s)

## Course Goals

### Goals:

At the conclusion of the course, students in LGHT-4960 will be able to:

1. Explain the five main lighting units and the components in photometric reports that describe the performance of light fixtures.
2. Estimate the light levels and uniformity ratios of horizontal and vertical task planes commonly needed in general illumination calculations.
3. Use illuminance and other light meters to measure light levels in indoor spaces.
4. Explain the possible sources of uncertainty in lighting calculation software that could explain the differences between calculations and on-site measurements.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The course will primarily consist of short lectures, demonstrations, and computer based exercises. This includes a review of the main lighting units and definitions, a review of how photometric information of light fixtures is measured and prepared for use in software, and exercises to compare results from 1-2 commercial software programs as well as real life measurements.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Explain the five main lighting units and the components in photometric reports that describe the performance of light fixtures.
- Estimate the light levels and uniformity ratios of horizontal and vertical task planes commonly needed in general illumination calculations.
- Use luminance and other light meters to measure light levels in indoor spaces.
- Explain the possible sources of uncertainty in lighting calculation software that could explain the differences between calculations and on-site measurements.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Student assessment will be based upon a review of in-class assignments, computer exercises, laboratory assignments, and the degree to which the student participates in class discussions.

In-class assignments could include taking light measurements, basic calculations by hand or using computer software. Lab assignments will include the basic characterization of the light distribution of a lamp and the verification between software calculations and measurements. Class or homework assignments will build up to a final project where students will create a calculation report for different areas in a given space. Class participation includes attending all class sessions and active involvement in team assignments.
