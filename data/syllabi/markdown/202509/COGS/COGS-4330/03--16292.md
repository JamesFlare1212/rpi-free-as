## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- compare and contrast the techniques (direct and indirect measures of brain activity) that are used in Cognitive Neuroscience (cognitive tasks, clinical-pathological correlations, to imaging).
- identify brain regions that are involved in the basic aspects of cognition (sensation/perception, attention, memory, emotion, executive processing, social cognition).
- communicate in scientific language about important questions in Cognitive Neuroscience.
- describe findings and applications of research in Cognitive Neuroscience through articles published in professional journals as well as other media

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Course Assessment Measures:**

***Assignments***

Group presentation (20%):

Students will be required to participate in a group presentation of a research article in cognitive neuroscience. Groups of 2 or 3 students will be formed early in the semester based upon similar interests. Group members will choose an article published in a peer-reviewed journal in which the authors report and analyze their own experimental findings.

*The article to be presented must be approved by the professor before the presentation.*

***Quizzes & Exams***

LMS Quizzes (20%): The LMS quizzes will be multiple-choice or true/false questions covering assigned readings for the class meeting and the lecture materials that were covered in the previous week. They will be on Blackboard for 24 hours and you will have three attempts and the attempt with the higher grade will be kept in the gradebook. No make-up quizzes will be permitted. The instructor will drop the lowest quiz grade for each student at the end of the semester.

In-class short quizzes (10%): There will be recurring in-class short quizzes consisting of three to five questions. There will be a total of around 10 in-class short quizzes and taking 5 of these will be sufficient to earn the full assigned credit of 10% of the course grade.

Two Exams (50%; 25% each): There will be two exams during the semester consisting of multiple-choice questions. Questions will be on materials (e.g. lectures, readings from text, exercises) covered in class up to the exam date. While taking the exam you will be allowed to use *one US letter size cribsheet* on the front and back of which you may write anything from the textbook and/or lecture notes.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.
