## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is an undergraduate course that offers a survey of scientific and applied approaches to the study of human language.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

**Language Files: Materials for an Introduction to Language and Linguistics.** 13th Edition. The Ohio State University Press.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course will introduce students to a broad array of linguistic topics and prepare them to carry out more advanced study of descriptive, theoretical, and applied linguistics.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Topics to be covered include, non-exhaustively:

- phonetics
- phonology
- morphology
- syntax
- semantics
- pragmatics
- lexicography
- psycholinguistics
- sociolinguistics
- translation
- computational linguistics
- natural language processing for AI systems
- historical linguistics
- writing systems
- animal communication

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will have sufficient knowledge of linguistics to pursue advanced linguistic topics as well as practical work in areas like natural language processing.
- Students will develop problem-solving skills, which are key to linguistic investigation.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Student learning will be assessed by 4 exams, each worth 25% of the course grade.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

- The course grade will be based on 4 exams, worth 25% each.
- See the Attendance Policy regarding skipping class on exam days.
- Most test questions will be super-short-answer questions, defined as true/false, multiple choice, matching, and the like. Students will not be asked to recall (i.e., pull out of memory) vocabulary, though they will need to be able to recognize it.
- There will likely be a few opportunities for extra credit through in-class assignments. This extra credit will be available only to the students who attend those particular class meetings (that is, there will be no make-ups for extra credit assignments).
- There will be no grade restitution opportunities.
- Since the grading is objective, there should be no need to appeal grades. However, if a student chooses to appeal a grade, he or she will need to present his or her study log (described in Other Course Policies) along with the appeal.
- If a student uses technology inappropriately in class or engages in other non-collaborative behavior in class, 20% will be deducted from the next exam grade for each of the first three instances. Doing this more than three times will result in an automatic failure in the course.
- Grades are not directly penalized due to absences on non-exam days but some privileges are lost, as explained in the attendance policy.
- At the end of the semester, final grades will be assigned as follows: A *= 92 or higher; A- = 91-89; B+ = 88-86;B = 85-83; B- = 82-79; C+ = 78-76; C = 75-73; C- = 72-69; D+ = 68-66; D = 65-61; F = 60 or lower.*

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**NOTE: The policies below override any general institute policies that students might have heard about or that they might find on the web. Last spring the Provost publicly announced that professors can create policies that work best for their courses.**

- Students are expected to attend every class meeting. This will not only be the most efficient way to learn the material, it will also allow them to work on soft skills, such as going with the flow, operating as part of a group, and spending chunks of time away from their devices.
- Students are responsible for everything that happens during the entirety of every class, whether or not they attend. I highly recommend that students find at least two classmates with whom they agree to exchange notes if someone is absent. The professor will not be personally catching up every student who misses a class.
- Students must attend class on days when there are exams. Only official excuses (not emails to the professor) will be accepted for missing an exam.
  - If students are sick on the day of an exam, they need to go to the medical center to get an excuse. If they not do this, they will receive a zero on the exam with no option for a make-up.
  - If students anticipate not being able to attend an exam for some other officially excusable reason, they will need to get that excuse and let the professor know at least 2 weeks in advance so that the exam can be rescheduled for everyone.
  - If students have an official excuse for missing an exam, they will take that exam during the same class period as the next exam. For example, if someone is officially excused on the day of the first exam, he or she will take exam 1 and exam 2 during the exam 2 slot. Exams are not long--there will be plenty of time for this.
- Some of the course material will not be available in the textbook, it will only be available through lecture. Some lectures will have posted slide decks, others will not. Reading slide decks without having attended lectures is less useful than one might imagine, so students are highly encouraged to attend all class meetings.
- If students skip classes on non-exam days, this will have no direct effect on their grade but they will lose a privilege: they will not be permitted to use office hours to ask questions about that material.

### Other Course Policies:

- Students must attend the entirety of class meetings in order to be counted as present. See the implications of this in the attendance policy.
- The classroom will be a technology-free zone unless otherwise instructed. This keeps students from distracting themselves and everyone around them, including me.
- Students should always bring paper, scratch paper, a pen, a pencil with eraser, and their computer to class. We will sometimes be using computers for class exercises. If students have a digital textbook, they can use it during class assignments and study opportunities.
- Students must keep a log of study time if they plan to come to office hours or if they anticipate that they might not be satisfied with their grade. Without a history of *what* someone has studied, *how*, and *for how long*, we have nothing to do discuss. (Office hours can’t be used as a replacement for making oneself concentrate on one's own.) Studying needs to be active--mindlessly highlighting a textbook or flipping through slides doesn't count. I will give you study tips the first day of class and motivate them with research findings.
- Students should schedule study time so that they can ask questions during the class meeting prior to each exam.
- Students may drink from non-spill containers but no eating, please.
- Students should check their email regularly in case there are announcements about class meetings.
- If I am late to class, students are requested to wait for 30 min., during which they are welcome to use technology. Leaving earlier will mean being marked absent.
- All work must be done independently unless otherwise announced. Students may not consult the internet, AI tools, or other people for help or to look up answers.
- A standing homework assignment is to study the materials presented in class in preparation for the next exam. Readings and problem sets might also be assigned as homework.
- The whole semester’s class schedule will not be announced from the start. Instead, an evolving class schedule will be posted and regularly updated on LMS. Students will have a week or two’s notice about what’s coming next, but it is important to consult the LMS regularly for updates.
- Students should not plan travel so that they will miss any classes because the exam dates are not set in stone.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.
