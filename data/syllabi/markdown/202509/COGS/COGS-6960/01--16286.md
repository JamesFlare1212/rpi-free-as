## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course will provide students with a strong foundation in the basic principles and methods of Bayesian statistics and probabilistic modeling. Further, the course will introduce students to state-of-the-art computational methods for Bayesian inference and enable them to apply these methods to challenging real-world problems.

The course will commence with a self-contained introduction to probability theory and the basic logic of Bayesian estimation, model comparison, and decision theory. It will then cover the key differences between Bayesian and non-Bayesian methods, with a focus on the merits of uncertainty quantification and propagation. Participants will learn how to reason about sources of uncertainty and how to interpret the results of probabilistic inference. As the course progresses, we will dive into more advanced topics, such as the mechanics of Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods, hierarchical models, Bayesian meta-analysis, and simulation-based methods.

The course will also include multiple hands-on sessions using the popular R programming language to facilitate practical exposure to the theoretical ideas. In particular, we will cover all steps of principled Bayesian workflows, including checks for prior consistency, computational faithfulness, model sensitivity, and model validation. In the final part, we will discuss a selected set of topics at the frontier of Bayesian research, such as approximate Bayesian computation (ABC), variational inference (VI), and generative neural networks. By the end of the course, students will have a solid understanding of Bayesian principles and methods; they will be able to apply these methods to challenging real-world problems.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Critical and creative thinking about data analysis and probabilistic modeling in a trans- and interdisciplinary context.
- Ability to apply Bayesian methods, models, and visualization techniques to challenging inference problems using state-of-the-art (SOTA) statistical tools (R, Stan, brms).
- Demonstration of understanding of modern probabilistic modeling as it relates to data analysis in general and the cognitive sciences in particular.
- Ability to set up and perform principled Bayesian workflows, focusing on prior sensitivity, computational faithfulness, model adequacy, and predictive performance.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The final grade in the course will be based on the performance on the five (5) homework assignments and a final project including a presentation. The final project will involve carrying out a principled Bayesian workflow on a topic and data set of own choice and giving a short presentation of the analyses and results. Students will also have the opportunity to complete two (2) bonus writing assignments which can increase the final grade by 10%. The bonus assignments can be used, for example, to make up for a missed assignment or simply to systematize one’s understanding of a particular topic. The homework assignments intend to assess learning outcomes 1, 2, and 4; the bonus writing assignments will assess learning outcomes 1, 2, and 4. The final project and presentation will assess all learning outcomes. Graduate students are required to prepare and present a more challenging project compared to undergraduate students.
