## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course will provide an introduction to the world of optically active nanomaterials, focusing on their synthesis, properties, and applications. We will specifically cover the classification of different types of nanomaterials and structures based on dimensionality and shape, as well as their corresponding optical characteristics. The emergence of novel optical properties (e.g., localized surface plasmon resonance, quantum fluorescence, polarization) will be discussed in the context of the structure, shape, and material chemistry of the nanostructures and their assemblies, along with potential applications. The lecture materials will briefly cover the fundamental theory of nano-optics (i.e., complex refractive index, wave equations, Maxwell's equations), followed by illustrative examples of applications drawn from a variety of technologically important nanomaterials, including those in the fields of the environment, electronics, energy, and healthcare. After the first 10 lectures, students will need to proactively connect the lecture materials with emerging nanotechnology by participating in literature reviews, discussions, and design project assignments.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Design Projects**

Two (2) design projects will be assigned, collected, and graded. Adequate solution of these exercises requires a wave optics simulation using finite element methods (FEM). Design reports will be submitted on or before the due dates listed on the Schedule of Events. Failure to submit on time will lead to grade penalties – 5% point deducted for every hour of delay after the final submission time.

**Literature Review**

All students should prepare individual literature review presentation (40 minutes including 15 min Q&A session) with their own selection of materials (but selection will be consulted with the instructor by submitting the list in advance). The guidelines and template of the presentation will be provided.  
**Group Discussion**

Adequate solution of these exercises requires a group effort; for this course, the nominal group size will be Three or Four (3 or 4) students. Group discussion assignment should cover the two different technologies using optical nanomaterials for sensing or imaging applications.

**Homework**

Doing promotes reading research papers related to lecture materials. Hence, homework will be assigned on a regular basis. Homework will be collected and graded on a binary basis (OK/not-OK) that resulted in 100 or 70 percent . You will find it easy to abuse this approach. Resist the temptation. Failure to submit on time will lead to grade penalties

**Quizzes** At the beginning of class on quiz days, students will complete a short quiz consisting of 5 simple questions based on the previous lecture to reinforce key concepts and assess understanding.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

- Academic Integrity

We live in an age of ethical ambiguity; expectations are often wildly discordant with effort, and the entrepreneurial imperative appears to some to offer a generalized shelter for the morally impaired. This should concern us all. We therefore append the following statements regarding academic integrity:

1. Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments which students turn in are their own. Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The **Rensselaer Handbook** defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Students should familiarize themselves with this portion of the Rensselaer Handbook and should note that the penalties for plagiarism and other forms of cheating can be quite harsh.

2. A local definition of "cheating" seems necessary. For this course, let us use the following: collaborative work of any kind on excessive inter-individual cooperation on the design projects, constitutes cheating. "Collaboration" and "cooperation" include sharing your work with others, as well as receiving and using the work of others.
