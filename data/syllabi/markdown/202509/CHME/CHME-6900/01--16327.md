## Course Goals

### Goals:

This seminar-based graduate level course provides a broad exposure to the chemical and biological engineering discipline. Attending and participating in seminars from recognized experts will form the core of the course. Topics include energy and the environment, nano and biotechnologies, computational molecular science, polymers and advanced materials, synthetic biology, metabolic engineering, and the future of our discipline. The course is required for chemical engineering Ph.D. students.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Recognize the problems and related scientific and societal impacts of the problems in the field of chemical and biological engineering presented by the speaker.
- Understand the methodologies and technical aspects to solve the problems presented by the seminar speaker.
- Evaluate the key results and conclusions presented by the seminar speaker.
- Identify the relevance of the seminar materials to students’ own research projects for future project developments and other directions.
- Ask questions that will clarify relevant materials and results of the talk based on the students’ own critical review of the seminar.
- Learn the presentation skills utilized by the seminar speakers.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

You are required to attend all departmental seminars during the semester. You should make every effort to avoid commitments that would interfere with your ability to be present at the seminar time. Unexcused absences bring the risk of grade consequences. **A student is allowed a maximum of 2 unexcused absences per semester.** **A student who has reached 2 unexcused absences will receive an electronic warning, and the third absence will fail the course. Late attendance and leaving during the seminar will be considered as an absence. Students must be in the seminar room before the seminar starts.** **Please adjust your schedule ahead to attend seminars. An absence excuse, which could be avoided ahead, will not be accepted.** Do email me (lees27@rpi.edu) beforehand if you have an official duty that prevents you from attending the seminar that particular week. Please note that the two unexcused absences are given for unexpected urgent personal matters.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
