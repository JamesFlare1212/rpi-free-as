## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

The textbook for the course is Bioprocess Engineering: Basic Concepts, 3rd Edition, 2017. M.L. Shuler, F. Kargi and M. DeLisa. Not one single textbook is ideal for this course; however, this one provides a good read of the material and sets of problems. In addition, several documents will be assigned for reading, including published manuscripts and some websites

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This is an unusual course in that it enrolls both undergraduate and graduate students. Nevertheless, the course is meant as an introduction to the principles of biochemical engineering. It is meant for a student with little or no formal training in the life sciences and will focus on the engineering aspects of biotechnology. Such issues include enzyme technology, protein/enzyme engineering, microbial and mammalian cell growth and product formation, bioreactor design and mass transfer, genetic engineering, and biomanufacturing.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • To understand where biochemical engineering plays a key role in the broader field of biotechnology
- • To understand enzyme technology and using enzymes in biotechnology applications
- • To understand fermentation and mammalian cell culture
- • To gain a basic understanding of bioprocessing (upstream and downstream).

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**GRADING:** The course grading will consist of five parts. Part 1 is Readings. Readings will test the ability to understand and present a research/review article. Part 2 is homework, which will solicit a written summary of a scientific article. It will be due periodically throughout the semester. I will prepare the homework, and it will be graded solely by me. I will allow study groups to discuss homework assignments; however, the homework that you submit must be your own. Part 3 is a team project with a final presentation at the end of the semester. Standards of performance are set by the instructor, independent of average student achievement. Part 4 is a take-home exam that presents an open-ended problem and solicits creative thinking and application of topics discussed in the course. Exam grade will test the ability to think broadly and creatively. Part 5 will be based on attendance and active participation in class.

The following is a breakdown of the grading:

| Readings | 15% |
| --- | --- |
| Homework | 15% |
| Project | 30% |
| Exam | 30% |
| Attendance | 10% |

Acceptable excuses for absence from class are: 1) a medical excuse validated by the Student Success office or 2) an appropriate statement from the Student Success office, presented to the instructor in advance of the examination. I look for clear fundamental understanding of the material; a good measure of understanding is the ability to tackle new problems with acquired skills. Partial credit will be given on exams. If you are allowed extra time on exams due to personal reasons that are validated by the Dean of Students, you must provide the instructor with signed documentation from the Dean of Students by the second week of class so that appropriate arrangements can be made.

Grading decisions are the sole responsibility of the faculty member. I do not grade capriciously, but grading mistakes are inevitable. If you wish to contest a grade, please provide specific reasons for contesting thegrading in a clearly written explanation to me within two business days of receipt of your graded assignment. After I re-evaluate your grade, it is final and will not be further re-evaluated.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work.
