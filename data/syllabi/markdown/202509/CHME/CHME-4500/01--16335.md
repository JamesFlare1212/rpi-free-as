## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The heart of any chemical plant is the chemical reactor, which is *designed to transform raw materials into desired chemical and material products*. The proper design and operation of chemical reactors can make or break a commercial enterprise. This course covers the principles of chemical equilibrium and kinetics, reactor design, and detailed analyses for both homogeneous and heterogeneous (catalytic) reactors. Specific topics include: developing strategies for reactors involving simple and multiple reactions (achieving optimum selectivity and yield of the desired product), the analysis of reactor combinations, and the design of isothermal, adiabatic, and optimum temperature profiles for any given reactor and reaction sequence. These concepts are also applicable to a wide range of systems beyond the chemical industry, including semiconductor fabrication, wastewater treatment, pharmaceutical production, and even natural processes that occur inside living cells.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Use stoichiometric relationships to quantify species balances, extent of reaction, and conversion in reactive systems.
- Apply principles of chemical equilibrium to predict equilibrium conversion and assess the feasibility of reversible reactions in reactors.
- Formulate and solve mole and mass balances for ideal reactors (batch, semi-batch, CSTR, and plug flow) using appropriate rate laws and stoichiometry.
- Perform energy balances for isothermal and non-isothermal reactors, including analysis of temperature effects on reaction rates and conversion.
- Analyze and design fixed-bed catalytic reactors, considering reaction kinetics and transport limitations.
- Use computational tools (e.g., Python programming) to model reactor performance, visualize key variables, and explore how changes in parameters affect conversion, yield, and selectivity.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The course learnings will be assessed through *open-response questions on individual and group assignments, and exams*. In these assessment methods:

- Conceptual Understanding: Students will explain fundamental principles in words and equations, assessing their ability to remember, understand, and apply concepts.
- Problem Solving: Students will work through reactor design calculations, evaluating their ability to analyze and apply rate laws, and material and energy balances.
- Computational Modeling: Students will use Python-based Jupyter Notebooks to model reactor performance, visualize key variables, and explore how changes in parameters affect conversion, yield, and selectivity.
- Case Studies: Students will work on case studies to synthesize concepts, assessing their ability to evaluate and create solutions in realistic scenarios.
- Collaborative Skills: Through group assignments, students will demonstrate the ability to communicate technical results clearly and work effectively in teams.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance in this course is **not required** but is **highly encouraged**. Regular participation will prepare you more effectively for assignments and exams because:

- Many of the mathematical derivations central to reactor design will be worked out step by step during lectures. These derivations are best understood by following the logical progression in class.
- Python programming sessions will be conducted in class, where you will gain hands-on practice in modeling reactors and exploring parameter effects. Attending these sessions will give you direct guidance and troubleshooting support.
- Opportunities to earn **bonus points** may be offered during class. These are designed as an incentive for engagement. Students who do not attend class may not later claim that bonus opportunities were unavailable to them.

In short, while attendance is not mandatory, consistent engagement in class will strongly enhance your learning experience and your performance in the course.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

All students are expected to uphold the highest standards of academic integrity. Any form of cheating, plagiarism, or misrepresentation of work will not be tolerated.

- Collaboration: Collaborative or group work is encouraged when explicitly allowed (e.g., group assignments, case studies, and in-class problem-solving). However, unless otherwise specified, all homework assignments and exams are to be completed individually. You may discuss the individual assignments in a collaborative setting, but submitting work that is not your own will be considered a violation.
- Cheating: Communicating with others during exams, copying solutions, or submitting code/derivations that are not your own without citation constitutes cheating
- Plagiarism: Copying text, figures, or code from any source without proper citation is plagiarism. All work must be written in your own words and reflect your own understanding.

**Penalties for Violations**:

- First offense: A grade of zero will be given on the assignment, exam, or project.
- Second offense: The student will receive a grade of F for the course.

By remaining enrolled in this course, you acknowledge that you have read and understood this policy. If you are uncertain whether collaboration or use of resources is permitted on a specific assignment, please ask the instructor for clarification *in advance*.
