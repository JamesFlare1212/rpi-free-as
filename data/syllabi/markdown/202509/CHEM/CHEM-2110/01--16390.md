### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate a proficient knowledge of acid-base and redox equilibrium chemistry

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty, and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, the identity of those involved in the collaboration must be explicitly described for transparency. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy may result in a severe penalty. In this course, a first offense will be penalized with a grade worth 25% of the assignment’s graded value and a strict warning. A second offense will result in total failure of the course with a grade of F. Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively). If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

***A special note on plagiarism.*** Please see the definition of plagiarism given below, as outlined in The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights. Though this particular class does not contain a term paper component in its current form, you are advised to avoid plagiarism as a general practice. If the need arises to quote a source, appropriate reference must be made to attribute the work to that source. Get in the practice, early and often, of making a clear distinction between your words and thoughts in comparison to others. This will be very important as you progress in your courses and career. If in doubt, please, consult with your peers and your instructor to clarify when a source must be cited.

*“**Plagiarism** Representing the work or words of another as one’s own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference. For example, using sentences verbatim from a published source in a term paper without appropriate referencing, or presenting as one’s own the detailed argument of a published source, or presenting as one’s own electronically or digitally enhanced graphic representations from any form of media.”* - ACADEMIC INTEGRITY (2014-2016 Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights & Responsibilities, August 2014)
