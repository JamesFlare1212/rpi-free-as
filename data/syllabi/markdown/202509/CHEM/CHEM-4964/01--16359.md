## Course Content

### Content Details:

## Course Content

1. *Stereochemistry:* Properties of stereoisomers, concepts in stereochemistry (topism-prochirality), diastereoselectivity and diastereoselective reactions (Felkin-Ahn model, Chelation control, The Houk model, Zimmerman-Traxler transition state, etc.), conformational analysis: effects on stability and reactivity. (Ch. 32, 33, 34).

2. *Huckel molecular orbitals (HMOs), Frontier molecular orbitals (FMOs) and the concept of aromaticity*.

3. *Pericyclic reactions:*Electrocyclic reactions, cycloadditions (e.g. Diels-Alder) and cycloreversions, sigmatropic rearrangements (Ch. 35, 36).
4. *Organometallic Chemistry (Ch. 8, 48) and Introduction to Catalytic Cycle*

5\. *Retrosynthetic Analysis* (Ch. 30)  
*6. Aromatic heterocycles:* Structure, reactions and synthesis (Ch. 43, 44)  
*7. Saturated heterocycles and stereoelectronics* (Ch. 42)  
*8. Asymmetric Synthesis* (Ch. 45)

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- At the end of this course, the student will gain a better understanding of: a. The effect of stereochemistry on organic reaction outcomes (topic 1) b. The concept of HMOs, FMOs, Mechanisms and stereochemical outcomes of pericyclic reactions (topics 2&3) c. The reactions of organometallic compounds: C-C, C-O and C-N bond formation using metal catalysts (e.g. Pd) (topic 4) d. The way to design organic syntheses properly using retrosynthetic analysis (topic 5) e. The structures, reactions, and synthesis of heterocyclic compounds (topics 6&7) f. The importance and examples of asymmetric synthesis (topic 8)

**Semester Exams**

Each exam will be non-cumulative and will cover specific topics as specified in the table above.

**Homework**

Homework will be assigned at the end of topic #1, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8 and will be turned in on Thursday of the following week. Late assignments will be penalized at 10% per day late. It is well established that students learn much more through active learning than through passive learning. Attending and taking notes in lecture, and reading the textbook are passive learning, while working homework problems is active learning. Therefore, most of your learning will take place while you work homework problems. Some students may find that assigned homework is sufficient for them to master the concepts we study. Others will need more practice before they achieve full mastery. I strongly recommend that you try textbook exercises to supplement the homework assignments since the answers are available to you in the solution manual. It is up to you to assess your own understanding, and to work enough exercises to achieve your desired level of mastery.

**Final grades**

Final grades will be determined by one of two metrics, depending on average student performance.

**(i)** **If the average final grade is greater than or equal to 80%, then a traditional scoring system will be used:**

*≥* 93% *→* A *≥* 80% and *<* 83% *→* B- *≥* 67% and *<* 70% *→* D+

*≥* 90% and *<* 93% *→* A- *≥* 77% and *<* 80% *→* C+ *≥* 60% and *<* 67% *→* D

*≥* 87% and *<* 90% *→* B+ *≥* 73% and *<* 77% *→* C *<* 60% *→* F

*≥* 83% and *<* 87% *→* B *≥* 70% and *<* 73% *→* C-

The inequalities should make it clear that a final grade of 86.99999 earns a B, and does not round up to a B+.

**(ii)** **If the average final grade is below 80%, then a curve will be applied such that the average student score corresponds to the border between a C+ and a B-.**

If a curve is applied, the borderline between grades is not sharply defined, and the instructor reserves the right to adjust a grade up or down that is near a borderline (based on class attendance, participation, office hour attendance, etc.).

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work.
