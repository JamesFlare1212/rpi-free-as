## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course introduces the fundamentals of protein structure and function with an emphasis on chemical concepts and small molecule-protein interactions. The course is focused on the basic biochemical concepts required for advanced studies in biochemistry and biotechnology and is intended for first year graduate and advanced undergraduate students. The course will explore the fundamentals of protein structure on an interactive basis. Students will bring their laptop computers to class and we will conduct molecular visualization exercises during the lectures.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Introduction to the Protein Data Bank

Molecular Visualization: Discovery Studio/Swiss PDB/ MOE download

Amino acids: Chemical attributes, the peptide bond (acid-base chemistry, reactions, stereochemistry, optical activity, spectroscopic properties, separation)

Primary structure of proteins (sequence determination, protein sequence data banks)

Secondary structure of proteins (conformational analysis, torsional angles, secondary structure motifs, domains)

Interactive exercises: Secondary structure of proteins

Secondary and Super-secondary structure of proteins (protein folding and determinants of stability)

Interactive exercises: Super-secondary structure of proteins

Tertiary and higher order structure of proteins

Interactive exercises: Tertiary structure of proteins

Enzymes: Kinetics and molecular mechanisms (structure and function, enzyme kinetics, MM/BH analysis, enzyme inhibition)

Catalytic mechanisms of enzymes (properties of the active site, topology and function, catalytic models, general acid catalysis, metal ion catalysis, covalent catalysis, categories of biochemical reactions, catalytic mechanisms)

Interactive exercises: Enzyme active sites

Class Presentations

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

I. In-class interactive exercises: These will assess-  
(a) Students ability to search for and download coordinates of protein structures from the Protein Data Bank (Rutgers Center for Structural Biology) (Worldwide web resource for obtaining three-dimensional structural coordinates of proteins and enzymes) and (b) use of shareware software, Swiss PDB Viewer (molecular visualization program to manipulate and display the three-dimensional structure of proteins and enzymes), to model and visualize the protein coordinates that are downloaded from the Protein Data Bank.  
II. In class quiz: This will assess-  
(a) Students ability to explain the primary structure of proteins including sequence determination and the use of protein sequence online databanks and (b) ability to describe the primary sequence of proteins and conduct homology analysis of proteins.  
III. In-class interactive exercises: These will assess-  
(a) Students ability to identify the structural elements of the secondary structure of proteins (including conformational analysis, torsional angles, secondary structure motifs and domains) and (b) ability to conduct interactive molecular visualization exercises to examine the secondary structure of proteins.  
IV. In class quiz: This will assess-  
Students ability to identify the secondary and super-secondary structure of proteins.  
V. In-class interactive exercises: These will assess-  
(a) Students ability to conduct interactive molecular visualization exercises to examine the super-secondary structure of proteins and (b) describe the tertiary and higher order structure of proteins.  
VI. Final presentations: These will assess-  
Students ability to describe the catalytic mechanisms of enzymes (including the properties of the active site, topology and function and catalytic models) and examine the active catalytic sites of enzymes.
