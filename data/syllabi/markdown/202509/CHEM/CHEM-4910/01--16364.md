## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Objectives**  
**CHEM-2910: Mentor First-Year Chemistry Students (New Mentors)**  
As a Freshmen Chemistry Mentor, the student is assigned to meet and provide leadership and support for two groups of students every week. The mentoring sessions will address topics such as problem-solving skills for chemistry, coping skills, time-management skills, etc...  
**CHEM-4910: Leadership in Chemistry Mentoring I (Returning Mentors)**  
As a Freshman Chemistry Mentor, the student is assigned to meet and provide leadership for two groups of students every week. The mentoring sessions will address topics such as problem-solving skills for chemistry, coping skills, time-management skills, etc... As an experienced mentor, the student is expected to provide academic and program support for new mentors.  
**CHEM-4920: Leadership in Chemistry Mentoring II (Senior Mentors)**  
As a senior chemistry mentor, the student will meet and provide leadership for a group of mentors every week. The supervisory sessions will address topics such as facilitation skills, problem-solving skills, coping skills, time-management skills, etc... As a senior chemistry mentor, the student is expected to train and supervise new and returning mentors, as well as helping with other administrative tasks.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Expected Outcomes CHEM-2910: Mentor First-Year Chemistry Students**  
The student will

1. Develop effective leadership skills for freshmen chemistry
2. Establish a positive, supportive, and inclusive community of learning
3. Enhance communication, presentation, and facilitation skills
4. Increase their awareness and knowledge of campus resources
5. Use various motivational techniques to motivate student learning
6. Improve foundation knowledge of chemistry
7. Be more familiar with testing and assessment techniques

**CHEM-4910: Leadership in Chemistry Mentoring I**

The student will

1. Increase implementation of active and collaborative learning strategies
2. Identify transferrable skills that align with personal and professional goals
3. Identify strengths of students to enhance learning
4. Increase knowledge about students, their development, and the mentoring process
5. Enhance communication and training skills
6. Provide feedback in order to improve the mentoring program
7. Establish a positive, supportive, and inclusive team environment and community of learning

**CHEM-4920: Leadership in Chemistry Mentoring II**

The student will

1. Develop leadership and supervisory skills
2. Provide suggestions to improve the mentoring program
3. Provide academic and other support for new and returning mentors
4. Establish effective working relationships between peers and faculty members
5. Establish a positive and inclusive team environment for productive collaboration

**Class Requirements** **CHEM-2910: Mentor First-Year Chemistry Students**  
The student is required to

- Prepare for weekly mentoring sessions for two different groups
- Attend all mentor sessions, supervisory meetings, office hours, and other mentoring functions
- Attend, actively participate in, and complete all summer and online training
- Regularly check the homework status of the mentees, discuss progress with them, and offer help if needed
- Take attendance at weekly mentoring sessions and promptly update to spreadsheet
- Hold two study hours each week
- Be an available resource for mentees inside and outside of sessions

**CHEM-4910: Leadership in Chemistry Mentoring I**

The student is required to

- Prepare for weekly mentoring sessions for two different groups
- Attend all mentor sessions, weekly meetings, training sessions, office hours, and other mentoring functions
- Regularly check the homework status of the mentees, discuss progress with them, and offer help if needed
- Take attendance at weekly mentoring sessions and promptly update to spreadsheet
- Hold two study hours each week
- Be an available resource for mentees inside and outside of sessions
- Be a role model and a resource for new mentors in mentor training and throughout the semester
- Help facilitate weekly supervisory meetings if needed.
- Assist senior mentors in program activities (e.g. observation, training, proctoring tests, feedback, etc.)

**CHEM-4920: Leadership in Chemistry Mentoring II**

The student is required to

- Prepare and meet weekly with faculty program director
- Attend all weekly meetings, training sessions, office hours, and other mentoring functions
- Regularly monitor the homework status of mentor groups, discuss progress with them, and offer help if needed
- Take attendance at weekly supervisory and study hours as well as monitor attendance of mentoring groups
- Regularly hold and supervise study hours
- Be an available resource for mentors inside and outside of sessions
- Help lead and facilitate discussion groups in weekly meetings
- Observe and provide constructive feedback for mentors in your team
- Assist in preparation, proctoring, and grading of exams as well as any other administrative work

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

The relationship between students and faculty is based upon trust and the continued maintenance of this trust is necessary for education to be successful. Students need to trust faculty to make appropriate judgments about the content and structure of the course. Faculty members need to trust that the work turned in by students represents their own effort. Violation of this trust undermines the educational process. In addition, as professionals, there is no tolerance for breach of academic integrity such as cheating, plagiarizing, or inappropriate sharing of laboratories, data or related materials (past and present). **Students found to use any form of Academic Dishonesty will receive an F in the course.**

The Rensselaer Student Handbook and the Dean of Students Office Webpage (http://doso.rpi.edu/update.do?artcenterkey=676) defines various forms of academic dishonesty. Please familiarize yourself with these and ask us if you have questions about what constitutes academic dishonesty.
