## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This seminar is intended to provide engineering and science sophomore and juniors participating in the accelerated preMBA program with a compelling look into our Master’s program, gaining a better sense of how an MBA can empower engineers and scientists to become well-rounded professionals capable of driving innovation, leading teams, and making strategic contributions to their organizations' success. Through this seminar, pre-Eng/Sci MBA students will gain a better understanding of what topics they will be learning in the MBA program, what career paths will be open for them, how an MBA can complement their undergraduate degree, and what outcomes they can expect.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students will be able to identify key concepts and principles in business management, including strategic management, operations strategy and supply chain, entrepreneurship, marketing strategies, financial management, and ethical considerations in business**.**

Students will be able to synthesize information from guest speaker presentations, class activities, and class discussions to develop strategic insights into current trends and issues impacting the business world

## Course Content

### Content Details:

This seminar series draws on the expertise of RPI faculty, program alumni, and industry leaders, offering a flexible and dynamic curriculum that rotates every semester through various contemporary topics. Students may explore areas such as artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, data analytics, supply chain challenges, and ethical considerations in management. In addition to addressing these cutting-edge subjects, the seminars emphasize the professional development of students, focusing on the discovery, enhancement, and application of skills necessary for effective collaboration within diverse teams. The seminar employs a variety of instructional methods, including class hands-on activities, guest lectures, projects, and potential field visits, to ensure a comprehensive learning experience.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
