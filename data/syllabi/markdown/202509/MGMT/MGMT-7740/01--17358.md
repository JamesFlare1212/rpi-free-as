## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course covers the principles of financial and managerial accounting, focusing on both the user and preparer perspectives. Participants will learn to prepare and interpret accounting information for external reporting and internal decision-making. The financial accounting component focuses on the construction and analysis of external financial statements, while the managerial accounting section emphasizes the use of accounting data in decision-making and performance evaluation.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Course Objectives:**

- Develop a comprehensive understanding of fundamental financial accounting concepts.
- Enhance proficiency in analyzing and working with accounting data using rigorous analytical methods.
- Acquire the skills to prepare, interpret, and conduct analysis of financial statements.
- Understand accounting data to support effective managerial decision-making and performance evaluation.
- Enhance proficiency in using Excel for the analysis of accounting data.

**Learning Outcomes:**

- Demonstrate an understanding of fundamental financial accounting concepts and key financial statements.
- Prepare and interpret core financial statements: balance sheet, income statement, statement of retained earnings, and statement of cash flows.
- Analyze financial statements using ratio analysis and other analytical techniques.
- Analyze accounting data and apply insights to inform managerial business decisions.
- Apply intermediate-level Excel skills to effectively analyze accounting data.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Assignments:**

The most effective way to learn accounting is through consistent practice. The assignments in this course are designed to engage you in hands-on learning. You are expected to complete all chapters and related assignments according to the Schedule of Assignments. **All assignments are due by 11:59 PM ET on their respective due dates.** Submissions made after this time will be considered late and will not be accepted.

**Homework:** Homework assignments are completed online. You are allowed up to three attempts, with the highest score counting toward your final grade. Each attempt will offer a new set of problems to provide essential practice in mastering key accounting concepts. To maximize your success in this course, I strongly recommend the following approach for each homework submission:

- Review the feedback provided after each submission to identify any mistakes.
- For problems answered incorrectly, revisit the corresponding sections of the chapter to strengthen your understanding.
- Once you have clarified the concept or technique, reattempt the same problem and ensure that you can arrive at the correct answer on your own.
- If the concept remains unclear, seek assistance from your instructor without delay.

**Projects:** All projects must be completed using **Excel** for Microsoft 365 and submitted as an Excel file through the LMS. No other file formats will be accepted. After submitting your file, ensure that it is readable by downloading and reviewing it. If there are any issues, make the necessary changes and resubmit the file. Strict adherence to project instructions is crucial; failure to follow them may result in a score of zero. These projects are designed to apply key course concepts to real-world business scenarios while enhancing your proficiency with Excel. A substantial portion of your project grade will be based on the accurate application of Excel commands, formulas, and functions. Students being able to troubleshoot and fix their mistakes on their own is one of the major goals of these projects.

**Attendance Quizzes:** Each chapter may feature one or more attendance quizzes, aimed at assessing your comprehension of the material and recording class attendance. These quizzes contribute significantly to your overall grade, making it essential to perform well. Attendance quizzes can only be submitted by students physically present in class. Submitting an attendance quiz without attending will receive -20 points for that assignment, and it will be considered academic dishonesty. In the case of a missed attendance quiz, a documented excuse (e.g., a doctor’s note or institutional documentation) is required to arrange a make-up.

**Exams:** The Exams are timed assessments administered in class. Accounting concepts build on top of each other. As such all exams will be cumulative because you need the foundational concepts from previous chapters to be able to answer questions from later chapters. Any form of collaboration or cheating during the exams is strictly prohibited and will be considered academic dishonesty. Sharing or discussing exam content or answers, whether directly or indirectly, with students who have not yet taken the exam also constitutes academic dishonesty. In the event of a missed exam, a documented excuse (e.g., a doctor’s note or institutional documentation) is required to arrange a make-up. Please note that **make-up** exams will differ from the original and will be significantly more challenging. Do not leave for vacation before the **Final Exam**; make-up exams will not be allowed to accommodate early travel plans.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).
