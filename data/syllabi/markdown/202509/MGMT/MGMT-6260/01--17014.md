## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides a general overview of the entrepreneurial finance market as reflected in the entrepreneurial firm life cycle. We will cover, among many things, how to break down the valuation problem of early-stage ideas, the sources of financing for young businesses and which sources are more vs. less appropriate for different types of young businesses, investment strategy for fundraising, and how contract terms change over a business's life-cycle, potential exit strategies, the inner-workings of the VC industry and the incentives of the different parties involved in the process.

As such the primary objective of the course is to provide an understanding of the concepts and institutions involved in entrepreneurial finance and private equity markets, with a focus on VCs. Entrepreneurial finance and VC investing are intrinsically linked, and the course is designed to be broad and comprehensive, with the emphasis being on entrepreneurial finance. In order for an entrepreneur to make the appropriate decisions from a financing perspective and to obtain the most suitable financial contracts, they have to understand the motivations of the various investors that invest in entrepreneurial ventures. Conversely, investors need to understand things from an entrepreneur’s perspective and what incentivizes them in order to evaluate the various investment opportunities with which they are presented. Consequently, the course is designed for students who are interested in either pursuing entrepreneurial activity or financing entrepreneurial ventures or both.

Success in this 4-credit (3-credit) course is based on the expectation that students will spend a minimum of 10 hours of study time per week in preparation for class (readings, cases/assignments, group projects, preparation for exams, etc.)

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Homework Assignments: Assigned during lectures – reading, research, and problems. Homework assignments will not be graded. However, it is a very important aspect of the course that prepares you for quizzes and exams.
- Case Studies:\&nbsp;These are cases at the end of the chapters. These will be discussed in class. You will first discuss it among your group members, summarize your answers to be turned in, along with\&nbsp;a general class discussion.
- Exams:\&nbsp;\<span> \<span> \<span> There are two exams for the course, a midterm and one at the end of the semester.\&nbsp; The midterm will be administered about halfway through the semester and the second exam will be administered during the last week of classes. For the graduate class, there will be additional problems.\&nbsp;\&nbsp;\&nbsp; \&nbsp;\&nbsp;\</span>\</span>\</span>
- \<i>\</i>Quizzes:\&nbsp;There will be 4 quizzes. They will be comprised of multiple-choice questions and problems (The best 3 out of 4 counted).\&nbsp;
- Group Project - This is a capstone project. The capstone project simulates the process of evaluating and pitching an investment in a startup. Students will form teams to act as either entrepreneurs seeking funding or investors evaluating the opportunity. The deliverables will be a write up along with a 12-slide pitch deck. This will be accompanied by a presentation. Graduate students will complete an additional individual financial analysis and investment memo based on their team’s work.
- Flash Presentations: 3-minute individual presentations on "A finance concept in the real world that relates to entrepreneurial finance for the week of the presentation."

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Upon completion of the course the students are expected to:

1. Understand business valuation in the entrepreneurial context
2. Understand financial contracting in addressing information and incentive problems
3. Understand core elements of financial planning for an entrepreneurial venture
4. Understand the sources of internal and external finance for entrepreneurial ventures
5. Understand the relevant elements of a deal structure
6. Understand venture finance in a portfolio context
7. Understand the economics behinds the venture capital market and deal structure

Although the students learning outcomes are similar between undergraduate and graduate students, it is expected that the graduate students will have a deeper understanding of the topics covered. As such, it is expected that their answers to cases will be more complete and offer greater insights into the issues discussed in each of the cases. In addition, it is expected that their answers to questions asked in class will be more insightful. Notice also, as mentioned below, that this difference will also be reflected in the tests taken during the semester.
