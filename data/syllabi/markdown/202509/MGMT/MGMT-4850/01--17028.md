## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This undergraduate course provides an overview of basic processes in human behavior that influence the effectiveness of individuals, groups, and organizations. Its focus is on understanding what happens during interpersonal interactions in work situations, and what can be done to make employees more effective. Topics covered include organizational socialization, motivation, decision-making, team dynamics, virtual teams, influence, and conflict management. Numerous exercises and case analyses are used in class to help provide students with insights into these processes.

The increased professionalization of the workforce and high value placed on technology and knowledge work in the global economy emphasize the importance of good workforce management skills for managers and professionals. To be effective, we need to act as "applied" behavioral scientists, that is, we must carefully study our organizations, units, and direct reports and develop an ability to recognize what makes each one tick. In this course more than most others, we need to know not only the conceptual material presented but also how it applies to our work lives and ourselves. People who understand the work environment, know themselves, and can assess their effects on others are more likely to be effective than those who are blind to these dimensions.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Communicate more effectively in written and oral presentations.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Exam 1 (10%) / 2 (10%) / 3 (10%)**

Exam 1 will consist of 20 multiple choice questions spanning all material covered up to and on September 19. Exam 2 will consist of 20 multiple choice questions spanning all material covered *after* Exam 1. Exam 3 will consist of 20 multiple choice questions spanning all material covered *after* Exam 2. Exams are meant to assess your ability to identify and understand the class content. Information in both lectures and assigned reading will be included on exams. Lecture slides will be posted on Blackboard to aid you in studying.

**Group Activities (30%)**

Every class with a lecture on and between September 2 and November 21 will contain an activity that requires you to apply course concepts to solve a problem, analyze a situation, and/or engage in discussion/debate. These activities are meant to apply concepts taught during the lecture portion of the class to streamline the learning process and stimulate engagement. Activity performance will be assessed at the group level based on a set of measures which I provide access to in the syllabus. Out of the 21 total activities that will occur, only the 15 activities with the highest grade will contribute to the overall grade. If you do not attend class, you will not receive credit for your group’s activity performance.

**Group Presentation (20%)**

Your group (same as your activity group) will be given a classic organizational issue that must be critically analyzed using class concepts. Groups will each have 10 minutes to give their presentations (see grading measures in the syllabus).

**Final Exam (20%)**

There will be a final exam which will assess your cumulative understanding of the class content. The final exam will consist of 50 multiple choice questions. Please check the school website for timing and location of the final exam.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
