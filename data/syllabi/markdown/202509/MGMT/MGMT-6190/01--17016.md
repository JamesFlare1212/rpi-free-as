## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course introduces key concepts in corporate and investment finance and accounting, providing students with a solid foundation in financial decision-making principles and practices. It is designed for learners with minimal prerequisites, basic statistics, algebra, and introductory accounting, making it accessible to beginners, while also offering deeper insights and reinforcement for those with prior finance knowledge.

The course begins with asset valuation principles and applies them to a range of real and financial assets, including personal finance, corporate projects, debt instruments, equities, and firms. It examines the relationship between risk and return, explores corporate capital structure and tax effects, and integrates these elements into firm valuation, which serves as the culmination of the course.

As a broad survey, the course emphasizes both the “how” and the “why” of financial decision-making. While not all topics can be covered in depth, students will gain a strong conceptual framework and practical tools. Active participation, consistent attendance, and thorough preparation are essential to maximize learning and overall performance.

The classes will be a combination of PowerPoint slides, in-class discussions, exercises, computation-oriented spreadsheets, and examinations.

### Attendance Policy:

Policy on Missing Classes and Exams for Interviews

We strongly advise that students schedule job and internship interviews around their class times and exam schedules. Employers understand that academics are your top priority. For off-campus interviews at the employer’s site, most will accommodate a student who needs to schedule an interview around a class or exam. For on-campus interviews, you should sign up quickly- as soon as possible- since these timeslots are fixed and are available on a first-come first-served basis only. In the event that an interview conflicts with a scheduled class, you must notify the professor in advance; the sooner you do that you demonstrate professional courtesy and a sense of commitment to the professor. How the professor treats the absence is at the professor’s discretion in accordance with the course syllabus or other means of communication. An interview conflict is not a valid reason for missing an exam. If you experience or anticipate problems, you should seek advice from your advisors.
