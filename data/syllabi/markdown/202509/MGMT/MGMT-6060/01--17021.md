## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course gives students an in depth understanding of the strategic issues involved in managing technology and innovation within firms. Building on these principles, the course then gives students exposure to the strategic implications of Artificial Intelligence, and the managerial challenges it creates within firms. Through this course, students will:

1. Develop a realistic understanding of how technologies evolve over time, and the organizational, cognitive and economic foundations that underpin technological change.
2. Be exposed to the multiple constraints that arise in established firms in adapting and reacting to disruptive technologies, and have a sense of the issues involved in managing disruption.
3. Gain an appreciation of how various elements, such as markets, customer characteristics, and value creation/value capture interact to influence profitability in technologically intensive environments.
4. Understand how to use predictive and generative AI to identify and exploit business opportunities.
5. Develop insight into issues such as the future of work, and the role of agentic AI systems in today’s organizations.

This course is relevant for students interested in managing technology strategy and organizational change in large firms. Additionally, it is relevant for entrepreneurs and managers interested in starting a new venture in today’s AI rich environment. The course would also benefit those that are interested in pursuing careers in venture capital, or management consulting.

It is important to note that the course is not about AI alone- rather it is about understanding the dynamics of technological innovation more broadly, with AI providing a specific and contemporary context. Accordingly, the syllabus is divided into topics that cover technology management as well as management of AI.

In terms of material, the course will utilize a combination of classic articles, cases, team projects and workshop presentations. A few of the articles assigned are academic and research oriented in tenor, and the objective is to expose students to the foundational concepts and thinking on the subject. Students are nevertheless expected to understand the main principles of these articles, and how they build on the topics covered in class. Theoretically the course will draw on insights from several disciplines, including strategy, entrepreneurship, organizational theory and finance.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Describe how technologies evolve over time, and the organizational, sociological and economic factors that affect evolution.
- Identify the constraints that arise in mature firms in adapting and reacting to disruptive technologies, and some potential ways to overcome them
- Explain various types of innovation and how they impact firms and strategy
- Frame how emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence and machine learning are reshaping the business world in practical and strategic terms

**Class Participation**

Class contribution is an essential and significant component of evaluation and will make a significant difference to your grade. In order to facilitate a constructive discussion, please go through all the material and cases assigned for a particular session (see the class schedule below). You should be able to offer insights into the central themes being discussed in the session, and outline the main ideas/concepts in each reading and case. Depending upon how the class progresses, I may also assign additional articles from time to time. You are expected to go through these additional readings as well, and come prepared accordingly.

Your class participation will be graded based not just on how often you participate, but on consistent evidence and demonstration of the knowledge of assigned content. If you participate but do not connect the discussions to assigned material, then that will be reflected in your grade.

An attendance sheet will be circulated at the beginning of every class. It is your responsibility to make sure that you record your attendance. You are allowed a maximum of *two absences* (unexcused without documentation). Any additional absences will require clear and supporting documentation (e.g. a doctor’s note) giving reasons for why you should be excused from class. In general, unless there is an emergency (e.g. a well documented health emergency), I would expect your total absences to be minimal. Unexcused absences and excessive excused absences will reduce your class participation grade. For instance, three unexcused absences will reduce your class grade one-half of a full letter (ex. B to a B-), after which two percentage points will be deducted for every additional absence.

Note: Attendance alone will not count toward your class participation grade, and the two will be graded separately.

Student athletes- you will be excused from class only with supporting documentation, and for travel etc. for games. Please arrange practice and other schedules so they do not interfere with class attendance.

**Case Analysis- Report and Presentation (Group)**

The case analysis report and presentation accounts for 12.5% of the final grade. You will be responsible for preparing a written report and making a formal presentation of your analysis and recommendations in class.

The enclosed schedule indicates the date each case is to be presented (apart from the Kodak case, which is a general discussion case for the whole class). Broadly speaking, your presentation will cover the background of the case, the issues identified (see the assignment questions for the cases below), recommendations for the future, and any other points that you think are relevant.

For this assignment you will be required to prepare a report that is no longer than 12 pages, excluding the cover page (please see the guidelines for written reports below). In addition, you will make a presentation in class for a maximum of 20 minutes (guidelines are also provided below for presentations). Note that the report is due the day the case is scheduled to be presented. Please also email me a copy of the slides you intend to use ahead of time. Late submissions will not be accepted.

The cases discussed in class are designed to illustrate specific concepts and decision scenarios. Please therefore do not look up any online material or ‘solutions’ for the case, including any LLMS. Any attempts to do so will be treated as a serious ethical violation, and in the minimum equivalent to a first offense (see below section on Academic Integrity).

**Brief Report (Group)**

In this report (maximum of 3 pages, excluding the cover page), you are required to identify the central issues in the case, develop a brief analysis, and provide your main recommendations. The brief report is due the day the case is scheduled to be presented, and should be submitted to me ahead of class. Late submissions will not be accepted, and failure to turn in a report on the due date will lead to a lower grade.

**Workshop Assignment (Group)**

The workshop assignment requires you to develop an insightful presentation on an assigned topic (e.g. why are some company cultures good for innovation; what are some current practices to counter bias in AI systems). You will be given brief guidelines ahead of the assignment, and your task is to develop a presentation of about 20 mins as a group. The purpose is to not only get you thinking about the topic, but also to inform the rest of the class and enhance their understanding of the subject. It is essential that you develop an in depth analysis and come well prepared to present on the day that the workshop assignment is scheduled, since you will be playing the lead role in these discussions.

Note- For the workshop, you may use LLMs to provide a basic framework/ starting point; However be prepared to identify and discuss what insights you added, what are your unique thoughts, how you expanded on the LLM output and tied it to class discussions etc. A simple ‘cut and paste’ will not be acceptable and you need to be ready to defend yourself.

**Innovation Project-Mid term**

This project carries a weight of 15%. The purpose of this project is threefold. To understand (1) how an emerging technology impacting today’s business landscape is taking shape, (2) what strategies firms in this space are adopting to compete and gain market share, and (3) how incumbent firms and technologies are responding to the threat and fighting back.

Further details on the assignment are provided below. This project has the following deliverables: (1) a choice of technology to be made latest by September 16th , (2) a mid term presentation covering the issues outlined (October 17th , October 21st ), (3) an interim report due on October 21st

Note: All work related to the Innovation Project is expected to be original work undertaken in this class. Repurposing previous projects or material from another class constitutes a serious ethical violation.

**Final Project- End of term**

This project carries 10% of the weight. Further guidelines related to this project will be provided over the course of the semester.

**Quiz (2)**

Two quizzes worth 32.5% will be held on the dates indicated in the course schedule. The quizzes will cover the material discussed in class and the readings, including the cases. The emphasis will be on testing your understanding of the material, rather than on how well you remember various concepts.

Please note the dates of the quizzes and ensure you are in class on those dates. Makeups will not be provided, barring very exceptional circumstances such as health related reasons (supporting documentation will be needed).

1. Give a brief history and overview of your technology, and how it has evolved up to this point in time. When did your technology first emerge?
