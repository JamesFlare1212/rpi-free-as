## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course offers an exciting introduction to the mindset of inventors, innovators, and entrepreneurs. It is designed for students who are curious about how new ideas are born, how they evolve, and how they become successful products, services, or ventures. Whether you are interested in launching your own startup, developing new solutions inside an organization, or simply thinking more creatively, this course will help you develop the tools and perspective to do so. Throughout the semester, we will explore the full journey of innovation and entrepreneurship, from identifying opportunities and generating ideas to evaluating, refining, and presenting them. Along the way, you will engage with real-world challenges, learn from entrepreneurs who have walked the path before, and participate in hands-on exercises and team projects that bring concepts to life.

We will tackle questions such as: Where do good ideas come from? What role does creativity play in entrepreneurship? How do you build a team around an idea? And how do you move from concept to execution in a world that’s constantly changing?

Fundamentally, this course is about learning to see the world differently, and acting on what you see with imagination, courage, and purpose.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course is designed to help students build both the mindset and the skill set necessary for invention, innovation, and entrepreneurship. By the end of the course, students will be able to:

1\) Understand the key competencies and capabilities essential to innovating successfully and launching new ventures.  
2\) Practice identifying, developing, and evaluating ideas for new products, services, or business models, and assess whether they are viable and worth pursuing.  
3\) Explore the cognitive and emotional foundations that influence entrepreneurial thinking and behavior.  
4\) Develop an understanding of creativity and structured problem-solving in business and management contexts.  
5\) Analyze the characteristics and dynamics of high-performing entrepreneurial teams.  
6\) Gain insight into how new ideas and discoveries evolve into viable opportunities, and how they are ultimately brought to market.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- By the end of this course, students will have developed a deep understanding of the mindset and methods that drive successful invention, innovation, and entrepreneurship. They will be able to apply these concepts to real-world challenges across business and society.
- For MGMT-4510 (Undergraduate Level): Students who complete this course will be able to: • Generate innovative and entrepreneurial ideas and develop them into viable opportunities with potential for business or societal impact. • Explain and analyze key concepts in entrepreneurship, and illustrate them with real-world examples. • Assess entrepreneurial talent, team dynamics, and the factors that influence team performance. • Identify both theoretical and practical challenges commonly faced by innovators and entrepreneurs.
- For MGMT-6640 (Graduate Level): In addition to the outcomes listed above, graduate-level students will also be expected to: • Articulate with clarity and depth how the concepts taught in class inform their ability to develop innovative and entrepreneurial ideas. • Explain how these ideas can be converted into opportunities that contribute meaningfully to business and society.

**Profile of an Innovator/Entrepreneur (PIE) Presentation (10%)** **(Individuals or in pairs)**

In this assignment, you or your pair will choose an innovator, inventor, or entrepreneur and explore the story behind their creativity, motivations, and path to success. If possible, we encourage you to conduct a direct interview with your chosen individual. Otherwise, your analysis should be based on high-quality published sources. Your task is to distill what makes this person innovative or entrepreneurial and connect those insights to concepts, frameworks, and discussions from class. A formal PowerPoint presentation is required and must be submitted to the instructor (tranm2@rpi.edu) at least **two hours** before the start of class on the day you present.

Each group will deliver a 10-minute presentation, followed by a 5-minute Q&A and class discussion. Your presentation should conclude with one or two thoughtful questions or insights designed to spark discussion among your peers. The audience will also have the opportunity to ask your team questions following the presentation.

Presentations will be scheduled throughout the semester (see the course schedule at the end of the syllabus for details), with up to **two teams** presenting during each session. A full assignment brief and grading rubric are available on the course LMS.

**Homework Assignments (10%)**

There will be **FOUR homework assignments** throughout the semester, each tied to a specific case study. For each assignment, you will respond to a set of questions designed to help you analyze and engage more deeply with the case material. Each homework assignment will be worth 2.5% of your final grade, for a total of 10% across the semester (4 assignments × 2.5% each = 10%).

Your completed homework must be submitted **on Blackboard before the start of the class** in which the corresponding case is discussed. Late submissions will not be accepted, as these assignments are meant to prepare you for in-class discussion.

Assignment deadlines are listed in the course schedule at the end of this syllabus.

**Quizzes (15%):**

There will be **three in-class quizzes** distributed throughout the semester. Each quiz will be **15 minutes long** and worth **5% of your final grade**, for a total of **15%** (3 quizzes × 5% each). Quizzes will assess your understanding of key concepts from lectures, textbook chapters, and assigned readings. They are designed to reinforce learning and encourage consistent engagement with the course material.

If you miss a class when a quiz is administered, you will receive a **zero** for that quiz. Make-up quizzes will **NOT** be offered except in cases of **documented emergencies** with prior notice.

The quiz dates are listed in the Course Schedule at the end of the syllabus.

**Invention/Innovation Concept and Business Model Assignment (Team Final Project – 20%):**

This team-based final project challenges you to apply the full range of concepts covered in the course by developing and presenting an original invention, innovation, or new business idea. Teams will consist of **2** to **3** students and are responsible for submitting both a **written report** and delivering a final **presentation** during our Demo/Pitch Days in the **last two** class sessions (**Monday, December 8** and **Thursday, December 11**).

The goal of this assignment is to generate a creative and feasible concept that addresses a real-world problem and presents a clear value proposition. Your project should describe the problem being solved and the proposed solution, explain how the concept works and why it is needed, and provide a compelling case for how the idea could generate revenue or be sustained over time. You should outline where and how the product or service will be sold, identify your intended market, and propose a realistic timeline for launch. In addition, your report should describe how you plan to market the idea and acquire customers.

A key part of the assignment is reflecting on your team’s idea generation process, including how course material on creativity, opportunity recognition, and innovation strategy influenced your thinking. You will also describe your customer discovery process, detailing how you identified and interviewed **at least 12 potential customers**, what insights you gained from those interviews, and whether your team made any pivots based on the feedback received.

Final grades will be based on the originality, value, and feasibility of your idea, as well as the clarity and depth with which you communicate how your customer discovery process informed your concept development. During your scheduled presentation session, your team will have 10 minutes to pitch your idea and 10 minutes to respond to questions from your classmates and instructor, simulating an investor-style pitch environment.

A detailed assignment guideline and grading rubric are available on the course LMS.
