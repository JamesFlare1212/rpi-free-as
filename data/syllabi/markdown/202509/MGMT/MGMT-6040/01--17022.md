## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The increased professionalization of the workforce and high value placed on technology and knowledge work in the global economy emphasize the importance of good workforce management skills for managers and professionals. To be effective, we need to act as "applied" behavioral scientists, that is, we must carefully study our organizations, units, and direct reports and develop an ability to recognize what makes each one tick. The techniques in behavioral science, no less than the techniques in marketing, finance, and accounting, are essential for managers and professionals to understand and apply. In this course more than most others, we need to know not only the conceptual material presented but also how it applies to our work lives and ourselves. People who understand the work environment, know themselves, and can assess their effects on others are more likely to be effective than are those who are blind to these dimensions.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Framework for the Course**

This course will move from the "micro" side of O.B. to the "macro." We will examine increasingly complex levels of social behavior, moving from the individual to the interpersonal to the group and finally to the organizational level.

**The Classroom as a Learning Laboratory**

We will bring a practice-oriented approach to the material in this course. In addition to analyzing behavior in cases and texts, we will use the classroom situation itself as a source of data to examine organizational behavior. Consequently, we will use class time for simulations, role-playing, and group and intergroup interaction. Considerable work will be done in your learning groups, which will be used for several class sessions and out-of-class work. You should be able to develop your analytic skills in this context, as well as gain insights into your own interpersonal styles. Also, if your group is effective, you will be able to create an open, low-risk, non-threatening environment where you can feel free to explore your behavioral tendencies and gain valuable feedback from your colleagues.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Exam 1 (20%) & Exam 2 (20%)**

Exams will be in-person and consist of an applied case analysis, in which you will be given one short case and answer a series of related prompts. This will be done electronically, with the final output being a Word document uploaded to Blackboard. Use of notes and AI tools are prohibited during the exams. Exam 2 is not cumulative.

**Group Activities (30%)**

Every class with a lecture on and between September 2 and November 18 will contain an activity that requires you to apply course concepts to solve a problem, analyze a situation, and/or engage in discussion/debate. These activities are meant to apply concepts taught during the lecture portion of the class to streamline the learning process and stimulate engagement. Activity performance will be assessed at the group level based on a set of measures which I provide on Blackboard. Out of the 20 total activities that will occur, only 15 activities with the highest grade will be counted. If you do not attend class, you will not receive credit for your group’s activity performance.

**Group Presentations 1 (5%) & 2 (5%)**

Your group (same as your activity group) will be given a complex organizational issue that must be critically analyzed and solved using class concepts. Groups will each have 5 minutes to give their presentations. The point of capping at 5 minutes is to push you to convey your work as simply and precisely as possible (see grading measures on Blackboard).

**Special Discussions (20%)**

Special discussion topics will be randomly assigned to individual students, who will then be responsible for leading a discussion on the day that topic is assigned. The rest of the class will be responsible for contributing to the discussion. Discussion leaders will be responsible for preparing novel content, as well as integrate previous class content. Since multiple students will lead each topic, collaboration is allowed but will not factor into the grading scheme.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
