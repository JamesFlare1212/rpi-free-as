## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Marketing is a dynamic and interdisciplinary field that blends analytical rigor with creativity to shape consumer behavior and drive business success. This course introduces students to the fundamental principles of marketing while emphasizing their practical applications through case studies, team projects, and real-world examples. Students will learn how to critically analyze marketing problems, develop strategies, and design a comprehensive marketing plan.

Through case discussions, interactive assignments, and a major group project, the course emphasizes key skills such as teamwork, communication, and critical thinking. Students will also explore the broader role of marketing in addressing global challenges, cultural diversity, and sustainability, while reflecting on the ethical and societal implications of marketing decisions. By the end of the course, students will be equipped not only with foundational marketing knowledge but also with the professional skills necessary to evaluate and present marketing strategies in diverse contexts

**More Details About the Course** This course provides a comprehensive introduction to marketing by balancing foundational principles with an exploration of marketing’s impact on all stakeholders—customers, firms, policymakers, society, and the environment. Students will develop a strong grasp of core marketing concepts such as the 4Ps (Product, Price, Place, Promotion), segmentation-targeting-positioning (STP), and market opportunity analysis, while also learning to apply strategic frameworks including SWOT, Porter’s Five Forces, the BCG Matrix, and perceptual mapping.

Consumer behavior topics such as Maslow’s hierarchy of needs, high- vs. low-involvement decisions, and brand loyalty will be paired with insights from consumer psychology and customer relationship management (CRM). Students will also engage with marketing mix decisions—from branding strategies and packaging to pricing approaches, channel design, and integrated promotional strategies guided by the AIDA model.

By integrating analytical tools with ethical, cultural, and environmental perspectives, this course equips students to approach marketing as both a strategic discipline for business success and a force that shapes society. Students will leave the course with the ability to critically analyze marketing practices, design effective strategies, and apply innovative solutions to real-world challenges

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate functional knowledge of marketing concepts, principles, and practices, including the 4Ps (Product, Price, Place, Promotion), segmentation-targeting-positioning (STP), consumer behavior, branding, and customer relationship management (CRM).
- Analyze and evaluate the marketing environment using strategic frameworks such as SWOT, Porter’s Five Forces, and perceptual mapping.
- Develop and present practical marketing solutions through case analyses and marketing plan projects, demonstrating clear communication in both written and oral forms.
- Apply marketing mix decisions—product, pricing, distribution, and integrated promotion—by using appropriate models and analytical tools to support decision-making.
- Critically assess consumer behavior, including decision-making processes, Maslow’s hierarchy of needs, and the role of perception, involvement, and brand loyalty.
- Deliver professional-quality oral presentations supported by structured arguments, data, and clear visualizations.
- Demonstrate an understanding of the marketing research process and the role of data in guiding marketing strategies.
- Evaluate the ethical, societal, and environmental implications of marketing decisions, including issues of diversity, sustainability, and social responsibility.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1. **Exams (40% total):** There will be two non-cumulative exams, each worth 20%. Exams will assess both conceptual understanding and application of theories and principles covered in class through a combination of objective and analytical questions. Make-up exams will only be considered in exceptional circumstances, with appropriate written documentation of a university-approved conflict.
2. **Case Presentations (15% total):** Teams will present assigned Harvard Business School cases, analyzing the focal company’s situation using tools such as SWOT and marketing frameworks covered in class. Presentations should summarize the case, propose recommendations, and stimulate discussion among classmates. Evaluation will be based on quality of analysis, clarity, presentation skills, and effective time management.
3. **Marketing Plan (35% total):** In groups, students will develop and present a comprehensive marketing plan for a chosen idea or product. The project consists of two components: i) Marketing Plan Presentation (25%) – A 40-minute presentation simulating a professional pitch to potential investors (e.g., “Shark Tank” style). ii) Marketing Plan Report (10%) – A written submission documenting the analysis and plan in a professional format.
4. **Individual Assignment (5% total):** A short written assignment will allow students to apply course concepts individually.
5. **Attendance (5% total):** Attendance is mandatory. All students are expected to arrive on time and remain for the full class session. Missing more than two class sessions without an approved excuse will result in the loss of attendance points. Excused absences (e.g., official university activities, medical reasons with documentation) will not count toward the limit. Students are responsible for catching up on any material or assignments missed due to absence. Students must be on time for the class. Anyone late by more than 10 minutes shall be marked absent. If the instructor is late by more than 10 minutes, everyone will be marked present.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
