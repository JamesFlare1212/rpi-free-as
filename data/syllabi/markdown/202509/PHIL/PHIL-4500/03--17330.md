## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Cultivate a critical framework for identifying and analyzing bioethical issues evident in medical, scientific, environmental, and corporate contexts.
- Account for the situational contexts—historic, cultural, social, and otherwise— that inform and influence ethical decision making.
- Recognize and evaluate competing approaches and interpretations of bioethical issues through case studies.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

*Journaling…* I’m envisioning journal writing as an essential part of what we do. As a communication intensive course, I’d like us to think of journaling as a way to begin cultivating our thoughts in their most generative, creative, and nascent forms. We’ll take on different journaling “challenges” throughout the semester and use various prompts to guide our efforts. Your entries will always be confidential, so feel free to take risks. I’m thinking of this as a space free from critique where you can articulate your fears and motivations, think about what you bring to the group, what you need from it, generate affirmations, ask questions, and begin to form ideas without being filtered. You won’t be asked to share your journals but, periodically, we’ll take time to reflect on this exercise and share our take aways from it.

*Performances…* In the improvisational spirit, we’re going to be doing a lot of unscripted low-threshold performances related to the materials we study. Like the journals, I’d like for us to think of this as an experimental, even playful, space where we have the opportunity to be creative and to test different techniques or ideas. We’ll also be doing a series of various individual presentations/monologues/stand-up sets. I’m thinking 3-5 minutes. 2-3 times in the semester. No notes or reading. I’d like for us to not only get comfortable speaking in front of a crowd but to enjoy it as well.

*Case Studies…* Bioethics committees are often charged with evaluating the ethical dimensions and implications of a specific case study. We’ll be doing this on different scales throughout the semester, including 2 lessor and 1 more extensive report.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

*Assessment methods*: There is no pre-set grading rubric for this course. We’ll partake in a number of iterative endeavors throughout the semester and rely on “good faith” contributions as the criteria for formative evaluations. You’ll receive feedback from myself and your peers, the goal of which will be to help cultivate an end-of-semester portfolio that represents the work you’ve performed for the course. We’ll also be involved in an ongoing dialogue of how best to (and to what extent we should) use grading in our evaluative efforts.

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

*Academic Integrity*: The foremost goal of this course is to cultivate your intellectual growth, which I think is best achieved by engaging in a form of intellectual play. This is where taking risks, making mistakes, and venturing failure can reap significant benefits for learning. We *invite, not punish*, these efforts. Plagiarism and other forms of academic dishonesty undermine them. Please avoid them.
