## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**DOCUMENTARY in the 21st CENTURY** is a production course, investigating documentary history leading to a focus on digital media representations today. This course will incorporate critical thinking with production. With focus on aesthetic and formal considerations, students will be asked to produce a series of multimedia projects investigating their vision of themselves in the world.

Taking a broad look at what defines "documentary" media, we will explore questions including the construction of voice and ideology, personal responsibility, truth versus fiction, the efficacy of video intervention, the ethics of representation, the authority of mass media, and new forms of community.

Alongside media production, students will view and theorize key historic documentary works including ethnographic films, cinema verité, propaganda films, reality TV, corporate news, auto-biographic and activist videos, experimental film, video, doc-media, web-/i-doc and social media. Students will concentrate on identifying paradigms and cinematic structures, and their evolution on the explosion of documentary representations in today’s digital sphere. Students will examine theory and practice including:

- POV
- cinematic coverage of action and composition
- the art of production
- the art of editing
- media management
- new modes of doc-media in the 21st Century

This knowledge will then be transferred to students’ own personal style and production, using cell phones and consumer media technology as well as optional access to professional production gear. Students will receive feedback from the professor as well as their class colleagues throughout the semester, with assignments structured to critically analyze and shape their own individualized representation.

The course’s main emphasis will be on a collective series of production assignments, incorporating a series of cumulative short production projects, including individual and collaborative work. Class time will be divided between critiques of student projects and screenings of a wide range of documentary forms, along with writing in journals, discussions of readings and oral articulation of different approaches to doc media and representation in the 21st Century.

With recognition that the media landscape this semester is driven by the Presidential Election, a participatory class research and discussion component will be incorporated into our course, to examine *media* representation in shaping the *political* narrative.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This is an important course for students to develop the critical skills necessary to disseminate information in the digital realm as well as creatively produce information in the complex mediated landscape of the 21st Century. Students will learn vital media and technology literacy skills, as well expand their critical thinking and independent voice.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The course’s main emphasis will be on a series of short production projects, including individual and collaborative work, culminating in a collective web-/i-doc project. Class time will be divided between critiques of student projects and screenings of a wide range of documentary forms, along with writing and discussions of readings, and written and oral articulation of different approaches to the documentary production and representation in the 21st Century.

There will be a mix of assignments which are individual and collective. In the case that students work in a team, each student takes on a specific role with clear responsibilities and is individually assessed for their work in the collective project.

**Communications Intensive**

All assignments are designed to fulfill Communication Intensive expectations. A Communication Intensive (CI) assignment is one that:

- *communicates to a specific audience.*
- *uses, practices, employs, or applies course content to address real or realistic problems through effective communication.*
- *requires students to write clearly and succinctly.*

Assignments are iterative: students will receive instructor feedback multiple times prior to submitting the final work. The final collective production project will be developed throughout the course, structured by Idea Development & Pre-Production, Production, Post-Production and Distribution phases. The course will build in CI skills, culminating in critiques during First Cut, Fine Cut, and Premiere presentations.

Our CI assignments will far exceed the core CI expectations, through writing in your viewing log, oral presentations and critiques, and producing individual and collective production assignments. The following is CI criteria for Rensselaer:

*CI assignments must add up, at minimum, to the equivalent of 4000 words, regardless of medium. Oral presentations may be included in the 4000 words at a rate of 130 words per minute. Thus, a 5-minute presentation could count as 650 words toward the total. Notetaking, daily logs, journals, or similar exercises (often called Writing to Learn) can count up to 20% of the total minimum word count, or 800 words.*
