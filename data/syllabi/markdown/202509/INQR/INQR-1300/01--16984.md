## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. analyze and attain knowledge of issues of race in relation to U.S history as represented in US films and in relation to the creation and history of U.S. films.
- 2\. write clearly and succinctly
- 3\. prepare and communicate effectively in class discussions and in presentations.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**1.) Attendance is required.**

Three unexcused absences and you fail. (Of course, allowances are made if there is grave illness or something of this nature.) Please notify Professor and TA of excused absences via email.

Attendance must be logged in for each class via the app supervised by TA Arma Yari.

If you forget to log in for a class, it is your responsibility to contact Arma to notify her that you were in attendance.

**2)** **Contribution to class discussions based on required readings, assignments, and lectures/presentations.** **(10%)**

Discussions will be founded on the lectures/presentations, required readings, assignments, and responses to films screened in class. Readings with an "*" indicate that there is a thesis or key ideas presented in this text (as opposed to an emphasis on factual knowledge, definitions, dates, etc.), which you will be expected to learn and address in your assignments and discussions. Readings will be available online or at Rensselaer's Learning Management System (LMS) site: https://lms.rpi.edu.

When possible, online links to films screened in the course will be included in syllabus.

**Class participation, notes:** It is expected that students will participate in class and take notes during class, and your attention to this matter may affect your class participation grade. If you have a learning issue where note-taking is a distraction, or, for some reason taking notes is a hindrance to your learning, please contact Professor Staniszewski about this.

**Laptops/Electronic Devices:** The use of laptops, phones, and electronic devices for non-class related activity will result in a lower final grade for the course.

**Summary of class participation guidelines**: If you do not contribute to class discuss or go online regularly using electronic devices for non-class activity, or never take notes: this is an D or F grade for the class participation portion of your grade; very occasional participation or some non-class online engagement with electronic devices or very occasional note taking is a C-, C, or C+; some participation with contributions to discussion, with some notes, and no online mis-use, is a B-, B, or B+; and full participation with regular contributions to discussions and notes, and no on-line mis-use, an A-, A, or A+.

**3) Quiz:**

**A quiz dealing with introductory concepts: 10%**

**4) Student film presentations and essays (15% each).** Students will give two 5-minute presentations dealing with a film, or a film or race topic (one before mid-term, one at the end of the semester). For each presentation, a summary-essay discussing the thesis and key ideas of the presentation is also due. The presentations and summaries are an opportunity to integrate students’ particular race and film interests into the course.

The summary essays should be a minimum of 2 double-spaced pages, with additional pages of supplementary Chicago Manual of Style “notes-and-bibliography-style” endnotes (not footnotes) and bibliography. You will be graded on your endnote and bibliography formatting.

**5) Introductory course-topics essay (20%) and a comprehensive-course essay (30%):**

There will be a midterm and a final essay based on the topics, films, and required readings of the course. The final essay will be cumulative.

The course topics essays should be a minimum of 5 double-spaced pages for the first essay and 7 double-spaced pages for the second, with additional pages of supplementary Chicago Manual of Style “notes-and-bibliography-style” endnotes (not footnotes) and bibliography.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grading/Evaluation of presentations and essays:  
A: Excellent presentations and essays, distinguished by originality of topic, and depth of research. Essays have good grammar, are spell-checked, and formatting requirements are met.  
B: Good presentations and essays, with solid research. Essays have good grammar, are spell-checked, and formatting requirements met.  
C: Minimal work on presentations and essays. Essays have satisfactory grammar, may have some issues with bibliography, citations, spelling, and formatting requirements.  
D: Poor quality work on presentations and essays with the latter having incorrect content, insufficient length, sloppy bibliographies and citations, and incorrect formatting, spelling, and grammar.  
F: Extremely inadequate work on presentations and essays, or incomplete assignments. Essays with incorrect content, insufficient length, incorrect bibliographies, no citations when necessary, incorrect formatting, spelling, and grammar.

Students are not permitted to re-do or re-submit papers or assessments. However, if improvements are made during the semester, there will be a strong emphasis on this improved work when determining the final grade.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**ACADEMIC INTEGRITY**

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy may result in a zero for the assignment and if this occurs again the student will receive and F for the course. **[i]**

See the Student Handbook for types of academic dishonesty, such as plagiarism, and for more information. Plagiarism, for example, is quoting or very closely paraphrasing a text without citing the source. All quotes, citations of data, and close paraphrases should be footnoted with a citation to the original source. If you are uncertain about how to properly source material in your assignments for this course, check with the Professor or TA about the proper procedures. Guidelines for proper sources will be reviewed in class and are listed at the end of the syllabus.
