## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course mobilizes key principles from the fields of rhetoric, communication, organizational  
psychology, and the arts to provide hands-on experience in creative leadership. Course learning is  
centered around experiential projects that provide opportunities for students to build skills in strategic  
professional communication and creative project design. The course will also address common obstacles  
to leadership and creative risk-taking, such as time management, difficult collaborations, burnout, and  
perfectionism. Students will leave this course with the foundational knowledge and skills to successfully  
communicate, lead, and innovate in their chosen fields.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Course texts are primarily in the form of “public scholarship” (academic research written for  
industry professionals), such as business and leadership articles from esteemed  
industry publications or university publications that cater to nonacademic audiences. Course  
“texts” also come in the forms of Ted/TedX videos and expert podcasts. All resources are  
available on LMS.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will analyze and synthesize key concepts in the fields of leadership and creativity studies.
- Students will analyze and apply rhetorical strategies to improve oral and written communications
- Students will demonstrate effective presentation skills, including the use of visual aids and participatory learning techniques.
- Students will apply course learning to intellectual and interpersonal challenges outside the classroom and articulate insights gained from these experiences.
- Students will demonstrate diverse leadership skills in simulated and real-world contexts.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Attendance & Participation: In line with the course focus on collaborative leadership, all  
students are expected to be avid participants, which includes productively managing challenges.  
Participation will be evaluated at mid-term and at the end of the course. See the attendance policy  
and the expectations and etiquette section of this syllabus for specific grading criteria.  
Quizzes: Each week, there will be a brief accountability quiz on the articles, videos, and/or  
podcasts assigned.  
Take-Action Reports (5): Each module presents a hands-on activity in which you will apply the  
knowledge and skills we are learning in the areas of communication, leadership, and/or creativity  
outside the classroom. You will report on your experiences through a take-action report and inclass  
sharing.  
Informational Presentation: Growing leadership skills entails becoming well practiced in  
informing an audience while engaging their attention. Once during the semester, you will present  
on a remarkable individual leader or collaborative leadership project with an aim to both inform  
and engage.  
Creative Leadership Project. This is a multi-stage project which student teams will plan and lead  
a seminar to reinforce a key element of course learning. In this core project, you will practice team  
building, critical and creative thinking, strategic listening and empathy mapping, data collection  
and analysis, brainstorming and ideation, and ultimately lead the group toward greater  
understanding and application of course material. Finally, you will reflect on and evaluate your  
performance and growth.  
Vision Statement & Inspirational Presentation. The culminating activity centers around a vision  
statement that articulates your professional story, leadership strengths, unique creative gifts, and goals as  
a leader. Composing the statement will be an iterative process with prewriting, drafting, and revising. You  
will present (not read) an adaptation of your leadership vision with an aim to inspire.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

This is a small seminar class, the success of which is entirely dependent on active student participation.  
Therefore, I expect all students to participate to the extent of their abilities. Everyone has one absence to  
use for illness, and one “freebie” absence to use for sleep, self-care, or other obligations, such as  
interviews or family events. Otherwise, I expect that you will come to class every class (athletes, see me  
at the beginning of the term). Each absence beyond two will drop your grade by ten points (1%). If you  
miss more than five classes, you will receive no credit for the course even if you are otherwise passing.  
Note that three tardies of half hour or more accrue to one absence.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
