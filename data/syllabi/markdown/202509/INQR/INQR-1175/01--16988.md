## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Welcome to *Well-being: Cultivating Curiosity!*

From the course catalog: How do we maintain a sense of well-being in our lives? Each person’s path to well-being in body and mind is unique—arising from an awareness of our needs, goals and what we find fulfilling. We will use the theme of curiosity to explore what makes us tick, what makes us feel balanced, stressed, or calm. This interdisciplinary course uses practice-based learning, in-class writing, lectures, creative play, and reading. The class is Communication Intensive.

Additional info: *Attendance and curious participation* in class are required. Your attendance and engagement is crucial because much of the class is centered on experiential learning right in the classroom. Experiential learning can be a rather slow process. It can be frustrating, because often we can *mentally* understand something quickly, but the body and mind may need time to embody this knowledge. We acknowledge that each person in class arrives with different backgrounds, skill-sets, curiosities, and bodies/brains/minds. Please be patient with yourself, your classmates, and your professor.

Our class is geared toward heightening your awareness of experiences and to develop your writing about these experiences, building your own well-being toolbox, as well as nourishing intellectual insights about the material. You will have time during each class meeting to encounter a wide variety of experiences in order to generate ideas and to write on the spot. This process of generating text is expected to be exploratory and even messy at times. Know that generative writing stages can provide new insights and you will have opportunities to re-write, edit and compose as necessary. As a Communication Intensive course, students will receive detailed feedback on at least one draft before the final submission of papers. Expect to write often in class and for graded assignments.

We will be reading one novel this semester and several other chapters. Although they will be on different topics, they are similar in that we will be considering different lived experiences and relating these topics to well-being from different viewpoints. Thinking about our own identities in relation to the world around us as well as the authors and characters in our readings is a key component of our class.

In addition to daily writing in class, students should expect to regularly work in pairs or small groups and engage fully in class (discussions, dialogues, in-class generative and creative projects, etc). Please expect that you will be asked to share your thoughts, questions, and work each class period. Expect to contribute to our regular conversations about the material covered in our class as well as the readings.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\) Students will be able to communicate their ideas clearly, effectively, and creatively.
- 2\) Students will engage curiosity and consider well-being through different forms of expression.
- 3\) Students will be able to demonstrate their understanding of stress and well-being from their own and different perspectives.
- 4\) Students will review and compare a range of well-being practices and technologies.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Attendance grading:** Attendance will be taken each day to determine your attendance grade (percentage of classes attended). I will not count two absences; I understand that health and other emergencies arise.

**Participation grading:** Participation grades will be determined by the percentage of assigned reading observations, reflections, and other activities during class-time that are completed. Work must be turned in on time and will be graded complete/incomplete. Papers and presentations will be graded 0-100 points. All assignments are to be completed **by the date noted** and turned into LMS (papers) or presented in class (presentations). Late written assignments will only be accepted up to the next class period that we meet (with a 3 point deduction for each day the assignment is late). ## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

***Attendance*** ***policy*** Attendance is expected and will be taken. Any unexcused absences will be reflected by a lower attendance grade. Late attendance to class (more than 5 minutes) is disruptive to our community and you are likely to miss the important “sparks” we do at the beginning of most classes (every 2 days late = 1 absence). Certain situations do arise that preclude coming to class that are understandable, such as when you are feeling sick; I will not count two such instances against your attendance grade. If you are missing class because of team obligations, please provide the memo from your coach. If you need to obtain an official excuse for an extended absence of five days or more, please contact the Office of Student Success success@rpi.edu. Important: you must attend all project presentation days, even if you are not presenting that day; if you are absent for a presentation day, you will also lose 10 points on your own grade for the presentation. Please note: If you are late, not fully participating, or leave early (without prior approval), points will be deducted from your attendance/participation grade.

### Other Course Policies:

#### *Class participation* To facilitate community building and class participation, please be present and engaged. Participation in class includes discussions, in-class and out-of-class non-graded writing, and other expressive projects. You will be expected to participate in each class. *Please: no texting, emailing, cell phone calls, or other mediated communications during class* (or you will lose participation points).

#### *Lateness policy* Late written assignments will be accepted up until our next class period, but for each day an assignment is late, 3 points will be deducted from the grade. Because of how peer and professor feedback is provided for assignments, no assignments will be accepted outside of these guidelines.

#### Policy on Collaboration and Cheating/plagiarism In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must be the student’s own work and completed for this class only (i.e. do not turn in work you have turned in for any other class). In cases where help was received from human or artificial intelligence (applies to graded assignments only), a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in penalties. Penalties for a single offense of cheating on an assignment will result in a zero grade for that material. In instances of such cheating over multiple occasions by the same student, this can result in failure of the course. If you have any questions or require clarification of these policies, please see me before turning in the material.

#### *Incomplete Grades* In the case of an extended excused absence from class, and/or inability to complete coursework by the end of the semester due to circumstances outside your control, an Incomplete may be given. The student needs to contact me to work out the logistics and deadlines for the Incomplete before the last day of class. This is worked out on a case-by-case basis with me.

### Additional Course Specific DEI Information:

**Professor Walf’s I.D.E.A. Statement** In this classroom, we will acknowledge and aim to respect **i**nclusivity, **d**iversity, **e**quity, and **a**wareness. In our classroom, all students are welcome and will be supported regardless of race/ethnicity, gender identities, gender expressions, sexual orientation, socio-economic status, age, disabilities, religion, regional background, neurodiversity, citizenship status, nationality and the many other diverse identities that we each bring here. Tthe diversity that the students bring to this classroom should be viewed as a strength, resource, and benefit.

We will be focusing on the material from a scholarly humanities and social sciences perspective. We must be aware that the social sciences, like other sciences, for example, are not always objective and unbiased and historically has not included representation of diverse individuals in the experiments or the researchers themselves. For example, individual differences between human subjects in experiments were often minimized or considered confounds because of the added complexity they may have contributed to the analysis of the data. However, in this classroom, we will strive to integrate a diverse set of experiences for a more comprehensive understanding of the material in this course, despite the potential for added complexity. An important part of a college education, and HASS Inquiry courses in particular, is learning about the complexity of various issues. We will also expand our understanding by hearing from diverse voices in the field through our readings and discussions of the material. Moreover, students in this class are encouraged to participate during class meetings and all efforts will be made for students to have an equitable opportunity to contribute and succeed in a supportive learning environment. Grading will also be equitable. Final course grades are non-negotiable and will be based upon the same metrics described in the syllabus for each and every student.

Our classroom will represent a diversity of individual beliefs, backgrounds, and experiences. We all must listen and show respect for every other member of this class even if we do not necessarily agree. Please honor the uniqueness of our classroom community and appreciate the opportunity we must learn from one another. If you have any questions or concerns, please discuss with me.
