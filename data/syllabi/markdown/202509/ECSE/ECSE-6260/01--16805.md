## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Special problems of semiconductor operating at high voltage and high current levels. Devices include p-i-n and Schottky diodes, bipolar junction transistors, power MOSFETs and thyristors. Topics include space charge limited current flow, microplasmas, avalanche breakdown, junction termination, high-level injection, emitter crowding, double injection, second breakdown, triggering mechanisms, plasma propagation, switching and recovery characteristics. Introduction to the Insulated-Gate Bipolar Transistor.  
Graduate level elective course.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

We plan to teach students how to analyze and understand high voltage power semiconductor physics and 2 and 3-terminal devices (pin junction and Schottky diodes, power BJT, power MOSFET, power thyristor, IGBT). The ability to perform both hand calculations and 3D TCAD device simulations is stressed. Particular emphasis is placed on first and second order effects of device operation physics and physics-based analytical expressions. We also present the dependence of performance robustness on device structures and development trends. We expect, of note, that students will become familiar with the meaning of common terms and acronyms, and critical issues associated with basic power semiconductor device technologies.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

1. High-Level Injection, Recombination and Generation Lifetimes.
2. Single and Double Injection.
3. Micro and Mesoplasmas, Second Breakdown.
4. Avalanche Junction Breakdown and Junction Termination.
5. PiN Junction and Schottky Power Diodes.
6. Power Bipolar Junction Transistor.
7. Power MOSFET.
8. Power Thyristor.
9. Insulated Gate Bipolar Transistor.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

No Course Learning Outcomes are available for this course.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1. Familiarize with the semiconductor phenomena involved in the high voltage power semiconductor device operation.
2. Learn the physics of operation of basic high voltage power semiconductor devices.
3. Be able to utilize the equations of first- and second- order terminal I-V characteristics to estimate the performance of these basic power devices.
4. Develop an understanding of device models, with the associated parameters, usually used in circuit simulations.
