## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course develops a solid basis for students to model and simulate cyber-physical systems using computer-based object-oriented equation-based modeling languages and tools with the goal of building models with high reusability. The course covers both theoretical and practical issues related to numerical simulation methods for CPS, including continuous time, discontinuous/discrete and timed clocked systems. Aspects of code-generation and real-time simulation for embedded systems are introduced. These foundations allow for the modeling and simulation of embedded systems which will be carried out “virtually” (by simulation) and physically using the Arduino.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Cyber-physical systems (CPS) are engineered systems that are built from, and depend upon, the seamless integration of computation and physical components. Advances in CPS will enable capability, adaptability, scalability, resiliency, safety, security, and usability that will expand the horizons of these critical systems. CPS technologies are transforming the way people interact with engineered systems, just as the Internet has transformed the way people interact with information. New, smart CPS drive innovation and competition in a range of application domains including aeronautics, building design, energy, electrical power grids, healthcare, manufacturing, and transportation, to name a few.

In this course students will develop and master a tool set of theory, methods, computer languages and software tools for modeling and simulating cyber-physical systems. Utilizing these skills, the students will be able to architect, model, design, simulate and analyze dynamic characteristics of cyber-physical systems that are critical for society and have the possibility to transform the way humans interact with engineered systems.

1. Comprehend the fundamental principles of modeling and simulation of continuous, discrete, hybrid and timed-clocked systems that lead to the formulation of cyber-physical system models.
2. Comprehend and apply computer-and-equation based object-oriented languages for modeling of cyber-physical systems using the Modelica language.
3. Comprehend and explain the methods used for symbolic transformation of computer models, efficiency issues in numerical solutions and effect of nonlinearities, higher-and-varying index problems, initialization methods, event handling, and other numerical issues related to mathematical solvers used for simulation and co-simulation.
4. Construct simulation models for cyber-physical systems using the Modelica language in Modelica Environments such as Dymola and OpenModelica.
5. Comprehend and explain the concept of real-time simulation, and hardware-in-the-loop simulation.
6. Comprehend concepts of embedded systems and apply solutions for real-time simulation.

Project Specific Learning Outcome: Successfully Apply learning outcomes 1-6 in cyber-physical systems simulation to one of the projects from a list to be distributed by the instructor or a problem specific to your own research (only with permission/approval of the instructor based on a project proposal).

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Topics:

1. Introduction to M&S for CPS
2. Introduction to source code version control, GIT and Dymola GIT integration.
3. Fundamentals Numerical Algorithms: Numerical Integration for Differential-Algebraic Equations
4. Developing Equation-Based Object-Oriented System Models using Modelica and the Modelica Standard Library
5. Understanding Numerical Problems and Debugging Models
6. Building Models and Simulations for Re-use - Interfaces, Architectures and Templates
7. Hybrid Modelling: Discontinuous, Sample-Data Systems, Discrete Event Simulation and Timed Clocks
8. Deploying and integrating models with other tools.
9. CPS Applications and Standards in Modeling and Simulation: Model Exchange and Co-Simulation, Real-Time Simulation
10. Introduction to system representation for control and analysis of control loops.

Undergraduate Students

For undergraduates (4170):

- For a C or Below: you must apply the knowledge from all the course sessions until the topic on “Model Variants and Data Management”.
- For C+ to B: you must apply the knowledge from at least two course sessions until the topic on “Workflow Automation and Scripting”.
- For B+ to A: you must apply the knowledge of at least one of the sessions until the topic on “Real-Time Simulation Principles and Applications”.

The final grade will be determined by the instructor. It will take into account both the Quantitative and Qualitative Assessment metrics shown above. For example, if you attain a quantitative score of 85, your final grade will be either a B- (if you don’t meet the qualitative criteria for a higher grade) or higher, e.g. A-, depending on how well you meet the qualitative rubric criteria.

In particular, your performance in the course project can merit a change in your grade to a higher level than those stipulated in the Quantitative Criteria, even if your quantitative performance is below the highest achievable letter grade. For example, if your quantitative score in the table above results in a B-, but your project is excellent, that can qualify you (depending on your project submission) for grades B and above. This assessment is made on the basis of your ability to meet the intended learning outcomes, and it is entirely based on the judgment of the instructor.

Graduate Students

For graduates (6170):
