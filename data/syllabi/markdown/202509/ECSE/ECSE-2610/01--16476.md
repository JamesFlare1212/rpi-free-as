## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Design-oriented introduction to computer components and operations. Standard codes, number systems, base conversions, and computer arithmetic. Boolean algebra, minimization and synthesis techniques for combinational and sequential logic. Races, hazards, and asynchronous behavior. Registers, arithmetic logic units, memory structure, buses, and control units. Machine language programming, instruction fetch and execution, input-output devices, interrupts, and microprogram sequencers. Software and hardware tools.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The students who finish this course in a satisfactory manner will be able to demonstrate: 1.) Convert among various digital numeric representations (decimal, binary, hexadecimal, 2’s-complement). 2.) Analyze Boolean expressions and logic circuits to determine outputs from inputs and inputs from outputs. 3.) Understand, categorize, design, and simulate combinatorial and sequential logic circuits. 4.) Design, synthesize, debug, implement, and test combinatorial and sequential logic circuits, using FPGA-based hardware.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Homework:  
• Homework will typically be assigned on a weekly basis.  
• Homework assignments will be posted on Piazza and will be due electronically on Gradescope by 11:59pm on the given due date.  
• The submission website will automatically close at the deadline. Late homework will not be accepted. Deadline extensions may be granted under exceptional circumstances and only with prior notification of the instructor or an official Dean’s excuse.  
• You may discuss/work in groups, but you must write up your solution individually and independently.  
• Copied homework will receive a grade of zero. Repeated violations will lead to failing the course and a report of academic misconduct will be filed with the School of Engineering.  
Studios:  
Studio exercises will be posted on Piazza and will be due on Gradescope.  
• The studio submission will be considered valid only if the student (or team of 2 students) has their work checked off by a graduate Teaching Assistant (TA) or Undergraduate Student Assistant (UGSA) during their respective studio time or any other designated studio check off times (office hours specified for studio check-off).  
• For later studio exercises, you will be allowed to work in teams of 2 students each. You may discuss with other teams, but you must work and submit one report per team.  
• Copied studio submissions will receive a grade of zero. Repeated violations will lead to failing the course and a report of academic misconduct will be filed with the School of Engineering.  
• All students will complete Studios 0, 1, and 2. Students will then have a choice between completing Alpha or Omega Studios.  
• Omega Studios are a design-based alternative to the traditional structure of Alpha Studios which is procedure based.  
• Students that complete Omega Studios have the option to be exempt from specific questions on the final exam that relate to lab-oriented concepts for which they have demonstrated a comprehensive understanding of during their design process.  
• Specific details regarding the difference between Alpha and Omega studios, their implementation in the studio sessions, and the impact of Omega labs on the final exam will be discussed during lecture, and details will be posted on Piazza for students to review.  
Homework and Studio Regrade Requests:  
• To appeal a homework or studio grade, you should apply for a Gradescope regrade request within three days after receiving your graded assignment. In your request you must specify why you believe you deserve higher credit for the solutions you are appealing.  
Midterms and Final Exams:  
• All students are expected to take both midterm exams and the final exam on the scheduled dates located in the table above (Course Assessment Measures section) and in the course calendar on Piazza during the semester.  
• Exam logistics will be discussed before each exam during lecture. There will be a lecture dedicated to final exam review at the end of the semester.  
• All Exams will be scanned after the test session by the student into Gradescope. There will be a period of time given (15-20 minutes) after the exam to allow you enough time to scan and place it there. Keep your exam until the exam is graded just in case.  
Exam Grade Appeal:  
• To appeal an exam grade, you should submit a Gradescope regrade request to the professor within seven days after receiving your graded exam. In your request you must specify why you believe you deserve higher credit for the solutions you are appealing.  
• Important Note: The only circumstance under which an appeal in the assignment or exam grades will be considered is a demonstrable factual error in grading, meaning either that scores were incorrectly totaled, or a correct answer was marked incorrect. Uniform standards for partial credit are applied for the class, so we will not revisit the amount of points awarded for an incorrect or incomplete solution.
