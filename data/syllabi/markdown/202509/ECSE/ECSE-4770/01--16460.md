## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course aims to enable students to learn the fundamentals of digital design, comprehend the design principles of computer architecture, and implement a simple microprocessor. Students will learn digital logic, gates, and combinational and sequential building blocks in hardware description language (HDL), as well as advanced topics in computer architecture.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Sarah L. Harris and David Harris, Digital Design and Computer Architecture. RISC-V Edition, Morgan Kaufmann (2022). (Recommended)

## Course Goals

### Goals:

- Evaluate the design trade-offs of modern microprocessors
- Appreciate the beauty of complex computing systems
- Know the current trends of computing platforms
- Equip with the skillset for innovative designs in the future

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand the fundamentals of digital design
- Comprehend the basic principles of modern computer architecture
- Learn how modern microprocessors work from bottom up
- Apply the design principles in the implementation of a simple microprocessor
- Test and debug the design systematically
- Manage the design complexity of microprocessors

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- There are no exams. Grading is based solely on Lab Preparation, Participation & Performance, and Lab Reports.
- **All labs are individual assignments. You must write your own code and report.** All work is to represent the individual’s independent work and capability, though group discussion on the design hints and debug methods is encouraged. No discussions on code, i.e., HDL, are allowed.
- Discussions on any unusual problems that arose and how they were solved are always welcomed. Reports are graded both on content and presentation (i.e., quality of written content and writing style).
- There are **no homework or exam** in the course. However, there are many activities that may or should be done outside of the lectures and labs, such as writing HDL code, building circuit schematics, simulations, and debugging.
