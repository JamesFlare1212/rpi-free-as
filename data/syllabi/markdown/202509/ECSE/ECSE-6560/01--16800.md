## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Analyze different analog and digital modulation schemes in terms of their performance advantages and disadvantages.
- Construct different source and channel coding schemes used in wireless communication.
- Describe how OFDM and MIMO operate and are used in cellular communication standards.
- Contrast the relative advantages and disadvantages of different multi-access schemes for multi-user wireless systems.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Assessment for ECSE 4560**

1. *Homeworks and MATLAB/LabVIEW Assignments:* Weekly, 30% of the grade

A total of 12 assignments, assigned weekly, alternating between regular homeworks and programming (MATLAB/LabVIEW) assignments. The homeworks will include some qualitative and quantitative questions, and the MATLAB/LabVIEW assignments would require some programing using these tools.

1. *Midterm Exams:* 2 exams, 40% of the grade (20% each exam)

Midterm exams will be conducted during regular class hours. The midterm exams are not comprehensive; Midterm Exam 1 will cover channel modeling, modulation, and source and channel coding. Midterm 2 will cover OFDM, MIMO and multi-user systems. Exams will comprise of a mix of qualitative and quantitative questions – similar to the homeworks but without the MATLAB and/or LabVIEW assignments.

1. *Final Project:* 30% of the grade

Student-defined project proposal or choice from list provided by instructor at the beginning of the semester. Students can build upon the MATLAB or LabVIEW assignments assigned in the homeworks into a more comprehensive project. Can be done individually or in groups of two. Students will be expected to make a presentation on the project at the end of the semester.

**Assessment for ECSE 6560**

1. *Homeworks and MATLAB/LabVIEW Assignments:* Weekly, 30% of the grade

A total of 12 assignments, assigned weekly, alternating between regular homeworks and programming (MATLAB/LabVIEW) assignments. The homeworks will include some qualitative and quantitative questions, and the MATLAB/LabVIEW assignments would require some programing using these tools.

*In the homeworks, ECSE 6560 students will be assigned one or more problems/subproblems that are more advanced than those assigned to ECSE 4650 students, requiring deeper thinking and focusing on applying mathematical tools to analyze performance of communication systems.*

1. *Midterm Exams:* 2 exams, 30% of the grade (15% each exam)

Midterm exams will be conducted during regular class hours. The midterm exams are not comprehensive; Midterm Exam 1 will cover channel modeling, modulation, and source and channel coding. Midterm 2 will cover OFDM, MIMO and multi-user systems. Exams will comprise of a mix of qualitative and quantitative questions – similar to the homeworks but without the MATLAB and/or LabVIEW assignments.

1. *Final Project (includes term paper):* 40% of the grade

Student-defined project proposal, in consultation with the instructor. *Students will assess the limitations of the current state-of-the-art for analyzing emerging communication systems and develop a proposal that can lead to original research.* Can be done individually or in groups of two. Students are expected to report their findings and observations as *a term paper* in addition to making a presentation towards the end of the semester.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and students must make themselves familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed (such as the final project), a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

In general, while discussions with your classmates are allowed (and encouraged!) for homeworks, the work submitted for grading must be entirely the student’s work. A similar policy applies to any MATLAB and/or LabVIEW assignments as well. For exams, no information exchange with anyone (other than the instructor) is allowed.

A grade of zero will be given on the first assignment where a violation is detected, and a referral to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education for undergraduate or graduate students, respectively). If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of “F” for the course and referral to the appropriate Dean.
