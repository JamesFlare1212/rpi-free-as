## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

In the semiconductor industry, the manufacturing process for advanced device nodes requires an ever increasing number of micro patterning processes. Accordingly, microlithography plays the key role in the success of the integrated circuit patterning. In addition, the acceleration of integrated circuit shrinkage is challenging the lithographers to push the limit of optical lithography by precision engineering and innovation of the lithography tools. This course is designed to train students for a career in the semiconductor manufacturing industry, in particular in the lithography sector and numerous upstream and downstream careers such as equipment and mask manufacturing, wafer fab process engineer, image simulations, or circuit design optimization.

This course introduces advanced semiconductor microlithography. Core topics covered in this course are microlithography fundamentals (including geometrical and physical optics), lithographic metrics of imaging performance and capability, photomask (DUV and EUV) requirements, design and performance, photoresist, lithography imaging defect metrology, overlay metrology, computational lithography, and other advanced lithography topics.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

| **Week** | **Topic** |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
| **Week 1** | · Review of semiconductor manufacturing process and metrics: device nodes, Lithographic needs for Front End of Line (FEOL) & Back End of Line (BEOL) process · Optical microlithography: historical evolution of imaging techniques and methods |
| **Week 2** | · Ray optics to understand the projection and illumination system of the imaging tools. Scanner system: NA, pupil, magnification, depth of focus · Resolution limit & scaling of image: Scaling of NA & wavelengths (l) |
| **Week 3** | · Imaging: scanner vs. stepper , Projection & illumination optics · Aerial Image formation: diffraction orders, reconstruction of image by combining orders, two beam vs. three beam imaging |
| **Week 4** | · Components of imaging system: scanner, mask & photoresist · Fourier transform and analysis: mathematical treatment. |
| **Week 5** | · Metrics of imaging capability: focus exposure matrix(FEM), process control, Bossung curve, process window, metrology verification by SEM · Issue of multilayer patterning: Pattern shift & Overlay (OV), Interfield and Intrafield OV, imaging vs. OV performance |
| **Week 6** | · Image defects: source of defects (scanner, mask, or photoresist & processing) · Aberration: impact on imaging performance, metrology, test patterns & imaging results |
| **Week 7** | · Introduction of computational lithography. Large scale simulation (chip level/ fast : Tachyon-Brion SW), small scale (small clip level /slow & rigorous: Hyperlith-PanoramicTech SW) · Special Topic 1: Imaging and imaging simulations |
| **Week 8** | · More on large scale litho simulation using Tachyon-Brion SW. Capabilities and examples · Special Topic 2: Optical proximity correction |
| **Week 9** | · Mask: DUV & EUV mask. Thin film optics: effect on mask design and photoresist process, optical property of materials at different wavelengths, M3D effects · Special Topic 3: Illumination and source optimization |
| **Week 10** | · Resolution Enhancement Techniques (RET) and its usage in the industry: Sub-resolution assist features, OPC (optical proximity correction), Phase shift mask : Alternating phase shifting mask, Attenuated phase shifting mask (DUV & EUV) · Special Topic 4: Optical aberrations and its impact |
| **Week 11** | · Photoresist: evolution of photoresist chemistry per l scaling · Special Topic 5: EUV mask |
| **Week 12** | · Recent trend of microlithography-Most advanced EUV system: High NA EUV scanner · Special Topic 6: Electro magnetics and mask diffraction |
| **Week 13** | · Practice of advanced patterning in the industry and moving trends: litho road map of the industry. · Special Topic 7: Resolution enhancement technique |
| **Week 14** | · Summary & Review. Potential career paths per majors from learning this course · Final Exam |

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Understand and explain the relationship between the role of microlithography and downscaling of the device. 2. Explain the operational principle of imaging tool and performance using knowledge of ray and diffraction optics 3. Explain the lithographic metrics and terminology which are being used in the semiconductor manufacturing industry 4. Understand the capability of computational lithography and perform lithography simulation 5. Explore potential career path in the semiconductor manufacturing industry

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Course assessment will be done through homework, two midterm exams, and a final exam. Homework will mostly consist of quantitative & conceptual problems, but also include hands on computational lithographic simulations using commercial SW. The two mid-terms will be conducted during the class hours. The final exam will be conducted during the official final week period.
