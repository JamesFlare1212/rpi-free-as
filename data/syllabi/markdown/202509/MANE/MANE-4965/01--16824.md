## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Laboratory experiments on supersonic nozzles, solid rocket propellant, and propeller-based propulsion systems. Topics covered will include applications to jet engine, rocket engine, turboprop, and electric propeller systems. Propulsion system theories will be reviewed and compared to the experimental results. Students will have the opportunity of selecting the test parameters in some of the experiments.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. Provide students with opportunities to experimentally test principles relevant to aerospace propulsion systems
2. Train students on experimental techniques and operation of equipment for studying subsonic and supersonic flows and the combustion of rocket propellant
3. To correlate experimental observations to theory previously learned

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Experiment Sets**

1. Subsonic and sonic nozzles and ducted flows
2. Supersonic nozzles and shock waves
3. Preparation of solid rocket propellant
4. Combustion and regression rate of solid rocket propellant
5. Propeller momentum theory – Wind tunnel
6. Propeller efficiency – Wind tunnel

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

The course is divided into six sets of experiments. As this is a hands-on course in laboratory methods, attendance is an essential element of completing the course. You are expected to attend all of your group’s sessions. Failure to inform the instructor of an excused absence 24 hours before the start of class, or adequately provide documentation for an unexpected absence after a missed class, may result in a zero on that lab’s report grade (regardless of what is submitted for the report). Any additional absences will result in consequences solely left to the discretion of the instructor.

### Other Course Policies:

Please read the lab manual (and appendices) before coming to complete each lab session. Students will learn much more from completing the experimental procedures if they have first familiarized themselves with the topics and concepts involved rather than just blindly following the process. Some laboratory pre-lab assignments will focus on the creation of test matrices (i.e. a list of the desired variation in control variables).

Should extra time be needed, see the course instructor. Time may or may not be able to be made available outside of class. Do not count on extra time though. This is solely at the discretion of the instructor.

Each section will be divided into two teams (A and B) of 8 students; the teams will conduct their labs in alternate weeks. For example, the “A” team might meet in the lab weeks 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, and 12. The “B” team would be in the lab weeks 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 and 13. The specific schedule for this term is on the last page of the course syllabus - Please see this, as the actual schedule may vary by section.

**Lab Reports:**

The teams will submit group reports for the experiments through LMS. The report formatting should be neat, professional, and consistent. Each report should contain the following sections: abstract, introduction, setup and procedure, results and discussion, conclusion. Two group members should work on each section, with the exception that two members should both work on the abstract and conclusion together. Please make it clear on the title page of the report which two group members worked on each section. Each student will receive a grade based on the section they worked on (with the abstract and conclusion sharing a grade). Both students working on a given section will receive the same grade. Despite sections being graded separately, the report as a whole is still a team effort. Contradictions between sections may impact the grades of both sections. The two group members writing the abstract and conclusion together are also responsible for the final formatting (and submission on LMS) of the report. They will receive either up to 10% extra credit or a 10% penalty on their report grade based on the formatting of the report. Group members should take turns writing the different sections, and every student should write each of the sections at least once. Further guidelines for writing the reports will be discussed on the first day of class.

Lab reports are due one week after the date the experiments were completed, by the end of the scheduled time of the class. Only one submission needs to be submitted through LMS. It must be formatted as a pdf.

**Pre-labs Assignments & Quizzes:**

Prelab assignments will be assigned for each experiment. These are due on LMS before the beginning of the corresponding lab session and are graded individually. There will be a 5 minute quiz at the start of each lab session, which will be directly related to the prelab and/or the lab manual.

**Final Exam:**

There is no final exam in this course.

**Graduate Level:**

This course is taught at both the undergraduate (MANE 4965) and graduate (MANE 6965) level. Students taking the course on the graduate level will be asked to complete considerably greater work on each prelab assignment. This work will largely be open-ended and will require students to seek out additional sources of information from the literature related to the topics studied. Graduate students may also be asked to propose their own methods for predicting the results of the experiments.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

This statement is intended to cover most, but not all, forms of academic dishonesty. (You should refer to *The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* for detailed information on Institute policy in this regard.)

**Prelabs & Lab Reports:** Collaboration among the students in the class is permitted and, in fact, encouraged. Seeking help from other sources is also encouraged. However, the work you submit for prelab and lab reports should be your own and your group’s, respectively. The data used in your report should be only the data you collected in the lab, unless you are given additional data by your instructor and told to use it as well. Evidence of plagiarism in a prelab will be considered academic dishonesty and may result in a zero grade for all prelabs for the semester; plagiarism in a lab report may result in an F grade in the course for all parties involved and a formal report of academic dishonesty.

**Quizzes:** Collaboration during a quiz (exchanging verbal or written information or copying) is prohibited. In addition, use of email, the internet or cellphones during quizzes for any purpose is strictly prohibited. All parties involved in academic dishonesty in a quiz may receive a zero grade for all quizzes for the semester. If you have any questions concerning this policy, please ask for clarification **before** submitting an assignment.
