## Course Goals

### Goals:

Introduction to heat transfer including conduction, external and internal convection, and radiation. Emphasis is on applications of heat transfer to a wide variety of engineering systems including HVAC, automotive design, materials processing etc.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- On successful completion of this course students will be able to: • to select simplifying assumptions in analyzing a complex thermo-fluids system. • to analyze thermo-fluids systems by applying the conservation of mass and energy. • to analyze heat transfer in thermo-fluid systems by identifying heat transfer modes–convection (forced/natural, internal/external), conduction (transient and steady-state) and radiation using the appropriate equations to evaluate them. • to analyze the performance of heat exchangers and design heat exchangers given a set of specifications.

**Module 1,2,3,and 4:** A grade of 80 and up on each of these four modules will bring your grade up two levels. A grade between 60-79 will bring your grade up one level. A module is considered unsuccessful if its score is less than 60. In this case, no level is earned. Module tests are closed book, but a crib sheet is allowed. The crib sheet must be turned in with the test. **Passing Module 1 is required to pass the class.**

**The grade of each module (1, 2, 3 and 4) is calculated as follows:**

Module grade

**Class Project - The class project must have a passing grade in order to pass the class.** In order to pass the class each student must turn in a class project that addresses all design constraints. Not turning in a project that addresses all design constraints results in automatic course failure.

**Two grade modifiers** (i. e. the grade increases by two levels, for example from C to B-) are applied if the heat sink design is correct and it meets the specification (i.e. it is not overdesigned nor underdesigned) and the approach is entirely correct and the project is completed and turned in by November 6th 2025.

**One grade modifier** (i.e. the grade increases by one level, for example from C to C+) is applied if the original heat sink is incorrect/ does not meet specification, but the team addresses the weakness and returns a correct design that also meets specification (i.e. it is not overdesigned nor underdesigned) by December 2, 2025.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Any cheating that is detected will result in a lowered test and/or course grade and will be reported to the Dean of Students Office.

*Quiz questions and homework problems cannot be posted on chegg. The instructor has a chegg account and will check this regularly. If a quiz/homework is seen on chegg prior to its deadline, the instructor will report the event to the Dean of Students and chegg will be contacted to identify the poster. In the eventuality that somehow homework solutions are found to be copied from chegg, the student will be graded with a zero for that homework and reported to the Dean of Students. Note that chegg “experts” are typically college students or fresh graduates, and usually make mistakes in the solution if the problems are home-made.*

*If the final exam will be posted on chegg prior to the deadline for turning it in, assigning course grades will be delayed for the entire class until the poster is identified. Regardless of whether a solution was posted or not for the exam, the student who posted the final exam will get a zero for the exam.*
