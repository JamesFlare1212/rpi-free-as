## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

*Engineering Acoustics* course provides introductory materials of engineering acoustics for students with basic knowledge in mathematics (at least on level of first-year College). Much of course materials are taken from the textbook **Acoustics for Engineers** by *Xiang & Blauert (**3rd Ed. Springer**)*. The course includes mechanic and acoustic oscillations, the wave equations in fluids, governing equations for horns and ducts, spherical sound sources and arrays, piston membranes, diffraction and scattering, dissipation, reflection, refraction and absorption, insulation of air-borne sounds and isolation of structure-borne vibrations, noise propagation and noise control. ***College level calculus, including complex numbers / functions, partial differential equations, and multi-variables are required. Students taking acoustic minor need to show the required preparation during an interview with the instructor prior to taking the course.***

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Topics covered:** Introduction to engineering Acoustics; The course includes Introduction to basic concepts/definitions of sound and vibration systems, mechanic and acoustic oscillations, the wave equations in fluids, governing equations for horns and ducts, spherical sound sources and arrays, piston membranes, diffraction and scattering, dissipation, reflection, refraction and absorption, insulation of air-borne sounds and isolation of structure-borne vibrations, geometric acoustics and diffuse sound fields, noise propagation and noise control.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Course learning outcomes: The students which finish this course in a satisfactory manner will be able to demonstrate: i) to use wave equations to explain wave nature and wave phenomena of acoustics; ii) An ability to solve acoustics fundamental problems related to engineering acoustics; iii) An ability to use basic concepts of acoustics science (such as wave propagations, dissipation, reflection, refraction, and absorption) to respond to field-specific questions Additional learning outcomes for 6000-level students iv) An ability to explain transducers (such as microphones, speakers, accelerometers) properly to conduct acoustic measurements; v) An ability to use the fundamental knowledge to implement pertinent calculations to demonstrate consolidation of understanding

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

(x) 3.1 an ability to identify, formulate, and solve complex engineering problems by applying principles of engineering, science, and mathematics

(x) 3.2 an ability to apply engineering design to produce solutions that meet specified needs with consideration of public health, safety, and welfare, as well as global, cultural, social, environmental, and economic factors

(x) 3.3 an ability to communicate effectively with a range of audiences

(x) 3.4. an ability to recognize ethical and professional responsibilities in engineering situations and make informed judgments, which must consider the impact of engineering solutions in global, economic, environmental, and societal contexts

(x) 3.5 an ability to function effectively on a team whose members together provide leadership, create a collaborative and inclusive environment, establish goals, plan tasks, and meet objectives

( ) 3.6 an ability to develop and conduct appropriate experimentation, analyze and interpret data, and use engineering judgment to draw conclusions
