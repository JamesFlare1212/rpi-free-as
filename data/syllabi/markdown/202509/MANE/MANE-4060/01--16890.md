## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Undergraduate course. This course is designed to introduce the concepts and methods used in the analysis of primary aircraft/aerospace structure, including principal components of the wing and fuselage. Students will become comfortable with the basic aircraft structural elements loads, and materials. Building from the concepts of structural mechanics and elementary elastic continuum mechanics, students will be able to determine the stresses, strains, and deflections of simple thin-walled monocoque and semi-monocoque aircraft/aerospace structures subjected to torsional, bending, and shear loadings. A link is then made between this analysis and the failure of aerospace structures and materials by yielding, fatigue, fracture, and buckling. At the end of the course, the students are presented with a brief introduction in the mechanics and design of composite materials for aerospace structures.

This aerospace structures and materials course focuses on formulation, analysis and solution of aerospace structural engineering problems. The course facilitates graduate and professional work in advanced aerospace structures and aerospace materials (metallic and composites). Aspects of novel intelligent aerospace structures and structural health monitoring methods will be also addressed anticipating new professional challenges.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Beam structures under combined shear, bending and torsional loads. Semi-monocoque structures: idealizations involving wings, ribs, and fuselage bulkheads. Effects of taper and cutouts in stiffened shell structures, shear deformations and warping, location of elastic axis in open and closed sections, torsion of multicell sections. Stability of beam and membrane elements. Introduction to materials used in aerospace vehicles including metals, ceramics, and composites with special emphasis on fiber-reinforced composite materials. Methods for material analysis and selection for various aerospace components. Introductory concepts on nondestructive evaluation (NDE) and structural health monitoring (SHM). Introductory concepts on smart and multifunctional structures.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Knowledge of aerospace structural analysis.
- Ability to identify, formulate and solve aerospace engineering problems.
- Basic understanding of the behavior of aerospace materials and structures and appropriate selection based on material/structural analysis.
- The student should gain physical insights into basic principles with in-class assignments, and computer experiments (e.g. MATLAB).
- Develop intuition about the nature of aerospace structures and materials.
- Develop a hands-on, minds-on, can-do attitude.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1. Homework problems will be assigned for each thematic area. Submitted homework assignments should be presented in a professional format, i.e., legible work that is presented in a logical order such that it can be readily followed. Homework is practice, not a trade secret, and you are encouraged to work with your classmates, teaching assistants, and instructors. Seeking help from other sources is also encouraged. How- ever, the work you submit should be that of you and your group, developed collaboratively (not independently). Evidence of your merely transcribing another person’s homework will result in a zero grade for the homework assignment in question, for all parties involved. There is a strong correlation between high homework scores and high exam scores – and few reasons to do poorly on homework. The problems that appear on exams will be similar to the specific homework problems and the example problems worked in class. If you can work the homework problems and the class example problems, you will do well on the exams.
2. There will be two take-home exams during the semester, counting for 55% of the total grade. Each exam will be of open-book open-note format. Any request for a make-up exam must be accompanied by an official excuse from the Student Experience Office, x8022, se@rpi.edu. The make-up exams will be given at the earliest possible date at a common time for those who missed the test.
3. There will be a comprehensive take-home final exam given during the final exam period. The final exam counts 30% of the total grade. The same rules for a make-up final apply as to make-up exams. These rules are consistent with Institute policy in the Catalogue.
4. Students have the opportunity to participate in a structural design and analysis project that ac- counts for 15% of the total grade. Students may form teams (up to 4 students per team) and define a topic of interest related to aerospace structures and materials in agreement with the instructor. Additional information is provided in the first lecture of the semester.
