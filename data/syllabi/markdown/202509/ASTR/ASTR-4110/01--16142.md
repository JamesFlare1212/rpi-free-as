## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Cosmology is the study of the Universe as a whole. In this course we will

try to answer the following questions (among others): What are the contents of the Universe? How

has the history of the Universe looked until now? How will the Universe evolve from here? How

come one location in the Universe is not more special than another? And how come one direction

is not more special than another?

### Goals:

By the end of the course, and upon satisfactory completion of all homework assignments, reading

bang cosmology and the inflationary paradigm.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- To be able to derive the Friedman equations employing general relativity.
- Understand how the dynamics of the universe depend on its contents.
- To be able to use methods statistical mechanics to analyze equilibrium processes such as neutrino decoupling in the early universe
- Understand the WIMP miracle.
- Understand the formation process of the light elements during Big Bang Nucleosynthesis.
- Understand the problems with standard hot big band cosmology.
- To understand how cosmic inflation solves the above problems.
- To be able analyze different models of cosmic inflation.
- Understand conceptually the theory of reheating.
- To be able to quantitatively analyze the cosmological perturbations that lead to structure formation.
- Understand how the cosmological perturbations originate from inflation.
- Understand how physical processes and quantities determine the shape of the CMB power spectrum.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Reading Assignments:

There will be a reading assignment in advance of every class period. In order to receive credit

for the reading assignment, the student has to submit a question on the topic to the instructor

through LMS no later than 6:00 pm the day before the scheduled class time. The question has to

be substantial, e.g. ”Can you explain equation XYZ?” will not receive credit. The lowest reading

assignment score will be dropped to compute your final grade in the course. Therefore, if you can

miss one reading assignment without a penalty. The reading assignment will account for 20% of

the final grade.

Homework:

There will be weekly homework assignments. The homework assignments will be due at the begin-

ning of class on the due date. Homework solutions are to be submitted on paper. In general, late

homework will not be accepted. The homework will account for 20% of the final grade.

The reading and homework assignments are crucial in mastering the course material.

Exams:

There will be 2 oral exams: the first one on or around October 17, the second one on or around the

last day of class, December 12. The better exam score will count double. So your exam average

will be calculated from effectively 3 exam scores. The exams will account for 60% of the final grade.
