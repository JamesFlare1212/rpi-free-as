## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

CIVL 2050 is the introductory course in fluid and flow phenomena applicable to engineering problems. It is also the laboratory course in fluids and flow phenomena. It is intended to draw to student's attention the behavior of real fluids and to expose students to the experimental method.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. Utilize the fluid properties with the right units to solve fluid problems.
2. Change from absolute to gauge pressure or vice versa under appropriate conditions.
3. Solve manometer problems through application of fundamental equation of fluid statics.
4. Determine the pressures, forces, and line of action of a fluid at rest on plane and curved surfaces.
5. Define and identify different types of fluid flows.
6. Identify a control volume.
7. Be able to apply the conservation of mass principle to a control volume.
8. Be able to apply the conservation of energy principle to a control volume.
9. Be able to apply the conservation of momentum principle to a control volume.
10. Undertake basic dimensional analysis.
11. Use dimensionless numbers in establishing model/prototype relationships.
12. Use dimensionless numbers to examine pump performance and selection.
13. Use dimensionless numbers to examine drag and lift on objects immersed in flow
14. Observe the difference between the behavior of a real and an ideal fluid.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Analyze pressures and forces exerted by fluids at rest and in uniform flow
- Apply the fundamental principles of conservation of mass, energy and momentum to a control volume
- Analyze energy losses in pipe flows
- Use dimensionless number to evaluate pipe friction, pump performance and model/prototype relationships
- Acquire skills in analyzing and solving engineering problems.
- Make measurements of flow phenomena
- Apply the fundamental principles of hydrostatics and conservation of mass, energy, and momentum to a control volume

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

***Attendance Policy***

Attendance is mandatory for this course. Because consistent presence is essential for learning and participation, attendance will count toward your final grade.

- You may miss up to three (3) classes without excuse during the semester. These “free” absences are intended to cover minor illnesses, travel, or other personal circumstances. No documentation is required for these first three absences.
- For any additional absences, you must notify the instructor in advance (e.g., for university travel, religious observances) or provide an explanation during office hours for consideration.
- Attendance is worth 5% of the total course grade.

**Grading of Attendance:**

- The course has 25 lecture sessions (excluding exams).
- Each unexcused absence beyond the first three will result in a ~4.5-point deduction (out of 100), applied proportionally to the remaining attendance points.
- **Example**: If you miss 5 total classes without valid excuse, the first 3 are excused, but the 4th and 5th absences each deduct ~4.5 points from your attendance grade.

### Other Course Policies:

**Organization:** The course is structured into:

- **Lecture**: two-110-minute lectures per week. Each class will be divided into lecture sessions and in-class participation sessions. Lectures notes will be posted on Blackboard LMS.
- **Laboratory experiments**: three laboratory experiments will be held over the semester. Typical labs run for 60-90 minutes and are conducted with small teams of 4-6.
- **Recitation/office hours**: the instructor and TAs host weekly recitations or office hours as listed above.

**Laboratory experiments:** Students will engage with three laboratory experiments to familiarize themselves with the experimental method. This may be described as observing, modelling, predicting and evaluating fluid behavior. A theoretical model will usually be available from lecture; the model will predict results of the experiment and can be compared to the measured findings. Students will write a detailed report on the experiment and discuss the experimental observations in the context of the theory. Students must submit all lab reports to pass the class. Students unable to complete lab reports on time should contact the instructor.

**Exams:** Three closed-book exams are scheduled: **Exam 1**: Thursday, September 25th, 2025; **Exam 2**: Thursday, November 20th, 2025; **Final Exam**: TBA. Make up exams will be given at the discretion of the instructor. A crib sheet is permitted with the following specifications:

- Single sided, written by the exam taker (includes name and RIN).
- Hand-written (not on a tablet).
- Can include equations, brief notes, basic diagrams.
- Cannot include solutions to problems or extensive sections of course notes.

**Opportunity for extra credit**: After the two midterm exams, students may submit a Root Cause Analysis (RCA). This brief report requires an analysis of exam performance. Students who complete an RCA may redeem 5 points (based on option 1 grade distributions, outlined below) on their original exam score. See “RCA guidelines” on LMS. **Homework presentation:**

1. It is encouraged that students use engineering “ruled” paper (physical or digital). Each problem should begin at the top of a new page with the problem # shown.
2. The upper margin of the first page of your homework should show your name, the homework assignment number, the date the homework is turned in, and the page number. The upper margin of the first page of each problem should show the problem number and page number. Other pages should include the page number. The page number should be given in the upper right corner in the format A/B where A is the current page number and B is the total number of pages in your homework.
3. Computations should be arranged in logical order and each step recorded in full (other engineers should be able to easily follow your work). The order of presentation of work should be as shown below. Any work that does not substantially follow this order of presentation will receive no credit.

- List given data and diagrams
- List required results

------------------------------------- (use a line to separate information given to you from your work)

- Calculations and diagrams
- Final answer (boxed in)

4. Do not make short-cuts. Perform one operation at a time and make a complete record of it. Only experienced engineers can safely take short-cuts. An exception may be made for repetitive or tabular computations, in which case it is enough to provide a complete record of a sample calculation.
5. Results should be clearly indicated by *boxing* them in.
6. The correct units should be provided for intermediate results and final answers. A result is of no value if units are not provided. Note that careful consideration of units will often prevent mistakes.
