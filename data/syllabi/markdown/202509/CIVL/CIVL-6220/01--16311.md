## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Graduate level course intended to provide the students with a broad understanding of current methodologies in transportation modeling and economics not thoroughly covered in other courses and emerging issues pertaining to transportation research and practice.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The specific course objectives and outcomes include:

- Students will have the ability to analyze complex transportation problems considering economic, social, environmental and political factors.

- Students will be able to understand and use commonly available data sources to identify and propose solutions to transportation problems

- Students will have the ability to incorporate worldwide best practices in transportation in analyzing transportation issues.

- Students will have the ability to clearly communicate to a broad audience complex technical concepts concerning transportation issues.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Paper 1 – Benefit Cost Analysis –** Each student will prepare a summary of a benefit-cost analysis of a project or policy. The analysis should include a clear description of the policy or project, a set of assumptions about economic service life, discount rate, unit values of travel times, capital, operating costs and user benefits. IF a sensitivity analysis were undertaken, this should be reported as well. A discussion of the treatment of non-monetary benefits should be included. This paper will include a presentation and will be due on Thursday, September 21

**Paper 2 – Data Driven Analysis** – Using a range of datasets, each student will prepare an assessment of a change in a local, state, or federal policy. Examples might include revenue raising from various methods of charging electric vehicles for roadway use or an estimate of traffic fatality reduction from a variety of autonomous vehicle safety features. Another might be how incentives to replace conventionally powered vehicles with electric vehicles might work. This will be due on Thursday, November 16.

**Paper 3- Case Study** -The third paper and presentation is a paper describing how a community (city, state, country) addressed a particularly complex transportation issue. This paper should be about a policy not a project. The paper should include a clear articulation of the problem, alternatives considered, methods of assessing alternatives and the results. The paper does not have to be a “success story” but rather it should focus on the practical problems associated with analyzing transportation issues. Paper length is about 6-8 pages. This paper is due on the last day of class.

**Paper 3 – (alternate) Comparison of US vs. Foreign Practices –** An alternative paper satisfying the requirement is a comparison between a US practice or policy and a comparable policy in another country (preferably an OECD country). This should be a paper on a major policy. You should discuss the merits of the foreign policy and the barriers to implementing such a policy in the United States. Paper length is about 6-8 pages. This paper is due on the last day of class.

**Attendance and Absences:**

You are expected to attend class promptly and regularly. If you are unable to attend class, it is your responsibility to obtain class notes from a classmate. If you arrive late to class, or must leave early, please be considerate with your classmates, and do so as quietly and unobtrusively as possible. If you are unable to submit a homework assignment or take and exam or quiz due to an absence, you will be granted a waiver of the late homework policy and be given the opportunity to make up for the missed exam or quiz only under special circumstances. These include 1) illness or personal injury and 2) university-related extracurricular activities. Illnesses and personal injuries include you or your children or spouse, and extracurricular activities include athletics. A written statement from a responsible party is required to be granted these special considerations. In the case of illness or personal injury, a letter from a physician must indicate that you were incapacitated from attending class. In the case of athletics, a letter from your coach or trainer must explain why you had to miss class
