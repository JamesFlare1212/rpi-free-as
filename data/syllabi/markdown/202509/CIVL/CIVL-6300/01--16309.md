## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This graduate level course will introduce a number of econometric models that are important to transportation data analysis. The application of such models ranges from traffic safety analysis to travel demand modeling and policy evaluation. Students will learn to specify the models based on data characteristics and model properties. They will also learn to estimate these models using appropriate techniques. Equally importantly is that students will learn to interpret the analysis results in a rigorous and meaningful way. This course provides an opportunity to develop the skills of studying transportation issues through statistical data analysis and to gain hands-on experience with statistical analysis software. The course will emphasize application, but the underlying theory and limitations will also be discussed to ensure that the methods are fully understood, properly applied and potentially enhanced.

Prerequisites for the course include basic linear algebra, matrix analysis and probability and statistics. Therefore, students will be expected to understand the basic concepts of statistical distributions, hypothesis testing, graphical methods, matrix manipulation, etc. upon entering the course.

### Criteria Details:

For the problem sets, you may (are encouraged to) discuss with other students but the final written solution should be your own work. The Midterm exam will be a closed book exam; the Final exam will be open book or take-home exam.

**Homework:**

Most homework will involve the use of a computer and an econometrics package. Homework problems will be assigned approximately bi-weekly and must be handed in at the beginning of the period in which they are due. After that time, they will be considered late and given no credit unless accompanied by a medical certificate. I also encourage students to attempt additional problems related to the topic. Though students are authorized and encouraged to work on homework assignments together, I would strongly advise against students relying too heavily on other students. All written work handed in by a student is considered to be his/her own work, prepared without unauthorized assistance.

**Course project:** The course project will involve an individual research paper and an in-class presentation at the end of the semester. A specific transportation issue is to be analyzed using one or more econometric models. More detailed requirements and schedule for the course project are listed in the attached “Introduction to Course Projects.”

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Discussions among students are encouraged, but the homework assignments must be done and handed-in individually. Use of AI tools is permitted; however, students must properly document exactly what content was created by the program and must also be able to demonstrate that the material generated has been cross-checked from a different source/reference. Please bear in mind that each student is ultimately responsible for what he/she takes away from the course. In that regard, exams are a measure of individual progress and must reflect individual effort. Plagiarism and other forms of cheating are serious violations of the trust between student and teacher and among students. Penalties for such violations are severe. The first violation will lead to an F on the assignment/test. The second violation will result in failure of the class, and the student will be subject to disciplinary action through the Rensselaer judicial process as described in the Rensselaer Handbook.
