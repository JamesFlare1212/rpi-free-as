## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Catalog Description: Subsurface investigation. The application of the principles of soil mechanics to the design of footings, retaining walls, pile foundations, bulkheads, cofferdams, bridge piers and abutments, and underpinnings.  
This course builds on knowledge gained in CIVL 2630 and applies this knowledge to the analysis of foundations, both shallow and deep, and to the analysis of retaining walls. After a brief review of soil mechanics, students learn about site investigation techniques and methods to determine relevant soil properties. Students then learn how to analyze shallow foundations, deep foundations and retaining walls through lecture material, class examples and homework assignments. Practical aspects of foundation design are stressed and modern tools used in foundation engineering are discussed, whenever appropriate.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The primary goal of this course is to equip students with the knowledge and skills to analyze and design shallow and deep foundations, as well as retaining structures, with an emphasis on professional engineering practice. Students will learn both theoretical concepts and practical approaches used in modern geotechnical design. The course also encourages independent learning and effective use of technical resources.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

This course covers the application of soil mechanics principles to foundation engineering. Topics include:

- **Introduction and Review** of essential soil mechanics concepts.
- **Site Investigation and Characterization**: drilling, sampling, in situ testing, soil property evaluation, and preparation of site investigation reports.
- **Shallow Foundations**: bearing capacity of spread footings, settlement analysis in granular and cohesive soils, and mat/raft foundations.
- **Deep Foundations**: axial load capacity of single piles, pile groups, pile load tests, pile-driving formulae, and settlement analysis.
- **Earth Retaining Structures**: active and passive lateral earth pressures, stability of cantilever walls, sheet pile walls, and braced excavations.
- **Practical Aspects of Design**: use of modern tools, web and library resources, and AI-based methods for foundation engineering research.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Describe the methods used to conduct site investigation
- Interpret Standard Penetration Test (SPT) and Cone Penetration Test (CPT) data to evaluate soil properties
- Determine the allowable bearing pressure of shallow foundations considering bearing capacity and settlement
- Evaluate the allowable axial load capacity of single piles and pile groups
- Estimate the settlement of single piles and pile groups
- Compute active and passive lateral earth pressures
- Assess the stability of simple earth retaining structures
- Identify, access, and effectively use web, library, and AI resources for foundation engineering topics

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students will be evaluated through a combination of homework assignments, a course project, midterm exams, and a cumulative final exam.

- **Homework Assignments (15%)**  
  Approximately 8 problem sets will be assigned throughout the semester. Submissions are expected to be professional, neat, and complete, with supporting sketches and diagrams where appropriate.
- **Course Project (25%)**  
  A short research-based project requiring students to use web, library, or AI resources to investigate a foundation engineering topic not fully covered in lecture. Students will prepare a brief written report and presentation of their findings.
- **Midterm Exam (30%)**  
  One midterm exam (1–2 hours) will test shallow a foundation analysis, as well as earth pressure concepts.
- **Final Exam (30%)**  
  A semi-comprehensive 2–3 hour exam covering all course topics but focusing on deep foundations.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic integrity is a fundamental expectation of this course. All submitted work must be your own, except where group collaboration is explicitly permitted. Collaborative or group work is encouraged for homework discussions, but each student must prepare and submit their own original solutions.

**Prohibited Actions**

- Copying homework, exam solutions, or project reports from other students or online sources.
- Using solution manuals, unauthorized websites, or past assignments without permission.
- Plagiarizing text, figures, or data without proper citation.
- Sharing or receiving information during an exam, unless explicitly allowed.

**AI Use Policy**  
The use of AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Copilot, or similar) is **permitted** for brainstorming, drafting, or research support. However, all use of AI must be **documented** by including the prompts and responses as an appendix to the submitted assignment. Undocumented AI use will be treated as a violation of academic integrity. Students are ultimately responsible for the accuracy, correctness, and originality of their submitted work.

**Penalties**

- **First Offense:** A grade of zero will be assigned for the assignment or exam in which the violation occurs.
- **Second Offense:** The student will receive a failing grade (F) for the course.
- All violations will be reported in accordance with Institute policy.
