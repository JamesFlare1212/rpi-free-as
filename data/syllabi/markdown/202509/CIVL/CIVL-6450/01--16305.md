## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Analysis of elastic and inelastic single and multiple degree-of-freedom structural systems under time-dependent loads including harmonic, impulse, earthquake, and other general dynamic loads. Development of equations of motion. Analytical and numerical evaluation of free and forced vibration response. Identification of dynamic system properties. Modal analysis. Vibration isolation and force transmissibility. Dynamic measurement sensors. Shock loading spectrum. Frequency-domain analysis.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Describe the distinguishing characteristics of static and dynamic structural analyses.
- Construct mathematical models of structural systems subjected to dynamic loading and derive the associated equation of motion.
- Solve the equation of motion using analytical and numerical methods.
- Solve the equation of motion using modern software tools.

Homework Policy:  
1\) Homework assignments will be posted on LMS. The primary purpose of the homework is to help students learn the course material and thus to be well prepared to participate in class, to present and discuss homework, and to take exams.  
2\) To aid in the learning process, students should initially work on the homework problems on their own. Having done that, students should discuss their work with other classmates to help each other arrive at the correct solution or to check solutions to verify that the correct result was obtained. However, this must be done without becoming overly reliant on other students. In addition, students should consult with the instructor if they have questions about the homework.  
3\) On or after the homework due date, a student will be selected at random to present their solution to the class. Students should come to each class sufficiently prepared to present their solution (for presenting the solution in class, it is not required that a student's solution be completely correct but it should be well thought out and organized so the student can clearly explain what they did and can present it in an efficient manner). Furthermore, to maximize the homework presentation grade (20% of course grade), students should exhibit good oral and written communication skills when presenting homework solutions.  
4\) Homework will not be submitted or graded. However, students are expected to solve all homework problems and eventually obtain the correct solution. Students should attempt to obtain the correct solution on their own or by working with others as needed; if this is not successful, students will have access to the correct solution and final answers via homework solutions that are presented and discussed in class.  
5\) It is strongly recommended that each student maintain an organized set of completed homework problems since they will be useful for understanding the material in the course and thus preparing for exams.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

In this course, **all solutions that are developed by a student must represent the student’s own work**, although, after attempting to solve problems on their own, students are encouraged to discuss their work with other classmates to help each other arrive at the correct solution or to check their solution. Development of a solution that is in violation of this policy (e.g., one student copying work done by another student or a student copying from a previously developed solution including a solution from a previous semester or a solution that is contained within a solution manual) will result in a course grade reduction of up to one full letter grade (e.g., from B to C). In addition, subsequent work done by the student will be scrutinized to evaluate potential cheating. Finally, the Dean of Students, who maintains records of academic dishonesty, will be notified. Additional disciplinary actions may be taken by the Dean of Students.

In this course, **all exams must represent the student’s own work**. If cheating occurs during an exam, the student will receive a grade of zero for that exam. In addition, and in accordance with the *Handbook*, the Dean of Students Office will be notified of the incident of academic dishonesty. Additional disciplinary actions may be taken by the Dean of Students. In addition, all **mobile/electronic devices (cell/smart phones, computers, etc.) must be stored securely away during an exam** and are not to be used unless specifically directed otherwise by the instructor. Any use of, or interaction with, a mobile/electronic device during an exam without explicit permission of the instructor will be interpreted as the illicit transfer of exam data and thus will be considered an act of cheating.
