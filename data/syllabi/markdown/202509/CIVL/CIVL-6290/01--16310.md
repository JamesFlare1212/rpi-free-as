## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course discusses freight transportation systems with specific emphasis on operations, economics, and planning. As part of the course, the students are asked to prepare presentations and research papers.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

No textbook required

## Course Goals

### Goals:

To provide the students with a solid understanding of the fundamentals of operation and planning of freight transportation systems, from the public sector point of view.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

## **Block I: Introduction, Freight Management, Policy, and Planning, Supply Chain Agents**

1. About the Course and the Instructor
2. Myths and Realities
3. Freight Transportation Management, Policy Planning
4. a) Objectives: Economic, efficiency, Environmental, Infrastructure, Congestion, Safety, Urban and Regional Structure
5. a) Public sector role and policy tools: Regulation, Incentives / Penalties, Public investment
6. Supply Chain Agents: Carrier and non-Carriers
7. Impacts of Emergent Trends
8. Overall Approach

## **Block II: Introduction, Background and Socio-Economic Aspects**

National land use patterns

1. Metropolitan land use patterns
2. Supply chain examples
3. Demand for freight transport
4. The freight transportation system
5. a) Size
6. b) Key components
7. Environmental and energy impacts

## **Block III: The Distribution System and Participating Agents**

1. Participating agents
2. a) Brokers, freight forwarders, regulatory agencies, trucking companies, railroads, intermodal terminals
3. b) Other meaningful distinctions: Full-truckload (FTL) vs. Less-than-truckload (LTL); Own-account/Private vs. For-hire/Common carriers
4. The distribution system
5. a) Role of various freight transportation modes
6. b) Pick-up and deliveries, consolidation practices
7. c) Intermodal terminals, consolidation and distribution centers
8. Industry structure
9. a) Trucking
10. b) Rail
11. c) Ports
12. d) Air freight
13. Freight technology
14. a) Trucking technology
15. b) Rail freight
16. c) Warehouse technology
17. d) Ports and intermodal terminals

## **Block IV: A System Perspective of Freight Activity**

1. Commodity types
2. a) Coding systems
3. b) Aggregation procedures
4. c) Freight origin-destination surveys
5. Urban freight
6. a) Tonnage transported
7. b) Mode split
8. c) Vehicle-trips produced, vehicle fleet
9. d) Empty trips and trip chaining behavior
10. Intercity freight
11. a) Tonnage transported
12. b) Mode split
13. c) Vehicle-trips produced, vehicle fleet
14. d) Empty trips and trip chaining behavior

## **Block V: Behavior of Freight Agents**

1. Supply and demand concepts
2. a) Supply function
3. b) Demand function, elasticity
4. c) Relationships between them
5. d) Welfare
6. Review of cost functions
7. a) Fixed and variable costs
8. b) Average and marginal costs
9. c) Relationships between them
10. d) Scale economies and diseconomies
11. Basic postulates of economic rationality
12. a) Profit maximizing
13. b) Marginal gross revenues vs. Marginal costs
14. Optimal pricing and market structures
15. a) Pricing objectives: profit maximization, welfare maximization, 2nd best
16. b) Market structures: Competitive, Oligopoly, Monopoly, Oligopsony, Monopsony
17. The role of market segments
18. a) Technology used
19. b) Commodity transported
20. c) Economic sector
21. Empirical evidence
22. a) E-ZPass use
23. b) Attitude towards City Logistics concepts

## **Block VI: Public Sector Initiatives to Improve Freight System Performance**

1. Infrastructure Management
2. a) Major Improvements
3. b) Minor Improvements
4. Parking / Loading Areas Management
5. a) On-Street Parking and Loading
6. b) Off-Street Parking and Loading
7. Vehicle-Related Strategies
8. Traffic Management
9. a) Access and Vehicle-Related Restrictions
10. b) Time Access Restrictions
11. c) Traffic Control and Lane Management
12. Pricing, Incentives, and Taxation
13. a) Pricing
14. b) Incentives
15. c) Taxation
16. Logistical Management
17. a) Cargo Consolidation
18. b) Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS)
19. c) Last Mile Delivery Practices
20. Freight Demand / Land Use Management
21. a) Demand Management
22. b) Land Use Policy
23. Stakeholder Engagement

## **Block VII: Strategic Freight Transportation Planning and Freight Demand Modeling**

1. Dimensions of freight demand, modeling platforms, and limitations
2. a) Vehicle-trips based
3. b) Commodity based
4. c) Classification of freight demand models: trip based vs. tour based, commodity based vs. vehicle trip based, aggregate vs. disaggregate
5. Freight generation models
6. a) Vehicle-trips based
7. b) Commodity based
8. Vehicle and mode choice
9. a) Impact of shipper-carrier interactions on mode choice decisions
10. b) Results of research on behavioral modeling and experimental economics
11. c) Analytical formulations
12. Aggregate distribution models
13. a) Direct demand models
14. b) Spatial Interaction Models
15. c) Spatial Price Equilibrium and game theoretic formulations
16. d) Input-Output models
17. e) Empty trip models
18. f) Freight origin-destination synthesis
19. g) Entropy tour distribution model
20. Disaggregate distribution models
21. a) Logistic based models
22. b) Behavioral models
23. c) Hybrid models
24. d) Agent based models

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand the basic role an importance of the various components of freight transportation systems (ABET 3a).
- Understand the fundamental rules of decision making in the freight sector (ABET 3j).
- Formulate freight transportation alternatives to solve urban congestion (ABET 3e).
- Produce basic reports and give presentations (ABET 3g).
