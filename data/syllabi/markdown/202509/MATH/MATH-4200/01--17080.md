## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- The students will be able to rigorously prove basic facts from the construction of rational, real, and complex numbers, basic inequalities, metric-space properties of the real axis and complex plane, compactness, continuity, differentiability, Riemann integrability, Taylor expansions, numerical and functional series.
- The students will demonstrate their ability to write coherent mathematical proofs.
- The students will be able to demonstrate their understanding of how the results in calculus follow from the principles of mathematical analysis.
- The students will demonstrate their ability to use tools from mathematical analysis in providing rigorous solutions for problems from calculus and its applications.
- The students will be able to choose the appropriate techniques from mathematical analysis to generate mathematical proofs.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

For the homework, you are encouraged to collaborate by exchanging ideas and brainstorming solutions. You can work in groups in which you discuss the problems and help each other out if you are stuck. You are also allowed to use books and informative websites such as Wikipedia. However, in all cases, what you turn in must be your own work, and copying of any kind is forbidden. Examples include copying from fellow students (this includes partitioning the homework among several students without everybody's participation on every problem), contacting helpers outside of exam rooms using electronic or other means, wholesale copying of solutions from the internet (both recycled problems from past versions of this class or solutions of the assigned problems from any other source), using solvers such as Maple or Mathematica (unless allowed for a specific problem), using cheat sites such as Cheggor Course Hero, or using AI helpers such as Chat GPT unless specifically permitted for a problem.

For the exams, you will be allowed a one-page, two-sided crib sheet of notes in your own handwriting. When taking the exams, you are not to discuss the questions with anyone but the proctoring instructor or TA. Copying from fellow students is strictly forbidden, as is using any type of books, notes, or electronic aids.  
Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. Acts, which violate this trust, undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. Violations of the above policies constitute violations of student-teacher trust, as well represent taking an unfair advantage of your fellow students. Therefore, such violations will result in a score of zero for the homework or exam on which they were committed. Penalties for repeat oﬀenses can be quite harsh: you should check the Academic Integrity Policy page provided by the Dean of

Students Oﬃce for more information about this: https://doso.rpi.edu/student-rights-responsibilities-and-conduct.
