### Additional Course Description :

Functions, limits, continuity, derivatives, implicit differentiation, related rates, maxima and minima, elementary transcendental functions, introduction to definite integral with applications to area and volumes of revolution. (from the [Rensselaer Catalog](http://catalog.rpi.edu/))

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate basic and advanced symbol manipulation skills.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to convert between calculus concepts and their graphical, numerical, and symbolic representations.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to apply calculus concepts to selected problems in science and engineering.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to apply certain fundamental theorems, basic concepts, and rules from calculus to solve symbolic and graphical problems.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to present problems related to the theory of calculus and its applications to science and engineering disciplines.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance:** Students are responsible for all material covered in lectures whether they are present or not. Attendance is expected in lecture, but will only be checked on quiz days.

### Other Course Policies:

**Exams:** Exams will be given during test block with the exception of DSS students and excused absences as outline below. Attendance will be taken at all exams using sign in sheets. Details about each exam, including the material to be covered on the exam and time limit, will be announced on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/) when the date of the exam is near. The dates for Exams 1–3 are listed above in the section **Grading.**

DSS students will be taking their exams at the testing center. DSS students are responsible for booking their own testing appointments with the testing center following the guidelines that the instructor provides you. Note that these appointments **must be made well in advance** of scheduled exam dates. If a DSS student fails to schedule an testing appointment with the testing center more that 48 hours prior to the scheduled exam date, it is possible that their accommodation can not be met and they may have to take the exam as scheduled with their classmates.

A make-up exam is scheduled for each in-semester exam as noted above in the **Grading** section. Any student who misses an exam, must take the make-up exam at the time scheduled. If a student misses both the scheduled exam and the make-up exam dates, the student will receive a score of 0 on the exam. The only exception to this policy is a student who has an excused absence (e.g. official excused absence documented by Student Success to attend a conference, athletic competition) on the day of the exam AND on the day of the make-up exam. In such a case, the student must have notified the instructor of said absences as soon as they learn of it and no later than one class period prior to the regularly scheduled exam. Documentation excusing the student from class at the time of the regularly scheduled exam and make-up exam ([**excused absence documented by the Student Success office**](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/current-students/academic-and-personal-support/requesting-excused-absence)**, similar documentation directly to instructor or documentation from a coach with game departure time noted)** must be received by the instructor before the day of the regularly scheduled exam. Note that if you know ahead of time that you are going to miss an exam, for any reason, you must discuss this with the instructor no later than one class period prior to the exam. Note that the policy outlined here is for in-semester exams only. The policy for the final exam is outlined below.

**Final Exam:** The Final Exam will be cumulative and will be administered during the finals period December 16-19, 22. **All students must take the final exam at the time scheduled by the Office of the Registrar. Exceptions will only be made if there is a scheduling conflict as defined** [**here**](https://registrar.rpi.edu/services/class-scheduling/final-exam-schedule)**, or if a student is granted a NE grade by the Student Success office.** Please note that the Student Success office will only issue an NE for final exams for extreme circumstances, such as hospitalization (Read more here.) All students **must** take their exam at the assigned time for MATH 1010 Sections 1 - 8. You may NOT take a MATH 1010 final exam given by a different instructor. ***Do not make arrangements for travel at the end of the semester until the Final Exam Schedule from the Registrar’s Office is published or schedule your travel after the last day of final exams - December 22.***

**Homework:** Achieve homework assignments will be due weekly on Tuesdays and Fridays at 11:59 PM and will be graded automatically by the system. Students may submit these assignments late up to two days after the original deadline, but a penalty will be applied to the score. A 10% penalty will apply to assignments submitted the day after the deadline and a 20% penalty will apply to assignments submitted two days after the deadline. **No other due date extensions will be granted for any reason.** To further compensate for this, several homework assignment scores will be dropped at the end of the semester.

Additional problems will be assigned from the textbook and will be posted on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/) in the **Achieve & Homework** content area found in the left toolbar. Though these problems will not be collected nor graded, they should not be viewed as optional. Success in the course depends on successful completion of all homework, both Achieve homework and textbook homework. Exam problems are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples. Full solutions to odd-numbered textbook problems can be found on Achieve. When logged in to our Achieve course, view content by assignments and scroll to the bottom of the page. The student solutions manual for chapters 1-6 may be found below No Due Dates. (Tip: you can download and save these files on your computer.)

**Quizzes:** Quizzes will be given in-person during Tuesday lecture most weeks of the semester. Tuesday quizzes will be on sections covered the week before the quiz. A detailed schedule will be made available on LMS in the **Quizzes** content area. Students must take quizzes during the lecture time that is listed on their schedule on SIS. No make-up quizzes will be given for any reason. To compensate for this, two (2) in-person quiz scores will be dropped at the end of the semester. Students who miss a quiz due to pre-scheduled absence (e.g. official excused absence documented by Student Success to attend a conference, athletic competition) may be excused from a quiz if they provide documentation to the instructor prior to the missed quiz. A student with two(2) or more excused absences must discuss with the instructor how their quiz average will be calculated for the course.

**Disability Services for Students (DSS):** Students with a memorandum from the [Disability Services for Students (DSS)](http://studenthealth.rpi.edu/disabilityservices) office identifying accommodations for exams must present the memorandum to the instructor, ideally by Tuesday, September 9. Providing the accommodations requires advance planning. Students will not be granted accommodations unless a memorandum from DSS has been submitted to the instructor **and** the student has followed emailed instructions for signing up for a testing appointment through the Testing Center. Students who fail to make their testing appointments more than 48 hours in advance may forfeit their accommodations.

**Calculator Policy:** Calculators will **not be allowed** on exams or quizzes. Problems will be designed so that this does not create an issue. Occasionally, a calculator may be required to complete a homework problem, but students should not rely on calculators for simple arithmetic or trigonometric calculations.

**Health and Safety:** Students are required to follow the health and safety protocols of RPI, which are subject to change throughout the semester.

**Visitors:** Visitors are prohibited during all class meetings. Only students who are registered for the class should be present in the classroom. Students may only attend class meetings at the time and place listed on their schedule on SIS.

**Classroom Decorum:** Students must be on time for class and should refrain from leaving and reentering the classroom during class. If a student has a legitimate reason for arriving late to class or being excused early from class, then they should discuss this with the instructor before class. Phones may not be used during class and should be silent. Computers and tablets may not be used for anything other than taking notes. Students who are unable to courteously comply with these policies may be asked to leave the room.

**Extra Credit:** No extra credit opportunities will be available during the course. Thus students are encouraged to give their best effort on exams and homework assignments throughout the semester.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://rpi.app.box.com/s/d4ndpn3f7qdwxh9jaz5n1hkkr0mnd25e) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams must be the student’s own. A student may not change an answer on an exam before submitting it for an appeal. In addition, no calculators, computers, tablets, open books, open notes, phones, headphones, or smartwatches may be used during exams. Talking or communicating with other students in any way during exams is also prohibited. Students may discuss homework with other students, CAs, TAs, and instructors, but the work submitted must be the student’s own.

The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
