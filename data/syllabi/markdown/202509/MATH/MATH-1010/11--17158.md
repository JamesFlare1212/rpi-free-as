## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate basic and advanced symbol manipulation skills.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to convert between calculus concepts and their graphical, numerical, and symbolic representations.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to apply calculus concepts to selected problems in science and engineering.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to apply certain fundamental theorems, basic concepts, and rules from calculus to solve symbolic and graphical problems.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to present problems related to the theory of calculus and its applications to science and engineering disciplines.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Class Policies and Grading Scheme

Your final grade will be computed as follows:

5% In-Class Problems assigned each class (3 lowest scores will get dropped)

5% Recitation Problems assigned weekly during recitation unless announced otherwise (1 lowest score will get dropped)

10% Achieve Homework assigned each class (5 lowest scores will get dropped)

80% Exams (4 exams scheduled for 9/25, 10/20, 11/10, and 12/11 will count at 20% each) NO exam scores will be dropped

You must get AT LEAST a 50% exam average to pass the course

Letter grades will be assigned according to the following scheme (there is NO rounding or curving of grades at the end of the semester): A 93-100; A- 90-92.99999; B+ 87-89.99999; B 83-86.99999; B- 80-82.99999; C+ 76-79.99999; C 72-75.99999; C- 67-71.99999; D+ 62-66.99999; D 57-61.99999; F 0-56.99999

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance

Students are responsible for all material covered in class and recitation, so attendance of every lecture and recitation is strongly recommended and encouraged. If a student is absent, they should get the class notes posted each week on LMS and keep up with assignments as assigned on Achieve, during lecture, and during recitation. The best way to stay on top of the course, besides submitting all assignments on time, is to come to class and recitation, attend the Wednesday block, and think about the material as it is presented.

### Other Course Policies:

Additional Policies

Calculator Policy

Calculators will not be allowed on exams. Problems will be designed so that this does not create an issue. Occasionally, a calculator may be required to complete a homework problem, but students should not rely on calculators for simple arithmetic or trigonometric calculations.

Classroom Decorum

Phones may not be used during class and should be silent. Computers and tablets may not be used for anything other than taking notes. Students who are unable to courteously comply with these policies must note that they are probably missing most of the lecture, including practice questions, common errors or hints that may be given about graded assignments or exams.

Health and Safety Protocols

Students are required to follow the health and safety protocols of RPI, which are subject to change throughout the semester. Please be courteous to others when feeling sick. Consider wearing a mask, covering your face when sneezing and/or coughing, and washing your hands more frequently.

Visitors

Visitors are prohibited during all class meetings. Only students who are registered for the class should be present in the classroom. Students may only attend class meetings at the time and place listed on their schedule on SIS unless cleared by the instructor.

THERE ARE NO EXTRA CREDIT ASSIGNMENTS GIVEN AT THE END OF THE SEMESTER. All course credit comes from the homework, in-class assignments, recitation assignments, and exams and administered regularly throughout the semester. Successful completion of the class indicates an in-depth understanding of the content AND the ability to correctly and precisely communicate this understanding by solving relevant problems using symbolic representation. If you cannot do the actual math, you cannot pass the course.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

Exams: Exam problems will consist of problems for which the students must show all their work, as well as multiple-choice, multiple-select, true/false, and fill-in-the-blank questions. Many exam problems are similar to review questions, homework assignments, and lecture/recitation examples. You must be able to correctly solve and show all your work, as well as fully understand your answer to these to consider yourself well-prepared.

Missed exams may only be made up if a student has notified the instructor prior to the exam (in the rare case of an emergency, students may have until midnight of the exam day to notify the instructor). Other than the 4th exam, which will be administered on the last day of class, exams may only be made up during the Wednesday test block that follows that exam or receive a grade of 0. There is no final exam for this course.

Exams will be uploaded to Gradescope by the instructor, where students will be able to check their grades and feedback a week after the exam is administered. It is the responsibility of the student to review their exam for any discrepancies between their work and points awarded as described by each rubric.

In-Class Problems: Each class, I will assign some problem(s) to check understanding and get extra practice. Students can work on these in groups or individually, then submit them using Gradescope (take a picture and upload your submission). Make sure your submission goes through; many students like to download the Gradescope App which notifies you when your submission goes through successfully. However, you can use any device by going to www.Gradescope.com. Students can submit their work from the time we start working on it in class until midnight two days later. So in-class problems will be due on Wednesday and Saturday nights, unless announced otherwise. After that, submissions will not be accepted. Submissions will be graded for correctness as they would be on exams. If you miss lecture, you can find the in-class problems in the notes posted on LMS and still submit them on time.

Homework: Achieve homework assignments will be due twice weekly on Mondays and Thursdays at 11:59 PM and will be graded automatically by the system. Students may submit these assignments late up to two days after the original deadline, but a penalty will be applied to the score. A 10% penalty will apply to assignments submitted the day after the deadline and a 20% penalty will apply to assignments submitted two days after the deadline. No other due date extensions will be granted for any reason.

Getting help: don’t hesitate to ask and get help. You can ask math questions during class, office hours of the instructor, and office hours of the Teaching Assistants. You can also use email. When asking questions, it helps to be as precise as possible and to have a picture of the work you have tried. For example, if you ask a homework question by email, please attach a picture of your work so that I may check it to provide better and faster assistance. There is also tutoring and support offered by ALAC and our undergraduate CAs.
