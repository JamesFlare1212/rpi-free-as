## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The theory underlying vector spaces, algebra of subspaces, bases; linear transformations, dual spaces; eigenvectors, eigenvalues, minimal polynomials, canonical forms of linear transformations; inner products, adjoints, orthogonal projections, and complements.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Introduction to Linear Algebra, Sixth ed., by Gilbert Strang, Wellesley-Cambridge Press, 2016, ISBN: 978-17331466-7-8.

Other sources may be used for some of the topics. Notes or references will be provided.

Textbook Supplemental Resources: There are videos by Strang that supplement the textbook.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to

- demonstrate knowledge and understanding of the basic ideas of Linear Algebra, including concepts of linear systems, independence, theory of matrices, linear transformations, determinants, vector spaces and subspaces, bases and dimension, eigenvalues, eigenvectors and diagonalization, inner products, etc.
- write clear and accurate proofs using the concepts of this course,
- apply Linear Algebra concepts of this course to solve problems.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are responsible for all material covered in lectures whether they are present or not. Attendance is expected, but will not be checked. All students are expected to participate and contribute to discussions during lectures. All students are expected to read the text to fill in the material not covered, or partially covered, in lecture.

### Other Course Policies:

**Exams:** Exams will be given in person, during lecture. Details about each exam will be announced on LMS when the date of the exam is near. The dates for Exams 1–3 are listed above in the section Grading. There will be **no Final Exam** for this course.

**Missing an exam should not be taken lightly.** If a student has a pre-scheduled absence (e.g. an athletic competition, official excused absence to attend a conference) on the day of an exam, then they must notify the instructor via email as soon as they learn of the absence and no later than one week before the regularly scheduled exam. Documentation excusing the student from class at the time of the regularly scheduled exam must be received by the instructor before the day of the exam. This documentation must be either an [excused absence documented by the Student Success office](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/current-students/academic-and-personal-support/requesting-excused-absence) or, if the absence is due to an athletic competition, documentation from a coach. Provided the above conditions are met, the student will then take a conflict exam at a different time.

Students who miss an exam due to an unforeseen absence (e.g. illness on the day of the exam) must make every effort to notify the instructor **before the exam begins** to avoid grade penalties.

- If a student misses an exam due to an unforeseen absence and notifies the instructor before the exam begins, then the student may take a make-up exam without penalty.
- If a student misses an exam due to an unforeseen absence and notifies the instructor after the exam begins but before 11:59 PM on the day of their scheduled exam, then the student may take a make-up exam and a 20% penalty will be applied to the make-up exam score. A higher penalty may be applied to a student with repeated late notifications for missing exams.
- If a student misses an exam due to an unforeseen absence and **fails to notify the instructor before 11:59 PM on the day of their scheduled exam, then the student may not take the make-up exam and receives a score of 0 on the exam.**

**Homework:** All homework assignments will be of equal weight and are typically assigned weekly. Homework assignments must be submitted through Gradescope in PDF format and will be due by 11:59PM on the due date. If you are unable to hand in your homework on time due to a valid reason (work in other classes is not a valid reason), then you must make arrangements with the instructor in advance and **not** on or after the due date. Extensions may not be granted if extension requests are made on the due date. Late submissions will be subject to penalties as follows:

- Late 0-2 hours. 2% will be deducted
- Late 2-24 hours. 10% will be deducted
- Late 1-2 days. 30% will be deducted
- Homework late more than 2 days will not be counted.

The lowest homework assignment score will be dropped at the end of the semester. Successful completion of all homework problems is essential in learning Linear Algebra. Clarity in writing and presentation will be taken into account in grading. Submissions that have been nicely formatted in LaTeX can receive a 4% bonus on the assignment. The .tex file for each assignment will be provided, so you can download it and build on that. Submissions that are messy, difficult to read, and/or contain scratched out worked can receive a 4% penalty on the assignment.

**Homework Collaboration Rules:** Before students work together on the homework, they must first individually read the problem, collect relevant definitions, formulas and theorems and think of at least one or two possible approaches to a solution. After this, students may work together, but they must separate and write their solutions individually, without copying from a shared set of notes. This entire process may be iterated on a given homework assignment. List collaborators. Each problem of a homework submission must include the names of all those students **with whom you worked.** Students should not use human sources outside the class and students must know the names of all students within the class with whom they are collaborating.

**Grade Appeals:** If upon reviewing the graders’ feedback and posted solutions for an exam a student feels that their work was graded incorrectly, then the student may submit a grading appeal directly on Gradescope. Specific dates will be given for regrade request periods for exams at the time that scores are released. Regrade requests for homework will also be available on Gradescope for one week after the homework scores are released or until the last day of classes, whichever comes first. You may only appeal scores based on factual criteria (e.g. a correct answer was marked wrong, incorrect rubric item checked in Gradescope). Grade appeals should not be used to request more partial credit based or for disagreement with the point values on the rubric.

**Lectures:** An unmarked version of the lecture notes will be made available the day before lecture. At some point after lecture, a marked up version of the notes will be posted to LMS. Students are encouraged to preview the textbook sections to be covered in lecture **before the class**. It is not necessary to have a thorough understanding of the material after a first reading, but knowing the basic facts and content should help in following the class discussion. Students are expected to read the relevant sections of the textbook to supplement the material covered in lecture.

**Classroom decorum:** Students must be on time for class and should refrain from leaving and reentering the classroom during class. If a student has a legitimate reason for arriving late to class or being excused early from class, then he or she should discuss this with the instructor before class. Phones may not be used during class and should be silent. Computers and tablets may not be used for anything other than taking notes. Students who are unable to courteously comply with these policies may be asked to leave the room.

**Disability Services for Students (DSS):** Students with a memorandum from the [Disability Services for Students (DSS)](http://studenthealth.rpi.edu/disabilityservices) office identifying accommodations for exams must [email](mailto:browne10@rpi.edu) the memorandum to the instructor by **Friday, September 12**. - ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://info.rpi.edu/sites/default/files/Rensselaer%20Handbook%20of%20Student%20Rights%20&%20Responsibilities%20%C2%BB.pdf) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams and homework must be the student’s own. A student may not change an answer on an exam or homework problem before submitting it for an appeal. In addition, no calculators, computers, tablets, open books, open notes, phones, headphones, or smartwatches may be used during exams. Talking or communicating with other students in any way during exams is also prohibited. Students may discuss homework with other students, the TA, and the instructor, but the work submitted must be the student’s own. Submissions with glaring similarities or submissions found to be copied from external sources or generative AI tools (such as ChatGPT) will violate academic integrity polices.

The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
