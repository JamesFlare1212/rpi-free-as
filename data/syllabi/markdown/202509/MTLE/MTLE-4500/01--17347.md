## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course will provide the background and a set of examples of how computational methods can be applied to design of materials with desired structure and properties. The methods will span multi-length and time scales, including continuum level modeling, molecular dynamics simulations, stochastic methods for optimization and sampling, and first-principles approaches. Lectures will be complemented by computer labs with hands-on exercises using publicly available or commercial software packages.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\) Demonstrate solid understanding of theory behind computational methods.
- 2\) Pose well-developed research questions in materials science and engineering that can be addressed through computational methods
- 3\) Use software packages to effectively design materials with desired structure and properties.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

## Homework assignments (outcome 1-3):

Homework assignments focus on simulation work and data analysis. Each problem set is designed to build upon the material covered in the preceding lectures and computer laboratorie, sand to use software packages that illustrate different theoretical and simulation approaches.

## Lab reports (outcome 3):

Short lab reports are due at the end of each lab. Labs are designed to teach students how to use software packages. Students need to run simulations to provide solutions to problems in the reports.

## Exams (outcome 1-2):

There will be a mid-term exam (90 minutes) and a final exam (110 minutes).

## Term project (outcome 2-3):

Students will be asked to complete a team term project on computational design of materials with desired structure and properties.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

The final grade will be based on: class participation (5%), lab reports (10%), homework (30%), mid-term exam (15%), final exam (20%), term project presentation (10%), term project report (10%).
