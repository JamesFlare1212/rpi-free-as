## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Identify and recall the key biological and physical systems that influence the Earth's environment
- Develop transferable skills: a. Critical Thinking: Gather, analyze, synthesize, evaluate, and apply information. b. Interpersonal: Interact collaboratively to achieve common goals. c. Quantitative and Empirical Reasoning: Apply mathematical, logical, and scientific principles and methods. d. Technology: Use appropriate technology to retrieve, manage, analyze, and present information.
- Describe the impacts of humans on Earth’s systems and resources and how diminished ecosystems impact humans.
- Apply scientific principles and methodologies to analyze ‘wicked’ environmental challenges
- Generate strategies to address environmental challenges in personal contexts and broader societal issues

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Engagement:** Active participation in class is an essential part of the learning process. You are expected to participate in class discussions and hands-on learning activities, as well as peer instruction and evaluation. You are encouraged to share your experience, perspective, questions, and analysis of text, reading materials and assignments. If you need to miss class and you let me know at least 1 hour in advance, you may be given the opportunity to make up any in-class activities. Emergencies will be accommodated.

**Homework and Reflections:** There will be a number of out-of-class assignments to complete throughout the semester. These are designed to check for preparation and understanding, generate discussion, encourage participation, and deepen comprehension of course material. When we go on a trip or have a guest speaker, these may be reflections. Depending on the assignment, these will either be due at the beginning of the next class session, or one week from assigned.

**Lab activities:** Environmental science is a field-based observational science, and as such, laboratory sessions provide an excellent opportunity to learn environmental concepts and methods through applied activities. Most of the labs will take place outside-expect to be outside even if conditions are uncomfortable and dress appropriately. Material covered in labs will appear on your exams, including plant and organism identification.

On rare occasions, illness, family emergencies, and certain school-sponsored events may make it necessary for a student to miss a lab session. You must notify me BEFORE the day of the absence in all but the most extreme emergencies. Typically, it will NOT be possible for you to make up the lab, as many of the labs are conducted off-campus. I will do my best to have an alternate assignment available, but it will not always be possible and should not be counted on.

**Exams:** In this course, there will be 2 exams. Exams will be cumulative, consisting of both new material and review of previously tested concepts. Exams will cover material from the lecture and lab, including tree and plant identification. This approach encourages continuous engagement with course content throughout the semester, rather than relying solely on memory recall from distant units.

**Research Project:** Group research project based on Ecoregions of NY. On Saturday, 11/1, we will travel to 5 eco-regions of NY as a class, and your group will design a research project. This project needs to include data collection and address one of the “wicked problems” in Environmental Science.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

Based on experience, here are some suggestions for success:

1. Read before and after class. By introducing yourself to key terms, it will make following class easier.
2. If you have a question at any point, ask it! This could be in class, during office hours, an email, slipping a note at the end of class.
3. Focus on concepts. How does information build on each other? How are concepts related to each other?
4. Make sketches as notes!
5. All PowerPoint notes will be posted after class. Instead of focusing on getting down all the words on the slide, focus on key phases, questions, and concepts. Your notes should be in our own words, not mine!
6. Don’t forget to take notes on class discussions, activities and videos

Allow yourself to have fun!
