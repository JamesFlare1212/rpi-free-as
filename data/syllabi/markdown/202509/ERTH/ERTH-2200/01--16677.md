## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Design environmental science experiments that can be statistically tested and avoid bias
- Identify which graphing and statistical methods are most appropriate for different types of data
- Perform exploratory analyses and apply statistics to draw conclusions from environmental datasets
- Assess the validity and interpretation of data presented in scientific and mainstream publications
- Exhibit proficiency in using Excel and JMP Pro for data exploration, graphing, and statistical analysis
- Demonstrate how data presentation and statistics is used as an effective means of communication

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Lecture quizzes – Lectures will be given on Tuesdays and students will complete Gradescope quizzes during or after each lecture. The quizzes are not supposed to be tricky but are designed to reinforce key points covered in the lecture and assigned textbook readings. The lowest lecture quiz grade will be dropped.  
Data labs – All data labs will be conducted in person on Fridays. Students will work in small groups to complete and submit a lab assignment that asks them to apply graphing and/or statistical methods learned in class to analyze and draw conclusions from an environmental dataset or assigned readings. Working groups will be assigned by the instructor. Each working group will turn in a single lab assignment and all members per group will be given the same grade. The lowest lab assignment grade per student will be dropped.  
Semester project – There will be a final project in which students will work individually to analyze and statistically test hypotheses using an environmental dataset of their choosing. Students are encouraged to self-select their dataset based on their research or personal interests, as they relate to environmental issues (e.g., climate change, agricultural development, urbanization, energy policy). The final project is composed of several sub-assignments that are detailed below. Written project components will be submitted via Gradescope and oral project components will be presented in person during class.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.
