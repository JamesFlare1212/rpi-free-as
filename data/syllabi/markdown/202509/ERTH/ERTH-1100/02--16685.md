## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Age and origin of the Earth, internal constituents and energy sources; how plates move, oceans develop, and mountains rise. The course aims to give a quantitative picture of the Earth’s major processes and the ways in which they interact.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Analyze mineral and rock properties to evaluate their potential as energy resources and environmental materials
- Compare the formation processes and environmental impacts of fossil fuel energy sources (coal, oil, natural gas) and renewable geothermal energy
- Apply plate tectonic theory to predict the distribution of energy resources and geological hazards
- Evaluate geological hazard risks (earthquakes, volcanoes, landslides) using scientific data and monitoring techniques
- Assess the environmental and societal trade-offs involved in energy resource extraction and use

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Pre-Class Reading:** I will often request that you complete a brief exercise prior to lecture, on your own, as a warm-up for class. This may mean reading an article, textbook chapter, or watching a video. For most pre-class activities, you will complete a short assignment on BlackBoard. These will be due at the beginning of class and available on LMS at least a week in advance.

**Mini-Assignments**: Throughout the semester there will be 12 short mini-assignments where you will have the opportunity to apply what we are learning to a problem or to dig into a topic in more depth. These will be posted on LMS at least a week in advance.

**note—there will be either a pre-class reading or mini-assignment due before every class!*

**In-class engagement:** In class, we will be utilizing a variety of techniques in the classroom to improve your learning and retention. Chiefly, this will include working independently and in small groups on activities, taking surveys to gage overall classroom understanding, and generating discussion. For some of these activities, points will be awarded for participation/completion and for some, points will be awarded for accuracy. There is no make-up for missed engagement, however there will be more than 12 throughout the semester-so missing a class due to illness/travel/etc. will not have a detrimental impact.

**Quizzes:** There will be 7 quizzes throughout the semester to check for content understanding and to ensure you are engaging in distributive studying. Each quiz will be administered at the start of class on Tuesdays. Late arrivals will not be given additional time. *One make-up will be allowed for quizzes but must be made-up within 48 hours and arranged prior to the quiz.* The dates of the quizzes are on the schedule and will not change.

**Journal Entries:** The geology journal is designed to help you be reflective about what you are learning in class and connect course material to your everyday life. You can complete up to 5 journal entries throughout the semester.

**Geologic Feature Brochure:** Create an informative travel-style brochure showcasing a geologic feature or location of your choice. You will research and explain the formation processes of a specific geologic feature, connect geologic processes to observable landscape features, communicate scientific information to a general audience, and evaluate the significance of a geologic features in human and environmental contexts. You will “sell” your location while staying scientifically accurate.

**Exams:** There will be 3 exams throughout the semester. Each exam will cover the topics preceding that exam (**not cumulative**-however, keep in mind that we will be building upon prior knowledge). There will be **no** make-up of exams without a legitimate excuse discussed **prior** to the exam (I reserve the right to determine what is “legitimate”).

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grading for this class will unfold a little differently than many of you are used to. Using what can be called a ‘gameful’ or an ‘additive’ system, **your performance in the class is based on the points that you earn**–much like in many games (some video games, table-top role-playing games, or what have you). Also like in some games, or in many popular films/TV, there’s a ‘clock’ for some assignments, defined by milestones or due dates in the semester.

**Everyone begins with 0 points.** **At the start of the semester, there are 600 points available.** As you accomplish tasks in the class, you build up points towards your final grade.

**You choose which assessments you will do to build points.** ***All assignments in the class, including exams, are on a self-selected basis***. There’s quite a variety (see below) to choose from; it is up to you to figure out which activities you will take on to build up enough points to get the ‘rank’ you want by the end of class.

***Goal of this approach to assessment******:*** What I’m looking to do here is to get you to consider a different way to assess and evaluate how to gain skills and knowledge in a class. **Think of a grading system as a ‘medium’ for your evaluation/assessment**; by using an alternate method, I want to put into relief the nature of the assessment systems you generally use. **I want you to be active in your own assessment.** This grading system is meant to encourage personal and active engagement with the material. Ranks are levels of achievement in building knowledge of geology – try to think of them as traditional grades only secondarily.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

Once upon a time, I was also a student in an Introductory Physical Geology course because it was the only science course that fit into my schedule. It was a hard course, and I had to work my butt off in that class! There was lots of new terminology and quantitative reasoning, and I often felt lost and overwhelmed. In fact, the grade I received in the course was the lowest I received in college! However, it ended up being my favorite class and resulted in me switching my major to Geology. I am not smarter than you, I’ve just been thinking about geology longer than you. You may not think you are a “science person”, but you have all the skills to be successful in this class. Plus, you have me here to help you!

Based on experience, here are some suggestions for success:

1. Read before and after class. Even if you don’t buy the text, if we are talking about igneous rocks, google igneous rocks and see what you find out. By introducing yourself to key terms, it will make following class easier.
2. If you have a question at any point, ask it! This could be in class, during office hours, an email, slipping a note at the end of class.
3. Focus on concepts. How does information build on each other? How are concepts related to each other?
4. Make sketches as notes!
5. All PowerPoint notes will be posted after class. Instead of focusing on getting down all the words on the slide, focus on key phases, questions, and concepts. Your notes should be in our own words, not mine!
6. Don’t forget to take notes on class discussions, activities and videos

Allow yourself to have fun!
