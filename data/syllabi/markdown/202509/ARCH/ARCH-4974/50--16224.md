## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Architecture of the City**

The aim of this course is to understand, analyze and represent the layering of Rome’s urban form. Historical, theoretical, and technical information will be introduced, both in class and on-site visits, to take full advantage of your experience as students at the Rome Center. Architecture of the City will foster the observational drawing skills of students with practical drawing exercises on proportion and perspective to prepare the student for further analytical diagramming of ideas. The opportunity to exercise critical thought and understand the urban system through sketches of architectural elements such as geometries, axis, thresholds, materials, volumes and/or voids, historic layering and placemaking is an important feature of the course. This course is a point of departure for further individual research and design, according to your specific goals and design interest.

The course will use the following five principles of perception (from K.Lynch, The Image of the city):

Edges as boundaries, both natural and artificial to understand the limits of the investigation.

Paths as the axial movement within a city space, pedestrian or vehicular, large, narrow, etc.

Nodes as strategic points, crossing streets and piazzas, sometimes gathering points.

Districts as typologies, height, volumes, details of buildings forming a neighborhood.

Landmarks as identity presences within the city, able to orient citizens, target points.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Course Aims:

Provide an overview of the city’s history and transformations.

Support a focus on urban form, historic layering and placemaking.

Promote the understanding architectural projects within the urban context.

Exercise critical thought by developing an argument through drawings and keynotes.

The acquisition of a deeper understanding of the city layout, its topography, and infrastructures.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand the city’s history and transformations through direct experience and representation.
- Communicate effectively through drawing and graphic layouts.
- Critically assess urban space: learning and applying the visual vocabulary and theory associated with architecture and urban planning.
- Identify building materials & methods, elements of style & structure.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- Sketchbook: each student will produce sketches and analytical hand drawings using the course sketchbook, both during the on-site visits as well as outside of class meeting times. - ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process.

**Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism**

Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust merits a failing grade in this course and the violator will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.

Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of the course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student. Any misrepresentation of work is evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student results in a failing grade for the course.
