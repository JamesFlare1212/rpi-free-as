## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This seminar introduces students to constructing systems and approaching computation through the lens of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML), with applications in architecture, urban systems, built environment, and other *complex* domains (where uncovering interdependencies is essential). The course introduces students with the ability to identify, analyze, and represent complex, often “wicked” problems—those without straightforward solutions—by leveraging data mining and AI as tools for design optimization, system modeling, and speculative design.

Students will explore how AI can be used both as a lens to analyze the underlying logic and behaviors of systems, and as a driver to generate new system logic. They will engage with existing datasets and computational models to uncover system mechanisms and feedback loops. Through a series of applied research exercises, students will construct, simulate, and evaluate systems using ML models, optimization techniques, and data-driven workflows. Emphasis will be placed on how to move from analysis to prototyping, modeling, and visual representation of systems—culminating in architectural forms, tool-based representations, or new interface environments.  
The course is graduate-level.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

# Course Introduction

This seminar introduces students to the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) methods and system modeling approaches for exploring *complex* systems, ranging from urban environments to ecological and infrastructural systems. The course emphasizes using AI not just as a tool, but as a lens through which students understand, analyze, and reimagine complex systems. No matter the system—urban, environmental, social, or infrastructural—students will be encouraged to identify and interrogate the underlying issues and optimization challenges within these systems.

A key objective is to help students use available data to decode the mechanisms and behaviors of interdependent systems, such as urban morphology or environmental networks. Through machine learning and AI methodologies, students will explore how to conceive the core logic and dynamic workflows that govern these systems or system dynamics. Students will be encouraged to construct unique perspectives by deploying AI not just to analyze systems, but to perceive the formation and reconfiguration of the systems themselves.

Students will explore how machine learning (ML) and AI approaches can help uncover and define systemic logic, structure, and workflows. By leveraging available data, students will construct informed perspectives on system behaviors, learning to identify optimization problems and morphological challenges within systems such as urban collective forms or environmental networks.

Once students develop insights or new systemic logics, they will be expected to translate these understandings into representations—through analytical tools, drawings, or model developments. They will also be guided to create interfaces or prototypes that visualize the systemic environment and dynamics. These interfaces will serve as platforms for analysis, simulation, and preliminary optimization - helping students improve system performance and test alternative scenarios.

Through assignments, students will use AI-driven tools and interfaces to model, visualize, and analyze systems. This hands-on approach includes creating representations (e.g., architectural drawings, diagrams, interactive interfaces) and developing preliminary system analyses (e.g., data analysis, optimization models, design prototypes) to optimize system performance. The seminar highlights AI as both an analytical method and an interactive medium, allowing students to prototype plausible solutions to "wicked problems" - complex, interconnected challenges resistant to conventional analytical methods.

Optional: In parallel with the system exploration through AI and data sources, the seminar maintains a theoretical and historical dimension. It traces the evolving relationship between technology, representation, and architecture - from an architect’s agency to design computation and contemporary digital humanities. Through readings in architectural theory, history of science and technology, and design criticism, students will critically assess the cultural and epistemological implications of AI and automation in shaping the built environment and design thinking.

# Description of Assignments

**Assignment 1**: Representation of An Environment

Submission Assignment 1: Each student will develop a series of Drawings/Diagrams/Analyses that describe an environment fitting their research interests; 24x24” for drawings; PDF digital submission.

**Assignment 2**: Translating An Environment into A System

The aim of this assignment is to discuss Big Data, Artificial Intelligence, Cybernetics and Information Theory in their complex relationship with systems of representation and technologies in architecture. Students will be asked to study information theory, machine learning, and A.I. in relation to architecture and built environment, crossing relationships between how architects represent an environment using computation and how organizational structures emerge in this process. The various means to process information, from optimization algorithms to Machine Learning inform how a built environment is processed and represented in computational workflows.

Submission Assignment 2: Each student will submit a 500~1000-word text describing the translation process along with drawings of the translation process; 24x24” for drawings; 8.5x11” text submission format; PDF digital submission

**Assignment 3**: Representation of A System

A series of drawings that integrate cultural criticism in terms of history/theory of representation and technologies through the implementation and understanding of an environment and the data analyzed from the environment. The format for the final assignment can be a cultural history/theory paper discussing issues of representation or technology; a scientific demonstration implementing or developing a technology of representation, robotics, fabrication, or material; or a drawing series of a computational workflow that explains the system itself.

Submission Assignment 3: Final PDF digital submission
