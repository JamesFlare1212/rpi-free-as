## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**History of Italian Design: From Origins to Contemporary Innovation**  
This course explores the evolution of Italian design from 1871 to the present, tracing its development through key movements such as Art Nouveau, Futurism, Rationalism, and Post-WWII reconstruction. A central focus will be the transition from aristocratic to bourgeois use of art and design, reflecting broader socio-political shifts in Italy. The course will also highlight the role of new materials—such as plastics, metals, and composites—in transforming Italian design, making it more accessible to the masses. Through a combination of lectures, site visits, and case studies, students will examine how design has responded to Italy’s changing political, cultural, and social landscapes. The course will emphasize the continuous dialogue between historical movements and contemporary Italian design, showing how past innovations continue to shape today’s creative practices.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to identify the main movements and key moments in Italian design history and connect them to broader socio-political and cultural changes, drawing from the works of important authors.
- Students will be able to understand how new materials and production methods have shaped the evolution of Italian design.
- Students will be able to critically reflect on the role of design in shaping both the daily lives and the collective identity of Italy.
- Students will be able to apply Italian design concepts to their personal projects, integrating historical movements and contemporary practices.
- Students will develop the skills to engage with Italian design discourse, both academically and practically, through site visits and case studies.

3. **Class work** **(35%)**

These in-class group activities are graded and constitute 25% of the course grade. In-class group activities are integrated into each lesson and are designed to reinforce students’ understanding of the course content in a collaborative and engaging way.

Each activity is based on selected materials — including readings, visual documents, video excerpts, or design-related prompts — and directly connects to the topic and historical framework discussed during the lecture.

These activities are particularly useful in the context of a condensed academic term, as they help consolidate key concepts and foster continuous engagement.

**Structure:**

- Students are divided into small groups.
- Each group analyzes the assigned material and prepares a short synthesis to present and discuss in class.
- Presentations are followed by open discussion and feedback from the instructor.

**Evaluation Criteria:**

- Clarity and depth of content analysis
- Collaboration and equal participation among group members
- Ability to contextualize the material within broader historical and design narratives
- Relevance and originality of insights

**Learning Objective:**

To strengthen critical thinking, teamwork, and the ability to connect primary and secondary sources to the development of Italian design across different historical periods.

**Student Progress Monitoring:**

Students will always be informed of their performance throughout the semester. Feedback will be provided regularly, allowing each student to monitor their progress and improve accordingly.

4. **Final Assignment – Exhibition Project at MAXXI** **(25%)**

**Format:** Individual or pair work

**Submission:** Digital (PDF or PowerPoint – 6–10 slides max.)

**Deadline:** 12/2/2025

**Note:** Project teams must be confirmed by the second week of class.

**Objective:** This final assignment invites students to engage critically with the themes explored during the course by designing a curated exhibition proposal. The goal is to translate historical and theoretical knowledge into a compelling visual and conceptual narrative. Students will take the role of curators, imagining a temporary exhibition to be ideally hosted at the MAXXI – National Museum of 21st Century Arts in Rome.

**Assignment Description:**

Each student (or pair) will select a topic from the course and develop a curated exhibition proposal that includes the following components:

- **Title and Concept:**

Define a clear and original curatorial vision. What is the central theme? What message does your exhibition aim to convey?

- **Historical Context:**

Situate your exhibition within the broader historical and cultural framework discussed in class and supported by the course readings.

- **Selection of Works:**

Choose 3–5 key design case studies (objects, interiors, designers, materials, or documents) featured in the lectures or readings. Additional research is welcome.

- **Curatorial Narrative:**

Organize your exhibition into sections or thematic zones. Explain the rationale behind your choices and layout.

- **Short Curatorial Texts:**

Write a brief introductory wall text (approx. 100–150 words) and 1–2 lines for each object or section, as if addressing a museum audience.

- **Contemporary Relevance:**

Reflect on how your exhibition topic connects to current cultural, social, or design issues. Why is it important and timely today?

- **Visual Material (optional but encouraged):**

Include sketches, sample images, floorplan suggestions, or other visual content that supports your vision.

**Suggested Topics (non-exhaustive):**

Students are encouraged to define their own exhibition theme, but may draw inspiration from the following:

- Design and national identity in post-Unification Italy
- The Liberty Style and bourgeois interiors
- Futurism and the concept of the total work of art
- Postwar domesticity and the middle-class interior
- Women designers and the redefinition of modernity
- Craft vs. industry in 20th-century Italian design
- Radical visions and experimental design movements

**Learning Goals:**

- To develop critical thinking and curatorial skills
- To synthesize knowledge across lectures, readings, and discussions
- To understand the cultural impact of design across historical periods
- To articulate connections between past and present in Italian design

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process.

**Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism**

Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust merits a failing grade in this course and the violator will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.

Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of the course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student. Any misrepresentation of work is evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student results in a failing grade for the course.
