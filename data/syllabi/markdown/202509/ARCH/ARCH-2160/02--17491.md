## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course explores a breadth of concerns within the history, theory, and critical practices of architecture and especially the representation of architectural ideas. In a series of lectures, discussion sessions, workshop assignments and a final project, we will examine the manner in which a number of key historical and contemporary figures in architecture have used different mediums to generate, explore, and convey architectural ideas. We will focus specifically upon the mediums of photography, drawings, publications, constructs and new media.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. To introduce students to the use of various media in the design and production of architecture.
2. To introduce students to ways of assessing critically in both verbal and graphic means the theories and application of various media for both the design and representation of architecture and architectural ideas.
3. To introduce students to the key ideas in the history and theory of media in architecture.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate how various media are used in the design and production of architecture.
- Assess critically in both verbal and graphic means the theories and their implications of various for both the design and representation of architecture and architectural ideas.
- Identify and discuss key ideas in the history and theory of media in architecture.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Deliverables + Grading Structure:

Deliverables will include weekly assignments related to the week’s lecture topic. These assignments will be completed by students working individually and in groups. All assignments must be completed and uploaded prior to the start of the class. This work will be reviewed onscreen and be the focus of group discussion. Weekly assignments will be provided by the instructor via email.

Grade 1: Photographic Studies + Drawing Studies (weekly assignments) = 30% of semester

Grade 2: Publication Studies + Construct Studies + New Media Studies (weekly assignments) = 30% of semester

Grade 3: Final Project = 30% of semester (*combines the content of the semester’s work into a final document)

Grade 4: 10% of your grade will be devoted to class participation

Definition of Grade Scale:

“A” indicates an unusual development of insights based on the relevant materials considered.

“B” indicates a thorough understanding of the relevant materials.

“C” indicates a relatively perfunctory engagement.

“D” and “F” indicate two degrees of insufficiency, the latter, of less than a passing level.

Plagiarism is also understood to be a serious offense that will result in the automatic failure of the studio as well as other possible disciplinary actions as described in Rensselaer’s policies on Academic Integrity.

Attendance: Consistent attendance and participation in class as scheduled is required. Attendance at the mid-term and final presentation / review is mandatory. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Intellectual integrity and credibility are the foundation of all academic work. A violation of Academic Integrity policy is, by definition, considered a flagrant offense to the educational process. It is taken seriously by students, faculty, and Rensselaer and will be addressed in an effective manner.

If found responsible for committing academic dishonesty, a student may be subject to one or both types of penalties: an academic (grade) penalty administered by the professor and/or disciplinary action through the Rensselaer judicial process described in this handbook.

Academic dishonesty is a violation of the Grounds for Disciplinary Action as described in this handbook. A student may be subject to any of the following types of disciplinary action should disciplinary action be pursued by the instructor: disciplinary warning; disciplinary probation; disciplinary suspension, expulsion and/or alternative actions as agreed on by the student and hearing officer. It should be noted that no student who allegedly commits academic dishonesty will be able to drop or change the grade option for the course in question and is not eligible to request an F examination for the course.
