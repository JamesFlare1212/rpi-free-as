## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Goals**

The Studio aims at investigating the following architectural problems:

\- Typology, modality, and organization: the idea that type and typology involve a reductive, essentialist approach to architecture, and that the notion of modality by-passes both characteristics without relinquishing the will to organization

\- Classical order and singularity: an approach to classicism in architecture where the rules of classical orders and the genius, beauty, and inscrutability of architectural singularity are extremes of a single form of thought and production

\- Models (generality through variability): the operativity of variables, variations, variabilities, ranges, thresholds, singularities in the understanding of models as regimes of differentiation, transcending the mechanistic modern paradigm

\- Architecture as medium of urbanity: the role, extent, and capabilities of architecture in the thinking and transformation of a project for the contemporary city, and the critique of the understanding of architecture as building design

\- Multiplicity and masterplan: the critique, through the idea of multiplicity, of the modern idea of masterplan as top-down hierarchical organizational system, and the revalidation of its capacity as an affirmative medium for a project for the city

\- Construction and representation: the approach to the architectural project as a medium for the abstract construction of building organizations (in a general sense), and the corresponding reevaluation of the idea of representation

\- Individual and collective authorship: the ethos of multiple ideas of authorship, by learning from the lessons of classicism and by developing systems of variable collaboration without eluding the self-reliance and responsibility of authorship

\- Consistency as systematic difference: the overcoming of the false dichotomy between coherence and freedom as conditions of architectural project, and the integration of both in regimes for the consistent management of difference

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**The City of the Global *Populus*** Design Studio

**Course Overview**

**00 The Joyful *Imperium* of Systematic Idiosyncrasies**

Neither blank and absolute generic systems, qualitatively poor and devoid of sensibility, nor anecdotal self-mystified relative contingencies, inherently lacking ever larger forms of order and ever more complex forms of thought: the vital ethics of a simultaneously serious and light-hearted design investigation (its acute attention, its revised categories, its rigorous procedures, its overflowing objects) is to carefully contain the virtues of systemic thinking while daringly overcoming its limitations. The Studio has the ambition of unfolding such ethics by constructing a joyful *imperium* (a new cultural ground) through the systematic differentiation of culturally wealthy architectural idiosyncrasies, which it will treat as forms of material organizational life.

**01 The life of architectural materials (material culture as surplus)**

Architecture is built upon material organizations. But material organizations are cultural constructs, rather than unaffected natural evidence. Culturally loaded, their internal componentry is cut across and troubled by manifold layers of knowledge and tradition that accrue in the form of language, constituting their meaning, influencing their usage, and expanding their spectrum of sense. This is why material organizations resonate and vibrate, as if they were alive, and why they cry, scream, weep, roar, or complain when they are transformed, as if they were delicate beings. Material organizations behave as if they had a propensity, a susceptibility, an inertia to protect. Their cultural life bestows them of preciousness and value, of surplus.

**02 Architectural lineages: gradations and shifts**

Simultaneously saturated of manifold layers of knowledge and forgetful of their burden, material organizations permeate across the architecture that we know and value. Master strokes pile up and decay along history, guiding the becoming of culture with constructive and destructive impulses comparable to the alternations of delicate wind blows, elusive atmospheric silences, warm-air stagnancies, histrionic storms, dramatic rainfalls, catastrophic landslides, slow-motion avalanches, or infernal earthquakes. Architectural lineages gradually unfold as they shift from peak to peak, not to indicate what is good or what is evil, but to illuminate, through poignancy, what lies as potential in the present, and to annunciate, through their bursting, what is inexorably coming.

**03 *Hypercanon*: integration, variation, differentiation, singularity, desecration**

“The (ancient) Romans were never particularly disposed to invention and creativity, but they were masters of co-option and assimilation.” Mike Duncan, The History of Rome (podcast), 038-The Catiline Conspiracy (https://thehistoryofrome.typepad.com)

What if, in this context, classical architectural projects are embraced, abstracted, varied, and integrated with one another, and if their integration is taken to extremes of differentiation? What happens with the canon when its norm, which defines the normal as we know it, is stretched beyond its limits, as if it was pliant? By configuring complex artifacts through differentiation, the creativity of the normative is forced to arise out of the conventionality of the normal, and the profane out of the sacred, constituting ever more sophisticated standards of intelligence. *Hypercanon* engenders new forms of canon through the saturation of existing ones. Its profanity disputes the canon from within, through the management of excess and redundancy.

**04 The Classical-Avant-garde trans-historical simultaneity**

Classical architecture, the architecture of the Greeks and the Romans that articulates the civilized world with its primeval sources, is charged with a Dionysian-Apollonian mask of reality that has become inscrutable to the understanding of our times. However alive and omnipresent in the background of western culture, its impetus acts as a bold, sensual, and tempestuous drive, as its perfection appears as a harmonic, unrehearsed, and stable virtue. This is why, although placidly resting behind us, as a solid ground that emanates meaningful references, classical architecture assertively advances before us, as the sharpest weapon, pulling us into the future. No believable forward-looking avant-garde and no real search for a lasting authenticity can escape from the dual condition of its magnetic spell without falling in the trap of mere innovation or dull essentialism.

**05 Oxymoronic mixtures (between and beyond)**

The decay, rottenness, breakdown, rundown, and decadence of classical architecture, its turning into ground, earth, debris, ruin, atmosphere, quasi-nature after nature, intensify the double-life of its vividness, its being alive while being dead. They have become archetypes, not because they are expressions of an original truthfulness to be restored, but because of the newly discovered (or rather, invented) origin that their self-contradictory presence involves and redeems. The ruins of Rome by Giovanni Battista Piranesi are such oxymoronic constructs. While they discover, survey, measure, and reveal, they more structurally invent, imagine, falsify, and construct. Their inventiveness turns hard science into a rigorous form of artistic creativity, a form of creativity that is not indetermined or diffuse but overtly grounded on the profound roots of culture. Bursting from within the sadness of loss, these oxymoronic inventions mix the true and the false, the real and the fake, their utter presence lying between them and beyond both. Melancholy turns into force, and virtual latency turns into absolute objects: phantom archetypes.

**06 Typology and modality**

Architectural typologies are disciplinary settlements that stabilize otherwise unstable forms of order, crystallizing them into immaterial essences, sharply distinguishable from one another, naming, classifying, and making them allegedly atemporal concepts. In turn, the variability of types is regularly regarded as contingent, accidental, inconsequential, or ornamental nuances inhabiting the margins of tolerance of those concepts. And yet, that variation precisely constitutes the vehicle through which types mutate and become other than themselves, containing and eventually surpassing their history by the elevation of those nuances into consolidated forms. The congregation of these variations in softly constrained clusters, modalities that are not simply elastic or flexible, acquire recognizability beyond the typological essences, communicating two mutually exclusive realms, typological variety and gradual variation, in a medium where one turns into the other, constituting forms consistently, before they decline into either essences or nuances.

**07 Variability in Models**

A model is a consistent set of such modalities, that is, the medium by which modalities overwhelm typologies from in-between. Constituted as systems of variable cause-effect relationships between the internal attributes of an architectural organization and external ones, the variability of models enables relationships to be systematized as constituents of a differentiating processes, rendering organizations both malleable and systematic, sturdy to various degrees, rather than rigid: powerful, rather than dogmatic, outreaching, rather than self-isolating, opening the determination of their stability to its transformation. A model is a system of control of consistency, not a doctrine, a *hypercanon* growing between and beyond the canon, in consistent relativism before the establishment of an absolute. The variability of models does neither pursue flexibility nor crystallize ideals. Neither spotless clarity nor passive adaptability, neither sound nor noise, they constitute the medium of differential consistency: white noise.

**09 The Archetype-Model**

The Archetype-Model is a systematic construct by which a multiplicity of architectural modalities participates in a consistent and variable building medium without losing their discreteness, strengthening their presence, diversifying their influence, and intensifying their effects onto the whole. The Archetype-Model operates as a bold and integrated building organization, across which a series of architectural modalities of various scales unfold and differentiate. The variables of these modalities vary in degrees according to their relative position in the model and in relation to external conditions and to other models of a similar kind. The Archetype-Model is a dynamic battlefield of interacting individuals, by which a high variety of architectural forms claim for power and fight for dominance over the others.

**10 Singular objectuality**

An Archetype-Model is a form of architecture that absorbs a multitude of components within its objectuality, enriching its singularity without compromising its boldness. Rather than instituted as a thematic proposition, the edges of which must be defined to protect coherence and disallow conceptual violations, knowledge deviations, technical spills, and performative ambiguities, its singularity is constructed as an object that contains an open disciplinary investigation, systematization, and creation, constantly driven to challenge and complexify itself: both passionate and rigorous, both affective and destructive toward its cultural materials, and driven to architectural singularity, the singular objectuality of the Archetype-Model is a form of authorship in and by itself.

**11 Indexicality and variety**

An Archetype-Model is set as a multiplicity of components (debris, amenities, services, equipment, subsystems), indexed and proliferated across an increasingly poignant and robust form. All components and modalities in an Archetype-Model vary relative to their location in the three-dimensional map of its multiple determinations, systematically configuring different individuations embedded with rules responsive to contingencies. The varieties of components negotiate with the physical presence of others of the same or different kind. Organizational frictions, collaborations, competitions, migrations, or synergies result of the intertwinement of local relationships.

**12 Singularity-Multiplicity Masterplan**

Articulated through multiple Archetype**-**Models, a Singularity-Multiplicity Masterplan for a city is not reduced to a top-down series of hierarchically structured organizations that relate to one another simply according to top-down dependences that coordinate the systems at play in a deterministic fashion. Nor is it an indeterminate or immaterial form of urbanism that struggles to resemble or evade the contingencies of everyday life, the complexity of power relations, or the unpredictability of coordination through bottom-up, scenario-driven, or picturesque situational formats. Instead, a Singularity-Multiplicity Masterplan is articulated by a manifold of idiosyncratic singularities, the complex internal relationships of which are as contingent as they are systematic.

**13 White Noise**

White Noise is the generic organization that results from the excessive accumulation of multiple idiosyncrasies, generating a highly diversified but globally homogeneous noise, with the appearance of calm and perpetuity. As the fourth generation of globalization (the post-politically correct era) looms on the horizon, we find ourselves facing a completely new form of generality, dispersed and headless, obnoxiously rarefied, that appears as a synthetic collective project, the ideology of which is still dangerously uncertain, but the ethics of which appears to overcome the false dichotomies between systems and singularities. This new ubiquitous form of generality is paradoxically composed of multiple, bellicose, and intransigent stereotypes, often insignificant, extremely delicate idiosyncrasies, made of the microscopic hyper-individualistic politics of millions obsessed with broadcasting their profile in a world now devoid of any form of authentic subjectivity. In anxious search of equality and freedom, these structurally inauthentic profiles corrupt them both, but the resulting proliferation of their hollow nuances paradoxically gives rise to a polyphonic chaotic noise, the tone of which, as impatient as serene, is provocative through sheer passive-aggressiveness. Its voice is exasperating but peaceful, its effect as aggressive as innocent. White noise is the self-fulfilled prophecy of multiplicities rendered singularities.

**14 The *Forundome***

The *Forumdome* is a singular yet multiplicitous architectural organization that instals its powerful presence, organizing, through the architectural richness of its white-noise, its immediate and mediate surroundings, and thus capable to launch the city as a new global node. The *Forumdome* is a bold, primitive, archaic architectural form, whose figure surpasses any naturalism, loading its organization with an intertwined manifold of cultural resonances, strategically indexed, superficially stamped, and systemically embedded. The *Forumdome* works simultaneously as an absolute form and an open forum, integrating the organizational richness of the latter within the iconic synthesis of the former, thus constituting an auratic amalgamation that absorbs the city within its anti-urbanity. The *Forumdome* is a generic multifarious organization and a programmatically tendentious enclave. Born in Rome, City of the Global Populus, it projects itself as an archetype of the over-touristic global city. The *Forumdome* explores the major over-touristic assets of the city of Rome: art, education, fashion, gastronomy, landscape, music, religion, waterscape, wellness, absorbing their dispersion in single architectural white-noise artifacts.

**15 Over-tourism**

Like other European cities of the globalized world, Rome has become a global touristic node. But given its singularity as an all-encompassing source of western culture, its sheer touristic wealth, its millennial cultural diversity, the extension and variegation of its ruins, its mythological food scene, its wide-ranging landscape panoramas and sceneries, its appetite for design and fashion, its tackling of well-being through the *dolce vita*, its memorable waterscapes and infrastructures, and the various material traditions around them, the over-burdened tourism generated by the wide assortment of the contemporary masses (disciplines, practices, cultures, races, ages, geographies) visiting the ancient center of the western world has become particularly extreme. Irreducible to a theme park, the local population of the over-touristic global city is being expelled out of the historical center in inexorable migration process to the surroundings.

**16 The over-tourism abstract machine**

Is it structural enough, in this context, and, above all, is it daring enough to put at work a series of tactics of survival of the local population through mitigation policies that forcefully but vainly confront over-tourism by means of confining it, with no economic return involved, like most European urban policies are currently attempting? Are we not, rather, in front of the unprecedented opportunity of embracing a new form of city and, with it, envisioning a new role for architecture, by radically intensifying and accelerating tourism at its very core by grounding and channeling over-tourism, repositioning the very concept of Rome strategically as the new model of the global city? Is this somber panorama perhaps the ultimate means contributing to its always yearned-for destiny? What is the abstract machine that, in this context, can ground, through architecture, rather than policy, such process, using its force as a new disciplinary opportunity?

**17 The Uber-soul of the lost souls**

In the over-touristic city, people behave as zombies. They frantically wander through the hysterical masses that they add up to, following the invisible mediatic forces that carry them all to the same places, to do the same things, in the same ways, at the same time. All routines and gestures are prescribed in the over-touristic city, and when gaps arise within the abstractly managed domestic multitudes, they get lost, stay stagnant, get stuck, start turning in circles, or simply shut down, until new instructions appear. In the over-touristic city, people move in small homogeneous clusters (between 02 and 40) that go through one another ignorant of anything that is not their efficiently prescribed aims (tours inside or around the given attractions, never detours from and out of them), abandoning them only as they have been consumed according to the written protocols. The over-touristic city is populated by blank, lost souls. Paradoxically, what they share is indifference, the common key to their ritual, the path of their eternal return to the same. This empty uber-soul, the new form of the metropolitan, traverses their bodies and does not fly above them, overwhelming sign of their radically secular god: *Tornos*.

**18 The collective anti-urbanism of pure interiority and no exteriority**

The pattern is clear: navigate (as if shopping), move straight to the aims, slow down as you get tired, do not get bored, turn around several times, look around, move around, gather and join groups of equals, remain stagnant and structurally indifferent, and shut down. We all participate in the multidirectional congregation with no otherness and no exteriority of the *Forumdome*: pure interior without time, like a mall, like a video arcade, like a multiplex cinema, like an *Apple Store*, like an urban church without a recognized religion. Distracted concentration is the precondition of its effectiveness, its warranty of success, its appearance of inevitability. In the *Forundome*, a multitude of modalities of classical architectural types configure together such inescapable magnetic interior.

**19 Roman Taxonomy of (Auratic) Public Interiors**

Storytelling columns, radiant colossal pyramids, and thick elongated walls; rotten aqueducts, broken arcades, and semi-collapsed vaults; outlandish block, round, and tower tombs; bridges on bridges, soaking emissariums, and sunken porticoes; hybrid mausoleums, rotondas, and temples; grandiose pieces of baths, fountainheads, and reservoirs: a series of case studies adopted from Giovanni Battista Piranesi’s inventive surveys of Ancient Rome are adopted as the material to construct *Forumdome* models. Each model, built from an architectural form saturated with debris, integrates the organization of three case study projects, opening the generality of the architectural type into a formally rich and organizationally profuse Archetype-Model.

**20 The (Atemporal) City of the (Captive) Global *Populus***

*Tornos*, God of the eternal return, turns people around Rome and holds them inside a single timeless enclosure, from which they cannot (and do not want) to escape. Rome is the (atemporal) city of the (captive) global *populus*. The critical mass of its cultural paraphernalia creates the feeling that there is nowhere else to go, that everything outside is trickery, that the world was turned inside out and the illusion lies beyond the Aurelian Walls, and that the city is the clear evidence that reveals the very real, the only real, the non-beyond, the unique here-and-now, looping over and over in eternal return. Rome is driven and compelled to assume this inevitable destiny for which it seems to have been created, and for which it has persistently endured, self-overwhelmed, over millennia. Its challenge is now to incarnate its new fortune and turn it into the new form of global city: the (Atemporal) City of the (Captive) Global *Populus*.

**21 *Umbilicus Urbis* of a Multi-centric World: “All roads lead (again) to Rome”**

The City of the Global *Populus* is the new state of the generic city: the global city that achieves ubiquity through uniqueness, because everybody wants to be there. It is the forever-land, not because it is everywhere, the non-place dystopia, but because its place has become the inescapable order of reality, *logos*. Unlike its generic predecessor of the millennium, which looks and works like an airport (blank, clean, white, insipid, qualityless, homogeneous, and neutral), the City of the Global *Populus* looks and works like a desecrated quasi-religious multiplicity, a complex of building organizations, of newborn residues (both remains and reminders), of supreme cultural artifacts rendered everyday objects. It is a city of ruins-in-advance that both takes over nature and is taken over by nature: the umbilical down-to-earth center of a multi-centric world.

**22 The anti-urban collective as entertainment infrastructure**

The City of the Global *Populus* II, the *Forundome*, is an anti-urban collective made of rich and saturated public interiors, formally sophisticated and programmatically poignant, a sacred yet profane venue. Its anti-urban collective works as an erratic set of indiscriminate but systematic accumulations formally crisscrossed, tensed with one another, and integrated in a singular architectural forms taking over any exterior public space: idiosyncratic over-touristic interventions, the singularity of which is constituted by pools of amenities and entertainment-related programs, urban equipment and public spaces, and architectural debris and ruins distributed across its surfaces, positioned within its kinks and intersections, and sheltered by their auratic envelopes.

**23 The architectural set of signatures of an eternally undecipherable hieroglyph**

The architecture of the City of the Global *Populus* II results of the infinite resonances of its unique archetype-models radiating into one another. The *Forundome* architecture of the City of the Global *Populus* II is made of singular white-noise architectural models (agglomerations of classical architectural master strokes) that together configure a singularity-based multiplicity. Its figure-figure-without-a-canvas model inspires (or perspires) without projecting a coherent meaning but many undecipherable messages. The *Forundome* is the signature of the trans-historical hieroglyph of the City of the Global *Populus*, the idiosyncratic writings that refuse to release the clues to its enigma.

**Site (Material)**

The historic city of Rome, more specifically the urban territory inside the Aurelian Walls, the Campo Marzio area is both the site of the project and its source of materials of study and transformation. Following the precedence of the two major mid XVIII century scientific surveys yet radically inventive projects by Giambattista Nolli and Giovanni Battista Piranesi, the *Roma Intempestus Urbis* City of the Global *Populus* II The *Forumdome* design research proposal aims at boldly ordering the medieval fabric through the sheer presence of intrinsically architectural (anti-urban) archetypes of over-touristic infrastructure and amenities that absorb and integrate the public realm and the vividness of its routines within the private domain and the artificiality of its benefits. The *Forumdome* is constructed through the development of a series of models out of the abstraction, idealization, integration, and variation of Piranesi’s influential inventions, understood as modalities of differentiation of daring ancient urban forms that, transformed and loaded with minute debris and amenities, configure newly enriched forms of anti-urban architecture.

**Program (Venue)**

The Singularity-Multiplicity Masterplan is constituted by highly enriched models of building organization that ground the configuration of novel anti-urban over-touristic archetypes, constituted by various types of touristic amenities and equipment, vertical, horizontal, and diagonal circulation systems, common services and common areas, highly commercially and economically driven areas, and privately-owned public spaces, all of them directed to a promiscuous mixture of public and private domains. Framed within the phenomenon of over-tourism and its allegedly harmful impact on contemporary European cities, the Studio is challenged to revert that assumption by constituting a new, generic yet singular notion of the global city, irreducible to neutral and repetitive standards. New over-touristic forms are grounded on the organization of classical building forms: the column, the pyramid, the wall, the aqueduct, the arcade, the vault, the (block, round, and tower) tomb, the bridge, the emissarium, the portico, the mausoleum, the rotonda, the temple, the bath, the fountainhead, and the reservoir. Their highly condensed, convoluted, peculiar, influential, mystical, and quasi-religious idiosyncrasy of which is regarded as having the potential to swallow the urban within the architectural object, turning its extraordinarily culturally loaded anti-urbanity, in turn, into the ultimate device of urbanity.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 01 Research. 01a_Ability to use/develop specialized knowledge. Students live in Rome throughout the semester. Through the direct interaction with canonical architectural pieces, the involvement with urban conditions and phenomena, and the fluid access to museums, artwork, and bibliographical resources in the city, students augment their disciplinary baggage and construct an ability to attentively observe and document multiple forms and scales of knowledge about buildings, cultural artifacts and sites that ground the architectural procedure of the project, nurture its design techniques, and substantiate the decision-making process. 01b_Ability to use / develop broad and integrative knowledge. Students live in Rome throughout the semester, engaging in a culturally immersive process that, aside primarily consisting of regular visits to Renaissance and Baroque buildings in the city center and of participating in real time in the cultural life of the city in the context of its omnipresent history, is fostered by a series of field trips to the outskirts of the city, to Tuscany, and to Sicily, providing a rich and multilayered context of historical and cultural significance to the architectural projects, which broadens the understanding and thinking both of their causes and of their consequences.
- 02 Intellectual design skills. 02a_Ability to design. Students are conceptually and technically nurtured to develop an ability to ground design decisions on a hands-on operative approach to architecture as material. This approach is articulated by studying and literally using the organizational knowledge embedded in existing buildings as sources of design intelligence. 02b_Ability to design using information resources. Students construct architectural models from the integration of visual resources, formal descriptions, and organizational data, programming operative models that are built upon existing case-study buildings in Rome, thus developing a process of architectural abstraction that turns information resources into form, and vice versa. 02c_Ability to design engaging diverse perspectives. Students are trained to produce projects as a synthesis of various viewpoints that are progressively integrated in a single artifact by means of an iterative design process that goes from individual work to teamwork, to all-Studio work. These viewpoints incarnate multiple perspectives: cultural, commercial, civic, and urban. 02d_Ability to design with ethical reasoning. Students are confronted with the contemporary urban problem of overtourism (at work, particularly, in major European cities like Rome), and are challenged to think ingenious solutions for the overloading of historic centers with touristic infrastructure and the overwhelming ephemeral population that brings radical changes into the social fabric. 02e_Ability to design with quantitative fluency. Students are taught to construct, program, control, and manage complex abstract architectural models: systems of architectural organization that determine form and space through the control of quantifiable variations of variables and relationships between them, learning to go from quantifiable data into form, and vice versa. 02f_Ability to design with communicative fluency. Students are challenged to fluidly communicate with (and be influenced by) different interlocutors at different stages of the course, from local professors of various backgrounds to student colleagues of other universities in the Rome Center, to guest professors that participate in reviews and the lecture series program.
- 03 Collaboration. 03a_Ability to collaborate with team. Students develop the ability to work in three-student teams, learning and using collaborative methods based on design integration through digital modelling, and supported by interpersonal skills. After an initial individual phase (framed by architectural typologies associated to student teams), an intermediate phase involves the development of teamwork strategies grounded on the sharing of acquired knowledge, the synergetic intertwinement of design techniques, the project ideation through conceptual and organizational integration, and a management and workflow ethics that fluctuates between the differentiation and the integration of tasks. A final stage integrates the lineages of teamwork development into a single all-Studio proposition for central Rome. The change of roles along the process provides of evidence of different strengths of the individual students, who document the process in a portfolio that allows its clear understanding. 03b_Ability to collaborate with a community partner. Students develop the ability to feed the work with the influence of potential community partners and users that are surveyed from the local community and from disparate global sources and cultures by registering their behavioral patterns. These patterns are embedded in the project through the recognition of habits and rituals, and the abstraction of forms, typologies, and material traditions. The community, understood in a non-paternalistic materialistic sense, contributes, with these patterns, to the strategic thinking, the programming, and the organizational enrichment of the projects.
- 04 Civic and global engagement. 04a_Ability to engage the civic realm. Students demonstrate the ability to engage with an urgent issue of public interest: the overpopulation of the historic center of the city of Rome due to the rapidly growing influx of global tourists, from which they are being expelled due to the increase in the living cost and the changes in the real estate market, while becoming economically dependent from it, and the transformation of the historic center into a new form of generic city: the singular city. Students develop models to accommodate local conditions within global ones, rather than disappointingly resisting them. 04b_Ability to engage the global realm. Students demonstrate the ability to engage with the positive aspects present in the rapid growth and transformation that global overtourism is bringing into the historic center of the city of Rome, rethinking and expanding the capabilities of typologies (piazzas, courtyards, atriums, naves, facades, and landmarks) to absorb public space within architecture and integrate communities that appear to be, in principle, in conflict. Students develop models to accommodate local conditions within global ones and use their synergy to think the current state of the global city.
- 05 Design execution. 05a_Midterm Review. Students demonstrate the ability to implement the learning outcomes 01/02/03/04 in a clear, complete, sophisticated, and compelling way through a public presentation to guest jurors and an intermediate submission of the production, including: a portfolio presentation and submission containing organizational descriptions, photographic surveys, source material, digital models, and conventional orthographic drawings (plans, elevations, axonometries, perspectives). 05b_Final Review. Students demonstrate the ability to implement the learning outcomes 01/02/03/04 in a clear, complete, sophisticated, and compelling way through a public presentation to guest jurors and a final submission of the production, including: a book-like portfolio presentation and submission containing front and back matter, organizational descriptions, photographic surveys, source material, digital models, urban diagrams, evaluation drawings, conventional orthographic drawings (plans, elevations, axonometries, perspectives).

**Grades**

Grades are established according to the following policy:

A (EXCELLENT) Exceptional performance strongly exceeding the requirements of the course, showing strong academic initiative and independent resourcefulness.

B (GOOD) Performance above the norm; accurate and complete; beyond the minimum requirements of the course; work demonstrates marked progress and initiative.

C (AVERAGE) Satisfactory work that adequately meets minimum requirements and demonstrates satisfactory comprehension, communication skills, and effort; demonstrates little initiative to investigate the problem without substantial input from the instructor; work shows little improvement.

D (INFERIOR) Unsatisfactorily meets minimum requirements; demonstrates minimum comprehension, communication skills, and effort at an inferior level; initiative lacking; improvement not noticeable. (D grades not available to graduate students).

F (FAIL) Does not meet minimum requirements; fails to adequately demonstrate comprehension, communication skills, and effort.

**Peer Grading**

Each student is required to submit a weekly peer grade to the Studio professor. The grade is scored on a scale between 0 and 10, accompanied by an optional one-sentence written description summarizing the performance of his/her two teammates that week. Peer grades are due by 08:00pm on Thursday every week via email to the Studio professor. Faculty does not send reminders for submitting peer grades.

**Self-assessment**

As part of the assessment of the learning outcomes, students are required to complete a Student Self-Assessment Survey prior to receiving a grade for the semester. At the end of the semester, students are provided with a link to the survey.

**Pin-ups**

Students are periodically required to present their work in a formal review process. Presentations are a critical part of the architectural education. They are opportunities to judge the project and critically evaluate the design process. It may be appropriate at points in the semester to evaluate only selected projects to convey information relevant to the entire class. In this situation, it is important for each student to witness and participate in the review process. Even if his/her project is not reviewed, the criticism and discussions inform the evaluation of his/her progress and design. Failure to attend one or more pin-ups for the full class period may result in a grade deduction.

**Desk Critiques**

The Studio environment involves two-way communication. The role of the Studio professor is to evolve and strengthen the student’s design process. Every time the student meets with the faculty, he/she must have new iterations of work based on previous conversations. If the student has not produced new work, the faculty reserves the right to skip the critique and continue with other students who have produced new material.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process.

**Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism**

Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust merits a failing grade in this course and the violator will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.

Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of the course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student. Any misrepresentation of work is evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student results in a failing grade for the course.
