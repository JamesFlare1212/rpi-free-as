### Prerequisites or Other Requirements:

Prerequisites or Other Requirements

Corequisite: Students in ARCH 4820 Comprehensive Design Studio 1 are required to co-register for this class, ARCH 4540.

PP1 is a co-requisite for students taking ARCH-4820 Comprehensive Design Studio 1 (CDS1 or “CD1”) before graduating. The CDS1 course attempts to realize the design process from programmatic and contextual understanding on a specified site through the schematic design phase in preparation for design development (explored in CDS2), integrating many aspects that lead to actual construction. Students must be concerned with laws, codes and regulations governing construction at an actual site, as well as building systems including structures, material assemblies and services, and how these systems inform design decisions. Students will develop the discipline to design a code-compliant building and develop compliance documents for all or major portions of the work.

Because students are expected to take the material from the PP1 course and use it to inform studio project development, ARCH-4820 and ARCH-4540 are conceived as complementary to each other, and ARCH-4830 students will continue the benefit of this instruction. Students who are not Architecture majors may take PP1 without also taking the CDS1 Studio. While they may not benefit from immediate application of the course material in a design setting, they will acquire a general and logical understanding of how codes, sustainability, costs and conduct inform the design process.

## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

For undergraduate students: An introduction to architectural practice as related to accomplishing design projects. An overview of professional obligations, registration and conduct, architects’ roles in project delivery, and office organization and management for delivering professional services. In-depth examination of architects’ responsibilities for health, safety, and welfare in design; building code requirements for fire protection, life safety, and accessibility; economics of building systems and assemblies; design and construction contracts; and design documentation.

Professional Practice I (Pro Prac 1 or PP1 for short) explores how architects realize design projects—taking them from conception through development, documentation, and construction and the collateral efforts beyond pure conceptual and schematic design, essential for successful projects. This requires investigation and proving an understanding of/ability to navigate legal, code/regulatory, professional, contractual, and stakeholder & marketplace environments, within which designs become the built environment. The course will also require students to bring direct and tangentially-related field experiences to the class for comparison, analysis and integration with the semester’s work.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Regulatory Context (NAAB SC.3): students will understand the fundamental principles of life safety, land use, and current laws and regulations that apply to buildings and sites in the United States, and the evaluative process architects use to comply with those laws and regulations as part of a project.
- 2\. Technical Knowledge (NAAB SC.4): students will understand the established and emerging systems, technologies, and assemblies of building construction, and the methods and criteria architects use to assess those technologies against the design, economics, and performance objectives of projects.
- 3\. Design Synthesis (NAAB SC.5): students will develop the ability to make design decisions within architectural projects while demonstrating synthesis of user requirements, regulatory requirements, site conditions, and accessible design, and consideration of the measurable environmental impacts of their design decisions.
- 4\. Building Integration (NAAB SC.6): students will develop the ability to make design decisions within architectural projects while demonstrating integration of building envelope systems and assemblies, structural systems, environmental control systems, life safety systems, and the measurable outcomes of building performance.
- 5\. Health, Safety and Welfare in the Built Environment (NAAB SC.1): students will understand the impact of the built environment on human health, safety, and welfare at multiple scales, from buildings to cities.
- 6\. Professional Practice (NAAB SC.2): students will understand professional ethics, the regulatory requirements, the fundamental business processes relevant to architecture practice in the United States, and the forces influencing change in these subjects.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance in person is required unless otherwise arranged ahead of class. IF there is a need to switch to hybrid or fully remote classes, we will pivot to those media as required by the Institute in the interest of all on-campus health. LMS may also be used to gauge access time by those students not attending class in person. As mentioned in both the syllabus and the first lecture of Introduction, this counts for 20% of your grade. This translates to roughly 1 point for each lecture, field trip (even though they may be virtual this semester) or desk crit session.

Since topics presented in this class require extensive explanation and discussion of the subtleties of Professional Practice I, **attendance is mandatory, especially for quizzes**. Absences from class WILL likely affect your grade negatively. Students who cannot attend class are expected to communicate via email with the Instructor before class begins. Students who cannot attend class are expected to review lecture slides posted in LMS, read or view material as instructed by faculty, and submit a minimum of one page of notes, comments and questions to make up for attendance; they are responsible for completing any work that may be missed. If a quiz or workshop date is missed by a student, the student is responsible for scheduling the quiz/workshop with the Instructor. For make-up quizzes, the student may be given a paper quiz in lieu of online LMS quiz. Medical excuses are only required for absences longer than one week. Students who adhere to these policies will not be penalized per this attendance policy. Students are responsible for preparing themselves with the necessary texts and handouts for each class and be prepared for pop quizzes. If any school-wide lectures fall on the evening of a class, class attendance is optional if attending the school-wide lecture, but students will still be responsible for obtaining the lecture information from the professor or another student. **The class may be required to attend the school-wide lecture in lieu of having PP1 class lecture, at which attendance will count for the substituted class.**
