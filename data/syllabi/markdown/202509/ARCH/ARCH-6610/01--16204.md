## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This studio is guided by four central questions that frame architecture as a critical agent in shaping the future of the academy. Taken together these questions invite students to think critically and expansively about adaptability, community, construction and material innovation, and spatial identity. As provocations, these questions are points of departure for design inquiry that will unfold across collaborative and individual phases of work. As a collective, the questions ask how architecture can imagine the future of learning, reinforce and extend the social and cultural role of the campus, engage with advanced forms of renewable construction, and negotiate between object, field, and in-between conditions to redefine the fabric of academic and civic life.

This studio investigates the design of three interconnected programs—an Innovation Center, a Social Commons, and an Academic Forum—on a central site within the historic campus of Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute. Launching the process in collaborative groups (workgroups), students will approach the site as a limited master plan, identifying key questions of placement, adjacency, and the design of open space at the campus core. In the second phase, students will move toward individual authorship of each building while maintaining a shared framework of spatial, social, and material ambitions.

**DESING QUESTIONS: Q1**  
What is an adaptable architecture for the future of the academy, promoting rich social engagement as a platform for future learning—open, flexible, and capable of meeting both present and future demands?

**Q2**  
*What is an architecture that strengthens the experience of campus life while extending and reinforcing connections to the local and global communities it engages?*

**Q3**  
*What is an architecture of advanced renewable construction—timber, upcycled concrete, and recycled steel—as a defining expression of 21st-century building?*

**Q4**  
*How can architecture operate as a discrete identity, as a field condition, or as something in-between—redefining the spatial fabric of the campus and recasting the social sphere in unexpected ways?*

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Ability to design and develop an adaptable architecture for the future of the academy, promoting rich social engagement as a platform for future learning—open, flexible, and capable of meeting both present and future demands.
- Ability to design and develop an architecture that strengthens the experience of campus life while extending and reinforcing connections to the local and global communities it engages.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
