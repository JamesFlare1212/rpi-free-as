## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The focus of this studio is the design and programming of a civic building in an urban setting. In this course, “students develop the ability to make design decisions within an architectural project while demonstrating synthesis of user requirements, regulatory requirements, site conditions, and accessible design, and consideration of the measurable environmental impacts of their design decisions.” NAAB (SC.5 Design Synthesis). This course is required of all fourth-year architecture undergraduates in the B.Arch. Program. Any student receiving a second ‘D’ in any design studio must repeat the second design studio and receive a grade of ‘C’ or higher to proceed to the next design studio.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**TOPICAL ISSUES IN COMPREHENSIVE DESIGN STUDIO 1**

Comprehensive design includes, but is not limited to, the following cross referencing topics:

**Architectural Design Development**

Bulk Mass

What are the dimensional characteristics of the building?

What is the perceived mass / formal character of the building? What is the volume?

How do these actual characteristics leverage projective concepts and practices?

What is the relationship between bulk / mass and program / performance?

Materials

What materials can be used to achieve the desired effect with technical integrity, and economic feasibility, and how can they be effectively detailed to achieve that result?

What material colors and finishes are most effective in achieving desired effects?

How are materials combined to achieve intended effects, and how can that be effectively studied and represented in the design development phase?

Illumination: Natural, Artificial and in Dialogue

How are day-lighting, building geometry, materials, finish and color interrelated?

How is artificial lighting considered in the design, and what is the appearance of the building at night vs. in the day?

How do lighting and color relate?

**Program And Stakeholders**

Program

What programmatic standards, ratios, and planning strategies exist for the building type?

How does your building relate to/measure against these standards?

What are the programmatic requirements, ranging from overall intent to specific spaces, and their detailed requirements?

Are there strategic organizations and/or systems that underlie the organization of these programs?

Can a programmatic strategy be calibrated according to the architectural concepts established for this project – and can it contribute to the architectural concept?

Life-Safety: Regulation And Codes

What ordinances, regulatory agencies, and codes apply?

How is the building design impacted by laws and agencies?

How can these constraints be re-framed as opportunities?

How can circulation be strategically and tactically organized in response to code?

Accessibility / Universal Access

What are the first principles behind universal access?

How to address accessibility from the curb to all spaces in the project?

**Environment And Site**

Site Design And Development

What are the germane site issues relative to the making of form in a particular landscape, and how does the intervention operate within the larger landscape system and place?

What are the infrastructural features associated with the design and how will you include them as an integral part of the architectural scheme?

What site materials and detailing (surface, plantings, etc.) are most appropriate and effective for the site, location, and design?

How are flood resiliency issues handled?

Energy Performance And Human Comfort

What is the local climate and its multi-scalar impact on design?

How can the building be naturally heated, cooled, and ventilated, and what technologies / systems are needed as a supplement? To what degree can the building design rely on passive / active strategies with respect to day lighting and energy systems?

What are the spatial/material implications of the environmental systems?

How can solar energy and light best be harnessed and/or shielded?

What ventilation strategies best suit the project?

**Construction Systems And Building Physics**

Structural Systems

What is the appropriate structural approach for the project to address gravity and lateral loads?

How does the building touch the ground?

What materials and systems should be used, and why?

How do the structural systems integrate with other building systems in support of the architectural concepts?

Building Envelope

How does the building enclose and affect program and social activities within?

What is the perception of the building from multiple perspectives (night/day)?

How is the enclosure operating as an integrated system? How does it perform?

How is the building day-lighted? Does natural light reveal or impact architectural form?

Does the fenestration respond to orientation and/or program?

How do the various choices affect the aesthetic qualities of the building?

How do issues of heat loss/gain affect the characteristics of the enclosure?

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is required at all class meetings during the semester. Students are expected to arrive prepared and to use the class period for development of their projects. Two (2) unexcused absences from class will result in the lowering of the grade by one (1) letter. Four (4) unexcused absences from class during the term result in an automatic F. Attendance is mandatory for milestone reviews, including the midterm review, penultimate review, and final review as well as other all-class pinups. Failure to present at milestone reviews will result in an automatic F.

Students must legitimate excuses for missing class, late arrival to class, and late hand-in of an assignment. The legitimate excuses are: illness (which must be accompanied by an authorized excuse), serious accident or injury, family emergency, religious obligation, or participation in an off-campus intercollegiate athletic competition (this event must be preceded by a written excuse from your coach).

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process. Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust will merit a failing grade in this course and the violator(s) will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.
