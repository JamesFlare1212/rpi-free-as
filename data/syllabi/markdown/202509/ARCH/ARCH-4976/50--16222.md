## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

INTO TO HISTORIC PRESERVATION  
The course aims at introducing historical and theoretical issues of architectural preservation in Europe and, more specifically, in Italy. Three main topics will be addressed:  
• history and theory of restoration, with lectures and visits to sites of specific interest.  
• basic principles of architectural conservation and analysis of thematic issues in reference to case studies.  
• methodology in the study and praxis of preservation applied to modern architecture.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • have a basic knowledge of historic preservation theories.
- • deeply understand architectural design principles in historical architecture.
- • recognize specific issues related to historic preservation.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- In the first half of the Course, students will be also required to deliver a short essay and a Pechakucha analyzing several case-studies of restoration, to be presented in class.
- A second part will be more practical and developed as an individual project. Students will analyze first the degradation of a selected historic building through a direct survey and then apply preservation principles of design.

# EVALUATION CRITERIA

The mid-term and final presentation will be graded based on the following evaluation criteria:

- Analytic understanding of the degradation problems
- Synthetic understanding of the restoration issues.
- Coherent project between innovative transformation and preservation.
- Quality of drawings and coherence of written, spoken, and graphic communication.
- Description of the project using multiple scales.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process.

**Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism**

Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust merits a failing grade in this course and the violator will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.

Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of the course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student. Any misrepresentation of work is evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student results in a failing grade for the course.
