## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This Study Abroad program enhances students’ experience in Rome with a focus on the practical and hands-on use of the language in everyday situations. Through a communicative approach, students can engage with the city of Rome and the local community and put into practice linguistic and cultural components. The program introduces students to Art in Rome, History, Made-in-Italy, Cinema, and Food and aims at immersing students in the city’s culture and society.

The course will be a combination of language classes and lectures on Italian culture with some hands-on activities.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- ● Basic information in Italian about themselves, their interests, likes and dislikes.
- ● Simple conversation on everyday topics (order in a bar or restaurant, book a room in a hotel, ask for or give street directions), use and understand essential vocabulary related to everyday life (e.g. days of the week, colors, numbers, time, food etc.);
- ● The cultural background of the Italian country.
- ● The real understanding of Italian culture, of its variety and complexity, away from any stereotyped image.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- provide basic information in Italian about themselves, their interests, likes and dislikes.
- participate in a simple conversation on everyday topics (order in a bar or restaurant, book a room in a hotel, ask for or give street directions), use and understand essential vocabulary related to everyday life (e.g. days of the week, colors, numbers, time, food etc.);
- become familiar with the cultural background of the country and reflect on the different topics proposed in the course.
- have a real understanding of Italian culture, of its variety and complexity, away from any stereotyped image.

### Attendance Policy:

Students have the responsibility to attend classes and to actively engage in all learning assignments or opportunities provided in their classes. There may be times, however, when illness, family crisis, or university-related activities make full attendance or participation impossible. In these situations, students are responsible for making timely arrangements with the instructor to make up for the work missed. Examples of justified absences include those resulting from the following: illness of the student, serious illness or death of a member of the student’s immediate family, or other family crisis. The instructor has the right to request appropriate documentation from students for any absence for which they wish to be excused.

As far as unjustified absences are concerned, students may miss up to a maximum of 3 contact hours. After the third contact hour is missed, students will be formally notified by the instructor. From the fourth contact hour missed, the final grade may be affected at the instructor’s discretion. Courses meeting once a week have 3 contact hours per class, therefore students may miss only one class without major consequences.

Every contact hour missed will affect your attendance percentage of 5%.

The syllabus may change due to impediments with partner institutions, guest lecturers etc. Changes will be notified via email and in the announcement section.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process.

**Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism**

Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust merits a failing grade in this course and the violator will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.

Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of the course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student. Any misrepresentation of work is evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student results in a failing grade for the course.
