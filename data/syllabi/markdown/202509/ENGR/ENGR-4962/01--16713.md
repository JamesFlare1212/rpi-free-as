## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is designed to prepare engineering students for success in professional environments by building essential workplace skills. Students will develop and refine their communication abilities, strengthen professional interactions, explore ethical decision-making, and craft effective career planning strategies. Through interactive modules and experiential learning, students will gain confidence in public speaking, enhance written and verbal communication, and master practical tools for navigating the workplace with professionalism and integrity.  
This course is open to Undergraduate and Graduate students.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate effective written and verbal communication appropriate for professional engineering contexts, including emails, presentations, and workplace interactions.
- Apply ethical reasoning and professional standards to analyze workplace scenarios and recommend appropriate courses of action.
- Design a personalized career development plan that incorporates goal-setting, networking strategies, and preparation for interviews or internship opportunities.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Regular and Timely Attendance**

Students are expected to attend every class session, arrive on time, and come fully prepared. Attendance will be recorded at each meeting, and you will lose points if you miss more than one class without an excused absence. Habitual lateness will also result in a loss of credit, as consistent punctuality is an essential part of professional behavior. Excused absences require appropriate documentation and must be reported to the instructor in a timely manner. If you miss a class, you are responsible for completing any missed assignments and obtaining lecture notes and discussion summaries from a classmate.

### Other Course Policies:

**Participation**

Students are expected to come to class having read assigned materials and prepared to discuss the content and engage in planned activities. Meaningful participation includes contributing to discussions, collaborating effectively in group work, asking thoughtful questions, and interacting with peers and the instructor in a respectful, professional manner. Consistent participation is essential to your success; failure to contribute regularly, even if you attend class, will negatively affect your grade.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic integrity is essential. As defined in the *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities*, academic dishonesty includes, but is not limited to, cheating, plagiarism, and misrepresentation of work. You are responsible for reviewing and understanding these definitions.

In this class:

- All work submitted for a grade must be your own.
- First violation of academic integrity (e.g., plagiarism or cheating) will result in a zero on the assignment and a report to the Dean of Students.
- A second violation will result in a final grade of F for the course.
