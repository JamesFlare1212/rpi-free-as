## Course Goals

### Goals:

The objectives of this course are to enable the student to analyze external and internal force systems acting on particles and rigid bodies in static equilibrium. The student shall be able to “model” engineering systems by making simplifying assumptions, developing “free-body” diagrams of the model, applying the conditions for equilibrium, and utilizing vector and linear algebra methods in their solution. The student shall learn to present problem solutions in a well-organized, neat, and professional manner.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- An ability to solve systems of linear equations and use vector algebra to solve static equilibrium problems.
- An ability to apply the principles of static equilibrium to solve 2-D and 3-D problems of particles and rigid bodies.
- An ability to analyze the equilibrium of truss, frame, and machine 2-D structures.
- An ability to solve static equilibrium problems involving frictional forces.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is expected for each class meeting. A grade of zero will be given for each missed in-class assignment without a valid excuse.

### Other Course Policies:

The course is delivered in a “studio” format which will allow students to interact with the instructor, teaching assistant, and each other during in-class assignments. These assignments are graded and, hence, attendance is expected for each class meeting. There will be no make-up for problems assigned during a class session. A homework is assigned for each lecture. Any missing homework or in-class assignment without a valid excuse will receive a grade of zero. The two lowest homework grades and the four lowest in-class assignment grades will be excluded from the final grade calculation.

Students’ progress and learning is assessed by class attendance and participation, in-class assignments, homework, three exams, and a comprehensive final exam.

If there is a legitimate reason for missing an original examination, a student may take a make-up exam, provided a valid excuse is presented to the professor. However, missed make-up exams cannot be rescheduled under any circumstances.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

In this course, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. Note however that collaboration on in-class assignments and *homeworks* is permitted and encouraged. Seeking help from other sources (e.g., Tas, ALAC, etc.) is also encouraged. However, the work you submit must be your own and simply transcribing another person’s homework does not qualify as collaborative work, is not acceptable and will result in a zero grade for the homework assignment in question.

In cases of teamwork or where help was received, a notation on the assignment should indicate whom you collaborated with. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a grade of zero for that particular assignment. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

*Exams:* Soliciting help of any type from any source during an exam is prohibited. This includes collaboration, verbal or written exchange of information, copying, etc. The use of email (including IM) or the internet during exams for any purpose is strictly prohibited. All parties involved in prohibited activities will receive an academic penalty, which will range from a numerical grade of zero for the exam to a failing grade in the course. Submission of an exam for re-grading after having changed an answer will result in a similar academic penalty. All incidents will be reported to the Dean of Students.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

Actively solving practice problems during in-class and homework assignments is essential. You should also consolidate the learned skills by practicing other problems on your own or during review and tutoring sessions (such as the ones organized by ALAC).
