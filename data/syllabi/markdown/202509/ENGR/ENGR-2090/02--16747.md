## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

An integrated development of modeling-and problem-solving techniques for particles and rigid bodies emphasizing the use of free-body diagrams, vector algebra, and computer simulation. Topics covered include the kinematics and kinetics of translational, rotational, and general plane motion, energy and momentum methods.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- LO-1-The student should master the basic principles of particle and rigid body dynamics
- LO-2-The student should be able to solve the mathematical equations that govern these systems, and physically interpret the results
- LO-3-The student should master vector algebra at a high level.
- LO-4-Develop intuition about the time-dependent nature of dynamic systems
- LO-5-The student should gain physical insights into basic principles with in-class problem solving, and computer experiments (e.g. MATLAB).

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. If it would be unacceptable and rude behavior in the workplace, it is likewise unacceptable and rude in the classroom,

You may not be allowed to take the next quiz (thus earning the grade of zero) as penalty for any of the following: (1) obviously inattentive class activities such as sleeping, (2) being late for class, (3) doing other unrelated things such as homework for another course, (4) leaving early, (5) gaming the system by only attending the quizzes by just coming to alternate classes, (6) **texting and other non-essential use of phones.** You are free at any time to step out of class and text as much as you wish, or likewise to go to the restroom, get a coffee, etc.
