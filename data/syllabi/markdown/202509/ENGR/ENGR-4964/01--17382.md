## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course offers a unique opportunity to explore how engineering principles can be applied in innovative ways, equipping you to excel in internships, co-ops, and full-time roles across both technical and non-traditional career paths. Guided by experienced industry alumni and professionals as instructors, and through hands-on case studies and projects inspired by real-world challenges, the course provides insights into fields like banking, consulting, and entrepreneurship, helping you build skills and confidence for diverse industries.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate the ability to identify, model, analyze, and solve challenging, real-life problems in any circumstance or industry
- Demonstrate the ability to perform effectively on diverse teams, both as leader and contributor.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Assessment and Grading**

The following are the components in the assessment of your performance, along with percent of total grade:

- **Introduction Activity:** Professional introduction and draft job application materials, highlighting student's career expectations and background **(****10% of the grade****)**
  - o Draft Resume/Cover Letter
  - o Draft LinkedIn Profile
  - o Draft Self-Assessment (to be introduced during the first meeting)
- **Segment Assignments:** There will be course segments dedicated to different areas/careers/industries, taught by different instructors, each culminating in an assignment (done in a team), usually consisting of a written report and a presentation **(****15% each if 4 segments****)**

*Within each segment:*

- o Participation/attendance: 30%
- o Peer review: 20%
- o Segment deliverable/presentation 50%:

- **Final Deliverable(-s):**

**Exact format to be finalized, exact assignment may change.**

Preliminary: pick an industry of interest (among those not covered in course segments). Research possible positions and projects relevant to that industry where your IME (or your major if other) skills, experience and strengths are applicable. Prepare your application package for a position in your chosen industry/possible solution to a problem you identified. Give a 10-minute presentation/flash interview during the last class meeting, graded both by the instructors and your peers. (**30% for 4xxx students, 15% for 6xxx students**) Students in ISYE 6961 course will also prepare a written report (15%).

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
