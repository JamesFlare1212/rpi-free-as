### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Attendance (Q&A)  
To foster active engagement with the material, the final 20 minutes of every lecture will be a  
student-led conceptual Q&A session.  
Process: I will randomly name a student to ask a question (this counts as your attendance).  
This process will repeat with new students until the class period ends.  
Your Task: When selected, your job is to ask a question, not answer one. The question must be  
conceptual, related to the topics covered in that day’s lecture, and not about a specific homework  
problem.  
Objective: The goal is to shift from passive listening to active inquiry. True understanding  
comes from identifying what you don’t understand. This session is your platform to explore  
those questions with the class.  
A Note on Difficulty: We will discuss each question as a group. And remember the old  
saying: “Anyone who is not shocked by quantum theory has not understood it.” I don’t have  
all the answers, so I welcome (reasonably) challenging questions that push all of us—myself  
included—to think more deeply.  
Homework  
You should attempt all the problems yourself, but you may argue with your colleagues about the  
basic ideas underlying the problems. Simply copying each other’s solutions or downloading so-  
lutions is, however, counterproductive for all parties, and contrary to the honor code. Homework  
will be posted on the course LMS web site. All problems will be from the textbook.  
A new problem set will be assigned each Thursday and is due before class begins the following Thurs-  
day. Late submissions will automatically have their maximum possible score cut in half before  
grading. Its solution manual is floating around, but it is required for you to work out your own  
solution both according to the honor code, and because (of course!) that is the only way you will  
learn the material for yourself.  
Exams  
Cumulative mastery of homework is intended to be the primary learning tool in preparation for  
exams. There will be 2 midterm exams during the normal lecture time. No final exam.  
All exams will be in person. No Exceptions.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

mates: performing your share of the work, prepare properly (see above) so as not to disadvantage  
your peers, and discussing your ideas openly. You are permitted to discuss any aspect of your  
work with anyone, but the homework solution you hand in must clearly be your own work. All  
work must be handed in individually. Simply copying someone else’s notes or simply copied  
from internet or existing reference will result in a grade of zero. I will discuss suspected cases of  
copying with you before taking action.
