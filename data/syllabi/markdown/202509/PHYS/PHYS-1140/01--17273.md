## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

An introduction to modern physics with emphasis on special relativity; concepts in quantum mechanics; elementary particles and fundamental forces; and applications to nuclei and atoms.

All of the information for this course will be provided on the Rensselaer BlackBoard LMS site. Be sure to look there early and frequently.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Required: Special Relativity for the Enthusiastic Beginner by D. Morin

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Student should demonstrate understanding of special relativity by showing when and how to use Lorentz transformations in basic relativistic motion and energy problems.
- Student should be able to make logical knowledge-based arguments to describe the fundamental structure and behavior of light and matter in terms of quantum physics.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Completion of collaborative classroom worksheets.  
Examinations  
Homework assignments

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Lecture class times involve both formal lecture, interactive activities, and quizzes. The purposes of the lecture itself are to highlight key ideas and address students’ questions and difficulties. Attendance at lecture is not taken, but a large fraction of your grade is associated with lecture-class activities and quizzes. Quizzes will be given during the lecture time slot.  
Attendance and participation in Mentoring is directly related to 5% of the grade for students for whom Mentoring is required.

### Other Course Policies:

Homework is due on the date indicated, ***usually*** 10 ***pm***. Late homework will not be accepted for full credit without *prior* approval from the instructor. Homework handed in up to 24 hours after the first due date may have a 10% deduction, with 10% additional deduction for each additional 24 hours late until a maximum possible grade of 50% is hit. After that, homework will earn a maximum of 50% up until November 26. After Nov 26, late homework will not be accepted.

Most of your learning will be outside of class as you do homework. I encourage you to form regular study/discussion groups and to work collaboratively. (This practice will be encouraged through the mentoring sessions.) I encourage group meetings in the Huntington Room (JROWL 1C28) or Resnick Center (JROWL 1W20) so you can visit me, the TA's, or Mentors when questions arise that you can't resolve among yourselves.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own.
