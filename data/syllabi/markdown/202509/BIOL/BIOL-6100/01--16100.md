## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

A detailed survey of important topics in the neurosciences. Some of the topics to be covered in this class are: basic mechanisms of neural signaling (neurophysiology, synaptic transmission, and molecular signaling); understanding of sensation/perception and in particular how it relates to neuroanatomy; neurodevelopment and how the mature brain can change (plasticity); complex brain functions and neurological disease. May be used to fulfill the Culminating Experience requirement in Biology. BIOL 4100 is an undergraduate course taught together with the BIOL 6100 graduate section.  
From Neuron to Behavior is a lecture course and assignments are comprised of major exams and article critiques. Students enrolled in the 6100 graduate section have an additional paper and presentation assignment.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 4100/6100 - Describe the basic electrophysiological and biochemical properties of brain cells by the conclusion of the course.
- 4100/6100 - Discuss the development of the brain by the conclusion of the course.
- 4100/6100 - Discuss how cells of the central nervous system communicate with other cells in the central nervous system and with the peripheral nervous system.
- 4100/6100 - Describe how the brain interprets its surroundings through the senses (sight, hearing, taste, touch, and smell).
- 4100/6100 - Describe the role of the brain in higher, complex behaviors (i.e., cognition).
- 4100/6100 - Describe the pathophysiology of neurological, neurodevelopmental, and psychiatric disorders.
- 4100/6100 - Apply basic concepts in neurobiology to challenging questions in the field of neuroscience by the conclusion of the course.
- 6100 - Apply basic concepts in neurobiology and be able to analyze and interpret neuroscience data in research papers by the conclusion of the course.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Exams and article critiques will be use to assess content-based (i.e., neuroscience knowledge) student learning outcomes for all students enrolled in the 4100 or 6100 sections.  
A review paper and classroom presentation will be used to assess graduate student's (6100) ability to analyze and interpret neuroscience data.

This means that students should spend at least 12 hours per week working on the material for this course outside of classroom contact time. This time should include reading the assigned portions of the textbook, reviewing/studying the textbook and class notes, and preparing for exams.

LECTURES - Lectures are not designed to cover everything in a textbook and no textbook is comprehensive. The majority of the lectures will be comprised of selected topics from the book. Not all of the material found in the text will be covered in class lectures, but students are still responsible for all of the material in the assigned readings. Some of the material covered in class will not be found in the textbook, so it is important that students attend class and take good notes. Students are encouraged to ask questions and will be held responsible for knowing all assigned readings and lecture information.

CRITICAL REACTION PAPERS – The course will be roughly divided into 4 sections, one for each of the 4 major exams, and students will be required to find and read a peer reviewed empirical/research journal article relevant to some aspect of the material covered in each of the 4 sections of the course. Students will then be required to write a critical reaction of the article they selected using MS-Word. Students may select an article relevant to any of the material that was covered in that particular section of the course.

A peer reviewed primary empirical/research journal article is a publication in a peer reviewed journal that represents the first reporting of results from one or more experiments. Such articles traditionally have an introduction, methods (including a clear description of the procedures utilized), results (normally involving statistical analyses and data presentations in tables and/or graphs), and a discussion or conclusion section. Students are encouraged to contact Dr. Flint with questions regarding the appropriateness of an article if they are at all in doubt.

Each Critical Reaction will be comprised of a 700-750 word analysis of the article, excluding any title or reference pages, and students must provide a reference citation of the article being critiqued at the end of the assignment. Students should also be prepared to provide a copy of the article to the TA and Dr. Flint if requested. Since this assignment is restricted to a single research article, and because students must provide a reference citation, there will be no need to provide any in-text references/citations in the paper unless they are referencing material from a source other than the article they are critiquing. Please note that this is an exception to the rule, where paraphrased material should normally always be cited in the text. Students are prohibited from using direct quotes. Any information from the article should be paraphrased in the student’s own words. The use of AI technology is not permitted on these assignments. If AI technology was used, the student will automatically be assigned a 0 on the assignment.

Critical Reaction Papers must be submitted online in LMS/Blackboard by 11:59 pm the day before the scheduled exam. Papers submitted late, but within 24 hrs of the deadline will receive a 25% deduction (5 pts). Papers received between 24 and 48 hrs late will receive a 50% deduction, etc.

Critical Reaction Papers will be worth 25 points each. These papers are NOT summaries of the articles that were read, but instead should be critiques of the articles. In composing each critical reaction, students might consider one or more of the following:

1. How does the information in this article enhance or expand your knowledge of a topic covered in the class?
2. Is the hypothesis for each experiment clearly stated and do you think that it is an important issue to study? Why or why not?
3. What are the strengths of the study and why do you consider them strengths?
4. What are the weaknesses of the study and why do you consider them weaknesses?
5. Do the authors under or over generalize the impact of their results? Please explain.
6. Do the authors use appropriate statistical analyses of their data and/or appropriately present their data in tables and/or graphs?
7. Are the author’s findings correctly interpreted? Please explain.
8. Are the techniques, methods, and/or procedures utilized in the experiment(s) appropriate for the purpose of the study.

All critiques in your assignment should be appropriately explained, and whenever relevant, the citation of other sources should be provided to support your critique. For example, if you claim that a weakness of a study examining spatial memory in the Morris water maze was that the authors used only male rats, you should clearly explain why using female rats might have provided different results and cite sources to support your argument (hint: male and female rats are known to perform differently in the Morris water maze).  
Critical Reach Papers will be graded using the following rubric:

- Writing = 5 points
  - 5 pts – Paper is well-written and organized in a meaningful manner with appropriate paragraphs. Paper is devoid of spelling, grammar, and punctuation errors.
  - 4 pts – Paper is moderately well-written and/or moderately well-organized and/or has 2-4 spelling, grammar, and/or punctuation errors.
  - 3 pts – Writing and/or organization is average and/or there are 5-7 spelling, grammar, and/or punctuation errors.
  - 2 pts – Paper is poorly written and/or poorly organized and/or the paper has 8-10 spelling, grammar, and/or punctuation errors.
  - 0-1 pt – Paper is poorly written and/or poorly organized and/or had more than 10 spelling, grammar, and/or punctuation errors.
- Content = 20 pts
- - 18-20-15 pts – Paper provides an excellent critique, examining the article thoroughly for at least 4 key issues. There is no unnecessary summary of material from the article.
  - 15-17 pts – Paper provides a good critique, thoroughly examining the article for 3 key issues and/or there is one instance where there was unnecessary summary of material from the article.
  - 10-14 pts – Paper provides an average critique, presenting shallow examination of 3 or more issues or a thorough examination of only 2 key issues and/or there are two instances where unnecessary summaries of material from the article were provided.
  - 0-9 pts – Paper provides a below average or poor critique, presenting a shallow examination of fewer than 3 issues or a thorough examination of only 1 key issue and/or the paper contains significant unnecessary summaries of portions of the article.

Note: Critiques that do not fall within the word limit for the assignment or that use quotations from the article will receive additional point deductions.

EXAMS - There will be a total of four major exams during the semester. Each exam will be comprised of multiple-choice questions covering the assigned readings, lectures, and presentations that have occurred since the previous exam, and each exam will be worth 100 points (50 questions worth 2 pts each). Students should anticipate that there will be questions from the text that were not discussed in class, so it is important that students have a firm understanding of all the assigned readings. Make-up exams need to be completed within 1 week of returning to campus. Contact Dr. Flint immediately if you miss an exam for any reason.

Paper Formatting Requirements:

- Paper must be written and submitted in MS-Word
- Body of the paper must be 8-12 pages in length, excluding title and reference pages. Images, graphs, tables, etc., may be included in the paper, but do not count toward the paper length requirement.
- Size 12 of a standard font such as Times New Roman should be used
- Double spaced
- 1 inch margins
- Use of headings and subheadings to organize the paper
- Title page including paper title, student’s name, course, and date
- Reference pages that include all references cited in the paper. Do not provide references if they are not cited in the paper
- Reference style and in-text citation style – student may use an accepted formatting style for their discipline
- No quotations – all material referenced in the paper that comes from another source must be paraphrased into the student’s own words and the sources should be cited in the text appropriately as well as on the reference page

Paper Content Requirements:

- Review of an issue relevant to behavior and/or cognitive neuroscience
  - o Approximately 50% of the paper should be focused on behavior or cognitive aspects of the topic and 50% on the neuroscientific aspects of the topic (e.g., neuroanatomy, neurochemistry, neurophysiology, etc.)
- Minimum of 15 peer reviewed empirical research articles must be cited in the body of the paper. Additional articles are welcome, as are other sources (e.g., meta-analyses, review articles, books, book chapters) as long as there are at least 15 peer reviewed empirical research articles included. Please avoid referencing web sites, dictionaries, and other secondary sources.
