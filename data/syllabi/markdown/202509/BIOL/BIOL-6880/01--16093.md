## Course Text(s)

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Describe the major types of ecosystems that occur globally and theories describing ecosystem structure and function.
- Describe the major types of ecosystems that occur globally and theories describing ecosystem structure and function.
- Describe major ecological changes, including climate change impacts, eutrophication, responses to acidification, and impacts of deposition of major pollutants such as mercury.
- Demonstrate mastery in reading, interpreting, and discussing the primary literature; that is, scientific articles in peer-reviewed journals.
- Demonstrate mastery reviewing and synthesizing peer-reviewed literature and in writing a review manuscript suitable for a peer-reviewed journal.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Grades for students enrolled in this class will be based on:  
\- Three tests, spaced roughly evenly throughout the semester.  
\- Occasional pop-quizzes, to confirm you are reading and staying on top of the material.  
\- Writing a review of the primary literature on topic of the student's choice within the overall context of global environmental change.  
\- Leading one or more discussions during class on various topics  
\- Attending class and actively participating in discussion and contributing to discussion threads online.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

3 tests, spaced approximately equally throughout the semester, worth 100 points each (300 points total; 50% of final grade)

Attendance, taken every class period (50 points; 8.3% of final grade)

Leading discussion of a peer-reviewed article (50 points, 8.3% of final grade)

Contributing to discussion of peer-reviewed articles (100 points, 16.7% of final grade)

Writing a review article on a global environmental change topic (100 points; 16.7% of final grade)  
Exams will cover material from lectures, assigned readings, and in-class discussion and will consist of multiple choice, definition, short answer, and essay questions designed to gauge the extent students have acquired a basic literacy in global environmental concepts. Exams will predominantly focus on the material covered following the preceding exam, but students should be aware that the topics covered build on each other and so links to materials in previous lectures will occur. Participation in class presentations and discussions will be graded based on each student’s contributions to an online discussion board and in-class discussions. Additionally, occasional in-class quizzes will be used to assess whether students are adequately reading the primary literature assigned for each class.  
Monitoring your progress: I will return all assignments, so you should be able to calculate your overall grade. Grades are calculated as a percentage of the total possible points for all assignments and determined as follows: A: 93 or above A-: 90-92 B+: 87-89 B: 83-86 B-: 80-82 C+: 77-79 C: 73-76 C-: 70-72 D+: 67-69 D: 60-66 F: <60  
Students enrolled in 6880 cannot get D+/D or D-. There will be no make-up exams. If you miss one of the exams due to an excused emergency absence (interviews and family trips do not qualify as emergencies), your final grade will be based on the total points from the remaining exams. If you miss an exam due to an emergency (illness or serious injury or a death in your immediate family), you must submit, in writing, your request for an excused absence. Verbal communication with the faculty member is not sufficient. Please use the following guidelines when preparing your request:  
1\. Your written request for an excused absence must indicate your name, the nature of the emergency, and the date of the exam that you missed.  
2\. Your request must be detailed, typed, and signed by you. This request must be given directly to Prof. Rose no later than one week after the missed exam.  
3\. If you miss an exam due to a medical condition, you must include documentation. At the minimum, you must submit a signed letter from your physician. Failure to comply with these guidelines will result in a zero recorded for the missed exam. If you miss more than one examination you should discuss possible options available with your instructor. Late arrivals to exams will be given the exam during the time that remains for the designated examination period.

## Course Calendar

### Calendar Details:

### Calendar Details:

After the first week of class I will circulate a daily schedule for the semester. The schedule will be adapted to the interests of students, who will lead discussion on peer-reviewed literature in each class period. Each student will lead at least one class discussion. I will attempt to pair students with topics for which they are most interested. This course will cover topics including:  
1\. Basics of global environmental cycles and patterns  
2\. Lithosphere  
3\. Atmosphere  
4\. UV radiation and ozone  
5\. Terrestrial ecosystems  
6\. Wetlands ecosystems  
7\. Inland waters  
8\. Oceans  
9\. Global carbon cycle and climate change  
10\. Global phosphorus and eutrophication  
11\. Global nitrogen cycle and agriculture  
12\. Land use modification and biodiversity  
13\. Global sulfur cycle, acid deposition, acid rain, and recovery from acidification  
14\. Global mercury cycle  
15\. Emerging global environmental issues and syntheses  
Additionally, we will cover a number of ecological theory concepts, including:  
1\. Biodiversity and ecosystem stability  
2\. Island biogeography theory  
3\. Space-time relationships and macrosystems ecology  
4\. Alterative stable states and regime shifts  
5\. Ecological stoichiometry and metabolic theory
