## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

The course will not follow any textbook and has no textbook requirement. Instead most reading will be based on primary literature, which will generally be provided as links or pdf files. The following books may be a helpful as references:  
Molecular Biology of the cell, 6th edition, Alberts et al.  
Evolution, 5th edition, Futuyma and Kirkpatrick

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate knowledge of cells and the modifications of underlying molecular, structural, and physical mechanisms that have contributed to their diversification over time.
- Demonstrate knowledge of experimental tools and approaches used in modern evolutionary cell biological research.
- Read and critically review primary scientific literature in evolutionary cell biology.
- Analyze a specific topic of evolutionary cell biology and design a set of next-step experiments, and package these in a persuasive research proposal.
- Review critically evaluate grant proposals and provide constructive feedback.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Examinations and evaluations: The exams will be short answer questions and will be used to assess knowledge in both BIOL 4950 and 6950. Makeup exams may be scheduled if arranged prior to the test date and approved by the instructor. The format of these exams may differ significantly from the standard exams. If you miss the exam due to illness or some other emergency, a note needs to be produced from Student Health, the Dean’s Office, ALAC, etc. before a makeup exam will be scheduled.  
Paper presentation: Students will be assigned current scientific papers and present the findings to the class. Details on the presentation format will be described in class.  
Homework: For each paper discussion students will write a report on the paper. This will be due on the LMS at 22:00 the day before the disucssion. For each paper students will also submit two questions, which are due at the same time. Details and structure of the homework will be announced in class. Presenting students are not required to submit a homework response. They still need to submit their questions.
