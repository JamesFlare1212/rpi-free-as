## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is an in-depth study of current research within the broad field of cytoskeletal biology. Students will read, interpret, and critique recent primary literature, present new findings, lead discussions, and identify rising questions in the field.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The goal of this course is to familiarize students with the current work in cytoskeletal research. Students will learn to analyze and present a primary paper and critically evaluate experiments.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will demonstrate the ability to analyze primary literature by reviewing, discussing, critiquing, and presenting primary research related to cytoskeletal biology.
- Students will communicate the contents, interpretations, and conclusions of new studies to their peers and place new findings within the context of the field.
- Students will apply their ability to analyze, interpret, and critique primary research papers by synthesizing their analysis in writing.
- Students will generate crucial new questions in cytoskeletal research and propose an experimental path towards addressing these questions.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Assessment BIOL 4140: Students will be assessed by participation in class discussion, written reviews of primary research papers, and in-class article presentations.

- Class Participation – 30 %

- Paper Reviews – 50 %

- Presentation – 20 %

  Assessment BIOL 6140: Students will be assessed by participation in class discussion, written reviews of primary research papers, paper presentations, and production of a paper that generates questions to advance the field of experimental cytoskeletal biology.

- Class Participation – 20 %
- Paper Reviews – 20 %
- Presentation – 20 %
- Future questions paper – 40 %

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
