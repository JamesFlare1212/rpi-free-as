## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Many people are familiar with Socrates’s assertion that “the life which is unexamined is not worth living.” But he also said, “the greatest good of man [humanity] is daily to converse about virtue [*aretē*].” Here, virtue doesn’t mean morality. It means something like the cultivation of excellence or potential, which allows you to live well. Why is reading literature important to living well? What do we want from literature? What even *is* literature? How and why should we integrate reading and the discussion of literary texts into our lives? And what barriers to this activity stand in our way? Literature, this class proposes, instigates the social elaboration of meaning needed to live “virtuously”—with oneself and others. It calls on us to deliberate what constitutes a worthwhile existence and enact the means to build it.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the work students turn in is their own work. Acts that violate this trust undermine the intellectual rigor and integrity of this course and of the educational process in general. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of academic dishonesty and you must familiarize yourself with these.

I take plagiarism and other forms of academic dishonesty seriously, including generating assignments (in part or whole) with AI. Anyone engaging in academic dishonesty will be referred to the Dean of Students, who will determine appropriate disciplinary measures. Students will also earn 0% on an assignment with plagiarized or AI-generated content. You can visit RPI’s Student Rights, Responsibilities, and Conduct page for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy.
