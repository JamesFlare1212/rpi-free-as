## Course Goals

### Goals:

The overall objective is simple: learn the basic methods used in numerical linear algebra. This isn’t so straightforward, and a quote due to Yogi Berra is particularly relevant to computing, and it’s “In theory there is no difference between theory and practice. In practice there is.” What this means is that we will spend considerable time investigating error, where it comes from and how to control it. In conjunction with this we will consider the basic theory underlying the methods as well as learn how to implement the methods using MATLAB. Another important objective is that you learn to communicate your results in a clear and concise manner. This is important and it will be used in grading your homework.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

I. LU Method: review (1 week) a) Factorization

b) Error and condition number  
c) Software (LAPACK, BLAS, MKL, MATLAB, etc) and

hardware (multicore, multiprocessor, and cluster computing systems)

II. Computing an Eigenvalue (1 week)  
a) Review and applications  
b) Power method  
c) Inverse and Rayleigh quotient iteration

III. Computing Multiple Eigenvalues (2 weeks) a) Orthogonal iteration  
b) QR method  
c) Applications

Exam 1 (week of Feb 22)

IV. QR Factorization (1 week) a) Householder matrices  
b) Computational methods

V. Computational Optimization (3 weeks)  
a) Least squares (normal equations, pseudoinverse, and QR) b) Conjugate gradient method and sparse linear systems

Exam 2 (week of March 29)

VI. Singular Value Decomposition (2 weeks)  
a) Derivation and computational algorithm(s)  
b) Application: low-rank approximations and image compression

VII. Data Analysis (3 weeks)  
a) Principal Component Analysis  
b) Independent Component Analysis c) Modal Data Analysis

Exam 3 (last day or two of class)
