## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

The main text may be taken to be:  
James Ward Brown and Ruel V. Churchill, *Complex Variables and Applications*, 9th edition; McGraw-Hill, 2015.  
For mathematically strong students who would like a somewhat deeper treatment, I recommend as an alternative:  
Mark J. Ablowitz and Athanassios S. Fokas, *Complex Variables: Introduction and Applications,* 2nd edition; Cambridge University Press, 2003.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

- A student successfully completing the course will be able to use complex variable techniques to solve certain kinds of problems in the applied sciences.
- A student successfully completing the course will develop analytical reasoning skills.
- A student successfully completing the course will gain practice in formulating logical arguments.
- A student successfully completing the course will gain some familiarity with how complex variables provide connections between geometry and analysis.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Your course grade will be determined primarily by the homework grade. The first homework will be due on or about February 8. Homework must be submitted as single PDF files on LMS. After each homework, a subset of students will be interviewed over WebEx about their homework to validate the independence of their work. Inadequate performance on these interviews will result in a penalty applied to the score of the homework in question as well as all previous homework scores since the previous interview (or all previous homework scores if there was no previous interview). The final exam, conducted one on one over WebEx, will similarly serve to validate the homework grade. Inadequate performance on the final exam will result in a penalty to the overall homework score, and therefore the final grade. The current plan is to require only the highest scoring students to take the final exam, with the precise cutoff to be determined. Students suspected of poor collaboration practice, meaning an inordinate reliance on the thinking and work of others in completing their homework, may also be required to take a final exam. If poor collaboration practice appears widespread in the class, the final exam will be imposed on the class as a whole.

Regular final exams for the highest scoring students will be administered over WebEx during the final exams period. Final exams imposed due to suspected poor collaboration practice will be scheduled between April 19 and May 3.

Your course grade can be enhanced by active and substantive contribution to discussions in class and on piazza. You can be awarded as much as 5 percentage points on your final grade for exceptionally insightful and thoughtful input and questions.

Late homework will be penalized 10 points (out of 100) per working day. Once I post solutions, homework will no longer be accepted for credit. If you have need for an extension on your homework, you should ask me in advance of the due date. Homework is due at the specified time and date; submissions later in the class or day will be penalized 5 points.

You will be able to monitor your cumulative score and standing in the class throughout the semester via the My Grades tool on LMS.

Your grade is based solely on performance, not effort. I do sometimes adjust the final grade in your favor if I see a compelling reason. Such grade adjustments are given purely at my discretion, and are not motivated by external matters such as your need to maintain a certain GPA for financial aid, domestic tranquility, etc. In particular, there is no point in asking for a grade adjustment; I will ignore any such requests. I view grades simply as a means of reporting, as accurately as possible, the demonstrated level of your mastery with the material.

You may only appeal scores on homework based on factual criteria (a correct answer was marked wrong, or scores were added incorrectly). Any such legitimate appeals will only be accepted within one week of the date on which the graded homework was returned to the class. I will not entertain quibbles about how many points of partial credit you think you should have been given on some problem. Students making frivolous appeals will be given one warning, after which further nuisance appeals will be penalized by as many points as the student was frivolously arguing for.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Beyond these general institute-wide policies, here are some course-specific clarifications

\* If you obtained assistance from anyone outside of the course or any written material beyond the lecture notes and the course text, you must explicitly acknowledge the source. You may under no circumstances query outside sources to solve in substance the homework problems for you. Inappropriate use of external sources will generally result in a grade of 0 for the entire assignment.

\* You should show your work and explain your calculations clearly and coherently in order to achieve most of the credit on homework problems. You may cooperate in small groups on the solution of homework problems, but your homework submission must represent your own understanding, thoughts, and expression. It is not permissible to copy the solutions of other students, even if working together. If there is evidence for copying, all students involved will generally receive zero credit for the implicated work if the collaboration is not acknowledged. If there is evidence for copying from an acknowledged collaborator, a grading penalty will still be applied for copying, possibly as severe as dividing the points among the collaborating parties. Note in particular, students who share their homework solutions with another student, who then copies their work, will suffer the same grade penalty as the student(s) who copied their work.

\* Students whose homework appears not to reflect their independent understanding and expression will substantially increase their likelihood for being interviewed about their homework. Students whose homework interviews do not reveal a level of understanding commensurate with their homework will suffer a grading penalty (as described above), and substantially increase their likelihood to be interviewed regarding future homeworks.

If you have any question concerning these policies before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

Students who violate the spirit or letter of these rules are subject to penalties according to the principles outlined in the Rensselaer Handbook, in addition to the grading penalties described. In addition, I will report all instances of academic dishonesty to the Dean of Students.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

I will direct the class according to the following learning strategy:

\* Students should view the pre-recorded components of lecture and at least preview the corresponding sections of the textbook prior to the live class. It is not necessary to have a thorough understanding of the section after a first reading and viewing, but knowing the basic facts and content should help in following the class discussion. The live class will involve some demonstration or application of the new material from the pre-recorded lectures, and be an opportunity for students to ask general questions or request clarification. Generally, the whole class discussion will take place during the first hour of the scheduled class time (2:30-3:30 PM), while the second hour (3:30-4:30 PM) will serve as office hours for individual 15 minute appointments to be scheduled over WebEx.

\* The material covered in class is intended to supplement, and not simply restate, the material in the textbook. Moreover, the class discussion will emphasize those aspects of the material that I find most important.

\* Students work either alone or with a small group of colleagues to solve the homework problems. While collaboration is encouraged to stimulate learning and understanding, each student is responsible for understanding any solution submitted under his or her name. If the assigned homework problems are too difficult, students should work out some of the simpler problems from the related sections of the textbook until they have gained enough confidence and skill to tackle the assigned problems. If students do not feel sufficiently confident with the material through self-study or consultation with colleagues, they should seek further assistance from the professor during his office hours.

LMS will be used for some aspects of course management. You can get started by going to https://lms.rpi.edu/ and consulting the document describing the use of LMS in this class. Questions about the class outside of live meetings will be handled through a piazza site for the class.
