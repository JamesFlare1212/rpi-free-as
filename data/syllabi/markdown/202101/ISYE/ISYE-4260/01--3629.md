## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

RPI places great importance on the principle of integrity in the work performed by both faculty and students. For example, both faculty and students are subject to harsh disciplinary measures if they present others’ work as their own, or if they help others to do so. Strict policies on intellectual property (e.g., patents) and authorship (e.g., of professional publications) are manifestations of this principle and are widely applied in the professional world. At RPI, principles of academic integrity are enshrined in the Statement of Academic Integrity, (part of the Rensselaer Handbook and available at this link): https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs  
With each assignment, I will therefore ask you to affirm that the work you have submitted is yours and yours alone. No assignment will be considered complete without your signature on this affirmation. If for any reason you cannot make this affirmation, you should contact me in advance of the assignment’s due date. The affirmation reads as follows:  
I affirm that I have neither given nor received assistance for this assignment, and that all work presented in it is my own: Signed (type your name here): \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_  
Possible Violations: If I suspect you have not abided by this affirmation (i.e., I suspect you have either given or received help on an assignment), you will take a one-on-one, live examination over WebEx so that I can determine if the work is yours. Depending on the outcome, your case may ultimately be referred to the Dean of Students for further action. See the Statement of Academic Integrity for a complete description of this process, the most severe outcome of which is expulsion from RPI.
