## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

With choreographed series of challenges, the four Digital Constructs prepare students for full control of digital tools and establish design authorship. Digital Constructs IV culminates this successive dialogue, with more advanced knowledge, skills, and techniques of digital tools and representations in a fourfold continuum: I. Physical to Digital; II. Algorithmic Design; III. Building Information; IV. Advanced Representation. Thus practiced techniques will not only lead you to success in finding architectural formulae within the coursework, but also be of help in developing your work in other courses, specifically AD4, IDS, and IDD. Students are strongly encouraged to actively think and explore opportunities beyond this course frame, in order for the digital skills acquired to match your personal interests and become part of your digital proficiency.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Develop a high proficiency of polygon modeling strategies and techniques, and exploit their advantages
- Become proficient in formulating algorithms to initiate design thoughts and to develop digital projects
- Demonstrate an ability to incorporate architectural scales and details through the use of BIM software
- Demonstrate an ability to produce representations of digital design work through a variety of media
- Demonstrate an ability to produce and present multiple presentation drawings in a coherent way on a single board

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

\- Students will receive assessments of their weekly assignments, either from an instructor or in collaboration with other students.  
Group critique between students will help encourage a self-critical approach to design.  
\- Projects will be assessed by both the instructor and outside experts that will offer unique perspective from their own biases and  
points of view.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grades will be assessed based on the quality of digital models and drawings produced.  
40% Weekly Assignments and Attendance  
30% Project I + II (Midterm)  
30% Project III + IV (Final)  
Interim grades will be provided to students following completion of our Mid Review.  
Grades will be established according to the following policy:  
\- A (EXCELLENT) Exceptional performance strongly exceeding the requirements of the course, showing strong academic initiative  
and independent resourcefulness.  
\- B (GOOD) Performance above the norm; accurate and complete; beyond the minimum requirements of the course; work  
demonstrates marked progress and initiative.  
\- C (AVERAGE) Satisfactory work that adequately meets minimum requirements and demonstrates satisfactory comprehension,  
communication skills, and effort; demonstrates little initiative to investigate the problem without substantial input from the  
instructor; work shows little improvement.  
\- D (INFERIOR) Unsatisfactorily meets minimum requirements; demonstrates minimum comprehension, communication skills, and  
effort at an inferior level; initiative lacking; improvement not noticeable.  
\- F (FAIL)

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of this course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student. Any missrepresentation of work shall be evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student will result in a failing grade for the course.
