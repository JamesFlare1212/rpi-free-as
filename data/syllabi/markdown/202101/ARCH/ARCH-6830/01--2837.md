## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The *Graduate Thesis Seminar* is designed to provide support to graduate students who are engaged in independent thesis research projects. It is a required course for all graduate students in the Architectural Acoustics Program. This provides students with feedback on their projects from the faculty and their peers encompassing multiple perspectives and bodies of knowledge. It encourages an ongoing discussion about research activity and its diversity and commonality. These discussions are intended to build the research community in the school by providing a context in which individual work is actively shared, examined, evaluated and strengthened. Skills of aural, poster presentations, critical review and excellence in scientific writing will be sharpened.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Research, however individual and isolated, is fundamentally a social activity; drawing upon prior work and proposing new understandings, relationships and additions to a collectively held body of knowledge. Research as a cultural conversation communicates through a number of formal channels. The presentation format used in the Graduate Research Design Seminar and again in this course provides for the development of communication, presentation and publication skills that are required in contemporary research culture. Presentations appropriate to these venues and to the audiences they suggest will be stressed. In addition, we will encourage the early development of a thesis, project or dissertation document by posting documents for discussion in the format required of formal submissions to the Graduate School.

Each student will have the opportunity to make two oral, one graphic presentation, and one web-based presentation to the seminar. In addition, written documents will be posted and commentary about them is required of each student.

Writing assignment: The current state of your research in the format required by the graduate school. Specifically include the abstract, literature review, thesis statement and methodology. Writing style is that of a formal thesis or dissertation. Post for group comments and questions one week prior to your presentation in the seminar.

Presentation 1: The current progress of your thesis research. Although this is a formal presentation of your work, it is also a working session so that your presentation must end with a list of outstanding or problematic issues, and a detailed schedule. Your own critique of the work is central to this assignment. Be prepared to discuss methodological issues such as validity, reliability, the operationalization of your theoretical constructs, methodological strategy and projected analysis as was discussed in the Research Design Seminar last semester.

Presentation 2: The current state of your research. For those intending to finish this semester it will be considered a trial run for the oral defense of your thesis. For others it is a formal presentation of your work. Include specifically, but not exclusively changes, discoveries, challenges, weaknesses and modifications to your schedule.

Poster presentation: This will include the five minute version of your research presented in front of the poster as might happen at a conference poster session (Poster in Gallery for broad evaluations).

Web-based presentation: This will include a front page and possibly several layers of web pages about your research as suitable for posting on the website of the School.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Carry out research independently under the guidance of an advisor
- Demonstrate the ability to write a scientific document in the field of acoustics according to the fields guidelines and conventions.
- Assess the scientific validity of document research.
- Collect and analyze research data and discuss the findings systematically.
- Orally present scientific work at scientific conferences.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

No mid-term/ final exams, but a series of homework assignments are expected. There will be a midterm and final project for which a written report is required.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

20% participation, quality of discussion, web postings, attendance

20% quality of writing, citing, and formatting written documents

20% clarity and effectiveness of graphic presentations (poster and projections)

20% clarity, organization and interest of oral presentations

20% Thesis progress, interaction with the advisor

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
