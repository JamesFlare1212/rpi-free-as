## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course builds on the fundamentals learned in ARCH-4840 Architectural Acoustics I. There will be a focus on the practical application of architectural acoustics principles and analysis techniques to room acoustics and noise control design. There will be continuing discussion throughout the semester of the role of the acoustical consultant in the design process: How to communicate design recommendations and guidelines most effectively — to the right people, in the right form, at the right time — throughout the course of a real project. A major component of the course is a semester-long team design project. There will be three progress presentations and one final presentation of the projects. Final poster presentations are to be entered in the Acoustical Society of America’s Student Design Competition. There will be several guest lectures given by practicing consultants and researchers in architectural acoustics.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Topics covered will include:

- Site planning and environmental noise control
- Noise perception, quantification, and metrics
- Wall, floor, ceiling, and roof constructions for noise insulation
- Mechanical and Electrical systems noise control
- Ground-borne and structure-borne noise control
- Room acoustics design considerations for concert halls, recital halls, opera houses, theatres, classrooms, worship spaces, rehearsal rooms, multi-function rooms, and outdoor venues.
- Applied coupled-volume performance spaces: Design, usage, measurement, and perception.
- Room acoustics prediction and auralization using CATT-Acoustic™ / EASE™.
- Stage Technology and Theater design
- Sound reinforcement systems

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

After completing this course, successful students will have demonstrated through class participation, written assignments, and oral presentations:

- A clear understanding of the traditional phases of the building design and construction processes, and the role of the professional architectural acoustics consultant in each phase

- The ability to convincingly communicate acoustics recommendations and conceptual designs, both as an individual and as a member of a team

- An advanced understanding of practical acoustical design guidelines for different types of built environments, depending on function

- A working knowledge of computer room acoustics modeling techniques. This includes an understanding of the basic algorithms behind modern computer modeling programs, the ability to create three-dimensional models, and analyze prediction results and auralizations using CATT-Acoustic™ / EASE™ software

- An advanced fundamental understanding of the principals of acoustic wave propagation, and the interaction of acoustic waves with surfaces and materials (reflection, transmission, modes, absorption, scattering, and diffusion), with a concentration on phenomena relevant to building acoustics.
