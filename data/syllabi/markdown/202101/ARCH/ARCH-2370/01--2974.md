## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Amid an industrialized and growing world, there are movements in architecture to create buildings that, by their form, materials, orientation, and arrangement, make use of the energy and materials available on a site to achieve energy, environmental, and other design goals. Energy, Comfort and Ecology is the first course in the environmental systems series and will lay the foundational concepts to be expanded on in Environmental and Ecological Systems and Building Systems and Environment. It will investigate topics of vernacular architecture, classical ecology, thermal comfort, the uses and losses of energy in buildings, climates and climate change, and solar site planning.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Demonstrate an understanding of the principles underlying environmental systems design, how design criteria can vary by geographic region, and the tools used for performance assessment.
- • Describe specific ways that traditional architectural forms evolved from thermal and lighting needs.
- • Explain the relevance of classical ecology, including ideas of the ecosystem, succession, population dynamics, and adaptation to the practice of architecture.
- • Calculate quantities of heat transferred by conduction, convection, or radiation in one-dimensional steady-state problems.
- • Determine conditions providing standard or adaptive thermal comfort in a building, and evaluate the potential of those conditions to optimize thermal comfort.
- • Use climate information for a particular location to quantify solar, wind, and daylighting resources, as well as to design a group of buildings compliant with proposed solar-access codes.

Attendance for lectures and pinups will be taken for each class automatically and accounts for 20% of your total grade. **Group project**

Throughout the semester, students will work on an iterative research and representation exercise. Students will work in groups of 3-4 and remain with their group for the entire semester. The group project counts towards 20% of the final grade and all group members are expected to demonstrate their active participation.
