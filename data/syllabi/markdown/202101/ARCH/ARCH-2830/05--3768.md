### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**LEARNING OUTCOMES**  
Through their work on all assignments, students will produce architectural design  
proposals and various materials that support their design proposals. In producing this work,  
each student will have demonstrated the capacity to respond to the following set of  
“performance criteria” currently established by the NAAB:  
**A.3: Investigative Skills:** Ability to gather, assess, record, and comparatively evaluate relevant  
information and performance in  
**A.4: Architectural Design Skills:** Ability to effectively use basic formal, organizational and  
environmental principles and the capacity of each to inform two- and three-dimensional design  
**A.5: Ordering Systems:** Ability to apply the fundamentals of both natural and formal ordering  
systems and the capacity of each to inform two- and three-dimensional design,  
**A.6: Use of Precedents:** Ability to examine and comprehend the fundamental principles present in  
relevant precedents and to make informed choices about the incorporation of such principles into  
architecture and urban design projects  
**A.8: Cultural Diversity and Social Equity:** Understanding of the diverse needs, values, behavioral  
norms, physical abilities, and social and spatial patterns that characterize different cultures and  
individuals and the responsibility of the architect to ensure equity of access to sites, buildings, and  
structures. [partial fulfillment]  
**B.1: Pre-Design:** Ability to prepare a comprehensive program for an architectural project that  
includes an assessment of client and user needs; an inventory of spaces and their requirements; an  
analysis of site conditions (including existing buildings); a review of the relevant building codes and  
standards, including relevant sustainability requirements, and an assessment of their implications  
for the project; and a definition of site selection and design assessment criteria. [partial fulfillment  
**B.2: Site Design:** Ability to respond to site characteristics, including urban context and  
developmental patterning, historical fabric, soil, topography, ecology, climate, and building  
orientation, in the development of a project.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

GRADING / ASSESSMENT Course grades will be determined as follows:  
12.5% – Peer Grades for Collaboration  
12.5% – Precedent Studies of Housing and Adaptive reuse.  
12.5% – Programming and Site Analysis  
12.5% – Midterm Design Review, Schematic Design  
12.5% – Interim Design Review  
37.5% – Final Design Solution and Documentation  
Grades will be established according to the following policy:  
**A (EXCELLENT)** Exceptional performance strongly exceeding the requirements of the course, showing  
strong academic initiative, independent resourcefulness, uncommon insight and skill. The work is complete,  
on-time, and thoroughly and beautifully documented. The design problem is engaged with criticality  
and creativity. The argument is compelling and consistent with the design work presented.  
B (GOOD) Project and performance that exceeds requirements of the course. The work is on-time and  
complete, it engages the problem given with a critical perspective and creative solutions; work  
demonstrates sustained progress and an increasing understanding of the problem through the agency of  
the design process.  
**C (AVERAGE)** Satisfactory work that adequately meets minimum requirements and demonstrates  
satisfactory comprehension, communication skills, and effort; the project demonstrates little initiative to  
investigate the problem without substantial input from the instructor; work shows little improvement.  
**D (INFERIOR)** Meets the minimum requirements for a professional education; demonstrates the  
minimum acceptable comprehension, communication skills, and effort; initiative lacking; improvement  
is slight. Be reminded that in the School any two D’s in studio will require remedial work as designated  
by the faculty. Typically, the student cannot move forward towards graduation without first undertaking  
this required work.  
**F (UNACCEPTABLE)** Does not meet minimum requirements; fails to adequately demonstrate  
comprehension, communication skills, and/or effort. Work is incomplete or late. Students who fail to  
appear for a studio review should anticipate this grade as a matter of course.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic Integrity  
The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic  
Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all graded assignments must  
represent the students’ own work. Where help was received or teamwork allowed, a notation on the  
assignment should indicate your collaboration.  
Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty of dropping one  
letter grade from the final semester grade. A second infraction will result in a failure of the studio. Any  
and all violations of academic integrity are required by Institute regulations to be reported to the Dean  
of Students who maintains files for the duration of a student’s academic career. Multiple reports to this  
office can result in academic dismissal. A record of disciplinary action is permanently maintained by  
the Institute and can be disclosed to federal, state or local government entity, law enforcement,  
licensing or certification board, or corporate entities under certain conditions.
