## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This second course in the Digital Constructs sequence will focus on refining speculative and analytical drawing skills. Drawing will continue to be discussed as the medium through which architects generate and express forms and concepts. Work will be done in two and three dimensions, in vector and raster formats, with a range of software packages. The semester will begin with an analysis of an architectural photograph, which will be subsequently built into intricate, complex structures with particular aesthetic characteristics. These structures will then be visualized to gauge their potential architectural affect. Class meetings will incorporate critique, tutorials and working sessions. The semester is organized into three phases that will build on each other:

**Phase 1** – Glitching, Projecting and Advanced Modeling

Phase 1 will build on two and three-dimensional drawing skills acquired in Digital Constructs 1. It will start with a focus on modifying an existing architectural photograph through various techniques in Photoshop including glitching and pixel sorting. Effects produced in two dimensions will be translated into three dimensions and vice versa. Moving between two and three dimensions will be discussed as a fundamental problem/opportunity within the discipline and discussed from a technical standpoint through workflows between Rhino, Illustrator and Photoshop. Phase 1 will also introduce more advanced Rhino modeling, specifically how to manage detail and curvature with precision.

**Phase 2** – Associative Modeling

Phase 2 will introduce associative modeling and computational drawing through Grasshopper. Using Grasshopper, students will learn to associate elements of geometry, operate on them parametrically, and then through multiplication and transformation, produce abstract, speculative drawings. By customizing Grasshopper templates presented in tutorials, students will begin to author their own drawing procedures. The final drawing in Phase 2 will be a multi-view composite created in three dimensions and visualized using a combination of Rhino rendering, VRay rendering, Photoshop and Illustrator.

**Phase 3** – Portfolio

Phase 3 will develop a portfolio of all Digital Construct 1 and 2 work. Building on the exploration of how a photograph of architecture is a focused representation of work, the portfolio will be discussed as a vehicle through which one’s work can be framed, focused and refined. The final multi view drawing from Phase 2 will continue to be refined in conjunction with portfolio development. This drawing demonstrate mastery of the range of techniques introduced during the semester. Final deliverable for the course will a portfolio including all work done in Digital Constructs 1 and 2.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Student Learning Outcomes**

Students who successfully complete this course will be able to:

- Demonstrate the ability to use drawing as a communicative tool within architecture.
- Demonstrate the ability to use drawing as a generative tool with architecture.
- Develop three-dimension models in a variety of ways, through explicit modeling, parametric modeling, and algorithmic techniques.
- Differentiate between digital and real environments, leverage the advantages of both in order to communicate effectively, and work between both environments in an efficient manner.
- Become proficient in procedural thinking, and how to use the computer to compliment this way of working.
- Develop a sound methodological basis for the creation of drawings, digital models, digitally fabricated physical models, and renderings of digital models.

**Assessment Measures**

- Students will receive, on a weekly basis, critical feedback and direction in the form of collective or individual critiques. Students are expected to respond to feedback by proactively reworking various aspects of their projects.
- Students will receive the opportunity to present ideas to external experts, who will provide critical feedback to students.
- Students will be introduced to comparative theories and concepts related to the content of the course.
- Students will receive two major reviews at the midterm and end of the term. For both reviews, students will receive feedback on their progress and accomplishments.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grades will be based upon the quality of drawings and models produced as well as attendance and participation in class discussions.

- Mid Review: 35%
- Final Project: 35%
- Attendance and Participation: 30%

Mid-semester evaluations for the course will be provided to each student. Grades will be established according to the following policy:

- A (EXCELLENT) Exceptional performance strongly exceeding the requirements of the course, showing strong academic initiative and independent resourcefulness.
- B (GOOD) Performance above the norm; accurate and complete; beyond the minimum requirements of the course; work demonstrates marked progress and initiative.
- C (AVERAGE) Satisfactory work that adequately meets minimum requirements and demonstrates satisfactory comprehension, communication skills, and effort; demonstrates little initiative to investigate the problem without substantial input from the instructor; work shows little improvement.
- D (INFERIOR) Unsatisfactorily meets minimum requirements; demonstrates minimum comprehension, communication skills, and effort at an inferior level; initiative lacking; improvement not noticeable.
- F (FAIL) Does not meet minimum requirements; fails to adequately demonstrate comprehension, communication skills, and effort.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own performance. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

**Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism**

Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of this course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student. Any miss-representation of work shall be evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student will result in a failing grade for the course.
