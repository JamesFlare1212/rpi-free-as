## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will use the ‘visual essay’ as a focus of study, leading to a deeper understanding of visual rhetoric from both experiential and academic perspectives. The course will begin by delineating the parameters of the genre, examining iterations of the ‘film essay’ and ‘videographic essay,’ as well as ‘photo essays,’ ‘comics essays,’ and instances of ‘essays’ that exist in video game and other formats. As a final project, in a medium of their choosing, students will create visual essays of their own, on a topic of personal, professional, or cultural relevance.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

ASSESSMENT: MODIFIED POINTS-BASED ‘GAMEFUL’ GRADING

Introduction:

Grading for this class may unfold a little differently than you’re used to. In many of my classes, I’ve been using something called ‘gameful grading.’ In this class we’ll use a simplified version of it. Your performance is based on the points that you earn, much like in a game system (a video game, a table-top role-playing game, or what have you)

Everyone begins with 0 points. At the start of the semester, there are 300,000+ points to be earned. As you accomplish tasks, you build up points towards your final rank (translated to a final grade). There are required tasks for everyone to complete, and then there are optional tasks through which you can gain more points. If you only do the required tasks, you can achieve a respectable grade in the class, but if you want a higher rank then you’ll need to undertake one or more of the optional tasks, or go above and beyond the scope of the assignments for the required tasks and daily participation.

In this class, the optional tasks are largely the ‘explorations’--that are opportunities to 1) apply knowledge from each of our learning units in new ways, or 2) expand your understanding or repertoire in connection with one of our study unite, or 3) undertake a creative exploration of one of the visual essay formats that we’re covering. The ‘explorations’ can be thought of as precursors to your larger project, in that you are encouraged to expand it into your final project, or have it feed into that; you’re not required to do so (it’s fine if your exploration ends there)

‘Medal’ Based Scoring

Assignments will be assessed using a ‘medal’ system. For most daily and small assignments, that ‘medal’ will reflect the following categories. For certain larger projects, we may establish a more detailed rubric for grading, or reach an understanding through discussion and through my pedagogical expertise. In many cases, particularly for larger assignments, you may be invited (or ask) to revise an assignment in order to improve its medal.

Medal Points % Description

Diamond* 120% A sparkling exemplar for the class, full of dazzling insight

Platinum* 110% A perceptive, clever, and resilient exploration of assignment

Gold 100% Weighty, polished, addressing all required aspects of assignment

Silver 90% A response with clarity and strength, but maybe a tiny tarnish

Bronze 80% Relatively solid response; some clarity, some weaknesses

Copper 70% Mostly complete, but may bend under the stress of examination

Nickel 60% Partial or relatively flimsy assignment that needs strengthening

Tin\*\* 50% Dull or lifeless response, crumples in the breeziest of discussions

*To receive a ‘Platinum’ medal requires that you go above and beyond the requirements of the assignment, and the ‘Diamond’ requires even more in-depth work of great quality.

\*\*Below ‘Tin’ means you simply failed to turn in the assignment, or it’s too incomplete to grade
