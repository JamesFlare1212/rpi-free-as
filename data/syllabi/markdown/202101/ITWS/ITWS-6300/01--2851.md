## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course introduces engineering and science students to the business issues that are critical in turning new technology into market-winning products. Students should take this course if they desire to maximize their effectiveness as technologists by understanding the business, customer, competitive and financial considerations that impact the work involved in bringing new products to market and making them successful. It is intended for advanced undergraduate and graduate students in engineering or science curricula.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course investigates business-related considerations in successfully commercializing new technology.  
It exposes technologists to the critical business issues an existing enterprise faces in creating successful products/services and investigates how these issues impact technical work:  
\- market and customer analysis  
\- beating the competition  
\- planning and managing for profitability  
\- high-tech marketing and sales  
\- business partnerships and acquisitions.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Upon successful completion, the student will have a rudimentary capability to: 1. analyze a company’s financial performance and assess its opportunities and challenges for future growth 2. assess the barriers to success in a particular market and select the appropriate target customers 3. identify a company’s competitive advantages by performing a comprehensive analysis of its direct and indirect competition 4. identifying the “whole product” and decide on appropriate product differentiators 5. select appropriate sales/distribution channels and promotional methods 6. integrate the course learnings to determine a plan for specification of a new product 7. identify best practices in new product development and understand the challenges in integrating the marketing, operations and product development functions 8. understand how technical people interact with marketing, sales and operations personnel, and how they contribute to resolution of business issues 9. understand the ways in which business issues can and should impact technical work.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Class sessions will be a combination of instructor-led presentation, interactive class discussion and group analysis of real businesses and business situations (case studies). Assignments will largely involve analyzing real-world business problems, understanding the business/technology interplay, and identifying resolutions and courses of action. A majority of the learning in this course will occur during class discussion/dialogue about the weekly readings and associated assignment questions (readings include discourses on business concepts, opinion-based pieces on business best-practices, and “cases” that describe specific business situations faced by real companies). Accordingly, completing the weekly readings, answering the assigned homework questions, attending class regularly, and participating actively in class discussions are all required for successful completion of the course.  
Homework: Each week, questions will be assigned based on the readings. (Class Session Topics and weekly readings are listed in this syllabus.) Usually, the student will be asked to analyze aspects of a business situation/problem, and then to develop an opinion and/or recommendation. Each week, each student must submit brief, written answers to the assigned questions. Homework submissions will be graded acceptable or unacceptable, and will be returned with comments only at the request of the student, since homework will be reviewed in class.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grades will be determined in equal part (25% each) by: (1) active and insightful participation in class discussions (based in part on the readings/assignment for the week), (2) performance on the weekly-assigned homework questions (percent of assignments graded as acceptable), (3) case-based mid-term exam, and (4) case-based final exam.

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own.
