## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

## Fundamentals of aqueous geochemistry as applied to the evolution of natural waters. Principles of chemical equilibrium, activity models for solutes, acid-base chemistry, redox chemistry, mineral solubility, aqueous complexes, ion exchange, and stable isotopes will be covered. The carbonate system, weathering reactions, and redox chemistry are examined in detail. Students learn theory, the basics of analytical techniques, computation methods, and the use of computer programs for speciation, mass balance and reaction path calculations.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course aims to provide a framework for understanding the chemistry of natural waters by examining water-rock reactions in a variety of geologic environments. By the end of the course students should be able to interpret geochemical data, apply thermodynamic principles to constrain geochemical models, and identify major geologic and geochemical processes that affect the chemistry of natural waters.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- hrough class lectures, practical sessions, written assignments and projects, students should: • Interpret, illustrate and explain basic geochemical data that pertain to natural and contaminated water chemistry. • Apply thermodynamic constraints to geochemical models • Calculate equilibrium chemical speciation in simple aqueous systems • Demonstrate knowledge of key geochemical concepts including acid-base chemistry, redox chemistry and water-rock reactions • Develop activity, solubility and redox diagrams to interpret geochemical processes • Identify analytical techniques best suited for different data requirements

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

*Problem Sets:* There will be ~biweekly problem sets during the course of the semester. The purpose of these is to reinforce material covered in lecture, and will focus on the quantitative aspects of geochemistry.

*Exams:* There will be three midterm exams distributed over the semester. There will be no final exam in this course.

*Attendance:* Attendance will not be taken. However, if you do miss class, you are responsible for obtaining any material, announcements and assignments covered in class.

*ERTH6690/CHEM6690/ENVE 6110:* Students seeking graduate credit will be required to complete an additional project that demonstrates the application of fundamental geochemical concepts to a specific research topic of the student’s choosing. An oral in class presentation will be scheduled at the end of the semester. Grading for this assignment will become part of the students Problem Set grade (45%).
