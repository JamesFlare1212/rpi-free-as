## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course brings together ideas, concepts, and case studies from sound studies, sensory studies, disability studies, media studies, and science and technology studies to explore how we sense and experience the actual, virtual, and mediated worlds we live in and shape. We consider how we individually and collectively develop and circulate situated knowledge through and in our bodies in these diverse spaces. We will draw on case studies from across music/sound and social justice/protest, social media, video games and navigable narratives, mobile media apps, music/sound and dance, ASL translations and interpretations circulated by the D/deaf community on YouTube, Pauline Oliveros’ Deep Listening practices, how we feel sound at Electronic Music/EDM events, and other approaches to multisensory listening. There is also room in the course for arts and applied practice and we will explore various movement traditions in class that will be applied to our diverse pandemic spaces. The exploration of movement often reveals sensitivities concerning the body, identity, gesture, culture, sensory awareness, perception, space, orientation, kinesthesia, time, relationships to people and objects, as well as cultural notions of embodiment and the senses. This course employs practice-based work, creative experimentation, and traditional forms of research (reading, viewing media, writing, discussions) as the primary methods to illuminate the issues concerning enactive (movement) knowledge.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will have significant contact with diverse perspectives on and examples of global music cultures of movement and sound.
- Students will gain a detailed understanding of cultural notions of embodiment and the senses
- Students will develop analytic and auditory skills to understand and communicate experiences of sensory awareness, perception, space, orientation, kinesthesia in music and sound.
- Students will become sensitive and informed listeners and will be able to articulate their experience of movement, sound, and the senses through practice-based work, creative experimentation, and traditional forms of research.
- Students will gain an appreciation of the incredible diversity of contemporary musical and movement practices and traditions and will be able to situate them within a broader historical and cultural context.
- Practice developing, supporting through different kinds of research evidence, and communicating research questions and arguments.
- Practice in analytic writing and explication of assigned texts.

## **Grading Criteria**

**[A]: Truly Outstanding**

- presents well-articulated, well-structured analysis of relevant issues
- develops arguments with *specific* points and relevant examples
- articulates a clear position on the issues at hand
- demonstrates superior command of standard written English, with few or no errors

**[A-/B+/B]: Strong**

- presents a generally thoughtful, well-ordered analysis of relevant issues
- develops arguments with *specific* points and relevant examples
- presents a clear position on the issues at hand
- demonstrates facility with standard written English, but may contain minor errors and/or awkward constructions

**[B-]: Satisfactory**

- presents a competent analysis of the relevant issues, with some flaws
- develops arguments with relevant but insufficient and/or vague reasons and examples
- presents some issues with organization and/or focus
- demonstrates sufficient control of English language to express ideas with acceptable clarity, but may contain errors

**[C+/C/C-]: Seriously Flawed**

Exhibits one or more of the following major problems:

- unclear or seriously limited in addressing main issues
- provides few, if any, relevant reasons or examples to support arguments
- poorly focused and/or organized
- serious problems with control of English language, interfering with clarity of and expression of meaning

**[D and Below]: Fundamentally Deficient**

Exhibits one or more of the following major problems:

- provides little evidence of understanding the issues
- shows little or no evidence of the ability to develop an organized response (including extreme brevity)
- severe issues with control of English language, interfering profoundly with clarity and expression of meaning
