## Course Goals

### Goals:

After successfully completing this course, students will be able to:  
• Interpret, compare and critique a variety of forms of contemporary art  
• Develop a daily artistic process  
• Develop and articulate a personal philosophy toward contemporary art and artmaking  
• Complete a project or body of work for public exhibition  
• Effectively document work and create a portfolio  
• Contribute to the planning and execution of public presentation of their work, in exhibition, performance, or online venue  
• Complete a written academic thesis articulating their artistic intent, influence, process, and project outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students are expected to commit to the project idea and to develop their work progressively over the course of the semester, both in execution of production and writing. In this second semester students will:  
1\. Complete the production of their project, with regular progress updates.  
2\. Complete the written thesis, building on the draft written in Creative Seminar I.  
3\. Participate in class discussions and critiques  
4\. Document their work and create an online portfolio  
5\. Plan and mount a public presentation in the form of a physical exhibition, performance, or online exhibition.  
Students must demonstrate satisfactory achievement of course objectives through fulﬁllment of course projects and by contributing to class discussions.  
If a student completes all assignments adequately, participates in class discussions and activities, and has a good attendance record, she/he can expect to receive a grade of C. Grades of B and A are given for work, participation and engagement that substantially exceed the average expectation.  
All appeals must be brought to the instructor during ofﬁce hours or at a scheduled time convenient to both parties. Keep in mind that an appeal has the potential to raise or lower your grade.

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
