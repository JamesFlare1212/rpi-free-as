## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

In this course, students will acquire a broad foundation of theoretical knowledge, musicianship skills, and practical experience in order to create and understand music in a contemporary context. We will explore sound as a physical phenomenon and will cover the basics of music theory and literacy in a way that can be applicable to a wide variety of musical traditions and practices. In addition to continuing work on theoretical concepts and musicianship skills, we will pursue creative projects, learn how to approach the music around us critically, and examine the ways in which music can acquire extramusical meanings and resonances. No formal musical background is required.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will gain a fluency in musical notation.
- Students will be able to recognize and identify (both on paper and by ear) the fundamental building blocks of music theory, including pitches, intervals, rhythms, scales, meters, and chords.
- Students will be able to sing in movable-DO solfege.
- Students will be able to create original melodic, rhythmic, and harmonic compositions.
- Students will develop the foundational skills of critical listening and acquire a vocabulary for articulating what they hear.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Class Participation:** This is a hands-on, experiential course. Therefore, students are expected to participate in all class activities, including discussions, singing, rhythm exercises, improvisation, and other forms of in-class interaction. Students will be graded on effort, integrity of interaction, and improvement over the course of the semester.

**In-Class Workshops:** Throughout the term, we will do in-class workshops to apply the theoretical principles we have covered and to explore different ways of interacting with musical and non-musical sound. These will be graded in terms of engagement, open-mindedness, and written work (when applicable).

**Final Project:** The semester’s work will culminate in a final project which ties together the material covered throughout the semester. The project will be graded on grasp of the material, clarity of presentation, and critical understanding.

**Written Work:** Written work will demonstrate students’ grasp of analytical concepts and the application of theoretical principles. It is graded on grasp of the relevant theoretical concepts.

**Creative Work:** Creative work provides students the opportunity to apply theoretical concepts and skills to their own creative practice. Depending on the unit, students are invited to strike a balance between the limitations of a given historical technique and their own creative intuitions.

**Performance Work:** Performance activities are assigned for each unit. It is understood that everyone in the class comes to performance with their own distinctive history and level of experience. Performance work will be graded according to effort and improvement throughout the semester.

**Ear-Training Work:** Ear-Training work allows students to relate a sonic perceptual experience to a conceptual understanding. As with performance, students will come to class with different levels of experience and different levels of inherent aural sensitivity. The work will be graded according to effort and improvement throughout the semester.
