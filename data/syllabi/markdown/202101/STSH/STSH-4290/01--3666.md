Coursework Overview

Responses to Media

You will respond to a mix of readings, films, podcasts, lectures, workshops, etc. Your responses will also take a variety of forms, including written and oral responses. You will be encouraged, throughout, to find concepts, principles, and ideas and run with them - i.e. let us know how these media changed your thinking and/or practice. A key part of your responses will include the creative generation of specific, interesting, useful questions.

Participation

You are expected to come to class prepared to discuss the readings/watchings/listenings/etc., and to bring ideas with you from other classes, things you’re reading, discussions with your friends, various experiences, etc. You will be asked to help contribute to a culture that creates both a safe(r) space (built on a foundation of respect) and encourages students to stretch a bit beyond their comfort zones and push their edges (answering a question if you’re not entirely sure what to say, for example). You will also give regular updates on your progress in your design/project work and how they relate to the readings, workshops, and other material we encounter. Your participation in this class should take into account the following guidelines for developing a thriving class culture, collaboratively developed on the second class:

Project Proposal Iterations

Early in the semester, you’ll write up an initial project proposal that focuses heavily on your plan for understanding the contexts you want to work in/on, for developing your goals, and for your initial analysis and assessment of existing conditions and existing attempts at solutions. A second proposal will build on the first one asking about your plan for developing design directions (to meet your goals) that are firmly rooted in your analysis and assessment, for developing detailed designs, for implementing your designs, and for evaluating your designs.

Documented Design Work

This grade is really about what you do - the work of design. In order to familiarize yourself with the whole design process and principles that make up the backbone of this course, you will work on individual (but not isolated!) projects within these primary design elements: context, goals, analysis and assessment, design, implementation, and evaluation. There will be a variety of in class activities and homework assignments that contribute to this deliverable. You are expected to work on your design project for at least two hours a week. By the end of the semester, you should have a “capstone” that draws all of this work together. This should be the equivalent (as far as effort, breadth, depth) of a 15 page (double-spaced) research paper, with about 20 solid references, but can actually take other forms, and you are strongly encouraged to go out and build something (a garden, an organization, a database, new connections, etc.). If you wish to focus your efforts on implementation of your design, or an experimental/alternative mode of expression, make sure you’re checking in regularly with the professor (and other advisors you cultivate) because it’s much harder to spell out specific expectations when every project can take such different forms. You will largely be graded on whether you really tried on the design process and principles taught in this class and for a few of you, your designs might just so beautifully embody this that relatively little formal writing is needed. For many of you, it will be important to have a solid writing component that accompanies the other parts of your design to tell us how this embodies the design process and principles. Near the end of the semester we will have a science fair style final party, inviting the broader community to engage with your work.

Experiential Learning

You will connect with an organization or other experiences (on campus or off) that somehow resonates with your design project. This is an opportunity for ethnographic research, user-centered design, collaboration, drawing together theory and practice, etc. You should complete at least 10 hours total, provide weekly journal updates (solid entries tend to be at least 150 words), and write a four page (double spaced) reflection paper due near the end of the class. Some questions that might get the ball rolling in your writing, along with resources for finding an organization to work with, are here.

Presentations /Expressions

Throughout the semester you will present updates on your progress regularly. These reports on your development (as a researcher and designer) will culminate with more formal presentations near the end of the semester. You are strongly encouraged to go beyond the typical (boring) powerpoint approach to presentations.

Other Work

You will have some other fairly short assignments throughout the semester. Many of these will ask you to reflect on yourself (your values, lineages, skills, passions, etc.) and how you fit into your designs.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic honesty is an expectation in this course. It is unacceptable to re-submit work prepared for another course, or to submit work prepared by another as your own. Citations, using a standard citation style of your choice (such as APA or MLA) must be included for all written work, particularly those that draw on either direct or indirect quotations. Please see the Student Handbook for complete guidelines on academic honesty. Students who are found to have violated the academic dishonesty policy are likely to fail the course. If you find yourself considering academic dishonesty, or any act that might remotely resemble academic dishonesty, you should come talk to me. Students who are found to have violated the academic dishonesty policy will fail the assignment in question and are likely to fail the course.

A final grade of incomplete will not be given to any student unless negotiated with me in advance of the final day of class/final assignment deadline.

You may appeal your final grade through a written statement describing the grounds on which a change of grade should be considered appropriate. Before initiating a formal appeal, please feel free to share your concerns with me. If you decide to pursue an appeal, the written statement must reference one of the three criteria identified in the Student Handbook as grounds for appeal.
