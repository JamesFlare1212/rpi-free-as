## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course involves a philosophical analysis of some of the basic moral issues raised by recent and anticipated developments in the areas of biology and medicine. The general question “What are moral problems, and how does one resolve them?” is examined in the context of concrete cases involving issues such as racial healthcare disparities, abortion, euthanasia, organ transplants, experimentation on human patients, patient rights and emerging ethical issues in the biomedical field. This is a communication-intensive course. Students will be introduced to some of the prominent ethical theories in philosophy (Utilitarianism, Kantian theory, Virtue ethics, etc.) and will use these theories to examine contemporary issues in medicine.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will recall the content and make inferences on key important points.
- Students will be able to demonstrate critical thinking and create solutions to problems based on learned content.
- Students will demonstrate ability to compare and contrast differing perspectives in the cases of particular ethical issues.
- Students will be able to evaluate multiple perspectives concerning bioethical issues and recognize that different value systems may lead to different ethical decisions.
- Students will be able to assess complex bioethical issues and the processes used to build resolution.
- Students will have the ability to express philosophical ideas and defend them effectively in argument, both in writing and orally.
- Students will be able to understand the process of writing and revision for an academic audience.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Course requirements consist of the following:

1. **Tests (3)** **(45% --- 15% each):** These open book tests (you may use your notes and textbook) will consist of multiple choice and short answer questions on reading and lecture material. **Test 1 – 3/1/21**, **Test 2 – 3/29/21 and Test 3 – 5/3/21.** There is no final exam for this course.
2. **Reflection Paper (****5%):** The student will find an article in the popular media regarding a contemporary ethical issue and write an analysis of the position presented in the article. More information on this assignment will be distributed during the second week of class. This must be submitted to LMS by the last day of class **(5/3/21)**. The sooner you hand them in the sooner you will have your grade.
3. **Journals (10%):** During each lecture students will be given a prompt to reflect and write on. These reflections will be recorded online through the LMS “Journal” tab under “Assignments” each day. They will be evaluated by the instructor throughout the course and a grade will be assigned on **4/26/21.**
4. **Final Paper (35%)**: The student will submit a paper of approximately 2500 words that examines an ethical issue of particular interest to them. The student will explain the ethical issue and evaluate the issue using Utilitarian and Kantian theory. An extensive explanation of each theory and thorough application of the theories is required. Basically, the student will explain the issue, explain the two theories, apply the two theories to the issue and then explain which theory best analyzed the issued including a discussion of the strengths and weaknesses of each theory. More information on the assignment will be provided in class. There will be five steps to be completed for this paper:
   1. **Peer Conference (3%) – 2/22/21 (in class assignment) –** bring at least two articles you are considering using for your paper**.**
   2. **Consultation at The Center for Global Communication and Design (COMM-D) (****http://www.commd.rpi.edu** **) (3%) – Due: 3/15/21**
   3. **Submission of Draft for Instructor’s Review (4%) – DUE: 4/1/21**
   4. **Final Paper (25%) – 4/29/21**
5. **Class Participation (5%) :** I cannot emphasize enough how important class participation is. The online classroom environment will be a safe and stimulating place for the critical examination of the topics we cover. Through these discussions we will delve deeper into the subject matter of the readings. Participation includes coming to class prepared, which includes doing the assigned reading(s), and actively and thoughtfully participating in class discussions. Participation may also consist of contributions to the weekly discussion board questions. We will discuss this requirement on our first day of class. Please do not mistake the participation grade as a “freebie.” Many people mistakenly believe that they can throw anything out there, no matter how illogically construed, and it counts as “doing philosophy.” This is not the case. Your discussion contributions must convey a thoughtful analysis of the material being covered.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
