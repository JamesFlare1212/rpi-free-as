## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will introduce you to the field of motion picture studies from aesthetic, critical, reflexive, historical, and conceptual/theoretical perspectives. The aim is to examine, enhance, and transform your personal relationship to film in a dialogue-based setting. Using student-centered learning techniques, the course will reflect your interests as a learning community, with the aim of developing within you a thoughtful, informed, critical, and empathetic approach to motion pictures, informed by an awareness of the global mediascape. We will focus on the traditions of fictional filmmaking. We’ll watch lots of movies and think deeply about how they do what they do.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Course Goals

1\) You will develop a faculty for critical media engagement with motion pictures, learning how to apply aesthetic, historical, and critical frameworks as tools towards deepening your understanding of them. You will learn some of the fundamental terms and concepts used in film studies. Of particular importance is learning how to make connections between conceptual issues and cinema as practice (in viewing, critiquing, sharing, and creating).

2\) As a complement to these critical tools, you will enhance your reflexive media awareness—your processual and dialogic understanding of your relationship to motion pictures—so that you can more fully articulate your experience of motion pictures, contextualize it, and empathize with others’ perspectives. You will develop your communicative/generative skills by articulating and refining your thoughts in oral, written, and presentational forms, and (if you are so inclined) by creating media works in various formats as a way of engaging with concepts we encounter in this class.

3\) Although this class focuses on film/motion pictures, it’s lessons can be applied to, and contextualized in, the larger mediascape; you should gain a deeper understanding of how media shapes us, as individuals, as groups and communities, and as cultures.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

ASSESSMENT: ‘CINEMA POINTS’-BASED ‘GAMEFUL’ GRADING

Introduction:

Grading for this class will unfold a little differently than you’re used to. Using what might be called a ‘gameful’ approach, your performance in the class is based on the points that you earn, much like in a game system (a video game, a table-top role-playing game, or what have you). Like in some games, or in many popular films, there’s even a ‘clock’ defined by milestones in the semester; time runs out quickly!

Everyone begins with 0 points. At the start of the semester, there are 460,000+ points to be earned. As you accomplish tasks in the class, you build up points towards your final grade. There are central tasks for everyone to complete, and there are some optional tasks as well as ‘cinematic explorations’ that are opportunities to apply knowledge in new ways, or simply explore some aspect of motion pictures that fascinates you. You can often revise or expand daily assignments or required tasks into explorations, and/or expand these explorations and assignments into your final project.

Ranks:

Below are ‘ranks’ that you can achieve by earning points, and how they will correspond to traditional grades at the end of the semester. You can figure out what kind of a rank you are looking for, and work towards that goal by accumulating points. You can convert this to percentages if that’s easier for you, but I’d like this to be a different way for you to engage with the material. You can choose whether you’d rather think of yourself as a media maker (the left side of the rankings), or as a film critic/film viewer (the right side of the rankings)…

‘Medal’ Based Scoring

Assignments will be assessed using a ‘medal’ system. For most daily and small assignments, that ‘medal’ will reflect the following categories. For certain larger projects, we may establish a more detailed rubric for grading, or reach an understanding through discussion and through my pedagogical expertise. In many cases, particularly for larger assignments, you may be invited (or ask) to revise an assignment in order to improve its medal.

Medal Points % Description

Diamond* 120% A sparkling exemplar for the class, full of dazzling insight

Platinum* 110% A perceptive, clever, and resilient exploration of assignment

Gold 100% Weighty, polished, addressing all required aspects of assignment

Silver 90% A response with clarity and strength, but maybe a tiny tarnish

Bronze 80% Relatively solid response; some clarity, some weaknesses

Copper 70% Mostly complete, but may bend under the stress of examination

Nickel 60% Partial or relatively flimsy assignment that needs strengthening

Tin\*\* 50% Dull or lifeless response, crumples in the breeziest of discussions

*To receive a ‘Platinum’ medal requires that you go above and beyond the requirements of the assignment, and the ‘Diamond’ requires even more in-depth work of great quality.

\*\*Below ‘Tin’ means you simply failed to turn in the assignment, or it’s too incomplete to grade
