**Course Description**

A continuation of CHME 4010. Course includes topics on multi-dimensional transport processes, potential, boundary layer and turbulent fluid flows, convective heat and mass transfer processes, friction factors and drag in and around solid objects, heat and mass exchangers, and radiation heat transfer. The course extends the use of numerical methods to apply to multidimensional problems, convective heat and mass transfer problems, and the simulation of more complicated fluid flows including turbulence approximations. Credit not allowed for both this course and ENGR 2250.

**Homework**

Students are encouraged to discuss the underlying concepts and approaches with their classmates and collaborate on homework assignments in teams or groups. Each student team will be required to turn in their own individual assignment. Note that the work turned in should reflect only the team’s efforts (no copying or duplicate assignments should be turned in). Homework will be **administered through the course LMS** and will be downloaded and submitted through the LMS website. Late homework will not be accepted. Homework is graded based on good faith – a fair effort will receive 100%. 0 – not turned in; 1 – partially done; 2 – good faith effort.

**Quizzes**

Multiple-choice quizzes will be **given on LMS** to review concepts introduced in lecture and reflect the most recent homework assignments. The frequency of quizzes will vary depending on the class progression but will generally be due every other week. The lowest quiz grade will be dropped. This is done to provide for sickness, interviews, emergencies, personal problems, and other such exigencies that inevitably occur during the semester. No official excuses are necessary.

**Exams**

Two equally weighted take-home exams (25% each) will be given throughout the semester. The first exam will cover chapters 8, 9 & 10, and the second will cover chapters 11 & 12. The final exam will be a student-created problem set due at the close of the semester. Details on the final are provided on the course LMS.

**Class Policies**

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational process. The *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty, and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work.

*Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty of a zero on the assignment/quiz/exam for the first offense and an **F** grade for the course for any subsequent offense.* If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

**Note About Showing Work:**

In this class, the process of getting to an answer is as important (if not more) than the actual answer, and you need to be able to clearly communicate that process. Therefore, you must show, explain, and justify all your work to receive credit. ***If you do not show your work, you will receive zero credit (even if the answer is correct)****.* For example, a series of equations without explanation will receive minimal credit even if all equations are correct. If you are algebraically manipulating equations to simplify, you should state what steps are being taken.

Unless specifically requested in the problem statement, it is not acceptable to use packages such as MATLAB, Maple, Mathematica, etc. to perform manipulations, integrals, etc. ***If software is used to arrive at a solution the program name and the capacity in which it was used should be stated***.
