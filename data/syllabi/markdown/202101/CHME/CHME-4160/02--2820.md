## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The second term of a laboratory course on experimental analysis of the operations and processes of chemical engineering. Emphasis is placed on planning of experiments, data evaluation, and report writing.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Laboratory manual(s) and lecture notes (electronically posted on LMS)

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The students who finish this course in a satisfactory manner will be able to:  
i) communicate results of experiments effectively in written form via laboratory reports.  
ii) gather information from multiple sources (books, databases, journal articles, dissertations, web pages, etc.) to supply necessary information to analyze results from experiments.  
iii) work together as a team to collect, analyze, and interpret data, draw conclusions, and present results in laboratory reports;  
iv) apply the theoretical principles traditionally used by chemical engineers (e.g., fluid flow, heat and mass transfer, reaction kinetics, and mathematics) to analyze and interpret laboratory-scale unit operations.

## Course Content

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Letter grades will be assigned to (team-based) laboratory reports by the instructor in charge of each experiment. Course grades will consist of an average grade over all reports submitted. The instructors may adjust individual students' course grades upward or downward at their discretion to account for the contributions of individual team members to report writing, based on the Foreman Report submitted by each team.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

All work in this course is to be done independently by each laboratory group. No collaboration between different groups is permitted; copying from previous years' laboratory reports is considered cheating and will result in disciplinary action. Questions should be addressed to the Instructor or TAs. Evidence of collaboration or falsification of laboratory data will be dealt with according to the Student Handbook. Additionally, a signed ‘Foreman Report’ must be completed and attached to each final report documenting the contribution of each individual member in the group.

Failing to contribute to writing of a given laboratory is justification for receiving a grade of zero on the report, even if you were present when the experiment was being performed. Students who contribute less to the writing of their reports than expected by their teammates may receive substantial deductions on the final report grade; there is no stipulation that all team members will receive the same grade on a given report. The instructor will use the Foreman report and brief interviews with team members to determine "% effort" contributions when complaints or disputes about effort levels arise.
