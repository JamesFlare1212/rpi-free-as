## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The experiments in this course have been designed to represent the basic process by which a biotechnology product is developed and manufactured. You will create a recombinant organism that produces a protein product of interest. You will grow this organism and then extract, purify, and assay the protein product. In this way, you will get a feel for the world of biotechnology and biomanufacturing. The protein product that we will be producing in this course is b-galactosidase (also referred to as LacZ). This is an enzyme that catalyzes the hydrolysis of lactose to glucose and galactose. Indeed, low levels of this enzyme in humans give rise to lactose intolerance, and it is used commercially in Lactaid and other milk products to break down lactose to produce low-lactose dairy products. It is also commonly used as a reporter gene in many molecular biology studies, so its gene is widely available in a variety of cloning systems. In addition, several artificial substrates for the enzyme exist which yield colored products; hence, there are several relatively easy assays for its presence and activity.

This lab class incorporates many assays in which quantitative data is collected and analyzed. You will obtain hands-on experience in generating sets of data, as well as gain a realistic understanding of concepts such as dimensional analysis, experimental error, standard curves and linear ranges, and data fitting.  
In this class, you will also learn how to prepare for laboratory experiments through watching tutorial videos and completing pre-lab assignments. These pre-labs help ensure that you understand (prior to starting the experiment) the technical concepts involved in each experiment, the materials and supplies needed, and the relevant safety concerns.

The main assignments in this class are designed to mimic an industrial setting. As such, concise, accurate, and professional communication skills are required. Many aspects of the experimental setup will be performed by the TAs. However, you are ultimately expected to take ownership and responsibility for understanding how to conduct your lab experiments, analyze your results, and present your findings.

You will notice that we are using many premade reagents and kits. While this is not standard practice in university research laboratories (although it is becoming more common), it is fairly common in industry. Purchased reagents and kits offer better consistency and reliability but are relatively more expensive. Furthermore, many buffers and reagents in this class will be prepared ahead of time by the TAs. You are still expected to know the contents of all reagents, solutions, buffers, and kits used in class. (Those of you planning on going to graduate school will eventually need to learn to prepare your own reagents.)

Working in biotechnology requires a strong understanding of the nature of biological systems; this is best undertaken by direct laboratory experience. The systems in biochemical engineering work are quite distinct from almost any other in engineering, chemical or otherwise. This uniqueness is founded on the fact that biochemical engineering systems are usually living systems, i.e., the biochemical engineer very often deals with microorganisms that are alive and consume things common to all living plants and animals. Even when microorganisms are not strictly involved, as in enzyme engineering, the protein molecules one works with require special treatment, unlike the treatment of non-biologically-derived chemicals handled in the chemical process industries. The special features of biochemical engineering systems are not intuitively appreciated except by hands-on experience.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students who successfully complete this course will be able to demonstrate:

- Practical mastery of bioprocessing laboratory techniques, including the ability to plan and execute a coherent series of measurements and analyze them quantitatively
- The ability to search for, comprehend, and properly implement experimental protocols relevant to bioprocess engineering
- The ability to plan and carry out a series of assigned tasks as part of a group consistent with norms in the engineering profession
- The ability to write coherent reports based on the laboratory work, consistent with formats and standards found in engineering and scientific papers
- The ability to effectively communicate the goals, procedures, results and conclusions, including a critical analysis, of a series of experiments.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Lectures:** All lectures will be delivered remotely via Webex. Students are expected to attend online in real-time to allow for an interactive learning experience, except for those who have been approved to be remote. In addition to taking attendance, quizzes or other graded activities may be administered during the lecture. If a student is unable to attend the live lecture (e.g., for illness, job interview, other emergency), the instructors must be notified as soon as possible, and an excused absence may be granted. Lectures will be recorded and archived on LMS (Blackboard).

**Labs:** For Spring 2021, to ensure that the density of personnel in the lab remains low and that a safe 6-ft working distance can be maintained between workstations, each team will be split into “Group A” and “Group B”. Group A and Group B students will take turns attending lab in person, typically on an alternating week basis. The group assignments for in-lab attendance are listed in the syllabus schedule. Students who are not attending lab in-person in a particular week are considered to be attending “virtual lab” and are expected to be interacting with their in-lab teammate through Webex during their lab session to advise and observe the lab work. Wide angle webcams with microphones will be provided to each team to facilitate this experience, but each student attending lab in-person must bring their own laptop. Disposable keyboard covers will be provided so that you may work with your laptop with gloves on within the lab.

Hands-on learning is a *crucial* aspect of this laboratory course. ***Thus, in-person attendance during your assigned timeslots is required***. If a student is unable to attend lab for excusable reasons (e.g., illness, job interview, required quarantine, other emergency), the instructors must be notified as far in advance as possible, and arrangements should be made for the other group member to perform the lab instead. ***More than one unexcused absence in lab will result in failing the course*.**

### Other Course Policies:

Laboratory Safety

- Face masks, safety glasses, lab coats, gloves and laptop keyboard covers are mandatory at all times in the laboratory. Lab coats, gloves and keyboard covers are provided. If you do not have safety glasses and lab coat on, you will be told to leave the laboratory and receive no credit for the experiment. There will be no exceptions!
- **Do not wear contact lenses in the laboratory.**
- Additional safety procedures will be covered during the first laboratory period/orientation
- Specific safety precautions are included within the laboratory procedures and proper use of any equipment will be demonstrated in class.
- *Always ask the instructor or TA if you have any questions or concerns about the proper handling of chemicals and biological materials or the proper operation of equipment.*
- Materials safety data sheets (MSDS) for chemicals and reagents used are provided in the lab and on the lab website (LMS).
- An eye wash station, safety shower and fire extinguisher is located next to each entrance to the lab.
- No food or drinks are allowed in the lab.

Laboratory Waste Procedures

- All materials that have touched live biological samples (*E. coli*, yeast, other cells) must be disposed of as biohazard waste – including disposable gloves.
- The instructor or TA will dispose of liquid cell culture waste. Petri dishes may be placed in the red biohazard waste container.
- All sharps (razor blades, needles) should be placed in red “Sharps” containers.
- All pipet tips should be placed in the “Biohazard Benchtop Keepers” provided to each group.
- Additional disposal instructions for waste generated in the lab will be provided by the TA or instructor. *Always ask if you have any questions or concerns about the proper disposal of laboratory waste.*

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:** All quizzes, worksheets, pre-labs, and lab notebooks are expected to be individual work. As described above, memos, final written reports, and oral reports will be completed in teams. You may discuss or share your laboratory results with other classmates; however, the writing must be your team’s. All reference materials (including web materials) used in preparation of reports must be cited appropriately using footnotes and/or a bibliography. Results obtained by other teams may be discussed in your reports with their permission but must be clearly attributed. One instance of unacceptable collaboration or plagiarism will result in a reduction of the final course grade by one letter grade (e.g., B to C). A second instance of academic dishonesty will result in failure of the course. When in doubt about what may qualify as plagiarism, ask the instructor before the assignment due date!

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

**Notebooks:** Lab notebooks will be maintained electronically using OneNote, which is available to all students as part of the Office 365 software suite. See https://itssc.rpi.edu/hc/en-us/articles/360010037611-Office-365-and-OneDrive-RPI-Access-Terms-and-Conditions for installation information. Note that you can install OneNote on your own laptop and run it locally or you can run it in the cloud on OneDrive @ RPI. We recommend that you download this software as soon as possible and become familiar with its basic operation. When attending lab, students should bring their laptop so that notes can be taken in real-time. Pre-Lab assignments and lab notebooks will be submitted via Blackboard for periodic evaluation by generating PDFs from your OneNote pages, signing and time-stamping each page via Adobe Reader (or Acrobat). More details on keeping a lab notebook and using OneNote will be provided in the second lecture.

**Preparation for Lab Work and Pre-labs:** Students should watch all relevant tutorials and lab videos and complete the pre-lab for the experiments of the week by **8 PM the evening prior to your lab session**. This is an important exercise for preparing to conduct experiments accurately and safely. Pre-labs must be submitted as individual assignments.

**Memos:** Each team will prepare three memos throughout the semester. The memos are graded on a 10-point scale. The rubric and a sample memo will be available on LMS. They will be due by 5 PM on the dates listed in the syllabus. *Late memos will receive 10% off per day late and may be submitted up to one week late.*

**Quizzes and Short Assignments:** Quizzes will occur occasionally during lecture. They will cover material that you should have read prior to coming to lecture and your understanding of previous experiments. In addition, there may be occasional short assignments to help you prepare for lab sessions and to reinforce material discussed in lecture and during lab.

**Final presentation:** The final oral presentation will be done jointly with your teammate. Detailed guidelines for the presentations will be made available near the end of the semester.

*In all oral and written presentations, proper use of the English language and effective communication is a significant fraction of your grade. Moreover, in oral presentations, the quality of your presentation materials will also weigh into your grade. Remember that these should be considered like reports that you would present to your supervisor, clients, and colleagues in an industrial setting. Therefore, the highest level of professionalism is expected.
