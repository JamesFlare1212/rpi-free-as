## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is designed for students who have completed Chinese I, with the ability to read and use at least 300 basic words (with their collocations), recognize and write approximately 150 Chinese characters, read Chinese Pinyin, and use several basic grammar structures. Students should already be able to communicate on the following topics: exchange greetings; express and reply to appreciation and apology; introduce oneself or others; ask about someone’s nationality; talk about family members and pets; ask and answer questions pertaining to one’s age, profession, and language ability; express time, dates, days of the week, and months; talk about daily life schedules; make an appointment; wish someone “Happy Birthday”; discuss various dishes and their flavors; order at a Chinese restaurant.

Chinese II helps students further develop their skills in listening, speaking, reading, and writing. Students will have the opportunity to participate in interactive communication during class as well as through the Chinese Mentor Program meetings and the Conversation Corner & Mandarin Club (CCMC) activities. Together with the instructor, students will create and enjoy a friendly, supportive, and collaborative Chinese learning community, in which everyone can study in ease, support each other, and grow to reach their own maximum potential.

In addition to developing language skills, this course will provide students with the opportunity to deepen their understanding of Chinese society, people, history, tradition, and culture through a variety of interactive mediums by going beyond the limitations of the textbook. These include,

\* Exploration of topics related to Chinese people’s everyday lives and ways of thinking

\* Exposure to selected Chinese classical poems, folk songs, tongue twisters, or cross-talk art

\* Presentation on Chinese culture

\* Discussion of Chinese sayings or idioms

\* Discovery of the origin and evolution of Chinese characters

\* Watching relevant video clips

\* Enjoying 1-2 Chinese movies

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**COURSE REQUIREMENTS**

1. ATTENDANCE

- (1) Attendance is mandatory. Language training is different from other knowledge learning, which you may make up by reading the materials yourself. Every language practice class directly builds upon the previous ones. Any absence means the loss of an invaluable opportunity to participate in the group exercises and communicate with others, and this loss continues to negatively affect the following classes. In other words, if you miss class even once, you actually miss much more than that two-hour class.
- (2) Each attendance earns 0.5 points towards the final grade (14 points for 28 full attendances each semester if the “active participation” grades achieve 100%). There will be no distinction attempted by the instructor to judge which absences are “excused” and which are “unexcused.” However, students are provided sufficient opportunities to make up the absences if they want (see “Chinese Mentor Program” and “CCMC activities”).
- (3) When you must miss a class, you are responsible for getting the notes/assignments from a classmate. You should also find out what you have missed and catch up before arriving at the next class meeting. ----I will distribute a list of your classmates with email addresses for your convenience.
- (4) Please be punctual. Arriving late for class interrupts the learning process for everyone, and should be avoided as much as possible. Every three times you are marked late counts as one absence.

### Other Course Policies:

2. ACTIVE PARTICIPATION

- (1) All students are strongly encouraged/expected to actively participate during class. This class strives to achieve a comprehensive development of students’ language skills, cultural appreciation, and communicative competence. Therefore, active participation in vocabulary and grammar exercises, role plays, simulations, class discussions, and activities is vitally important.
- (2) Classroom interactions are meant to be enriching and collaborative. Students will be expected to have prepared for the new vocabulary, narrative, and dialogue in each lesson. Class participation should provide some evidence that you have prepared beforehand.
- (3) Students are encouraged and highly appreciated to keep the lines of communication open. I will always stay in the WebEx meeting room and warmly welcome you to stay after class to chat with me or set up a separate appointment.
- (4) Regardless of the virtual instruction model and all kinds of difficulties, I have been putting forth my utmost effort to hold all classes in real time and promote active participation and communicative interaction among students. They expressed great appreciation about our class being “like a family” and “always friendly and inviting”, as well as my “willingness to go the extra mile to answer any questions or concerns that students had”. In this warm family, no one worries if they make a mistake. Any type of participation is highly appreciated. Whether you are asking or answering a question, jumping in to help your classmates, sharing an experience, or sharing a joke, you contribute a lot to the class dynamics. This will be reflected in your grade.
- (5) “Active participation” grades will be assessed using the following rubric:

100% = Excellent participation; makes frequent contributions that add greatly to the class dynamic

90% = Makes regular contributions to class activities; supportive of deepening classmates’

comprehension, shows the curiosity and passion about new knowledge

70% = Makes occasional contributions; may often be quiet but still very attentive

20% = Never initiates to say or ask anything or actively contributes to the class activities; only

passively completes the assigned classwork; occasionally uses cell phone or falls asleep

5% = Frequently absent-minded, falls asleep, or does work unrelated to Chinese

study (including computer and cell phone use), merely maintains a physical presence.

3. ASSIGNMENTS

- (1) Completion of all assignments on or before the due date.

It is the instructor’s belief that students are responsible adults and are to be treated as such. Students will receive full credit ONLY for assignments submitted in advance or on time. Students will STILL be able to receive 50% of the score for the assignments and quizzes made up throughout the semester.

If you do submit work late AND you want credit, you are responsible for keeping track of it. During the LAST WEEK of the semester, please provide me with ONE complete list of the late work you submitted and the date it was submitted. Without this list, you may not receive credit for late submissions.

- (2) Important exercises in both the textbook and workbook will be assigned with some additional work related (if necessary).
- (3) Presentations on Chinese language and culture need to be well prepared and well revised. All students are expected to use what they have learned in the class (vocabulary, grammar, expression, and cultural information) and are very much encouraged to search for and try out some new things.
- (4) Listening exercises, words used in sentences, interviewing native Chinese speakers, news report in Chinese, authentic reading, and/or composition writing may be assigned.
- (5) Reading Fluency Check and Dialogue Performance

Students are expected to fluently read the dialogues (characters, not pinyin) in each lesson. Reading fluency will be checked and graded for each lesson (8 times during the semester). Students are also expected to apply what they have learned from each unit to create and perform/act out their own dialogues without reading from scripts. Dialogue performance will be due for only two out of four units covered. Scores from both reading fluency check and dialogue performance will be counted as the speaking evaluation for this course.

4. QUIZZES / EXAMS

- (1) There will be no mid-term exam. We may watch a Chinese movie and students are expected to complete their initial draft of culture presentation during the mid-term week. Attendance remains mandatory unless otherwise discussed.
- (2) There USED TO be a closed-book final exam covering three language skills (writing, listening, and reading) (The speaking skill is evaluated separately through the Reading Fluency Check and Dialogue Performance). Since all classes were moved to remote instruction, students have been given two options:

They can choose to take the closed-book Final Exam

Then can submit an open-book final writing assignment in Chinese. Detailed instructions and requirements can be found in our shared Box folder.

- (3) There USED TO be 1-2 closed-book quizzes in every lesson to check students’ preparation of new vocabulary and learning of Chinese characters and key sentences from each dialogue. These have been accommodated and replaced by Quizlet. Please see details regarding submission requirements in the Tentative Schedule included in this syllabus. If anyone prefers the closed-book quizzes, please contact me with your request. Then you do not need to submit your Quizlet scores.

5. CHINESE MENTOR PROGRAM (virtual)

It is required for every student to meet with one (any) of our Chinese mentors for a minimum of six (6) hours total during the entire semester (accounting for 6 points in the final grade). Mentoring sessions start from the second week and end in the second to last week of the semester (mornings, afternoons, evenings, or even weekends, per request). Please start early and plan well.

Students who meet with our Chinese mentors for more than 6 hours per semester may use that time to make up absences or earn extra credit. Every 3 hours of mentoring session may be used to make up one class missed. If students have full attendance, they can earn 0.2 points (extra credit) for each additional hour, which will be added to their final grade.

6. CCMC ACTIVITIES (virtual)

The Conversation Corner & Mandarin Club (CCMC) holds regular meetings twice a week (the meeting times and locations are to be announced during the second week of the semester). All students are strongly encouraged to participate as much as possible. Every three times a student fully participates in the CCMC activities, that student may make up one absence.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.   Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute strives to make all learning experiences as accessible as possible. If you anticipate or experience academic barriers based on a disability, please let me know immediately so that we can discuss your options. To establish reasonable accommodations, please register with The Office of Disability Services for Students. After registration, make arrangements with the Director of Disability Services as soon as possible to discuss your accommodations so that they may be implemented in a timely fashion.
