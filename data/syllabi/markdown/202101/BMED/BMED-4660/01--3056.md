## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This graduate/advanced undergraduate hybrid course examines the structural and physiologic properties of muscle, as well as its force production and overall biomechanical function. Muscle structure and function will be explored at the protein, single fiber and whole tissue levels. Discussions will focus primarily on skeletal muscle, and topics will include muscle morphology, cross-bridge theory, molecular motor and actomyosin interaction, Hill-type and Huxley-type models, electromyography, fatigue, muscle inhibition, history-dependent phenomena, *in vitro* and *in vivo* muscle function, and the response to injury. Each topic will be introduced and developed utilizing seminal articles in the literature as well as excerpts from texts, and further discussion on current problems and state-of-the-art experimental techniques will draw on the current scientific literature.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Topical composition of the course (tentative, topics subject to change):  
• muscle morphology  
• the cross-bridge theory  
• mechanical properties of muscle  
• muscle modeling  
• history-dependent behavior  
• muscle injury  
• special topics including:  
\- cardiac muscle  
\- adaptation and sarcomerogenesis  
\- fatigue  
\- muscle control and force-sharing  
\- functional, genetic and molecular response to injury

Students are responsible for all of the course content taught by the instructor, assistant instructors, and student colleagues. All listed readings are part of the course content upon which the student can be evaluated.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Have an ability to relate muscle structure to biomechanical function, at a variety of scales: actomyosin interaction, sarcomere, single fiber, and whole tissue.
- Have an ability to diagnose the functional impairment or dysfunction caused by an irregularity in the Excitation-Contraction coupling process, and how this would change with a proposed intervention.
- Have an ability to demonstrate the application of experimental techniques and muscular contraction theories to explain force-length and force-velocity relationships in skeletal muscle, and history-dependent behavior.
- Have an ability to locate, retrieve, read, comprehend, critique and discuss current and classic muscle literature.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**BME 4660-Level Grading Scheme**:  
30% Exam 1  
25% Special Topic Exam (Exam II)  
30% Exam III (final)  
15% Presentation(s)

Quizzes and Exams: Exam 1 and the Special Topics Exam will be written exams, either in-class or take-home, for all students in the course. Make-up Exams will only be given to students with written and valid excused absences, such as documented illness or varsity athletic event. There will be a **FINAL EXAM** in this course, date and time of Final Exam to be announced. The format for the Final Exam will differ between undergraduate and graduate students. Students taking the course at the undergraduate level (4000 level) will take a written Final Exam, whereas those taking the course for graduate credit (6000 level) will have an oral Final Exam.

Presentations: Presentations are an important, and significant, part of this course. All students are required to make presentations to the class. Presentations will be evaluated by both the instructor and their peers.

Undergraduate students (4000 level) will present an assigned article to the class, either alone or in a group of two, on one or two separate occasions during the semester (depending on student enrollment). For each presentation the student must also provide a one-page written summary of the article, and an electronic copy of the presentation. The instructor and the graduate students will provide evaluation of these presentations.
