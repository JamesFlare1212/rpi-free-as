## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

# Course Texts

Two or three readings (representing a range from 10 to 25 pages) are assigned for each class. It is a moderate amount of reading but it has to be done twice a week and, as we will see below, students have to write summaries of the readings

We will not use a textbook in the course but journal articles and excerpts from books.

All assigned readings are selected primary and secondary sources which will be available on the class LMS site as we go along.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Student Learning Outcomes / Course Objectives By the end of this course, students will be able to: - Better understand the interactions between war, culture, and society. - Appreciate the wide range of perspectives on the nature and function of war. - Identify ways in which war engages with society both in so-called traditional and modern cultures. - Relate class material to contemporary ethical or cultural issues. - Gain a basic understanding of some key concepts and theories in social sciences and the ability to apply them to present-day debates. Major topics that we will address in the course: - War and Violence in human society and history. - Different cultures and different ‘ways of war’. - The correlations between conquest, culture, and military technology. - The notion of “just war”. - The relations between war and the state. - The relations between war and the nation. - Differences and similarities between civil war, colonial war, foreign war. - The militarization of society. - War and sports - Rituals and the military. - War and technology - Empires and clash of civilizations. - Reactions to military and cultural conquest. - War and religion - War and terrorism. Communication and Critical Thinking A practical objective is to improve students’ oral and written communication based on reading, writing, in essay questions and a term paper, class presentations, and on class discussion. Discussion should be based on listening and on trying to understand (not necessarily agree with) another point of view and acknowledge the value of different perspectives for critical thinking.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

# Course Assessment Measures / Assignments

The requirements for this course include three different assignments:

## 1. Summaries

A brief summary of each assigned reading must be sent (email, Google drive) to the instructor before the beginning of class.

\- No summaries accepted during or after class.

\- No points are given for summaries, but points are taken off the final grade for each missing summary.

## 2. Class presentations and Research Paper

Each student will conduct some original research or “fieldwork” on a theme of her/his choice.

A guideline will be handed out and “fieldwork” explained in class.

## Exams:

\- There will be three in-class exams scheduled during the class period.

\- Each exam will cover materials (readings, films, lectures) studied since the beginning of the course for the first exam, and since the last exam for the second and third exams.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grading is based on a total of 100 points (100%).

Each exam is worth 25 points (total: 75 points).

Presentation / Fieldwork Report: 25 points.

For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Cheating on an exam. Anyone cheating on an exam will be given a zero for that exam. Two cheating episodes will result in an F in the course and may be reported for academic discipline. No notes, crib sheets or other written materials are allowed during exams. Looking at other student’s work is not allowed.

Do not write anything on an exam paper returned to you after grading by the Instructor. If an exam paper has new markings, this will disqualify you from any consideration of re-grading. Anyone submitting an altered exam for re-grading will be given an F in the corresponding exam.
