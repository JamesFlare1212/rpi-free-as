## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course invites you into the world of philosophical ideas and reasoning -- to join a great conversation that has unfolded over the 2,500 years since philosophy began in Ancient Greece and in certain other places around the world. We will explore such issues as the nature of Philosophy, personal identity, free will vs determinism, art and beauty, the relationship between persons and society, and meaning and purpose in life. This course will aim to help you develop your skills in reading and evaluating philosophical texts. It is designated as Communication Intensive.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Students will be able to explain and communicate the essential characteristics of Philosophy and the uses and value of Philosophy.
- 2\. Students will be able to identify and explain several conflicting positions on what constitutes personal identity.
- 3\. Students will be able to identify and explain several contrasting positions in regard to free will and determinism.
- 4\. Students will be able to identify several philosophical issues related to art and aesthetics.
- 5\. Students will be able to identify and discuss several philosophical issues regarding the relationship of the individual to society, and regarding democracy and citizenship.
- 6\. Students will be able to identify and discuss several Eastern and Western perspectives on meaning and purpose in life.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

In this class, all work that is turned in for a grade (i.e., exam answers, discussion papers, and discussion board posts) must represent the student's own work.  
If found to be in violation of the Academic Integrity Policy, students may be subject to two types of penalty: The instructor may administer a grade penalty and the student may be reported to the Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education, as appropriate. The first violation will result in a grade of 0 for that exam or assignment. A second violation would result in failure for the course.
