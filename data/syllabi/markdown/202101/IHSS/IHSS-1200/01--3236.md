## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- to gain a factual knowledge of theories, data, and methods of analysis used by economists
- to evaluate multiple diverse perspectives on contemporary and complex global issues
- to analyze policies with regards to social, cultural, environmental, and economic outcomes
- to consider ethical responsibilities of protecting the public’s health, safety, and welfare
- to practice data analysis within the economic context

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

MyLab Assignments: Assignments using the Pearson MyLab online system will be due quarterly. For the first three sets of assignments, there is a 7 day period of time following the due date during which assignments may be completed late for a 10% penalty on all questions unanswered by the due date. After the 7 day late period, assignments are not accepted. The fourth and last set of assignments allows a shorter late completion period.

Tests: There will be four tests administered online via MyLab. The basis for tests will be all assigned textbook readings, all MyLab assignments, and all lecture material. Each test will be made available for a period of seven days. Test format is multiple choice and numerical answer. Each student will receive a unique test. Tests must be completed in one session and will be timed at 110 minutes. *Makeup tests will only be allowed for absences having an official Rensselaer excuse issued by the Health Center or the Dean of Students, and submitted to the professor by two days after the test. The makeup test is scheduled during the week after the test. If you miss a test and do not have an official Rensselaer excuse, you must take the optional final exam to improve upon your test average.*

Final Exam: An optional final exam will be offered during the last week of class as a way for students to attempt to improve their grade. The final exam will cover all course material. It will not simply be a repeat of questions from the mid-semester tests. If you take the final exam, the score you obtain on the final exam will be compared with the average of your mid-semester tests. You will be awarded the higher of these scores as your grade for the test portion of the course. *If you miss any of the four mid-semester* test(s) and do not have a proper excuse (see policy on makeup tests above)*, then plan on taking the final exam to improve your grade.*

HASS Inquiry: This course is identified as a HASS (Humanities, Arts, and Social Sciences) Inquiry course. The purpose of a HAAS Inquiry course is to provide opportunity for the discourse of diverse and divergent ways of thinking about complex global issues. In particular, we strive to be thorough in understanding all perspectives held with regards to important issues and the outcomes they create for society, culture, the environment, the economy, and many other realms. Additionally, we must evaluate the ethical responsibilities of protecting the public’s health, safety, and welfare. These are the specific objectives charged to a HASS Inquiry course. Being that this HASS Inquiry course is also an economics course, we will gain additional benefit from learning the ways in which economics helps us to understand and evaluate events, phenomena, and outcomes. For this semester’s HASS Inquiry challenge, the IHSS-1200 class will focus on the topic of the COVID-19 pandemic. The assignment associated with this challenge involves the writing of an essay and participation in an online discussion. A general description of the Spring 2021 IHSS-1200 HASS Inquiry Assignment is as follows:

1. Choose one aspect of the COVID-19 pandemic that you would like to give thought to.
2. Write an essay of 500-1000 words to address the aspect of COVID-19 you have chosen. This essay will need to be completed by mid-April; see course calendar for details.
3. Between April 8th and 21st, the class will engage in an online discussion surrounding the essays written by students.

Specific instructions are more detailed, are posted to LMS, and must be followed in order to complete the assignment for full credit.

For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

By enrolling in this course, all students are committed to absolute agreement to and understanding of the course policy on academic integrity.

- \- With regards to MyLab assignments, teamwork is allowed. However, students are expected to put forth their own effort and not simply take answers from other students. By doing one’s own work on assignments, one gains deeper understanding and is better prepared to do well on tests.
- \- It is expected that students will do their own work to complete tests. Tests are open book/open note. Teamwork on tests is counterproductive because each student receives a unique and timed test.
- \- Plagiarism involves the intentional copy of sentences or paragraphs from published or online sources without citation or from work completed by a current or former student. Written work submitted will be monitored for plagiarism from any source, including present and former students. Plagiarism, either in entirety or in part, is a violation of the policy on academic integrity.

In general and to a certain extent, there is a degree of “you get out of the class what you put into it.” Professors cannot possibly monitor all dishonest activity, nor can they force students to learn. It is your responsibility to get your money’s value out of this and any college class. Any student found to engage in dishonest activity in any aspect of this course will be penalized with failure of the course.
