## Course Goals

### Goals:

By the end of the course, and upon satisfactory completion of all HW assignments, class activities and exams, the students will have an elementary understanding of Quantum Mechanics and, more importantly, its interpretation.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The course will extend over 14 weeks (see schedule) and we will cover 13 chapters as listed below. We will cover all the chapters in details (with the exception of chapters 8 and 13, for which I will cover selected subtopics).

1. Stern–Gerlach Experiments
2. Rotation of Basis States and Matrix Mechanics
3. Angular Momentum
4. Time Evolution
5. A System of Two Spin-1/2 Particles
6. Wave Mechanics in One Dimension
7. The One-Dimensional Harmonic Oscillator
8. Path Integrals
9. Translational and Rotational Symmetry in the Two-Body Problem
10. Bound States of Central Potentials
11. Time-Independent Perturbations
12. Identical Particles
13. Scattering

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand superposition principle
- Calculate probability and probability amplitudes
- Understand meaning of Pauli matrices, and being able to calculate matrix representation of spin 1 systems
- Establish Hamiltonian as the generator of time evolution operator
- To be able to combine two spin-1/2 particles
- To be able to calculate the probability density in simple 1D wells
- Understand the impact of commutation relationships on the solution of the bound states of a spherically symmetric system
- To understand the solutions of the 1D Harmonic Oscillator in quantum mechanics
- To understand time-independent perturbation theory up to second order for degenerate and non-degenerate cases
- Understand the implication of indistinguishably on the possible symmetry of wave-function under the effect of the exchange operator
- To be able to solve the problem of the hydrogen atom

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Learning will be assessed using a combination of quizzes, homework assignments, and exams, as described elsewhere in this syllabus.
