## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a one semester overview of physics fundamentals with an empha- sis on concepts and applications. Topics will include mechanics, including equilibrium and statics, fluids, oscillations, and waves; basics of thermodynamics and heat flow; electricity and magnetism; electromagnetic radiation and optics; and elements of atomic and nuclear physics.

The class style will be a combination of lecture and interactive learning through in-class problem solving activity. The time spent for each part will be roughly half of the class time.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Upon successful completion of the course, the student will have gained an insight into the fun- damental concepts of physics that govern our every day lives. The student will be able to apply this insight to better understand every day phenomena in their personal and professional life. The student will also gain an understanding of the processes of the scientific method. Furthermore, the student will be able to approach a physical situation in a quantitative way through calculations and be able to interpret the result.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Reading Assignments:

There will be a reading assignment in advance of every class period. (Please see the class calendar.) In class I will be covering only the highlights of each chapter, assuming that you have already read the chapter and are familiar with the material. This is a lot of material, so don’t get behind! Get in a routine of reading the before the lecture. That way you will have a basic understanding of the

1

concepts and good questions to ask. Reading the book is the most important part of this course! While you are reading, engage with the “Check your understanding” and “Check your figures” boxes within the text. Almost all of the boxes are also accompanied by videos. This is a good check for yourself if you comprehend the material.

Please also consider following the tip to read quickly, but multiple times. “You’ll better learn physics by going over the same material several times. With each time, it makes more sense. Don’t worry if you don’t understand things right away – just keep on reading.” - Paul Hewitt.

Homework:

There will be a homework assignment after every lecture. The homework assignment is due the afternoon before the next lecture. Late homework will not be accepted.  
The homework assignments are crucial in mastering the course material. The homework will be worth 20% of your final grade. The lowest three homework assignment scores will be dropped.

Quizzes:

There will be a short quiz in the first 5 minutes of every class, so be on time. (The quizzes are designed to take you only half of the 5 minutes you are given.) Subject of the quizzes will be the reading assignment for that class, including sample questions worked in the text. The quizzes will account for 10% of the final grade. The lowest three quiz scores will be dropped. There will be no make up or late quizzes.

In-Class Practice Activity:

To promote the understanding of the material presented in lecture, we will do problem solving activities during each class meeting. The in-class activity will make up for roughly half the class time. You are encouraged to seek help while doing the activity. We will discuss the questions and solutions towards the end of the class meeting. This will greatly promote your understanding and mastering of the material. The in-class activity will not be graded for correctness but for completion. It is due by the end of class. The in-class activities will account for 20% of the final grade. The lowest two activity scores will be dropped.

Office hours:

You are encouraged to seek help and assistance with your general understanding of the material and the assignments as often as you want and is needed. But you must come to office hours at least once throughout the semester. This will account for 10% of your final grade. You will get full credit when you show that you are able to engage with the material in a conversation with the instructor. This is not about knowing all the answers. This is about being able to ask informed questions and being able to have a conversation about the material.

Exams:

There will be three exams to test your progress and mastering of the material. The exams will be given during class time on the following dates: March 1, April 1, and during finals week TBA. The lowest exam score will be dropped. The exams account for 40% of your final grade.  
No makeup exams will be given. If a student misses an exam, that will be the dropped exam score.

Adaptive Practice:

WileyPLUS possesses a feature called Adaptive Practice. This is a great tool to give you (and the instructor) feedback on your proficiency with the material. You will have the opportunity to complete the adaptive practice at the end of each chapter. Your overall score will account for amaximum of 2% extra credit on your final grade.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic Integrity:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Re- sponsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the students own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

You are encouraged to discuss solutions to homework problems with one another, but all work that you hand in must be your own. You may find it useful to form a working group - many people learn best from teaching and learning interactions with peers - but do not substitute a partner’s understanding for your own.

Any type of collaboration on examinations is considered cheating.

The first occurrence of academic dishonesty will result in an F for the course and a letter to the Dean of Students for appropriate action.
