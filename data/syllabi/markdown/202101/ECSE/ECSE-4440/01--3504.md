## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course trains the students in the application of linear feedback theory to the design of large-scale, integrated control systems, derivation of complex mathematical models of physical systems, synthesis of appropriate control laws to provide stability, simulation of complex control systems on digital computers.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

**Textbook:** G. E. Franklin, J. D. Powell, A. Emami-Naeni, *Feedback Control of Dynamic Systems* 8th ed, Prentice Hall, 2019, or a recent edition thereof (6th edition or later).

Additional texts:

- K. J. Astrom, R. M. Murray, *Feedback Systems*, Princeton University Press, 2010. Available online at: http://www.cds.caltech.edu/~murray/amwiki/Main_Page
- MATLAB Control Systems Toolbox documentation. https://www.mathworks.com/help/control/getting-started-with-control-system-toolbox.html

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**List of Topics:**

- Review of mathematical preliminaries; complex numbers and ODEs
- Systems models and simulation
- System stability
- Feedback systems
- Transient/Time-domain specifications
- Root locus analysis
- PID control and dynamic compensators
- Frequency-domain analysis and Nyquist stability theory
- Frequency-domain controller design
- State-space models
- State feedback and Linear Quadratic Regulators (LQR)
- Digital control implementation

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to demonstrate the ability to design and analyze dynamic models of systems including mechanical and electrical systems.
- Students will be able to demonstrate an understanding of the basic principles of feedback, including disturbance rejection, robustness to parameters, and tracking accuracy.
- Students will be able to demonstrate the ability to apply feedback control design and analysis techniques, including root-locus, frequency response, and state-space.
- Students will be able to demonstrate the ability to build and analyze models and design feedback controls in MATLAB that achieve design requirements.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Collaboration and Academic Dishonesty:**

Intellectual integrity and credibility are the foundation of all academic work. A violation of Academic Integrity policy is, by definition, considered a flagrant offense to the educational process. It is taken seriously by students, faculty, and Rensselaer and will be addressed in an effective manner. If found responsible for committing academic dishonesty, a student may be subject to one or both types of penalties: an academic (grade) penalty administered by the professor and/or disciplinary action through the Rensselaer judicial process described in **The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities** linked here.

Three relevant academic integrity violations to emphasize include:

**Collaboration:** Collaboration is defined as deliberately facilitating an act of academic dishonesty in any way or form; for example, allowing another student to observe an exam paper or allowing another student to "recycle" one's old term paper or using one another's work in a paper or lab report without citing it as another's work.

**Copying:** Copying is defined as obtaining information pertaining to a graded exercise by deliberately observing the paper of another student; for example, noting which alternative a neighboring student has circled on a multiple-choice exam.

**Plagiarism:** Plagiarism is defined as representing the work or words of another as one's own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference. Examples include using sentences verbatim from a published source in a term paper without appropriate referencing, or presenting as one's own the detailed argument of a published source, or presenting as one's own electronically or digitally enhanced graphic representations from any form of media.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines the full list of forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

**Specific examples:** We require that you complete the exams without communicating with other students or any external party. You are not allowed to interact with Chegg.com or any other online/internet assignment sharing system for any graded assignment. Interaction with it is considered a violation of the academic integrity rules.

Submission of any assignment/exam that is in violation of the policies described above will result in a penalty of 0 grade. Repeat violation will result in a penalty of an F grade in the class. **All instances of academic dishonesty will be reported to the student’s academic department and to the Dean of Students.**
