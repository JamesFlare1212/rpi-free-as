## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will explore how linguistics, the scientific study of the properties of human language and languages, can be applied to the development of intelligent agents that can fluently and meaningfully communicate with people in natural language. It will focus on linguistic phenomena that have so far been particularly resistant to effective machine processing, such as lexical ambiguity resolution, reference resolution, ellipsis, indirect speech acts, implicature, and non-literal language (e.g., metaphor and irony).

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

McShane, M., & Nirenburg, S. (2021). *Linguistics for the Age of AI*. MIT Press.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students will understand why natural language understanding is a difficult problem for machines and how the field is trying to solve this problem.

Students will understand how work on natural language processing has changed over time in response to both scientific and extra-scientific factors.

Students will have a better appreciation for the scientific process as it actually plays out in the world, not as an idealized abstraction.

Students will improve their oral and written communication skills, including their ability to collaborate with their peers.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will demonstrate that they have understood why natural language understanding is a difficult problem for machines and how the field is trying to solve this problem.
- Students will demonstrate that they understand the scientific and extra-scientific factors influencing changes in scientific approaches over time.
- Students will demonstrate that they understand the challenges in (a) carving out a linguistic problem to be studied, (b) making near-term progress on long-term problems, (c) investigating unobservables (such as the cognitive underpinnings of language use), (d) succeeding in the competitive world of scientific research, etc.
- Students will demonstrate their communication skills through class discussions, break-out sessions, writing assignments, and exams. They will show the ability to engage in group discussions with appropriate etiquette: not dominating or being silent, and being able to both offer and accept constructive criticism.
