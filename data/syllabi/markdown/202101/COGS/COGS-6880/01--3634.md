## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This class will be devoted to investigating the ways of making machines that think, communicate and act by means of understanding how people think, communicate and act.

Among the topics expected to be covered are: the basics of artificial intelligent agents and cognitive systems; issues related to content – knowledge, memory, human-like learning; a variety of topics in deep language understanding, including human-robotic interaction; decision-making, reasoning and mindreading; emotions; case studies of cognitive systems.

We will also address methodological issues related to developing cognitive systems as well as selected societal and philosophical issues related to this area of research and development.

The class will combine lectures and seminar-style discussion of assigned literature. Reading will be an essential component of this class. Homework and classwork assignments will include summarizing the readings, critiquing them, and generating sets of research and general scientific questions that the readings elicit.

Due to the mode of delivery, exams will be oral. The exams will test the students’ understanding of the basic concepts presented in lectures and in assigned literature.

There will be no traditional programming projects. However, the instructor reserves the option of assigning tasks related to knowledge programming – acquisition of knowledge elements, such as ontological concepts and semantic lexicon entries – both in homework and on exams.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course will familiarize students with a broad array of topics related to the development of artificial intelligent agents. It will help them to appreciate the challenges faced by intelligent agents tasked with fluently conversing and collaborating with people. It will prepare students for further study in the areas of general computational cognitive modeling, cognitive robotics and intelligent agent building as well as natural language understanding.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

I am required to define what is meant by cheating, a task that I believe is practically impossible to do comprehensively: any definition I write will be open to crafty interpretations by individuals who want to cheat. The following is an attempt to fulfill this requirement of defining what cheating is. The list is representative, not exhaustive:

- Unless otherwise specified by the professor, you must carry out every assignment and test exclusively using your own brain power. This means that:
- you may not collaborate with, give help to, receive help from, or steal help from anybody during a test or assignment
- you may not use cribs or any outside material during tests or assignments.
- You may not plagiarize. If you convey in writing any ideas that are not your own, you must use proper attribution.
- If you are permitted to do work collaboratively, you must name the collaborators and, if applicable, detail their specific contributions.
- You may not look up answers to problem sets or questions on the web, unless specifically permitted to do so by the professor.
- You may not reproduce, verbally convey, or otherwise reveal the content of any exam to any individual at any time.
