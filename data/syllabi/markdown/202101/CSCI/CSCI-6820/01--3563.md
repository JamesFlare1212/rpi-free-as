## Course Goals

### Goals:

The students will

- ⋄ be able to design/formulate some numerical methods for ODEs;
- ⋄ be able to understand and carry out mathematical analysis for consistency, stability, convergence and other properties of the methods;
- ⋄ to be able to implement the methods efficiently and robustly, and
- ⋄ to be able to evaluate/compare various methods, such as in their accuracy, efficiency, reliability, either numerically or mathematically.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

- – Formulation, analysis and implementation of Runge-Kutta methods and linear multi-step methods for non-stiff and stiff initial-value problems, and symplectic methods for Hamiltonian systems
- – Other possible topics: numerical methods for two-point boundary value problems and differential- algebraic equations

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

Collaboration: You are encouraged to discuss the problems with others or to refer to other references. However, the work you turn in must be written by yourself and represents your own understanding on the subjects. It is absolutely not acceptable to copy any part of the homework solutions from others. Any violation will result in no grade for part of or the entire assignment. If your work involves significant help from other people, reference books or papers, explicitly state this in your report to avoid penalty in grade.

Late policy: No late homework is accepted in general. Under special circumstances, approval might be granted for extension. Any request for extension needs to be made at least 24 hours before the deadline. You can be granted for at most one extension without penalty. Beyond that, any granted extension, as long as it is not for the entire class, will result in a 10% reduction of the grade per day.
