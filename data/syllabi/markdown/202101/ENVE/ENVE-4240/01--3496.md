## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The design and operation of different laboratory experiments to provide experience for the environmental engineer in the practical application of chemical and biological theory. Design parameters are developed via bench scale testing. Topics can include biological treatment, phytoremediation, water and wastewater treatment processes, composting of solid waste and soil columns, and sorption.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- You should be able to present results (data analysis) in numerical and graphical form.
- You should be able to apply knowledge regarding typical laboratory procedures related to environmental engineering
- Learn the language and mathematics of environmental engineering in regards to environmental engineering experimental work

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

***Tasks/Homework:*** Homework and written assignments are due in class at the **beginning** of lecture on due date. Class assignments turned in late will be penalized 10% per day or fraction thereof. Exceptions to this rule will be made only by previous consent to the instructor.

***Tutorials:*** Each team will meet with the instructor for 15-30 minutes per week to discuss any progress. The write-up will be constructed in sections and the instructor will define what is due each week. Each group may have different things due each week. Complete sentences will be required. The instructor is specifically looking at a critical analysis of the work that was performed. The instructor will be discussing the understanding of the topic and performance in hopes that the groups will understand how to improve their analytical skills.

***Final Project:*** During the semester, group presentations on predetermined and approved subjects will be delivered in the form of poster and oral presentations in addition to a written final product in the form of a journal article. You should discuss your choice of topic with the instructor **during the first two weeks of classes.** This is mandatory and will be part of your grade. Presentation will take place the last week of classes to highlight the exciting work we have done in this class.

***Requirements:*** This course requires an individual and group performance as well as collaboration with the instructor. YOU WILL NEED TO READ YOUR ASSIGNMENTS! Your prompt attendance is required at all lectures and possible field trips. If you cannot attend a scheduled session for some reason, it is requested that you notify the instructor to arrange to make the class period up. **Due to the nature of this course all laboratory periods will have to be made up!**

***Learning:*** The learning process is iterative, can be slow, and will be sometimes frustrating. Steady sustained effort is required to make progress. In addition to normal class hours, you are expected to spend about 7 hours each week on the course.

***Computers:*** Computer literacy is a necessity in the engineering profession. This class will stress the use of computer spreadsheets as simple but powerful modeling and analysis tools. Learning by doing is rather easy if you have not had formal training. I have no preferences --- use Lotus, Exel, Quattro, Sigma Plot etc. There is good access to spreadsheets and word processing packages on RCS workstations, on PCs and Macs at various locations around the campus. Please, contact me if you have any problems!!!!

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

***In general:*** Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts, which violate this trust, undermine the educational process. Therefore, in this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. All individual assignments must be done and submitted by each individual student. In cases where teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration and each member receives the same grade. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with the guidelines. Therefore, submission of any assignment that includes plagiarism and/or other forms of cheating is serious violations of the trust between student and teacher, and among other students. Penalties for such violations are severe and will result in a failing (F) grade for the assignment. Repeating violations will result in a failing (F) grade in the course.

***More in detail:*** These statements are intended to cover most, but not all, forms of academic dishonesty. (You should refer to *The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* for detailed information on Institute policy in this regard). You can find the RPI Student Handbook of Rights and Responsibilities online at http://www.rpi.edu/dept/doso/2008-2010RPIStudentHandbook.pdf. Please, pay close attention to the different forms listed.

***Tasks/Homework:*** Collaboration among the students in the class is permitted and, in fact, encouraged, as described above. Seeking help from other sources is also encouraged. However, the work you submit should be that of yours. Evidence of your merely transcribing another person’s homework will result in a zero grade for the homework assignment in question, **for all parties involved**. A second offense will result in a zero grade for homework for the entire semester **for all parties involved**, with a report made to the Dean of Students Office.

***Final Project:*** Collaboration during a testing situation (exchanging verbal or written information or copying) is, of course, prohibited. Also prohibited is the use of unauthorized materials during an examination. In addition, the use of email (including IM) or the Internet during exams for any purpose is strictly prohibited. All parties involved will receive an academic penalty, which will range from a numerical grade of zero for the exam to a failing grade in the course, deemed by the instructor to be appropriate to the infraction. Submission of work for re-grading after having changed an answer will result in a similar academic penalty. **All incidents will be reported to the Dean of Students**.

***Mobile Devices:*** All mobile devices (cell/smart phones, computers, pagers, etc.) must be stored securely away during lecture and are not be used unless specifically directed otherwise by the instructor. Use of (or **ANY** interaction with) a mobile device during an exam without explicit permission of the instructor will be interpreted as the illicit transfer of exam data, will be considered an act of cheating and will be treated as such.

***Other:*** Outrageous offenses such as arranging for a substitute to take an examination, attempting to obtain examination questions or solutions in advance, or attempting to change a grade record will result in a failing grade for the course and a recommendation to the Dean of Students that the student(s) in question be suspended. Any written assignments that are graded will be checked for cheating and plagiarism. If cheating and/or plagiarism are found, an academic penalty will be given. This penalty will range from a numerical grade of zero for the exam to a failing grade in the course, deemed by the instructor to be appropriate to the infraction.

**NOTE:**

**[1]** **Any** violation will be reported to the Dean of Students using the SoE form. **No exceptions!**

**[2] An academic dishonesty** agreement will be provided first class period and the form is required to be signed by each student.
