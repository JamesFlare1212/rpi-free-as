### Other LMS Information:

This course will provide an overview of environmental assessment to help future environmental professionals to evaluate and replicate sustainable projects and programs. The course will present tools to assess natural resources protection, improvements in population health, positive social impacts, economic appraisal that include sustainability measure, and an overview of environmental management. Students will practice assessment methods at project and strategic levels. Integrated Assessment using all methods will focus on the energy, water, air, noise applying both project and program analysis. At course end, students will apply practical methods that inform prudent investment decisions and support economic growth, social development, and environmental sustainability. Weekly case studies will supplement reading assignments and promote class discussion.

### Prerequisites or Other Requirements:

The pre-requisite for this course is ENVE 2110 – Introduction to Environmental Engineering. Some previous knowledge in ENVE 4962 – Environmental Investigations and Site Assessments is desired, but not a requirement.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate an understanding of the legislative and regulatory mandates surrounding Environmental Impact Assessments
- Understand the different steps within environmental impact assessment
- Communicate both orally and in written form the key aspects of environmental impact assessment
- Develop their own perspectives on impact assessment and environmental management and be able to relate this to other subject areas and to their wider understanding
- Understand and elaborate EIA reports and be able to access different case studies/examples of EIA in practice

***Learning Report (Undergraduate Students Only)***

Students will have a weekly report containing highlights about what they learned on that week. The reports should be no more than one page and should bring a personal statement from the student explaining the most interesting things they learned on that week.

All reports should be **handwritten**. The report should be uploaded on the LMS before the next lecture starts (by 4:45 PM on the following week)

Strategy for a good report: during the lecture, highlight a topic you would like to bring to the report. After the lecture on Tuesday, write one or two paragraphs and then add the remaining paragraphs on Friday after class. It doesn’t take much time and will help you to reinforce your notes and assimilate information.

***Paper (Graduate Students Only)***

Graduate Student will have a side task to write a paper about a specific topic that will be presented during the development of the course. More information will be provided later in the course.

***Mentorship (Graduate Students Only)***

Graduate Students will be grouped with undergraduate students during the seminars. There will be different teams (1 graduate student and 1 undergraduate student) for each seminar. Undergraduate students will provide a report about the activities and evaluate their partnership during the development of the projects.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities (www.rpi.edu/dept/doso/handbook.html) defines various forms of academic dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Students should familiarize themselves with this portion of the Handbook and should note that the penalties for academic dishonesty can be quite severe.

**Homework:** In this class, the homework questions and assignments must be your own. You are encouraged to work in groups to discuss the proposed questions, but each student should hand their own homework separately.

**Exams:** All exams are a measure of individual progress and must reflect individual effort. For each exam you must sign an honor code indicating that you have neither given nor received unauthorized aid. The use of mobile devices (cell phones, computers, pagers, etc) is prohibited during exams or quizzes. Use of such devices, unless when otherwise instructed to use them, will be interpreted as illicit exchange of information and will be considered an act of cheating.

**Mobile devices**: Please store mobile devices during lecture unless specifically directed otherwise by the instructor.

**A grade of zero will be given on the first assignment where a violation is detected, and a referral to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education for undergraduate or graduate students, respectively). If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of “F” for the course and referral to the appropriate Dean.**
