## Course Goals

### Goals:

## Course Goals

This is a primarily self-directed, open-ended design course that builds upon and synthesizes knowledge and skills gained in other engineering, humanities, social science, and professional development courses. Most importantly, you will be expected to gain new knowledge through independent investigation of the literature including books, journal articles, design manuals, government publications, and web resources.

Through the design process, you will gain an appreciation for the historical context and societal importance of *engineering* to improve quality of life through design, and more specifically, the role of *environmental engineering* design to protect public health and the health and functioning of ecosystems.

You will be expected to consider engineering solutions in a societal context, including their technical, environmental, societal, political, legal, aesthetic, economic, and financial implications. You will develop and apply an understanding of professional and ethical responsibility to protect public safety, health, and welfare, and to carefully weigh alternatives when values conflict.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

***Course Outcomes***

By completing this course you will gain the knowledge, skills, and attributes to:

Design a system or process to fulfill desired needs while meeting realistic constraints.

- To understand design methodology, including problem definition, scope, and analysis.
- To identify, evaluate and synthesize alternatives.
- To apply the iterative nature of design in a real world context
- Identify and satisfy multiple design constraints; possible constraints include aesthetics, codes, constructability, cost, legal considerations, maintainability, manufacturability, marketability, regulations, safety, standards, sustainability, or usability.
- Identify stakeholders and consider their perspectives and expectations.
- Where needed, to work across multiple disciplines to achieve design goals.
- To integrate information, organizations, people, processes, and technology.
- To estimate engineering and project costs.
- To develop project schedules.

- Prepare, present and defend technical material pertaining to design and the design process, which is used to educate, inform, and persuade colleagues, clients and the general public about technical solutions and the design process.

- When appropriate, function on a design team as both a leader and a contributor. To do so you will apply your i) understanding of team formation, evolution, and dynamics; ii) appreciation of the role of interpersonal relationships and personality; iii) skills in collaboration, problem solving, and time management; and iv) ability to foster and integrate diversity of perspectives, knowledge, and experiences.
- Investigate a problem independently, as a prototype for how you will engage in life-long learning to develop your career and contribute to the environmental engineering field.

“A” level work in each element of the course is characterized by the following attributes:

***Communication Competencies***

Rensselaer graduates must be able to communicate effectively in a variety of media (written, spoken, visual, and electronic) and in a variety of genres (reports, proposals, etc.) Whatever the medium and genre, as a Rensselaer student you are expected to:

1. Understand the context in which you are communicating
   1. Understand the audience
   2. Clearly state the goals of the communication
   3. Choose appropriate media, language, and content (including visual aids such as tables, graphs, figures, schemes, cartoons, etc.) to meet the stated goals
2. Organize your work
   1. Establishing a clear structure or principle of organization
   2. Clearly organize your content by providing: introductory statements, grouping of ideas and supporting details into paragraphs, transition statements, effective use of headings and sub-headings, and concluding passages.
3. Develop content appropriately
   1. Displaying a clear ethical sensibility (e.g., reporting data accurately, citing sources of information)
   2. Provide supporting details for design assumptions and decisions based on literature citations or design calculations
   3. Addressing the questions and/or topics that are essential for success with a given assignment
   4. Understanding, and, as appropriate, applying principles of visual communication (graphs, charts, animations, pictures) in their written or spoken work
4. Edit your written work carefully
   1. Observing the conventions of Standard English (e.g., correct usage, sentence structure, spelling, and punctuation)
   2. Use established professional (as opposed to colloquial) terminology.
   3. Observing the conventions (e.g., terminology and page format) of a particular discipline or workplace
   4. Cite ALL sources of information (books, journal articles, reports, web pages, personal communication, course notes)?

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

***Project Selection***

1. Project ideas may come from your personal experience and interests; sources may include environmental problems you have observed, issues in the media, problems you have worked on during internships, previous courses, books you have read, etc.

2. A source of project ideas may come from the International Environmental Design Contest:

https://iee.nmsu.edu/

This contest is organized by WERC, a consortium of New Mexican educational and research organizations, including Sandia National Laboratories and Los Alamos National Laboratory, which focuses on environmental education and technology development. Originally known as the Waste-management Education and Research Consortium, the organization is now called WERC: A Consortium for Environmental Education and Technology Development, which reflects the consortium’s expanded mission and accomplishments. Since 1991, WERC has also hosted the International Environmental Design Contest, a unique event that brings industry, government, and academia together in the search for improved solutions to environmental challenges.

The Design Contest includes several specified Tasks, including one Task designated as “Open” to “create an opportunity for teams to present solutions to issues that are focused on the general area of the environment, including, but certainly not limited to, energy and water.”

**Project Development**

Problem Identification, Definition, and Background

Define the problem and provide a context for the reader. Briefly outline the history of the problem, and any conditions leading up to the problem. Describe why the problem is important, and who is affected. Outline previous attempts, if any, to solve the problem. Define the requirements of the system to meet design objectives and define constraints that must be met, including pertinent standards, codes, regulations, and permitting requirements.

Alternatives Assessment and Process Selection

Generate multiple potential solutions to your design problem, and provide a critical evaluation of various alternative approaches to meet your specified design objectives. Evaluate alternative approaches against constraints and requirements, and discuss the risks and tradeoffs inherent in each alternative. Assess sustainability metrics in terms of public health and safety, and global, cultural, social, environmental, economic factors. Select an alternative that meets constraints and requirements, and which has acceptable risks and tradeoffs.

Process Sizing

Using appropriate equations, models, or other design relationships, determine the appropriate size or capacity needed to meet your design objectives (for example, pump, pipe, tank, or channel size and material; chemical dosing; necessary appurtenances and controls).

Process Risks & Tradeoffs

## Provide a quantitative analysis of design performance and stability (reliability). Identify the risks of process failure, and recommend strategies to minimize these risks. Perform a sensitivity analysis indicating the response of the chosen process design to variation in inputs and parameters (for example, flow rates, loading rates, temperature) and discuss tradeoffs that must be considered and possibly optimized.

Process Cost and Optimization

Using literature, government reports and guidance documents, and cost estimating publications, develop an engineering cost estimate for the construction or implementation of your design. Using judicious engineering judgment, discuss approaches to minimize capital and operating costs, and maximize sustainability goals, while at the same time assuring system performance and stability.

Design Details and Drawings

Select materials and appurtenances. Provide flow sheets or process schematics. Provide connection and construction details. Provide construction or assembly drawings to communicate your design to a contractor.

Format for deliverables Design Reports

- Executive Summary - problem statement, approach, and summary (1 pg)
- Introduction - theory and background (3 to 5 pgs)
- Methods - comprehensive outline of your approach (models, calculations, etc) (2 to 4 pgs)
- Discussion and recommendations (performance, stability, cost, optimization) (3 to 5 pgs)
- References & Bibliography (as needed)
- Appendix- list of variables, design data, example calculations, extra figures, tables, etc.

Design Reports must be typed, double spaced with one-inch margins. Present the results of your calculations in tabular, graphical, and pictorial form. Your final report must include a letter of transmittal, table of contents, and have numbered figures and tables. The appendix should contain example calculations, supporting charts and figures, manufacturer’s data, etc.

All group members must contribute to writing design reports. The sections written by different group members should be roughly equal in length and their authors should be designated clearly. You will be graded on individual writing, and how well the different elements of the report are integrated to form a coherent whole.

Progress Reports

Progress updates should be brief presentations having the same basic structure as the Design Reports. Progress reports should focus on recent accomplishments and critical project milestones achieved. Consider this report a justification to receive payment for your work.

Presentations

Presentations should be in Power Point or some other presentation software. Interim presentations should be on the order of 15 minutes (8 to 10 slides) and should summarize your work. Final presentation should be on the order of 30 min (15 to 20 slides). All group members must participate in group presentations, and the same number of slides (approximately) should be presented by each member. You will be graded individually on your part of the presentation, and you will also receive a team grade based on how well different parts of the presentation are integrated.
