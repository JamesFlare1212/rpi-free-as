## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

What are the processes that allow humans to detect information about their surroundings, recognize people and objects, and perceive depth and motion? This course will focus on the physiological and neural mechanisms underlying sensation (sight, hearing, and touch), the qualitative aspects of human perceptual experience, and how perception and action are interconnected. Color perception, object recognition, space and motion perception, and perception and action are all examined.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Wolfe et al. (2020). Sensation and Perception (6th Edition). Oxford University Press.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Identify fundamental puzzles in perception and compare and contrast various solutions.
- Describe the basic physiological and neural mechanisms that underlie perceptual systems.
- Describe the psychological processes that support basic perceptual abilities, such as object recognition, color perception, space perception, and motion perception.
- Describe how perceptual systems support routine, everyday activities.
- Think like an experimenter – that is, understand what it means to turn vague questions into rigorous, scientific hypotheses that can be tested in the laboratory.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- Quizzes

- Experiments

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are expected to attend and actively participate in instructor meetings and subgroup meetings. Attendance at these meetings will be taken. Students who skip meetings will struggle to learn the material in this course, perform well on quizzes, and contribute to the experiments. They will also have points deducted from the Discussion and Experiment portions of their grades. If you must miss a meeting for a legitimate reason, you should contact the instructor beforehand by e-mail. If you are taking classes remotely and are unable to attend meetings in real time due to time zone differences, you should contact the instructor at the beginning of the semester. It is the student’s responsibility to discuss these issues with the instructor.

### Other Course Policies:

***Recording of meetings.*** Lectures from the first four classes will be recorded and made available on LMS. In general, instructor meetings will not be recorded. However, if the group that is meeting with the instructor includes one or more students who are unable to attend in real time due to time zone differences, the meeting will be recorded and made available on LMS. Subgroup meetings will not be recorded as Webex does not currently allow for recording of breakout sessions.

***Email.*** For the purposes of this course, email should be used as a tool for scheduling a time to meet (virtually) with the instructor or TA if office hours conflict with your schedule. Please include times during which you are available to meet and a brief (1-2 sentence) description of the purpose of the meeting. In general, we will not respond to emails asking for further explanation of material covered in class or the textbook, or for clarification on assignments. This policy encourages you to ask questions about the assignment during class time and to seek help by meeting with the instructor or the TA in person, which is far more effective than trying to get help via email.

***Use of electronic devices.*** Laptops may not be used during in-person meetings with the instructor. During on-line instructor meetings, laptops may only be used for the purpose of virtually attending via Webex.
