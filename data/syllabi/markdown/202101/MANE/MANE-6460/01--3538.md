### Text Details:

F. Kanninen and C. H. Popelar, “Advanced fracture mechanics,” Oxford Univ. Press.,

## Course Content

### Content Details:

1. **Introduction and elements of solid mechanics**
2. **Fracture mechanics**

Elastic fracture mechanics

Energy, stress concentration and fracture toughness concepts,

Mixed mode loading, Elastic crack boundary value problems.

Path independent integrals,

Brittle fracture

Brittle fracture in tension and compression,

Role of stress concentration and crack nucleation,

Statistical approach to failure prediction, size effects

Fracture in ceramics: failure mechanisms,

Toughening of ceramics.

Elastic-plastic fracture mechanics

Cohesive zone models of crack tip plasticity,

Crack tip plastic zone and propagation criteria,

Elastic-plastic boundary value problems, the R-curve.

Elements of dynamic fracture mechanics.

3. **Fatigue**

Introduction

Type of loading, examples of failure,

Role of stress concentration,

Cyclic loading stress-strain, saturation.

Micro-mechanical aspects of fatigue

Phenomenological approaches

Stress-life approach,

Strain-life approach,

Multiaxial loading,

Temperature effects – comparison with static loading.

Fatigue crack nucleation

Mechanisms,

Nucleation at stress concentration sites.

Fatigue crack growth – subcritical and Paris regimes

Variable amplitude effects and effect of overstress; cumulative damage

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- \- Students will learn the physics and mathematical formulation of fracture mechanics and will be able to design against fracture
- \- Students will be able to apply fracture mechanics concepts to analyze the micromechanics of fracture in specific complex engineering materials
- \- Students will be able to predict the fatigue life of materials subjected to specified loads

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

All CLOs will be assessed via HW assignements. The Midterm will asses CLOs 1 and 2.
