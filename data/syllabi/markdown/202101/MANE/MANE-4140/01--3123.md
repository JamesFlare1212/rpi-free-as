**Course Description:**

An introductory undergraduate to graduate course in computational fluid dynamics. This course provides the knowledge necessary to model engineering applications consistent with fluid dynamic physical laws. Topics include: Overview of finite difference, finite volume, and finite element discretization approaches, problem classification, discretization, and numerics. Applications include compressible and incompressible flows, laminar and turbulence flows, with introduction of turbulence modeling, followed by boundary layer and parabolized flow approximations.

**Course Objectives:**

- *To impart an understanding of introductory concepts in computational fluid mechanics with emphasis on numerical solution of fluid flow governing equations.*

- *To familiarize students with discretization methods for different types of physical phenomena in fluid dynamics**, including conservative approaches and turbulence modeling.*

- *To provide an understanding of concepts involving numerical stability, accuracy, and convergence for a numerical solution and physical model approximation.*

**Student Outcomes:**

*By the end of the course, students will be able to:*

- *Understand and apply mathematical knowledge to fluid-flow phenomena in a discrete numerical sense.*

- *Be able to select and characterize numerical methods for applicability to “real-world” fluid flow problems.*

- *Be able to apply computational numerical tools to solve problems in several engineering disciplines*

- **Class Participation Discussions**

LMS discussion boards will be used to facilitate student-student communication as well as student-instructor communication. It is expected that every student participates in group discussions. Your participation in the discussion will be a major factor in determining your class participation grade. There are 2 discussion gates: (1) address gate and (2) reply gate. The address gate requires the student’s addressing the discussion directly; whereas, the participation gate requires the student’s feedback to other student’s viewpoint(s) (at least once). **The discussion address and reply gates are to be done throughout the module week, before the assignment deadlines for the week module.**

In general, the discussion address gate (1) simply address the discussion question. Discussion reply gate (2) requires participation in discussions and it may come in many forms. Examples of reply participation may include, but are not limited to:

- raising a question
- answering the question of a classmate
- explaining a step required to solve the problem
- explaining the process for solving the problem
- commenting on the post of a classmate
- guiding your classmates to the proper equations or section of the textbook
- providing a numerical answer to **a part** of the problem
- providing an expression necessary for solving the problem
- providing a schematic to add clarity to the problem
- Simple interactions like: “ I agree “ or similar will not be sufficient and will not be considered as relevant participation in discussion

Discussions will be used to facilitate student-student communication and exposure to different perspectives. The format of each discussion board question will vary. My expectations for your responses (content and length) will be explained for each discussion setup. In general, responses should adhere to all standard writing, grammar, and punctuation rules. Abbreviations or “lingo” commonly used in texting is not acceptable

Discussion board questions will not have a single “correct” answer and may intentionally be left vague to allow for variety in the responses. In some discussion board questions, I will try to better understand how you are thinking about the material. Generally speaking, the instructor will provide the path for the best solution coming from different points of view. Thus, you will not lose points for wrong answers. You will only lose points if you do not put forth a good faith effort or ignore the instructions. Otherwise, **I encourage you to think and express yourself freely.**

Your class participation grade will be based on the level and quality of involvement in the class module. The goal for the class is that, through participation, students not only have their own learning experience, but can see and be part of the learning experiences of their classmates. **Therefore, I would like to see everyone participate in online discussions, promote a positive learning environment, and work together with your classmates.** Much of this participation will occur in the discussion boards, pseudo office hours, and/or face-to-face sessions. In general, quality course response interactions will be required worth 30% (i.e., 15% for address gate and 15% for reply gate). However, I reserve the right to give less than 30% for students who are blatantly apathetic to discussions and/or the group nature of the class.
