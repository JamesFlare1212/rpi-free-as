## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Projects:**

The projects for this semester will focus on the development of a parcel in Town of Colonie. Existing topographic maps are in LMS as are traffic and soil boring data..

During the course of the semester, each structural-geotechnical (building) team will be assigned a building to design. The teams will be given an architectural rendering, a typical floor plan and an elevation. These designs might include a commercial building, hotel, commercial office, or a medium density residential building. Students, who through internships or personal relationships have access to architectural renderings, floor plans and elevation drawings may**, upon authorization of the instructor**, use these plans for their project.

The task of the **structural-geotechnical** (building) teams will be to develop a plan for the building project, structural and geotechnical design for one of the facilities, develop a construction plan and prepare a construction cost estimate.

The tasks of the **transportation-environmental** team is to perform a site design including parking, pedestrian and vehicle circulation and develop a traffic study for the site, including appropriate traffic control devices. Modern site design concepts such as “complete” streets should be included in your design. The team is also responsible for site drainage and water supply requirements.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The specific objectives and outcomes of the course are as follows:

- Students will further develop design skills, especially the application of engineering standards and strengthen problem solving skills.
- Students will develop written and oral communication skills, in particular the ability to communicate technical material to a variety of audiences in a variety of media.
- Students will work effectively in an environment where they work in multi-disciplinary teams to integrate prior course work and skills to solve problems taken from a "real world" context.
- Students will be able to incorporate and apply current technology used in the practice of their discipline.
- Students will understand their professional and ethical responsibility.

**Progress Reports and Meeting Minutes**

Each team will prepare a progress report which will be due each **Sunday at 5:00PM a**ccording to the schedule. Responsibility for submission of the progress report must be rotated among the team members. The weekly report will include the following:

1. description of work accomplished during the past week
2. description of work to be accomplished over the next few weeks with staff assignments.
3. description of problems encountered or expected and what the remedy is for resolving these issues.
4. minutes of meetings conducted by the group over the past week. It should be noted that in some cases, a subset of the group (for example, structural engineers) may meet. Any resolution of issues or assignments should be recorded in the meeting minutes and of course, minutes should be shared with all team members. While you may choose the level of detail, **I should be able to ask any team member about the progress of any aspect of the team’s work at any time.**

The weekly report (including meeting minutes) are to be submitted in **.pdf format** **after 5:00PM on Friday and before 5:00PM** on Sunday of each week to the instructor and the TA’s.through LMS. The title of the report will be: **Capstone Weekly Progress Report #x [Team Name].** The x refers to the progress report number in the schedule which follows. They must be submitted via electronic mail. **Examples of exemplary progress reports are on LMS**. You should have an internal control system to assure timely submittal[1]. Needless to say, all meeting minutes should be circulated among all team members.

Progress reports should also be sent to the assigned mentors.

Minutes should be very clear and focus on what was agreed to at the meeting with responsibilities and due dates. Minutes and progress reports should ***not*** include phrases such as “We worked on…..” Focus on who specifically is responsible and due dates.

Every other progress report will show conformance to the work schedule and an assessment will be made of whether you are ahead or behind schedule. If you are behind schedule a remediation plan must be presented.

The author of the weekly progress report must meet with the course instructor by Tuesday, end of business to discuss progress. It might be helpful for other team members to attend such meetings. In addition, each team member or subteam (Geotech and structural) should meet with the course instructor at least every other week.

The first progress report is due on Sunday, February 7, 2021

**Assignment #3 – Project Plan**

Each team will prepare the scope of the project. This is a clear articulation of project design criteria and description of the project. For an office building, for example, design criteria might include a 20,000 square feet footprint, 3 levels, entrances on two or three sides, parking level as required by building code. A description of design tasks (about 30) should be prepared. **You should consult the work scopes on LMS to complete this task.**

The purpose of this is to provide the baseline against which the design progress is measured. This avoids the situation where a critic, for example, asks why a larger structure was not designed. The answer is that it was not in the scope. Any deviation from the established zoning and building code must be identified. The Town building code is available on LMS.

You should discuss this plan with your mentors during the first meeting.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance and absences:**

You are expected to attend class promptly and regularly. In addition, your team may require additional meetings to accomplish the work. I suggest that **at least one** weekly meeting outside of the class time be used for project work. Students who are consistently demonstrating poor teamwork will have their grade appropriately adjusted.

### Other Course Policies:

**Important notes:**

There will be an interim and final presentation for the course. Professional engineers from the area will be invited to review your work. This is a good networking opportunity.

While grading is, for the most part, the responsibility of the instruction staff, each team member will honestly critique the performance of other team members. This will be part of the grade. Further instructions will be provided during the semester.

While the course is intended to integrate your technical skills, a very important part of the course is developing professional practice skills such as project management and communication. Inevitably, there will be problems in your work. The measure of success for this course is how you can anticipate and resolve them. At all times, you should know your role in the project, how your team is meeting schedule, proposed remedial actions and your specific activities for the next two weeks. The instructional staff should be able to ask any team member the status of all major work elements ***at any time*** of the project and get consistent answers.

Each design project will include some interesting design feature such as a cantilever or covered entrance which will require some structural analysis. A strictly “big box” design will not be sufficient.

Each team will be required to develop and maintain a project schedule. The baseline schedule in assignment #1 should be used throughout the semester as a guide for progressing the project. It is recommended that each team have a copy of some scheduling software such as Microsoft Project. A freeware project management software system is available at www.jxproject.com.

**Sustainability –** A major cross-cutting theme of this course will be sustainable design in both the building design and the site design. Teams will take into account future sustainability of their projects. For building teams, review of LEED criteria and life cycle cost analysis will be helpful. For transportation teams, permeable parking lots, energy efficient circulation systems (roundabouts) should be considered. Complete streets including design for safe transportation of pedestrians, bicyclists, transit patrons etc. should be undertaken. At least one person in each design, transportation and environmental team should be charged with lead responsibility for sustainability. See usgbc.org for additional information.

**Team communication** – You should develop a means of communicating and sharing documents electronically and document this in your initial proposal.

**Other:**

Design is a process of **several iterations** to find the best functional and economical solution.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic integrity:**

Students are reminded: "RPI expects the highest standards of honesty and integrity in the academic performance of its students. Any act of scholastic dishonesty is regarded as a serious offense, which may result in expulsion. RPI defines scholastic dishonesty as submission of false records of academic achievement; cheating on assignments or examinations; plagiarizing; altering, forging, or misusing an academic record; taking, acquiring, or using test materials without faculty permission; acting alone or in cooperation with another to obtain dishonestly grades, honors, awards, or professional endorsement. Aiding and abetting an act of scholastic dishonesty is also considered a serious offense."

The course project is assigned to student teams collectively. However, there will be individual assessments of performance particularly in the area of communication. The level of effort among all team members should be comparable. Students who are not active participants in their group as characterized by things such as consistently missing team meetings, not adhering to deadlines etc. may be considered in violation of academic integrity standards. Students will be required to assess the performance and contribution of individual team members in their projects.
