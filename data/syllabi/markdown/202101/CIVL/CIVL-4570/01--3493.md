## Course Goals

### Goals:

The course is designed to achieve the following skills and expertise:

1. Identifying, formulating, and solving CEE problems that meet specified performance, cost, time, safety and other quality needs and objectives.
2. Solving civil and environmental engineering (CEE) problems by applying fundamental knowledge of engineering judgment, math, and science.
3. Understanding CEE solutions in a global, societal and environmental context, consistent with the principles of sustainable development.
4. Organize and deliver effective verbal, written, and graphical communications.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

This course is an application-oriented course covering basic analytical tools for modeling and optimization of various civil and environmental engineering systems. Application domains in civil and environmental engineering that will be discussed include: construction management, infrastructure management, environmental engineering, transportation and logistics planning, water resource planning, among other civil and environmental engineering systems. An overview of different optimization techniques, with a particular focus on linear programming will be provided. Specific modeling tools (Solver in Excel) to solve these problems will be used throughout the class.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students who successfully complete this course will be able to: • Understand and evaluate existing data acquisition methods for collecting transportation data. • Design, develop, implement and evaluate an actual behavioral survey, including sampling, experimental design, and administration methods. • Use relevant software and methods to analyze travel survey data. • Properly interpret and analyze data and how to communicate the analysis.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Discussions among students are encouraged, but the homework assignments must be done and handed-in individually. Please bear in mind that each student is ultimately responsible for what he/she takes away from the course. In that regard, exams are a measure of individual progress and must reflect individual effort. Plagiarism and other forms of cheating are serious violations of the trust between student and teacher and among students. Penalties for such violations are severe. The first violation will lead to an F on the assignment/test. The second violation will result in failure of the class, and the student will be subject to disciplinary action through the Rensselaer judicial process as described in the Rensselaer Handbook.
