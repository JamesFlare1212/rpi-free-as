## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The applications of the principles of soil mechanics to the analysis and design of foundations, at the graduate level. Design of footings, retaining walls, pile foundations, flexible structures, bridge piers, abutments, embankments, and natural slopes. Slope stability analysis and landslide prevention. Earthquake effects.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Soil Mechanics, Lambe and Whitman, Wiley, January 1991.  
Foundation Analysis, Ronald Scott, Prentice Hall, 1981.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

•conduct a slope stability limit equilibrium analysis.  
•use the limit theorems to assess the bearing capacity of foundations and verify model predictions  
•Understand the equilibrium equations and failure mechanisms of foundations.  
•use Winkler spring soil models and soil-foundation spring models to analyze soil-structure interaction.  
•use Winkler spring soil models and soil-pile spring models to analyze pile foundations.  
•use commercial software and computer tools to analyze and design foundation and soil structure systems

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Course overview and Introduction

Overview of elastic and inelastic stress-strain response of soils

Slope stability:

· Limit equilibrium analyses

Load-displacement behavior of undrained clayey soils and bearing capacity of shallow foundations:

· Limit theorem analyses· Continuum solution

Static soil-structure interaction analysis of shallow foundation:

· Flexible beam and slabs on elastic and elastic-perfectly plastic foundations· Spring models of soil-foundation systems

Static soil-structure interaction analysis of pile foundation:

· Elastic soil models· Elastic-perfectly plastic soil models

Pile group
