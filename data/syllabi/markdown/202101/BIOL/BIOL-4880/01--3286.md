## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will explore global environmental cycles, patterns, and changes through an ecological lens. We will discuss major elemental cycles such as phosphorus, nitrogen, and carbon, and pollutants such as mercury, lead, and CFCs, and how changes in these cycles influence organisms and ecosystems. We will also discuss large-scale biotic processes and theoretical ideas about the dynamics of food webs and ecosystems. Major environmental issues such as acid rain, eutrophication, climate change, and land use conversation will also be discussed.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Course content**

After the first lecture, I will circulate a daily schedule for each class during the semester. The schedule will be adapted to the interests of students, who will lead discussion on peer-reviewed literature in each class period. Each student will lead at least one class discussion. I will attempt to pair students with topics for which they are most interested. This course will cover topics including:

1. Basics of global environmental cycles and patterns
2. Lithosphere
3. Atmosphere
4. UV radiation and ozone
5. Terrestrial ecosystems
6. Wetlands ecosystems
7. Inland waters
8. Oceans
9. Global carbon cycle and climate change
10. Global phosphorus and eutrophication
11. Global nitrogen cycle and agriculture
12. Land use modification and biodiversity
13. Global sulfur cycle, acid deposition, acid rain, and recovery from acidification
14. Global mercury cycle
15. Emerging global environmental issues and syntheses

Additionally, we will cover a number of ecological theory concepts, including:

1. Biodiversity and ecosystem stability
2. Island biogeography theory
3. Space-time relationships and macrosystems ecology
4. Alterative stable states and regime shifts
5. Ecological stoichiometry and metabolic theory

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Grading criteria**

***4880 enrolled students:***

Grades for students enrolled in this class will be based on leadership (15%) and participation (25%) in class presentations and discussions and three exams (20% per exam). Exams will cover material from lectures, assigned readings, and in-class discussion and will consist of multiple choice, definition, short answer, and essay questions designed to gauge the extent students have acquired a basic literacy in global environmental concepts. Exams will predominantly focus on the material covered following the preceding exam, but students should be aware that the topics covered build on each other and so links to materials in previous lectures will occur. Participation in class presentations and discussions will be graded based on each student’s contributions to an online discussion board and in-class discussions. Additionally, occasional in-class quizzes will be used to assess whether students are adequately reading the primary literature assigned for each class.

***6880 enrolled students:***

Grades for students enrolled in this class will be based on leadership (15%) and participation (25%) in class presentations and discussions, an article to be written suitable for a peer-reviewed journal (15%), and three exams (15% per exam).

Participation in class presentations and discussions will be graded based on each student’s contributions to an online discussion board and in-class discussions. Students enrolled in 6880 are expected to demonstrate superior competence in reading and discussion. Occasional in-class quizzes will be used to assess whether students are adequately reading the primary literature assigned for each class.

The article to be written assignment is described here: Each student will be required to write a manuscript that reviews a major aspect of a global environmental cycle, pattern, or phenomena related to content we cover in the course. The specific topic is to be identified by the student and approved by the instructor. The purpose of this exercise is to give students in 6680 an opportunity to dive deeper into a course topic. The manuscript should be written in a format suitable for submission to a peer-reviewed journal. The length if flexible, but should be at least five pages (single-spaced, standard 1 inch margins, Times New Roman, 12 size font). You should identify a specific journal that could be suitable for the manuscript and follow the formatting rules for that journal. Due dates for the manuscript will be circulated with the course schedule that will be created following the first class. The due dates will include the following items:

1. a) each student must identify and select a focus topic and submit a proposed title by the beginning of the third week of class.
2. b) each student must submit a first draft of their manuscript by the ninth week of class.
3. c) Each student must submit a final draft of their manuscript by the last day of class.

The article assignment will be evaluated with components including the first draft (40%), the final draft (60%).

*Monitoring your progress:*

I will return all assignments, so you should be able to calculate your overall grade. Grades are calculated as a percentage of the total possible points for all assignments and determined as follows:

A: 93 or above A-: 90-92

B+: 87-89 B: 83-86 B-: 80-82

C+: 77-79 C: 73-76 C-: 70-72

D+: 67-69 D: 60-66

F: <60

Students enrolled in 6880 cannot get D+/D or D-. There will be no make-up exams. If you miss one of the exams due to an excused emergency absence (interviews and family trips do not qualify as emergencies), your final grade will be based on the total points from the remaining exams. If you miss an exam due to an emergency (illness or serious injury or a death in your immediate family), you must submit, in writing, your request for an excused absence. Verbal communication with the faculty member is not sufficient. Please use the following guidelines when preparing your request:

1. Your written request for an excused absence must indicate your name, the nature of the emergency, and the date of the exam that you missed.
2. Your request must be detailed, typed, and signed by you. This request must be given directly to Prof. Rose no later than one week after the missed exam.
3. If you miss an exam due to a medical condition, you must include documentation. At the minimum, you must submit a signed letter from your physician.

Failure to comply with these guidelines will result in a zero recorded for the missed exam. If you miss more than one examination you should discuss possible options available with your instructor. Late arrivals to exams will be given the exam during the time that remains for the designated examination period.
