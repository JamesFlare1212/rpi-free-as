## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Lecture Description:**

This is an introductory course in cell structure and function. It is intended for Biology and Biomedical Engineering majors, and covers such topics as chemical composition of cells, molecular structure function of organelles, membrane transport, and the basic biochemistry underlying cellular behavior including cancer cells.  
**Lab Description:**

This is the laboratory portion of the course. Here, you will gain practical experience with the concepts and techniques covered in the lecture portion of the course. The lab and lecture are linked; the curriculum covered in lab and lecture is intended to give you an integrated experience. This is an introductory course in cell structure and function. It is intended for Biology and Biomedical Engineering majors, and covers such topics as chemical composition of cells, molecular structure function of organelles, membrane transport, and the basic biochemistry underlying cellular behavior including cancer cells.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course aims to provide an understanding of the fundamental mechanisms of cellular function and to develop critical thinking skills in the context of modern cell biology.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Lecture - Understand and properly use scientific vocabulary common for cell and molecular biology.
- Lecture - Describe the structure and function of the biomolecules found in all living organisms.
- Lecture - Describe the synthesis of DNA, RNA, and proteins.
- Lecture - Explain the processes involved with gene expression.
- Lecture - Explain the processes by which animals acquire nutrients and eliminate wastes.
- Lecture - Explain the structure and function of membranes and intracellular compartments.
- Lecture - Link modern advances in cell and molecular biology to a better understanding of diseases.
- Lab - Demonstrate understanding of prokaryotic and eukaryotic cell structure and function by answering multiple choice and short answer/drawing questions on in-class exams and homework problems.
- Lab - Perform common techniques in prokaryotic and eukaryotic cell biology research in laboratory exercises.
- Lab - Accurately record laboratory activities in a laboratory notebook.
- Lab - Orally present scientific data.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Lecture: 4 tests, 1 cumulative final exam, and online homework are used to assess students. Reading the textbook, coming to lecture and asking questions, and doing the homework gives students the best learning experience.  
Lab: 3 quizzes, 10 pre-lab assignments, 10 lab write-ups in lab notebooks with 3 notebooks checks, 10 lab discussions on LMS (online students only), and 1 presentation are used to assess students. Reading the lab manual ahead of time, answering pre-lab questions, and maintaining an accurate and up-to-date notebook gives students the best learning experience.

### Attendance Policy:

**Lecture Attendance:**

- Attending the live WebEx class sessions is highly recommended; while failure to attend class will not result in any direct penalties, having live interactions with Dr. Klein and your TA's will certainly enhance your grasp of the subjects covered in class. **Lab Attendance:**

- Attendance is **REQUIRED** for in-person and synchronous labs. **Unexcused absences are NOT allowed** - missing a lab will result in a deduction of **10 points** from your lab grade (see grade calculations below).
- If you miss a lab, you must obtain a Dean’s excuse. Students who need to report an illness should contact the Student Health Center via email or call 518-276-6287. Then, you must submit this documentation to BIOL-2120 Lab LMS under the “**Excused Absences**” section. For example, if you receive a Dean's excuse or doctor’s note via email, "print" this email as a PDF and submit the PDF to LMS; if you receive physical documentation, you must and then submit a scanned image or picture of the document to LMS. This is the only way in which your lab TA will know about the excused absence and to avoid a penalty for the absence.
- For any excused absence from lab, your lab work must still be made up. If you foresee yourself missing a lab, contact your lab TA and instructor well ahead of time. For any absence, it is your responsibility to coordinate a make-up time. Failure to make up lab work will result in the same penalty as missing the lab.

**Other Lab Policies:**

- **Lab Safety:**
  - Follow all rules that your lab TA or instructor issues.
  - Do not engage in any activities that will endanger yourself or those around you.
  - **Wear gloves** when interacting with any laboratory equipment and change gloves often. You will be provided gloves.
  - **You must wear safety glasses or goggles** at all times during lab except when using microscopes - disinfect eye pieces of microscopes with ethanol before and after use.
  - Lab coats are optional but encouraged; lab coats are not provided. Wear clothing that entirely covers the legs and midriff.
  - You must wear shoes that enclose the entire foot; footwear like sandals and crocs are not allowed.
  - Report any spills or accidents to your TA or instructor **immediately**.
  - When finished, you must clean your work area and return it to the condition you found it in; **disinfect your work areas with 70% ethanol**.
  - TAs, instructors, and other students will use 70% ethanol to disinfect working areas and equipment before, during, and after lab exercises.
  - To reduce risk of COVID-19 transmission, maintain a 6 ft (1.8 m) distance from other personnel in the lab; do not cross into other students' work spaces.
  - Follow traffic flow guides in lab to help maintain social distancing; do not crowd other students while they are working at a shared location or on shared equipment.
  - Always **wear a mask correctly** while in lab or any institute building.
  - **Use of cell phones is not allowed in lab**. Laptops are required, however, unlike laptops, cell phones are placed on or near the face, creating a serious COVID-19 transmission risk and increases the risk of contamination by other hazardous reagents used in the lab.
  - Book bags, backpacks, and other personal items must be stored under your lab bench, away from other student's belongings. Be sure to avoid making your personal belongings a tripping hazard.
  - Violation of any rules stated here, in your lab manual, or verbally by your lab TA or instructor will impact your **Lab Citizenship grade**, and major violations can have you removed from the lab.

- **Appealing a Grade:** If you feel that the grade you received on an assessment is not justified, contact your TA for clarification on your grade. If the issue is not resolved by your conversation with the TA, you can appeal your grade to your lab instructor (Dr. Bonocora for in-person labs or Dr. Klein for remote/online labs), but you must submit justification explaining the reasons why you believe your grade should be changed. You should submit your justification via email, and be sure to CC your TA on this email.

- **Late Assessments:** Late quizzes and presentations will **not** be accepted. Late pre-labs and notebook checks may be accepted for reduced credit (-10% after the first day, -20% after the second day, not accepted after that).
