## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Introduction to biological systems. Discussion of problems associated with biological organization, scaling, and hierarchy. Major topics covered include evolution, genetics and medicine, and ecology. The course considers the biological components of various environmental, social, and individual problems. Course is taught using both traditional and research-based pedagogical methods.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

To provide an understanding of fundamental principles, to develop analytical thinking skills in the context of modern biology, and to develop data dexterity for understanding and solving grand challenges in the life sciences

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate mastery of the foundational knowledge and core concepts of evolution, genetics & medicine, and ecology.
- Apply foundational knowledge and core concepts in these areas to solve novel problems, analyze realistic scenarios, and make predictions.
- Apply reasoning and methods to the analysis and solution of biological problems.
- Utilize information and data from a wide range of sources to construct knowledge and communicate that knowledge effectively.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1. Evaluation: Answer multiple choice questions through exams
2. Evaluation: In-class discussion, homework and online exams
3. Evaluation: homework, online exams
4. Evaluation: In weekly homework, examine a current topic in biology that presents ethical or moral concerns. Evaluate what scientific knowledge/information would be needed to address the problem.

The activities that will help measure your progress towards mastering these goals will include both formative and summative assessments. Formative assessments take place during the learning process. In some of our in-class sessions we will use discussions to illustrate the use of data analysis in biological systems to help us understand how well the class has grasped the basic concepts and factual knowledge associated with the given area of biology. Many other in-class activities will also serve as important formative indicators of course performance and lead to discussion of the material being covered. Finally, there will be a post-class session on LMS after each in-class session containing exam review information that will help you assess your learning and prepare for exams (summative assessment). This material will provide information on the state of your knowledge as you prepare for the hour exams.

The summative assessments of your learning in the lecture sessions take the form of hour exams at regular intervals during the semester. The homeworks aim to keep you up to date and provide an alternate mechanism beyond grades on exams.

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own.
