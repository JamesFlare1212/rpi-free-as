### Additional Course Description :

## Course Description

This is an introductory course in biological systems focusing on major topics in evolution, human genetics & medicine, and ecology. It is an undergraduate level course. It is intended for non-majors and majors and covers a variety of topics that impact our daily lives and the grand challenges of this decade and your generation. Activities, such as student presentations and written responses to lecture questions enable students to interact, and discuss these topics. The goal is to provide an understanding of fundamental principles, to develop analytical thinking skills in the context of modern biology, and to develop data dexterity for understanding and solving grand challenges in the life sciences. This is a D1 Data Intensive Course.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

## Course Goals / Objectives

1. Principle Concepts: Students will be able to grasp and master introductory biological principles.
2. Protocols: Students will be able to connect and link core concepts, and apply them to the bases of protocols used in the corequisite laboratory course.
3. Peer Interaction: Students will be able to discuss and analyze topics in the classroom with peers.
4. Pre-requisites: Students will be able to satisfy prerequisites, in order to take advanced courses in the biological sciences.
5. Plan of Study: Students enrolled are mostly non-biology majors, and after taking this course will be able to connect biological concepts, and compare and contrast these principles, in an interdisciplinary manner, with what they are learning in their major line and plan of study.
6. Present Problems: Students will be able to connect biological concepts, and apply concepts learned in the biology classroom to presently occurring complex current issues in the outside real world.
7. Predict and Propose: Students will be able evaluate solutions to complex issues, predict fates of current plans, and propose possible alternatives or improvements to avoid any negative plan outcomes.

## Course Assessment Measures

Lecture Tests (10 points each): There are 3 lecture tests per unit. Each is worth 10 Points. Each must be submitted prior to 10:10 AM of the next class day. Each lecture Test has 2 Parts. Entry Part I and Entry Part II are each graded according to the following rubric:

Mastery of concept (1 point). For example, if you are tested on ecosystems, you should be able to clearly define what an ecosystem is.

Apply core concepts (1 point). For example, if you are tested on ecosystems, you should be able to clearly apply the definition of ecosystem to describe the components of a new or different example.

Apply reasoning to issue (1 point). For example, if you are tested on ecosystems, you should be able to clearly explain how the components are interacting with each other in a normal or abnormal way, and make predictions as to the fate of this ecosystem’s structure or function.

Review sources (1 point). For example, if you are tested on ecosystems, you should be able to find, research and review a citation/article/paper.

Submit a citation (1 point). This can just be a weblink to the source you reviewed to compose your answer.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

## Academic Integrity

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

1. Learning is interactive, and we encourage this. However, when you are writing anything for submission, answers to questions on Lecture Tests, you must write your answer in your own words. You must not copy from the websites that you used for research.
2. You may not copy verbatim from any source or peer submission. Plagiarism will be assessed 0 Points. The penalty for plagiarism or cheating or not following the Honor Code is as follows: A grade of zero will be given on the first assignment where a violation is detected. If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of F for the course.
3. NOTE: If you have any doubts or concerns of whether an action constitutes academic dishonesty, please discuss it with any of your instructors or TAs.

Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

HONOR CODE:

- You promise that all your answers are a product of your own individual effort.

- You promise that you did not take help from anyone else when answering the questions posed in this online lab test and in the associated online lab.

- You promise that you will not discuss the particular questions or answers posed in here, in the discussion forum, or anywhere else.

Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy may result in a penalty of

- A grade of zero will be given on the first assignment where a violation is detected.
- If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of F for the course.
