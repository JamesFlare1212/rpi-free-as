## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

A detailed survey of important topics in the neurosciences. Some of the topics to be covered in this class are: basic mechanisms of neural signaling (neurophysiology, synaptic transmission, and molecular signaling); understanding of sensation/movement and in particular how it relates to neuroanatomy; neurodevelopment and how the mature brain can change (plasticity); complex brain functions and neurological disease. May be used to fulfill the Culminating Experience requirement in Biology. Taught together with BIOL 6100.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

### Goals:

Upon completion of this course, students should have acquired a solid foundation of knowledge for many of the major topics relevant to the field of neuroscience.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- BIOL 4100 - Demonstrate a proficiency in the basic electrophysiological and biochemical properties of brain cells by the conclusion of the course.
- BIOL 4100 - Discuss the development of the brain by the conclusion of the course.
- BIOL 4100 - Discuss how the cells of the central nervous system communicate with other cells in the central nervous system and with the peripheral nervous system.
- BIOL 4100 - Demonstrate a proficiency in how the brain interprets its surroundings through the senses (sight, hearing, taste, touch, and smell).
- BIOL 4100 - Demonstrate a proficiency in the understanding of the role of the brain in higher, complex behaviors (i.e., cognition).
- BIOL 4100 - Demonstrate a basic understanding of the pathophysiology of neurological, neurodevelopmental, and psychiatric disorders.
- BIOL 4100 - Apply basic concepts in neurobiology to challenging questions in the field of neuroscience by the conclusion of the course.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**GRADED ASSIGNMENTS - GENERAL INFORMATION:** All assignments for this course should be completed independently. Exams must be completed without the assistance of any other sources or devices.

Students who violate these requirements will be penalized. See the "Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information" at the end of the syllabus for more details. **EXAMS:** There will be a total of five major exams. Each exam will be comprised of 60 multiple choice questions, worth 2 points each, covering the assigned readings, lectures, and presentations that have occurred since the pervious exam (i.e., the new material on which students have not yet been assessed). Students will be allotted a total of 45 minutes to complete all of the multiple choice questions on each exam, and students will be required to complete the exam during a designated 24 hr period. Questions on the exams will be presented one at a time, and once an answer is submitted, the student will be prohibited from going back to any prior questions (i.e., no backtracking). Students are not permitted to make recordings or take screen shots or pictures of the exam questions for any reason. Exams are closed notes/book exams. Students are not permitted to use any sources for assistance during exams and must complete the exams independently.  
**CRITICAL REACTION PAPERS:**  
The course will be roughly divided into 5 sections, one for each of the 5 exams, and students will be required to find and read a peer reviewed primary empirical journal article relevant to the material covered in each of the 5 sections of the course. Students will then be required to write a critical reaction paper of the article they read using MS-Word. Students may select an article relevant to any of the material that was covered in that particular section of the course. A peer reviewed primary empirical journal article is a publication in a peer reviewed journal that represents the first reporting of results from one or more experiments. Such articles traditionally have an introduction, methods (including a clear description of the procedures), results (normally involving statistical analyses and data presentations in tables/graphs), and a discussion or conclusion section. If you have any questions regarding whether or not an article qualifies as a peer reviewed primary empirical journal article, please do not hesitate to ask Dr. Flint about it. Each critical reaction will be comprised of a 700-750 word analysis of the article (excluding any title or reference pages) and students must provide a reference citation of the article being critiqued at the end. Students should also be prepared to provide a copy of the article to the TA or Dr. Flint if requested. Since this assignment is restricted to a single research article, and because students must provide a reference citation, there will be no need to provide in-text reference citations in the paper. However, students are prohibited from using direct quotes. Any information from the article should be paraphrased in the students own words. Critical Reaction papers will be worth 20 points each. These papers are NOT summaries of the articles that were read, but instead should be critiques of the articles. In composing each critical reaction, students might considering one or more of the following:

- Is the hypothesis for each experiment clearly stated and do you think that it is an important issue to study?
- What are the strengths of the study and why do you consider them strengths?
- Are there any flaws or weaknesses? Please explain.
- Do the authors under or over generalize the impact of their results? Please explain.
- Do the authors use appropriate statistical analyses of their data and/or appropriately present their data in tables and/or graphs? Why or why not?
- Are the author's findings correctly interpreted and/or generalized? Please explain.

Critical reaction papers will be graded using the following rubric:

- Writing (worth 5 pts)
  - 5 pts - Paper is well-written and organized in a meaningful manner with appropriate paragraphs. Paper is devoid of spelling, grammar, and punctuation errors.
  - 4 pts - Paper is moderately well-written and/or moderately well-organized and/or has 2-4 spelling, grammar, and/or punctuation errors.
  - 3 pts - Writing and/or organization is average and/or there are 5-7 spelling, grammar, and/or punctuation errors.
  - 2 pts - Paper is poorly written and/or poorly organized, and/or the paper has 8-10 spelling grammar, and/or punctuation errors
  - 1 pts - Paper is poorly written and/or poorly organized and/or has more than 10 spelling grammar, and/or punctuation errors.
  - 0 pts - assignment not submitted
- Content (paper clearly focuses on providing a critical reaction to the article and does not summarize the article beyond what is necessary to place the critical reaction into context) = 15 pts
  - 14-15 pts - Paper provides an excellent critique, examining the article thoroughly for at least 5 strengths/weaknesses/flaws etc. There is no unnecessary summary of material from the article.
  - 12-13 pts - Paper provides a good critique, thoroughly examining the article for 3-4 strengths/weakness/flaws, etc. and/or there is one instance where there was unnecessary summary of material provided.
  - 8-11 pts - Paper provides an average critique, presenting shallow examination of the 3 or more issues or a thorough examination of only 2 strengths/weakness/flaws, etc. and/or there are two instances where unnecessary summaries of material from the article were provided.
  - 1-7 pts - Paper provides a below average or poor critique, presenting a shallow examination of fewer than 3 issues or a thorough examination of only 1 strength/weakness/flaw etc. and/or the paper contains significant unnecessary summaries of portions of the article.
  - 0 pts - assignment not submitted.

Note: Critiques that do not fall within the word limit for the assignment or that use quotations from the article will receive additional point deductions.

### Other Course Policies:

**TIME COMMITMENT:** It is expected that students will read the assigned material prior to listening to the audio lecture and that students will spend a minimum of 12 hrs a week working on the material for this course (4 hrs/credit/week). This time should include reading the textbook, listening to the audio lectures, reviewing/studying the textbook and notes, completing critical reaction papers, etc.  
**LECTURES:** Lectures are not designed to cover everything in a textbook and no textbook is comprehensive. The majority of the lectures will be comprised of selected topics from the book. Not all of the material found in the text will be covered in the audio lecture, but students are still responsible for all of the material in the assigned reading. Some of the material covered in class will not be found in the textbook, so it is important that students listen to the audio lectures and take good notes. Students are encouraged to ask questions. Since this is an asynchronous course, students should write down questions they develop as they review the course material, and then attend office hours with the instructor or the TA.  
**MAKE-UP EXAM POLICY:** Given the asynchronous format of this class and the fact that students will have a 24-hr period during which to complete the 45-min exam, make-up exams will be permitted only under very extenuating circumstances where official, written, and verifiable documentation can be provided. Such documentation must specifically indicate that the student was unable to complete the remote exam during the 24-hr allotted exam period. Dr. Flint reserves the right to verify and decide on the legitimacy of all excuses.  
Students who miss an exam, and cannot provide a legitimate excuse, may take a make-up exam but will receive an automatic 25% deduction.  
All make-up exams must be completed within 1 week of returning to campus (i.e., the student is attending one or more classes on campus or virtually). It is the student's responsibility to schedule the make-up exam with Dr. Flint. Failure to schedule and complete a make-up exam within 1 week of resuming one or more classes will result in an additional 10% deduction for each week that passes without making up the exam.  
For Exam #5, at the end of the semester, make-up exams may not be available for students who cannot provide a legitimate excuse. Students who miss Exam #5, but do have a legitimate excuse, may be required to take an incomplete in the course.  
**CRITICAL REACTION PAPER LATE POLICY:** Critical Reaction papers must be submitted by 11:59 pm on the scheduled due date. Assignments received after this deadline, but within 24 hrs, will receive a 50% deduction on the assignment. Papers submitted between 24 and 48 hrs late will receive a 75% deduction. Critical Reaction papers received more than 48 hrs after the submission deadline will not be accepted.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

As indicated previously, all exams must be completed independently and without the assistance of any other sources or materials. Students caught cheating on an exam will automatically receive a "0" on that assignment and an academic integrity violation will be filed with the Institution.  
As a general rule, students should always provide in-text reference citations accompanying any material that is paraphrased from another source. However, for the Critical Reaction papers required for this course, Dr. Flint is making an exception to the in-text citation requirement. Since each Critical Reaction is on a single article and because a reference citation must be provided for that article, students will not be required to provide in-text citations every time they paraphrase material from that source or reference that source. Students are prohibited from quoting material directly from the article and the paper should focus on a critique of the article as opposed to a summary. For the Critical Reaction assignments, and only these assignments, charges of plagiarism will not be made for not providing in-text citations.  
Students caught cheating a second time will automatically receive an "F" in the course and a second academic integrity violation will be filed with the Institution.
