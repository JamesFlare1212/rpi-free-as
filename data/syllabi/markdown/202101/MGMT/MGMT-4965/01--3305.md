## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The objective of this course is to convey the benefits of using a systematic and analytical approach to marketing decision-making, and to build skills and confidence in undertaking such analyses on your own. We will study various tools for generating marketing insights from empirical data in such areas as segmentation, targeting, positioning, customer choice, new product design, and forecasting. This will be a hands-on course based on the Marketing Engineering approach and an online software (equivalent to Excel), in which you apply the tools studied to actual business cases.

You will become adept in systematizing decision-making based on powerful modeling methodology. We will cover various analytical techniques typically bundled under “marketing analytics”, including such fun (but powerful!) stuff as regression, cluster analysis, discriminant analysis, logit model, conjoint analysis, bass model, etc. We will NOT be approaching these topics theoretically, that is, via equations, proofs and other things most people hate. Instead, we’ll learn how they work, when to use them, and what they tell marketers. Students from MGMT 6965 will also complete a group project in which you collect and analyze data or introduce your fellow students to a marketing analytics technique not studied in class.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Unlike conventional integrative courses that focus on conceptual material, this course will attempt to provide skills to translate conceptual understanding into specific operational plans -- a skill in increasing demand in organizations today.

Specifically, the course objectives are to:

1. Provide students with an understanding of the role that analytical techniques and computer models can play in enhancing marketing decision making in modern enterprises.
2. Improve students’ skill in viewing business processes and relationships systematically and analytically.
3. Expose students to numerous business examples demonstrating the value of the analytic approach to marketing decision making.
4. Provide students with the software tools that will enable them to apply the models and methods taught in the course to real problems.
5. Improve students’ skills in interpreting and utilizing the results of statistical modeling to enhance decision making in real-world marketing practice.

## COURSE STRUCTURE

The basic pedagogical approach is to employ a mix of learning methods consistent with the relatively compressed ‘module’ format of this course. There will be lectures, discussions, video presentations, software tutorials, case analyses, presentations, and an exam. Class sessions will be devoted to probing, extending and applying the material in the readings and the cases. One could call this “Tell-Show-Do”, a sequence providing hands-on experience in using the course materials for making marketing decisions. Lectures will cover the concepts and models you need in order to understand – and to apply – a scientific approach to marketing. Applications are illustrated in the cases, readings, and the examples; the software tools allow for hands-on opportunities to apply the concepts and models to resolve real-life marketing problems.

During a case session, one or two group will present the case. The instructor together with the presentation groups will lead you through a discussion of the case. The instructor will act as a facilitator, asking questions, driving conversation, and keeping track of ideas as they emerge. You will learn more if you come to class prepared to share your ideas and your experiences, and open to listening to your fellow participants as they share theirs.

**Groups:** All assignments in this class, including case write-ups, case presentations and final project presentations, involve group work. I will assign you to a group of about 4 members, preferably with diverse background, with the same learning mode (synchronous or asynchronous) and registered at the same level (4965 vs 6965), but I will consider your preference. Please find your friends and let me know your preference in the first two weeks. **Critical Note**: Your case and project grades rely on your group’s performance and your individual performance. Group members will regularly rate one another in terms of their relative contribution in group work. As such, shirking group responsibilities is by far the surest route to oblivion.

**EXAM**

There is an open-book final exam. It will be OPTIONAL. Full details of this mysterious process will be provided later. The main idea is that, if grades really matter to you, you will have the opportunity to distinguish yourself via the exam. If grades aren’t that important, you can take the exam for fun, or elect not to.

Only if there is emergency, severe medical problem, or officially sanctioned Institute activities can you schedule a makeup exam. To do so, you need to get approval from me and submit the formal Excused Absence Request through your class dean.

**CASE ANALYSIS (GROUP)**

This course focuses on “learning by doing.” In that spirit, teams will empirically analyze five assigned case and develop their recommendations. **Case Summaries**: For each case that we will discuss, your team will submit an absolutely-no-more-than-two-page executive summary that contains your recommendations and rationale for them. Use the case discussion questions as a guide in developing your recommendations.

**Case Presentation**: Each team will also do at least one in-class **case presentation** (20 min) and **facilitate the discussion** in the case session (the number will depend on the number of teams in the course; we will have at least one team, but preferably two, presenting and leading discussion on each case). This is not shorthand for “blow off the cases you are not presenting”. Presentations will be arranged well in advance for your team to prepare appropriately. These will be assigned randomly, once groups stabilize. You **need not** submit an executive summary for the case you present. Instead, send me your slides and analyses (as below). **Anal-Retentive Note**: Please send summaries in PDF; for presentations, I’ll need both your PPT and “native” format (XLS, DOC, etc.) Include your group number (I’ll send those out) in all filenames and on all documents. These case write-ups are due before class: 2PM on the day of case sessions.

**Expectation:** You are expected to present your work in a manner that is consistent with a business professional. Work should be well-organized and flow in a manner that allows the reader to understand the purpose without having to try to figure out what you are saying. Write the paper from the third person point of view (grammar). Make sure that you are diplomatic to the client and that you are recommending these actions. Proofread for spelling and grammatical errors. Use headings, subheadings, and bullets as needed. If a section does not make sense, multiple points may be deducted. These points will be taken off the final grade.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Students can take this course either synchronously (in person/ online) or asynchronously (online)**. Students who are taking this course in person should bring in a laptop and a headset to participate WebEx meeting discussion, to collaborate with online students, and to clearly listen to the instructor. Students who are taking this course synchronously online should get on live meetings via WebEx and participate in class discussion via WebEx Meeting & WebEx Team. If a student needs to take this course completely asynchronously, please inform me your reason (e.g., different time zone, medical issue, etc.) at the beginning of the semester. This is important for me to assign you to a group in which students are taking class under the same learning mode and can work together without scheduling difficulties.

**Everyone should feel free to contribute actively to class discussions and to offer elaborations and examples during or after** the live meetings. This component will count both formally and “on the margin” in terms of course grades. After each class, I will award participation points to each student based on the rubrics below. **Synchronous students** should submit a brief one-sentence summary in the WebEx Team about what you have said during the class participation. **Asynchronous students** should view the recordings of the meeting later on and submit written discussion posts to in the WebEx Team before the deadline. If a **synchronous student** cannot attend a live meeting for some reason, please notify me in advance and submit the written posts like the asynchronous students do.

Please note that, no matter which learning mode you are using (in class, synchronously online, or asynchronously), **discussion** **quality counts more than quantity**! That is, your contributions should enhance both the content and process of our discussions, build on answers of others, grounded in evidence, or stimulate thinking. Please don’t take things personally. Have an open mind -- be inquisitive and skeptical, but not dogmatic. For written discussion answers, I look for quality of answers (e.g., meaningful and thoughtful), and to a certain extent, length of your written answers. Short written responses to discussion questions will not earn full points.
