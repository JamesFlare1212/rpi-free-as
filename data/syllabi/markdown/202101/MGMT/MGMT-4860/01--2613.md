## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Executives love to say that their employees are their organizations’ most important assets. Although there is sometimes a gap between what executives say and what they do, in this instance, they are right. A high performing workforce is critical to success in current day organizations. A company with the best reasoned strategic plan, a solid balance sheet, next-generation manufacturing or service operations, and a creatively developed marketing plan, will fail to reach the performance standards it seeks if its employees are not working at a high level.

This observation is particularly true of companies like the ones where many of you will work, which emphasize technology and knowledge work in the global economy. High technology managers are very aware that engineers, scientists, and other professionals who walk out the door take their intellectual capital with them. In knowledge-based companies, ideas, creativity and innovation, technical expertise, and experience reside in the brains of employees. When engaged and motivated, they create the next-generation products and services that contribute significantly to company success.

Employees will engage most actively with their work when tasks are challenging, they have autonomy in determining how to approach their work, and they have a sense of purpose. Supportive human resources systems allow employees to concentrate on their work. Most of you will not work in the human resources function, but knowledge of how it works and how it can work for you as a professional or manager will give you an advantage in navigating the often-complex dynamics of organizational life.

Where does the human resources function contribute? Among other ways, it:

- formulates plans for the types and numbers of employees needed to get the work done
- develops practices to attract the right kinds of job applicants
- enables managers to select the best of them for job offers
- coordinates training and development opportunities to keep employees up-to-date on their skills and to hone the people skills necessary to manage effectively
- sets up the tools to ensure that employees operate at a high level and receive worthwhile feedback on how to improve their performance
- aligns pay and benefits in ways that are attractive to employees
- provides them incentives to work effectively
- ensures that employees work in a safe and healthy environment
- protects their right to fair, non-discriminatory treatment whether or not they belong to unions
- facilitates company international divisions to align policies and practices that balance uniform global systems with adaptations to local socio-cultural and politico-economic conditions
- conducts all of these activities in ways that actively value and encourage diversity

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Framework for the Course**

This course examines the human resources activities needed from initial hiring plans through employee departures. In contrast to an outmoded concept of HR as a record-keeping function, it presents a proactive approach that positions it as a critical contributor to success.

**The Classroom as a Learning Laboratory** In this course, we will bring a practice-oriented approach to the material. In addition to analyzing behavior in cases and texts, we will use the classroom situation itself as a source of data to examine human resources activities. Consequently, we will use classroom time for discussions, simulations, role-playing, and group and intergroup interaction. Considerable work will be done in your learning groups, which we will use for several class sessions and out-of-class work. Ideally, we will offer a good feel for how human resources functions while enabling you to further develop analytic skills. If your group is effective, you will create an open, low-risk, non-threatening environment where you are encouraged to learn and gain valuable feedback from your colleagues.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

### Regular Chapter Quizzes

Quizzes need to be taken by the date and time their associated chapters are listed on the syllabus. Each quiz will be equally weighted. They are scheduled once a week, to be taken by a designated time on the day of asynchronous sessions. The lowest score will be eliminated from the calculations.

Individual Write-Ups and Entries

All write-ups and entries should be clearly written, well-organized, and submitted by their due dates. Late submissions will be penalized by 10 points per session.

In written work, it is important to analyze as well as describe. Description tells what happened. Analysis assesses how and why it happened. Description should serve the analysis. Carefully think through the issues and present logically-reasoned arguments to support your case.

The **write-ups** are based on the cases or videos identified on the syllabus. Each should be 250-300 words. They should be analytical and reflective rather than simply descriptive. That is, they should consider the whys? and hows? rather than simply reporting the whats? They will be submitted in the Assignments section of Blackboard.

The **entries** are a separate item. I will post questions in Blackboard’s Discussion Board section and will expect each student to post at least one entry in response to each question. Entries can respond directly to questions I post or to any follow-up questions or to entries by other students. It will help if they can incorporate material from relevant classwork, chapters, readings, and/or lectures.

Write-ups and Discussion Board entries will be due on alternate weeks, completed prior to the live session each week. While each write-up will be graded, assessment of Discussion Board contributions will be based on the quality and quantity of the cumulative contributions over time.

**Group Exercises**

We will engage in three group exercises during the course of the semester, one each for recruiting, training and development, and performance management. For each exercise, your group will be responsible for submitting a 2-3 page paper and making an 8-10 minute class presentation. Information on the nature of each exercise is contained in the Group Exercises section near the end of MindTap.

Relevant criteria for judging the presentations will include:

- (1) Scope - did the presentation cover the relevant material in sufficient breadth and depth?
- (2) Clarity - was the explanation of ideas and concepts clear?
- (3) Quality of analysis – how well did the group analyze the main issues?
- (4) Quality of application – how well did the group connect conceptual material to the topic of the presentation?
- (5) Contribution – to what extent did the group increase our understanding of the topic? and
- (6) Interest – how well did the group hold our interest?

**Final Exam**

We will discuss the nature of this component in detail toward the end of the semester.
