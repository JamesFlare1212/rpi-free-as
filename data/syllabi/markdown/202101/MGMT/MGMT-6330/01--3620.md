### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Participation in Class Discussions:

This is a case-based course. Students should read the assigned materials, analyze the cases and come prepared to discuss their findings in the class. Students will be asked to lead case discussions and are expected to contribute to the in-class learning process. Students should be prepared to lead the discussions of all the cases. It is not sufficient to make simple comments in the class. Students are expected to take ownership of class discussions and help the class to develop a shared understanding of issues related to a topic or a case. Please come prepared to express your views, defend your ideas, raise questions, probe other students’ viewpoints, and engage in rigorous discussions.

Simulation Games:

Simulation games will be used to provide students an opportunity to understand the complexities in managing operations and supply chains. Two simulation games will be played in the class.

*Beer Distribution Game* simulates a multi-tier supply chain where communication across tiers is not allowed. Students play this game as a team with the goal of minimizing their team’s backorder and inventory holding costs. Each student will be responsible for ordering and shipment decisions for their individual role in the team.

*Harvard’s Global SCM*, which simulates a global cell phone manufacturing firm, would require students to engage in forecasting, demand planning, production planning and contracting. Students play this game in several rounds and are evaluated on their supply chain performance along with the quality of their report (see course calendar for the deadline). Students play individually in this game, however, the report should be prepared in groups of two. This game requires subscription. Details on how to subscribe will be provided in class.

If you miss the class for any simulation game session, there will be no make-up opportunity.

Exam:

There will be one exam. Check out the course calendar on the last page for the exam date. Exam requires individual work; students cannot collaborate in any way. Copying or communicating during the exam is cheating.

Homework Assignments:

The homework assignments are primarily of a “concept check” variety and are designed to make sure you are keeping up with the fundamental concepts. They are also intended to keep your workload manageable and allow you to focus on building the basic intuition. All homework assignments should be done in groups of two. Prior to the assignment due date, do not discuss your answers with any student other than your partner, do not allow any student to view your work or answers, do not ask any student about her/his answers, and do not view any other student’s work or answers. Homework assignments should be submitted through LMS at the beginning of class on assigned due dates.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
