## COURSE DESCRIPTION:

Today, we are truly in a digital economy, fueled by advances in social, mobility, analytics, cloud and Internet of Things technologies. The Internet and associated technological innovations have helped in restructuring the global economy into a networked economy, characterized by unprecedented levels of electronically-mediated communication, collaboration, and commerce. From a mere 2% of the gross domestic product (GDP) in the nineties, the digital economy now constitutes over 12% of the GDP and continues to grow. The IT sector is now the single largest sector in S&P 500.

As forward-thinking chief executives of tomorrow’s businesses, it is essential that you develop an understanding of cutting-edge technological trends and their potential business impact. Managers in today's corporate environment cannot be effective without knowing the role of IT and its impacts in terms of transformations on the organizational, process, market, and global enterprise levels. Insightful executives of leading companies across the world are increasingly leveraging IT to create value and win competitive advantage.

This course brings together technical and managerial perspectives to discuss key issues relevant to IT. Understanding of technology-enabled changes in contemporary business environments and how insightful executives leverage IT is key to creating value and winning competitive advantage. The course will also explore the business drivers of technology-related decisions in firms and stimulate thought on new applications of technology for commerce, including new products, processes, and business models (that are often very disruptive). Some of the topics we focus on include:

- Understanding how different business models that firms pursue necessitate different kinds of investment in IT. How can we make a business case for creating IT assets that lead to a competitive edge?
- Competitive dynamics of platform models such as Google, Apple and Facebook that emphasize network effects or demand side economies of scales rather than supply side economies of scale.
- Delve into search engine and online marketing.
- Understand how IT coupled with Big Data Analytics impacts different industries, including entertainment industry as well as bricks and mortar firms like Gap.
- Whether and how IT hastens and aids the growth of disruptive innovation upstarts like Coursera and iPhone. We will learn from the demise of other incumbents such as the recording companies, Barnes and Noble, etc.
- How P&G used open innovation network, Innocentive, to improve its R&D productivity by over 35%
- Adapting to the peer economy

At a conceptual level, this course is designed to make the students knowledgeable of the fundamentals underlying the design, implementation, control, evaluation, and strategic use of modern information systems. While some of the effort will be devoted to hands-on work with some business software, the major emphasis will be on the understanding the managerial and strategic aspects of information technology. The course will be useful for managers, consultants, technology entrepreneurs, venture capitalists, and active investors, or anyone who is looking to lead an organization in the digital economy.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

## STUDENT LEARNING OUTCOMES

1. *Terminology*: The student will be able to explain the meaning of terms used to describe common techniques and concepts in business information systems.
2. *Advanced Information Systems Concepts*: The student will be able to describe the ways in which computers are and will be used in business and management. These concepts include telecommunications, electronic commerce, business analytics, and future directions of information systems.
3. *Business Strategy and Systems Development*: The students will be involved in a small-scale IT project and experience how it is to work in a team setting. They will not need to do programming in this course but will evaluate a business lifecycle for their product.
4. *Behavioral and Organizational Issues*: The student will be able to identify and suggest appropriate responses to managerial and organizational issues stemming from development, implementation, and use of computer-based information systems.
5. *Social and Ethical Issues*: The student will understand the major social and ethical issues involved in the development and use of information technology.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**1. Team Projects and Presentation (30% of your grade):**

This course places a lot of emphasis on experiential learning through team projects. You should form into groups of 4-6 as early as possible. Specifically, we will explore two powerful concepts that affect how technology affects the business world:

1. Products to Platforms (15%)

This assignment will be posted before the second session of the class in week 2, but it will not be due until Week 6. For the submission, you will be asked to identify a business that is employing a “pipe” model that you feel would be ripe for transition to a “platform model.” You will be asked to describe the business model, the company’s market position and considerations for making such a transition. You will be asked to articulate ways that the business might proceed with a transition to a platform strategy, possible variants of such a strategy, important considerations, pricing and revenue, etc. This assignment and presentation will therefore be submitted on LMS; more details on this will be provided in Week 2.

Timeline:

- Select the product company by Week 4.
- In-class presentation in Week 7 (Session 13).

B. Industry Transformation Analysis (15%)

Information technologies are catalyzing significant transformations in the business models, products, services, or pricing strategies across a variety of industries. While some of the industries are already deep into transformation, other industries are just beginning to experience digital transformations and yet others are potential candidates for the future. The goal of this project is to help students analyze and discover how industries are likely to be impacted by the current and emerging information technologies. Each student team will select a specific industry and develop an analysis of the digital transformations occurring or likely to occur in that industry. A detailed structure for the analysis will be provided at the beginning of the term (tentatively in Session 1). The deliverables will include an in-class rapid fire presentation. More details on deliverables and grading are provided at the end of this syllabus.

We will have in-class presentations using the Ignite model (visit http://www.ignitetalks.io/). Their motto is “Enlighten us, but make it quick.” The operating model is:

“In talks that are exactly five minutes long, Ignite presenters share their personal and professional passions, using 20 slides that auto-advance every 15 seconds.” We’ll incorporate this model with 12 slides that auto-advance every 30 seconds, with one minute for Q&A.

Timeline:

- Select industry by Session 7 (right after Project 1 presentations).
- In-class presentation in Week 14 (Session 28).

Post your presentation file on LMS in the folder that I will create for group projects; the due date is 8 AM on the day of session 28. Your PPT/alternative format presentation file must contain slide(s) listing all the references/ resources/back up materials that you used for your research towards the end. Try to make slides self-explanatory and focus on key issues in class.

If anything is not clear on the project assignments, please bring your questions to class so that all of us are on the same page with respect to what we discuss and decide.  
**2. Individual Tech Assignment (5% of your grade):**

A Google Document will be made available for signing up for the individual tech assignment session with schedule relying on a first come-first serve basis. If you do miss your session without a valid excuse, it may be difficult to reschedule and you may face a penalty. Please email me if you have any questions.

The goal of this is assignment is to have an in depth understanding of a specific technology related to IT/IS. The idea is to dive deeply into the technology foundations and associated technologies. How has the technology developed or evolved? Are there underlying systems on which the technology relies? How do you see this technology affecting a firm’s business strategy? Examples of technologies include cloud computing, IOS app store, Linux operating system, Hadoop.

Make a brief presentation (max 3 minutes) on a technology of your choice. Bonus points if you could relate it to one of the course themes. You need to upload the link and/or reference article and your presentation on the discussion board on LMS.  
**3. Weekly quizzes (35% of your grade):**

There are no exams in this course. However, there are weekly quizzes (usually in the form of short answer questions) that will provide an opportunity for you to demonstrate your mastery of theoretical concepts and vocabulary presented in lectures and readings. Active class participation will help improve your chances of doing better in the quizzes.

These quizzes will focus on the topic from the previous week. Given the class schedule, most quizzes will be on Tuesday and will be administered on LMS. I also drop your worst quiz score (e.g., if there are 10 quizzes, I will use the best 9 out of 10 quizzes in your score calculation). ***If you miss one, there won’t be any “make up” pop up quizzes*** unless you can convince the Student Experience office and me of the ***extreme circumstances***.  
**4. Assignments/Case Submissions (20% of your grade):**

*Written Analyses*: Where specified, you will submit written analyses for designated cases/readings throughout the term. The analyses will be responses to the specific questions provided by the instructor and will be posted on LMS, with due dates and times specified.

The submissions should not be more than one and a half (single spaced) pages long and *should* address the questions posed. In case you are late in submitting the assignment, a typical penalty would be docking of some points off the assignment. For instance, if you submit a 10 point assignment a day late, I would dock 2 points and grade it out of 8 points; if you submit it two days late, I would dock 4 points and grade it out of 6 points, and so on. Submissions late by 5 days or more would receive 0 points. Please email me if you have any questions.

**5. Class Participation (10% of your grade):**

I expect everyone to come to class after reading all the assigned articles and cases and contribute to the discussions by offering your unique perspectives and reacting to that of others in a thoughtful manner. I might cold call (yes, even via WebEx) – so please make sure that you have something to say if I ask you to summarize an article or case in your words or to respond to someone else’s summary, question or comment.

Because some of you may be remote, here are my expectations for remote class participation:

1. You will attend the lecture synchronously. If you are unable to do so, please notify me with your reasons.
2. During the class, your cameras shall be **on** at all times. If it is not possible for you to do so, please notify me immediately.
3. You will do in-class group activities through break out rooms on WebEx or via WebEx Teams (more on this in the first lecture).
4. This is a communications intensive class; therefore, class activities are built in for intense team discussions and in-class presentations.
5. I also encourage online participation (through LMS or WebEx Chat). If you cannot make it to a class that involves an article reading, you should post your viewpoint about the article in the Blog/discussion board of the class before that class to partially make up for your absence.
6. At the end of each week, I would post an online survey in which you can detail how you participated in class for that week.

A proactive and positive attitude towards class participation is expected and will be a factor in grading. While frequency and consistency of contributions are important, you should try to surface and prioritize issues not previously raised, offer persuasive arguments, extend the discussion and ask thoughtful questions. Questions need not be directed to the instructor all the time –be willing to ask, clarify, and respond to your classmates’ questions. Class participation may also involve some work outside or inside the class to reinforce learning of course concepts. For instance, active and cogent participation in in-class (online) activities would also contribute towards your class participation grade or sharing of an interesting article with a thoughtful commentary would enrich your and your classmates’ experience. Keep in mind that the current COVID related situation **necessitates** our active online participation and interaction.
