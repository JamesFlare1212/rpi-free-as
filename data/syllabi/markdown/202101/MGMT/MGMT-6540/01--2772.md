## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a cross-listed course mainly focusing on integrated marketing communication (IMC). IMC includes advertising, sales promotions, public relations, personal selling, direct marketing, event sponsorship, etc. Students will develop skills in creating, planning, implementing and evaluating client-oriented projects by developing an integrated marketing communication campaign. Students are responsible for applying the content in Marketing Principles (MGMT 2430) to the project in this course. Students need to be especially familiar with the material pertaining to promotion, consumer decision making, segmenting, targeting, differentiation, and positioning.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course is interactive and hands-on by design. There will be varied learning opportunities in formats such as traditional lecture, in-class experiential exercises, assignments, and an experiential group project. The goal of the course is to help you understand how to develop an innovative and effective integrated marketing communications plan with potential to make a positive impact for an organization.

The objectives of this course are:

1. To help students gain an understanding of target customer needs and how IMC is being used by marketers to develop brand identities that leverage those needs
2. To help students identify and explain the major components of the IMC process
3. To acquaint students with analyzing IMC situations and problems
4. To provide students with the opportunity to develop skills in creating, implementing and evaluating IMC plan
5. To challenge students to consider ethics, diversity and global issues relevant to marketing communication

**EXAMS**

There will be three non-cumulative multiple-choice midterm exams. The exams are open book/ notes with tight time limit. The exam questions will only be available online within certain time period (normally one hour) and you will need to submit your answers before the end of the session. Exams will cover materials from assigned readings, lecture notes, class videos, and class discussions.

Only if there is emergency, severe medical problem, or officially sanctioned Institute activities can you schedule a makeup exam. To do so, you need to get approval from me and submit the formal Excused Absence Request through your class dean.

**ASSIGNMENTS**

There are two assignments for students in MGMT 4490: 1) Target Audience Persona and 2) Super Bowl Advertising Evaluation and Presentation. For students in MGMT 6540, there is one extra assignment, 3) Social Media Auditing and Presentation, for which the students need to choose a lecture day to present your findings at the beginning of the class. For more details, please see related assignment description files.

**GROUP PROJECT**

The primary objective of this team-based project is to acquaint students with the complexities facing companies as they communicate product offerings to the marketplace. The Integrated Marketing Communications (IMC) Plan Project involves student groups developing, organizing, and managing student-run IMC agencies. Each group needs to adopt a formal agency name (e.g., Brink Media) and develop a detailed IMC plan and prepares the deliverables for a client.

**Deliverables:** The IMC Plan Guideline will be posted on LMS and updated regularly. Project deliverables include two status reports, one final report, and one final presentation. Each group will give a 25-minute group presentation during the last week of class. The entire class is expected to participate and vote for the best presentation and presenter.

**Expectation:** You are expected to present your work in a manner that is consistent with a business professional. Work should be well-organized and flow in a manner that allows the reader to understand the purpose without having to try to figure out what you are saying. Write the paper from the third person point of view (grammar). Make sure that you are diplomatic to the client and that you are recommending these actions. Proofread for spelling and grammatical errors. Use headings, subheadings, and bullets as needed. This is expected for all homework assignments as well as the project. If a section does not make sense, multiple points may be deducted. These points will be taken off the final grade.

**Groups:** You will be allowed to form your own groups, but I reserve the right to place latecomers into appropriate homes and assign students using different learning mode (synchronous vs. asynchronous) to different groups. Groups should have about 4 members and strive for heterogeneity in composition. Please submit your group decision on LMS before the due date listed on Course Calendar. **Critical Note**: Your project grades rely on your group’s performance and your individual performance. At the end of the semester, group members will rate one another in terms of their relative contribution in group work. As such, shirking group responsibilities is by far the surest route to oblivion.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Students can take this course either synchronously (in person/ online) or asynchronously (online)**. Students who are taking this course in person should bring in a laptop and a headset to participate WebEx meeting discussion, to collaborate with online students, and to clearly listen to the instructor. Students who are taking this course synchronously online should get on live meetings via WebEx and participate in class discussion via WebEx Meeting & WebEx Team. If a student needs to take this course completely asynchronously, please inform me your reason (e.g., different time zone, medical issue, etc.) at the beginning of the semester. This is important for me to assign you to a group in which students are taking class under the same learning mode and can work together without scheduling difficulties.

**Everyone should feel free to contribute actively to class discussions and to offer elaborations and examples during or after** the live meetings. This component will count both formally and “on the margin” in terms of course grades. After each class, I will award participation points to each student based on the rubrics below. **Synchronous students** should submit a brief one-sentence summary in the WebEx Team about what you have said during the class participation. **Asynchronous students** should view the recordings of the meeting later on and submit written discussion posts to in the WebEx Team before the deadline. If a **synchronous student** cannot attend a live meeting for some reason, please notify me in advance and submit the written posts like the asynchronous students do.

Please note that, no matter which learning mode you are using (in class, synchronously online, or asynchronously), **discussion** **quality counts more than quantity**! That is, your contributions should enhance both the content and process of our discussions, build on answers of others, grounded in evidence, or stimulate thinking. Please don’t take things personally. Have an open mind -- be inquisitive and skeptical, but not dogmatic. For written discussion answers, I look for quality of answers (e.g., meaningful and thoughtful), and to a certain extent, length of your written answers. Short written responses to discussion questions will not earn full points.
