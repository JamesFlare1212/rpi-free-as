## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course describes how individuals (and firms) make financial decisions, and how those decisions might deviate from those predicted by traditional financial or economic theory. Throughout the semester we will examine how the insights of behavioral economics complement the traditional finance paradigm by introducing some of the main psychological biases in financial decision-making and examining the impacts of these biases in financial and other economic decisions. This course will also introduce students to behavioral and experimental methodologies used in finance, economics and other disciplines.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will learn, know, and comprehend some of the main psychological biases in financial decision-making
- Students will become aware of the current research on how these biases impact financial markets and other financial settings.
- Students will gain an understanding of how individuals actually make financial decisions.
- Students will know how to improve financial decision-making in themselves and others.
- Students will come up with promising research questions in behavioral finance.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Paper Discussions**

Synchronous Students:

- For each of these classes, we will discuss two papers in class. These will be either published or unpublished papers (chosen and assigned by me). Half of the class will be assigned to one of the papers and the other half will be assigned to the other paper. So for each of these classes, you will be responsible to read one paper.
- Each of these classes will be led by 2-3 students. These discussion leaders will read both papers and lead the discussion in class and in Slack. Each synchronous student will lead only once.
- I will lead the first paper discussion class in which we will discuss only one paper and all students will be required to read it.
- The papers will be assigned and posted about a week in advance. You should start the reading paper as soon as you can. A couple of days before the discussion day, I will create a slack channel for each paper and both synchronous and asynchronous students will be expected to start discussing the paper in slack by asking questions, providing comments etc. That week’s leaders are expected to moderate it by providing comments, answering questions etc.

***Grades:*** Your grades will be determined based on how well you led the discussions (both in class and in slack). I will also elicit peer-feedback.

Asynchronous Students

- These students are also required to read the assigned papers and engage in slack discussions (which will help determine their participation grade).
- Instead of leading discussions in class, they will prepare a 15 minute presentation of an academic paper (assigned by me). Students will record their presentations and share them with me by its due date and time. Recording presentations is easy to do and there are many free ways to do this. I will guide students on this if needed.
- These recorded videos will be posted on LMS. All students will be responsible to watch them and engage in slack discussion.
- ***Grades:*** Your grades will be determined based on how well you presented your assigned paper and how well you managed the slack discussion on your paper. I will also elicit peer-feedback.

**Reading Quizzes:**

- There will be a 24+ hour long online quiz for each paper discussion day. The 24-hour period ensures that each student, independent of their time zone, receives an equal opportunity.
- Each student will be responsible to answer the questions for their assigned paper only. If you are one of the leaders for that week, then you will be responsible to answer questions for both papers.
- There will not be any make-up quizzes. However, I will drop your lowest quiz grade in calculation of your grade.

**Participation:**

I will determine your grade based on your participation and your general classroom citizenship. In determining your grade, I will take your special circumstances (e.g. being in a different time zone) into account. If your circumstances change, make sure to communicate this to me as soon as you can.

I will keep track of your active engagement weekly. During our mid-term self-evaluation class, I will individually discuss how well you are meeting the course expectations.

Synchronous Students:

- I expect you to attend class, participate in in-class activities such as MobLab, participate in and contribute to in-class discussions (by asking questions, providing answers or comments etc), be an active participant in all slack discussions, and have read the materials that will be assigned.
- In general, you are expected to join all lectures and be an **active** participant. If you cannot join lectures for any reason, you are expected to communicate this to me as soon as possible.

Asynchronous Students:

- I expect you to watch all lecture videos, participate in MobLab activities (whenever possible) and engage in Slack by asking questions, contributing to discussions by making meaningful comments, answering other students’ questions etc.
- Whenever possible, I will make MobLab activities available for 24 hours for those who cannot join synchronously. But note that this won’t be possible for all activities since some require multiple players.

Slack Discussions:

- All students are expected to be an active participant in Slack. If you have a question, you should post it in Slack (#questions channel). Other students should reply in thread and provide their answers.
- We will have discussion channels for the papers you will be assigned to read (or lead/present). Everyone is expected to be actively participating in these discussions.
- I expect students to internalize the concepts we discuss in class and notice them in their everyday lives. Students are encouraged to share these encounters in Slack (#random channel). For example, suppose you saw a meme that is related to a topic we learned in class. Post it! You came across a related article/news? Post it. You experienced a situation that is related to a topic discussed in class? Post it! All in all, students are expected to post and respond to others’ posts in Slack.

Note: Derogatory and sarcastic comments or jokes that marginalize anyone will not be tolerated. You are expected to use proper language and practice common sense in all of your engagements in this class.

**Homework Assignments:**

***Write-Up Submissions***

- Each student will produce a short write-up (around 300 words). This write up must involve

1. i) a short description of a financial situation and
2. ii) a discussion of how one or more of the topics we discussed in class impacted that financial situation.

- The financial situation has to be a real-life example, and the situation can be personal, professional, or something from current events.
- You are expected to use a professional language. Make sure to proofread your write-up for grammatical errors, typos and so on before submitting.
- You are required to upload these write-ups to LMS by their indicated deadlines (listed on course calendar).

***Peer-Evaluations***

- You will read and evaluate two write-ups uploaded by other students. These peer-evaluation deadlines are also listed on the course calendar.
- You are expected to evaluate the write-ups based on the following criteria:
  - o Overall Quality
  - o Quality of the financial situation
  - o Appropriateness of the topic for the chosen financial situation
- Make sure to create a sub-section in your peer-evaluation for each of the above criteria and carefully comment on them. Your evaluation will help me determine how well you understood the concepts discussed in class.

***Homework Grades***

Each student will submit a total of 2 write-ups (40%) and evaluate 4 write-up submissions (60%). You will receive a grade for each of these items. In determining your grade, I will consider how closely you followed the instructions. Each write-up will constitute 20% and each evaluation will constitute 15% of your homework grade.

***For ECON 6340 Students***

**Proposal Presentation:**

- Each student who signed up for ECON 6340 will produce a research proposal and present their proposed research in class at the end of the semester.
- Your topic selection is flexible as long as it is somewhat related to the topics we discussed in class. In general, you will be expected to introduce and motivate your research question(s), provide some literature review, propose a research method to investigate your question(s). Your research method needs to be empirical, either using pre-existing data sets or experiments.
- Each presentation will be about 15 min long. More instructions on research proposals and presentations will be provided later in the semester.
- If preferred, students can work in pairs. If you would like to work in pairs, get in touch with me to discuss the details.
- I will elicit peer-feedback which will help me determine your grade.
