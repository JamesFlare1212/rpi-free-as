## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides an introduction to experimental methods in economics. It teaches you how to test economic theories, as well as introducing you to the experimental methods that are used to investigate questions that may not be possible to study using existing data sets. Students will be introduced to state-of-the-art research methodology in experimental economics.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will identify and understand the elements of experimental design
- Students will analyze and critique an experimental design
- Students will analyze human motivation and behavior across different economic paradigms
- Students will evaluate existing work on selected topics in behavioral and experimental economics
- Students will design an experiment and produce a research proposal

***Research Proposal:***

Overview:

You are expected to find a research question that interests you and design an experiment to study that research question. You are expected to write a research proposal that outlines your research question, its importance, and how you plan to study it (i.e. detailed explanation of your experimental design).

- You are free to work with another student if you like. Working in a group will not decrease your workload (e.g., you will not write less). For example, instead of coming up with one research question, you would be expected to come up with two questions. However, these two research questions can be *related*, one could be an extension for example. Working with another student may make it easier to brainstorm ideas and come up with an experimental design to study your research questions. I encourage students to work in pairs as I believe it boosts the overall learning experience.

- More detailed instructions will be shared in class and on LMS. However, here is an outline:

**Idea Pitch #1**

**March 15th**:

By midnight on March 15th, students pitch their ideas in Slack by posting a short description (150-200 words). In this idea pitch, they are expected to

1. i) explain what their specific research question is,
2. ii) why they think this is an interesting/important question

**March 15th – 18th**:

Students are expected to read each other’s ideas and provide feedback. You can comment on the things you like about it, things that need to be clarified, improved etc. If you have any questions, ask those. The proposer should also engage with students by answering their questions, providing clarification, revising their research idea in light of the feedback etc. In other words, you are expected to engage with one another and provide each other feedback on your ideas.

**Idea Pitch #2**

**March 29th:**

By midnight on March 29th, students pitch their ideas on Slack by posting an updated version of their first idea. In this idea pitch, they are expected to

- iii) explain what their specific research is,

1. iv) why they think this is an interesting/important question

**March 29th – April 1st:**

Students are expected to read each other’s ideas and provide feedback. You can comment on the things you like about it, things that need to be clarified, improved etc. If you have any questions, ask those. The proposer should also engage with students by answering their questions, providing clarification, revising their research idea in light of the feedback etc. In other words, you are expected to engage with one another and provide each other feedback on your ideas.

**Research Proposal Draft**

**April 19th:**

You will prepare the first draft of your research proposal (you must use the template that I will provide) and you will receive feedback on it.

It is due by **midnight** via email.

**Research Proposal Presentation**

**May 3rd:**

Present your research idea in class. Each student will give a 10-15 min presentation. This includes presenting your motivation and research question as well as a detailed explanation of your design. I will provide a template for this presentation.

Asynchronous Students: They will record their presentation and share it with me by **May 2nd midnight**. This is easy to do and there are many free ways to do this. I will guide students on this if needed.

**Final Research Proposal**

**May 10th:**

You are expected to revise your proposal based on the feedback that you will receive on your proposal draft. This final version of your research proposal is due by **midnight** via email.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy may result in a grade of zero. If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of F for the course. If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of F for the course.
