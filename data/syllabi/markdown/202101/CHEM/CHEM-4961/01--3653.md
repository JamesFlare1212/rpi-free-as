## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is designed to expose students to specific technical and leadership skills geared to promote success in future academic or industrial settings. While the learning approach is a combination of lecture and interactive discussion formats, students will also have the opportunity to learn visual statistical data analysis software that is both advanced and user-friendly for industrial problem-solving. The course is segmented into four (4) modules; Grant Writing, Project Leadership/Management, Statistical/Six Sigma Tools, and Intellectual Property (IP). Students are introduced to an industrial scientist's endeavor and perspective to: (i) garner federal funding for R&D projects (proposal writing, and general Principal Investigator (PI) training), (ii) effectively manage project work (administrative and programmatic aspects and tools, including effective presentations), (iii) execute on a project/problem leveraging Six Sigma or so-called ‘Design of Experiment’ (DoE) tools aided by user-friendly statistical data analysis software, and (iv) protect project work-products through intellectual property (IP) filings and speak ‘patent-ese’.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The learning approach is a combination of lecture and interactive discussion formats, delivered by Dr. B or a guest lecturer/professor/instructor every Monday and Thursday for 60-90 minutes throughout the semester. Guest speakers are scheduled throughout the semester to provide their real-world perspectives on aspects of each of the four (4) Modules and include career scientists, study-section proposal reviewers, patent attorneys, R&D management professionals, and technical leaders in applied statistics. Students will also have the opportunity to download and learn visual statistical data analysis software from a guest instructor. The advanced tools presented and discussed are embodied in very user-friendly software geared for academic and industrial experimental design and problem solving.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Upon completion of the course, students will have been exposed to specific technical and leadership skills geared to promote success in future academic or industrial settings. Grant Proposal (and Aspects of Thesis) Writing – Students will appreciate, (i) various funding agencies and types of grant opportunities available, (ii) evaluation of and structuring proposal layout as well as ‘funneling the reader’, and (iii) programmatic aspects/implications of federal R&D funding through the National Institutes of Health, the National Science Foundation, and the Department of Energy. Project Leadership/Management – Students will be able to identify project leadership knowledge areas, e.g., scope-schedule-budget, and leverage leadership/management tools such as work breakdown structure (WBS), Gantt charts, Ishikawa diagrams, and effective oral presentations. Statistics – Students will appreciate basic statistics, e.g., distributions, confidence intervals, error bars, hypothesis testing, regression, and be able to execute on a project/problem applying so-called ‘Design of Experiment’ (DoE) tools (Taguchi designs) aided by user-friendly statistical data analysis software. Intellectual Property – Students will be equipped with a basic understanding of the requirements and importance of protecting work products from (federally or corporate) funded programs through patents; types of patents, reading, licensing and searching patents, plus patent infringement and workaround strategies.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

*Attendance & Participation*: Class participation is an essential part of this (or really any) course. A contribution to the discussion and/or a question during formal lecture is expected at EVERY CLASS and will meet the *Attendance & Participation* measure of performance; this includes participation during in-class exercises. Further constructive contributions/stimulating questions earn additional participation performance points that will exceed *Attendance & Participation* expectations. Points will be awarded and tracked at every class to determine an average course performance. Excused absences are to be discussed/cleared in advance with Dr. B to determine if such clearance is possible. Excused absences from class consist of confirmed illness, unavoidable emergencies, etc. Class absences that are not excused will result in a zero for the *Attendance & Participation* measure of performance for those missed classes.

*Writing Assignments*: As described in the **Outline of the Course** (see above), our learning approach involves interactive discussions and/or formal lectures by guests from the real-world scheduled throughout the semester, e.g., career scientists, study-section proposal reviewers, patent attorneys, R&D management professionals, and technical leaders in applied statistics. After EVERY *guest appearance* and by the next regularly scheduled class, a 200-250 word write-up *must be submitted* based on the guest lecture. These writing assignments will give you pause and encourage you to reflect on all you witnessed, to describe takeaways and learnings, and/or to provide direct honest feedback (an opinion/critique) on aspects that you wish were explored in more depth. These writing assignments must be hand-written on paper (old school, not typed in Microsoft Word). To give you a sense of length, this paragraph is 219 words. The completed assignment should either be brought to class (for those in-person) or photographed/scanned and uploaded to your personal class folder (for those online). As with *Attendance & Participation*, excused absences are to be discussed/cleared in advance with Dr. B to determine if such clearance is possible. Excused absences from class consist of confirmed illness, unavoidable emergencies, etc. In the event of a class absence, the writing assignment *must be submitted* after watching the lecture/interview asynchronously on LMS/Mediasite at your earliest possible convenience.

*Homework*: Numerous exercises will be formally assigned throughout the semester for all four (4) Modules, with variation in both the amount of homework per Module and point-value per exercise assigned. Formal assignments will be announced during lecture and made available through LMS. As mentioned above in **References**, one should view suggested **References** as “books of the month”, therefore there will always be “homework” albeit not formally assigned. Module 3 (Statistical Thinking) is a special case. When the time is right, you will be provided instructions and asked to download a 30-day free trial of JMP software (tentatively March 8). DO NOT attempt to download the software until instructions are provided and you are requested to download as *the trial only lasts for 30 days* – you will need access to JMP to complete an exercise of significance at the end of Module 3 (tentatively due April 5). Furthermore, throughout the month (Module 3), you will be asked to work through supplemental online JMP learning. Proof of completion of online learning modules will be evidenced by emailing Dr. B “Badges of completion”.

### Other Course Policies:

**Academic Integrity**

**You are expected to work independently and without collaboration** on Homework and Writing Assignments. One (1) instance of unacceptable collaboration or plagiarism will result in a reduction of the final course grade by one (1) letter grade (ex. B to C). Another instance of academic dishonesty will result in failure of the course. Plagiarism is taken very seriously. Engaging in plagiarism robs others of their work, has a permanent impact on your credibility as a scientist, and may severely affect your academic performance and future career.

The relationship between students and faculty is based upon trust and the continued maintenance of this trust is necessary for education to be successful. Students need to trust faculty to make appropriate judgments about the content and structure of the course. Faculty members need to trust that the work turned in by students represents their own effort. Violation of this trust undermines the educational process. In addition, as professionals, you may be designing products upon which lives depend, as a result, there is no tolerance for breach of academic integrity such as cheating or plagiarizing. The Rensselaer Student Handbook and the Dean of Students Office Webpage (http://doso.rpi.edu/update.do?artcenterkey=676) defines various forms of academic dishonesty. Please familiarize yourself with these and ask us if you have questions about what constitutes academic dishonesty.
