**Course Description: Welcome**. I am pleased to welcome you to NMR Spectroscopy 6140/4140. This class is designed to create an optimal learning environment by fostering active participation in the classroom and beyond. I hope that you will enjoy the class as much as I will enjoy teaching it.

This course provides a qualitative treatment of modern techniques in multi-dimensional NMR spectroscopy, including the history of magnetic resonance, principles of NMR spectroscopy, 13C and 1H NMR, exchange and dipolar couplings, multinuclear NMR, 2D homonuclear and heteronuclear methods, nuclear Overhauser effect, relaxation, solid-state NMR and the nuts and bolts of NMR spectrometers. It is intended for graduate and upper-level undergraduate students in the School of Science and Engineering and is structured so as to facilitate routine NMR applications for research. There are no prerequisites for this course.

Attendance is a requirement. Absence for more than 3 lectures without eligible reasons will result in a compromised grade.

**Why is this Course Needed**: This course provides students with the basic concepts of NMR spectroscopy, which is a powerful tool that is widely used for the structural characterization of molecules. While most students use NMR spectroscopy in their independent graduate or undergraduate research, this is the only course in the curriculum that provides a ‘nuts and bolts’ understanding of the basic concepts of NMR, an introduction to the vast repertoire of techniques, the design of spectrometers and probes, sample preparation and optimization of experiments. NMR is a tool that is widely used in chemistry, chemical engineering, physics, biology, materials science, etc. Although this course is being offered in Chemistry, graduate and upper-level students in Chemical Engineering, Physics, MSE, Earth and Environmental Science, Biology and BCBP have found this course to be beneficial for their training due to the wide-spread utility of NMR techniques.

This course is lecture based, but it could include demonstrations on the departmental NMR instrument to strengthen concepts.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**TENTATIVE LIST OF TOPICS FOR NMR SPECTROSCOPY 6140/4140:**

History of magnetic resonance

Introduction to magnetic resonance

What is a spin?

Pulse and FT NMR

Basic one-pulse experiment

Chemical shifts

Spin relaxation

Spin-spin couplings

Instrumental aspects

Spin echo experiment

Magnetic interactions

Two-dimensional NMR

COSY, DQF-COSY, TOCSY, HSQC, HMQC,

HMBC, NOESY and ROESY experiments

Solid state NMR

Sensitivity enhancement by polarization transfer
