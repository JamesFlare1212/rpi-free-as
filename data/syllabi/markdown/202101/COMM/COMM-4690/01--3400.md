## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course covers theory, research, and practice in interactive, multimedia interface design. Topics include visual perception, information architecture, rapid prototyping, usability testing, cross-cultural design, interactive data visualization, augmented reality, and intellectual property.

## Course Text(s)

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course will focus on the design of user interfaces for interactive, multimedia computer programs. The class will present theoretical and technical issues in user interface design. Lectures and discussions will include topics such as:

- information architecture for interactive computing
- effective screen design for user interfaces
- effective use of social media
- augmented reality
- data visualization
- cultural dimensions of interface design
- intellectual property
- usability testing

To create an effective interactive program or website, you must know how to analyze your audience and their tasks. You must also understand how cultural differences impact interface design. Then you need to know how to design a program structure and user interface that meets the needs of your audience. The course readings and the class discussions are designed to give you a foundation for making critical evaluations and creative decisions for your academic and professional design projects.

You must also be aware of the complicated legal issues that are continually evolving with electronic communication. In this course, we will discuss intellectual property law and how it applies to interactive, multimedia design.

In addition to the theoretical discussions, you will receive hands-on experience by designing programs. For prototype programs, you are free to select the software you would like to use for the project.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Assignments and required class workshops/exercises described below. Details for each assignment will be covered in class lectures and handouts that will be distributed in class. The Analysis of Interactive Programs assignment is an individual project. All other projects are team projects. **All papers should be submitted as email. Due dates are listed in the class schedule.**

**Analysis of Interactive Programs** **(Individual Project) 15% of final grade**

You will analyze the strengths and weaknesses of two websites and/or interactive programs, **one of which should be an interactive data visualization**. You should read the article “The Impact of Aesthetics on Attitudes towards Websites” (https://www.usability.gov/get-involved/blog/2009/07/aesthetics-and-attitude.html) and address the points outlined in the class handout. You should include screens from each website/program in the paper (500-750 words total for the analysis of both programs). See the assignment handout for additional requirements. **Class presentations are required. If you fail to do a presentation, your grade for this project will be dropped one letter grade.**

**Data Visualization Project (Team Project) 20% of final grade**

You will use interface design principles and data visualization research to create an interactive design to visualize data relationships and analyze feedback on the design. You will select the data to analyze, the audience and objectives for data visualization, and use data design research and theory to determine the best way to visualize the data using specific charts, data filters, and interaction to enable the user to compare different relationships. This project will involve a class presentation and a written paper. A handout with more details will be distributed later in the semester. **Class presentations are required. If you fail to do a presentation, your grade for this project will be dropped one letter grade.**

**Design and Development of Interactive Program (Team Project). There are** **three stages; each stage is 20% of final grade.**

You will have an opportunity to develop an interactive, multimedia website or stand-alone program on a topic of your choice. The project that will be divided into three stages: 1) Design Document, 2) Prototype, and 3) Usability Testing.  
Each stage of the project will be graded separately, and the grades will be weighted as follows in your final grade:

**Design Document 20% of final grade**

**Prototype 20% of final grade**

**Usability Testing 20% of final grade**

There will be class presentations for each of these three stages in the project. **Class presentations are required. If you fail to do a presentation, your grade for this project will be dropped one letter grade.**

**Progress Reviews 5% of final grade**

The progress reviews are additional class presentations on your progress with the development of the prototype and the materials for the usability testing (for the interactive website or stand-alone interactive, multimedia program on a topic of your choice). Your grade for the progress reviews will be determined as follows:

You complete four presentations: A

You complete three presentations: B

You complete two presentations: C

You complete one presentation: D

You do not complete any presentations: F

**Class Workshops and Exercises**

There will be a Social Media Workshop and an Augmented Reality Workshop in class. Each workshop will include a team exercise on social media and augmented reality interface design. There will also be a required class exercise near the end of the semester. For this exercise, each of you will try all of the prototype programs (except the one you created) and provide written comments for the teams to use, so they can make final revisions before they begin testing.

If you do not participate in these workshops/exercises in class, you must complete the exercises on your own and present the work in class. **Failure to complete either of these exercises will result in an Incomplete for the course.**

**Final Grade**

To summarize, you final grade will be determined as follows:

Analysis of Interactive Programs 15%

Data Visualization Project 20%

Design Document 20%

Prototype 20%

Usability Testing 20%

Progress Reviews 5%

In this class, I will assign letter grades for each stage of the interactive project and the progress reviews. Your final grade will be determined by translating each letter grade into a numerical equivalent based on the following scale: A=4.0, A-=3.67, B+=3.3, B=3.0, B-=2.67, C+=2.3, C=2.0, etc. Your average for the course must be a 4.0 to receive an A. Your average must be at least 3.67 for an A-, at least 2.67 for a B-, etc. Also note the following:

**When class presentations are required for a project, you must do a** **presentation or your grade for that project will be dropped one letter grade.**

**All projects must be completed in order to receive a passing grade.**

If you have any questions about your grade at any time during this course, you should see me as soon as possible.

**Late Projects**

All projects are due on the designated due date. Late projects will be lowered one letter grade for each day they are late. Saturday, Sunday, and holidays count as late days.

If you feel that a personal or academic problem is going to prevent you from meeting a project deadline, you should speak to me at least two days before the due date (not the day the project is due) to see if an extension is possible. At that time,

if I grant an extension, I will also set another due date. That deadline will be firm. **Projects that are handed in after the extended deadline will be lowered one letter grade for each day they are late.**

**Assessment of Progress**

Class discussions, grades on projects, and progress reviews will provide assessment of your progress throughout the semester. Class discussions and progress reviews will provide opportunities to get feedback on work in progress so you can improve your projects. By mid-semester, you will have your grade on the first project, Analysis of Interactive Programs, as well as feedback from class discussions on your Design Document.
