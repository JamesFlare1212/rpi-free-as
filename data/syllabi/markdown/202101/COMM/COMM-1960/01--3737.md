## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This graduate-level course is designed to provide international teaching assistants with communication training so that they are able to present academic information clearly, interact effectively with students, practice classroom management skills, accommodate different learning styles, and understand cross-cultural aspects of teaching and learning.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

1. *Required text:* Smith, J.A., Meyers, C.M., & Burkhalter, A.J. (2007). Communicate: Strategies for international teaching assistants. Waveland Press.

2. *Required program subscription:* NativeAccent

3. Supplementary readings:

   Gorsuch, G., Meyers, C.M., Pickering, L., & Griffee, D.T. (2013). English communication for international teaching assistants. 2nd ed. Waveland Press.

   Sarkisian, E. (2006). Teaching American students: A guide for international faculty and teaching assistants in colleges and universities. 3rd ed. Harvard University Press.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

No Course Learning Outcomes are available for this course.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

By the end of the course, students will:

- demonstrate knowledge and competency in pronunciation, spoken grammar, fluency, and listening skills in English
- develop presentation styles that meet the needs of different types of learners
- gauge undergraduate students’ (audience) comprehension and respond to questions effectively
- understand cultural differences and how they impact teaching and learning
- detect fossilized errors in their own speech patterns and set goals for on-going improvement

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Since this course is remote, attendance will not be taken as it would in a face-to-face course. Rather, student participation will be assessed by students’ presence in weekly course activities, as defined in the Community of Inquiry model (Garrison et al, 20001):

cognitive presence: demonstrating critical thinking in class discussions and collaborative work; going beyond rote repetition of information, welcoming new ideas and connecting new facts and ideas with existing knowledge and lived experiences  
social presence: engaging meaningfully with peers, asking complex questions, identifying with the learning community, participating equitably in group work

Participation comprises 20% of the grade in this course, which equates to one point per week, earned by students’ demonstration of presence in class and participation in other assigned activities.

Please communicate with me if your ability to participate in class is affected by illness. Grades will not be affected by a genuine inability to join synchronous sessions, but students who are quarantined are expected to continue to keep up with online work unless illness prevents it. I recognize the unpredictability of life circumstances this semester, so communication with instructors and creation of a mutually acceptable work plan is always the best course of action.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
