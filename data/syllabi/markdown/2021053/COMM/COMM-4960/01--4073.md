## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course not only aims at providing a venue to understanding contemporary Chinese social media and its western equivalents but also making the best use of class participation to stimulate student research in relevant topics. Students who do not have a background in the Chinese language will gain a basic understanding of contemporary Chinese social media and popular culture and will deepen their understanding of the uniqueness and diversity of Chinese culture and society. Students who already have some background in the Chinese language will improve their intellectual skills and ability to interact with their counterparts by gaining a more sophisticated understanding of society in English. Ultimately, this course encourages critical thinking and analytical writing, asking students to work by exploring, comparing, connecting, critiquing, and extending the ideas put forth in the course readings.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Be aware of the differences between American and Chinese culture from a comparative and cross-cultural perspective
- Understand the various methodological approaches that can be used to study social media by applying readings and class discussion to case studies and academic writing.
- Practice thinking critically by applying communication theories to make sense of the unique social media in China and in the West, through readings, class discussions, papers, and presentations
- Engage in meaningful discussion with peers about important issues in the Eastern and Western societies
- Exercise and develop analytical writing skills using primary and secondary sources on topical concerns of social media in the East and West

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic dishonesty includes cheating, fabricating or falsifying information or sources, improper collaboration, submitting the same paper for different classes without permission, and plagiarism. Plagiarism occurs when writers deliberately or unintentionally use another person’s language, idea, or materials and present them as their own without properly acknowledging and citing the source. Familiarize yourself with RPI’s Student Rights and Responsibilities.

Plagiarism is defined as the act of taking credit for someone else’s work whether deliberately or unintentionally. This includes but is not limited to turning in all or part of an essay written by someone other than yourself (a friend, an internet source, etc.) and claiming it as your own, and including information or ideas from research material without citing the course.

Plagiarism in this class is unacceptable. Accidental or willful use of someone else’s words, ideas, or work without attribution will first be warned. If not corrected, will then be penalized by a failing grade on the paper, a failing grade in the course, and/or referral to the Dean of Students.
