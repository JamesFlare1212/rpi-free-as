## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Application of control volume balances of mass, momentum, energy in systems of practical importance to all engineers. Identification of control volumes, properties of pure materials, mass and energy conservation for closed and open systems, second law of thermodynamics, Bernoulli equation, fluid statics, forces and heat transfer in external and internal flows, conduction and radiative heat transfer.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

D.A. Kaminski and M.K. Jensen, Introduction to Thermal and Fluids Engineering, Wiley (2005/2011).

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course covers fundamentals of thermodynamics, heat transfer, and fluid mechanics (*e.g.* conservation of mass, momentum, and energy) to types of problems commonly encountered by a practicing engineer.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Introduction and course overview, systems, the first law

Specific heat, unit systems, thermophysical properties

Ideal Gas Law; Power, Work, and Internal Energy

Specific Heats & Enthalpy

Polytropic processes; Heat transfer basics

Heat transfer: Thermal resistance analogy

Lumped system approximation; combined thermal resistances

Fluid statics. Forces on submerged surfaces, buoyancy

Open system mass & energy balances

Bernoulli’s equation & flow measurement

Conservation of momentum

Conservation of momentum - examples

Thermodynamics: phase transitions

Thermodynamics properties; the state principle

Nozzles, diffusers, turbines, compressors, pumps

Throttles, mixing chamber, heat exchangers

Viscous flow, laminar pipe flow

Friction factors, turbulent pipe flow

Steady flow energy equation, head loss; minor losses

Boundary layer flows; drag force

Form drag & lift force

Forced convection, natural convection

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Active Learning Exercises will be distributed daily during class time, and students are expected to complete them during class. Class attendance and participation will therefore be measured by completion of ALE. Online assignment submissions (though LMS) will be required.

### Other Course Policies:

ALE Grading Policy:

1\) No make-up ALEs will be given for any reason.

2\) Students missing an ALE with an excused absence from Student Success will be given a grade of "X", which means the ALE does not count.

3\) Each student gets to drop (1) ALE at the end of the semester, which may be a missed class due to illness or a job interview. Otherwise, you may drop your lowest ALE score.

4\) No extra time accommodations are offered on ALEs.

5\) Some ALEs may be graded like quizzes, but most will be "participation" exercises in which everyone is given a score of 100% for completing the assignment.

Quizzes: Quizzes will be given in class on Thursdays. Students will be responsible for completing a written quiz (in-person) or scanning and submitting a PDF file of hand-written work (via LMS), if a prior arrangement was made with the instructor.

No quizzes will be dropped from the course grade. Quizzes will consist of two sections: Problem Solving and Conceptual. Format and types of questions will be announced in class prior to each quiz date.

Missed quiz policy:

1\) Students who request a make-up quiz due to school-sponsored activities should present the instructor with a letter from Student Success at least five days prior to the quiz date. Late requests may not be accommodated at the instructor's discretion.

2\) Students who request a make-up examination due to unusual personal circumstances (e.g., death in the family) must present the instructor with a letter from Student Success to substantiate the reason for the absence.

3\) A make-up quiz may be granted in the case of a medical emergency; however, a written excuse from a physician is required to substantiate the reason for the absence.

4\) Students missing a quiz without a valid, substantiated reason will generally be assigned a grade of zero for the quiz.

5\) Make-up quizzes will not be the same content/format as the regular quiz.
