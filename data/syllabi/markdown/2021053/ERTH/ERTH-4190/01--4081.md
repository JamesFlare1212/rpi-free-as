## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Use a lab notebook and construct lab reports to retain and evaluate environmental observations
- Formulate stock solutions and serial dilutions for calibration and measurement of unknown samples
- Conduct fieldwork activities while adhering to leave no trace principles
- Deploy in situ sensors and multiprobes for the collection and interpretation of continuous data
- Recover, analyze, and preserve grab samples for subsequent laboratory-based analyses
- Demonstrate proficiency in various laboratory and instrumental techniques for assessing water quality
- Synthesize analytical data to understand the overall condition and ecological health of local waterways

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Student progress will be evaluated based upon the following:

ACTIVE PARTICIPATION – This course is hands-on and 100% in person. To get the most out of this course, it is critical that you attend each class in person and actively participate. Roughly half of class periods will be spent in the field, where you will get wet, sweaty, and dirty. The other half of class periods will be spent in the laboratory, where you will be handling samples, chemicals, and using instrumentation. You are expected to participate in all of these course-related activities. If you happen to miss a class, you are responsible for obtaining any material, announcements, or assignments covered during the class you missed. The active participation grade (90 total points; max 30 points per category) will comprise 25% of your final course grade.

LAB REPORTS – The length, degree of detail, and format of each lab report will vary depending upon the research activity. Examples of what may be included in a lab report include: objectives and hypotheses, graphs and data summaries, statistical analysis, interpretation of data, and describing study limitations. Some reports will be written and submitted independently, whereas others will be submitted as a group (all group participants will receive the same grade). All reports will be typed and submitted via Gradescope. The weighted average lab report grade will comprise 40% of your final course grade.

LAB NOTEBOOK – As part of this course, you will learn how to keep a lab notebook as a useful reference for yourself and others. While there is no one “correct” way to keep a lab notebook, efficient and effective notetaking is an essential part of conducting well-rounded and successful environmental research. At the end of the semester, you will be graded on your effectiveness in keeping a lab notebook. The lab notebook will be submitted to Prof. Wagner at the end of the semester. The lab notebook grade (50 total points; max 30 points for Content; max 10 points each for Organization and Readability) will comprise 5% of your final course grade.  
INDEPENDENT FIELD STUDY – Students will take what they have learned over the course of the semester to formulate and carry out an independent field study. While the study site will be the same for everyone (Vanderheyden Preserve), each student will devise or select their own research objective and hypotheses, sampling plan, and analytical methods (from the list of methods covered in this course). *This is your opportunity to be creative!* Once all data is collected, each student will summarize and interpret the results, describe their findings, draw relevant conclusions, and describe study limitations. On the last day of class, students will present their field study in the form of a short PowerPoint presentation and submit a formal written report via Gradescope. Further instructions on the formal report and presentation will be provided later in the semester. The final field study grade (40% presentation; 60% formal report) will comprise 30% of your final course grade.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
