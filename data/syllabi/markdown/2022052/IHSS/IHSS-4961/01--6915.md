### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

This course functions like a professional book review club in which students read, discuss, interrogate, and evaluate potential books for next year's campus-wide community read selection in alignment with the campus Theme of the Year. In examining contemporary texts from the humanities, arts, and social sciences, this course provides students with interdisciplinary learning around current social, political, and environmental issues. Alongside opportunities to analyze and synthesize complex issues and texts, students will practice techniques to increase their reading comprehension, demonstrate research skills in the humanities and social sciences, and practice presentation and group facilitation skills. Active participation in seminar discussions and peer-to-peer learning are essential components of the course.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students will increase their reading comprehension skills while becoming more efficient advanced-level readers.   
Students will define, practice, and enhance their critical thinking skills through interdisciplinary study of texts and their contexts in the humanities, arts, social sciences, and beyond. Students will gain leadership and instructional design skills by planning and executing an active learning experience for their peers.   
Students will practice research skills, including identifying a research question, vetting sources, and familiarizing themselves with online databases and citation practices.   
Students will improve their professional and academic writing skills by practicing summary, analysis, and evaluation in the form of professional style book reviews.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

This is a small seminar class, the success of which is entirely dependent on active student participation. Therefore, I expect all students to attend every class, barring illness, and participate to the extent of their abilities. Students unable to attend or participate due to illness or instability should reach out to me as soon as possible so we can problem-solve together. Every student can miss one class for whatever reason, no questions asked. Two further unexcused absences will deduct ten points each from the student’s final grade**. A student who exceeds three unexcused absences will automatically fail the course even if they are otherwise passing.** Excused absences (including Covid related absences) should be processed through the Office of Student Success. You must both attend *and* participate (see student-centered learning above) in order to earn your participation points for the session.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
