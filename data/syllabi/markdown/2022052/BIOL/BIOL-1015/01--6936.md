## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is an introductory course in biological systems focusing on major topics in evolution, human genetics & medicine, and ecology. It is an undergraduate level course. It is intended for non-majors and majors and covers a variety of topics that impact our daily lives and the grand challenges of this decade and your generation. Activities, such as online labs and simulations enable students to experience the fundamental biological concepts hands-on. The goal is to provide an understanding of fundamental principles, to develop analytical thinking skills in the context of modern biology, and to develop data dexterity for understanding and solving grand challenges in the life sciences.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. Principle Concepts: Students will be able to grasp and master introductory biological principles.
2. Protocols: Students will be able to connect and link core concepts, and apply them to the bases of protocols used in the corequisite laboratory course.
3. Peer Interaction: Students will be able to discuss and analyze topics in the classroom with peers.
4. Pre-requisites: Students will be able to satisfy prerequisites, in order to take advanced courses in the biological sciences.
5. Plan of Study: Students enrolled are mostly non-biology majors, and after taking this course will be able to connect biological concepts, and compare and contrast these principles, in an interdisciplinary manner, with what they are learning in their major line and plan of study.
6. Present Problems: Students will be able to connect biological concepts, and apply concepts learned in the biology classroom to presently occurring complex current issues in the outside real world.
7. Predict and Propose: Students will be able evaluate solutions to complex issues, predict fates of current plans, and propose possible alternatives or improvements to avoid any negative plan outcomes.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The laboratory course content is divided into different types of laboratory experiments:

1. In vitro Labs
2. In vivo Labs
3. In silico Labs

Each laboratory draws from fundamental principles from one, two or all of the units, at once, that taught are in the corequisite BIOL 1010 coursework, such as those listed below, but also additional research work from the real world:

1. Ecology
2. Evolution
3. Genetics and Medicine

Thus, each experiment encourages students to find connections between disparate biological concepts and fundamental principles in the textbook, classroom and the real world.
