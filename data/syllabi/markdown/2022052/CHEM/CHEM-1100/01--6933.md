## Course Goals

### Goals:

Principles of chemistry, with particular focus on atomic and molecular structure and bonding, periodicity, basic thermodynamic principles, introduction to acid-base chemistry and elementary chemical equilibrium, and introduction to organic chemistry.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

\- Quantum Mechanical Model of an Atom  
\- Periodicity and Trends of the Periodic Table; Ionic Bonding  
\- Chemical Bonding I: Lewis Model and VSEPR Theory  
\- Chemical Bonding II: Valence Bond Theory, Molecular Orbital Theory, and Band Theory  
\- Organic Chemistry with Polymers and Biomolecules  
\- Aqueous Solution Reactions  
\- Gas Laws  
\- Chemical Equilibrium and Acid/Base Equilibria  
\- Thermochemistry

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate a proficient knowledge in the core areas of chemistry (i.e., analytical, inorganic, organic, and physical) in order to prepare themselves for advanced courses in the various subject areas of science and engineering.
- Solve quantitative problems in chemistry and chemical systems using fundamental concepts of chemistry and mathematics.
- Effectively communicate ideas and inquiries related to topics in chemistry to professional chemists in academia and in the industry.
- Demonstrate safe and proper chemistry laboratory techniques relating to mass measurements, volume measurements, solution preparation, dilution, titration, elementary separation, and synthesis methods.
- Utilize resources to identify various properties of chemicals and use proper precaution in handling these chemicals during experimentation.
- Collect, analyze experimental data, and determine experimental errors using various scientific methods.
- Identify and use scientific literature and other reputable sources as references for experimentation and research.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Class Participation**:

Class participation is an essential part of this course and is measured primarily by attendance. The participation portion of your grade will be assessed by your class attendance, participation in discussions and laboratories, participation in class room i>clicker questions, and mentoring session attendance. Classes with be stream “live” online via the Webex platform. They will be recorded for your review at a later time. The links to the recorded lectures will be posted on the LMS. If you are unable to attend some lectures “Live”, you may review the recording later on the same day and submit the answers and the reasoning for each correct answers and email them to Dr. Ma by noon for following day.

(Attendance is Mandatory) There is a 125-minute lecture delivered by your course professor on Mondays, Tuesdays, Thursdays, and Fridays. The material presented in the lecture will cover and expand on the material in the required reading assignments. The pace of the lectures will be very fast. Please read the relevant chapter(s) prior to each lecture. Student must makeup absences by watching the recorded lecture videos and submit the clicker question solutions to Dr. Ma by 12:00pm EDT the next day in order to receive credits for participation.

**Discussion/Recitation and Quizzes:**

During these sessions, your discussion faculty member will go through several problems related to the current lecture topics. At the beginning of each discussion session, you will have a short quiz based on lecture material, homework, and/or laboratory material. Make sure logon to Learning Catalytics® with your laptop or a smart device to answer the quiz questions. Discussions will last approximately one hours and will be held life during the first hour of each lab period. They will be recorded so you can review them later. If you missed the quiz, you must contact the discussion faculty member to schedule a makeup quiz.

**Laboratory:**

Chemistry is an experimental science. Labs will be performed in-person in Walker Laboratory. All pre-labs are handed in prior to each lab in-person; however, simulations and post-labs are submitted via Hayden-McNeil (Achieve Labs). The lab simulations will focus primarily on applying your lecture gained knowledge to a hands-on but virtual laboratory experience. All post labs MUST be submitted via the Achieve online course. After completing the experiment, you will analyze the data, perform any necessary calculations, and answer all post-lab questions. Experimental data is not to be shared – the data you record for your experiment is the data that you will be using for your laboratory report. You will need a lab notebook to record the data and write down any observations, and perform your calculations. All excused laboratory absences must be cleared and rescheduled through Dr. Tysoe.

Three-Strike Policy for UNEXCUSED Absences:

1. One absence with no makeup will result in a grade of “zero” for the corresponding lab session.
2. Two absences with no makeup will result in a grade of “zero” for the entire lab portion of the final grade (25%).
3. Three absences with no makeup will result in an “F” for the entire course.

You are expected to collaborate with your lab partner(s) to perform the experiment and collect the data. However, each lab partner is expected to analyze the results and work on the postlab questions independently. Make sure your postlab assignments are in your own words. Anyone who is caught copying from someone else’s work or work from past years will be subjected to academic disciplinary actions as follows:

Four-Strike Policy for PLAGARISM:

1. The first infraction will result in a verbal and a written warning.
2. The second infraction will result in a grade of “zero” for the corresponding prelab and/or postlab with a verbal and a written warning
3. The third infraction will result in a grade of “zero” for the entire lab portion of the final grade (25%). A second written warning will also be issued.
4. The fourth infraction will result in an “F” for the entire course.

**Course Material Exams**:

There will be three 60-minute exams given during the term. The syllabus provides an outline of the materials that will be covered for each exam. Exams may include material covered in lecture, discussion, reading, homework, or laboratory. All exams will be multiple-choice and/or a combination of MCs, short answers, and open response. The exams will be given “Live” and in-person at 10:30am EDT. If a student misses an exam for an unavoidable and compelling reason, the student may be excused from the exam at the instructors’ discretion provided that the student presents a reasonable excuse. A written excuse or email is required from the Student Experience Office, a doctor, or a coach. Students who are excused from exams will have to schedule for a makeup exam. The three hourly exams are scheduled on:

- Exam 1: 6/3 (Fri)

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:**

The relationship between students and faculty is based upon trust and the continued maintenance of this trust is necessary for education to be successful. Students need to trust faculty to make appropriate judgments about the content and structure of the course. Faculty members need to trust that the work turned in by students represents their own effort. Violation of this trust undermines the educational process. For example, as professionals, you may be responsible in designing products (aircraft, the computers that run them, or the runways on which they land) where lives are at stake. As a result, there is no tolerance for any breach of academic integrity such as cheating, plagiarizing, or inappropriate sharing of laboratories or quizzes. (e.g., Using someone else’s REEF code is considered cheating.)

Cheating can include sharing answers, as well as stealing answers and looking up answers online during exams. Plagiarism means copying words from someone’s work, even if you “change the sentence a bit.” If you share your laboratory report, you are as guilty as the person copying it. This includes copying results and answers from previous years’ lab reports. If you do use material from an appropriate source, make sure you reference it properly in your reports. The Rensselaer Student Handbook and the Dean of Students Office Webpage (http://doso.rpi.edu/update.do?artcenterkey=676) defines various forms of academic dishonesty. Please familiarize yourself with these and ask us if you have questions about what constitutes academic dishonesty.
