## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is an introductory course in biological systems focusing on major topics in evolution, human genetics & medicine, and ecology. It is an undergraduate level course. It is intended for non-majors and majors and covers a variety of topics that impact our daily lives and the grand challenges of this century. Activities, such as student presentations and written responses to lecture questions enable students to interact, and discuss these topics. The goal is to provide an understanding of the scientific method, and its’ fundamental principles, to develop analytical thinking skills in the context of modern biology, and to develop data dexterity for understanding and solving grand challenges in the life sciences. This is a D1 Data Intensive Course.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate mastery of the foundational knowledge and core concepts of evolution, genetics & medicine, and ecology by answering multiple choice questions in graded Simulations Modules and Online Lab Tests.
- Apply foundational knowledge and core concepts in these areas to solve novel problems, analyze realistic scenarios, and make predictions. Evaluation by answering multiple choice questions in graded Simulations Modules and Online Lab Tests.
- Apply quantitative reasoning and methods to the analysis and solution of biological problems. Evaluation by answering multiple choice questions in graded Simulations Modules and Online Lab Tests.
- Utilize information and data from a wide range of sources to construct knowledge and communicate that knowledge effectively. Specifically, given a current topic in biology, gather information from research articles, texts, the web, and other relevant sources. Analyze, organize, distill, and synthesize this information and present as a Final paper or Final Project.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

1. Learning is interactive, and we encourage this. However, when you are writing anything for submission, answers to questions on Lecture Tests, you must write your answer in your own words. You must not copy from the websites that you used for research.
2. You may not copy verbatim from any source or peer submission. Plagiarism will be assessed 0 Points. The penalty for plagiarism or cheating or not following the Honor Code is as follows: A grade of zero will be given on the first assignment where a violation is detected. If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of F for the course.
3. **NOTE**: If you have any doubts or concerns of whether an action constitutes academic dishonesty, please discuss it with any of your instructors or TAs.

Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
