## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is an introductory course in biological systems focusing on major topics in evolution, human genetics & medicine, and ecology. It is a course that is intended for non-majors and majors and covers a variety of topics that impact our daily lives and the grand challenges of this decade and your generation. The goal is to provide an understanding of fundamental principles, to develop analytical thinking skills in the context of modern biology, and to develop data dexterity for understanding and solving grand challenges in the life sciences.  
*Note that this is an approved DI-1 course.*

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

***Exams***

There will be four exams: Unit 1 (evolution), Unit 2 (genetics and human medicine), Unit 3 (ecology), and the Final Exam. All exams are equally weighted. Your lowest exam score will be dropped; thus, the Final Exam is optional. Exams will consist of a combination of true/false, multiple choice, matching, short- and long essay questions. You will have one hour to complete your exam.

***Quizzes***

There will be three quizzes, one for each unit: Unit 1, Unit 2, and Unit 3 and will consist of exam like questions. There will be lecture/activities in classes after quizzes is administered. Quizzes will occur during the first 30-40 minutes of class.

***Attendance and participation***

Because this is an interactive class, attendance and participation are expected for all scheduled meetings. Attendance will be recorded, and you will be expected to read, watch videos and be able to reflect upon and discuss course content. Given our current circumstances (COVID et al.), I expect that unpredictable circumstances may arise, and as such, “perfect” attendance cannot be expected. To reconcile any unpredictable circumstances, only the 5 best attendance and participation scores per unit will be counted.

***Pre-class assignments (PCA)***

Unless there is an exam, you will be asked to complete a pre-class assignment. For these assignments, you will be asked to watch a video, read a popular article, and/or write a short paragraph (~200 words). There will be 5 pre class assignments per unit and you will be responsible to complete them even if you miss class. PCAs will be assigned at the end of “most” lectures.

***“Biology in the news” assignments***

For all three units, you will be tasked with finding a non-peer reviewed, popular article that relates to that specific unit (evolution, genetics and human medicine, and ecology). You will use the online, RPI library resource to find a single peer reviewed article that supports, and a second that conflicts with the author’s view. You will discuss how the author’s view is in concert, and or conflict with peer reviewed literature in the biological sciences and will properly cite “in-text” and in a bibliography using APA format.

***Team Based, Group Presentation***

Presentation topics will summarize an in-depth investigation and analysis of a current issue in the life sciences. Early in the semester, you will be assigned to a team of (4-5) students, but if you have a someone whom you would like to work with, please email Dr. Hunt so accommodations can be made. Presentations will be approximately 12 minutes, with 2-3 minutes after your talk for questions. To be accountable to me, and your group mates, the presentation will be scaffolded with checkpoints as the semester progresses.

***Group presentation checkpoints***

Group presentation checkpoints will encourage communication within your group and with your instructor. This will provide opportunities to exchange ideas with your group, myself and to receive feedback as the semester progresses. There will be four total group presentation checkpoints, 2 individual and 2 group based.

*Checkpoint summary:*

- **Individual:** each group member will state the topic of their presentation (group portion), they will then provide three, peer reviewed references supporting their presentation topic/goals (individual portion). You will now have 12-15 possible resources!
- **Group:** each group will submit a general outline for their presentation, including each group members role.
- **Group:** each group will submit a draft of their power point presentation and infographic.
- **Individual:** each individual group member will submit an evaluation of their teammates and reflect upon their experience during this group project.

***Presentation infographic***

Each team will use an infographic to communicate the broader implications of their group presentation. An infographic is a graphical representation of a concept, pattern, or other form of information. For this assignment, your group can present an infographic as a graphical abstract, public service announcement (PSA) or analogous visual tool.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Relationships within the academic community are built on trust. For example, students must trust that their instructors have made the appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and the faculty must trust that the assignments that students submit are their own. Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty, and as a Rensselaer student, it is your responsibility to read and understand these. In addition to what is stated in the Handbook, we provide the following guidelines for BIOL-1010:

- Class Activities: We encourage students to discuss all activities in pre- and in-class sessions with fellow students. Learning is interactive, and we encourage this. However, when you are writing pre-class or in-class assignments, you must write your answer in your own words.
- Exams & Quizzes: You may not give or receive assistance by any means during these. This is all about you!
- Class Presentation: This is a team project; therefore, you must work as a team. The expectation is that each person will contribute equally. Further information will be given during the first part of the semester.

NOTE: Because of the ability to use the internet as a research tool, the greatest academic integrity problems arise because of copying and pasting electronic words without thinking. It is critical that you learn early to use these tools and then to put the ideas in your words and provide a reference of the source of the information. In addition, while Wikipedia is a wonderful tool, we do expect you to read the literature we assign and get beyond “fast facts”, some of which are incorrect.

The second most common academic integrity issue at Rensselaer is taking advantage of previous students’ papers, essays, problem sets, etc. and submitting them as your own. While some may view this as a game and part of the competitive process, your faculty and the Institute consider this as another academic integrity violation. Think carefully about your moral compass and the person you want to be. It is your choice.

If you have any doubts or concerns of whether an action is academic dishonesty, please feel free to discuss it with your instructor or TA.
