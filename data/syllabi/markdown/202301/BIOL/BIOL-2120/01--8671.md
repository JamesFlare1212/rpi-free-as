## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is an introductory course in cell structure and function. It is intended for Biology and Biomedical Engineering majors and covers such topics as chemical composition of cells, molecular structure function of organelles, membrane transport, and the biochemistry underlying cellular behavior including cancer cells.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand and properly use scientific vocabulary common for cell and molecular biology.
- Demonstrate understanding of prokaryotic and eukaryotic cell structure and function.
- Describe the structure and function of the biomolecules found in all living organisms.
- Describe the synthesis of DNA, RNA, and proteins.
- Explain the processes involved with gene expression.
- Explain the processes by which animals acquire nutrients and eliminate wastes.
- Explain the structure and function of membranes and intracellular compartments.
- Link modern advances in cell and molecular biology to a better understanding of diseases.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

4 tests, 1 cumulative final exam, and homework are used to assess students. Reading the textbook, coming to lecture and asking questions, and doing the homework gives students the best learning experience.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

- ­ All assessments for this course are done electronically, either through LMS for exams and extra credit discussion or through SmartWork for homework assignments. The nature of online assessments can temp some students to cheat, abusing the trust placed in students by Dr. Klein and your TA’s. Cheating is considered to include, but is not limited to, the following:
  - » Collaborating with other students on exam and homework questions
  - » Passing exam answers or information about exam questions to or from other students
  - » Making transcripts or copies of exams
  - » Submitting answers to free-response questions in words other than your own (i.e., plagiarism)
  - » Accessing any materials outside of the exam for help with exam questions; this includes your textbook, lecture slides, websites, and AI like ChatGPT
  - » Defeating or attempting to defeat timed exam lockouts on LMS to gain extra time
  - » Attempting to take an in-person exam in any location other than the proctored exam space
- ­ If a student is found to be cheating, Dr. Klein reserves the right to issue a score of **0 for the assessment** and an **F for your course grade**. These policies also apply to students who assist others in cheating. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assessment, please ask for clarification.
- ­ Any act of academic dishonesty will be **reported to the Dean of Students**. Institutional consequences for academic dishonesty are spelled out in the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities (https://rpi.app.box.com/s/3f1cl3w1lt1mq8gqla68v6uk3vh1c19f). This handbook also defines various forms of academic dishonesty; you should make yourself familiar with these.
- ­ In the unlikely event an exam must be held remotely, the exam will differ significantly from an in-person exam is considered an “open-book” assessment where your textbook and lecture slides can be used to help answer questions. However, this does not mean you can copy answers from your textbook or anywhere else or get help from anyone else (including AI like ChatGPT); all answers must be **in your own words**.
