## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Description:** Techniques and applications of integration, polar coordinates, parametric equations, infinite sequences and series, vector functions and curves in space, functions of several variables, and partial derivatives. Prerequisite: MATH 1010 or equivalent. (from the [Rensselaer Catalog](http://catalog.rpi.edu/))

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Basic symbol manipulation skills pertaining to integration.
- Ability to state and explain basic Calculus definitions and theorems and their applications.
- Ability to describe curves parametrically and apply Calculus concepts and techniques to curves described in this way.
- Ability to express curves via polar equations and to solve Calculus problems in the polar coordinate system.
- Ability to apply Calculus to selected problems in Science, Engineering and Mathematics.
- Ability to use convergence tests to analyze the behavior of infinite series.
- Ability to represent functions using power series and solve problems using these representations.
- Ability to describe lines and planes in space in equation form and solve problems using these representations.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance:** Students are responsible for all material covered in lectures whether they are present or not. Attendance is expected, but will not be checked.

### Other Course Policies:

**Exams:** The material covered on each exam will be announced on [LMS](https://lms.rpi.edu/) when the date of the exam is near. Exams will be given in person during the test block on Wednesdays at 10–11:50 AM in SAGE 3303. **Students who receive testing accommodations from the** [**Disability Student Services (DSS)**](https://studenthealth.rpi.edu/list-services/disability-student-services) **office will also take their exams on Wednesday, but most likely at a different time.**

**Make-up exams will only be given to students with an** [**excused absence documented by the Student Success office**](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/current-students/academic-and-personal-support/requesting-excused-absence) **or, if the absence is due to an athletic competition, documentation from a coach.** Thus, if a student misses an exam because they overslept, did not know where the exam was, or did not know there was an exam, to give a few examples, the student may not take the make-up exam and will receive a score of 0 on the exam.

**Missing an exam should not be taken lightly.** Thus, students who miss an exam due to an excused absence must make every effort to notify the instructor **before the exam begins** to avoid grade penalties.

- If a student misses an exam due to an excused absence and notifies the instructor before the exam begins, then the student may take a make-up exam without penalty.
- If a student misses an exam due to an excused absence and notifies the instructor after the exam begins but before 11:59 PM on the Thursday after the exam, then the student may take a make-up exam and a 30% penalty will be applied to the make-up exam score.
- If a student misses an exam due to an excused absence and fails to notify the instructor before 11:59 PM on the Thursday after the exam, then the student may not take the make-up exam and receives a score of 0 on the exam.
- If a student misses an exam and does not have an excused absence, then the student may not take the make-up exam and receives a score of 0 on the exam.

It is the sole responsibility of the student to notify the instructor to make arrangements for a make-up exam when an exam is missed due to an excused absence.

**Final exam:** The final exam will be cumulative and will be held on a date and time determined by the [Office of the Registrar](https://info.rpi.edu/registrar/grades/#FinalExamScheduleandConflicts). **All students must take the final exam at the time scheduled by the Office of the Registrar. Exceptions will only be made if there is a scheduling conflict as defined** [**here**](https://info.rpi.edu/registrar/grades/#FinalExamScheduleandConflicts)**, or if a student is excused by the Student Success office.** Please note that the Student Success office will only issue an excuse for final exams for extreme circumstances, such as hospitalization, and not for travel. Thus, students should not make end-of-semester travel arrangements until the final exam schedule is posted, or should only schedule travel for the time period after the last day of final exams, which is Friday, May 5.

**Homework:** Achieve homework assignments will be due once or twice weekly on Mondays and Thursdays at 11:59 PM and will be graded automatically by the system. Students may submit these assignments late up to two days after the original deadline, but a penalty will be applied to the score. A 10% penalty will apply to assignments submitted the day after the deadline and a 20% penalty will apply to assignments submitted two days after the deadline. **No other due date extensions will be granted for any reason.** To further compensate for this, several homework assignment scores will be dropped at the end of the semester.

Additional problems will be assigned from the textbook and will be posted on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/). Though these problems will not be collected nor graded, they should not be viewed as optional. Success in the course depends on successful completion of all homework, both Achieve homework and textbook homework. Exam and quiz problems are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples. Full solutions to odd-numbered textbook problems can be found on Achieve. When logged in to our Achieve course, view content by assignments and scroll to the bottom of the page. The student solutions manual for our textbook may be found below No Due Dates. Tip: download and save these files for use in future courses!

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:** Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://rpi.app.box.com/s/d4ndpn3f7qdwxh9jaz5n1hkkr0mnd25e) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams must be the student’s own. In addition, no calculators, computers, tablets, open books, open notes, phones, headphones, or smartwatches may be used during exams. Talking or communicating with other students in any way during exams is also prohibited. Students may discuss homework with other students, tutors, the TAs, or instructor, but the work submitted must be the student’s own.

The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.  
**Disability Student Services (DSS):** Students with a memorandum from the [Disability Student Services (DSS)](http://studenthealth.rpi.edu/disabilityservices) office identifying accommodations for exams must present the memorandum to the instructor **by Friday, January 20**. Providing the accommodations requires advance planning. Students will not be granted accommodations unless a memorandum from DSS has been received.
