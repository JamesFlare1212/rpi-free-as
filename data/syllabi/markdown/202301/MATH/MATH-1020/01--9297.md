## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Description:** Techniques and applications of integration, polar coordinates, parametric equations, infinite sequences and series, vector functions and curves in space, functions of several variables, and partial derivatives. Prerequisite: MATH 1010 (from the [Rensselaer Catalog](http://catalog.rpi.edu/))

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Student Learning Outcomes:** Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate

- basic and advanced symbol manipulation skills,
- the ability to convert between calculus concepts and their graphical, numerical, and symbolic representations,
- the ability to apply calculus to selected problems in science and engineering, and
- the ability to apply certain fundamental theorems, basic concepts, and rules from calculus to solve symbolic and graphical problems.
- the ability to present problems related to the theory of calculus and its applications to science and engineering disciplines.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Basic symbol manipulation skills pertaining to integration.
- Ability to state and explain basic Calculus definitions and theorems and their applications.
- Ability to describe curves parametrically and apply Calculus concepts and techniques to curves described in this way.
- Ability to express curves via polar equations and to solve Calculus problems in the polar coordinate system.
- Ability to apply Calculus to selected problems in Science, Engineering and Mathematics.
- Ability to use convergence tests to analyze the behavior of infinite series.
- Ability to represent functions using power series and solve problems using these representations.
- Ability to describe lines and planes in space in equation form and solve problems using these representations.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Exams:** Exams will be given during during the Wednesday test block beginning at 12 PM. Attendance will be taken at all exams using sign in sheets. Details about each exam, including the material to be covered on the exam and time limit, will be announced on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/) when the date of the exam is near. The dates for Exams 1–3 are listed above in the section **Grading.** Details on exams for DSS students are given below in the **DSS** section.

**Make-up exams will only be given to students with an** [**excused absence documented by the Student Success office**](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/current-students/academic-and-personal-support/requesting-excused-absence) **or, if the absence is due to an athletic competition, documentation from a coach must be provided.** Thus, if a student misses an exam because they overslept, didn’t know where the exam was, or didn’t know there was an exam, to give a few examples, the student may not take the make-up exam and will receive a score of 0 on the exam.

**Missing an exam should not be taken lightly.** Thus, students who miss an exam due to an excused absence must make every effort to notify the instructor ***before the exam begins*** to avoid grade penalties.

- If a student misses an exam due to an excused absence and notifies the instructor before the exam begins, then the student may take a make-up exam without penalty.
- If a student misses an exam due to an excused absence and notifies the instructor after the exam begins but within 48 hours of the exam, then the student may take a make-up exam and a 30% penalty will be applied to the make-up exam score.
- If a student misses an exam due to an excused absence and fails to notify the instructor within 48 hours of the exam, then the student may not take a make-up exam and receives a score of 0 on the exam.
- If a student misses an exam and does not have an excused absence, then the student may not take the make-up exam and receives a score of 0 on the exam.
- Note that the Health Center **does not** give excused absences with a few exceptions like COVID. Emails sent between 12 AM - 12 PM on exam days will not be read or responded to thus if you are feeling sick, you will need to speak to the instructor **in person**, in the exam room prior to the exam to determine if you will be allowed to take a make-up exam.

It is the sole responsibility of the student to notify the instructor to make arrangements for a make-up exam when an exam is missed due to an excused absence.

**Final Exam:** The Final Exam will be cumulative and will be administered during the finals period May 1 - May 5. The final exam will include True/False, Multiple Choice/Multiple Select, Fill-in and Short Answer problems. The date of the final exam is determined by the scheduler and all students **must** take their exam at the assigned time for MATH 1020 Sections 1 - 8. ***Do not make arrangements for travel at the end of the semester until you receive the Final Exam Schedule from the Registrar’s Office.***

**Homework:** Achieve homework assignments will be due once or twice weekly on Tuesdays and Fridays at 11:59 PM and will be graded automatically by the system. Students may submit these assignments late up to two days after the original deadline, but a penalty will be applied to the score. A 10% penalty will apply to assignments submitted the day after the deadline and a 20% penalty will apply to assignments submitted two days after the deadline. No other due date extensions will be granted for any reason. To further compensate for this, several homework assignment scores will be dropped at the end of the semester.

Additional problems will be assigned from the textbook and will be posted on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/) in the **Homework: Achieve & Textbook** content area found in the left toolbar. Though these problems will not be collected nor graded, they should not be viewed as optional. Success in the course depends on successful completion of all homework, both Achieve homework and textbook homework. Exam problems are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples. Full solutions to odd-numbered textbook problems can be found on Achieve. When logged in to our Achieve course, view content by assignments and scroll to the bottom of the page. The student solutions manual for chapters 5-7 and 10-14 may be found below No Due Dates.

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:** Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://rpi.app.box.com/s/d4ndpn3f7qdwxh9jaz5n1hkkr0mnd25e) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams must be the student’s own. In addition, no calculators, computers, tablets, open books, open notes, phones, headphones, or smartwatches may be used during exams or quizzes. Talking or communicating with other students in any way during exams is also prohibited. Students may discuss homework with other students, mentors, TAs, and instructors, but the work submitted must be the student’s own.

The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
