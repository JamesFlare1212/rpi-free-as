## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course description:** The course provides an opportunity for the development of theorem-proving skills in the field of mathematical analysis. Expansion of a knowledge base comes as a by-product of energy expended in theorem proving and subsequent exposition. Analysis topics included are: sets, functions, the real numbers, cardinality, induction, decimal representations of real numbers, Euclidean spaces, abstract vector spaces, and metric spaces. This is a communication-intensive course. (from the [Rensselaer Catalog](http://catalog.rpi.edu/))

### Other Course Policies:

**Exams:** The material covered on Exams will be announced on LMS when the date of the exam is near. Exams will be given during lecture and the quiz/exam block in person when the Institute allows it. Exams will include some combination of definitions, true/false, multiple choice, short answer problems and proofs. Exams will be closed book and closed notes and will be timed. Exams will be given on two days. Part 1 of each exam will be given in lecture on Tuesday and will consist of proofs while Part 2 of each exam will be given on Wednesday and will consist of definitions, multiple choice/multiple select, true/false and short answer questions.

Make-up exams will only be given to students with an [excused absence documented by the Student Success office](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/student-success/excused-absence). **Missing an exam should not be taken lightly.** Thus, students missing an exam due to an excused absence should notify the instructor to make arrangements for a make-up exam ***before the exam begins***. If a student with an excused absence fails to inform the instructor within 24 hours of the exam, it is at the discretion of the instructor to allow the student to take a make-up exam with possibility of a penalty applied. If a student misses an exam due to an unexcused absence (e.g., did not know there was an exam, overslept, etc.), then the student must inform the instructor within 24 hours of the exam. The student may elect to take a make-up exam at a time arranged by the instructor and a 30% penalty will be applied to the score.

**Homework:** There will be 11 homework assignments due on Friday nights. Each homework consist of 2-3 problems that must be done in LaTeX (see below). Homework assignments will be submitted on Gradescope as a compiled pdf file and for some submissions the LaTeX code will be submitted on LMS.

The rules for collaborating on homework problems with others are as follows:

Before working with anyone, each student must read the **entire** homework problem set and (minimally) do the following

- Look and memorize all relevant definitions and Theorems.
- Make an outline of what needs to be shown.
- Make and attempt at writing the proof.

Once students have satisfied the above criteria, students may seek help and collaborate with others (inside and outside of office hours) on figuring out how to write, refine and improve proofs using the following guidelines:

- Students may get help on problems from an instructor, TA or another student currently taking the course.
- Students may not get help on problems from anyone outside of the class without explicit permission from the instructor.
- Students are responsible to independently write their submission from their own notes taken during any above interactions.
- Students must include a list of anyone with whom they collaborated (outside of office hours) at the end of each homework assignment.
- LaTeX code and LaTeX files **may not** be copied or shared with other students.
- Submissions with glaring similarities will violate academic integrity policies. The penalties for violating academic integrity policies are shown in a subsequent section on this syllabus.
- Students are responsible for protecting their work and preventing it from being copied by others. In the event of an academic integrity policy violation, all students involved will be held accountable and penalized.
- In order to receive full credit on homework assignments, proofs must be constructed based on the definitions, properties and theorems presented in the course (book and lecture).

Homework is due at the times posted on LMS. Late homework will receive a 10% deduction of total points for each day, or part thereof, late and will not be accepted after 4 days late. For homework handed in late due to an **excused absence,** an adjustment to the late penalty will be made *at the discretion of the instructor*. This is generally reserved for extended illness and when notification of the absence was done in a timely manner.

At the end of the semester, a student’s homework grade will be calculated out a smaller number than the total number of homework points available. For example, if there are 375 homework points at the end of the semester, the homework grade might be calculated out of 350 points with a cap of 350 so that the max homework grade is 100%.

**Presentations:** There will be two problem presentation assignments made during the semester. Presentations will be due during week 8 and week 15 of the semester. Details regarding problem presentation assignments will be posted on LMS when the presentation dates draw nearer.

**Lecture Worksheets:** Worksheets will be distributed at the beginning of lecture with an online version available on Gradescope. These worksheets consist of definitions, short problems and proofs, which will be discussed and filled out during lecture. A student’s credit for lecture worksheet discussions will be a combination of participation/attendance in lecture and uploads of (partially)completed worksheets to Gradescope. No make-up worksheets will be allowed. To compensate for this, several worksheet discussion scores for each student will be dropped at the end of the semester.

**Quizzes:** There are 2 types of quizzes given in this course — Online Lecture Quizzes and in-person Quizzes. All Online Lecture Quizzes will be administered on LMS as short, 5 minutes quizzes that will be due **before** the start of lecture with the exception of Lecture 1. All online lecture quizzes will be located on LMS in the Quiz Information content area found in the left sidebar. Online lecture quizzes will be incorporated into the Quiz portion of the course grade.

In addition to online lecture quizzes, in-person Quizzes will be given during the Wednesday block of the course. A tentative in-person quiz schedule is given below. In-person quizzes will be 20 minutes in length and start at the beginning of the quiz block on Wednesdays when scheduled. They will typically consist of 3-4 questions including definitions, multiple choice/multiple select, short answer and one proof. No make-up in-person quizzes will be given for any reason. Students who miss a quiz due to an [excused absence documented by the Student Success office](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/student-success/excused-absence) will be excused from a quiz. Additionally, if a student has zero (0) or one(1) excused absence from in-person quizzes, then one (1) in-person quiz will be dropped when calculating the overall quiz grade at the end of the semester. In-person quizzes will carry more weight than online lecture quizzes in the Quiz portion of the course grade. The weighting will be 75% in-person quizzes (9% of overall course grade) and 25% online quizzes (3% of overall course grade).

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic integrity:** Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://rpi.app.box.com/s/3f1cl3w1lt1mq8gqla68v6uk3vh1c19f) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams and quizzes must be the student’s own. A student may not change an answer on an exam or quiz before submitting it for an appeal. Additionally you will be asked to “sign” an honor code that indicates your work is your own and you have not given or received any help on exams and quizzes. The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

**Health and Safety Protocols:** Students are required to follow the health and safety protocols of RPI, which are subject to change throughout the semester.

**Visitors:** Visitors are prohibited during all class meetings. Only students who are registered for the class should be present in the classroom. Students may only attend class meetings at the time and place listed on their schedule on SIS.

**Classroom Decorum:** Students must be on time for class and should refrain from leaving and reentering the classroom during class. If a student has a legitimate reason for arriving late to class or being excused early from class, then they should discuss this with the instructor before class. Phones may not be used during class and should be silent. Computers and tablets may not be used for anything other than taking notes. Students who are unable to courteously comply with these policies may be asked to leave the room.

**WebEx Decorum:** Please adhere to the following during Webex meetings, including office hours, for this course.

- Join Webex meetings promptly at scheduled times for individual appointments.
- Be courteous when joining Webex meetings that are drop-in office hours.
- Turn your camera on, if at all possible. In case of connectivity issues, upload a photo in your Webex profile, which will be displayed when your camera is off.
- Use good microphone etiquette when attending a Webex meeting with several students in attendance. Mute your microphone unless you are speaking. If you are having microphone issues, you may participate by using the chat feature.
- Whenever possible, set yourself up to attend meetings in a space where you are not distracted and your surroundings are not distracting to others in the meeting.
