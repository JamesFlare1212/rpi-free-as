## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

In this course students are encouraged to design projects for themselves, keeping in mind these questions:

- How do you intend your design to operate personally and beyond?
- How will you engage with stakeholders and collaborators in order to realize your project?
- Which tools and strategies will you employ to maximize a positive impact?
- How will you manage project timelines?

This course introduces students to concept design, development and realization. Prior iterations of this class have focused on entrepreneurship, organization planning, project management, branding and marketing—all in service of advancing skills as designers and innovators. We will touch on all of these areas however; the core theme of this course is collaboration. As with other DIS studios, assignments require students to synthesize social, technical, and formal concerns in the design of innovative objects, environments, and systems.

As a studio, class time is used for self-guided design initiatives as well as structured group activities. Students are expected to participate in individual and group research, including hands-on exploration of design ideas; interviews, surveys; project reviews; and informal and formal presentations. The course also requires readings TBA.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Class project**:

Students will concentrate on semester long projects. They may choose sub-sets that inform the larger project. Design proposals will encompass insights inspired by the resources explored, experiences beyond the classroom, personal passions, societal needs and skills that will serve ones career.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Efficacy using engineering design methods and formal design processes, as well as critical deconstruction in ways that are useful to the professional practices as a designer. This course focuses on introducing students to a more structured design process grounded in engineering practice, including specific design methods found in the field. These methods are used to support problem formulation, concept generation, evaluation, iteration, and execution. The goal is to encourage critical reflection of these methods in order to develop diverse approaches to design. The objective also includes a focus on complex social systems and the capacity to analyze systems, identify and define systemic problems, and develop meaningful solutions.
- Developing skills in prototyping, fabrication, evaluation, and design aesthetics. Drawing from and enhancing student’s technical skills and abilities.
- Demonstration of effective group work and other professional skill sets. We will be conducting our class as a professional design studio, and expect professional conduct throughout.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Students’ grades will be determined by: Class participation, concept generation, prototypes, final product, oral presentations and design reports. Late work will not be accepted without legitimate excuses. Two sessions may be missed without penalty; any missed classes beyond this will impact grades.

General grading weights:

- Participation 20%
- Project Proposal 20%
- Prototypes 20%
- Presentations 20%
- Final Design Report 20%

## Course Calendar

### Calendar Details:

**General Schedule**

Each stage of design development and execution is broken into approximately 5 weeks each.

- Problem Identification and Concept Generation
- Mockups/ Rapid Prototypes
- Final Fabrication and Presentation

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
