## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Classical electromagnetic theory, together with classical and quantum mechanics, forms the core of present day theoretical training for physicists. A thorough grounding in these subjects is a requirement for more advanced or specialized training.  
This class is generally designed as a two-semester course in electromagnetic theory given to beginning graduate students. We will do it in one semester. Student taking this class are expected to have a solid grounding in electricity and magnetism. At minimum, students are expected to have had a semester of E&M beyond elementary physics. Students are expected to be familiar with the fundamental laws, laboratory verification, and elaboration of their consequences, circuit analysis, simple wave phenomena, and radiation. The mathematical tools utilized include vector calculus, ordinary differential equations with constant coefficients, Fourier series, Fourier or Laplace transforms, partial differential equations, Legendre polynomials, and Bessel functions.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

My aim in teaching this graduate course in electromagnetism is at least threefold. The first aim is to present the basic subject matter as a coherent whole, with emphasis on the unity of electric and magnetic phenomena, both in their physical basis and in the mode of mathematical description. The second, concurrent aim is to develop and utilize a number of topics in mathematical physics which are useful in both electromagnetic theory and wave mechanics. These include Green’s theorems and Green’s functions, orthonormal expansions, spherical harmonics, cylindrical and spherical Bessel functions. A third and perhaps most important purpose is the presentation of new material, especially on the interaction of relativistic charged particles with electromagnetic fields.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Short Review of some Mathematics, Nomenclature and Notation Review of Electrostatics  
Boundary-Value Problem in Electrostatics  
Multipoles, Electrostatics of Macroscopic Media, Dielectrics Magnetostatics, Faraday’s Law, Quasi-Static Fields

Maxwell Equations, and Conservation Laws  
Plane Electromagnetic Waves  
Waveguides, Resonant Cavities, and Optical Fibers Radiating Systems

Introduction to Relativity  
Dynamics of Charged Particles and Electromagnetic Fields Electromagnetic Fields of, and Radiation from, Relativistic Point-charges

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- This is a graduate level course. Students will be expected to know the background and to consult the book assigned.
- Students are expected to be present during lectures and take their own notes.
- At the conclusion of this class, we expect the students to have a more mature view of e&m, understand it more deeply, and gain a considerable technical ability in analytic methods of solution

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The homework is an essential part of the course. All students must attempt all the problems on your own, but you may argue with your colleagues about the basic ideas underlying the problems.

Student solutions should go beyond the usual “just writing down” solution, in that special care should be given to the presentation and discussion of the logic of the solution

Cumulative mastery of homework is intended to be the primary learning tool in preparation for exams. The exam will be 70% of your grade. If you have not attempted to solve the HW on your own you will struggle during the exams
