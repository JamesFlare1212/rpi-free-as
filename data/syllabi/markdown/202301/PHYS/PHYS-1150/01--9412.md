## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Introductory physics for students seeking a more intensive experience. Newton’s laws are introduced using *differential calculus*, with solutions based on *integral calculus*. Material on fluids, thermodynamics, and special relativity is included. Laboratory exercises are carried out emphasizing measurement uncertainty and clear, concise reporting.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

**text:** *University Physics with Modern Physics*, Young & Freedman, 15th Edition (Pearson) with **Modified Mastering Physics**

## Course Goals

### Goals:

After successful completion of this course, students will be able to employ fundamental physics concepts and theories to set up and formulate problems and experiments, and analyze data in mechanics, thermodynamics, and special relativity at the introductory level; students will be able to apply concepts of differential and integral calculus to solve associated problems.

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

**Graded material:**

It is your responsibility to check all returned and graded HWs, activities, labs, and exams, and to bring any discrepancies/contests to my attention. No contests will be considered after a week following the return of the material. Further, you must keep all your graded HWs and exams until the end of the semester in case there are any clerical errors in entering your grades into the spreadsheet.

**Lectures:**

There will be a number of short **follow-up problems/activities** which counts 5**%** of your overall course total, posted and the day of the lecture. It’ll be due the following day at 11:59pm (EST).

**Homework assignments:**

Most homework assignments are taken from the textbook, as indicated on the lecture schedule. Most assignment are submitted through Modified Mastering Physics. Typically, there will be one problem in each set which is also to be submitted in full “paper and pencil” comprehensive solution form as a PDF upload/submission through LMS. Homework is due on the date indicated by 11:59pm (EST). Late homework cannot be accepted without prior approval from the instructor.

**Laboratories:**

The laboratory schedule (posted on the course webpage) includes information on that day's laboratory exercise (with which you should be familiar ***before*** coming to class) as well as potentially useful links. Since labs involve data collection and analysis, you must bring your laptop to the lab activities. You are expected to take all of your data, keep notes, and begin some data analysis, during the laboratory class day for which the activity is assigned. We’ll encounter the inevitable problems dealing with laboratory equipment, so we may need to do some rescheduling on the fly. Do not expect to be “all done” with the laboratory exercise when you leave class. Your lab report should also reflect the work you’ve done on that exercise outside of the classroom. The completed lab report should be uploaded through LMS in ***by 11:59pm (EST) on the day of the following week’s lab*** (so you have a week to write up your lab report). ***You can only submit a lab report if you actually completed the lab activity (i.e., have to engage in the data collection/experiments).***

**Make-up exams and labs:**

Normally, there are no make-up labs. If you have a valid excuse (official letter from the *Dean of Students or Office of Student Experience*), you can be excused from the lab activities. In rare cases, you can perform the online versions of some missed labs with official excuse, with my prior permissions.

Normally, there are no make-up exams. You must have a valid official excuse (official letter from the *Dean of Students* or *Office of Student Experience*) to take them at some other times than the originally scheduled exam times and dates.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic integrity is one of the cornerstones of RPI. Students taking courses at RPI have a right to expect that their work will be evaluated fairly with respect to other students. They have a right to expect that other students will not attempt to enhance their own grades or the grades of their friends by cheating. Professors have a right to expect that their students are honest and submit work reflecting their own efforts. In an atmosphere of academic integrity, students and professors are on the same team trying to achieve the same learning objectives.

Collaboration and discussion is allowed and encouraged in homeworks, activities, lab activities, and lab reports. **However, you must carry out, write up, and turn in your own solutions and lab reports.** On exams, you’re on your own and not allowed to discuss anything with your classmates. Thus, don’t copy someone else’s homework or lab report, and don’t cheat on exams. If I suspect you of either, I’ll ask for an explanation. If your explanation is unsatisfactory, you’ll be given a grade of zero and reported to the Dean of Students. If this happens more than once, you will be given an *F* for the course.
