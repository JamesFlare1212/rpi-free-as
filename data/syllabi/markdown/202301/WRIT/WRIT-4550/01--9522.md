## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Make things happen: start a business, raise funds, solicit work, support research, win a place on a conference program, take initiative, change the way things are done around here. This course will teach students how to write proposals that persuade. Students will learn to turn situations into occasions for proposing, write a variety of proposals, locate Request for Proposals, develop a work plan for feasible projects that come in on-time and on-budget, use networks to strengthen proposals, detail a budget, and edit for clarity and grace.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

- Identify appropriate sources of funding for academic or business projects.
- Articulate and apply strategies from course readings to plan, write, and present persuasive proposals.
- Develop thoroughly researched, well-written proposals that clearly follow funding guidelines.
- Match project ideas to a funding organization’s state vision, mission, and/or goals.
- Respond to a solicited, field-specific, active conference call or request for proposals (RFP). [1]
- Learn and craft the most common “ingredients” for academic or business proposals: letters of intent/cover letters, abstracts, project narratives, project impact, evaluations, schedules, and budgets.
- Communicate complex ideas clearly and comprehensibly for a variety of audiences.
- Demonstrate written, spoken, and visual communication competencies.

[1] This assignment should align with individual student’s goals and progress toward degree.

This section will outline how you will be assessed during the course of the semester. ## **Participation** (15%)

Showing up to most things in life is the most meaningful; it’s even more meaningful when you are fully attentive, fully engaged, fully present. We are all human, and we all have an infinite number of other things we can (or could) attend to at any given moment. My invitation for the semester is to **be fully present and attentive** in class. *This means silencing your cell phones and keeping your computer away unless it is required for class that day* (which I will communicate to you the class meeting prior). We’ll be “practicing” this at the start of every class by taking a couple minutes to settle in and take inventory of ourselves before we even begin. **This means I will start class promptly to maximize our time together.**

**Attendance** (your presence in the classroom) is an important ingredient to your individual success and to the collective knowledge sharing and creating in our classroom community.

To “quantify” these invitations and expectations, I am going to base your participation grade on an accumulated points system: ***You can receive a possible 5 points for each class meeting: 1 for timely attendance, 1 for preparedness, 1 for active engagement in daily guided writing practice, 1 for active engagement during in-class activities, 1 for positive contribution to our community.*** I will post your participation grades at the end of each week. I will address any concerns I have with you individually in regard to your participation and active engagement in class.

If you anticipate missing a class for a **planned event** (e.g., traveling for varsity athletics, job/internship interview) or for a significant period of time (due to medical concerns, personal loss, etc.) you should notify me at least **2 class sessions in advance** so that we can make plans for how you will make up and submit the work that you will miss. ## **Guided In-Class Writing Practices**

At the beginning of each class period, we’re going to engage in a writing exercise/practice that encourages us to engage with your inner audience, speaker, and listener. These in-class writings will serve as entry points/sparks/challenges to help stretch your creative process, and prepare you for and potentially help polish your formal assignments. **While I will not be assessing these writing practices on a scheduled basis (or even collecting them), *they* *are a part of your class participation grade.***

## **Informal Assignments** (15%)

Over the course of the semester, you’ll be asked to engage in ***Writing Style Workshop* (WSW)** assignments to hone your writing style for formal academic and professional writing contexts. It’s important to note that this requires practicing moves and tactics in Standard Academic English. These readings and assignments will be posted in the **Writing Style Workshop** folder in Blackboard.

Reading for the course is a vital part of the accumulation of knowledge. To ensure that you are actively engaging, synthesizing, and evaluating the reading assignments, *random* ***Readiness Assessment Questions* (RAQs)** will be given at the beginning of class on multiple occasions. These RAQs are meant to help you apply the information from reading to projects both in this class and beyond.

These informal assignments will be graded out of three points based on the quality and thoughtfulness of your composition. If you have room for improvement on one or two of these assignments, your final grade will not be hurt too much. **Please take note, though:** A string of 3/3s will significantly help your final grade, whereas a string of 1/3s may negatively impact your final grade.

**An important note**: At the end of the semester, your lowest WSW **and** RAQ grade will be dropped from your final grade.

## **Formal Assignments for WRIT 4550**

**1) Problem/Solution Proposal** (30% – 5% for Plan & LOI; 20% for Proposal; 5% for presentation)

This *group project* is designed as an *unsolicited proposal* that invites you to come up with a creative, innovative solution to improve the Rensselaer student experience. You will be required to submit a concept plan and letter of inquiry in preparation for creating your formal proposal. You will work as a team to write a persuasive proposal for your target audience on campus that is research-based and considers both short- and long-term impact and sustainability. Your team will also create an 8-10 minute presentation to showcase your proposal, highlighting its key/main ideas. There is a possibility that your intended target audience will join us for your presentations. You will be required to reflect on your individual experience working on this project.

**2) Midterm Peer Group Evaluations** (5%)

Around midterms, you’ll be asked to evaluate yourself and your group members’ individual contributions to the *Problem/Solution* *Proposal*.

**3) Solicited Proposal** (30% – 5% for Plan; 20% for Proposal; 5% for presentation)

This *individual project* is designed to create a clear plan for responding to a Request for Proposals (RFP) or Call for Proposal (CFP) for the purpose of attending a conference, taking part in a workshop, or participating in a research project. You will then craft a persuasive proposal to convince your audience to accept/fund your proposal. You will also create an 8-10 minute presentation to showcase your proposal. Following presentations, you will be required to reflect on your experience writing this proposal.

**4) Final Peer Group Evaluations & Final Reflection** (5%)

At the end of the semester, you’ll be asked to evaluate yourself and your peer writing group members’ individual contributions and quality of feedback on formal writing assignments.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

*The* *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* and *The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement* define various forms of academic dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade *must represent your own work*. **If you plagiarize once,** you will receive an **F on the assignment.** If you plagiarize again, you will **fail the entire course** and may be reported to the *Dean of Students* as appropriate. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask me for clarification. The basics: when using information from another source **(including anonymous online sources**) always cite the original text or URL and distinguish it from your own commentary. If you directly cut and paste another person’s language, you need to use it as a **quotation**. *We will talk specifically about citation styles and how to make sure that you are giving “credit” when it is required of you in spirit of scholarly collaboration.* In regard to using AI (e.g. ChatGPT or other Open AI resources): While it may be tempting to use this resource, please note that there is no “check” to ensure the information that you are receiving is correct. Researchers are finding that ChatGPT can provide misinformation. Using this resource is NOT considered *your own work* and **will** be deemed as an instance of academic dishonesty.

The Center for Global Communication+Design (Comm+D) is a FREE resource for all members of the Rensselaer community: students, faculty, and staff. - ANY type of communication project – written texts, oral presentations, websites, visual elements
- For ANY purpose – classwork, research publications, personal projects, etc.
- From ANY discipline
- At ANY stage in the process – from brainstorming and hatching plans, to revising after receiving feedback (or before submitting for feedback!), to asking questions about citation styles, to polishing your final product.

I was a graduate tutor in Comm+D during my doctoral program at Rensselear. It REALLY is an incredible resource that should be utilized more. *You should **plan ahead** to use the support at Comm+D; last semester there was extremely high demand and tutors were booked out 2 weeks in advance. If you have any issues accessing this service, **please let me know**.*
