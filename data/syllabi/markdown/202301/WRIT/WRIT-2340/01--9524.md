## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course focuses on developing public speaking skills and critical listening abilities. Guided by rhetorical theory, theories of persuasion, and argumentation theory, students prepare several oral presentations, engage in extemporaneous speaking exercises, and criticize other performances.

## Course Text(s)

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course is intended to produce students with requisite knowledge and skill to be proficient public speakers. This includes the following:

- Ability to deliver introductory, informative, persuasive, proposal, and celebratory speeches.
- Ability to discern topics that are important and of interest to the audience.
- Ability to analyze and adapt messaging to accommodate various audiences.
- Ability to perform quality research necessary for effective speech composition.
- Ability to organize speeches in a manner that maximizes message retention.
- Ability to analyze speeches delivered by peers for strengths and weaknesses in both message content and form.
- Developing improved small group communication skills.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Below are brief explanations of the assignments for the class. Each of the assignments is explained at greater length in an assignment sheet on the course blackboard page.

Introductory Speech (15 points): In an introductory speech you introduce yourself to the audience in a manner that will encourage them to perceive you the same way that you would like to be perceived. This assignment will be graded based on completion so long as the performance meets the minimum guidelines which are outlined in the assignment sheet.

Informative Speech (30 points): For this assignment you will perform a speech that provides your audience with new and interesting information about a topic that they are unfamiliar with. This speech will be no shorter than 5 minutes, and no longer than 7 minutes. This assignment will be graded based on the rubric outlined in the assignment sheet on blackboard.

Persuasive Speech (30 points): For this assignment you will deliver a speech that attempts to prompt your audience to take a particular course of action. This speech will be no shorter than 5 minutes, and no longer than 7 minutes. This assignment will be graded based on the rubric outlined in the assignment sheet on blackboard.

Proposal Speech (30 points): For this assignment you will deliver a speech proposing a new idea to your audience. There are many different types of proposals, but two common examples are research proposals, and business proposals. This assignment will be graded based on the rubric outlined in the assignment sheet on blackboard.

Celebratory Speech (30 points): For this assignment students will perform a speech celebrating a special event. The event will be of the student’s choice, but some common examples might include: a wedding toast, a graduation speech, or a thank you speech upon receiving an award. This speech will be no less than three minutes, but many should be substantially longer. This assignment will be graded based on the rubric outlined in the assignment sheet on blackboard.

Self Criticism Assignment (15 points): Students will be asked to analyze a speech which they have delivered, both from a performative and content perspective and identify what strengths and shortcomings they have demonstrated in their speech, and what changes they need to make in order to improve their performance.

Reading Quizzes (50 points): There will occasionally be reading quizzes over material that is considered to be especially important. Students will not necessarily know when these quizzes will be administered-part of their function is to be an accountability mechanism to insure even and thorough participation for learning. Each quiz will be worth 10 points.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Grading Procedure:**

Each assignment will receive a proportion of points possible based on the instructor’s evaluation of student success at demonstrating knowledge/skill in keeping with course objectives. Grades will be determined based on the total number of points cumulatively earned on the assignments for the duration of the semester. Grades will not be subject to a curve or rounding, as the grade should reflect individual student knowledge and effort.  
.

**Grade Disputes:**

Grading is a challenging form of labor and mistakes are possible. If a student believes that a mistake has been made they are asked to contact the instructor for a meeting (in person or over zoom). In the meeting students must identify specific errors in the grading process which merit a specific change in the number of points assigned. Appeals rooted in the performance of other students or past work will not be successful. Students should note: simply asking for a modification does not mean one will be made. If the instructor feels confident that the original grade was correct then no modification will be made. If this process does not provide student satisfaction, there is a further procedure for appealing grading choices which is outlined in the student handbook.

**Extra Credit:**

Students should not anticipate extra credit assignments. Any that are afforded will be at the sole discretion of the instructor.

**Late Work:**

Late work will be accepted for most assignments, excluding any presentations, which must be completed on the date assigned. Late work will receive a 10% deduction per class period which it is turned in late. For example, if a student has an assignment due on Tuesday they may turn it in at any time after that class is completed until Friday when class meets again, and they will still receive up to 90% of the points available for that assignment. When an assignment is worth less than 50% of it’s original value the instructor will no longer accept it.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Absences:**

This class does not take attendance and attendance is not graded. That said, students who do not attend class regularly will not be able to effectively participate in many of the assignments and assessments, making it unlikely that a student who is frequently absent will successfully pass the class. If you cannot make class during the regularly scheduled times you are strongly advised to withdraw from the class. Below are the expectations for how occasional absences should be handled.

Foreseen absences: If a student knows of a forthcoming absence they are expected to contact the instructor of the forthcoming absence and make arrangements for the material to be made up. In some circumstances the instructor may not approve the absence because it is not in line with RPI policy for excused absences. For materials covered during excused absences, not all materials can be made up in a pedagogically equivalent manner, and circumstances may be such that the student will receive a more challenging substitute assignment.

Unforeseen absences: If a student experiences an unforeseen absence for reasons which fall in line with RPI’s policy for excused absences they will be allowed to make up the missed work with the same conditions outlined in the previous paragraph. If the unforeseen absence does not meet those criteria students are expected to contact the instructor to discuss what a reasonable accommodation and outcome would be. If the absence is both unforeseen and unexcused the student should be aware that the faculty member is under no obligation to provide an accommodation.

This policy is understood to be in keeping with RPI’s policy for absences. This policy is described in greater detail here: http://catalog.rpi.edu/content.php?catoid=13&navoid=306

### Other Course Policies:

**Classroom Conduct:**

When in class students are expected to demonstrate respectful and professional behavior toward the instructor and their peers. Usage of computers, phones, and other devices in a manner inconsistent with the classroom purpose is prohibited. Engaging in other conduct contrary to the classroom purpose is similarly prohibited. If a student is persistent in behaving in a manner that undermines the success of the classroom they may be asked to leave.

The nature of communication studies is that you will in fact be communicating with each other while learning what the best practices for communicating with each other look like. This means that mistakes are likely to happen, and have to be handled with a bit of grace, while at the same time engaging in corrective action. In order for this to work, we all have to begin with the assumption that we are all here with the best of intentions, and deserve to be respected as equals. This means that we should strive to be polite, supportive, and respectful while avoiding speech that is insensitive, intolerant, or offensive.

To the extent that circumstances may arise where questions of propriety need to be addressed by the instructor, I reserve the right to ask students to leave the classroom for the duration of the period. Subsequent problems may result in removal from the course entirely, and where appropriate reporting said conduct to the administration.
