## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Strategic writing is a course that aims to introduce students to a range of genres that are common at the college level and beyond. We focus on the mechanics of writing as well as how writing fits into a larger situation or context, and is written for a purpose, with a specific audience in mind, by a writer who makes certain rhetorical choices in order to communicate more effectively with a particular audience. Additionally, this class serves as an introduction to writing and researching at the college level, and will teach students how to locate and assess peer-reviewed scholarly sources through the library and how to incorporate the ideas of others into one’s own writing and how to cite sources in a way that makes it clear which ideas are the student’s own, and which are coming from outside sources. This is a writing-intensive, process-driven class, so students will submit papers in multiple drafts and receive comments from the instructor and from their peers at multiple stages throughout the process.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Learn to write focused, well-organized texts for a variety of academic audiences and rhetorical situations.
- Learn to view writing as a process, to receive constructive feedback from both instructors and peers, and to revise writing through multiple drafts.
- Learn to use the library databases to locate reputable, scholarly sources and to incorporate ideas from these sources into one’s own writing while providing appropriate documentation in the correct citation format.
- Learn to view writing within the context of an ongoing conversation, and learn to make worthwhile original contributions to the types of conversations that typically take place in the academy.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

More detailed descriptions will be provided as the semester progresses.

**Writing Assignment 1: Literacy Narrative**

A literacy narrative tells the story of how the author acquired a particular literacy or skill. Traditionally, literacy narratives tend to be focused on reading and writing, but we can expand the definition to include skills and literacies of all kinds. An effective literacy narrative will employ many of the conventions of storytelling such as narrative, characterization, and dialogue in order to tell its story. In addition, good personal narrative essays have a strong sense of purpose, and it is clear why an audience would be interested in this narrative. A good place to start is with the skills and literacies involved in your major or career. How did you become interested in them, and what is the story of your learning process? WA 1 rough drafts should be 3.5-4 pages, double spaced, in 12 point Times New Roman, or a similarly sized font, and will be due on Mon 2/6. Final drafts should be at least 5 pages and will be due on Mon 2/13.

**Writing Assignment 2: Analysis of a Persuasive Text**

The student will select a persuasive text on a current issue or debate to analyze. Then, you will write an essay in which you: 1) Identify the conversation that the text is joining, comparing and contrasting it to other viewpoints in the same debate. 2) Determine who the audience is and how you can tell. How does the author appeal to the audience’s assumptions or values? Does the author attempt to persuade the audience to adopt or support any viewpoint they would not normally consider? 3) Identify the rhetorical appeals and techniques that the author uses to reach their audience, such as ethos, pathos, logos, and kairos, as well as other rhetorical techniques. 4) Assesses whether or not the argument is effective in persuading its target audience, and provides reasons why the argument is or is not persuasive, and 5) Assesses the strengths and weaknesses of the arguments contained in the article, pointing out any counterarguments the author does not consider, or any ways the argument could be made more effective. Rough drafts should be 3.5-4 pages double spaced, in 12 point Times New Roman, or a similarly sized font, and will be due Mon 3/13. Final drafts should be at least 6 pages and will be due on Mon 3/20.

**Writing Assignment 3: Research Paper**

The semester will culminate with a research paper on a topic of the student’s choice, which will draw on 6-8 peer-reviewed, scholarly sources, and which will aim to make an original contribution to a relevant academic conversation. These papers should be focused narrowly on a specific topic, and should advance a specific thesis or argument about the topic rather than merely summarizing information about the topic. Rough drafts should be at least 4-5 pages, double spaced, in 12 point Times New Roman, or a similarly sized font, and will be due Thurs 4/13. Final drafts should be at least 8 pages and will be due on the last day of class, Mon 4/24.

**Annotated Bibliography**

As part of the research process for the WA 3 Research Paper, students will be responsible for creating an annotated bibliography for 8-10 sources that you will be using. For each source, you will cite it in MLA format and than write an annotation, which should consist of a paragraph that summarizes the source, evaluates the credibility of the author and the venue where it was published, and explains how you will use it in your paper. Good summaries do not attempt to cover every detail in the original article, but have a strong sense of purpose and focus on the details, arguments, and ideas that are relevant to the student’s project. Annotated bibliographies will be due on Mon 4/10.

**Homework and Participation**

This is a process-driven, writing intensive class, and so it is expected that students will attend class regularly and make insightful and relevant contributions to class discussions. Homework assignments, rough drafts, revision activities, and in-class group work will also count toward this part of your grade. In general, so long as the student meets the requirements of the assignment, homework assignments, in-class activities, and process work such as rough drafts are graded on a pass/fail basis.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

All work submitted for this class should be the student’s original work, and should have been written for this class. When students include the ideas of others in their writing, they should make it clear in the text and through in-text citations which ideas and quotes are from outside sources. In cases where group work is assigned, the names of all members in the group must be listed, and the submission must contain only work by those students. Submission of any assignment in violation of this policy will result in a zero on the assignment. A second violation will result in automatic failure of the course and potential disciplinary action by the institution.
