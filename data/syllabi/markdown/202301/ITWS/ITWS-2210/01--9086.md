## Course Description

The hallmark of human-computer interaction (HCI) is the understanding of human behavior. The course starts with fundamental concepts related to human behavior through the lens of HCI, human-centered design, design thinking, user experience (UX) and user interface (UI). These concepts are applied experientially throughout the semester. Research design, persona development, information architecture, low and high-fidelity prototyping are used to develop a final product using software called Figma (collaborative interface design tool). The course long project is the culmination of material covered throughout the course and can be used as a portfolio item to showcase classwork for an interview or other employment situation.

**Course Catalog (2022-2023):** An introduction to the current theories, methods, and issues in human-computer interaction. Theory and research along with practical application are discussed within the context of organizational impact. The course provides the knowledge of HCI systems and research used for the implementation of safe, quick, and useable interactive technologies.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students who successfully complete this course will demonstrate the ability to apply Human-Computer Interaction fundamentals to the design of a product.
- Students who successfully complete this course will be able to define and explain the structure and practices of HCI using its tools, technologies, methods and specialized terms.
- Students who successfully complete this course will be able to frame, clarify and evaluate a complex challenge that bridges computer science, cognitive science and psychology, using theories, tools, methods and scholarship from those fields to produce independently and collaboratively an investigative, creative or practical work illuminating that challenge.
- Students who successfully complete this course will be able to demonstrate the ability to work collaboratively to develop synergies among team members.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance will be taken every class. You must attend at least 75% of the class meetings to be eligible to pass the class.

Both attendance and active participation in class will count towards your participation grade and may impact your contribution to the group project (details in grading policy).

This is a very hands-on class in which we will alternate between lectures and group exercises in class. You will be expected to take part in interactive discussions, group presentations, etc.

If you are unable to attend due to sickness or a family emergency, please contact the instructors **AND** your group members in advance.

**Remote attendance?**

Expect to attend every class in person. However, there may be reasons why you may not feel comfortable coming to class in person. If you are feeling any symptoms of COVID or you need to quarantine after an exposure, but you are otherwise able to attend the class synchronously, please reach out to us to receive permission to participate remotely for a specific class or a period of time. If you are unsure about whether you should come in or not, reach out to us. We will do our best to be accommodating.

For students who do get permission, we will have a live meeting where you can join from a remote location. From the meeting you will be able to listen to the lecture. Once any group activities begin, the remote meeting will end, and you can join your group’s activities via a video call with one of your group members. Remember that this option is only for people who have received permission from us to attend remotely for a designated period of time due to an issue related to the pandemic or illness. If you have not gotten permission to participate remotely, any attendance and participation in the remote meeting will not count towards your participation grade.

**Will there be recordings?** When students are out for an excused absence, we will record the parts of the class that cover lecture content and will also post lecture slides online. However, we reiterate that much of this class is interactive and includes discussions, group activities, and other interactive elements which will not be captured in the recording. You can use the recordings and slides to review content covered in class, but please do not rely on being able to do so as you will miss an integral part of the class experience.

Please communicate with us as soon as possible about any potential or actual absences.
