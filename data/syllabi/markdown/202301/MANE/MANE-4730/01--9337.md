## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Course Objectives**

This course is an introduction to heat transfer. The course will discuss the three modes of heat transfer - Conduction, Convection, and Radiation. Apart from learning the fundamental aspects of heat transfer, the course also emphasizes on applying this knowledge to solve a wide variety of engineering problems.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Analyze a complex thermo-fluids problem with appropriate simplifying assumptions
- • Apply conservation laws or principles to solve a thermos-fluids problem
- • Successfully evaluate the rate of heat transfer for a given thermo-fluid system, by identifying if a process is steady or unsteady, and if the mode of heat transfer is via conduction, or convection (forced or natural, internal or external) or radiation or a combination of different modes
- • Select the appropriate equations governing heat transfer for a given engineering problem
- • Analyze and design components to meet the performance requirements for a given application

**All homework submissions are going to be online (to be uploaded on Gradescope)**. You will receive an email notification confirming your enrollment on Gradescope in the first week of class. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the quizzes, assignments, and exams that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty, and you should make yourself familiar with these rights and responsibilities.

In this class, all documents that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate that. You may discuss approaches to problems with other people for doing homework, but I expect no copying of other people’s efforts. I expect the solutions to quizzes, homework, and exams to be your own individual work. Exams are open-book and open-notes and must reflect each student’s individual knowledge. No teamwork is allowed for exams. Any cheating that is detected will result in a lowered test and/or course grade and will be reported to the Dean of Students Office.

A violation of Academic Integrity policy is, by definition, considered a flagrant offense to the educational process. It is taken seriously by students, faculty, and Rensselaer and will be addressed in an effective manner. If found responsible for committing academic dishonesty, a student may be subject to one or both types of penalties: an academic (grade) penalty administered by the professor and/or disciplinary action through the Rensselaer judicial process described in the handbook. Three relevant academic integrity violations to emphasize include:

**Collaboration**: Collaboration is defined as deliberately facilitating an act of academic dishonesty in any way or form; for example, allowing another student to observe an exam paper or allowing another student to "recycle" one's work.

**Copying**: Copying is defined as obtaining information pertaining to a graded exercise by deliberately observing the paper of another student; for example, noting which alternative another student has circled on a multiple-choice exam.

**Plagiarism**: Plagiarism is defined as representing the work or words of another as one's own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference. Examples include using sentences verbatim from a published source without appropriate referencing, or presenting as one's own the detailed argument of a published source, or presenting as one's own electronically or digitally enhanced graphic representations from any form of media.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines the full list of forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

I expect that you’ll take exams without speaking to other students via another communication channel. You cannot interact with Chegg.com or any other online/internet assignment sharing system for any graded assignment. Interaction with it results in an automatic failure for the entire course. A result of the violation using online resources to assist in graded assignments or exams will result in a formal report.
