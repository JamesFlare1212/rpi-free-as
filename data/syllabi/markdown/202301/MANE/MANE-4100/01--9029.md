### Prerequisites or Other Requirements:

ENGR 2090 and MATH 2400 (Intro to Differential Equations) or equivalents.

It is assumed that all students have a good understanding of statics and basic planar (2-D) (Sophomore level) dynamics. Furthermore, students must be proficient in all of following basic mathematics concepts:

1. *Basic vector algebra and calculus*: dot product, cross product, chain rule for differentiation
2. *Basic Linear Algebra* : matrix multiplication, matrix inversion, etc.
3. *Trigonometric Identities and definitions* (plus the Law of Sines and the Law of Cosines)
4. *Basic* *Differential Equations*: formulation and solution of simple homogeneous, non-homogeneous linear differential equations; exposure to simple eigenvalue problems and their solution
5. *Multivariable calculus*: double and triple integration, differential equations in multiple variables
6. *Working ability with Matlab or equivalent tool* (numerical modeling will be performed extensively in this course)

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students who successfully complete this course shall be able to:

1. Identify, formulate and solve astrodynamics and orbital mechanics engineering problems.
2. Demonstrate a knowledge of basic rocketry.
3. Demonstrate a knowledge of spaceflight mechanics.
4. Become proficient with numerical methods as applicable to astrodynamics/spaceflight mechanics.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Introduction to spatial (3D) dynamics; Particle kinetics; Orbital kinematics, Reference frames for Astrodynamics; Frame transformation; 2-Body Problem; Kepler’s Laws and Kepler Equation; Circular Restricted 3 Body Problem, Lagrange Points, Stability; Satellite orbits (orbit determination, maneuvers, rendezvous transfers); Rocket Thrust, basic rocket design, staging; Rigid body dynamics and stability.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Lecture Notes:**

Lecture notes will be posted in the form of PowerPoint slides in .pdf format prior to the scheduled start of lecture. These notes will be provided through LMS.

**Homework:**

Nine (9) Homework sets will be assigned throughout the course, which will usually consist of approximately 3-4 problems. **These problems will generally require a combination of analytical and numerical solution techniques.** LMS will inform students of the due dates of the homework sets. Homework assignments are to be scanned to the portable document format (.pdf) and must be uploaded to LMS before the due-date. **No late homework assignments will be accepted**.

You are encouraged to collaborate with other students on homework assignments, however, each student is expected to submit their own homework. Merely copying of the solution is deleterious at best because this will not aid in preparing for the exams.

**In****-****Class** **Quizzes**

At the end of selected lecture days, an “in-class quiz” will be collected and graded. These problems will be given in class and a scheduled amount of time depending on the problem difficulty will be provided. The problems may be multiple-choice or might require analytical work. The solutions will be collected (physical copies only!) at the end of the lecture period and graded. **Note that these lecture days will not be previously known to students and the intention is to foster healthy attendance.**

**Project:**

This course will involve an individual project chosen by each student and approved by the instructor. Do not wait and ask the instructor about the appropriateness of a project topic for the first time when there is only couple of weeks left to the course. To remain current and relevant, engineering requires that learning be a life-long effort. The most important thing an engineer learns in school is not the methods engineers use, but that they learn how to effectively learn. As such, this project requires that student choose a subject of interest to them (which is beyond what has been taught in this course), then learn on their own and apply the key material associated with this subject. The student must extend material learned in class (e.g., simulation of trajectory associated with *n-body problem*; explore gravity assists; reverse-engineering the Saturn V; etc), and write an associated report with appropriate citations. This is a modeling and analysis course, so the projects MUST have a significant mechanics modeling and analysis component (in which you extend methods and tools learned in the course beyond what would be considered a glorified homework problem)[Just running a blackbox such as STK is not sufficient]. The overall project will be worth 20% of the course grade and should reflect ~25 hours of productive work.

**DO NOT PUT THIS OFF TO THE END OF THE TERM!!!**

**Format:** The project write-up should be in the form of a technical report (title, introduction, background and significance of problem, theoretical development, results and discussion, conclusions and recommendations, references, appendices). Conveyance of need for and importance of life-long learning will be explicitly graded. This report need not be long, but it does need to be well written in a professional manner (6-7 pages is normal [not counting references and appendices]. Reports where the body of the report runs past 7 pages will be penalized).

**Exams:**

Three exams (closed book, closed notes), *including the Finals*, are scheduled on the course calendar. **Exams must be completed**. If you are in need of a makeup examination, you must contact the instructor for a makeup exam. If for *any reason, including excused absences*, more than one exam must be missed, an incomplete will be given for this class, and you will need to retake the course.

The final exam must be taken to receive a grade for this course. This final exam will be administered during the final scheduled class time. This final examination will be comprehensive, in that the material of this course will continually build upon itself during the semester.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

***Academic Integrity***

This statement is intended to cover most, but not all, forms of academic dishonesty. (You should refer to *The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* for detailed information on Institute policy in this regard.)

**Homework:**

Collaboration among the students is permitted and, in fact, encouraged, as described above. Seeking help from other sources is also encouraged. However, the work you submit should be your own. Evidence of you merely copying another student’s homework (or from the homework solutions) will be considered academic dishonesty and will result in **a zero grade for homework for the semester for all parties involved.**

**Exams:**

Collaboration during an exam (exchanging verbal or written information or copying) is, of course, prohibited. In addition, use of email or the internet during exams for any purpose is strictly prohibited. The use of Chegg, Yahoo! Answers, Quora, Discord, Twitter, Facebook, messaging programs of any kind, or other materials that are not assigned through the course are prohibited.

**Unauthorized collaboration or compromising of any examination will result in investigations. All parties involved will receive an F in the course.**

**Other:**

Outrageous offenses such as arranging for a substitute to take an examination, attempting to obtain examination questions or solutions in advance, or attempting to change a grade record will result in a failing grade for the course and a recommendation to the Dean of Students that the student(s) in question be suspended.

If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
