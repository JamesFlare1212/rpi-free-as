## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Conceptual and preliminary design of manned and unmanned aerial vehicles to satisfy given mission requirements and aircraft specifications. Includes elements of initial sizing and weights, geometry selection, aerodynamics design, propulsion integration, stability and control, loads, structural design, materials, manufacturability, and cost analysis. This is a communication intensive course.

### Text Details:

### Other Course Policies:

## Course Format

The majority of class time in this course will be devoted to a studio-style format where students can work on their projects and the instructors will be available to answer questions. This is so that students have a designated time each week to work face-to-face as a group, as it can be difficult for groups to schedule time together outside of class. For part of the semester, 20-30 minutes of each class period will be used to review engineering concepts from previous courses and introduce new content related to the design process. Some of these topics have been preplanned, but students are invited to request topics that are of particular use to their selected project. The schedule of these topics is flexible so as to best accommodate the needs of the projects (see separate topic schedule document). Apart from these topics, students will be expected to seek out the resources and information they need to complete their chosen design objectives.

Students will work in groups of 5-6 (or 7 with instructor permission) to design a fixed-wing aircraft. The expectations for the elements of the design work will be enumerated in the grading rubrics for each reporting assignment in the design process. Students will also be required to individually keep engineering notebooks of their work. Any format (e.g. paper, live scripts, OneNote, etc.) is allowed so long as the student can submit a readable copy as a PDF. (Note, if spreadsheets are used in your notebooks, you must make sure that all equations are visible in the submitted PDF.)

This course is being taught with two instructors in a single section instead of two separate sections in order to give students greater access to the experience of both instructors. The groups will be divided between the two instructors, and their assignments will be graded predominately by their assigned instructor. If needed, some normalization will be applied at the end of the semester to accommodate for differences in grading between instructors. Students are welcome to ask technical questions of either instructor.

Two assignments (interim report and final presentation) will also have an element of peer evaluation in their grading.

Questions about grades should be resolved within a week of receiving the grade; otherwise the grade stands.

Without a formal excuse, an assignment will receive a 10% penalty if handed in within 24 hours of the deadline, a 30% penalty if handed in within 72 hours of the deadline, and a 100% penalty otherwise (i.e. no credit).

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
