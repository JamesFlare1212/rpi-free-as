## Course Goals

### Goals:

At the completion of this course, you will be able to:

- Explain the components of Earth’s climate system and how they interact
- To learn how scientists study and reconstruct past climates
- To understand the role of Earth system processes in climate changes in the past, present, and future
- To be able to critically assess climate change issues, reports, and articles encountered in the media
- To be able to communicate about climate science

## Course Content

### Content Details:

2\. Long-term Climate

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Engagement:** Research has shown that regular engagement with material through the semester increases retention and also leads to better grades. There will be a variety of engagement strategies employed throughout the semester (before class, in class, after class) in order to improve your learning and retention. These will include in-class discussions, hands-on learning activities and group work.

**Problem Sets**: There will be a number of problem sets you will complete during the course of the semester. The purpose of these is to explore and reinforce material covered in class. You will generally have 1 week to complete the problem sets when assigned.

**Quizzes:** There will be 4 quizzes throughout the semester. Each quiz will cover the topics preceding that exam (**not cumulative**-however, keep in mind that we will be building upon prior knowledge). Quiz dates are on the schedule and dates will not change.

**Journal Entries:** The climate journal is designed to help you be reflective about what you are learning in class and connect course material to your everyday life. These will include both directed entries and independent entries. You will need to bring your journal to class for directed entries. Journals will be collected randomly, but you can expect a collection every 2 weeks. ***There should be at least 1 independent entry/week.*** Additional information on journals is available on LMS

**Climate Story**: You will either write and record your own climate story, or conduct an interview and record this. Stories will be published to: https://www.climatestoriesproject.org/adirondack-climate-stories.html

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance:** I assuming you are coming to learn. Attendance will not be taken, as I also assume you are responsible for your own education and are paying for this course. Nevertheless, you should know that poor attendance at lectures is generally associated with a poor final grade. The lectures will contain material not covered in readings. Class notes will be posted on LMS. However, do not assume that looking at the lectures without coming to class will afford you the knowledge you need to pass the class.

### Other Course Policies:

**Late Policy:** If an assignment is due in class, it will be due at the start of class. If it is turned in at the end of class, it is considered late. Assignments will be accepted up to 1 week past the due date. Scores on late assignments will be penalized 10% each day they are late. So, if an assignment is due in class on a Tuesday at 2:00 pm and you turn it in between Tuesday at 2:01 pm and Wednesday at 2:00 pm, you will lose 10%. *After 1 week, the grade becomes a zero.*
