## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

## Course Description & Objectives

A primary objective of this course is to gain experience in design that builds upon and synthesizes knowledge and skills gained in other engineering courses, as well as humanities, social science, and professional development courses.

The design process emphasized here is model-based design, which is widely used in professional consulting and design firms. Models may be identified from the literature, or you may develop them as part of the design process. You will estimate model inputs (parameters) and document your approach. In some cases, laboratory measurements may be appropriate. Building prototypes is not precluded in this course, but is not the focus; other courses in the curriculum (Bench Scale Design) have been developed to emphasize this approach.

In contrast to most other courses in the curriculum, this course is primarily self-directed, and open-ended. Therefore, as is the case in professional practice, you will be expected to gain new knowledge through independent investigation of the literature including books, journal articles, design manuals, government publications, and web resources.

The goal of your design project is to provide an engineering solution to a problem of societal importance, to improve quality of life, protect public health, or the health and function of ecosystems.

You will be expected to consider engineering solutions in a societal context, including their technical, environmental, societal, political, legal, aesthetic, economic, and financial implications. You will develop and apply an understanding of professional and ethical responsibility to protect public safety, health, and welfare, and to carefully weigh alternatives when values conflict.

A second major objective of this course is to gain experience in the professional practice of engineering, by creating documents that are universally employed in practice, including a Statement of Qualifications, Engineering Proposal (including an estimate of the Cost of Engineering Services), and Design Report (including an engineer’s Construction Cost Estimate).

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Design a system or process to fulfill desired needs while meeting realistic constraints. o Understand design methodology, including problem definition, scope, and analysis. o Identify, evaluate and synthesize alternatives. o Integrate information and technology, and where needed, to work across multiple disciplines to achieve design goals. o Apply the iterative nature of design in a real-world context o Identify stakeholders and consider their perspectives and expectations. o Identify and satisfy multiple design constraints; possible constraints include aesthetics, codes, constructability, cost, legal considerations, maintainability, manufacturability, marketability, regulations, safety, standards, sustainability, or usability. o Develop project schedules. o Estimate costs of i) engineering services and ii) project construction.
- Prepare, present, and defend technical material pertaining to design and the design process, which is used to educate, inform, and persuade colleagues, clients and the general public about technical solutions and the design process.
- When appropriate, function on a design team as both a leader and a contributor. To do so you will apply your background in Professional Development courses: i) understanding of team formation, evolution, and dynamics; ii) appreciation of the role of interpersonal relationships and personality; iii) skills in collaboration, problem solving, and time management; and iv) ability to foster and integrate diversity of perspectives, knowledge, and experiences.
- Investigate a problem independently, as a prototype for how you will engage in life-long learning to develop your career and contribute to the environmental engineering field.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
