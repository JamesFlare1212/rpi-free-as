## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

An introduction to industrial and systems engineering (ISE). Major elements of the ISE disciplines are overviewed in the context of operations engineering problems. Topics include deterministic and stochastic applications of operations research methods, soft computing, applications of probability and statistics, engineering economics, discrete event simulation, and decision analysis.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Regular class periods are devoted primarily to guest lectures introducing the power of industrial and systems engineering in a wide range of topics and industries. Some class periods will be devoted to lectures, discussion, and in-class exercises for solving operations engineering and ISE design problems. No textbook is required.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

This class is graded on a Satisfactory/Unsatisfactory (S/U), or Pass/Fail, basis.

To pass the class, students must earn 11 attendance points, where each lecture is worth 1 attendance point. There are two ways to do this:

- (1) Attend a minimum of 11 live class lectures.
  1. This is the preferred way of earning attendance points.
  2. If you are unable to attend class and inform the professor of your absence, you may receive attendance points by watching the recorded lecture videos and submitting via LMS a 300-word response reflection on the presentation. The response should describe what the presentation was about and at least one piece of information that you found useful or new.

OR

- (2) Attend a minimum of 9 class sessions, attend at least 2 outside lectures, AND submit a reflection response.
  1. The outside lectures need to be preapproved by the instructor and require students to provide to the instructor (1) a piece of evidence of attendance (e.g., a selfie at the event or sign-up information and (2) a 300-word response on the topic of the seminar, and how Industrial and Systems Engineering could impact or inform the seminar topic.

Please note that for regular class sessions, attendance is sufficient.  
Students can make inquiries directly to the course instructor at any point during the semester to determine if they are in danger of failing.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
