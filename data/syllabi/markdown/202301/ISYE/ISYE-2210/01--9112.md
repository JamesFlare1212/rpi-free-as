## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course covers the basic methods of planning, organizing, and controlling the production of goods and services. Students will be exposed to the mathematical and graphical models which have been developed and applied to operations. Students will apply these models to problem instances supported by computer software and interpret the results in terms of actions or policies as they relate to the operations problem presented.

Overview of methods used in the design and operation of production and service systems. Topics include forecasting, capacity planning, line balancing, production scheduling, staff scheduling, inventory control, just in time, time study, and project planning. The goal of the course is to educate students in basic operations management principles and models.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Define key principles of planning, organizing, and controlling the production of goods and services
- Apply and interpret the mathematical models of operations management
- Design a production system or process to meet desired economic and volume goals
- Identify, formulate, and solve engineering problems using tools of operations management
- Use techniques, skills, and modern tools in designing, organizing, and managing operations

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

The final grade in the course is based on a 100-point scale:

- **Exams (3 Exams × 20 points = 60 points):** Students will have three exams (two within the semester and one final exam during the final exam week). The final exam covers all concepts discussed throughout the semester.
- **Labs and Class Participation Exercises (CPE) (30 points):**
  - o **Labs (20 points):** Lab work includes a written report with the solution to problems covered in the past lectures. Instructions will be available in LMS at least 10 days before the due date. Before the deadline, a PDF or Word document should be uploaded in LMS.
  - o **Class Participation Exercises (CPE) (10 points):** CPE assigned for the class lecture should be individually completed before class starts. Each student should provide their results through LMS before the class begins. Students may be called to share their answers at the beginning of the class.

At the end of the semester, five of the lowest CPE grades will be dropped, including missing CPEs with or without an excuse. If, for some extraordinary reason, you have more than five absences, you will be graded over the remaining CPEs.

- **Final Project (10 points):** To be developed towards the end of the course, including presentations the last day of class.
- **“The Goal” Quiz:** Students are encouraged to read “The Goal” throughout the course, which will help them obtain extra credit towards the final exam. Towards the end of the semester, the students who read the book will have the option of answering the quiz. Extra credit points will be added to the final exam grade.

The total points earned in the course will be the weighted sum of points on the assessment requirements. Point totals will be rounded up for assigning the final grade. The final course grade will be assigned using the ranges below. Final grades will not be changed unless a numerical error is made in computing your final grade. Please do not ask for extra credit or extra work after final exams have been administered.

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

**Missing Exams or Assignments:** Missing exams are only allowed if there is an emergency verified by the Dean of Student’s office. There are no makeups for exams. Missing assessments without official excuses will receive a zero.  
**Excusal from Course Exams or Assignments:** If you know ahead of time that you will miss an exam or an assignment deadline, then you must make arrangements before the assessment is due. Failure to do so will result in 25% off for each 24-hour period the assessment is late.

**Exam Protocol:** All exams are open book and notes, and use of calculator is allowed. All answers must be recorded on the exam answer sheet and full credit is awarded for correct numerical answers as well as direct answer to the problem presented in the question. Little or no credit is awarded for effort that yields incorrect numerical values since the professional world measures performance on correct results.  
**Exam Regrading Request:** Exams submitted for regrading must not be altered from the original work submitted for grading. Altering work for regrading is academic dishonesty and will be treated as such. All requests must be made in writing by attaching a well-phrased note to the original work explaining in full the reason for the request and the points you think you deserve for your error. Requests for a regrade must be submitted no later than the next class meeting after the grade is announced or the material is handed back to the class for the request to be considered.  
**Special Testing Accommodations:** If you have a special accommodation letter, make sure to email it within the first three weeks of the semester so that necessary arrangements can be made.
