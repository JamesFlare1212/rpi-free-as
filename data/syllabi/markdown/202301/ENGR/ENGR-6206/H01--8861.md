### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

### How to Approach This Course

So, the course is based on a series of projects – here’s the approach:

1. We’ll have a synchronous session timed to the beginning of each project, in which we’ll discuss and scope the scenario as well as the approach.
2. The digital classroom will walk you through a series of steps – the approach – and provide you with the learning resources you’ll need right when you need them.
3. I am available to assist you at any point where and when you need – Zoom, text, phone, in person if that suits you. Just reach out and we’ll schedule something mutually convenient.
4. As you progress through, you’ll prepare a series of “deliverables” – artifacts that show your work to that point. You’ll submit these deliverables in the digital classroom.
5. I will evaluate your deliverables and give you feedback – feedback you can incorporate as you continue forward to improve your project.
6. When you’ve completed a project, I’ll evaluate it according to the pre-established rubric (which you can see in the classroom before you submit). If your project meets the criteria, you can move to the next project. If additional refinement is needed, I’ll give you feedback to guide your work. You can continue to iterate and resubmit accordingly.
7. When you are done with all of the projects and you’ve received feedback that your projects have met the rubric criteria, you have completed the course!

My role in the course is to serve as your Mentor as you work through the projects. Rensselaer hires us to be Mentors based on our academic background as well as our years of being a practitioner in the field – and I still continue to actively practice in the profession as a full-time endeavor.  
Therefore, my role is to be your Mentor – to be your guide, to give you feedback, to be there whenever you have questions or need assistance. We can schedule individual times to meet and discuss, we can add synchronous sessions, we can do whatever is needed so that you have what you need, where you need it, to successfully master the course.  
Let me address “grades” for a moment – there is no penalty for needing to resubmit a project if needed to meet the rubric criteria – submitting is a low-stake activity. I want you to always be submitting your best work – work that you feel strongly meet the outlined criteria – but there isn’t a penalty if it requires additional refinement. See this for what it is - - a focus not on the “grade,” but on your mastery.  
Finally, I know that you are a working professional with many draws on your time and energy. I also know how big of a commitment it is to work on a graduate degree while employed – I did it myself. So, please: the key to success is communication. If you need additional time on a project, let me know – it’s highly likely we can work around that. If you are struggling, let me know – we can schedule time to resolve the issue. If you have commitments such that you cannot attend a synchronous session, let me know – and I will record the session so you can partake later. In any case, let me know - - the closer we are in sync about how you are doing, the less likely something will lead one of us to an unsuccessful path.  
The syllabus is posted – please take a look at it and plan ahead for the synchronous sessions and the project milestones as laid out. I hope you have an outstanding semester and remember remember – I’m here to help you develop your mastery of data analytics – I’m looking forward to getting to know you!

## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Working with a faculty member, students develop a big data inquiry model for complex business issue, question or problem of their choosing. Over the semester, the student frames the question to be analyzed, collects and prepares business data for analysis, performs the analysis and presents actionable results and recommendations back to the organization. ***Students cannot receive credit for both this course and ENGR 6206 or ENGR 6216.***

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Develop a business focused big data inquiry model for an issue, question or problem.
- Analyze, collect and prepare business focused data for analysis, perform the analysis and presents actionable results and recommendations back to an organization.
- Communicate with purpose and clarity in written and oral formats.
- Assemble and prepare data from multiple sources that inform analysis in a sound and ethical manner.
- Demonstrate use of data to inform and provide insights to business questions and decisions.
