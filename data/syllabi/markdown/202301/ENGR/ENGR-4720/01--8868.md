### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

The primary purpose of the Manufacturing Processes and Systems (MPS) II is to teach, by experience, how to build, and execute an efficient cost-effective manufacturing system that produces a useful, high quality, and economical product. MPS II allows students the opportunity to practice manufacturing lessons from MPS I thought the implementation of technical data packages produced in MPS I. MPS I is a suggested prerequisite for MPS II.

This course sequence develops technical communication skills through report writing (e.g., Technical Data Package) and oral presentations. An additional goal of this course sequence is for students to experience being on a team that takes existing product and manufacturing design specifications (Technical Data Package information) and delivers a product in small lot manufacturing quantities in a 14-week period.

In the process, you will learn to plan your work, carry out your plan, integrate tasks, and to effectively communicate with fellow team members and suppliers. MPS II teaches an integrated project approach to planning and producing a product using the skills developed in MPS I. While in MPS I you learned to plan your work, now it is time to execute your plan, integrate tasks, and communicate.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Upon successful completion of this course, students are expected to achieve the following three learning outcomes.

1. Students will demonstrate the ability to organize a team, plan work, execute plans, integrate tasks, and to effectively communicate with team members and staff.

Assessment of this learning outcome includes milestone 1 and midterm/final peer evaluations, and two project update individual questions.

- Milestone #1 – Establish updated project tasks and schedule, assign team member responsibilities including officers.

2. Students will demonstrate the ability to efficiently execute manufacturing plans from MPS I.

Assessment of this learning outcome includes class milestones 2, 3, & 4

- Milestone #2 – Completion of tooling and mfg. fixtures (e.g., plastic injection mold tooling, thermoforming tooling, and sheet metal progressive dies are machined and ready for use). This milestone also includes drafts of quality assurance plans and standard operating procedures.
- Milestone #3 – Completion of special purpose machines, automation equipment, end effectors, pallets, feeders, and packaging. This milestone also includes a preliminary demonstration of assembly system in the MILL including Octopuz robotic simulations.
- Milestone #4 – Functional assembly system demonstration with production parts.

3. Students will demonstrate the ability to build, implement, document (both with written and digital simulations and documents including VKS and Octopuz), and demonstrate a complete semi-automated manufacturing system (component manufacturing and product assembly) for producing agreed upon production numbers of the team’s product to specifications.

Assessment of this learning outcome includes team-based midterm and final evaluations by staff and industrial partners of presentations and technical data packages.

4. Students will learn basic concepts associated with advanced manufacturing in the areas of electrospinning, additive manufacturing, and micro machining. Students will select one topic for investigation. Assessment of this learning outcome will be a pass/fail evaluation.

**Course Schedule and Grading** - Your course grade is determined primarily by your team’s success meeting four milestones, TDP submission, and final number of QUALITY parts produced. These areas of the project account for 65% of your team’s grade. The additional 35% of your grade is based on your individual participation. This means that it is possible for people in the same team to have very different final grades! See the course assessment measures chart below for grade details.

**Individual Grades (Peer Reviews and Project Questions / Demo, and Simulation)** – Students will be responsible for understanding all project details throughout the semester. Each team member will be randomly questioned once during the semester on specific areas of the project and is expected to give a brief answer to address the question. In addition to the individual question, students will also be randomly selected during the semester to demonstrate their individual project work in the MILL. Example, show molds they are machining, run an assembly process they are developing, or demonstrate the packaging process they are working on, etc.

Students will participate in midterm and final peer evaluations. Your teammates will be evaluating you twice during the semester. There will be a grade penalty if you do not complete your evaluation by the date given above.

Each student will be required to submit a one-page report on their contributions related to a manufacturing or assembly process simulation they are working on to support the project. Options include Mastercam, Octopuz, or VKS

TDP- The TDP generated in MPS I will be modified and improved this semester to reflect what manufacturing operations, process parameters, and assembly processes are actually utilized in MPS II. The TDP organization and format does not change, but its content does; especially in the area of production and standard operation procedures (SOP) for producing product components. Examples of TDPs from previous spring semesters are available in the MPS.

Standard Operating Procedures – Each team will post standard operating procedures for each component at the appropriate processing area (Plastic injection molding, CNC machining, metal forming, assembly, etc.). Production will not run unless the procedure is posted and approved by Larry or Sam. These documents will be part of your TDP. Teams are required to use VKS to create SOPs for both manufacturing and assembly in a digital format.

Making devices for your project – You need to have final sign-off from Larry, or Sam BEFORE you start to make any type of component (fixture, feeder, robot end effector, packaging mold, etc.).

Gantt Charts – Each team will post (location to be determined) a weekly Gantt Chart update in the MILL.

Resource Coordination – As we start to make system components and run product production this year, teams need to continually check with lab staff and other teams on availability of resources in the lab. This is very important because we will need to coordinate machine use, staff time, and raw material resources so we can successfully produce all products.

Lab Safety Procedures - Safety always must be one of the chief concerns of the students, staff, and faculty in this laboratory. Each student must be safety conscious at all times, and learn and follow all safety rules and precautions. There are three main sources of safety information for this course:

- The general safety rules that are part of the MPS I Lab manual, and the general safety lecture usually given as part of the first lecture of MPS I.
- Specific safety rules for each type of equipment as listed in each of the various sections of the MPS Lab Manual.
- The references and user manuals provided by equipment manufacturers and vendors.

As the preceding bulleted items indicate, much of this safety information is part of MPS I class material. If you are in doubt about anything that has a potential safety issue, see Larry immediately.

Keep the Lab Clean - All team members are responsible for keeping the lab clean. It is expected that when you are done using a tool that it gets returned to its correct location. If you are not sure where a tool goes please ask. All machines should be cleaned after use and all work areas swept if required. If lab closing time requires you to stop work on a machine and you plan on completing it the next work day you can leave the tools and set-up on the machine as long as you inform an MPS TA or staff member of your plans.

Budget Information - Team will be required to account for an allocated budget of $3,200. This includes any purchased components, supplies, and the following:

AWJ time at $30 per hour

9”x8” molds at $500 ea.

3”x3” molds at $200

Teams should check MILL inventory for metals, plastics, and components before requesting a purchase. If what a team is looking for is in the MILL most likely it will be free to the team. If the item needs to be purchased it will cost your team.
