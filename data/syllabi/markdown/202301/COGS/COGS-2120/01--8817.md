## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Critically analyze and evaluate theoretical and conceptual foundations of cognitive science from the perspectives of philosophy, psychology, computer science, neuroscience, linguistics, and anthropology.
- Assess and critique various models of cognition
- Demonstrate in-depth knowledge of a specific topic and present this material clearly and thoughtfully

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Student grades will be comprised of three components: short quizzes, assignments, and a project.

Quizzes will accompany almost every class. These are short (~5 questions) and designed to reinforce important concepts discussed in class. Paying attention in class is a great way to prepare for these quizzes! These will be due by the start of the following class; so quizzes related to Monday’s class must be submitted by the beginning of class on Thursday and quizzes related to Thursday’s class must be submitted by the beginning of class on Monday. These quizzes will all be completed and submitted through LMS.

The “assignments" category consists of an assortment of digital assignments to be submitted through LMS throughout the semester. Students will submit these in pairs, which we will randomly assign at the beginning of the semester. Each student will do the assignment, meet to discuss it outside of class, and submit one assignment together.  
The project will be a highly flexible individual project that allows you to look more in depth into a cognitive science topic of your choosing. There is a list of prompts on LMS to guide you through this project. You will submit a project proposal for approval several weeks before the project is due.

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
