## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

What is the nature of religion and religious experiences? What kind of ‘being’ does the word “God” refer to? Can the existence of God be rationally demonstrated through arguments? What is the problem of evil? What is the relationship between ethics and God’s existence? Are contemporary scientific beliefs coherently compatible with religious beliefs? How does religion shape our perspectives on death and the meaning of life? These are just some of the questions and problems we shall think through as we explore and discuss key readings, both classical and contemporary, within the field of Philosophy of Religion.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- After completing this course, students will be able to: 1) think critically about a range of beliefs, theories and arguments that are salient to religious traditions. 2) demonstrate their ability to evaluate, discuss and debate arguments concerning: (a) the existence of God; (b) the problem of evil; (c) the nature of faith and reason; (d) the relationship between morality and religious belief; and (e) the interface between religion and science. 3) identify and explain key concepts, theories and thinkers that have shaped our understanding of Philosophy of Religion. 4) effectively communicate their perspectives through essays, seminar discussions and panel presentations. 5) conduct and present their research in The Chicago Manual of Style format.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Assessment Measures: Assignments & Evaluation**

Short Essays (1000 words): For each short essay assignment, students will be given constructive feedback and comments. After submitting their essays for a preliminary review, each student will be given two weeks to edit and make changes prior to submitting their work for a final grade. Essay prompts will be centered around specific theorists/arguments/cases in Philosophy of Religion. Essays will be evaluated according to one’s ability to: (1) write clearly; (2) apply ethical theories to specific cases; (3) employ the basic skills of critical thinking; and (4) evaluate and create arguments in defense of a normative position.

Research Paper (2000 words): Students will have the opportunity to conduct their own research upon a specific topic in Philosophy of Religion of their choosing. Students will decide upon and compose a short 100-word abstract detailing their research thesis; abstracts should be submitted no later than 2/13/2023. All papers will be submitted for a grade no later than 04/26/2023. Research papers will be evaluated according to one’s ability to: (1) write clearly; (2) apply the tools of critical thinking to specific cases; (3) identify and explain their research methodologies; (4) evaluate and create arguments in defense of a specific position; and (5) effectively incorporate no less than 7 academic sources/references.

Research Panel Presentation: Each student will participate in a panel discussion whereby they will have the opportunity to present their thesis, arguments and conclusions in regard to their research papers. They will also have the opportunity to engage in a dialogue with other panelists as well as field questions from the audience. Grading will be based upon one’s ability to: (1) effectively and clearly present their research and their arguments; (2) engage in panel discussion with other panelists; and (3) respond to questions from the audience.

Seminar Discussion Participation: This class is a communication intensive course, which means there will be regular seminar discussions that address specific theories, thinkers and issues that have defined the field of Philosophy of Religion. Discussion topics will be provided prior to class meetings so that students will have time to reflect upon their perspective and normative position. Participation will be evaluated based upon one’s ability to dialogue with students and articulate arguments that are relevant to the course readings.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Evaluation Breakdown:**

Two short Essays: 25% each

Research Paper: 35%

Final Presentation: 15 %

**Grading Policy:**

All assignments in this course – discussions, presentations and essays – will be evaluated in light of the following criterion: (1) ability to write clearly by following grammatical rules and conforming to style guidelines of The Chicago Manual of Style; (2) ability to demonstrate critical thinking by formulating and assessing arguments; (3) ability to effectively engage and reference key articles and philosophical perspectives included in the syllabus. Late assignments will not be accepted for a grade unless: (i) a student requests an extension in advance (48 hrs. prior to the deadline); and (ii) the request concerns a legitimate issue warranting an extension.

**Appealing Grades** If a student is dissatisfied with a specific grade for an assignment, they should arrange to meet with me during office hours to discuss their concerns. If the student remains unsatisfied with the grade they received, they can arrange to meet with the Chair of the Department of Cognitive Science, Dr. Sergei Nirenburg. Following this meeting, if the students believes that their case has not been treated fairly, they can arrange a meeting with the Dean of Humanities Arts and Social Sciences, Dr. Mary Simoni.
