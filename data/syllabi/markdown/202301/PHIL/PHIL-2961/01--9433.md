## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Environmental Ethics is an area of applied ethics that explores moral perspectives, duties and responsibilities towards non-human animals, the natural environment and human communities that are impacted by environmental policies and ecological concerns. In this class students will explore a range of ethical issues pertaining to the ethical theories that shape our attitudes about nature, as well as the application of such theories so as to address a range of environmental issues, including: (1) non-human animals welfare and rights; (2) wilderness preservation; (3) human overpopulation; (3) green-capitalism and land use policies; (4) sustainability and technology; (5) agriculture and food ethics; (6) pollution; and (7) climate change.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- In this class, students will: 1) be introduced to the general practice of philosophical thinking by demonstrating their ability to evaluate arguments in light of complex moral situations and issues pertaining to environmental ethics. 2) be able to identify and explain the dominant traditions of ethical theory (e.g., deontology, utilitarianism, virtue theory and feminism) and environmental perspectives (e.g., anthropocentrism, biocentrism, ecocentrism, deep-ecology and ecofeminism). 3) strengthen their ability to work cooperatively and empathetically with others in order to entertain multiple perspectives and cultural views. 4) demonstrate their ability to evaluate, discuss and debate arguments concerning environmental issues. 5) be able to identify and explain key concepts, theories and thinkers that have shaped our understanding of environmental ethics. 6) learn how to effectively communicate their perspectives through essays, seminar discussions and panel presentations. 7) be able to conduct and present their research in The Chicago Manual of Style format.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Attendance Policy**

Classes meet in Low 3130 on Monday’s and Thursday’s, from 4:00 – 5:50 p.m. Class meetings will be a combination of lectures and discussions. Students are expected to attend all classes prepared to ask questions and proffer personal perspectives. Attending classes regularly, on time and well prepared is necessary for making this class a success. If you need to miss a class for any reason, please notify me via email in advance. If a student misses more that 5 classes, they will not receive credit for the class.

**Assessment** *Short Essays (1000 words)*: For each short essay assignment, students will be given constructive feedback and comments. After submitting their essays for a preliminary review, each student will be given two weeks to edit and make changes prior to submitting their work for a final grade. Essay prompts will be centered around specific theorists/arguments/cases in environmental ethics. Essays will be evaluated according to one’s ability to: (1) write clearly; (2) apply ethical theories to specific cases; (3) employ the basic skills of critical thinking; and (4) evaluate and create arguments in defense of a normative position.

*Research Paper (2000 words)*: Students will have the opportunity to conduct their own research upon a normative topic in environmental ethics of their choosing. Students will decide upon and compose a short 100-word abstract detailing their research thesis; abstracts should be submitted no later than **02/14/2023**. All papers will be submitted for a grade no later than **04/26/2023.** Research papers will be evaluated according to one’s ability to: (1) write clearly; (2) apply ethical theories to specific cases; (3) employ the basic skills of critical thinking; (4) identify and explain their research methodologies; (5) evaluate and create arguments in defense of a normative position; and (6) effectively incorporate no less than 7 academic sources/references.

*Research Panel Presentation*: Each student will participate in a panel discussion whereby they will have the opportunity to present their thesis, arguments and conclusions in regard to their research papers. They will also have the opportunity to engage in a dialogue with other panelists as well as field questions from the audience. Grading will be based upon one’s ability to: (1) effectively and clearly present their research and their arguments; (2) engage in panel discussion with other panelists; and (3) respond to questions from the audience.

*Seminar Discussion Participation*: This class is a communication intensive course, which means there will be regular seminar discussions that address specific theories, thinkers and issues that have defined the field of environmental ethics. Discussion topics will be provided prior to class meetings so that students will have time to reflect upon their perspective and normative position. Participation will be evaluated based upon one’s ability to dialogue with students and articulate arguments that are relevant to the course readings.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Evaluation Breakdown:**

Two short Essays: 25% each

Research Paper: 35%

Final Presentation: 15 %

**Grading Policy:**

All assignments in this course – discussions, presentations and essays – will be evaluated in light of the following criterion: (1) ability to write clearly by following grammatical rules and conforming to style guidelines of The Chicago Manual of Style; (2) ability to demonstrate critical thinking by formulating and assessing arguments; (3) ability to effectively engage and reference key articles and philosophical perspectives included in the syllabus. Late assignments will not be accepted for a grade unless: (i) a student requests an extension in advance (48 hrs. prior to the deadline); and (ii) the request concerns a legitimate issue warranting an extension.

**Academic Integrity & Plagiarism Policy:**

Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute’s commitment to thoroughness and knowledge includes the values associated with intellectual honesty. Using the words or ideas of someone else without proper acknowledgment is immoral and illegal, and will result in a grade of F for the assignment and/or the course. If you have questions about plagiarism, be sure to reach out to me prior to submitting your writing for grade so that we can address any concerns you might have.

**Appealing Grades**  
If student has a question or concern regarding a particular grade for an assignment, they should plan to see me during my office hours to discuss the matter. If they are dissatisfied with the results of the meeting and wish to appeal their grade, they can arrange a meeting with the Chair of the Cognitive Science Department, Dr. Sergei Nirenburg, to address their case. If they believe their concern has not been addressed fairly, they can then arrange a meeting with the Dean of the School of Humanities Arts and Social Sciences (HASS), Dr. Mary Simoni.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
