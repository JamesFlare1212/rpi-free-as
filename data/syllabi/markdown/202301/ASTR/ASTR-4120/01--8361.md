## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

An introduction to astronomical observing techniques and instrumentation. Optical telescope design. Observatory site selection. Telescopes above the atmosphere. Imaging techniques: photography, charge-coupled devices. Optical photometry, spectroscopy, and polarimetry, and their applications. Infrared techniques and applications. Radio astronomy. Includes evening laboratory sessions. This 4000-level class is appropriate for graduate students and upper-level undergraduate students.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

**Required Text:** To Measure the Sky, by Chromey, second edition

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Course Description:** This course will introduce students to techniques used in modern observational astronomy research. We will practice these techniques on existing datatsets, and obtain our own data with the Hirsch Observatory 16” telescope. Students will carry out their own small research project and produce a report in an astronomy journal format. After taking this class, students will be prepared to carry out undergraduate or graduate research projects in observational astronomy. In particular, they will be able to reduce simple optical CCD images and spectra, and extract data from common on-line databases. They will have a working knowledge of LaTex, AstroPy and Stellarium.

**Course Themes:** Astronomical coordinate systems, Optical telescopes and detectors, Data reduction techniques, Photometric calibration of images, Wavelength calibration of spectra, Astronomical observations in other regions of the spectrum**.**

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to compose a descriptive astronomy-style research report in LaTex, with graphs created using Python.
- Students will be able to describe optical telescopes and detectors using common vocabulary.
- Students will be able to interpret the reliability of data using statistical techniques.
- Students will understand coordinate systems and observational constraints, allowing them to plan astronomical observations.
- Students will be able to generate imaging data using a telescope and CCD camera.
- They will be able to correct raw astronomical data (images and spectroscopy) using standard analysis techniques in AstroPy.
- Students will be able to calibrate photometric and spectroscopic data from optical telescopes.
- Students will be able to query on-line astronomical databases using SQL.

### Other Course Policies:

Homework, projects, and final projects will be due according the timeline below. Students are encouraged to discuss homework assignments with each other, but the work that is turned in must be the student’s original work. ***Submitted homework must clearly show the steps leading up to each answer. Solutions that can only be understood by looking at the methods on another student’s paper will be evaluated for academic dishonesty.***

Data-taking for observational projects should be done with a partner. However, each person should analyze the data separately and write up their own research report. Students can compare results, but the submitted project should be the result of the submitting student’s own analysis and writing.

Normally, the Tuesday class will typically be in a lecture format. The Friday class is typically an activity format, and **laptops are required**.

Requests for re-grades should first be made in writing to the teaching assistant. Prof. Newberg will decide grading in cases where the issue is not resolved with the TA.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students who cheat will receive a failing grade for the course. Cheating includes: copying, or allowing another student to copy, a test or assignment; altering a graded test or assignment before requesting regrading; copying submitted solutions from published solution manuals or previous years; using materials other than those that are allowed on an exam; misrepresenting the work of another group or individual as your own.
