### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

The course is conducted with full commitment to the principles of academic integrity built upon trust in student-teacher relationships. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate who the student collaborated with. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a grade penalty and/or academic sanctions.

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational process. The *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* (https://rpi.app.box.com/s/d4ndpn3f7qdwxh9jaz5n1hkkr0mnd25e) defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

Examples of activities that are considered an infringement of academic integrity for the purposes of this course include but are not limited to the following:

- Academic Fraud – The alteration of documentation relating to the grading process.
- Collaboration – Deliberate facilitation of academic dishonesty in any form. For example, allowing another student to observe an exam paper or allowing another student to “recycle” one’s old term paper.
- Copying – Obtaining information pertaining to a graded exercise by deliberately observing the paper of another student.
- Cribbing – Use or attempted use of prohibited materials, information, or study aids in an academic exercise.
- Fabrication – Unauthorized falsification or invention of any information in an academic exercise.
- Plagiarism – Representing the work or words of another as one’s own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference.
- Sabotage – Destruction of another student’s work.
- Substitution – Utilizing a proxy, or acting as a proxy, in any academic exercise

If there is reason to believe a student in this course may have been involved in academic dishonesty, the instructor will contact the student(s) and schedule a meeting to discuss the allegations. When meeting with the student, the instructor will review the circumstances and evidence related to the suspicion of academic dishonesty and allow him or her the opportunity to provide his or her perspective on the situation. If it is concluded that the student has violated the Institute academic dishonesty policy, the instructor will determine the appropriate academic (grade) penalty and will communicate this decision to the student in writing. In this course, penalties for academic dishonesty are as follows:

- For a first, minor violation – The student will be given a warning and reminded of the types of activities that are considered a violation of academic integrity.
- For a second offense – The student will be given a grade of “F” or zero on the assignment in question.
- For a third offense – The student will be given a failing grade for the course.

In cases where students are found to be in violation of the academic integrity policy, the written decision of the instructor will be copied to the Senior Judicial Administrator in the Dean of Students Office, along with a brief summary of the case facts and a copy of any supporting documentation (i.e., exams/assignments involving cheating, crib sheets, witness statements, etc.). A copy of the incident of academic dishonesty will be kept on file in the Dean of Students Office. If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
