## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

“Works of architecture embody knowledge, ideas, and imagination that express formally, spatially, and materially the ways of living and values of the civilizations in which they occur. A select number of modern and contemporary buildings that represent a high degree of sophistication in the way their architects have approached the breadth of design issues both within and external to the programs of those works will be analyzed critically in order to ascertain the significance and relationships of the multiple systems of order inherent to a work of architecture. Reflecting on the knowledge and understandings acquired in all the previous courses in the history, theory, and criticism sequence, this course is a critical inquiry into the principal ideologies and premises of the most substantive architectural practices in the contemporary world. As such, the content of the course must necessarily evolve as the intellectual and cultural parameters of both theory and practice in the contemporary world change. This course is required of all architecture graduate students in the M.Arch. program. Prerequisites/Corequisites: Prerequisite: ARCH 5110.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

This course will cover:

1. Vocabulary for understanding urban and landscape theories and practical knowledge for application in the field.
2. Historical trajectories of urbanism, including comparative studies of similarity and difference, and case studies of specific urban conditions in history; as well as the influence of the design professions within these urban morphologies.

3\. Contemporary urban design strategies for architects to consider when dealing with 21st century urban scenarios, especially those involving landscapes, urbanism, social justice, and the environment.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Career Paths (PC.1): Students will be able to understand the paths to a range of available career opportunities that utilize the discipline’s skills and knowledge, especially pertaining to Landscape Architecture and Urban Design.
- Ecological Knowledge and Responsibility (PC.3): Students will be able to understand the dynamic between built and natural environments, paths to mitigating climate change, professional responsibility to ecological, advanced building performance, adaptation, and resilience principles in architectural projects and advocacy activities.
- History and Theory (PC. 4): Students will be able to understand the histories and theories of architecture and urbanism, framed by diverse social, cultural, economic, and political forces.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic Integrity

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process. Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust will merit a failing grade in this course and the violator(s) will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.

Writing Center

It is strongly recommended that students with difficulties in writing or expressing thoughts in written form, set up an appointment with the RPI Writing Center as early as possible in the term. Summaries with major problems with sentence structure, grammar and spelling will be returned to the student for a redo.

Essay Formatting

All essays are due the Monday before the following lecture, by 8:00 PM. Any late assignments will automatically be reduced to a score of zero.

Essays must be submitted in the following format:

“LAST NAME_FIRST INITIAL_ESSAY ##.PDF”

Example:

“BENNETT_C_ESSAY 03.PDF”

All essays must be submitted in PDF format on LMS for Grading.

Single-spaced text in Calibri, Arial, or other approved font. Font sizes 10- 12pt are acceptable.

Essays must be 1-page in length. Your Writing must focus on a critical analysis of the texts. Quotes and re-quoting of text or others should be kept to a minimum, no more than 10-20% of the entire essay (with proper Chicago-style citation of the quoted or paraphrased authors). The essay must focus its attention on the readings assigned for that week, other outside sources may be brought in, but only as secondary information to the main discussion.

Please note: Essays submitted late will be marked as 0 (zero).

Essays: Grade Evaluation Criteria

All Reading Responses are graded on a 2-point scale:

1 point = proper use of citations (in text with associated endnotes)

*ALL of the week’s required readings must be addressed and properly cited in the essay

1 point = writing is a critical analysis (not a summary of the texts)

0 points = late submission or no submission

Half or partial points may be awarded in essays that do not fully meet the citation requirement or critical analysis requirement.
