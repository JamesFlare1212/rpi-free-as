## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Amid an industrialized and growing world, there are movements in architecture to create buildings that, by their form, materials, orientation, and arrangement, make use of the energy and materials available on a site to achieve environmental design goals.

This course addresses fundamental principles relating the environmental context to human needs and comfort. Topics include the principles of thermodynamics at the architectural scale, optimization vs variability, principles of building sites and orientations, psychometrics, and human comfort and an introduction to energy modeling.

Energy, Comfort and Ecology is the first course in the environmental systems series and will lay the foundational concepts to be expanded on in Environmental and Ecological Systems (EES) and Building Systems and Environment (BSE). This course is required of all students in the B.Arch. degree program.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

By the successful completion of this course, students will:

- Exhibit awareness of interrelationships within and between ecological systems
- Understand buildings as entities with multi-scaler energetic, material, and ecological territories
- Understand key principles impacting human comfort and thermal delight
- Demonstrate an understanding of the principles underlying environmental systems design, how design criteria can vary by geographic region, and the tools used for performance assessment.
- Use climate information for a particular location to quantify solar, wind, and daylighting resources
