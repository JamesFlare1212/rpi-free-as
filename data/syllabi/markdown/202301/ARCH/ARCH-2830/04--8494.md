### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**1. Architectural Documentation Skills** – Students will demonstrate ability to accurately construct a  
series of graphically refined two-dimensional drawings of an existing building from reference  
drawings.  
**2. Architectural Analysis Skills** – Students will demonstrate ability to communicate an analytical  
reading of an existing building in a series of clear graphically annotated diagrams.  
**3. Site Analysis Skills** – Students will demonstrate ability to understand and document site  
context in terms of the architectural qualities, configuration, and use of surrounding buildings;  
transportation infrastructures; zoning regulations; and solar orientation.  
**4. Generative Modeling and Drawing** – Students will demonstrate ability to think abstractly and  
imaginatively in two and three dimensions and will demonstrate how abstraction informs an  
architectural parti.  
**5. Programming and Massing** – Students will demonstrate ability to understand and organize an  
architectural program and show how it informs building massing situated on site.  
**6. Zoning** – Students will demonstrate how their design meets City of Troy zoning requirements.  
**7. Solar Orientation** – Students will demonstrate how their design is oriented to solar exposure and  
how the design responds to direct sunlight.  
**8. Multi-Unit Housing Design** – Students will demonstrate ability to design different types of  
housing units in terms of their interior configurations and in terms of how units relate to the  
building as a whole. Students must show how individual units aggregate, relate to structural and  
plumbing systems, light and air requirements, circulation, and egress.  
**9. Architectural Assemblies** – Through detailed drawings and models, students will demonstrate  
ability to understand and design realistic structural thicknesses and assemblies of wall, floor,  
and roof components.  
**10. Two-dimensional Orthographic Drawing** – Students will demonstrate ability to clearly represent  
architecture and accurately design a building at a range of scales from site to architectural  
assembly through the use of two-dimensional plans, section and elevations.  
**11. Large-scale Physical Modeling** – Students will demonstrate ability to construct a large- scale  
physical model that represents a portion of a building in detail. Students must demonstrate ability  
to bring together multiple materials and ability to skillfully craft a  
al model.  
**12. Three-dimensional Imagery** – Students must demonstrate ability to compellingly communicate conceptual and aesthetic aspects of an architectural project.  
**13. Writing** – Students must demonstrate ability to communicate an architectural agenda clearly and concisely through using writing as a complement to graphic and physical production.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

All deliverables described above must be submitted for the final review. Course grades will be determined as follows:  
\- 10% – precedent documentation and analysis  
\- 10% – site analysis  
\- 10% – generative drawings  
\- 10% – programming and massing  
\- 20% – floor plans, sections, and elevations  
\- 15% – unit design and detail chunk model  
\- 10% – three-dimensional imagery  
\- 05% – project manifesto  
\- 10% – peer grading (peer grades will be submitted to section leaders weekly)  
Grades will be established according to the following policy:  
\- A (EXCELLENT) Exceptional performance strongly exceeding the requirements of the course, showing strong academic initiative and independent resourcefulness.  
\- B (GOOD) Performance above the norm; accurate and complete; beyond the minimum requirements of the course; work demonstrates marked progress and initiative.  
\- C (AVERAGE) Satisfactory work that adequately meets minimum requirements and demonstrates satisfactory comprehension, communication skills, and effort; demonstrates little initiative to investigate the problem without substantial input from the instructor; work shows little improvement.  
\- D (INFERIOR) Unsatisfactorily meets minimum requirements; demonstrates minimum comprehension, communication skills, and effort at an inferior level; initiative lacking; improvement not noticeable. (D grades not available to graduate students).  
\- F (FAIL) Does not meet minimum requirements; fails to adequately demonstrate comprehension, communication skills, and effort.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is required at all class meetings during the semester. Two (2) unexcused absences from class will result in the lowering of the grade by one (1) letter. Four (4) unexcused absences from class during the term result in an automatic F. Whether an absence (health, immediate family death, officially approved field / sport trip, etc.) is excused or unexcused is determined by the Students Experience Office (4th Floor Academy Hall, x8022, se@rpi.edu), upon presentation of required documentation, according to established policies and procedures (see http:// http://se.rpi.edu/policies/ea/).  
Students are required to work in studio during studio hours, and have the required materials and basic tools to develop the assignments. Work includes participation in site visits, design and development, pinups, presentations, discussions and field trips. Failure to work in class with undivided attention and disruptive behavior (tardiness, leaving early, lack of participation, general socializing, etc.), as well working on assignments from other classes during class, will count as absences.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
