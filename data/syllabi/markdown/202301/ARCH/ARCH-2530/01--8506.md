## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This second course in the Digital Constructs sequence will focus on refining descriptive and speculative drawing skills. Drawing and visualization will continue to be discussed as the medium through which architects generate and express forms and concepts. Work will be done in two and three dimensions, in vector and raster formats, with a range of software packages. The semester will begin by documenting an architectural stair, which will be subsequently built into intricate, complex structures with particular aesthetic characteristics. These structures will then be visualized to gauge their potential architectural affect. Class meetings will incorporate critique, tutorials and working sessions. The semester is organized into three phases that will build on each other.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Project 1** – Documentation and Advanced Modeling

Project 1 will build on two and three-dimensional drawing skills acquired in Digital Constructs 1. It will start by documenting an architectural stair. The stair will be drawn to scale in a related series of orthographic drawings. The drawings will then be used to construct a detailed three-dimensional model. Every detail of the stair, down to the smallest bolt, must be drawn and modeled. The three-dimensional model will be represented in an exploded axonometric drawing.

**Project 2** – Associative Modeling and Speculative Structures

Project 2 will introduce associative modeling and computational drawing through Grasshopper. Students will learn to associate elements of geometry, operate on them parametrically, and then through multiplication, transformation and other technical opportunities presented by Grasshopper, develop a series of speculative drawings based on documentary drawings done in Project 1. By customizing Grasshopper templates presented in tutorials, students will begin to author their own drawing procedures. The final drawing for Project 2 will be a multi-view composite. Moving between two and three dimensions will be discussed as a fundamental problem/opportunity within the discipline and discussed from a technical standpoint through workflows between Rhino, Illustrator and Photoshop.

**Project 3** – Portfolio

Project 3 will develop a portfolio of all Digital Construct 1 and 2 work. The portfolio will be discussed as a vehicle through which one’s work can be framed, focused and refined. The final multi view drawing from Project 2 will continue to be refined in conjunction with portfolio development. This drawing must demonstrate mastery of the range of techniques introduced during the semester. Final deliverable for the course will a portfolio including all work done in Digital Constructs 1 and 2.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Ability to read and understand architectural drawings – Students will demonstrate ability to read and understand architectural drawings of an existing stair, how various components of the stair come together and how the stair meets ergonomic and safety requirements.
- 2\. Ability to draw architectural components at scale – Students will demonstrate ability to make detailed and accurate orthographic plan, section, and elevations of an existing architectural stair, and output the drawings at different architectural scales.
- 3\. Ability to digitally model architectural components at scale – Students will demonstrate ability to make a detailed and accurate three-dimensional digital model of an existing architectural stair, and output three-dimensional drawing and images using the model.
- 4\. Ability to create an exploded analytical drawing – Students will demonstrate ability to create an exploded analytical drawing of a stair that shows how all architectural componentry relates and fits together.
- 5\. Ability to use a Grasshopper template to produce speculative drawings – Students will demonstrate an understanding of Grasshopper fundamentals and an ability to use and adapt a given Grasshopper template to author unique speculative drawings of the stair.
- 6\. Ability to create a simple portfolio of work – Students will demonstrate ability to layout images and text in a simple portfolio containing all Digital Constructs 1 and 2 work.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- Students will receive, on a weekly basis, critical feedback and direction in the form of collective or individual critiques. Students are expected to respond to feedback by proactively reworking various aspects of their projects.
- Students will receive the opportunity to present ideas to external experts, who will provide critical feedback to students.
- Students will be introduced to comparative theories and concepts related to the content of the course.
- Students will receive two major reviews at the midterm and end of the term. For both reviews, students will receive feedback on their progress and accomplishments.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Assessment**

Grades will be based upon the quality of drawings and models produced as well as attendance and participation in class discussions.

- Project 1: 25% – quality and completion of Project 1 drawings
- Project 2: 25% – quality and completion of Project 2 drawings
- Final Project: 20% – quality and completion of Project 3 portfolio
- Attendance and Participation: 30% – attending class every week and handing in weekly assignments

**Mid-semester evaluations for the course will be provided to each student**. Grades will be established according to the following policy:

- A (EXCELLENT) Exceptional performance strongly exceeding the requirements of the course, showing strong academic initiative and independent resourcefulness.
- B (GOOD) Performance above the norm; accurate and complete; beyond the minimum requirements of the course; work demonstrates marked progress and initiative.
- C (AVERAGE) Satisfactory work that adequately meets minimum requirements and demonstrates satisfactory comprehension, communication skills, and effort; demonstrates little initiative to investigate the problem without substantial input from the instructor; work shows little improvement.
- D (INFERIOR) Unsatisfactorily meets minimum requirements; demonstrates minimum comprehension, communication skills, and effort at an inferior level; initiative lacking; improvement not noticeable.
- F (FAIL) Does not meet minimum requirements; fails to adequately demonstrate comprehension, communication skills, and effort.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is required at all class meetings during the semester. Two (2) unexcused absences from class will result in the lowering of the grade by one (1) letter. Four (4) unexcused absences from class during the term result in an automatic F. Whether an absence (health, immediate family death, officially approved field / sport trip, etc.) is excused or unexcused is determined by the Students Experience Office (4th Floor Academy Hall, x8022, se@rpi.edu), upon presentation of required documentation, according to established policies and procedures (see http:// http://se.rpi.edu/policies/ea/). Students are required to work in class during class hours, and have the required materials and basic tools to develop the assignments. Work includes drawing design and development, learning software, pinups, presentations and discussions. Failure to work in class with undivided attention and disruptive behavior (tardiness, leaving early, lack of participation, general socializing, etc.), as well working on assignments from other classes during class, will count as absences.

For students with an ADHD diagnosis, extra time applies only to exams, and quizzes completed in class. It does not apply to projects or labs where the students are able to plan more time outside of class to complete assignments. Students are encouraged to attend faculty office hours and tutoring sessions to help plan the completion of studio, lab, or project-based assignments.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own performance. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

**Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism**

Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of this course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student. Any miss-representation of work shall be evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student will result in a failing grade for the course.
