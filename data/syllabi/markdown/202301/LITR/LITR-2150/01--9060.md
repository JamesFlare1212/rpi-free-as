## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Given the polarized and vitriolic political landscape of America in 2022, people from both sides

of the aisle are passionately impelled to speak up in support of their beliefs and agendas.

Couple this vocal exigency with the myriad means available to make oneself heard afforded by

social media platforms, YouTube channels, and podcasts. Throw in the Zeitgeist-y and

pervasive influence of TED talks which have told us that our simple vernacular voices are the

best means to convey an important “idea worth spreading,” and logic would dictate that we

should be uniquely situated at this moment in American history to enjoy equity of voice as never

before. And while a superficial perusal of Twitter and “Insta” might lead one to believe this to be

true, having one’s voice be heard and counted remains absolutely contingent on questions of

privilege. This course will thus examine the politics of race, class, gender, sexuality, and

citizenship through the lens of voice and language. Using modern and contemporary literature, critical theory,contemporary essays, and an array of TED talks, we will unpack and problematize the notion of articulacy, the voice inherent to silence, and the #ownvoices movement on Twitter. We will also consider the “wholeness’ of purported “broken” English, invoke and celebrate the palimpsestic return of once subsumed and appropriated marginal voices.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Breakdown of Grading

Writing Responses/Creative Other Medium Responses (WR on Syllabus) 25 %

Over the semester, you will be asked to compose 4 short writing responses to the readings under study (WR’s). There will be 10 WR prompts offered; just make sure you have completed at least one by October 11th and all 4 by the deadline articulated in the syllabus, Friday, November 18. Two of these will be formal writing assignments (you pick which ones). The other 2 may also be formal writing responses or they may take the form of more creative responses (see list below of options or configure your own creative response that you pass by me for approval) that evince critical understanding of the texts under study. All WR’s, creative or straight critical writing, must absolutely demonstrate a substantive working knowledge of the texts under study and their critical significance and should be cited (the creative options should be accompanied by written explanation. The regular WR’s should be no more or less than about 2 double spaced 12 font pages (excluding formatting), and for these as well, you can use the prompt I offer or think of a topic of your own as long as you run it by me. These submissions should be meticulous in their formatting, grammar and argument. I am happy to confer with you during office hours or by email as you shape and refine them. A particular WR must be completed the week/day it comes up on the syllabus by the beginning of class.

Options for Creative WR’s: All of these options need to be accompanied by a brief explanation of their critical relevance to the texts under study, a task that can be best accomplished through footnotes.

1)Write a poem and an analysis of that poem that articulates your critical stance on the texts under study,

2\) Create a series of 10 live tweets by one of/or between two of the characters in the texts under study. Here is the template.

3\) Write a parody of a text by an author under study

4\) Create a linked playlist of songs that a character under study might assemble given their circumstances

5\) Choose your own creative option which you pass by me.

Class Participation GCD’s and Tweet Chats 25%

At the end of each week you will be given a Graded Class Discussion grade (GCD). It will be based on your TweetChat Participation and Your GCD (Graded Class Discussions).

1. Each Tuesday we will have a Tweet Chat with 3 Questions About the Reading Due Before Class. You will need to supply (no more than) 140 word tweets for each and respond to at least 3 other student posts. These chats will occur over the 24 hours preceding the class during which the texts will be discussed. You may always repost and like other tweets but your three official responses should be substantive tweets that further the observations of other students. Have fun, use images, GIFs, and the like and be respectful of others and also conduct yourself appropriately on this platform. We will practice the logistics of tweeting and tweet chats and Tweetdeck in class. My Twitter Handle for this class is @cccjayrpi and the hashtag for the twitter chats is #modconrpi

1. Each class will run in a Socratic Seminar Fashion. I will offer some discussion points in writing on Blackboard before each class to guide your preparation. That means that you will carry the responsibility of carrying the discussion in class with my intervention being mostly to keep the conversation on track and to pose new questions. You need to participate substantively 4 x a week (in a course that meets 2x a week). You must participate at least once a class to earn weekly credit. To participate substantively means to say something about the text under discussion that demonstrates you are familiar with and thinking deeply about it. It does not mean that you have to say something so profound that it recalibrates the universe. However, credit will not be given for comments that merely riff off the observations of other students and do not demonstrate that you yourself have read and thought about the texts. Should you dislike speaking in class, I am going to urge you gently but compassionately to move past this fear as speaking aloud and extemporaneously is a life skill you will need going forward. If you are truly very afraid of public speaking, please get in touch with me, and let’s talk and work together on this.

You are welcome to speak up far more than 4x in a week and thereby earn extra credit!

3\) Group Presentations 25%

There will be two presentation assignments (topics are provided below) which you will be assigned to complete in groups. Both will include an oral and visual presentation as well as a writing component.

4\) Final Paper and Presentation 25%

Your final project is to participate in our 2 class period mini-conference on issues of voice in Contemporary America. Having had your topic approved mid-semester, you will submit on the due date, an installation piece that offers a visual spectacle of your topic. You will be asked to present on your topic to the class for 5 minutes in a TED Talk style (we will discuss this more), using one other visual or aural element beyond your installation piece. You will also submit a final 4-6 page paper documenting your topic and the way in which you have critically unpacked it.

Absences

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
