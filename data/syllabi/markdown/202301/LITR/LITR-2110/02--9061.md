## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Upon successful completion of the course, students will: • recognize and appreciate how the humanities can inform and enrich scientific and technological perspectives
- read and analyze selected short fiction and poetry representing a range of perspectives on literature and culture
- develop analytical and interpretive skills and vocabularies, including both textual and contextual analysis,
- hone their personal communication skills through exploration and discussion of complex social and cultural issues as well as historical contexts
- develop basic writing and speaking skills in the formulation of arguments based on literary texts, including analyzing audiences and purposes; focusing on specific topics or ideas; stating claims, with main ideas first; identifying and using appropriate warrants and supporting data; developing logical patterns of organization; and writing and speaking in standard English
- participate in a learning community through feedback, encouragement, and accountability

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Participation (worth 20%)**

Be on time, well prepared, and present in mind throughout the duration of our class meetings. Contribute consistently, actively and meaningfully to our discussion. Often, we will do short writing exercises and group work in class. The spirit of our meeting is collaborative rather than competitive.

- It is your responsibility to have the assigned reading materials with you in class (either paper copy or digital copy on a device other than a cellphone).
- Avoid showing up late or leaving early. Whenever you come to class after I take the roll, it is your responsibility to make sure your presence is recorded by me at the end of the class session.
- Using your cellphone during class, even if only to ‘check the time,’ is prohibited and will impact your participation grade.

Grading: At the end of each class session, I will make a note (either check, check +, or check -) in terms of each student’s contribution to the class. Full credit will receive a student who regularly attends on time, brings the assigned reading to class, is always well-prepared, focused and engaged in our conversation, and who participates actively, positively, and respectfully.

**Introduc****ing** **the Literary Work** **(worth** **5****%****)**

Together with classmate, you will lead our class discussion once during the semester. The two of you will adopt the text portion for the day, close-read 5-7 short passages (take us to a relevant quote, read it out loud, comment on word use, determine its significance in context and to the work as a whole), possibly draw connections to other course texts, and prepare five to seven discussion questions. (The emphasis of this assignment is on the quote analysis, and not on plot, author, or literary/historical context. If you choose to include plot, biographical and other contextual information, keep it very brief.)

Prepare some engaging slides to help visualize your close-reading. Upload your presentation in .pdf format to LMS *before* our meeting (at the latest). Your speaking time should be roughly 20 minutes (excluding the ensuing class discussion) and should be balanced between the group members. Both of you should participate equally in the preparation and presentation of this assignment. A group member who does not contribute equitably to the assignment may be dismissed from the group. If your group consults other works (books, websites, etc.) to prepare, you must include a list of works cited.

There will be a sign-up sheet in class. Pick your slot until the end of week 3 (1/26). First come/first served.

**Discussion** **Board** **(worth** **1****5****%)**

Some of our discussion will happen via weekly discussion boards on Blackboard LMS. In advance of many classes, you will submit short responses to the assigned portion of the fictional, poetic, or dramatic text(s) we will discuss during the upcoming meeting. If several texts are assigned, it’s your choice whether you want to comment on several or just one of the readings. Similarly, it’s your decision whether you want to engage with an idea/quote from our textbook *Digging Into Literature* in order to discuss the literature. Your submission should be an original, focused discussion of a specific detail or short passage of the assigned text(s).

Your submission can take two forms: either you raise a new issue (start a new thread) or respond to a classmate’s submission (hit the ‘reply’ button). Of course, you can post more than once on a discussion board, but only one submission will count toward credit (your best one).

**Evaluation:**

3 points = excellent reading post; exceptional depth of discussion; response is based on a reflective analysis of course content; attempts to stimulate discussion and raise provocative questions, or makes productive comparisons; fulfills all criteria

2 points = good reading post; response shows clear critical understanding of the course content; fulfills all or almost all criteria and furthers our conversation

1 points = satisfactory reading post; fulfills many criteria, but needs, for example, more depth, focus, a quote, more efficient close reading

0 points = response engages only superficially with the course content; reading post barely meets the criteria

You need 30 points in order to earn 100% in the rubric “Discussion Board.” Overall, there are nineteen days (= opportunities to earn points) for which you could post a comment.

In order to receive one point for your Blackboard post, your submission must:

- discuss a question or an aspect of the assigned text (or texts, if applicable) in a thoughtful, analytical, interpretive, and original manner appropriate for college thinking and writing
- go into depth
- contain at least one quote (correctly formatted and with page number)
- be between 120 and 200 words, *excluding* quotes
- have a meaningful title (*not* “Post on ‘The Fall of the House of Usher’”)
- be relatively free of spelling and grammatical errors
- not be inflated by fillers and repetitions (it’s already a very short assignment)
- be submitted by noon *before* our class meeting on which we will discuss the assigned text(s)

Avoid coarse language, plot summary (e.g. *what is happening in the story*), personal evaluations (e.g. *I like, I didn’t like*), and opinions not supported by the text. Every claim we make about a text must be supported with evidence (= quote).

**T****hree Short Papers** **(each worth 20%)**

Students will write three short papers in which they engage critically and creatively with the course materials. All three papers involve the submission of a first draft and feedback from the instructor and/or peers. Specific prompts will be posted and due dates appear on the course schedule. All first drafts must be substantially revised based on the student’s evolving engagement with the course as well as their peers’ and the instructor’s feedback. Each piece will be evaluated based on *how well* it executes the assignment requirements as well as the extent to which the writer has demonstrated a commitment to the revision process.

Do not:

- submit a first draft of a writing assignment as your final draft
- only make minimal changes
- disregard the comments your reviewers make

**Submission of Assignments**

All written assignments must follow MLA formatting (double-spaced, 1-inch margin on all sides, header, page numbers, 12pt. Times New Roman or comparable font, no extraneous spacing, etc.). Here’s an MLA sample paper: [https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/mla_style/mla_formatting_and_style_guide/mla_sample_paper.html](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/mla_style/mla_formatting_and_style_guide/mla_sample_paper.html).

All written assignments will be submitted via Blackboard LMS. Do not email me your assignment, unless we’ve made such arrangements. Submissions are only acceptable as a .pdf.

Please polish and proofread all typed work before you submit. **You are responsible for ensuring your work is turned in appropriately and legibly. Illegible or blank assignments turned in** **won’t** **count as submitted, so check your submissions.**

**Late Policy**

Deadlines ensure that your work and my work remains manageable. Try to always submit assignments on time. The discussion posts and the collaborative oral presentation (Introducing the Literary Work) must be submitted by the due date in order to receive credit. For these assignments, late submissions won’t be considered. For the papers (first and final drafts) a grace period of 24 hours applies. Beyond the grace period, I will only accept your late draft if you requested and were granted an extension in advance, that is, before the due date or within the grace period. I cannot grant an extension on the final draft of our third paper.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**A****cademic Integrity**  
Student-instructor relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that instructors have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you are responsible for becoming familiar with these. Some examples of plagiarism include copying, paraphrasing, or summarizing without acknowledgment, submission of another student's work as one's own, the purchase/use of prepared research or completed papers or projects, and the unacknowledged use of research sources gathered by someone else. Failure to indicate accurately the extent and precise nature of one's reliance on other sources is also a form of plagiarism. Students are responsible for understanding legitimate use of sources, the appropriate ways of acknowledging academic, scholarly, or creative indebtedness.

All assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work completed for this class in particular. In cases where help was received or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. If you have a compelling reason for wanting to use previous work of yours for the class, it must be substantially and fundamentally revised, and you must get approval from the instructor first.  
Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty of a zero for the assignment and the potential to fail the course. Second offenses will result in automatic failure of the course and the potential for disciplinary action by the institution as the student will be reported to the Dean of Students as appropriate. If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

For more information, see Student Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs, [https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs), and consult *The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities*.
