## Course Goals

### Goals:

The objective of this course is to develop a working knowledge of the basic principles of structural behavior, modeling, and analysis. Students will do this by building on the knowledge gained through statics (ENGR 1100) and strength of materials (ENGR 2530).

Students will gain a basic understanding of:

- structural elements and systems, including structural framing, load paths, and anticipated structural behavior
- typical structural loads
- commercial structural analysis tools
- the relationship between mathematical and physical representations of structural elements and systems

Upon successful completion of this course, students will have the necessary skills to take senior-level structural engineering courses such as Matrix Structural Analysis (CIVL 4440), Steel Design (CIVL 4070), Concrete Design (CIVL 4080) and graduate courses such as Structural Dynamics (CIVL 6450), Wind Engineering (CIVL 6390) and Earthquake Engineering (CIVL 6490).  
The secondary objective of the course is to explore the role of the structural engineer. Students will be exposed to career options, hear from practicing engineers, and reflect on the relationship between structural engineering and equity in the built environment.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Sketch deformed shapes of determinate and indeterminate structures and compute or estimate internal force and moment distributions
- Compute (by hand and via software) external reactions, internal forces, and deflections for statically determinate and indeterminate structures, and plot internal force and moment diagrams
- Construct unit influence lines for statically determinate structures and utilize them to predict maximum load effects on structures
- Identify the response form of structural elements: axial (tension/compression) and/or bending
- Explain the importance of and determine load paths through a structure
- Critique the responsibility of civil engineers to ensure equity in infrastructure

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students will have the opportunity to demonstrate understanding of the course material through participation in lectures and in-class activities, homework assignments, lab reports, two midterm exams, a final exam, and a final project.

Students are expected to attend all classes. Full participation in the class, including asking and answering questions, is likely to be strongly correlated with final grades. Students are strongly encouraged to attend the instructor’s and/or TA’s office hours.  
All classes will be held per the RPI Academic Calendar and the final exam will be given as scheduled by the Registrar. Please do not ask the instructor to accommodate your personal schedule. Exceptions will be made only for students with a documented excuse that is obtained through the Office of Student Success (4600 Academy Hall, x 8022).  
There will be four lab sessions held throughout the semester, as highlighted in the course schedule. Students are expected to arrive promptly in the Structures Laboratory (JEC1042F) during their assigned time on designated lab weeks. HOMEWORK POLICY  
Submitted homework should be presented in a professional format (i.e., legible work that is presented in a logical order such that it can be readily followed). See detailed "Homework Requirements" at the end of the syllabus. If a problem is illegible or is not presented in a logical order that can be readily followed or does not substantially follow the guidelines in the "Homework Requirements", the problem will be regarded as not completed and will be assigned a grade of zero.

Homework submissions are due at the beginning of class (12:00pm) on Tuesdays. Late homework will be penalized by 10% each day and will not be accepted after 12:00pm on Fridays. The penalty applies to late portions only, so students should submit whatever work they have completed by the original due date/time. Students who have a valid reason for not submitting the homework on time should contact the instructor.  
Students are *strongly* encouraged to solve the homework problems through collaboration with classmates (after trying the problems themselves); however, each student must turn in their own work. Copying among students is not acceptable and if it occurs, all students involved will be assigned a grade of zero for the assignment.
