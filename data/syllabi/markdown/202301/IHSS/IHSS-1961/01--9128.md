## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course bridges multilingual students’ writing skills between high school level writing and college level and professional writing. Through genre-based, audience-centered approaches, the course cultivates communication skills of reading, writing, speaking, and critical thinking on topics such as current events, Englishes used in diverse sociolinguistic contexts globally, cultural diversity, and cross-cultural communication. Students learn structures and organization of academic writing, visual design of professional presentations, and academic writing conventions. Coupled along with the IHSS Mentor Program, the goal of this course is for students to gain communication skills for academic and professional contexts, identify and critically analyze local, regional, and global issues from multiple perspectives and deliver audience-appropriate proposals and problem-solving solutions.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- By the end of this course, students will be able to: Analyze academic texts in terms of audience, purpose, and genre Summarize and critically engage with academic text at the level of ideas Understand and use written and spoken academic structures and conventions to produce texts for global academic audiences Engage in the processes of academic writing and presentations including reading, outlining, drafting, peer review and editing Work collaboratively with classmates in the processes of writing, and presenting Find information from appropriate sources, understand these source and integrate these sources into writing and original ideas through the use of summary, paraphrase, quotation, and reflection Practice academic citation conventions and referencing

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1. **Argument Essay (5 Paragraph Essay)- 10%**

During the first week of class students will be asked to complete a 5 paragraph essay within the class period on the topic of an article that will be read together in class. During the first unit of the class, students will learn/review the basic structures and rules for writing an academic essay and edit their original essay to comply with these rules.

2. **Article Summaries and Analysis 1****5****%**

Students will select 2 of the articles provided by the instructor. Students will summarize the articles, analyze the articles according to the given guidelines, and write a short reflection on the content.

3. **Outline and Annotated Bibliography for the Research Paper- 15%**

Students will create an outline for a final research paper and an annotated bibliography for the paper using good summary techniques and correct APA citation style.

4. **Presentation-15%**

Students will give an academic presentation of their final research paper.

5. **Paper Proposal- 5%**

Students will write a short proposal for their academic research paper.

6. **Academic Research Paper- 20%**

Students will write a 5-7 page academic sourced research paper on a topic approved by the instructor. The students will write 3 drafts of the paper. The first draft will be graded for completion as a homework assignment and will be used for peer review in class. The second and third draft will both be graded and will both contribute to the final score of the paper.

7. **Plagiarism Quiz- 5%**

Students will take a quiz related to plagiarism and APA citation in class.

8. **Homework- 10%**

Periodic homework assignments will be given throughout the course.

9. **Participation- 10%**

Students will be expected to attend and actively participate in class throughout the semester. Students may lose points in this category if they do not attend class or attend class but are unengaged and do not participate.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance Policy**

Students are expected to attend on time and actively participate in all classes. As students cannot participate well in class without being present, students will be expected to be physically in class and participate actively to receive full points for participation.

If a student is more than 20 minutes late to class, they will be considered absent for the day. However, if a student is less than 20 minutes late, they will be considered late. **3 lates are equal to one absence**. If a student has come in late, they MUST talk to me at the end of class in order to update their attendance; otherwise, they may be considered absent for the day.

Two unexcused absences will be allowed during the semester without any affect on the participation grade. However, if there is a quiz, presentation, or in-class homework assignment on the day of an absence, it cannot be made up, and the assignment will receive a zero score.

Absences can be excused for the reasons listed on RPI’s Student Success Website: https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/current-students/academic-and-personal-support/requesting-excused-absence with the permission of the instructor. However, students must provide the correct documentation to the instructor and receive the instructor’s permission in order for the absence to be excused. In the case of an excused absence, students will be permitted to make up assignments within three weeks of the absence or by the end of the semester, whichever comes first. (Extensions will be granted in extreme cases such as prolonged hospital stays.)

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. In cases in which outside sources are used, students are expected to paraphrase, summarize, or quote and cite these sources properly following the citation style introduced in the class. Plagiarism will not be tolerated. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty. If found in violation of the academic honesty policy, students may be subject to two types of penalty: The instructor administers an academic [grade] penalty and the student is reported to the Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education as appropriate. The first violation results in 0 grade for that assignment. The second violation results in failure of the course. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
