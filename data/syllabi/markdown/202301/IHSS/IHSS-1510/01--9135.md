## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

We will not use a textbook in the course but journal articles and excerpts from books.

All assigned readings are selected primary and secondary sources which will be available on the class LMS site as we go along.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

A practical objective is to improve students’ oral and written communication based on reading, writing, in essay questions and a term paper, class presentations, and on class discussion.

Discussion should be based on listening and on trying to understand (not necessarily agree with) another point of view and acknowledge the value of different perspectives for critical thinking.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

## Major topics that we will address in the course:

\- War and Violence in human society and history.

\- Different cultures and different ‘ways of war’.

\- The correlations between conquest, culture, and military technology.

\- The notion of “just war”.

\- The relations between war and the state.

\- The relations between war and the nation.

\- Differences and similarities between different types of war.

\- The militarization of society.

\- War and sports

\- Rituals and the military.

\- War and technology

\- Empires and clash of civilizations.

\- Reactions to conquest.

\- War and religion

\- War and terrorism.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- \- Better understand the interactions between war, culture, and society.
- \- Appreciate the wide range of perspectives on the nature and functions of war.
- \- Identify ways in which war engages with society.
- \- Relate class material to contemporary ethical or cultural issues.
- \- Gain a basic understanding of some key concepts and theories in social sciences and the ability to apply them to present-day debates.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The requirements for this course include three different assignments:  
\- Summaries  
Summaries are extremely important in this course.  
\- Class Presentations  
Each student will conduct some original research on a theme of her/his choice  
\- Three Exams

The instructor will provide feedback on how you are doing:  
As students report on the progress of their fieldwork, they will get help on how to organize their presentation and paper.  
Each exam will be returned with comments on both the presentation / organization and content.  
A list of concepts and institutions studied in class is available to students (on the LMS site) before each exam.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grading is based on a total of 100 points (100%).

\- Exams: 60 points (20 points each).

\- Presentation / Research Paper: 15 points.

\- Summaries: 25 points ( 25 classes)

Participation in class and good summaries will earn bonus points.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
