## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

In History of Hip Hop, students will examine the development of Hip-Hop music and culture through an in-depth survey of its cultural artifacts, its changing political valence, and its place in broader American society. In order to understand the innovations of Hip-Hop culture, students will interrogate the economic, racial, societal and artistic conditions that led to its rise and track its evolution from the B-boys and B-girls of the Bronx in the 70s through its surge to national popularity in the 80s, the contentious culture wars around violence and misogyny in Gangsta Rap in the 90s, and its cultural undertaking and vehicle for radical creativity, self-expression, and protest today. Through listening assignments, scholarly and journalistic readings, written responses and critiques and group projects, students will gain a deep knowledge and understanding of the most significant musical genre of the last 50 years. This course satisfies the communication intensive requirement.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

In this course, students will learn to:

- \- Analyze the social, historical, and political contexts of Hip-Hop music and culture, with a focus on understanding its communicative potential and its ability to articulate individual and group identities.
- \- Form and articulate clear and well-informed arguments through bi-weekly listening, journal assignments and short aural presentations, formal written responses, and verbal dialogue.
- \- Write with clarity, precision, and intellectual rigor about popular music and broad cultural, technological, social, and political trends.
- \- Identify how extra-linguistic signs (sound, style, persona, etc.) can enhance interpersonal communication and community formation.
- \- Analyze specific communication situations, including audiences and purposes for effective communication.
- \- Communicate effectively in weekly journals, reading responses, analysis papers, in-class discussion and oral presentations.
- \- Identify and apply appropriate genre and design conventions, such as language, images, graphics, typography, organization, and tone.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

***Course Assessment Measures and Grading Criteria***

**Class Participation – 10%** In order to take full advantage of the opportunities offered by this course, active and enthusiastic participation in class discussions and activities is necessary. Students are encouraged to ask questions, express opinions, and support similar participation from their peers.

**Oral Presentations – 15%** Students are responsible for biweekly oral presentations. Presentations should be at most five minutes in length and should be reflective of the assigned prompt. Students can find all prompts in the Course Content folder of LMS.

**Concert Reviews – 15%** Students will be responsible for watching or attending five concerts before the end of the semester. Reviews should be two pages long, double-spaced in 12pt—Calibri font.

**Quizzes – 20%** Four in-class quizzes are given throughout the semester. These quizzes will test the familiarity with the people, groups, terms, texts, and music covered in class as well as the ability to articulate and support arguments.

**Midterm Paper – 20%** An essay of 1500 words to be submitted in MLA format. This is a chance to synthesize your knowledge from the class with your own original thoughts and insights, and to do more detailed listening on a piece or pieces of music of your choice. The paper should be submitted in PDF format through LMS.

**Final Paper or Projects – 20%** The essay of approximately 2000 words in MLA format will be due at the end of the course and will be similar in scope to the midterm paper. Students will have the option of doing a creative project in lieu of their final paper. All creative projects must be approved by the instructor, and guidelines for their documentation and presentation will be formulated. Creative projects will include a written portion of approximately 500 words. Papers/creative projects are to be submitted through LMS, with the writing portion in PDF, Word, or Pages format.
