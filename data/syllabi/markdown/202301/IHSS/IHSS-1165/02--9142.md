## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- After completing this course, students will be able to: 1) think critically about a range of beliefs, theories and arguments that are salient to religious traditions. 2) demonstrate their ability to evaluate, discuss and debate arguments concerning: (a) the existence of God and the meaning; (b) phenomenology and philosophy of mind; (c) the nature of faith and reason; (d) the relationship between morality and religious belief; and the meaning of life (e) the interface between religion, science ethics. 3) identify and explain key concepts, theories and thinkers that have shaped our understanding of phenomenology, existentialism and analytic discussions concerning the meaning of life. 4) effectively communicate their perspectives through essays, seminar discussions and panel presentations. 5) conduct and present their research in The Chicago Manual of Style format.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Assignments & Evaluation**

Short Essays (1000 words): For each short essay assignment, students will be given constructive feedback and comments. After submitting their essays for a preliminary review, each student will be given two weeks to edit and make changes prior to submitting their work for a final grade. Essay prompts will be centered around specific theorists/arguments/cases in Philosophy of Religion. Essays will be evaluated according to one’s ability to: (1) write clearly; (2) apply ethical theories to specific cases; (3) employ the basic skills of critical thinking; and (4) evaluate and create arguments in defense of a normative position.

Research Paper (2000 words): Students will have the opportunity to conduct their own research upon a specific topic on “Existentialism and the Meaning of Life” of their choosing. Students will decide upon and compose a short 100-word abstract detailing their research thesis; abstracts should be submitted no later than 2/13/2023. All papers will be submitted for a grade no later than 04/26/2023. Research papers will be evaluated according to one’s ability to: (1) write clearly; (2) apply the tools of critical thinking to specific cases; (3) identify and explain their research methodologies; (4) evaluate and create arguments in defense of a specific position; and (5) effectively incorporate no less than 7 academic sources/references.

Research Panel Presentation: Each student will participate in a panel discussion whereby they will have the opportunity to present their thesis, arguments and conclusions in regard to their research papers. They will also have the opportunity to engage in a dialogue with other panelists as well as field questions from the audience. Grading will be based upon one’s ability to: (1) effectively and clearly present their research and their arguments; (2) engage in panel discussion with other panelists; and (3) respond to questions from the audience.

Seminar Discussion Participation: This class is a communication intensive course, which means there will be regular seminar discussions that address specific theories, thinkers and issues that have defined the field of Philosophy of Religion. Discussion topics will be provided prior to class meetings so that students will have time to reflect upon their perspective and normative position. Participation will be evaluated based upon one’s ability to dialogue with students and articulate arguments that are relevant to the course readings.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Evaluation Breakdown:

Two short Essays: 25% each

Research Paper: 35%

Final Presentation: 15 %

**Grading Policy:**

All assignments in this course – discussions, presentations and essays – will be evaluated in light of the following criterion: (1) ability to write clearly by following grammatical rules and conforming to style guidelines of The Chicago Manual of Style; (2) ability to demonstrate critical thinking by formulating and assessing arguments; (3) ability to effectively engage and reference key articles and philosophical perspectives included in the syllabus. Late assignments will not be accepted for a grade unless: (i) a student requests an extension in advance (48 hrs. prior to the deadline); and (ii) the request concerns a legitimate issue warranting an extension.  
**Appealing Grades**  
If a student is dissatisfied with a grade for a specific assignment, they should plan to meet with me during office hours to discuss such. If they remain dissatisfied with the outcome that meeting, they should arrange a meeting with the Chair of the Cognitive Science Department, Dr. Sergei Nirenburg. Following such a meeting, if the student believes that their case has not been fairly treated, then they should arrange a meeting with the Dean of Humanities Arts and Social Sciences, Dr. Mary Simoni.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

**Academic Integrity & Plagiarism Policy:**

Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute’s commitment to thoroughness and knowledge includes the values associated with intellectual honesty. Using the words or ideas of someone else without proper acknowledgment is immoral and illegal, and will result in a grade of F for the assignment and/or the course.
