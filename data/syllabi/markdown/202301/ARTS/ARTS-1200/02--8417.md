## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course in an introductory studio course in drawing techniques and concepts. Students will develop perceptual and conceptual drawing skills through exercises, critiques, slide lectures, and strong drawing practice. This class stresses building hand-eye coordination and understanding of spatial relationships, perspective, and proportion through direct observation. Assignment subjects include still-life, architecture, live model and landscape. No prior experience is assumed or expected.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

No required texts for this course. However the following materials are required:

• Container to hold tools and supplies. Anything that will fit all of your supplies. Tool box, tote bag, whatever.

• 1 Drawing Board, with clips, big enough to accommodate 18x24 drawing paper.

• 1 Drawing Paper Pad, 18x24 for finished drawings

• 1 Newsprint Pad, 18x24 for sketches and preliminary drawings

• 1 Spiral-bound sketchbook, At least 8x10 in size

• Graphite pencils (1 of the following): 4H, 2H, H, HB, B, 2B, 4B, 6B, 8B. Best if purchased in a box set.

• 1 Box vine charcoal (soft)

• 1 Box compressed charcoal (soft)

• Charcoal pencils (soft) *optional*

• Blending stumps/ fabric squares *optional*

• 1 Pencil sharpener

• 1 Hard white Staedler eraser

• 1 Kneaded Eraser

• 1 Box Knife, preferably the kind with snap-off blades for sharpening pencils and erasers, and cutting paper

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**COURSE GOALS:**

The emphasis of the work in this class will be on the development of skills in perception, communication, and design as they relate to the medium of drawing. Students will be able to demonstrate skills in observational looking, basic handling of various drawing media and processes of image translation through skilled mark-making with an understanding of composition.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**COURSE WORK:**

Each time we meet, we will perform in-class exercises and assignments. Their will be six main projects required throughout the semester, each of which will require out-of-class work. In between assignments we will work on exercises that help develop your understanding of the concepts presented. You will be graded on both exercises and assignments. Each assignments builds on the previous one, so please do not miss any classes as there is no real way to make up for missed classwork. Be prepared to work right as soon as class begins (2:00PM). Students will be graded on their class participation, preparedness, and quality of work. Excessive absences will negatively effect your grade. Sign and date ALL of your work. Please keep all of your work throughout the semester for portfolio reviews for Midterm and Final points in the term. There will some light reading required in the course. I will provide these to your electronically.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- OUTCOMES: • Measuring techniques • Line quality • Proportional accuracy • Structural systems of various simple forms • Development of surface and texture • Translation of light information through the manipulation of value • Expanding knowledge of Art History

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Each assignment will be graded on both your effort (how much time and care given to project) and process (following instruction, demonstrating understanding of concepts, showing investigation)

Each assignment will be graded with the following criteria: (\_\_\_/100)

Effort toward accuracy (proportion, measuring): 40 points

Effort toward craftsmanship (quality of line (and/or) value/sense of light): 30 points

Effort toward composition (balance/arrangement of subjects/utilizing entire paper): 30 points

If your project is not hanging at the beginning of class on Crit day then it will be considered late. Unfinished work will not be critiqued. You will only receive a grade for finished work. Late work will suffer in grade.
