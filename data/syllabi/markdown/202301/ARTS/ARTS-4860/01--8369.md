## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Advanced Digital Imaging (cross-listed) emphasizes *concepts* as tools which supersede the technical mechanics of production. In this class, the focus is on the development of *ideas* (historical/ political/commercial etc.) versus a survey of technologies. Students will employ the skills that they have developed in both the Introduction and Intermediate Digital Imaging courses; researching how technical applications work, or how to achieve certain effects, will be done during lab time or outside of class. Students will learn how to create a conceptual framework which supports their use of digital tools and their aesthetic decisions, thus advancing their creative practice. As an advanced course, students are expected to discuss their projects, and the projects of others, with a sophistication gained from research and in-class discussions. The instructor and students will engage one another throughout the course, helping advance ideas and how they are realized in class assignments and projects. In addition to supporting the growth of a student’s individual practice, the course will rely on collaboration and skill-sharing as a way to foster community. Participation, therefore, is mandatory.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

In addition to the advancement of production skills and the familiarization with digital tools, students will develop a conceptual fluency which will aid in the generation of ideas for their final projects. Such ideas will be influenced by an awareness of traditional production techniques (composition, balance, etc.) and how they have been used and modified over time by cultural creators. Project concepts are expected to be rooted in topics such as contemporary or historical concerns (environmental, social, economic, political), or explorations of the self. Contemporary and historical digital projects, theorists, philosophers, and artists will inform a student's creative process. Presentations, project proposals, skill shares, and final projects will enhance the student's ability to communicate the framework of ideas and inspirations that have informed their projects and, ultimately, their creative practice.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

- **There are** **3 presentations** - **20% each:**

1. **Techniques in Practice**: students will be assigned to a group that will present a basic technique which they will discuss in class; in no particular order: unity, contrast, rhythm, emphasis, movement, balance, and pattern.

Students should meet in advance to coordinate a cohesive presentation and determine what aspect of the technique they will discuss, etc. (Please including me on early emails so that I am certain you are on the right path and that you’ll be in a good position to lead the class in a productive conversation.)There are many ways that a technique is defined, or aspects of how it is used, and students will choose one element of the definition (sub-definitions). Each student is responsible for presenting 3 slides:

1. a historical representation,
2. a contemporary representation
3. a personal representation

Slides 1 and 2 must include at least: one quote by an artist, philosopher, historian, or theorist, etc. (keep it short--three/four sentences-- so that you can discuss it, not just read) which you will put into context (who is the author? where is the quote from and why was it discussed? how does it relate to the topic? do you agree? etc); *and* one visual example (discuss who the creator is, the context for their work, and how you see this technique demonstrated in the work).

Slide 3 should be an image from your previous work which demonstrates how you’ve engaged with this technique: was it effective? what would you do differently? how do you hope to employ this technique in the future? Presentations will be 5-6 minutes long so please practice and use a timer if needed! It will be followed by an audience response/discussion with the presenter.

During the presentation, students in class who are not presenting will be required to write either a question or a comment for *each slide* of the presentation. Students will be chosen at random to respond to one of the slides. Note: audience engagement and eye contact during the response will be considered part of class participation; the questions and comments should demonstrate engagement (ie not “do you like it?” or “why did you do that?” or “why did you choose this quote?”).

1. **Project Proposal**: Students will be responsible for presenting three project proposals, aka proof of concept. The proposed projects will be each investigate similar ideas but should be distinctly different in form. Mock-ups must be professional and inspired (ie no last-minute doodles or ideas). For each project you must discuss:

1. What/who inspired the idea: at least one artist *and* one scholar.
2. What are three techniques that you expect to research and develop? How will you employ them?
3. How will the project look or feel? How will it function?
4. What is the ultimate goal of the project and what do you hope the audience will experience, or think about? Who is the intended audience?

Following the proposal presentations, students in the class will select one of the three project proposals and be prepared to discuss their decisions: What is it about this project in comparison to the others that inspires your decision? What about the idea is interesting, or what does it make you think of? What advice do you have? What would you enjoy about the experience? Again, these questions, ideas, and suggestions should demonstrate your engagement with the projects and your feedback is considered class participation.

1. **Work-in-Progress & Skill Share:** In lieu of mid-terms, students will present their works in progress in 5 slides:

1. How have you expanded on the original idea which you proposed? What new research (visual, theoretical, historical, contemporary society) have you conducted? Slides should be an image/s and a quote/s.
2. Show the work as it presently stands (Use more than one slide if necessary). What do you think is working and what would you like to improve?
3. What is your timeline? What are the goals of your milestones? Consider this a contract.
4. & 5. Demonstrate two tools that you have employed and show the class how you have used them.

- **Final Project Presentations - 30%:**

1. **Using slides and a pre-written script, discuss the following:**

- What were your goals for your project, and how do you think they were successfully fulfilled, or not? In either case, be prepared to address why.
- Since your work-in-progress presentation, what new research has influenced your project (artists, essays, etc.)? Slide should include both image/s and quote/s of these inspirations.
- What techniques did you ultimately employ, and for what reason?
- How did you expand your technical skill set? (This is an update on the tools which you presented on during your WIP or new tools you used.)
- How did these techniques reinforce your conceptual framework?
- How did you - and didn’t you - stick to your milestones (ie, what you were able to accomplish given your schedule, what ideas did you have to abandon, and how did you come up with a resolution).
- What challenges did you encounter? What would you do differently?
- What are you most proud of?

2. **Respond to your fellow student by taking notes on each aspect of the presentation. Be prepared to offer CONSTRUCTIVE criticism. These comments and feedback should not just repeat what has just been presented.**

- What concepts and ideas of the project have expanded since the WIP?
- What concepts have been successfully visualized by the techniques demonstrated in the skill-share?
- What project goals do you think have been achieved and how?
- What changes do you notice from the original WIP?
- What were the overall improvements?
- What suggestions would you make (how would you implement those changes if this was your project?)

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- This upper-level studio and seminar explores individual visual arts projects contributing to thesis or dissertation development. Topics in creative personal expression will focus on structuring a sophisticated conceptual framework in order to successfully employ digital tools including imaging, installation, digital/traditional mixed media, and emerging genres. Final projects may include high resolution digital photography, montage, archival printing, gesture drawing, painting, assemblage, processing, stencil art, projection, and evolving genres which lead to an independent final project and web portfolio. By the end of this course, students should have a fluency that describes their ideas and the concepts which they will pursue in the production of their final project.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students are assessed in five ways: three class presentations, final projects, and class participation.  
Class presentations will be assessed using a rubric that ranks: 1. effectiveness, 2. sequencing of information, 3. originality, 4. spelling and grammar, 5. use of graphics, 6. organization, 7. communication skills.  
Final projects will be assessed using a rubric that ranks: 1. creativity, 2. use of elements and principles, 3. craft and skill development, 4. understanding, achievement and completion, 5. participation, focus, and effort.  
Class participation will be assessed by: 1. attendance, 2. attention, 3. engagement, 4. communication with instructor and collaborators.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Students are assessed in five ways: three class presentations - 1. Techniques in Practice, 2. Project Proposal, 3. Skill Share: Works-in-Progress (20% each); final projects (30%); class participation (10%).

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are allowed no more than three absences of any kind and this includes absences due to illness. Each absence, beyond the allowed three, deducts 5 points from the student’s final grade. Students with more than 8 absences, excused or not, will fail the course. Continued late arrivals to class (15 minutes after the start of class) will result in an absence. Per the RPI handbook, if there are unforeseen events (ie. personal/family related) or preplanned opportunities (job interviews, etc), students must discuss this with the instructor prior to the class absence for approval. Note: active participation is an essential to be considered present.

### Other Course Policies:

Students are expected to communicate and troubleshoot with the instructor and with their peers to resolve any potential problems related to attendance.  
Class participation is determined by the degree of a student's engagement, both inside and outside the class. Eye contact with speakers, actively participating in class discussions with questions and ideas, and offering thoughtful constructive feedback to peers are essential elements of class participation. The Institute's policy on incomplete grades will be strictly adhered to, and students must familiarize themselves with the policy. There will be no exceptions to the rules of the Institute. To avoid any issues that would warrant discussion of the topic, students must remain transparent with the instructor and communicate any potential concerns. Students will create and modify a schedule to achieve milestones and goals that will serve as a contract.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic integrity is inseparable from academic excellence. There are a number of types of academic dishonesty which are described in *The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities.* Students should familiarize themselves with this portion of the *Handbook*. Students are expected to provide credit to all of their influences and resources. While appropriation art is an artistic tool which is discussed in class and a strategy which is encouraged, students must identify their sources and provide a conceptual framework for this type of creative borrowing. Borrowed materials must be altered in specific ways that articulated throughout the process of production. If students choose to use tools such as AI to generate material, transparency about how these materials are created is required; as with appropriated content, a creative intervention into such materials is expected. Students who repeatedly violate the standards of academic integrity will receive an F for the course.
