## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

2020 marks 103 years since the release of the first commercially successful Jazz recording. Since that time, the energy, vitality, and challenging nature of Jazz has had a major impact on music throughout the world. The question of “what is Jazz” remains a lively and contentious discussion to this day.

This course charts the emergence, evolution, and expanded influence of Jazz music, as well as the many controversial musical, social, and philosophical questions it continues to provoke. The work of influential figures in American and European improvised music will be examined in detail, and the continuing development of Jazz-inspired contemporary practices will be explored.

Through listening, analysis, discussion, writing, and creative projects, we will develop a greater appreciation and understanding of improvised music in a great variety of contexts, the crucial role American Jazz played in revitalizing improvisational practice throughout the 20th and 21st Centuries, and the conditions which lead to the creation of new musical languages and aesthetics. By the end of the course, students will be able to identify major figures in Jazz and Jazz-influenced improvised music, identify different styles and eras, and have a basic vocabulary for discussing music both analytically and in personal responses.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

By the end of the course, students will:

- Have a basic understanding of the arc of Jazz History, including its musical predecessors and expanded influence
- Be able to identify major historical figures in the Jazz canon and other related forms of improvised music
- Understand the role Jazz has played in the revitalization of improvisation in American and European music
- Be able to identify by ear different historical periods of Jazz and related music
- Have an appreciation of the social and political condition in which Jazz emerged, and the historical roles Jazz has had in the struggle for civil rights and racial equality
- Understand several important current trends of improvised music

**\*IMPORTANT NOTE ON COURSE CONTENT\***

As this is a history course, please be aware that some of the material we look at will be challenging in many ways, including words and images in original sources that are offensive and dehumanizing, particularly regarding race and gender. It is important to understand how these words and images were part of the context in which Jazz arose and were daily experiences for the very artists who were struggling against their influence and the embedded power structures which they represented and supported.

Please be sensitive and considerate about how we discuss these issues in class, as this material can provoke powerful feelings and reactions. Feel free to contact the professor directly regarding the treatment of this material, or with any related concerns.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Class Participation** **– 10%**

In order to take full advantage of the opportunities offered by this course, active and enthusiastic participation in class discussions and activities is necessary. Students are encouraged to ask questions, express opinions, and support similar participation from their peers.

**Journals – 25%**

Journal (oral and written) assignments are due each week's end. There will be two groups in the class: half of the students will submit written responses and the other oral. This process will alternate each week.

Journal prompts are available in the Assignments section of the LMS weekly materials. One group will present their aural journals at the beginning of class, and written assignments will be submitted online by the other. These assignments consist of short responses to questions or ideas from the course material. Writing responses should be at most 100-115 words; aural responses should be at most five minutes.

**Quizzes – 25%**

Four in-class quizzes are given throughout the semester. These quizzes will test the familiarity with the people, groups, terms, texts, and music covered in class as well as the ability to articulate and support arguements.

**Midterm Paper – 20%**

An essay of 1,500 words to be submitted in MLA format. This is a chance to synthesize your knowledge from the class with your own original thoughts and insights, and to do more detailed listening on a piece or pieces of music of your choice. The paper should be submitted in PDF format through LMS.

**Final Paper or Projects – 20%**

The essay of approximately 2,000 words in MLA format will be due at the end of the course and will be similar in scope to the mid-term paper. Students will have the option of doing a creative project in lieu of their final paper. All creative projects must be approved by the instructor, and guidelines for their documentation and presentation will be formulated. Creative projects will include a written portion of approximately 1000 words. Papers/creative projects are to be submitted through LMS, with the writing portion in PDF, Word, or Pages format.
