## COURSE ASSESSMENT MEASURES

The course requirements are as follows:

0% Book Report (Required for the 6000-level Students.)

## GRADING CRITERIA

**Quizzes (30%)**

Quizzes will be required for most of the modules. Each quiz will be equally weighted. Late submissions will be penalized by 10 points per session.

# Group Assignments (30%)

Each group will be required to submit three assignments about marketing analytics using R programming language and making a class presentation. Information on both will be provided before each assignment in the semester. All assignments should be clearly written, well-organized, and submitted by the dates they are due. Late submissions will be penalized by 10 points per session.

I will judge the quality of the group presentations and the class will provide input. Relevant criteria for judging the presentations will include:

- (1) Scope - did the presentation cover the relevant material in sufficient breadth and depth?
- (2) Clarity - was the explanation of ideas and concepts clear?
- (3) Quality of analysis - how well did the group analyze the main issues?
- (4) Quality of application – how well did the group connect conceptual material to the topic of the presentation?
- (5) Contribution – to what extent did the group increase our understanding of the topic? and
- (6) Interest – how well did the group hold our interest?

**Individual Portfolio and Presentation (20%)**

Each student will submit a portfolio that includes data analyses using R programming language to solve a business problem in marketing. The analyses should be analytical and reflective rather than simply descriptive. Description tells what happened. Analysis assesses how and why it happened. Any description should serve the analysis. That is, it should consider the whys? and hows? rather than simply reporting the whats? Carefully think through the issues and present a logically reasoned argument to support your point of view. Try to incorporate material from relevant classwork, chapters, readings, and/or lectures. All analyses should be clearly written, well-organized, and submitted by the dates they are due. Late submissions will be penalized by 10 points per session.

# Attendance and Contributions to the Class (20%)

Class participation is an integral aspect of this course. Please read and be prepared to discuss the assigned readings and cases for each session. Participation includes active contributions to your group, both during class activities and for the group paper and presentation. Quantity and quality will be considered in evaluating participation. If you are interested in participating but have difficulty initiating it, please let me know so that we can try to ease the process.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and the Graduate Student Supplement (For 6000 level and above courses) define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in (1) an academic (grade) penalty and (2) reporting to Lally’s Associate Dean of Academic Affairs and either the Dean of Students (for Undergraduates) or the Dean of Graduate Education (for Graduate students).

**In this course, the academic penalty for a first offense is retaking the quiz and assignment in question*.* A second offense will result in failure of the course as noted in Lally’s Three Strikes Policy.**
