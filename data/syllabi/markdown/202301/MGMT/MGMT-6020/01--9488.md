## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The objective of this course is to provide and introduction to a range of ideas in both corporate and investment finance, and to provide a solid foundation in the principles and practice of financial decision making. Relatively little is assumed in the form of prerequisites: basic statistics, algebra, basic accounting, and a general interest in business. The course is delivered as a first course in finance with a focus on corporate finance. We will cover topics including personal finance, capital budgeting, debts, equities, capital structure, and financial decision making in firms. We will also introduce the relation between risk and return and how these concepts can be applied to the fair valuation of financial instruments.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course seeks to provide you with the tools and concepts that every general manager, entrepreneur, or manager in other functional fields should know. As such, the course is presented from a corporate finance perspective and should not be seen as an investments, banking, or personal finance course. However, essential, and important aspects of each of these topics will be covered as these are all important from the manager’s perspective.

1. Students should develop an understanding of basic financial topics such as how firms create value, the use of financial statements from a finance perspective, the time value of money, risk versus return, the valuation and pricing of financial assets such as stocks and bonds, and the benefits of diversification.

2. Ability to apply the tools introduced throughout the course to assess firm value, managerial decisions and valuing financial assets.

3. The understanding of the core financial securities, namely stocks and bonds. In addition, the importance of monetary policy in determining firms’ decision making.

4. Ability to value securities such as stocks and bonds and consequently the firm, using the core principles discussed along with the tools introduced throughout the course.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Financial Literacy: Students should be able to apply the techniques learned to make financial planning decisions.
- Financial Skills: Students should be able to conceptualize and apply key financial theories to assess corporate financial decisions.
- Financial (Investment) Skills: Students should be able to value securities using basic financial ratios and data.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The grade for the course will be based on performance in five activities: a mid-term exam, a final exam, problem sets, quizzes, and class participation.  
**Homework/In-Class Assignments:**

Assigned during lectures – reading, research, and problems. Homework assignments must be done individually, unless explicitly stated otherwise (by the instructor). Discussions with classmates and the instructor are strongly encouraged, but eventual implementation, analysis, and writing must be individual work. Homework is due at the start of the lecture on the day that it is due. In-class assignments will be assigned during lectures. **No late homework will be accepted. Case Studies:**

There will be several case studies assigned during the semester. Cases will be “Data Cases” from the text and “Harvard Cases.” Students are required to work in groups (group size depends on enrollment) and each group must have access to all case material. The solutions are to be written and submitted via LMS. *Students are responsible for all material in the case.* Note that these cases, especially the Harvard Cases, will also be discussed in Class.

**Grading**: Cases are graded at the group level.  
**Exams:**

There are two exams for the course, a midterm and a final. The midterm will be administered about halfway through the semester and the final will be administered during Finals Exam week.  
**Quizzes:**  
There will be two quizzes during the semester. The first will be two weeks before the midterm and the second will be the week before the final week of classes. These quizzes will serve as a way of providing a review before the mid-term and final exams.
