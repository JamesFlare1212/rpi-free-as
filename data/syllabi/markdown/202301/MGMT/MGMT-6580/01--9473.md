## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

At a time when we most of us walk around with super computers in our pocket that connect us to vast troves of information, technology feels nearly ubiquitous. And yet, marketing high tech solutions is far from an easy task in any industry. This course is designed to help those with extant marketing knowledge further challenge their business acumen as it relates to the fast-paced and highly competitive arena of high tech offerings. Namely, we will explore if and how marketing strategies for this space differ from marketing of non-technical products/ services, and to determine how and why they vary. Notably, the purpose of this course is not to give you a one-size-fits-all solution, but rather to arm you with a variety of frameworks and critical thinking skills to analyze market trends, identify environmental threat and opportunities, and design integrated marketing strategies that will yield impactful results for future business challenges.

Please take the time to read through this syllabus fully and ask questions well in advance. The attached course outline and calendar should be used as a reference, with the understanding that it’s likely to change as the semester unfolds. I will announce any changes for the upcoming week before the end of each class meeting.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

As the pace of innovation continues to accelerate, high-tech offerings in both the B2C and B2B space grow ever more plentiful. Yet unlike traditional consumer product goods and business services, high-tech products can still present as complex, intimidating, and even unnecessary. This course will provide students with a robust exploration of relevant considerations for operating in turbulent and uncertain conditions, regardless of industry.

The objectives of this course are:

1. To help students understand critical distinctions between high-tech environments and more traditional environments
2. To provide students with opportunities to critically analyze high-tech business challenges and appropriately rationalize strategic marketing recommendations
3. To help students identify a variety of cognitive biases that may complicate marketing decision-making or communication efforts
4. To challenge students to consider ethics, diversity and global issues relevant to future high-tech marketing

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate functional knowledge of principles of marketing of high technology products. This will include product planning, pricing, segmentation, distribution, advertising, sales promotion, selling and sales force management, legal issues, research and product service.
- Demonstrate an understanding of how marketing high technology products differs from marketing products that are less technology intensive, such as consumer product goods.
- Integrate advanced marketing principles to analyze business challenges and demonstrate mastery of decision making through the study of business cases.
- Deliver high-quality oral presentations using facts and data.
- Demonstrate an understanding of and the ability to recognize potential ethical issues in marketing efforts.
