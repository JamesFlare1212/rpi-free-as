## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a required first course for management majors and minors. It emphasizes broad, basic principles of managerial functions and processes using an interdisciplinary approach to goal-oriented situations of private and public organizations.

The course is designed to introduce students to the key managerial functions and processes used in businesses. We will cover finance, accounting, marketing, operations, entrepreneurship, business analytics, and other general management concepts. For those of you that are business students, the class will serve as an introduction to these topics, such that you can be familiar with them as you work through your curriculum at Lally. For those of you that are not business students, the course will give you familiarity with these concepts so that you can use them, or at least have a basic understanding of them, as you go to work in organizations as engineers, scientists, architects, etc.

We will use readings and lectures to provide content, but then will use cases and exercises extensively in order to help you *apply* the ideas. In some deliverables you’ll apply concepts to specific questions but in others you’ll be tasked with making decisions and recommendations that would impact an entire business. That is one of the main objectives of this class: to get you experience in the complex, multi-faceted decision making that is done by organizational leaders. This culminates in your final project, wherein you’ll analyze a business and present your findings and recommendations either in a paper or in professional presentation format.

You will also build team, interpersonal, and communication skills.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will demonstrate an understanding of the core functional areas in business: Accounting, Marketing, Finance, Operations, Entrepreneurship, Strategy, Business Analytics and Management.
- Students will demonstrate the ability to research and analyze companies and industries.
- Students will demonstrate the ability to integrate and apply their knowledge behind each of the core functional areas in business.
- Students will demonstrate the ability to analyze complex business problems and formulate recommendations that could be used by managers.
- Students will demonstrate an ability to communicate using modern business methods.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

***Class Participation and Attendance:***

You’ll find that complex business decisions often don’t have a ‘correct’ or ‘best’ answer. In some areas, such as accounting, there is a correct way to do things, of course. However, much of what we discuss will require informed judgment, points of view, and differing opinions. This makes participation very important – the class is not just about me telling you what to think. Therefore, your contribution to discussions is necessary. Your participation grade is based on your “presence,” as opposed to mere attendance, and your contribution to discussions.

Please make every effort to attend our synchronous sessions when possible, and to contribute to class discussions. Contributions to class discussion can be either through direct communication, or in some instances, posing questions via our chat feature on WebEx or through the discussion board on LMS.

If you are going to miss class, please notify me in advance by email. An excused absence does not exempt you from the assignment due for that day. If you are sick and need to take the class asynchronously, you must notify me via email and let me know your situation. **Unexcused absences and excessive excused absences will reduce your class participation grade.**

Note that I will ask you to complete confidential peer reviews of your team members at the end of each project milestone, and again at the end of the term. Thus, your team members will be assessing you as well. Your grade on group assignments ***may*** be impacted by your teammate’s evaluation of your contributions.

***Homework and Case Assignments:***

Questions for each individual assignment will be given orally in the class prior to the due date and are also posted on the syllabus and LMS. You will submit electronically a professional quality document (proofread, clean copy, with appropriate references) for each assignment by the beginning of the class in which it is due. Your grade will reflect the quality of your analysis, the strength of your reasoning, the quality of your writing, the inclusion of outside research and the appearance of your submission where applicable.

***Quizzes:***

There will be three quizzes throughout the semester, and will primarily cover the topical content, i.e., finance, accounting, marketing, operations, etc. We’ll talk about this more in class ~1 week before each quiz. Each may consist of multiple-choice questions, fill in the blanks, and some calculations.

***Consulting Challenge Milestones:***

The Consulting Challenge is a research project in which you and your team will analyze one company (from an approved list) in depth. There will be one company chosen for this early in the term and all teams will analyze separate companies. Detailed instructions and evaluation criteria will be posted on LMS and discussed in time for you to do the work.

There are two types of deliverables for the Consulting Milestones. Each week give an update on progress for your team. Any issues, challenging, key findings, etc.

***Final Consulting Grand Challenge:***

Your “final” for this class is a team assignment and includes a presentation (synchronous or asynchronous -- TBD) of the company you have researched over the course of the semester. The presentation includes supporting documentation which illustrates the research, planning and preparation done by your team. The overall purpose of the assignment is for you to identify challenges the company is facing, come up with recommendations to improve the business, and provide your best assessment of where this company will be in five years. Your recommendations should be achievable, well thought out, and capable of having a substantial impact on the company.

The project should build off the entire course. Think of it in 4 phases: (1) assess the situation / market / company (using tools from readings/lectures/milestones #1-4); (2) generate ideas for recommendations; (3) select recommendations and present them, and (4) present your assessment of their 5 year outlook. We will discuss your ideas in individual team meetings the last week prior to the presentations.

Your team will be graded on the quality of your analysis, your recommendations, the quality and professionalism of your presentation, your use of concepts from the class, and your ability to provide a recommendation(s) with an accompanying detailed implementation plan and respond to challenges during a question and answer session.

In addition, the team will submit their presentation as a deliverable. A final written paper is not needed, so be sure the presentation is clear and well written. Any additional research, pro-forma financials, data gathered, supporting materials, etc can be submitted to the LMS drop box as separate appendices.

**Although this is a team project, the instructor reserves the right to allocate grades individually, based on each individual member’s contributions to the project including the presentation and to the question and answer session.**

Again, note that I will ask you to complete confidential peer reviews of your team members at the end of the term. Your grade on this group assignments ***may*** be impacted by your teammate’s evaluation of your contributions.

**NO LATE SUBMISSIONS OF ASSIGNMENTS WILL BE ACCEPTED.** Understandably, there are circumstances (e.g., job interviews, illness, family matters, etc.) that may cause you to miss class. However, all assignments are to be submitted electronically using the LMS Drop Box and are **due as posted on the LMS**. Requests for an extension of time will be handled on a case-by-case basis and are granted only when compelling reasons are given.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work.
