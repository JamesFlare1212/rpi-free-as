## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a graduate level class for those in the Life Sciences Entrepreneurship MBA program, and serves as a practical, project-based course where students work on real-life innovative concepts associated with the life sciences industry. Students will work directly in a consultant-type or developmental role for a life sciences startup company or concept where they will address fiscal and regulatory challenges, either working individually or preferably in small teams. We will begin the semester with lectures focused on basic business skills and the contemporary life sciences industry that you will apply throughout the semester. As the semester continues, there will be additional lectures on specific business aspects that impact life sciences companies delivered from the professor and from invited subject-matter experts. A group of subject-matter experts in the life sciences industry will be available throughout the semester to advise the students on industry-specific issues.

The course is divided into three stages – business and regulatory development; customer discovery; and financial/investor relations. Students selected projects or startups to work with as a result of Innovation Week, a regional collaboration to connect researchers, clinicians, physicians, and other professionals with a desire to develop the business foundation for their existing startup or to explore commercialization opportunities. All teams will produce either a full business/commercialization plan or go-to-market strategy, based on the concept or company’s growth stage, complete the National Science Foundation’s I-Corps program focused on customer discovery, and complete a startup funding proposal. The experience will culminate in a regional demo day, the first of its kind for Rensselaer and the Capital Region.

Case studies will supplement the experiential opportunity that mirrors the traditional life sciences startup journey. You’ll be tasked with making decisions and recommendations that would impact an entire business and work collaboratively in an effort to bring a concept to market or increase the company’s likelihood of investment. That is one of the main objectives of this class: to gain experience in the complex, multi-faceted decision-making process that all entrepreneurs face. You will also build team, interpersonal, and communication skills.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Additionally, students will gain experience in the following skills associated with the practice of management and entrepreneurship:

- Establish an understanding with a life sciences business/concept on the need for a consulting engagement on a specific topic, and the appropriate approach and resources to be deployed;
- Concretely define the scope of a consulting engagement;
- Break up a complicated problem into logical pieces that can be individually addressed;
- Efficiently gather the right data to build a relevant fact base;
- Effectively extract information from internal and external experts;
- Develop and present recommendations in a persuasive manner to gain buy-in;
- Influence others effectively using a range of ethical tactics.

The course has a number of educational objectives:

- You will understand (and hopefully develop) the skills, attitudes and judgment that enable successful life sciences business professionals to recognize and pursue high-potential opportunities;
- You will learn to identify, develop and execute a successful project plan, and/or learn how to use customer imperatives to drive your work effort with a diverse team of scientists and business leaders;
- You will gain experience in talking to global business leaders related to your project and others in the value chain;
- You will understand the issues faced by life sciences businesses today in multiple stages of development;
- Learn how to develop a project plan and to manage the workflow;
- Determine your own potential to be a business leader.
- You will learn how to present your work efforts to potential life sciences partners, customers, and investors;
- You will improve your written and oral communications skills to more effectively communicate in written and oral form.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The following deliverables are essential part of an iterative process followed by all startups, regardless of sector. Essentially, you are building with a purpose for each deliverable, leading to the final presentation during demo day. Opportunity to revise each deliverable will be given the last week of the course, but each serves as a critical milestone to what will be your team’s best by the end of the semester. Further details such as formatting and criteria are outlined on the course LMS, but the following offers details and clear assessment guidelines that will lead to your final course grade:

HBR Case Study Discussions (10 percent):

Questions for each individual case study will be provided prior to the due date which are also posted on the syllabus and LMS. Your grade will reflect the quality of your analysis, the strength of your reasoning, the application to your current company or concept, and the wider implications of the specific life sciences sector. Your submission will reflect your class discussion contribution for the day, which is a reflection of your understanding of the business side of the life sciences sector.

Business/Commercialization Plan or GTM Strategy (20 percent):

The first of three sections of the course will focus on the development of a full business/commercialization plan, or go-to-market strategy, depending on the concept or startup company's current position. This will be established during Week One so students understand the nuances associated with each. This stage of the process will allow you to understand the overall value proposition, the problem in which the inventor(s) or founder(s) is/are trying to solve and the eventual solution, the competitive landscape, target markets, and business model. Students understand that this serves as the first milestone in development and will be viewed as a work-in-progress with a final revision allowed in the last week of the course. The plan, formatting, and guiding questions/sections for the plan can be found on the LMS.

The student will be assessed on the following elements:

- The value proposition’s clarity (10 percent)
- Clear articulation of the problem (10 percent)
- Clear articulation of the solution’s likelihood of success (10 percent)
- Clear understanding of the competitive landscape (20 percent)
- Clear understanding and quantitative assessment of target markets (20 percent)
- Clear articulation of the concept or company’s business model (30 percent)

Business Plan/Commercialization Plan GTM Strategy due by Feb. 16

Customer Discovery Process/Presentation (20 percent):

As part of the second of three sections of the course, you will complete the National Science Foundation’s I-Corps process. The program is an immersive, entrepreneurial training program that facilitates the transformation of invention to impact. This immersive, experiential training program prepares scientists and engineers to extend their focus beyond the university laboratory — accelerating the economic and societal benefits of NSF-funded and other basic research projects that are ready to be commercialized. Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute is an I-Corps hub and offers services to all regional institutions of higher education. The emphasis is on customer discovery.

Students understand that this serves as the second milestone in development and will be viewed as a work-in-progress with a final revision allowed during the last week of the course. The plan, formatting, and guiding questions/sections for the plan can be found on the LMS. The deliverable will be presented within a pitch deck and follows the standard I-Corps format.

The student will be assessed on the following elements:

- Completion of at least 12 customer interviews between Feb. 21 – March 26 (20 percent)
- Business Model Canvas revision based on customer discovery interviews (20 percent)
- Quality of Personal Lessons Learned (8) (20 percent)
- Quality of Key Elements Concept or Company Still Wants to Learn (8) (20 percent)
- Attendance/Strategy Developed During Two “Office Hours” (20 percent)

Customer Discovery (I-Corps) “What We Learned” Presentation due on March 27

Startup Funding Proposal (20 percent)

As part of the third of three sections of the course, you will create foundational profit/loss statements to understand the immediate costs and/or potential revenues that can be generated by the concept or startup company within the first year of operation or the next fiscal year. A startup funding proposal will show command of all financial needs and potential and articulate the clear use of funds and understanding of startup capital. This prospectus will eventually be combined with the business plan, but also have the flexibility to present, depending on the stakeholder group in which you or your team may engage in the near future. Some areas, depending on the concept or the business, may overlap but it’s an iterative process.

Students understand that this serves as the third milestone in development and will be viewed as a work-in-progress with a final revision allowed during the last week of the course. The plan, formatting, and guiding questions/sections for the plan can be found on the LMS.

The student will be assessed on the following elements:

- Clear articulation of how you will generate profit (20 percent)
- Clear articulation of how the business can scale (20 percent)
- Clear understanding and articulation of the risks (20 percent)
- Clear plan of how you will use the funds (20 percent)
- Clear command of accurate and reasonable financial statements (20 percent)

Startup Funding Proposal due by April 24

Demo Day Presentation (30 percent)

Demo Day is the culmination of hard work, vision, and to put companies or concepts in the best position to be successful. It’s also a reason to celebrate this fantastic collaboration. Cornell has demo days. Stanford has demo days. Yale has demo days. This region and this collaboration should have a demo day.

If you’re unfamiliar with a demo day, it’s a cross between research-based poster presentations and pitch competitions but the approach and stakes are a little different. This isn’t a standard “Shark Tank” pitch with prize money; however, investors will be invited in addition to colleagues from research, medical, economic development, life sciences, and other communities. Demo days reduce pre-pitch anxiety considerably, but if this evolves into investment, then it’s a win for others.

Students, along with researchers/clinicians/physicians and other project leads, will present their business findings via a 10-15 slide presentation that embodies all three previous deliverables – business/commercialization plan or go-to-market strategy, customer discovery, and investor prospectus.

The student will be assessed on the following elements:

- Clear articulation and viability of the concept or business (20 percent)
- Clear articulation and feasibility of the concept or business (20 percent)
- Clear articulation and desirability of the concept or business (20 percent)
- Clear plan of how you will use the funds (20 percent)
- Clear command of accurate and reasonable financial statements (20 percent)

Demo Day will take place either Friday, April 28, or Wednesday, May 3.

Note: Although this is a team project for some, the instructor reserves the right to allocate grades individually, based on each individual member’s contributions to the project including the presentation and to the question-and-answer session. However, if this is a team endeavor, each student will receive the same grade. Again, note that I will ask you to complete confidential peer reviews of your team members at the end of the term. Your grade on this group assignments may be impacted by your teammate’s evaluation of your contributions.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

You’ll find that complex business decisions often don’t have a ‘correct’ or ‘best’ answer. In some areas, such as accounting, there is a correct way to do things, of course. However, much of what we discuss will require informed judgment, points of view, and differing opinions. This makes participation very important – the class is not just about me telling you what to think. Therefore, your contribution to discussions is necessary. Your participation grade is based on your “presence,” as opposed to mere attendance, and your contribution to discussions. Please make every effort to attend our synchronous sessions and to contribute to class discussions.

In the event we encounter an escalated public health or inclement weather situation, we will move the learning experience online and your cameras should be on in order for you to be marked present. If you are having camera difficulties, please make the appropriate arrangements ahead of time or access via your mobile device. If you do not have a mobile device, we can/should talk. There is a solution to every problem. It may not be the ideal solution, but in 2023 it’s a solution. You will be notified via Rensselaer e-mail of either the Institute’s decision or mine the morning of class.

If you are going to miss class, please notify me in advance by email. An excused absence does not exempt you from the assignment due for that day. Three unexcused absences will reduce your class grade one-half a full letter per each absence after (ex. B to a B-). Life happens, though, so I’m also extremely sensitive to what has happened the last 2-3 years. We will discuss mental health early in the semester. If you are struggling – with anything – please approach me after class, via email, or in any other matter and ask, “can we get coffee?” We will then do so and talk. I am extremely flexible and will always find time for any student.

Note that I may ask you to complete confidential peer reviews of your team members at random checkpoints during the semester and again at the end of the term if you are operating in a team environment. Thus, your team members will be assessing you as well. Your grade on group assignments may be impacted by your teammate’s evaluation of your contributions.

At the end of the day, the policy here, in business, and in life is simple — just communicate. Your future self will thank you.
