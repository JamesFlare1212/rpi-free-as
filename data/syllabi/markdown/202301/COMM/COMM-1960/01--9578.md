## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is designed to provide international TAs with communication training so that they are able to present academic information clearly, interact effectively with students, practice classroom management skills, accommodate different learning styles, and understand cross-cultural aspects of teaching and learning.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

1. *Textbook:* Smith, J.A., Meyers, C.M., & Burkhalter, A.J. (2007). Communicate: Strategies for international teaching assistants. Waveland Press. Available digitally on Amazon.

*2. Software:* NativeAccent

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- By the end of this course, students will be able to:  demonstrate knowledge and competency in pronunciation, spoken grammar, fluency, and listening skills in North American English  develop presentation styles that meet the needs of different types of learners  explain concepts in their field to listeners without expertise in the area  gauge undergraduate students’ (audience) comprehension and respond to questions effectively  lead engaging class discussion that contribute to student learning  understand cultural differences and how they impact teaching and learning  detect elements of speech that make their speech unintelligible to listeners and set goals for ongoing improvement

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1. **Native Accent- 30%**

Each TA’s speaking skills will be different. NativeAccent is a program designed to target students’ individual needs in the areas of English pronunciation, grammar, word stress, and fluency. Your grade in this area will be based on 1) your engagement (how often, and for how much time, you use NativeAccent) and 2) your improvement throughout the semester.

2. **Teaching Demos- 25%**

You will have two teaching demonstrations throughout the semester. These teaching demonstrations will be short mini-presentations to the class, in the style of a lecture you would deliver as a course instructor. The purpose of these teaching demos is to practice your public speaking in a lecture type class, apply the skills we are working on in the course, and build confidence and expertise discussing the content of your subject matter knowledge.

3. **Class Discussion- 15%**

You will lead one class discussion during the semester. You will pick a discussion topic and present it to the class. Then you will lead a short discussion among your classmates. When you are not the leader, you will be expected to participate in the discussions as a student. The purpose of this class discussion is to practice your ability to lead a discussion in a class, apply the skills we are working on in the course, and build confidence in interacting with and responding appropriately to the students.

4. **Video Journal- 10%**

You will demonstrate your understanding of and reflect on class content by making several video journal entries in response to prompts provided by the instructor.

5. **Preparedness and Participation- 10%**

Students will be expected to attend class, come prepared for class, and actively participate in class throughout the semester. Students may lose points in this category if they do not attend class or attend class but are unengaged and do not participate.

6. **Homework- 10%**

A few short homework assignments will be given throughout the semester usually in preparation for larger assignments.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance Policy**

Students are expected to attend on time and actively participate in all classes. As students cannot participate well in class without being present, students will be expected to be physically in class and participate actively to receive full points for participation.

If a student is more than 20 minutes late to class, they will be considered absent for the day. However, if a student is less than 20 minutes late, they will be considered late. **3 lates are equal to one absence**. If a student has come in late, they MUST talk to me at the end of class in order to update their attendance; otherwise, they may be considered absent for the day.

Two unexcused absences will be allowed during the semester without any affect on the participation grade. However, if there is a quiz, presentation, or in-class homework assignment on the day of an absence, it cannot be made up, and the assignment will receive a zero score.

Absences can be excused for the reasons listed on RPI’s Student Success Website: https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/current-students/academic-and-personal-support/requesting-excused-absence with the permission of the instructor. However, students must provide the correct documentation to the instructor and receive the instructor’s permission in order for the absence to be excused. In the case of an excused absence, students may be permitted to make up assignments within three weeks of the absence or by the end of the semester, whichever comes first. (Extensions will be granted in extreme cases such as prolonged hospital stays.)

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. In cases in which outside sources are used, students are expected to paraphrase, summarize, or quote and cite these sources properly following the citation style introduced in the class. Plagiarism will not be tolerated. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty. If found in violation of the academic honesty policy, students may be subject to two types of penalty. The instructor administers an academic [grade] penalty and the student is reported to the Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education as appropriate. The first violation results in 0 grade for that assignment. The second violation results in failure of the course. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
