COURSE DESCRIPTION

The world in which we navigate our lives is complex. The intensity of written, spoken and visual in- formation have increased in order to assimilate this data. “Flatland” or two-dimensional data presen- tations on paper or computer monitor are commonly flat and static. This course examines historic data presentations to determine the most efficient way to represent complex information without harming the data. The course will also touch upon how how data can be more personal or human. Information designers clarify these displays and enrich our understanding of our modern world. Class assignments and final projects must be produced using Adobe InDesign and/or Adobe Illus- trator. Scanning and photo manipulation may be done in Adobe PhotoShop. For the purposes of instruction and based on industry practices, Adobe InDesign will be engaged for page composition, Illustrator will used for drawing or redrawing and PhotoShop will be for photo scanning and image manipulation. It is up to the student to acquire the necessary proficiency to accomplish the

given assignments in an accurate and timely manner. The course of study is structured as a laboratory/design studio. All work will be done in the lab or as homework. Internet, web design, social media will be discussed as an opportunity for design composition use.

ACADEMIC DISHONESTY AND PLAGIARISM

The Rensselaer Handbook defines various forms of academic dishonesty and procedure for responding to them. Plagiarism and any form of academic dishonesty will result in an evaluation of zero (0) points for the assignment, with no chance for re submission. If you have questions concerning these defini- tions please consult with the instructor. Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments which students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Stu- dent Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty, and you should make yourself familiar with this information.

All assignments in this class must represent the student’s own work. In this class academic dishones- ty consists of plagiarism and copyright infringement, i.e., representing the work of others as your own through deliberate omission of acknowledgments or references. Plagiarism and copyright infringement include copying text, graphics, sound or music, video, animations, user interface designs, and computer programs created by others and submitting that work as your own. Public domain icons and software may be copied and used in your programs. You may discuss your assignments with others and solicit feedback from others to improve your work.

Plagiarism or copyright infringement in this class will result in an “F” for the project. A second inci- dence of this type of academic dishonesty will result in an “F” for the course. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
