### Other LMS Information:

COURSE DESCRIPTION

This course is a hands-on investigation into communicating effectively using motion graphics.  
Through a historical lens, students will learn the art of storytelling in a time-based medium using type, graphics, photos, and illustrations. Projects could include logo animations, animated infographics, title sequences, and short form narratives. Principles covered will include storyboarding and planning, design and composition, timing, pacing, sound and/or music development and synchronization. The course of study is structured as a laboratory/motion design studio. Students enrolling should have a working knowledge of Adobe Photoshop and Illustrator.

### Prerequisites or Other Requirements:

Prerequisites are COMM 2660: Introduction to Graphic Design and familiarity with Adobe After Effects and other design software and basic graphic design knowledge.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

EVALUATION CRITERIA

**PROJECT ASSIGNMENTS** (100% 5 projects each worth 20%)  
**• Aesthetics 25%** Does the project have strong aesthetic qualities?  
**• Conceptual Framework 25%** Are the concepts or ideas behind the project strong, original, thoughtful, creative. How well did you use animation to enhance and express your concept.  
**• Technical Skill 25%** Does the project use techniques and skills that demonstrate a mastery of the skills and techniques addressed and/or go beyond the technical expectation? Is the production value precise.  
**• Comp Work 25%** Did you complete the progress work on the due date? How far did you take the ideas from one due date to the next. Did you push your explorations and make variations on the theme or explore multiple ideas?

**NON-GRADED CLASS ASSIGNMENTS***

**• Initial Exercises + Homework.** These are for training and will help you learn the basics of animation and After Effects. They will also prepare you for the class projects. I trust you will do the (home)work.

**• From Around the Web:** Weekly Animation Reviews. Each week one of you will be responsible for presenting animation(s) you have found/seen online. Be prepared to lead a maximum of 5-7 minute discussion on why you chose this animation. Topics to con- sider: what inspired you; discuss pacing, timing, length, concept, visual styling, etc. Is this animation or animator of historical significance or historical style? If you were to change it what would you do? Is there a technique you’d like to explore after seeing this or anything else that inspires you? You will be assigned your week(s). If for any reason you cannot make your assigned week it is up to you to find someone to switch with. I recommend that all of you keep a running set of links or people you follow. Feel free to present additional animations on additional days other than your scheduled day.

\* If students are not participating in these non-graded assignments I will rework the evaluation criteria percentages for the course and assign grades to these assignments.

**CLASS PROJECTS*** (projects subject to change)  
A brief description of each project is listed below. Details of the projects with due dates will be posted on the class blackboard and will be come available upon introduction of each project.  
**Letter & Word Typographic Studies**  
Studies using kinetic or motion typography.  
**Logo Animations**  
A series of short animation studies using a logo and/or company graphics for brand identity use.  
**Title Sequence**  
Use video and/or photographs, and animated type and graphics to create an opening title sequence.

**Data Tells the Story**  
Students will identify a topic/issue and research data to communicate the problem using animated data.  
**Your Narrative**  
This final project will allow students to express their own animation voice or explore an idea on about a concept or topic of their choice.
