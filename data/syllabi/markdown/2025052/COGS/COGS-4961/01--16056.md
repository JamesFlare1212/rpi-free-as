## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Implement foundational NLP models using PyTorch, including word embeddings, RNNs, and transformers.
- Apply deep learning techniques to common NLP tasks such as sentiment analysis, text generation, and question answering.
- Design and evaluate prompting strategies for large pre-trained language models.
- Understand the architecture and training methods of modern transformer-based models.
- Independently explore recent advances in NLP and extend their knowledge using research papers and code repositories.

Assignments:

The assignments may involve reading, surveying, trying out software systems, or writing code yourself. You need to submit a report and be ready to give a presentation in class. For programming assignments, you need to make your project ready to compile/run, clearly document your codes, and supply a readme file. The readme file should describe the project, list and describe the included files and the main functions, and explain why you think your algorithm will work for the problem.

Late WorkYou should submit your work on time. By default, the assignments are due at midnight on the due date. 1 point will be deducted each day an assignment is late unless you have consulted with the instructors before the date the work is due.

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

ACADEMIC INTEGRITY

The policies of the Institute for Academic Integrity apply, and students are required to consult the instructor or the student handbook if they have any questions.

Student-teacher relations are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments which the students turn in are theirs. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The Rensselaer Handbook defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Students should familiarize themselves with this portion of the Rensselaer Handbook and should note that the penalties for plagiarism and other forms of cheating can be quite harsh.
