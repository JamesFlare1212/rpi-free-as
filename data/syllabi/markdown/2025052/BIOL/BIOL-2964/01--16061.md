## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is at the undergraduate level. It explores the status of our current knowledge on Human Evolution. The goal is to provide active learning in a collaborative environment, to seek the causal factors leading up to our own emergence. What makes a human? Data collection is from laboratory specimens (phenotypic traits), as well as from public databases (genomic/protein datasets) and Museums. Data analysis is accomplished with simulations. Data analysis utilizes the supporting evidence, while tracing the evolutionary relatedness amongst primate/mammalian species. Interactive discussions allow students to generate hypotheses, and design tests of their hypotheses. The impact of disease pathogens, lifestyle/culture and environment on human evolution is discussed.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. Principle Concepts: Students will be able to master introductory concepts underlying Human Evolution, traits of mammals, apes, monkeys and hominins
2. Predict and Propose: Students will formulate and test hypotheses, for example, compare their Phylogenetic predictions with the Fossil Record, and apply Phylogenetic principles to Human Epidemic Diseases such as HIV and SarsCoV2.
3. Pre-requisite Satisfaction: Students will be able to take more advanced courses in Evolution and Bioinformatics in future semesters.
