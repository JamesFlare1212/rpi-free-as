## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will demonstrate familiarity with a variety of creative writing genres.
- Students will demonstrate habitual engagement in creativity as a process, including analysis ofmodels, brainstorming, trial and error, seeking feedback, and reflection/revision.
- Students will demonstrate a consistent writing practice.
- Students will practice close reading peer texts and engaging in productive self-reflection.
- Students will demonstrate the intra and interpersonal skills necessary for positive participation in a creative community.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Attendance and Participation. I expect that each of you contribute to discussions, in large  
and/or small groups, every class. I will routinely evaluate your preparation for class in the  
following ways: observing small groups, posing questions, requesting questions, and informal  
presentations of group or individual work, in-class writing. I will note your contributions weekly  
and evaluate your class participation at the end of each week. Students with more than two  
absences will see a 1% penalty per absence in their participation grade.  
Inspiration Journals. Throughout the term, you will be seeking inspiration to keep the creativity  
flowing by reading and responding to Elizabeth Gilbert’s Big Magic (with the option to alternate  
with personal sources of inspiration). This will be evaluated P/F for completion.  
Creative Compass & Final Reflection. In weeks one and six you will be engaging in processes  
that help you reflect on your creativity, intentions, & learning. In week one, this will take the  
form of a hybrid graphic and text product to help you visualize your creativity and establish  
personal goals. In week six, you will write a letter reflecting on how you followed your creative  
mission and showed up for your creative self. The grading criteria are timeliness,  
formatting/proofreading, specificity, completion, and authenticity.  
Creative Project Portfolios: The project portfolio consists of 1) A draft of a creative writing  
project, including evidence of your drafting process 2) A non-evaluative response to peer projects  
3\) A brief project reflection.  
Reading Quizzes: There are 5 reading quizzes to provide accountability for each unit’s reading.  
Quizzes cannot be made up.  
Revision Portfolio. Rather than producing a major work at the end of the session, you will  
review your 3 creative projects and implement revisions to 2 of them, based on your course  
learning and peer feedback sessions.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance Policy  
This is a small seminar class, the success of which is entirely dependent on active student  
participation. Therefore, I expect all students to attend every class, and participate to the extent of  
their abilities. Every student can miss one class for whatever reason, no questions asked. Each  
subsequent absence will deduct ten points each from the student’s final grade (1%). A student  
who exceeds four absences will automatically fail the course even if they are otherwise  
passing.

### Other Course Policies:

Late Work Policy  
I do not accept late work in Summer II.  
Technology  
Access to the internet, LMS, and MS Word are essential. Technology fails are inevitable, but you  
are expected to do your best to insulate yourself from tech tragedies by testing your hardware,  
software, and connections before class, ensuring adequate bandwidth, and regularly backing up  
files. While I will do my best to make reasonable accommodations for technology mishaps, I  
cannot guarantee these accommodations. You are ultimately 100% responsible for your  
technology and for reaching out to me or to your peers in advance if you anticipate a problem.  
Students who are distracted by technology in class can expect this to drop their participation  
grade.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Failure to indicate accurately the extent and precise nature of one's reliance on other sources is  
also a form of plagiarism. Students are responsible for understanding legitimate use of sources,  
the appropriate ways of acknowledging academic, scholarly, or creative indebtedness. All  
assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work completed for  
this class in particular. In cases where help was received, AI was consulted, or teamwork was  
allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. This class focuses on  
generating new work. Therefore, using previous creative work of yours for this class will also be  
considered academic dishonesty and fall under the plagiarism policy. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty of a zero  
for the assignment and the potential to fail the course. Second offenses will result in automatic  
failure of the course and the potential for disciplinary action by the institution. If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for  
clarification.
