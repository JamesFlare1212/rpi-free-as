## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is designed for students who already have some background experience with the Chinese pinyin system, basic conversations, and character reading and writing, but strive to further develop their proficiency in Chinese language and culture.

Students will watch six well-known Chinese films produced by internationally acclaimed directors. These films will provide an opportunity for them to not only learn the language in a fun, meaningful, authentic, and contextualized way with real-world scenarios, but also gain a more in-depth understanding of Chinese history, society, customs, cultural nuances, and of Chinese people’s thoughts and lives. Students will transcribe dialogues, expand their vocabulary through conversation and language games, generate and answer questions, analyze social and cultural issues, and act out small performances using dialogue either straight from the movies or created by the students themselves including body language and other nonverbal cues. The discussions for this class are extensive and will cover the meanings of the words and expressions used in a particular context, people’s behaviors and viewpoints, the reasons why they hold such positions, how the story might happen differently if one does not say something or take a certain action, what role culture plays, and what might change if the same story were to happen in a different culture. Writing assignments will follow for students to reinforce and enhance what they have learned in class, further develop their thoughts and critical thinking ability, compare Chinese culture to their own culture, and elevate their studies to new heights.

Credits earned from this course may be used to fulfill a Chinese minor requirement (as one option for the requirement of “a topics course at the 4000 level”).

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Upon successfully completing this course, students will be able to: 1. Master 150 new Chinese characters, 300 new words (including their collocations/phrases), and approximately 40 grammar structures. 2. Interpret, explain, and discuss the contents of the six selected films, applying authentic expressions appropriate to the given scenarios. 3. Attain an in-depth comprehension of the historical, cultural, and societal backgrounds depicted in the selected films. 4. Analyze, compare, and present insights into the nuances of Chinese culture, drawing connections with their own cultural perspectives. 5. Enhance critical and creative thinking skills with global and multicultural awareness.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

- (1) Attendance is mandatory. Learning a language differs significantly from learning in other fields, where independent study suffices. Each language practice session directly builds upon previous ones. Missing any class means forfeiting valuable opportunities to participate in group exercises and practice communication skills with others, which will adversely impact future sessions. In essence, missing one class results in more than just the loss of a two-hour period itself.

- (2) Due to the unpredictable nature of life, everyone is granted TWO absences that will not be counted against the final grade ("free pass"). The instructor will not attempt to distinguish between "excused" and "unexcused" absences, as it is challenging to consistently evaluate individual circumstances. Despite my best efforts to be considerate, students may always find reasons to request exceptions. Every student may use these two “free” absences for doctor’s appointments, illnesses, sports waivers, job interviews, religious reasons, car trouble, family issues, funerals, alarm clock issues, and so on. Because the situations cannot be predicted, it is wise to save the two “free” absences for when you have to use them.

***Although the first two absences do not negatively impact a student’s grade, the third absence carries significant consequences. Upon the third absence, the final grade will be lowered one full letter: A to B, B to C, C to D, or D to F. In other words, accumulating six absences—regardless of whether they are excused or unexcused—will result in an automatic grade of F for the course.***

If you must miss a class, please take the responsibility to get the notes and assignments from a classmate in a timely manner so that you can be prepared for the next class meeting. ---- I will distribute a list of your classmates with their email addresses. Students are encouraged to become friends with each other. Together, we build a warm, supportive, positive, and productive learning environment.

- (3) Please be punctual. Arriving late for class interrupts the learning process for everyone and should be avoided as much as possible. Every three times a student is marked late counts as one absence.

- ### Diversity, Equity and Inclusivity

  RPI policy is that students from all backgrounds and perspectives should be well-served by their experiences both in and out of class. As a community we value the diversity that students bring to their classes and view such diversity as a resource, strength, and benefit. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty. If found in violation of the academic honesty policy, students may be subject to two types of penalty. The instructor administers an academic [grade] penalty and the student is reported to the Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education as appropriate. The first violation results in a 0 grade for that assignment. The second violation results in failure of the course.

If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs
