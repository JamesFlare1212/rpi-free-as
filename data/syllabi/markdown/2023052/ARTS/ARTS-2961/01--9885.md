## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

#### Course Description

A studio/lab course on creative strategies and production skills for video art. Informed by readings, research and beginner’s video production tools, students conceive and produce a series of short videos. Research areas include creative writing, photography, performance, visual composition, video and audio recording, video and audio editing.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

## Course Goals

### Goals:

#### Student Learning Outcomes

Students acquire practical, embodied knowledge of video art. Students explore theory and practice of video art. Students develop skills in creative writing, video production, performance and conceptual art. Students understand and employ a variety of strategies for visual composition and artistic expression. Students learn a specific vocabulary to apply critical thinking and articulate ideas about video art.

Project 1: Portrait, Performance, and Piece-to-Camera (MIXED)

Students will create a series of quick performance piece-to-camera in portrait mode with phone cameras, taking on a persona (a media trope, a sibling, an authority figure, their alter ego, an anthropomorphic object, etc.). Students then will do an interpretation of another student’s piece-to-camera. Students will then create a written proposal they will sketch out, rehearse (and rehearse again), shoot, re-shoot and re-shoot again and again. No editing or post-production required but possible. While students will be working alone, they will be playing off each other’s work.

Part 1: Piece-to-Camera Versions 1 and 2 (1 minute each)

Part 2: FINAL Project 1 (2-3 minutes)

Project 2: Editing, Collage and Montage (SOLO)

Students will create two music videos applying Eisenstein’s theory of montage. Students will come up with a concept for editing (through meter/rhythm) a set of pre-existing clips they will curate, including AI images if they want. They will then apply the skills they learn to make a longer work complete with a written proposal, sketches, storyboarding, stills, etc.

Part 1: Rhythmic/Metric Editing for Music Video (1-2 minutes)

Part 2: FINAL Project 2 (2-4 minutes)

Project 3: Skit, Story and Continuity (GROUP)

Students will create short video drama skits through audiovisual storytelling NOT mere theater in front of a camera. They will use continuity editing (editing for coherent storytelling). This means they will need to consider having a: beginning-middle-and-end, characters in conflict, set up with payoffs, etc. Students will consider recording voice on camera vs. dubbing. Students as groups will come up with three ideas they will pitch to the rest of the class and then decide which skit to do based on feedback. They will create a written proposal, sketches, stills, etc. They will set up, rehearse, rehearse again, and rehearse again; shoot and re-shoot and re-shoot again, then edit and add any graphics.

Part 1: Three Ideas Pitch

Part 2: FINAL Project 3 (1-4 minutes)

### Other Course Policies:

Other Policies and Advice

- Attendance matters. Attendance is required in every class. Each unexcused absence deducts 3 points from the final project grade. After 3 absences, regardless of being excused or not, each additional absence will deduct 5 points from the final course grade. To get attendance, make sure to check in with me on every class day, during class.
- Late projects are penalized. Each class after the project deadline deducts 3 points from the project grade.
- Manage time wisely. Researching, developing ideas and concepts, recording audio and video, and editing are all time-consuming activities. Students must reserve a minimum of 4 hours per week, outside of class, to work on class projects.
- Keep your files until the semester is over. As your skills improve, you may want to revisit earlier projects at the end of the semester. Keep copies of your raw footage files on your external drive.
- Always shoot more than you think you need. Do various takes on the same shot. Record the same audio a few times. Later, pick the best ones in editing.
- Even improv requires planning and practice. A lot of videos on social media appear “in the moment.” But be prepared to give your work fluidness through planning and liveliness through practice, if you want good results.

A studio/lab course on creative strategies and production skills for video art. Research areas include creative writing, photography, performance, visual composition, video and audio recording, video and audio editing.

#### Required Equipment

Personal items every student must have for the course:

- An external hard drive of at least 500GB.
- One SD or MicroSD card of at least 128GB.
- Decent headphones. I suggest a Sony V150 set, or something similar.
- A laptop capable of running video editing apps and processing large files.
- A sketchbook and/or blank copy paper is highly RECOMMENDED

#### Other Resources

- **Video and file sharing**. We use Vimeo and YouTube to share files and links. Students must create a Vimeo and/or a YouTube account, upload their videos there and share the links via email.
- **Cameras**. Students can check out cameras on campus. You can also use your own video camera if you have one. Our first project will require a cell phone camera. If you do not have one, don’t worry, I have options.
- **Audio recorders**. Students can check out digital audio recorders on campus. The use of high-quality, digital audio recorders is required on most projects.
- **Editing apps**. If you have access to Premiere, Final Cut or some other app you’re already familiar with, you may use that. If not, there are 2 free apps you may use: Davinci Resolve and Blender. Both take some time to learn, so make a decision and stick to it. Davinci Resolve is easier to use but more limited. There are many tutorials online. You may start with this. And there is Blender, a free, robust 3D modeling and animation app, which also comes with a video editor. It is a bit harder to learn, but it offers more in the long run - especially if you are also interested in 3D modeling and animation. There are many tutorials online. This is a good start, focused on the video editor.
