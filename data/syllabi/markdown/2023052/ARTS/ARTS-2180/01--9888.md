## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Deep Listening is a practice developed by pioneering composer and humanitarian Pauline Oliveros to enhance and expand listening abilities and to encourage creativity. Students will develop a heightened awareness to sound and their sonic environment through experiential exercises, creative projects, collaborations, readings, lectures, and discussion. Musicians and non-musicians of all abilities and backgrounds are welcome!

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

*How To Do Nothing: Resisting the Attention Economy* by Jenny Odell  
Students can purchase a copy of the book if they choose; however, digital copies of the text will be provided to students through our shared resources folder.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

- Communicate and engage with core Deep Listening philosophy, practices, and exercises
- Cultivate community and belonging
- Celebrate, appreciate, and respect our unique contributions, positions, experiences
- Understand and engage with the fluid and ever-shifting relationship between attention and awareness
- Listen to what’s going on *out there* **and** *in here* to cultivate multisensory receptivity
- Build a vocabulary and practice to diversify, engage, and expand our *sensory diets*
- Reflect on lived and sensed experiences in formal and informal ways
- Create spaces of exploration and discovery
- Embrace the opportunity to **just be** rather than **do**
- Develop a toolkit of listening, moving, dreaming, and imagining practices to carry into daily life
- Collect and archive experiences (both invited and improvised) in a consistent way that is an extension of our unique experience
- Understand, evaluate, and compose Deep Listening *scores*
- Stretch and strengthen the birth-rights of play, creativity, and curiosity

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Class Participation**

Showing up to most things in life is the most meaningful; it’s even more meaningful when you are fully attentive, fully engaged, fully present. We are all human, and we all have an infinite number of other things we can (or could) attend to at any given moment. My invitation for the semester is to **be as fully present and attentive** in class as possible. You will not need your computer or your phone for class, so I’m going to invite you to leave those in a place where they are out of sight/reach. For class, you will need to bring your Deep Listening Journal (see **Course Text & Materials** and your preferred writing tool(s). When our work in class requires materials outside of these two things, I will provide them or let you know to bring them (computer, phone, etc.). Additionally, as members of a community, we acknowledge each other’s listening and expression in a non-judgmental way and respect each other’s privacy and freedom to express through sounding and movement.

The possible five points for each class meeting are designated as follows: ***1 point for timely attendance, 1 point for preparedness, 1 point for active engagement in opening creative/exploratory practice, 1 point for active engagement during in-class activities (listening/moving/dreaming/imagining scores), 1 point for positive contribution to our community.***

I will keep track of your attendance daily. If you have anticipated absences (job/internship interviews, appointments, etc.), please let me know at least 2 class periods in advance so we can plan for you to make-up that absence if you choose to do so. If you miss class due to an unexpected event (illness, personal loss, mental health concerns, etc.), please notify me ASAP so we make sure that you get the support that you need, and then plan to make-up that absence if you choose to do so. Of import: “Make up” classes will be arranged on a case-by-case basis.  
**Reading Reflections**

The first three weeks of the semester we will be reading chapters from Jenny Odell’s book *How to Do Nothing: Resisting the Attention Economy*. Reading Reflections are meant to provide space/time/opportunity to actively share your experience while reading the text. Reflections can take the form of posing questions, sharing thoughts, opinions, connections, and/or noting discoveries. *Reading Reflections should be no more than one page, single-spaced. **Reading Reflections are due by 5 PM the day before reading is due in class, as noted on the Course Schedule.*** **Extended Practice/Weekly Reflections**

Each week, I will invite you to engage with an Extended Deep Listening Practice outside of our time together in community at least three times over the course of a week. These Extended Practices will be based on the practices/exercises/explorations that we engage with each week and will be “assigned” every Friday (except for Week 1). If you choose to engage in that week’s Extended Practices, you should reflect on each of your sessions in your Deep Listening Journal (and save/archive other media documentation, if desired) *immediately* after each Extended Practice session, allowing yourself the time and space to process your experience. You could also consider noting important contextual/environmental information for each of your Extended Practice sessions (perhaps the time of day, location, weather, beginning “state” of mind-body-heart, etc.). *Extended Practice/Weekly Reflections should be no more than two pages, single-spaced.*

In addition to reflecting on your Extended Practice for the week, you may also reflect on the week, possibly including in-class exercises, assignments, and/or the affects of cultivating and nurturing a Deep Listening practice and mindset.  
**Creative Challenges**

Creative Challenges are rooted in expanding your *sensory diet* and stretching your ideas for what is possible. These Creative Challenges can take any form of composing—written, audio, video, live performance, etc. I will provide the prompts for each of these challenges in class, and due dates for these challenges are noted in the **Course Schedule**. Creative Challenges will also be presented to the class as 5-minute presentations as noted in the **Course Schedule**. These presentations will get to the “heart” of your inspiration for your response to the challenge and showcase your composition. Note: These Creative Challenges are also rich with possibility for “trying on” the composing of Deep Listening/Moving/Dreaming/Imagining scores.  
**Final Jam Score**

After five weeks of engaging with scores written by members of the Deep Listening community, you’ll be invited to compose your own score as individuals or in groups *of no more than three*. Your written component will include the score itself as well as a brief explanation of the inspiration that led to the score’s composition. You will be provided time in class to actively work on these scores and receive feedback from me before our **Final Jam on Friday, June 30.**

**Final Reflection Letter**

As a writer and writing instructor, I have found letter writing to be a valuable and accessible way to process a large corpus of work and/or experience. The Final Reflection Letter invites you to write a letter to me (and only me) about your unique, individual experience as a member of our community of listeners-movers-dreamers-imaginers. The letter is also an opportunity for you to share how you have engaged with the (possible) dimensions of learning for the course. To support your narrative, you’ll be invited to share documentation from your **Deep Listening Journal** or any of the other **Means of Engagement** that you have completed for the course. I will provide a more detailed assignment sheet.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

## **Academic Integrity**

*The* *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* and *The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement* define various forms of academic dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade *must represent your own work*. **If you plagiarize once,** you will receive an **F on the assignment.** If you plagiarize again, you will **fail the entire course** and may be reported to the *Dean of Students* as appropriate. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask me for clarification*. In regard to using AI (e.g. ChatGPT or other Open AI resources)*: While it may be tempting to use this resource, the work that we do in this course is designed to help us—as humans—express our lived, sensed, embodied experience. AI does not have any of those things, nor are they tied to our full, unique presence in the world. Grappling with challenge and learning from those challenges are an inherent part of our growth and development as humans; don’t take away your own learning. To that end, there are other support structures available to help you through those challenges that foster collaboration and growth.
