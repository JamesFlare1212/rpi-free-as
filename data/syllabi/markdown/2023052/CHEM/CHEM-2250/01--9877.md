## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is the first term of the Organic Chemistry Lecture series. Students enrolled in this course are required to have successfully completed General Chemistry (or equivalent courses) which cover the fundamentals of the subject matter. In Organic Chemistry I, students will learn nomenclature and structure of functional groups, common reaction mechanisms involving organic compounds, and synthesis of organic molecules.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- (1) Students will be able to explain electronic structure and covalent bonding in organic molecules. Students will be able to explain why certain molecules are more acidic or more basic and how their structures affect acidity strength. Students will demonstrate knowledge of nomenclature and physical properties and how to represent molecules’ structures. (Ch 1-3)
- (2) Students will demonstrate knowledge of arrangement of atoms of organic molecules in three dimensions. Students will demonstrate knowledge of structures, nomenclature, and reactivity of alkenes. Students will demonstrate knowledge of stereochemical outcomes of addition reactions of alkenes. (Ch 4-6)
- (3) Students will demonstrate knowledge of reactions of alkynes and how to synthesize more complex molecules by multistep synthetic routes. Students will demonstrate knowledge of delocalization of electrons and their effect on stability and reactivity. Students will demonstrate knowledge about substitution reactions of alkyl halides. Students will demonstrate knowledge about elimination reactions of alkyl halides. (Ch 7-9)
- (4) Students will demonstrate knowledge of the reactions of alcohols, ethers, epoxides, amines, and thiols. Students will demonstrate knowledge of the oxidation of alcohols. Students will demonstrate knowledge of concepts and reactions of organometallic compounds. Students will demonstrate knowledge of radical reactions of alkanes. (Ch 10-12)

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students will be assessed through homework, quizzes, exams and attendance/participation.

**Homework:** Homework will be assigned for each chapter we complete, and will be turned in using LMS before the due date (generally 7 days). Late assignments will be penalized at 10% per day late. It is well established that students learn much more through active learning than through passive learning. Attending and taking notes in lecture, and reading the textbook are passive learning, while working homework problems is active learning. Therefore, most of your learning will take place while you work homework problems. Some students may find that assigned homework is sufficient for them to master the concepts we study. Others will need more practice before they achieve full mastery. I strongly recommend that you try textbook exercises to supplement the homework assignments since the answers are available to you in the solution manual. I will not assign specific problems from the textbook and they will not be graded. It is up to you to assess your own understanding, and to work enough exercises to achieve your desired level of mastery.

**Quizzes:** Each quiz will be semi-cumulative. Approximately half (or more) of the points on each quiz will be for questions involving material that has not yet been quizzed. The other half of the points will be distributed amongst previous topics. The reason for this is to discourage cramming and ensure that you achieve long-term learning of Organic Chemistry. No subject can be retained long-term if you cram the material. In order to do well on quizzes, you will therefore need to briefly review as much as two weeks’ worth of material. Hopefully, this will substantially decrease your final exam studying needs, since you will have been reviewing and reinforcing knowledge all semester. One consequence of this setup is that early in the semester, there will be no older material to cover, so the new material will account for the entirety of the quiz. Late in the semester, some topics will only be quizzed once or twice because we will run out of time. This means that early chapters will be somewhat over-assessed and later chapters will be somewhat under-assessed.

**Final Exam:** The final exam will be cumulative and will occur on **June 30th**. Attendance at the final exam is mandatory. A grade of 0% will be given for any unexcused absence and make-ups will not be allowed. Absence from the final exam due to travel is always unexcused.

**Semester Exams:** Each exam will be cumulative, with emphasis on material that has not yet appeared on an exam. The distribution of points will be more variable than on quizzes, since a single exam question can be more complicated than a quiz question and incorporate ideas from several chapters. Once again, this will lead to material covered early in the semester being assessed more often than material late in the semester. In order to provide some compensation for this over-emphasis on early material, the contribution of each exam to your final grade may increase throughout semester.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is required at every lecture and recitation and will account for 5% of the final grade. Since it is true that active learning is generally preferable to passive learning, why is attendance important?

*•* I cannot cover all material completely during the lecture time we have. Therefore, I will naturally focus on and emphasize those aspects that are most crucial. It is not always clear which ideas or details are most important from reading a text, where almost all information is given equally thorough treatment. By coming to class, you will learn which ideas are most important, and then you can manage your study and active learning time accordingly.

*•* Some students will learn easily from reading the text. Others will not immediately connect with the text and will benefit from hearing an alternative treatment of the same material with different worked examples. Most students will find themselves falling in both categories at different times throughout the semester. Coming to class will help you clarify and reinforce the understanding you developed by reading.
