## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a graduate level course on the theory and applications of fundamental concepts of Mechanics of Materials to the study of Advanced Solid Mechanics with emphasis on problems involving Elastic behavior. The course presents the key concepts and tools required for the determination of stresses and strains created inside solid materials subjected to external loadings using tensor language. The course starts with the quantitative formulation of motion and deformation and the associated strain and stress tensors. It then proceeds with the formal definition of balance laws and conservation principles. The above are then combined with the constitutive equations describing linear elastic and thermo-elastic material behavior. The course then covers the application of the above concepts through description and implementation of analytical and numerical solution methods for important problems in linear elasticity, including problems in two dimensions (Cartesian and polar coordinates) and three-dimensional systems. Students will work on a term project through the semester. The course is structured around individual students’ projects. Although students will all work on different project topics they must create their milestone reports conforming closely with the chronological structure of the course.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This main objective of this course is to help students develop enough intellectual maturity in advanced mechanics of solids so that they become capable of confidently tackling complex problems in linear thermal elasticity by constructing appropriate mathematical models and obtaining reliable solutions useful in engineering decision-making.  
The course covers the following key topics, each constituting a major learning milestone:

1. ***Motion, Deformation and Stress and Strain Tensors.***
2. ***Balance Laws, Conservation Principles and Constitutive Equations of Material Behavior***
3. ***Two-Dimensional and More Complex Problems in Linear Elasticity.***

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Describe, discuss, explain and formulate the concepts of deformation, stress and strain using tensor language.
- Describe, discuss, explain and formulate the concepts of momentum balance, conservation principles and constitutive relationships of material behavior of linear elastic bodies and beyond.
- Demonstrate a grasp of the analytical and numerical solution methods of problems in linear elasticity through skillful application to development of mathematical models for the analytical and/or numerical determination of stress and strain fields in important situations of engineering interest.
- Be able to critically examine the results of computational solid mechanics calculations and provide sound, authoritative judgement regarding their reliability.
- Create a well written, well-organized cumulative report of their work on a selected problem in mechanics of solids.

**Icebreaker (10%)**  
Students shall complete the “Icebreaker” and submit the requested deliverable no later than the first day of classes. The icebreaker assignment consists of the following FOUR items:

- A brief written bio-sketch (~ 100 words long in PDF format).
- A computer screen capture proving that you have downloaded and installed the required software for this class into a computer and that the software is ready for use.
- A signed statement indicating that you have carefully and thoroughly read through this course syllabus and are aware and in agreement with the information and instructions contained therein.
- The specific topic selected for your term project.

**Milestone Reports**  
Students shall demonstrate their grasp of the subject by creating three Milestone reports. Each Milestone will be directly related to each of the key course topics mentioned above as the topic applies to the student’s chosen term project topic.

**Milestone reports must demonstrate mastery of the course material through its application to the student’s chosen term project topic.** Reports must be carefully edited, well written, persuasive, typeset documents in order answer to earn full credit. All milestone reports MUST be submitted with a cover page containing your name and the milestone number and in PDF format.  
Please plan well ahead to be able to deliver your milestone reports by the indicated deadlines. A 20% grade deduction will be applied to reports submitted late.  
While Milestones 1 and 2 shall describe partial progress, Milestone 3 will be a comprehensive document that, while focusing on the most complex version of the student’s chosen topic, must also incorporate material from the reports produced for Milestones 1 and 2. With the Milestone 3 report students shall demonstrate their ability to independently apply the material covered in class to create a professional-looking, well documented, clearly articulated, concise but thorough narrative of their grasp of the finite element method and its application to the solution of a practical engineering problem of their choice.
