## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Students will integrate information about how the brain functions into their existing knowledge base and expand this to gain a greater understanding of the brain regions involved in specific cognitive functions.
- 2\. Some of the specific topical areas that students will demonstrate being able to describe the function and the brain region(s) involved are: attention, sensory processing, movement, memory, cognitive control, symbolic representations, and emotion.
- 3\. Students will be able to apply their understanding of Cognitive Neuroscience to analyze and interpret data reported in the primary literature as well as popular press, and communicate their perspectives.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Participation and attendance (25%):** Attendance and active engagement in class is mandatory. Students will receive an average grade for attendance (10%) and participation (15%). Participation includes working in groups on activities during class time, contributing to class discussions, and completing reading responses.

**Journal Article Critiques (10%):** We will read 4 journal article readings throughout the semester; articles will be chosen by student groups. Students are expected to read and write critiques of 4 primary literature articles related to the topics discussed in class. Each critique is due on the day that the article will be discussed by 11:59 pm (see schedule). For class sessions indicated, students must write a (1-2 pages, single-spaced, 12-point font) critique of the journal article listed.

**Reflection paper (20%):** Students will complete one reflection paper this semester. Papers should be 3-4 pages in length, single-spaced, 12-point font.

**Exams 1, 2, and 3 (45%; 15% each):** There will be three exams during the semester consisting of multiple-choice, short answer, and true or false questions based upon the material covered in each module. Questions will be related to all materials (e.g. lectures, readings, exercises) covered up until when the exam is given. Exams will be on LMS.

**\*\* Any of these above assignments that are worked on outside of class that will be graded need to be** **submitted via the LMS link by 11:59 pm EST on its due date to be considered on time. See lateness policy below for more details.\*\***

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

***Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism*** In this class, all exams and assignments that are turned in for a grade must be the student’s own original work. We will be focusing on the material a (neuro)science perspective. We must be aware that science is not always objective and unbiased and historically has not included representation of diverse individuals in the experiments or the researchers themselves. For example, individual differences between human subjects in experiments were often minimized or considered confounds because of the added complexity they may have contributed to the analysis of the data. However, in this classroom, we will strive to integrate a diverse set of experiences for a more comprehensive understanding of the material in this course, despite the potential for added complexity. An important part of a college education is learning about the complexity of various issues. We will also expand our understanding by hearing from diverse voices in the field through our readings and discussions of the material. Our classroom will represent a diversity of individual beliefs, backgrounds, and experiences. We all must listen and show respect for every other member of this class even if we do not necessarily agree. Please honor the uniqueness of our classroom community and appreciate the opportunity we have to learn from one another. If you have any questions or concerns, please discuss with me.
