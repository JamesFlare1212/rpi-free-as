## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

We will be using the following free and open source software:

- Gimp or Krita or Photoshop used for raster based image editing. PS will be used for any demos.
- Blender used for model manipulation, UV mapping, basic rigging, rendering.
- CodePen used for assignment turn-ins - requires a free account.
- Cloudinary for media storage and access via CodePen - requires a free account.
- OpenAI ChatGPT account
- AI image generator (Midjourney will be used for demos)

Discord used for class communication and assignment turn-in.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- By the end of the course students should be able to understand: How to author objects utilized in computer-based interactive media. Basic steps involved in creating content for interactive 2D and 3D environments.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- There will be a series of weekly exercises. All assignments will be posted and discussed at the beginning of the week during our official class time.
- References to readings, videos, example projects, and custom code templates will be included in the assignment posting.
- All completed work will be posted to CodePen and linked to in the class Discord. This will require learning basic HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.

We will discuss select projects in a group critique prior to the next assignment posting. Be prepared to screen-share your desktop and describe your process, as well as contribute to our class chat.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

- The exercises will be evaluated in relation to effort, risk, conceptual, technical, and presentation/aesthetic qualities, and ability to follow instructions.
- Completion of technical exercises, class participation during discussions/critiques, and attendance will all contribute to your overall grade.

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
