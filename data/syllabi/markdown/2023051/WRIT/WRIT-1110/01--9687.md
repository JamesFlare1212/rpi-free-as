## Course Goals

### Goals:

1\) Learn to write focused, well-organized texts for a variety of academic audiences and rhetorical situations within a variety of analytical and persuasive genres of writing.

2\) Learn to view writing as a process, to receive constructive feedback from both instructors and peers, and to revise writing through multiple drafts.

3\) Learn to locate reliable sources from credentialed, reputable authors and institutions and to incorporate ideas from these sources into one’s own writing while providing appropriate documentation in the correct citation format.

4\) Learn to view writing within the context of an ongoing conversation, and learn to make worthwhile original contributions to the types of conversations that typically take place in the academy.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

More detailed descriptions will be provided as the semester progresses. All rough drafts of major assignments should be at least 3-3.5 pages. Final drafts should be at least 5 pages. After rough drafts are due, you will receive constructive feedback from either the instructor or TA targeted toward helping you improve your paper. You should schedule a meeting with the TA during their usually scheduled office hours, or at another time that is convenient for both of you, in order to discuss your draft, before the due date for the final draft. Each of these conferences will be worth two homework grades (so, twice as much as the normal homework assignment).

**Writing Assignment 1: Analysis of a Place/Space**

The first writing assignment will ask you to visit a place or space and write an essay about how the way individuals within that space interact with that space, are influenced by that space, and how the way the space is designed/arranged influences the behavior of people within that space. In order to write this paper effectively, you will have to apply techniques such as description, narration, and the use of specific examples to highlight your points, but you will also have to have an overarching thesis/main point about the place/space you are analyzing, and you should be focused on supporting that thesis throughout your paper, so everything you discuss should have a clear connection back to a central thesis.

**Writing Assignment 2: Cause and Effect**

Choose an event or phenomenon where the relationship between cause and effect is unclear or disputed. You can choose either an unclear cause (For example, why are politics in the United States becoming more polarized? Is it because of social media, or some other cause?) or an unclear effect (What are the long term effects of the internet on our brains? Is it negatively impacting our memory and/or attention spans, or does it actually have positive effects?). Find at least 4 secondary sources from reputable authorities that help you better understand if there is a relationship between cause and effect in the first place, as well as how specifically that relationship works. In order to make this argument effectively, you will need to have a clear and focused account of the relationship between cause and effect, as well as an account of how that relationship works, using specific examples as needed in order to convey your points to the reader. You will also have to provide quotes, summaries, and citations from relevant secondary sources in order to show that your discussion is supported by reputable scholarship.

**Writing Assignment 3: Definition**

For the third assignment, students will be constructing a definition of a term that is unclear or disputed and writing an essay arguing for that definition and arguing that one or more examples either does or does not fit the definition (For example, what is the definition of a democracy, and how well do specific examples that are often considered democracies, such as ancient Athens or the contemporary United States, actually fit the definition of a democracy?). In addition, you may want to think about what makes your category distinct from similar or related categories (For example, what is the difference between a democracy and a republic, and which term is most accurate for specific examples such as the contemporary United States?). Students will also be expected to locate and incorporate at least 4 reputable sources in order to support their definition.

**Writing Assignment 4: Analytical Essay**

For the fourth and final writing assignment, students will be asked to write an essay about one or more political, social, and economic events that the student believes have defined their life, or a stage of their life. You can discuss more than one event if you want, but keep in mind the need to stay focused on a specific main point or thesis, so you should usually only discuss more than one event if they are directly related to advancing your thesis. In order to write this paper effectively, students will need to draw on at least 4 reputable sources to help them understand the events they have chosen to discuss as well as their own experiences. You will have to make an argument about cause and effect, select specific examples to show how these events have an impact, and apply any other patterns that you think will help you make your argument effectively.

**Homework and Participation**

This is a process-driven, writing intensive class, and so it is expected that students will attend class regularly and make insightful and relevant contributions to class discussions, and during the typical class session, participation grades will be assigned based on participation in that day's discussion and/or activities. Homework assignments, rough drafts, revision activities, and in-class group work will also count toward the homework and participation part of your grade. In general, so long as the student meets all the requirements of the assignment, homework assignments, in-class activities, and process work such as rough drafts are graded on a pass/fail basis.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

This is a small group class that places a high level of emphasis on writing as a process and on class discussion, so students are expected to attend class regularly and to actively participate in all class sessions. As such, students will get three unexcused absences for the semester with no questions asked. Student athletes should inform me at the beginning of the term regarding any classes they will have to miss during the semester. Other reasons for an excused absence can be submitted through the Office for Student Success. Unexcused absences beyond the third will result in the student’s final grade being lowered by a third of a letter grade (so a B would become a B-, and an A- would become a B+, and so on).

### Other Course Policies:

***LATE WORK POLICY***

Students are expected to submit work on time and to communicate with me in advance if you need an extension for any assignments. No extensions will be granted once the due date for the assignment has passed. Homework assignments and rough drafts will be due at the beginning of class on the day for which they assigned. After that, no late homework assignments will be accepted. Final drafts of major writing assignments will be due at the beginning of class on the day for which they are assigned. Starting the following day, they will be marked down a third of a letter grade for each day they are late (so a B would become a B-, an A- becomes a B+, and so on). If you need an extension for an assignment, contact me before the due date. If you need an extension on a more regular basis, accommodations can be worked out with the Office of Disabled Student Services or with the instructor.

***INCLUSION POLICY***

Our classroom and my office hours are spaces where all are welcome. Please note that discrimination or harassment in any form will not be tolerated in this learning community. Beyond simply avoiding discriminating against others, your presence in this class indicates that you agree to actively promote a welcoming and inclusive learning environment for all, including but not limited to taking the following actions: using inclusive language, such as referring to people by their preferred personal pronouns; understanding that it is your responsibility in class discussions to shape your comments for a diverse audience that may include native and non-native English speakers, people of various genders and sexual orientations, people from various cultural and religious backgrounds, and other identifiers that may differ from your own; listening and not defending when someone from another background takes the time to explain their position; understanding that everyone comes to the classroom with differing levels of access to the dominant culture.

***ACADEMIC INTEGRITY***

All work submitted for this class should be the student’s original work, and should have been written for this class. When students include the ideas of others in their writing, they should make it clear in the text and through in-text citations which ideas and quotes are from outside sources. In cases where group work is assigned, the names of all members in the group must be listed, and the submission must contain only work by those students. Submission of any assignment in violation of this policy will result in a zero on the assignment. A second violation will result in automatic failure of the course and potential disciplinary action by the institution.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
