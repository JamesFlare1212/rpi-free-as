## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Introduction of mathematical and computational methods to model physiological systems in biomedical engineering. Mathematical methods include solution techniques for systems of algebraic equations, systems of differential, and partial differential equations. Computational methods include finite difference, finite element, and lumped parameter methods. Computational methods are programmed using commercial programming software.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

S.M. Dunn, A. Constantinides, P.V. Moghe. Numerical Methods in Biomedical Engineering. Academic Press, New York, 2006.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course teaches students

- How to model biomedical systems that consist of algebraic equations, ordinary differential equations, or partial differential equations
- Different techniques for solving systems of algebraic equations
- A variety of techniques for numerical solution of ordinary differential equations
- Some basic numerical techniques for solving partial differential equations
- Concepts of feedback systems and how feedback can be used to influence the dynamical behavior of systems

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Students will be able to:

- Derive models for biomedical systems using first principles
- Identify key components of models and what needs to be specified for their solution
- Formulate numerical solutions methods for biomedical models
- Implement and simulate models in a commercial software package
- Estimate parameters of models describing biological systems

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Exams:**

There will be a total of three exams. Approximate dates are after 5 weeks, 10 weeks, and during the scheduled time of the final; exact dates will be discussed in class approximately 1-2 weeks before an exam is given. The plan is to give exams during class time to avoid scheduling conflicts, but coordinating with the other section may change that. Closed notes/book. Bringing a laptop to exams is mandatory as many exam problems require MATLAB for their solution.

There will be no make-up exams. If one exam is missed with an excused absence then the final will count for double its usual percentage. If an exam is missed unexcused then this will count as a zero score on the exam. If two exams are missed then the class needs to be retaken.

**Homework:**

Homeworks are the best way to study for the exams and as such, 10 homework sets will be uploaded on Piazza. Homeworks do not have to be handed in and will not be graded, however, we will be happy to discuss homework related questions during office hours.

**Participation in Discussion Forum:**

The class will make use of the Piazza class discussion system:

http://www.piazza.com/

If you have questions about a particular item discussed in class or the homeworks then check if this question has already been posted and answered here.

**Quizzes:**

There will be 5-7 unannounced quizzes given throughout the semester. The quizzes will be short (10 minutes) and the material will cover concepts from the last lecture or two. Some quizzes may require computation and as such, bringing your laptop to each class is highly recommended. There will be no make-up for missed quizzes; instead the quiz with the lowest score will be thrown out (a zero if a quiz was missed) when calculating the quiz average.
