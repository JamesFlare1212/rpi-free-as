## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Course Description: The study of cosmology is currently at the forefront of modern astrophys- ical research. As technology continues to advance, we continue to gain insights into questions about the Universe long thought unanswerable. At its roots though, Cosmology began as a philosophical quest towards explaining some of the most fundamental questions of nature. How did the Universe begin? Did it have a beginning in the first place? Is there an end? What is the nature of the Universe? Do we, as humans, play any role in it? Over the course of this semester we will pon- der these questions ourselves, explore the musings and observations of the early astronomers, and discuss the modern quests towards finding some answers to those fundamental questions. We will also explore some of the more exotic aspects of modern physics and astronomy such as: special and general relativity, black holes, the nature of space and time, and the Big Bang. We’ll take a more conceptual, rather than mathematical, approach to these topics and so this course will be suitable for majors and non-majors but knowledge of algebra and an understanding scientific graphs, tables, and equations is required.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- An ability to apply the scientific method, learned through the lens of its applications in cosmology.
- Knowledge of the history of cosmological thought that led to today’s understanding of the Universe.
- Appreciation of the content and implications of Einstein’s theories of special and general relativity
- Knowledge of the major elements of the Big Bang theory of the Universe.
- An ability to connect foundational knowledge of cosmology to give context to modern unsolved mysteries.
- An awareness of the modern tools being used to solve cosmological mysteries.
- An excitement for all the wonders and questions that the Universe still holds.
