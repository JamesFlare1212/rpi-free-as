## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Course Description

This is an undergraduate level introductory course in Human Evolution. It explores the current knowledge of our own ancestry and evolution. The goal is to provide active learning in a collaborative environment via experiential laboratory activities, interactive discussions, simulations and computational labs. Students will analyze the supporting evidence while tracing the evolutionary relatedness amongst primate species with other vertebrate species via phenotypic and genotypic datasets, and study the impact of disease pathogens and environment on human populations.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Course Goals / Objectives

1. Principle Concepts: Students will be able to master introductory concepts underlying Human Evolution.
2. Predict and Propose: Students will formulate and test hypotheses, for example, compare their Phylogenetic predictions with the Fossil Record, and apply Phylogenetic principles to Human Epidemic Diseases such as HIV and SarsCoV2.

Pre-requisites: Students will be able to take more advanced courses in Evolution and Bioinformatics in future semesters.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate mastery of the foundational knowledge and core concepts of Human Evolution by answering graded multiple choice questions and/or short answer questions in class workbooks and in the Final Exam, and/or by delivering oral presentations to peers in class.
- Apply foundational knowledge and core concepts to make predictions by answering graded multiple choice questions and/or short answer questions in class, and in the Final Exam, and/or by delivering oral presentations to peers in class.
- Propose, predict and present a model on the Future of Human Evolution by gathering information from research articles, texts, the web, and other relevant established sources, and by using the RPI Library, assessment is by oral presentations to peers in class.

1. Learning is interactive, and we encourage this. However, when you are writing anything for submission, answers to questions on Lecture Tests, you must write your answer in your own words. You must not copy from the websites that you used for research.
