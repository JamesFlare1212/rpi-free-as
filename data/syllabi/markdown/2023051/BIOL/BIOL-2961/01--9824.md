## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides students with an introduction to the biology and ecology of aquatic habitats found in upstate New York, with a focus on field sampling techniques and data analysis. Topics covered may include lakes, rivers, wetlands, and other inland water bodies, as well as the unique flora and fauna that inhabit these systems. Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute’s Troy, NY campus is uniquely situated near the Huron Valley, an aquatic ecosystem with both urban and rural land use. This location can provide excellent research opportunities for students who are interested in biotic responses to environmental stressors. In this class students will conduct a semester-long research project as individuals or in groups where they will derive a research question, collect and identify flora or fauna, and prepare novel environmental data for publication in a free data repository. Additionally, students will design a poster or oral presentation to be shared with the rest of class and instructors.  
Class sessions will be a mix of field trips to the Troy Country Club for sampling and laboratory time to identify specimens and curate data. See detailed schedule below for further information.  
While out in the field safety is the number one priority. We will be working in and around water, so extra caution is needed to limit slips / falls. We will be outside without access to washrooms. Please bring water and snacks. Sunscreen and or bug repellent may also be necessary. Ticks and Lyme Disease are common in New York State, and you should check yourself for ticks after each field session.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. Demonstrate basic understanding of aquatic ecology.
2. Derive a research question to assess the impact of an environmental stressor on a biotic community.
3. Design and execute an experimental analysis.
4. Collect, manage, and process data.
5. Prepare data for publication in a publicly accessible data repository.
6. Create and deliver a research talk and/or poster presentation.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Student Projects: Students will work together to complete a semester-long research project. This project will culminate in a poster or oral presentation. Students will have the opportunity to curate and publish their data in the Environmental Data Initiative.  
Skills and Assessments:

This course will introduce students to techniques commonly used in Field Biology. As such, students will be evaluated on essential skills that are required to be a successful field biologist. Students will complete hands-on skill assessments along with traditional written and/or oral exams as appropriate. Assessment topics will pertain to all assets of a field biology including (but not limited to):

Skill 1: knowledge and application of standardized field sampling protocols and equipment.

Skill 2: effectively communicate with team members, especially in field conditions where communication can be challenging.

Skill 3: accurately identify common aquatic organisms and use common identification aides such as dichotomous keys when appropriate.

Skill 4: properly use associated laboratory equipment.

Skill 5: manage, store, and visualize data.

Skill 6: effectively communicate scientific results to a wide range of audiences via poster and/or oral presentations.

\*A detailed rubric with skill assessments will be provided to students before the start of the semester.\*

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

S11757 Students will act as Field Crew Leader for part of a sampling event and will direct other students. A rubric will be provided before class. Peer-feedback will provide leaders with additional tips and ways to improve.  
S11758 This skill will have a cumulative score. Students will be evaluated weekly by their instructors on their ability to communicate and work together as a team. Communication is not always verbal, so digital communication such as email and discussion forms (e.g., Google Docs) will also be important.  
S11759 Students will be given a random subset of organisms and will be asked to identify them. This will be in the style of a traditional examination.  
S11760 Students will be observed by instructors. Instructors will record students’ knowledge and proper use of laboratory equipment.  
S11761 Students will be evaluated by their ability to prepare final scientific figures, and their ability to create uniform data sets consistent with publishing standards such as the Environmental Data Initiative (EDI).  
S11762 Students will deliver a final presentation or poster. Instructors will provide a detailed rubric.
