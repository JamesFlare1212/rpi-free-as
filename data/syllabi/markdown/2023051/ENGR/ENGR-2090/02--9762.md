### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Master the basic principles of particle and rigid body dynamics
- Systematically apply these principles to mechanical systems rising in diverse aspects of technology.
- Solve the mathematical equations that govern these systems, and physically interpret the results.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Each student is responsible for *all* materials presented and *all* announcements made in class. This course will be taught synchronously and asynchronously. You are responsible for viewing the lectures. You are encouraged to do so live on WebEx. However, all lectures will be made available through LMS after the scheduled lecture period.

### Other Course Policies:

**Lecture Notes:**

Lecture notes will be posted in PowerPoint format prior to the scheduled synchronous start of lecture. These notes will be provided through LMS.

**Homework:**

Eleven Homework sets will be assigned, which will usually consist of approximately 4 problems. The schedule of the homework due dates is included in the schedule at the end of this document, and LMS will also inform students of the due dates of homework sets. Homework assignments are to be scanned to the portable document format (.pdf) and must be handed submitted at the **beginning of class** on the dates given in the syllabus. **No late homework assignments will be accepted**. For homework grading, only one out of the assigned homework problems will be randomly chosen for grading. 50% of the credit will be given to effort in attempting all homework problems, and the other 50% will be given to solving the selected homework problem, which will be announced on the day the homework is due. **The lowest homework grade will be dropped.**

You are encouraged to collaborate with other students on homework assignments, however, each student is expected to submit their own homework.

**In Class Assignments**

Some lectures, an “in-class assignment” will be collected and graded. This problem statement will be due within 12 hours of the scheduled lecture period, to account for asynchronous students’ schedules. Some of these assignments will be single problems, while some lectures will feature digital assignments assigned through LMS. You may use any available resource to help you complete problem statements, including direct help from the professor. Digital assignments given through LMS are expected to be solely the work of the individual student. The lowest grade will be dropped at the end of the semester, in addition to any excused absences.

Project:

There will be a project to complete during the semester. This project will be the creation of a PowerPoint Presentation demonstrating how to solve a Engineering Dynamics Problem. Detailed instructions on how to create this project will be provided on LMS. Note : No Oral presentations will be conducted. **Failure to submit an acceptable attempt of this project will result in, at a minimum, a reduction of one letter grade at the end of the term. You must attempt this project.**

**Exams:**

Three exams (open book, open notes) are scheduled on the syllabus. **The exams will be online examinations, which are individually created and assigned on a per student basis.** Exams must be completed. If you are in need of a makeup examination, you must contact the instructor for a makeup exam If for *any reason, including excused absences*, more than one exam must be missed, an incomplete will be given for this class, and you will need to retake the course. Due to the external circumstances surrounding this fall semester, the timing of this exam will be determined on an ad hoc basis for each exam.

The final exam must be taken to receive a grade for this course. This final exam will be administered during finals week, however, as this is a core engineering class, you may find a conflict with another course. As such, you must submit a request to take a makeup exam no later than 1 week prior to the final examination. This final examination will be comprehensive, in that the material of this course will continually build upon itself during the semester.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Homework:**

Collaboration among the students in the class is permitted and, in fact, encouraged, as described above. Seeking help from other sources is also encouraged. However, the work you submit should be your own. Evidence of you merely copying another student’s homework (or from the homework solutions) will be considered academic dishonesty and will result in **a zero grade for homework for the semester for all parties involved.**

**Exams:**

Collaboration during an exam (exchanging verbal or written information or copying) is, of course, prohibited. In addition, use of email or the internet during exams for any purpose is strictly prohibited. The use of Chegg, Yahoo! Answers, Quora, Discord, Twitter, Facebook, messaging programs of any kind, or other materials that are not assigned through the course are prohibited. You will be required to take and complete an integrity quiz on LMS prior to the start of exams, indicating that you accept the terms under which the exams are given.

**Unauthorized collaboration or compromising of any examination will result in investigations. All parties involved will receive an F in the course.**

**Other:**

Outrageous offenses such as arranging for a substitute to take an examination, attempting to obtain examination questions or solutions in advance, or attempting to change a grade record will result in a failing grade for the course and a recommendation to the Dean of Students that the student(s) in question be suspended.

If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification
