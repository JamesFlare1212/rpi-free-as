## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course description:** The course provides an opportunity for the development of theorem-proving skills in the field of mathematical analysis. Expansion of a knowledge base comes as a by-product of energy expended in theorem proving and subsequent exposition. Analysis topics included are: sets, functions, the real numbers, cardinality, induction, decimal representations of real numbers, Euclidean spaces, abstract vector spaces, and metric spaces. This is a communication-intensive course.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Analysis With and Introduction to Proof, Fifth ed., by Steven R. Lay, Pearson, 2014.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate

- read mathematical proofs and verbally communicate how each sentence contributes to deducing the assertion,
- solve problems in analysis,
- write high quality mathematical proofs, and
- write LaTeX syntax for mathematics and mathematical equations.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Worksheet discussion group grades are based on attendance and participation. Quizzes can only be taken during class. Attendance required for full points on assessments both Mondays and Thursdays.

### Other Course Policies:

**Homeworks:** Homework is due at the times posted on LMS. Late homework will receive a 10% deduction of total points for each day, or part thereof, late and will not be accepted after 3 days late and thus must be submitted no later than 11:59 PM on Sundays. For homework handed in late due to an **excused ab-sence,** *an adjustment to the late penalty will be made* ***at the discretion of the instructor***. This is gener-ally reserved for extended illness and when notification of the absence was done in a timely manner.

At the end of the semester, a student’s homework grade will be calculated out a smaller number than the total number of homework points available. For example, if there are 320 homework points at the end of the semester, the homework grade might be calculated out of 300 points with a cap of 300 so that the max homework grade is 100%.

**Quizzes:** Quizzes will be 20 minutes in length and will be given on Thursdays during lecture. Quizzes will be over material covered the prior week in lecture. They will typically consist of 3-4 questions includ-ing definitions, multiple choice/multiple select, short answer and one proof. No make-up quizzes will be given for any reason. Students who miss a quiz due to an excused absence documented by the Student Success office will be excused from a quiz. Additionally, if a student has zero (0) or one (1) excused absence from quizzes, then one (1) quiz will be dropped when calculating the overall quiz grade at the end of the semester.

**Exams:** The material covered on Exams will be announced on LMS when the date of the exam is near. Exams will be given during lecture. Exams will be ***closed book and closed notes*** and will be timed. Ex-ams will include some combination of definitions, true/false, multiple choice, short answer problems and proofs. Make-up exams will only be given to students with an excused absence documented by the Student Success office. **Missing an exam should not be taken lightly.** Thus, students missing an exam due to an excused absence should notify the instructor to make arrangements for a make-up exam ***before the exam begins***. If a student with an excused absence fails to inform the instructor within 12 hours of the exam, it is at the discretion of the instructor to allow the student to take a make-up exam with possibility of a penalty applied. If a student misses an exam due to an unexcused absence (e.g., did not know there was an exam, overslept, etc.), then the student must inform the instructor within 12 hours of the exam. The student may elect to take a make-up exam at a time arranged by the instructor and a 30% penalty will be applied to the score. All make-up exams will be given on the Friday immediately following the scheduled exam.

**Grade appeals:** Changes to exam, quiz or homework grades will be made only if something has been overlooked. Grade appeals will be made on Gradescope. All appeals must be made **during the grade appeal window** specified on Gradescope for the exam, quiz or homework once the grade has been published on Gradescope. Before submitting an appeal please check Gradescope comments on graded assignments.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic integrity:** Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://rpi.app.box.com/s/3f1cl3w1lt1mq8gqla68v6uk3vh1c19f) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted for this class must be the student’s own. A student may not change an answer on anything before submitting it for an appeal. Additionally you will be asked to “sign” an honor code that indicates your work is your own and you have not given or received any help on exams. Students must follow the guidelines under the **Homework** section for all homeworks submitted for the course. The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
