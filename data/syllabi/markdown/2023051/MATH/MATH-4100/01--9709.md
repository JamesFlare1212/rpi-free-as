## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

There are plenty of good textbooks in linear algebra. Our course is based on the following four books. Two of them are: Linear Algebra, second ed., by Kenneth Hoffman and Ray Kunze and Introduction to Linear Algebra, Fifth ed., by Gilbert Strang, Wellesley-Cambridge Press, 2016, ISBN: 978-09802327-7-6

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Student learning outcomes: Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate knowledge and understanding of the basic ideas of Linear Algebra, including concepts of linear systems, independence, theory of matrices, linear transformations, determinants, vector spaces and subspaces, bases and dimension, eigenvalues, eigenvectors and diagonalization, inner products, etc. write clear and accurate proofs using the concepts of this course, apply Linear Algebra concepts of this course to solve problems.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

What do this course expect the students to be able to do?

1\) demonstrate knowledge and understanding of the basic ideas of Linear Algebra  
2\) write clear and accurate proofs using the concepts of this course,  
3\) apply Linear Algebra concepts of this course to solve problems.  
How can assessment provide evidence of students' progress toward these goals?  
In this course, we will have:  
1\) Three Exams over the semester- total 65% of their grade.  
There will be 3 exams over the semester (duration, around 1h and 40 min each). The students will be tested with exams with 5 questions each without using calculators, notes and formula sheets.  
2\) 3 Special Quizzes- total 5% of their grade  
They are gonna be given during the class before each exam. They will be tested with some true or false questions before their exams in order to recognize some topics they are not dominating.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams and quizzes must be the student’s own. A student may not change an answer on an exam or quiz before submitting it for an appeal. In addition, no calculators, computers, tablets, open books, open notes, phones, headphones, or smartwatches may be used during exams or quizzes. Talking or communicating with other students in any way during exams or quizzes is also prohibited. Students may discuss homework with other students, the TA, and the instructor, but the work submitted must be the student’s own. Submissions with glaring similarities will violate academic integrity polices.

The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
