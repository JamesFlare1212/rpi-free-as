- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

For the homework, you are encouraged to collaborate by exchanging ideas and brainstorming solutions. You can work in groups in which you discuss the problems and help each other out if you are stuck. You are also allowed to use books and informative websites such as Wikipedia. However, in all cases, what you turn in must be your own work, and copying of any kind is forbidden. Examples include copying from fellow students (this includes partitioning the homework among several students without everybody's participation on every problem), contacting helpers outside of exam rooms using electronic or other means, wholesale copying of solutions from the internet (both recycled problems from past versions of this class or solutions of the assigned problems from any other source), using solvers such as Maple or Mathematica (unless allowed for a specific problem), using cheat sites such as Chegg or Course Hero, or using AI cheat sites such as ChatGPT.  
For the exams, you will be allowed a one-page, two-sided crib sheet of notes in your own handwriting. When taking the exams, you are not to discuss the questions with anyone but the proctoring instructor and/or TAs. Copying from fellow students is strictly forbidden, as is using any type of books, notes, or electronic aids.
