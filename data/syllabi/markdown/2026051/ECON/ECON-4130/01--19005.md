## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Course Description: This course addresses classical and contemporary issues in the theory of money and banking. Topics covered include: financial markets and interest rates, commercial banking, the Federal Reserve System, theories of money demand and money supply, and the theoretical and practical aspects of monetary policy and economic stabilization. Subject matter classifies as macroeconomics, but also draws from microeconomic fundamentals.

Learning Outcomes: (1) to gain a factual knowledge of money, banking, and the financial system; (2) to practice interpreting data in the monetary context; (3) to understand monetary policy and its implications for national and world economies.

Course Format: ECON-4130 will meet in the classroom two times per week during the scheduled times for lecture, discussion, and problem-solving; Attendance is not required. All assignments and tests will be delivered and submitted online. Office hours are available via Webex video conference.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Tests: There will be three tests administered online via LMS. The basis for tests will be all lecture material. Tests will be completed out of the classroom and will be due on the date specified in the course calendar in this syllabus. Tests are open book/note/resource and utilize questions of multiple choice/numerical answer. *AI Policy: We will allow the honor-code use of AI as a learning tool. If AI is used to find an answer to a question, you are asked to turn in a written explanation of the answer after submitting the test in LMS. We will discourage the use of AI to simply copy answers without understanding the question or answer, as this undermines the teaching and learning process.* Additionally, test revisions are allowed; after you complete your test, turn in to professor by the next class session a written explanation on each question that you got wrong. **MAKEUP TEST POLICY:** *Makeup tests will only be allowed at the discretion of professor. If allowed, the makeup test is scheduled five days after the test.*

Fed Essay: Students will submit a written analysis of the most recent meeting (upcoming) of the Federal Open Market Committee, to be submitted in LMS by the due date specified in the course calendar; specific instructions for content are forthcoming. The policy for academic integrity with regards to plagiarism from any source including from former students is in full effect for this and all work completed. Late Policy: essays are accepted late only by permission of professor and only if permission is requested within 2 days after the due date.

Participation: Participation is a graded component of this course and is tracked as either attendance OR the submission of a journal for each class missed. The participation score is calculated as the percentage of classes attended OR submitted as journal out of total classes held; note: there will be 21 classes held. Professor will take attendance at the beginning of each class session. If you are in attendance, you will receive credit for that class session; if you are late and miss the attendance call, it is your responsibility to let professor know at the end of class. If you are more than 30 minutes late, your score for that day will be reduced by 20% and you may make up for the loss of credit by submitting a journal for that day. If you are not in class, in order to receive credit you must submit a 350 word essay in LMS to describe the topic of class that day; see the topics by date in the course calendar of this syllabus. The journal is due by 12 PM/noon on the day of the missed class. If the journal is late, credit will be reduced by 20%.

Final Grades: Final grades will be broken down as follows, rounded to the nearest whole number: A 94 to 100; A- 90 to 93; B+ 87 to 89; B 84 to 86; B- 80 to 83; C+ 77 to 79; C 74 to 76; C- 70 to 73; D+ 67 to 69; D 60 to 66; F 59 and below.
