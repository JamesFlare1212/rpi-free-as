Welcome to MANE6170 Mechanics of Solids, a graduate level class in solid mechanics. The foundation of the class is the analysis of static linear elastic problems. Depending on students’ interests, it can then be extended to the investigation of more complex problems such as those involving non-linear material behavior, contact mechanics, multi-physics and dynamic phenomena.

## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a graduate level course on the theory and applications of fundamental concepts of mechanics of materials to the solution of problems involving static elastic behavior and sets the foundation for tackling a much broader set of practical problems in mechanics of solids. Key concepts and tools required for the determination of stresses and strains created inside solid materials subjected to external loadings in multi-dimensional systems are presented using various coordinate systems and also concise tensor language. The course starts with the quantitative formulation of motion and deformation and the associated important concepts of displacement vector, deformation gradient, strain, and stress tensors. It then proceeds with the description of the fundamental governing equations (momentum balance and conservation principles). The above are then combined with the constitutive equations describing the material response to applied loads focusing on linear elastic material behavior. Students learn to apply the concepts above by working on individual projects in close collaboration with the instructor to problems that may go well beyond the initial focus on linear elastic materials through the implementation of analytical and numerical solution methods for carefully selected practical problems. The course is structured around individual students’ projects. While students work on separate and distinct project topics they learn the subject and report their progress in synchrony with the structure of the course.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The course covers the following key topics, each constituting a major learning milestone:

1. ***Motion, Deformation Metrics and Stress and Strain Tensors.***
2. ***Balance Laws, Conservation Principles and Constitutive Equations of Material Behavior***
3. ***Two-Dimensional and More Complex Problems in Linear Elasticity and beyond.***

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Describe, discuss, explain and formulate the concepts of deformation, stress and strain using tensor language.
- Describe, discuss, explain and formulate the concepts of momentum balance, conservation principles and constitutive relationships of material behavior of linear elastic bodies and beyond.
- Demonstrate a grasp of the analytical and numerical solution methods of problems in linear elasticity through skillful application to development of mathematical models for the analytical and/or numerical determination of stress and strain fields in important situations of engineering interest.
- Be able to critically examine the results of computational solid mechanics calculations and provide sound, authoritative judgement regarding their reliability.
- Create a well written, well-organized cumulative report of their work on a selected problem in mechanics of solids.

### **Milestone Reports**

Students shall demonstrate their grasp of the subject by creating three Milestone reports. Each Milestone will be directly related to the key course topics mentioned above. The student, in close collaboration with the instructor, is responsible for creating milestone reports that seamlessly integrate the subject matter contents with the specific term project topic selected. **Milestone reports must demonstrate mastery of the course material through its application to the student’s chosen term project topic.** Reports must be carefully edited, well written, persuasive, typeset documents in order to earn full credit. All milestone reports MUST be submitted in PDF format with a cover page containing your project title, your name and the milestone number.

Please plan well ahead to be able to deliver your milestone reports by the indicated deadlines.

Milestone reports are cumulative. Milestones 1 and 2 shall describe partial progress and detailed feedback will be provided upon review by your instructor. Students shall use the feedback provided to edit and improve their reports to aim to the production of the final Milestone 3 report which will be a comprehensive, high-quality document fully reflective of the student’s learning experience**. In their Milestone 3 report students shall demonstrate ability to apply independently the material covered in class to create a well-documented, clearly articulated, concise but thorough narrative of their grasp of the principles of advanced solid mechanics and their application to the solution of a relevant engineering problem.**
