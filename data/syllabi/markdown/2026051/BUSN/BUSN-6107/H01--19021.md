## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Change and innovation occur through the people of an organization. Students in this course use mentoring and coaching tools to invoke the highest performance from employees, and to focus on the achievement of organizational goals and strategy. Students in this course are mentored by a Master Mentor, and in turn use that methodology with three mentees to build their own mentorship capacity. Students help their mentees set SMART goals, make recommendations for performance improvement, and evaluate the achievement of goals. By using a mentorship approach, students ensure that each individual is directly aligned with and is contributing to the organization’s change and innovation.

Credits Allocated: 3 semester hours

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Determine the degree of change or innovation needed in order for the unit or organization to achieve its objectives.
- Communicate change or innovation as a purposeful activity to improve performance.
- Articulate change or innovation as a set of performance metrics to be achieved.
- Deploy the Rensselaer Development Model as a mentoring framework to engage employees and invoke the highest individual and unit performance.
- Use expertise, reflective listening, SMART goal setting, and planning to align people with organizational and unit direction.
- Facilitate meaningful and purposeful meetings with employees to achieve results.
- Deploy the individual problem-solving technique to help employees diagnose and solve problems.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

### ***Evaluation Criteria***

Learning is evaluated in this course through a sequence of four applied projects. Shown as well are the value of the project in the calculation of the overall course grade and the approximate due date of the project. The projects are listed below:

1. Building Mentorship Capacity (25% of total grade). Due in week 3
2. Mentoring Exercise 1 (25% of total grade). Due in week 8.
3. Mentoring Exercise 2 (25% of total grade). Due in week 10.
4. Mentoring Exercise 3 (25% of total grade). Due in week 12.

### Academic Integrity

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Dean of Students office website defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs). and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. Of course, learning with and collaborating with others in the class is encouraged – work submitted must be completely your own and original. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy may result in a penalty of failure of the assignment or of the course, depending on the severity. A grade of zero will be given on the first assignment where a violation of the policies described above is detected, and a referral to the Dean of Graduate Education. If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of “F” for the course and referral to the Dean. If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
