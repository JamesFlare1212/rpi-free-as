### Additional Information for the Instructor Section:

Welcome to the Data-Driven Decision-Making course! This course has been designed with the input of industry experts and employers to give you experience deploying an overarching analytical framework with which to address problems and decisions faced by organizations in every sector.  
Employers we interviewed are adamant – the course should be focused on the application of analytical approaches but should not focus too intently on any specific software tool. Tools change continuously over time – and every employer has a different set of software tools they use. In fact, industry experts make it clear that in many organizations, “analysts” may know how to use a particular tool very intently, but quite often are not well versed in the underlying analytic approach: being able to frame a question or problem in a way that can be informed with data, structuring the question or problem in an analytical manner, specifying which data/variables can be used to represent the question or problem, preparing the data, running the analytical tests, interpreting the results within the context of the question or problem, and finally articulating out actionable recommendations to stakeholders in the context of that problem.  
This course has been structured to specifically address this issue – the course is based on a series of projects, each of which presents a question or problem and then guides you through the steps described above. As you continue through the course and the program, you will become well versed in – and you’ll demonstrate mastery in – the sound underlying approach such that you will be able to use the approach in any context, any industry, any situation. You’ll have gained the wisdom of a seasoned analyst in that you’ll be able to adjust the approach to best fit the problem at hand so that you get results that are meaningful, useful, and actionable.  
Before you get started, review this document to familiarize yourself with the course, the course structure, expectations, outcomes, and the flow of work.

## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Students frame questions and problems in forms that can be analyzed using data analytic tools. Students use data wrangling and preparation methods to prepare for analysis. Students use analytical tools to evaluate analytic models using linear/nonlinear multivariate methodologies using Excel. Students validate results and develop recommendations, predictions and forecasts. Students work with stakeholders to scope and frame questions and problems so that actionable results can be achieved.

Prerequisites: Graduate Standing

Credits Allocated: 3 semester hours

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate use of data in an analytic framework to evaluate and respond, in context, to a problem, decision or research question.
- Utilize data management techniques to assemble data from multiple sources while maintaining the integrity of the inquiry.
- Build exploratory techniques and descriptive analytics to create insights from the data.
- Use regression techniques to estimate the relationship between variables within the context of a problem, decision or research question.
- Use statistical tools and techniques to evaluate the explanatory power of analytic results, within the context of the problem, decision or research question.
- Use the results of analytic inquiry to prepare predictions and forecasts that can be statistically and contextually defended.
- Apply leadership tools to facilitate inquiry and change processes within an organization.
- Create a business proposal that demonstrates actionable insights and recommendations to leadership.
- Communicate with purpose and clarity in written and oral formats.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

### Evaluation Criteria

This course is comprised of four overall projects. You will complete each project by completing multiple deliverables. The scenario, the challenge, the approach, and the deliverables for each project are detailed upfront, as are the criteria (rubric) that will be used to evaluate your work. You then interact with the provided learning resources and materials that will help guide you through the completion of the project. Synchronous sessions via ZOOM will also assist as you complete each project. In these sessions, you will have the opportunity to discuss options and ideas with other students as well as your Mentor.

- **Project 1.** Representing a question or problem in an equation
- **Project 2.** Finding appropriate data to represent relationships
- **Project 3.** Evaluating relationships using statistics
- **Project 4.** Evaluating more complex relationships using analytics

Each project builds on the one(s) prior - the goal is for you to successfully complete all four (4) projects while receiving feedback along the way. Therefore, all projects will be iteratively completed through multiple submissions and through multiple instances of feedback. Guidelines and further information about re-submissions will be covered in the details of your deliverables.  
For each project and milestone, you will be given a "rubric" which simply translates to a list of the criteria your Faculty Mentor will use to evaluate your project. You can also use this list to self-evaluate your work before you submit. You will receive feedback from your Faculty Mentor if your submission can be improved.  
If there are 10 elements in the rubric for a particular project, you must work toward a final deliverable that shows appropriate mastery of all 10 items - if you are good on 9, but not so good on 1, you must still achieve appropriate mastery on that project - in this program, each project builds on the prior so you want to make sure you are at the appropriate level before you move to the next. Otherwise, you may have problems in the next project.
