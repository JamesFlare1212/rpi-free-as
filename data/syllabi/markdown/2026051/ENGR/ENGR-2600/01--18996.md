## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Appreciation and understanding of uncertainties and the conditions under which they occur, within the context of the engineering problem-solving pedagogy of measurements, models, validation, and analysis. Problems and concerns in obtaining measurements; tabular and graphical organization of data to minimize misinformation and maximize information; and development and evaluation of models. Concepts will be supported with computer demonstration. Applications to problems in engineering are emphasized.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course teaches students

- An appreciation for uncertainty in the application of engineering models;
- The difference between probability and statistics;
- Discrete and continuous distribution functions;
- Marginal and conditional distributions;
- Descriptive statistics;
- Sample distributions and point estimates;
- Confidence intervals;
- Hypothesis testing.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Students completing the course successfully will be able to:

- Solve for the probability and conditional probability of events in various scenarios,
- Obtain and graph information about random variables and variable sets,
- Identify variable distributions correctly and apply various discrete and continuous distribution functions,
- Construct and interpret one-sample and two-sample confidence intervals
- Formulate hypothesis tests and perform the tests using the method of critical region, p-value, and confidence intervals.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Tests and final exam (80%):**  
– There are three 1.5-hour tests and a 2-hour final exam.  
– The three tests count for 15%, 20%, and 20% of the course grade, respectively. The final exam covers the entire semester and counts for 25% of the course grade.  
– All tests are cumulative, but with emphasis on the material since the last test.  
– All tests and the final exam are closed-book and in-person. Relevant formulas and statistical tables will be provided.  
– To pass the course, a student must take at least two tests and the final exam.  
– The lowest test grade (including a zero grade for an excused absence) can be replaced by the final exam grade if you have taken at least two tests before the final exam.  
– A zero-grade due to an unexcused absence cannot be replaced.  
One of the essential skills for an engineer is the ability to clearly communicate their ideas and processes in problem-solving. Therefore, the majority of the points are awarded for showing your work properly in tests and projects. Examples of sufficient, detailed work shown can be found on LINUS LMS past test solutions.  
**Projects (10%):** There are two projects based on R, each worth 5% of the overall grade. Late submissions receive a reduction of 25% per day from points earned.  
**Quizzes (10%):** There are quizzes taken remotely via LINUS LMS (https://mau.linuslearning.net/) on Thursdays. If you prefer to take the quizzes in person, they will be in the classroom immediately after the lecture on Thursdays. Missing quizzes with valid excuses will be substituted by the final exam grade. The lowest quiz grade will be dropped. To pass the course, a student must complete at least five quizzes.
