## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Course Description  
Ceramic continues to be deployed in contemporary buildings around the world in innovative ways  
using digital technology in the form of tiles, bricks, blocks and extrusions. The course will  
examine several clay concepts that criss-cross between architecture, sculpture and construction.  
This seminar will involve the design and construction of a 1:1 ceramic assembly. The objective will  
be to produce a set of modular prints that conform to the given envelope of a cone. The seminar  
will be run as a lab to investigate and test forms of assembling ceramic modules. The course will  
provide each student with an opportunity to study and produce prototypes and to develop methods for  
adapting designs to the limitations of the clay. A considerable portion of this course involves the  
preparing clay recipes for the extruder and printing elements on a weekly basis. Developing a high  
level of craft through making prints will be a priority. This course also provides a platform to  
actively deploy architectural & engineering principles. It will be conducted as a seminar and  
workshop and will introduce students to a variety of design methodologies that are unique to  
ceramic design. Designs will be carried out, both during class hours and outside of class time.  
Work will be pugged on given days and fired on given days in the SOA ceramic lab that may fall  
outside of class hours so that class time may be maximized. The course will engage in many of the  
considerations that are affiliated with CAD/CAM production; digital modeling, efficient use of materials,  
material methods, mold making, lightweighting, and optimization.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Outcome 1 Fabrication Research & Design: Ability to troubleshoot material and CNC limitations and integrate them into an realizable multi part extruded ceramic object (MPECO), and the use of ceramic case studies to deduce and inform a design methodology.
- Outcome 2 Fabrication Stability: Ability to resolve the gravity loads for a proposed design of an extruded ceramic object (ECO) through analysis and iterative material testing
- Outcome 3 Prototyping: Ability to successfully 3d print, mold, or build a vertically assembled multi part extruded ceramic object (MPECO) using a porous ceramic medium.
- Outcome 4 Fabrication Tolerance: Ability to compensate for the necessary material tolerances and mating of the parts in the production of a multi part extruded ceramic object (MPECO).
- Outcome 5: Fabrication Surface Finish: Ability to successfully minimize flaws, and glaze fire a multi part extruded ceramic object (MPECO).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic Integrity  
The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic  
Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between  
students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the  
structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students  
turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermine’s the educational process. Cheating,  
plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of  
another person’s work, violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust will merit  
a grade of zero on the first assignment where a violation is detected. If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of F for the course
