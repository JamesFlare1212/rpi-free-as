## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- OUTCOME 1 - HISTORY AND THEORY OF URBAN FORM: Students will live in Rome throughout the semester. Students will be able to differentiate between Renaissance and Baroque architectural history, theories, and urban developments of Rome. Students will have the ability to analyze either a palazzo or a church along with its associated public space.
- OUTCOME 2 - CULTURAL IMMERSION : Students will live in Rome throughout the semester. Students will engage in a culturally immersive process, primarily consisting of field trips and project site visits, to understand Rome's historical and architectural significance.
- OUTCOME 3 - RESEARCH AND ANALYSIS: Students will be able to demonstrate an ability to observe, document, and analyze architectural and urban precedents through the distinct formal ordering systems of fields, objects, vectors, and figure-ground.
- OUTCOME 4 - COLLABORATION: Students will have the ability to collaborate and work in teams.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process. Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust will merit a failing grade in this course and the violator(s) will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.
