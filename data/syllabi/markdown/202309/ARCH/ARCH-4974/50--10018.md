## Course Goals

### Goals:

Course Aims:

- Provide an overview of the city’s history and transformations.
- Support a focus on urban form, historic layering and placemaking.
- Promote the understanding architectural projects within the urban context.
- Exercise critical thought by developing an argument through drawings and keynotes.
- The acquisition of a deeper understanding of the city layout, its topography, and infrastructures.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- At the end of the course students will be able to: •Understand the morphology of the historic center of Rome.
- At the end of the course students will be able to: • Locate and understand the relationships between different crucial parts of the city.
- At the end of the course students will be able to: • Communicate effectively through sketching and on-site drawing.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**EVALUATION CRITERIA**

- *Sketchbook*: Each student will produce sketches and analytical hand drawings using the course sketchbook, both during the on-site visits as well as outside of class meeting times. A minimum of 5 distinct drawings is required for each site visit. These will be evaluated and graded for consistent maintenance and efficacy as a tool for recording and analyzing course material.
- Site Visit A3 Boards: Each student will, create a visual layout for each site visit, reflecting critical observation through synthetic juxtaposition.
- Final Exhibition Board. Students will compare two related sites chosen together with the instructor according to a specific theme.
- Visual Portfolio: *ARCH4653 Architecture of the City FALL2023 LASTNAME FIRSTNAME.pdf*
  - o A document of 3 pages with your selection of ANY THREE Site Visit A3 Boards (the Sheet remains in Horizontal Orientation).
  - o Uploaded Submissions must be received before students are permitted to pass the course – specific deadline and instructions to be indicated by the instructor.
- Attendance and participation: Demonstrate consistent, active participation in seminar discussions: posing questions, attempting synthesis of ideas in open inquiry throughout.

\*\* NOTES on Site Visit Boards

What to submit:

A layout reflecting critical observation through synthetic juxtaposition of hand drawings. A3 horizontal format (42cm x 29.7cm) consisting only of hand drawn work from your sketchbook (no vector or image-based augmentation), organized in a digital layout, with text labels either handwritten or applied digitally. Scan or save as:

- Last name. A3.01.jpg (one for each Site Visit 01 through 10)
- Flattened, no layers.
- Width 4961px
- Resolution 150ppi

A3 Boards should contain:

- Legible Signature & date in lower right corner
- Name of sites or buildings (with street names if shown in plan)

**A**: Excellent work demonstrates that the student has excelled in satisfying all objectives and work methods. Characterized by exceptionally well-crafted work product that demonstrates a heightened level of understanding, intellect, and care in execution. Work is completed on time through a rigorous, continuously evolving, iterative/recursive process. Presentation strategies exhibit superior quality verbal, graphic and intellectual enthusiasm that carry the work beyond directives, expectations, and goals articulated in assignments. Clear willingness to engage in critical dialogue about design (architecture, interior design, and landscape architecture) with both faculty and peers must be evident. Habitual study is evidenced by production of new work each time the studio meets.

**B**: Above Average work demonstrates the student has satisfied all objectives and work methods outlined for the course, often exceeding those expectations. Strong, well-crafted work that demonstrates care and consideration of program, place, and use. Work product demonstrates initiative and represents effort in

exceeding baseline expectation. The work is well developed intellectually, completed on time, and demonstrates continuous engagement with the design process. Effort demonstrates willingness to engage in the critical dialogue about design (architecture, interior design, and landscape architecture) with both faculty and peers, and exhibits strong evidence of iterative study. New work is developed for every studio meeting.

**C**: Average work demonstrates that the student has adequately satisfied all explicitly articulated course objectives and work methods. The work is characterized by competent craftsmanship, on time delivery and satisfactory effort. Presentation strategies demonstrate reasonable effort to communicate intellectual underpinnings and design process. Communication demonstrates an ability to listen and respond to the critical dialogue about design (architecture, interior design, and landscape architecture) with faculty and peers. Iterative development of work evident.

**D**: Below Average work demonstrates that the student has not satisfied all course objectives and work methods and that there is clear need for improvement. Work is incomplete and/or executed with inconsistent or flawed craftsmanship demonstrating a lack of effort, lethargy, or inability to engage the design process. Characterized by an unwillingness to listen and respond to critical dialogue about design (architecture, interior design, and landscape architecture) with either faculty or peers. Little to no evidence of iterative study.

**F**: Failing work demonstrates that the student has not satisfied many course objectives and work methods. Work is incomplete and executed with crude and rudimentary craftsmanship, lacking merit. Characterized by a demonstrated lack of engagement with the course content and/or objectives, an unwillingness to listen and respond to the critical dialogue of faculty, and an undeveloped command of the basic skills. Evidence of iterative study absent.

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
