## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**ANEXACT ANATOMIES I: Robotic Action Painting & Painterly Effects**

*Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute School of Architecture Seminar Fall 2023*

*Troy, NYS*

The aim for this course is to interrogate the interrelationships between machine tools, machine instruction, material behavior and 3D geometry. More specifically we will be developing robotic painting tools and techniques that target robotic toolpathing and painting actions through robotic motion. Drawing, image sampling and 3D geometry is transferred into a designed toolpath that a robot arm or CNC machine follows in space. Each toolpath may consist of a series of planar ‘drawings’ that are successively ‘dribbled’, ‘splashed’, ‘deposited’ or ‘smeared’ onto a canvas or planar material surface. Each instructed stroke will leave a trace of material; whether it is sharp lines, thick and viscous smears, dry and powdery smudges or light and watery dots. We will be as interested in the design of the toolpath as we are in the design of overall ‘painting’. The course will explore the anexact through precise geometry and toolpaths that will acquire character and articulation in the form of pattern and texture through painterly effects. It is where the precision of the digital realm may loosen as the result of a negotiation between geometry, machine and material/paint.

In addition to the fabrication of a series of canvas/material surfaces, students will also produce a series of robotic end arm tools such as a brush/pen mounts, dripping mechanisms, sponge grippers, or other. The course will culminate in a final exhibition displaying a series of artpices, illustrated tool paths and a record of your work in the robot lab.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

COURSE POLICY

Pin-ups

Periodically students will be required to present their work publicly in a formal review process. These presentations are a critical part of the architectural education. It is an opportunity for the student and the faculty to judge the project and critically evaluate the process of design. It may be appropriate at points in the semester to evaluate only selected projects in order to convey information relevant to the entire class. In this situation it is important for each student to witness and participate in the review process. Even if your project is not being reviewed, the criticism and discussion should inform the evaluation of your own progress and design.

Desk Critiques

The design course environment involves two-way communication. The role of the studio critic is to evolve and strengthen the student’s design process. Every time the student meets with the faculty, he or she must have new iterations of work based on previous conversations. If the student has not produced new work, the faculty reserves the right to skip the critique and continue with other students who have produced new material.
