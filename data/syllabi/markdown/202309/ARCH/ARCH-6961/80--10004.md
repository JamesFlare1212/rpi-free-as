## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will provide an introduction to geospatial representation and design methods through the context of an urban research and design project.  
Each project in this seminar will focus their research on one of many suggested urban areas threatened by climate change. These cities are significant urban populations under threat from flooding, drought, fire, or other cause. We will begin the semester by researching and drawing each site and its climate threat, paying special attention to the use of clear legible graphics. Our interests involve each site’s history and any urban development that has served as an aggravator of climate challenges. We will use both spatial and non-spatial representations to communicate the challenges faces by each site. After a thorough research phase, groups will propose responses intended to mitigate the climate threat. Proposals may range in type from clear design intervention to policy change.  
Our primary mode of representation in this seminar will be web-based maps produced using open-source data, QGIS, and Observable. Students will learn the basics of Javascript and the D3 coding language, as well as design standards for mapping and visual communication. In addition to mapping, students will produce a series of non-spatial visualization to describe their site both before and after their proposed project takes place.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Outcome 1 Data Collection: (B.Arch+M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to collect open-source data and manipulate data via Geospatial Information Systems software. (M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to represent relationships between different open-source data sets.
- Outcome 2 Data Representation: (B.Arch+M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to represent data sets and communicate importance via web-based software
- Outcome 3 Research: (B.Arch+M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to use data to quantifiably analyze, assess, and project outcomes of a site that is undergoing change over time. (M.Arch) Ability to predict how site outcomes might change given multiple future scenarios.
- Outcome 4 Data Creation: (B.Arch+M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to create data sets directly from course research, and represent these data sets alongside collected data. (M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to create multiple datasets that affect on another.
- Outcome 5 Communication: (B.Arch+M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to communicate existing and projected site conditions via web-based graphics that combine data sets and directly designed geometries. Demonstrate the ability to design and execute a data graphic that contains multiple layers and information to be revealed as a user investigates the work. (M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to create both spatial and non-spatial graphics

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of this course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student.
