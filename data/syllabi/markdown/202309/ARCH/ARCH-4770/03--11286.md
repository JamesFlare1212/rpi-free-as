## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**ABSTRACT**  
Mass is an increasingly elusive condition in contemporary architecture. It evokes associations to more archaic forms of architecture and modes of production, like the monolithic rock-cut architecture of Nabataean mausoleums and Indian cave temples, the hypostyles of Egyptian halls or the stereotomy of mediaeval cathedrals.  
By contrast, contemporary architecture is more often conceived as surfaces. The prevalence of sheet materials and use of digital software results in architectural tectonics that are determined by offsets and layers rather than by excavation and stereotomy. Indeed the geometry of digital space is itself described through the infinitely thin coordinate systems of Euclidean and topological space. Mass has become a pretence, fictitiously articulated through cladding and spatial enclosure.  
The studio revisits the notion of mass through the interrogation of computational tools for design and production and their interface with contemporary massive materials. More specifically, we will be developing fabrication techniques that target robotic carving in foam to construct a series of architectural elements (e.g. columns, entablatures, walls, arches, apertures, etc.).  
**MATERIAL USE: MASSIVE EPS FOAM BLOCKS**  
Most plastics are liquids that are moulded or formed into surfaces. Foam, however, is ‘cast’ in the form of blocks that are later cut or milled into shape with computer-controlled machines. Though lightweight, foam is solid and brittle rather than thin and flexible in its nature. It favours mass and void relationships rather than surfaces. In fact, a piece of white high-density foam sculpted by a hot wire may have a material sensibility that is closer to chiselled stone than to a typical consumer product made from plastic.  
Foam, though technically a plastic, seems to have a lot in common with the most archaic of materials in architecture. Like stone, foam can be processed to adopt visual qualities from other materials. It can indeed be shaped to look massive and chunky, like stonework, but also soft and supple, like the draping in a baroque marble sculpture.  
**FORMAL APPROACH: STRANGE RUINS AND FUSIONS**  
Our formal approach will be to investigate architectural fictions and sham ruins as a medium for the exploration and subversion of architectural form and style. Students will survey various historical architectural cannons in order to develop their own range of styles that target an alternative formalism that embraces cultural specificity without simply resorting to the reproduction of past styles.  
Students will develop end arm tools and machine logics that target the poché and its relationship to mass and void. This will be done through the building of architectural data sets from historical cannons which can be sampled, manipulated, corrupted, misaligned and misused in order to develop a series of architectural artefacts that re-examine the conception of an architectural ruin from one that has been disfigured through physical processes to one that has been disfigured through digital processes of translation. At once legible and illegible, complete yet incomplete, these architectural artefacts will have multiple sometimes even contradictory readings. They will estrange object and material relationships creating strange fusions that are at once familiar and at once uncanny.  
**DESIGN QUALITIES**  
All design projects should incorporate apertures and varying wall thicknesses. In addition, a selection of the following qualities should be addressed:  
*Sidedness:* Ways to make two sides of the wall / masonry unit appear different.  
*Niches:* Ways to create space inside the wall for supplementary programs.  
*Porosity:* Ways to subtract one or several intersecting volumes from solid/block so that its reference surface partially disappears.  
*Depth and flatness:* Ways to make apertures appear flat from one side and deep from the other.  
*Texture:* Ways of interfacing with the material such that the material surface gains a certain tactile quality and appearance.  
*Figuration:* Ways to have one or a group of several masonry units appear as a figure against a muted background. Breaks repetition and/or creates affordances.  
*Posture and proportions:* Ways of aggregating blocks so that the mass as a whole gains a certain posture or certain proportions.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**ASSIGNMENT 1 | DESIGN DEVELOPMENT & TESTING**  
*Weeks 1 - 4 | August 28 - September 21*  
OBJECTIVES  
This first phase will focus on establishing a design workflow that navigates between the design of an object and its translation into a physical object using computational design and digital fabrication. Students will investigate the relationships between 3D masses, toolpath articulation and stereotomic compositions. They will interrogate how machined geometries and toolpaths interface with materials. This means that we will be as interested in the toolpath and surface patterns as we will be in the overall geometry. Students will be introduced to a series of computational workflows that challenge the expression of blocks, grids, stacks and typical stereotomic ordering systems as an architectural aesthetic. Instead students will create masses with surface articulations that obscure tectonic compositions and break perceptual repetition, exploring new forms of aesthetic languages that are especially calibrated for robotic fabrication and massive geometries.  
Students will form design teams to submit a proposal for a large free-standing massing to be produced in Assignment 2.  
ASSIGNMENT 1 REVIEW DELIVERABLES  
A digital model of a minimum of 3 architectural elements with surface articulation (e.g. columns, entablatures, walls, arches, apertures, etc.)

4 elevation and 4 axon drawing-rendering hybrids at 1:10 scale (approx 1”=1’ in imperial measurements) of each element  
Analytical toolpath drawing of each element accompanied by fabrication data (see below)

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**METHODS**  
Working in teams of 2 or 3, students will get acquainted with an iterative, research-based method of working where designs evolve by moving back and forth between material experimentation, digital geometry, robotic fabrication and full-scale mock-ups.  
The studio will be complemented by a series of general studio-wide technical workshops as well as individualised technical support as needed in the following:  
Advanced 3D modelling (Rhino, Z-brush)  
Visual scripting (Grasshopper)  
3D scanning (Autodesk Recap)  
Robotic fabrication (ABB robot controller)  
2D representation (V-Ray, Photoshop, Illustrator)  
Video making (After Effects)

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- OUTCOME 1 - RESEARCH: 1A. Ability to use / develop specialised knowledge in robotic fabrication and prototyping using iterative feedback loops between material experimentation, digital geometry, robotic fabrication and full-scale mock-ups to improve a design, test material properties and calibrate material tolerances | 1B. Ability to use / develop broad and integrative knowledge in how robotics has the potential to rethink current standardised industrial mass production and supply chains in contemporary architecture
- OUTCOME 2 - INTELLECTUAL DESIGN SKILLS: 2A. Ability to design using generative design strategies that incorporate interlocking solids, poché space, monolithic objects and structural assemblies using advanced technical skills in generative design, visual scripting and direct modelling in architecture | 2B. Ability to design using information resources such as grasshopper libraries, machine language resources and machine code translation to produce robot files | 2C. Ability to design engaging diverse perspectives exploring human-machine interfaces and cyborg workflows, where robotic end effectors are used to interpret technogenic and anthrogenic form from multiple perspectives across multiple cultural and historical contexts to achieve new forms of economy and cultural expression | 2D. Ability to design with ethical reasoning engaging questions of labour, politics and architectural form in pre-industrial, post-industrial, digital and post-digital construction and design, and how robotics can advance new formats of sustainability and cultural specificity | 2E. Ability to design with quantitative fluency to document and represent design work through analytical drawings and diagrams | 2F. Ability to design with communicative fluency to document and represent design work through a variety of visual media including image making and film

- OUTCOME 4 - CIVIC AND GLOBAL ENGAGEMENT: Ability to contextualise the use of robotics in architecture as an emerging global phenomena across multiple cultural contexts and position their own work within this growing field
- OUTCOME 5 - DESIGN EXECUTION: Ability to translate digital subtractive fabrication logics into robotic toolpaths using visual scripting and the robot controller to construct building parts

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:
