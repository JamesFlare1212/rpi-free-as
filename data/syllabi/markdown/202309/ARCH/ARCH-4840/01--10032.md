## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides an overview of essentials for the architectural-acoustics design of performance and public spaces such as concert halls, theaters, classrooms, auditoria, and worship spaces. here are no prerequisites. The course may be used as the beginning of a Master’s degree in Architectural Acoustics, the starting point for a concentration in an architecture student’s professional electives, the starting point for a minor in Architectural Acoustics, or simply for added depth. The course covers the fundamental principles of sound, human hearing, and room acoustics as well as practical design basics. In addition to the optional textbook, supplemental reading material will come from books as well as journal articles. There will be several guest lectures from experts in the field, including researchers and consultants.  
Because acoustics is a physical science, the course entails some quantitative work; however, no mathematics beyond algebra is required. In-class demonstrations and projects will supplement lectures, reading, and written assignments.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

After taking this course, the successfully participating students will have learned the fundamental terms and concepts in Architectural Acoustics:

- The students will understand the physical principles of in- and outdoor sound propagation.
- The will learn the fundamental of room acoustical designs to optimize the sound quality for speech, music, and other applications.
- The students will learn the basics of noise abatement to mitigate the effects of outside noise (e.g., traffic) and inside noise (e.g., HVAC systems).
- They will also learn the fundamentals of human hearing and cultural influences on sound design.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

This course will include the following topics:

- Fundamentals of Sound
- Fundamentals of Human hearing
- Sound sources, reflection, superposition
- Absorption: Mechanisms and Materials
- Interference, Standing Waves, Modes
- Diffuse Sound Fields: Mean-Free-Path, Statistical Room Acoustics
- Room Impulse Responses: Reflections, Echoes, Reflection density
- Reverberation: Reverberation time, reverberant radius
- Room Acoustic Parameters, Subjective Attributes
- Computer Modeling and Auralization
- Diffraction and Surface Scattering
- Practical Design Basics and design of rooms for speech and music
- Sound transmission loss and noise isolation
- Noise: quantification, metrics, and perception
- Microphones and Loudspeakers

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

After completing this course, successful students will have demonstrated through class participation, written assignments, and oral presentation:

- able to understand the fundaments of the principals of acoustic wave propagation, and the interaction of acoustic waves with surfaces and materials (reflection, transmission,

modes, absorption, scattering, and diffusion), with a concentration on phenomena relevant to building acoustics;

- able to understand clearly the scope of practical building acoustics: from room acoustics design to vibration & noise control
- able to understand practical acoustical design guidelines for different types of built environments, depending on acoustical function
- able to understand the basic architectural forms of performing arts spaces, and the ability to analyze and communicate the acoustical merits of each
- able to understand the principles of statistical room acoustics, and have familiarity with standardized metrics used to quantify room acoustics and their correlation with subjective perception
- able to communicate, orally and in writing, an objective and subjective critical analysis of the acoustical design of a building.
