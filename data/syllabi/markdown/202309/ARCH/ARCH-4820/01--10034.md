## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- OUTCOME 1 - USER REQUIREMENTS: Demonstrate the ability to use programmtic standards, ratios and planning strategies for the proposed building type. Demonstrate the needs of stakeholders and user groups who would use this project.
- OUTCOME 2 - REGULATORY REQUIREMENTS: Demonstrate the ability to develop designs in response to ordinances, regulatory agencies, and zonng codes.
- OUTCOME 3 - SITE CONDITIONS: Demonstrate the ability to design and develop the site exterior of the project in coordination with surrounding properties and the interior program.
- OUTCOME 4 - ACCESSIBLE/UNIVERSAL DESIGN: Demonstrate the ability to achieve barrier free design from the curb to all spaces in the project.
- OUTCOME 5 - ENVIRONMENTAL IMPACTS: Demonstrate the ability to design the envelope according to needs of interior program, solar control, and daylighting requirements. Demonstrate the ability to locate program and use resilient materials with respect to flooding and sea level rise.

Attendance and Participation are assumed and expected and can only count to reduce your grade if lack of either becomes a concern. As part of our ongoing assessment of the learning outcomes, students will be required to complete a Student Self-Assessment Survey prior to receiving a grade for the semester.

Grades will be established according to the following policy:

A (EXCELLENT) The design work and technical development is exceptional with consistency throughout the semester. The student’s work has been undertaken with a high level of insight, consistency and discipline. The project must demonstrate a successful level of development from broad concepts through details, synthetic and technical resolution. The project evidences a strong ability to integrate architectural building systems while exceeding functional requirements. The resulting body of work, when taken as a whole, highlights the students excellence in architectural development and design synthesis well exceeding ‘ability’ as described in SC.5.

B (GOOD) Project is a creative and energetic response, delivered with consistency, to comprehensive architectural design and the criteria cited in SC.5. The work is inspiring, complete and well resolved. Overall performance is well above the norm and, in many aspects the project, rises to a level of excellence. Assignments are met in a timely manner, and generally considered comprehensive and complete, beyond the minimum requirements of the course. The resultant project, attendance, and in class involvement, is demonstrably strong.

C (AVERAGE) Work that meets minimum requirements and demonstrates a somewhat more than minimum level comprehension with both the design and technical requirements and ‘ability’ as described in SC.5. The project may not demonstrate initiative to develop without additional input from the instructor and may be summarized by an underwhelming level of improvement throughout the semester. Although the project as a whole may not inspire, the satisfactory completion of all required assignments and demonstration of above minimum level of resolution must be evidenced in the project as a whole.

D (INFERIOR) Project is complete, however to a generally low standard. Work must address the fundamental requirements, and demonstrate evidence of all Student Criteria described in SC.5 with the minimum requirement for the project to clearly demonstrate ‘ability’ for topics described in SC.5.

F (FAIL) Does not meet minimum requirements throughout the semester nor during the final review and final deliverables deadline at the end of the semester. The project provides insufficient evidence of the students ‘ability’ to address the integrated design and technical concerns referred to in SC.5. Typically, the failing project is marked by general incompleteness with substantial deliverables missing, deadlines not met, presentations avoided (or missed) with unexcused absences. Additionally it is important to note that projects may have potential, however remain substantially incomplete. The evaluation of a projects completeness is considered in stages throughout the semester and culminates in the final review and end of semester deliverables. Informal appeals for ‘incomplete’ status will not be entertained as a means of avoiding a failing grade, students should review institutional guidelines for any process related to ‘incomplete’ status.

Attendance is required at all class meetings during the semester. Students are expected to arrive prepared and to use the class period for development of their projects. Two (2) unexcused absences from class will result in the lowering of the grade by one (1) letter. Four (4) unexcused absences from class during the term result in an automatic F. ### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process. Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust will merit a failing grade in this course and the violator(s) will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.
