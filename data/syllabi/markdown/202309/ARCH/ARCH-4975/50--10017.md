## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- After successfully completing this course, students will be able to: • Recognize style and forms from the Renaissance and the Baroque
- After successfully completing this course, students will be able to: • Contextualize objects of art in each time
- After successfully completing this course, students will be able to: • Critically analyze forms and content of the architectural buildings
- After successfully completing this course, students will be able to: • Interweaving forms, context, and programmatic preferences
- After successfully completing this course, students will be able to: • Employ art historical methodology to collect evidence of human artistic production across time and across cultures.
- After successfully completing this course, students will be able to: • Synthesize the relationship between specific works art and their social, cultural, and historic context.

## EVALUATION CRITERIA

All students are expected to be engaged in project related course work for the full duration of class. The following schedule establishes the structure of work, products required, and important events above and beyond the daily course content delivery.

- **Class Participation**. Regular attendance and active participation in class activities are essential to be a successful learner. Therefore, you must prepare carefully for class by completing all the assignments in advance. The class participation and homework grade can be further affected by the student’s active participation.

Being absent does not excuse you from completing the assigned work or the alternative assignments that your instructor will assign.

• Exams. There will be a midterm and a final exam.

The midterm and the final exam will ask you to recognize 5 works of art (from a menu of choices) while placing them in a historical, social, cultural, and stylistic context. You will also be expected to use terminology and methodologies that are specific to the field of art history and to the topic that you are discussing.

Each exam should be about 1000 words.

- **Weekly Assignments**. You will be asked to create a discussion on a topic as indicated in the syllabus. Each analysis should be about 500 words and should follow a specific methodological approach.

You will be using Blogs on BB.

- **Final Research Paper**. This course will teach the process of writing and rewriting – drafting editing and revision – to help students mature as disciplinary thinkers/ writers and to continually improve the relationship between content and clarity.

There will be one 6/8-page research paper. The paper will focus on the work of a specific architect or a work of architecture.

The paper will need to identify a specific art historical methodology as well as standard discipline-based terminology.

This is a five-step writing assignment that will include:

1. An Outline due by October 25
2. An Annotated Bibliography due by November 8
3. First Draft due by November 15
4. Second Draft due by November 29
5. Final Draft due by December 13

Criteria to evaluate the midterm and the final exam:

**Exams:**

Each question/work of art will be evaluated as following:

- ¾ 25 points: identification
- ¾ 25 points: description and exact contextualization in the proper time frame
- ¾ 25 points: critical analysis of the subject matter and its meaning
- ¾ 25 points: quotes from bibliography or citing from

TOTAL: **100 POINTS**

Final Research Paper:

The final research paper worth 30 points in the overall evaluation of the course. The student is asked to follow the schedule of the five-steps assignment and to adhere to the following academic requirements:

- ¾ Choosing a theme: 10 points
- ¾ Analysis of its content: 20 points
- ¾ Description and the state of the art (bibliography, critical discussions, and so on): 30 points
- ¾ Personal and critical ideas: 40

TOTAL: **100 POINTS**

**A**: Excellent work demonstrates that the student has excelled in satisfying all objectives and work methods. Characterized by exceptionally well-crafted work product that demonstrates a heightened level of understanding, intellect, and care in execution. Work is completed on time through a rigorous, continuously evolving, iterative/recursive process. Presentation strategies exhibit superior quality verbal, graphic and intellectual enthusiasm that carry the work beyond directives, expectations, and goals articulated in assignments. Clear willingness to engage in critical dialogue about design (architecture, interior design, and landscape architecture) with both faculty and peers must be evident. Habitual study is evidenced by production of new work each time the studio meets.

**B**: Above Average work demonstrates the student has satisfied all objectives and work methods outlined for the course, often exceeding those expectations. Strong, well-crafted work that demonstrates care and consideration of program, place, and use. Work product demonstrates initiative and represents effort in exceeding baseline expectation. The work is well developed intellectually, completed on time, and demonstrates continuous engagement with the design process. Effort demonstrates willingness to engage in the critical dialogue about design (architecture, interior design, and landscape architecture) with both faculty and peers, and exhibits strong evidence of iterative study. New work is developed for every studio meeting.

**C**: Average work demonstrates that the student has adequately satisfied all explicitly articulated course objectives and work methods. The work is characterized by competent craftsmanship, on time delivery and satisfactory effort. Presentation strategies demonstrate reasonable effort to communicate intellectual underpinnings and design process. Communication demonstrates an ability to listen and respond to the critical dialogue about design (architecture, interior design, and landscape architecture) with faculty and peers. Iterative development of work evident.

**D**: Below Average work demonstrates that the student has not satisfied all course objectives and work methods and that there is clear need for improvement. Work is incomplete and/or executed with inconsistent or flawed craftsmanship demonstrating a lack of effort, lethargy, or inability to engage the design process. Characterized by an unwillingness to listen and respond to critical dialogue about design (architecture, interior design, and landscape architecture) with either faculty or peers. Little to no evidence of iterative study.

**F**: Failing work demonstrates that the student has not satisfied many course objectives and work methods. Work is incomplete and executed with crude and rudimentary craftsmanship, lacking merit. Characterized by a demonstrated lack of engagement with the course content and/or objectives, an unwillingness to listen and respond to the critical dialogue of faculty, and an undeveloped command of the basic skills. Evidence of iterative study absent.

**I**: University of Arkansas Policy stipulates that an Incomplete may be assigned to a student who has not completed all course requirements, if the work completed is of passing quality. An incomplete grade is not an automatic right/entitlement of the student who fails to complete the course requirements on time. An incomplete will not be assigned to a student as an extension of time at the end for un-documented absences during the course of the semester. An Incomplete will only be assigned at the discretion of the professor after consultation with the student to determine the particulars of the situation.   Student-teacher relationships are built on trust.
