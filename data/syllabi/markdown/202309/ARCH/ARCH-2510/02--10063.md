## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will introduce basic structural and material principles, and explore their creative and technical application to the architectural design process.

In the course, the students will develop know-how and intuition relating to structural and material functionalities, behavior, aesthetic qualities, and form/assembly opportunities through a series of exercises. Students will learn through digital simulations, building prototypes, trying things out, and documenting and reflecting on their processes. For final projects, students will study and analyze structural and material strategies in a project of their own design, communicating their proposal through language, images, drawings and diagrams.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

- Introduce basic structural principles including familiarity with forces and structural behavior.
- Introduce the physical and visual properties of materials. Learn how the composition, manufacturing process, and properties of various materials relate to their performance and ability to produce architectural effects.
- Introduce criteria for material and structural selection based on performance, fitness, and creative opportunities.
- Introduce basic environmental concepts of embodied energy, embodied carbon, and material life-cycle.
- Build basic representational and communication skills.
- Explore design processes borne out of structural and/or material exploration.

These investigations consist of hands-on exercises to apply and learn the basic structural content covered in class. Students will compose a PDF book documenting and analyzing their experiments through written, photographic, and drawing means.

**30% Materiality** (Assignment 2A = 10% | Assignment 2B = 20%)

These investigations consist of hands-on exercises to apply and learn the basic construction material content covered in class. Students will compose a PDF book documenting and analyzing their experiments through written, photographic, and drawing means.

**20% Communication + Representation** (Assignment 3 = 20%)

The final course assignment consists of a project-based design exercise to explore representation and communication. The students will synthesize the experimentation done in the class—structures and materials—by transposing and reimagining a previous M+D project as a small occupiable space. This project will be presented through physical models and careful representation including language, diagrams, measured drawings, sketches, photographs, and other representation.

**10% Materials + Design Quiz**

Students will take one quiz covering the lecture material and other content reviewed in class or workshops. This quiz will be open-notebook/sketchbook and will include a variety of question formats, including prompts which require hand-sketching or basic drawing. The use of laptops, printed lecture slides, or other material will not be allowed.

**10% Attendance, Participation, Collaboration**

Class attendance and participation is required. Particularly due to the course’s hands-on, project-based format, a great deal of the material for which the students are responsible cannot be found in the texts, online resources, or handouts. This includes special topics covered during lecture, student presentation, and demonstrations. Students will be asked to peer-grade and give feedback on their group-members – these peer-grades will inform this portion of the course grade.

As part of our ongoing assessment of the School of Architecture curriculum and learning outcomes, students will be required to complete a Student Self-Assessment survey prior to receiving a grade for the semester.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance will be taken each class. Faculty will also pose questions to students during class. Two unexcused absences may result in the deduction of a letter grade. Three unexcused absences may result in the failing of the course. Those students joining the class more than five minutes late will not be admitted into class, and will be marked as an unexcused absence.

### Other Course Policies:

This **2-credit** class will meet once a week for a 1 hour & 50-minute lecture, workshop, and/or hands-on session. The class will typically oscillate between lecture presentations by the faculty, reviews, and student presentations. The faculty will select examples of work that are demonstrative for group learning and it is expected that students will actively participate in these discussions. Not all student work will be discussed during reviews due to time limitations.

This class is based on learning-by-doing methodologies. The content of the lectures and workshops will be applied by the students through creative projects and hands-on experimentation with structures and materials. Review and critique of student projects and associated representation will be the primary means of feedback and evaluation by the course faculty.

Students are expected to keep a sketchbook or notebook dedicated to this course. This sketchbook will be the ONLY resource permitted to be used on the course quiz. Students are expected to keep detailed lecture notes with sketches/drawings/diagrams, in addition to sketches/drawings/diagrams/notes associated with the course assignments.

Materials and Design is team-taught and course sections are purely administrative. Students will receive instruction from all faculty members and all faculty will contribute to course grading. The class will make use of a WebEx group chat for announcements, file distribution, and questions/communication with students. For personal matters or in the event of an excused absence, email stradm3@rpi.edu.
