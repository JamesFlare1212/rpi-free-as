## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This undergraduate course provides an overview of basic processes in human behavior that influence the effectiveness of individuals, groups, and organizations. Its focus is on understanding what happens during interpersonal interactions in work situations, and what can be done to make employees more effective. Topics covered include organizational socialization, motivation, decision-making, team dynamics, virtual teams, influence, and conflict management. Numerous exercises and case analyses are used in class to help provide students with insights into these processes.

The increased professionalization of the workforce and high value placed on technology and knowledge work in the global economy emphasize the importance of good workforce management skills for managers and professionals. To be effective, we need to act as "applied" behavioral scientists, that is, we must carefully study our organizations, units, and direct reports and develop an ability to recognize what makes each one tick. In this course more than most others, we need to know not only the conceptual material presented but also how it applies to our work lives and ourselves. People who understand the work environment, know themselves, and can assess their effects on others are more likely to be effective than those who are blind to these dimensions.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Weekly Quizzes and Homework via MyLab – “Chapter Assignments” (25%)**

There will be weekly chapter quizzes and homework assignment completed through Pearson’s MyLab for this course. Unless otherwise noted in Blackboard/MyLab, quizzes and assignments are due before the chapter is discussed in class (i.e., 11:59 am for our 12:00 pm class each Tuesday/Friday). This helps ensure you’ve read and digested the material for that chapter before we meet on it. All due dates and expectations are found in Blackboard/MyLab. Since you will have at least a week to complete them, missed assignments cannot be made up.

**Section Exams (25%)**

There will be an exam given in class for each of the textbook sections/parts (at 5% each). They will consist of multiple choice, True/False, or short answer questions. See the course schedule at the end of this syllabus for due dates.

#### Group Project (20%)

Student teams will be developed and will be responsible for working together on a problem (to be provided in class). We will use these group interactions throughout the semester also as a learning tool itself in relation to the content of this course. A final written report and a presentation (i.e., PPT) based on that report will be the deliverables at the end of the semester. Information on both will be provided early in the semester as will grading criteria for the final project. See the course schedule for the due dates.

**Individual Assignments Outside MyLab (10%)**

There will be some assignments given throughout the semester that are not found on MyLab. The instructor retains the right to make these assignments throughout the semester. However, students will have at least one week to complete any of these types of short assignments. Late submissions will be penalized by 10%.

#### Attendance and Contributions to the Class (10%)

Class participation is an integral aspect of this course. Please read and be prepared to discuss the assigned readings, videos or other materials listed on the syllabus for each session. This portion of the grade is centered on your non-group participation in class discussions and activities. Quantity and quality will be considered in evaluating participation. If you are interested in participating but have difficulty initiating it, please let me know so that we can try to ease the process. *Note: Participation with your group, both during class activities and for the group paper and presentation, will be assessed in the Group Project area of grading.*

**Final Exam (10%)**

There will be a final exam which will assess your cumulative knowledge from the entire semester. The format and additional study guidance will be provided prior to final exams. The date of the final exam is TBD, but during Finals Exam week.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work.
