## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is designed to inspire the mindset of an inventor, innovator, and entrepreneur, essential for new products, services, and companies. Students begin learning the theory of entrepreneurship and explore case studies. We critique both good and bad examples to identify criteria for success. In-class creative exercises further challenge innovative thinking. Entrepreneurship is doing. Students practice teamwork and apply skills commercializing a new good or service. The instructor shares entrepreneurial experience to guide students through the steps of starting a small business.

Innovation Critique (10%, Individual):

You will self-divide into one of two categories: successful innovation or failed innovation. Then, you may choose any 21st-century example that interests you. It can be any. Ideas include virtual assistants, reusable rockets, social media, tablet computers, self-driving cars, cloud computing, and more. This exercise allows you to take a critical look at an innovation’s evolution, demise, or even worse, state of stagnation. You are required to answer the following four (4) questions and add context:

1. Why did you choose this innovation? Be specific and give three reasons.
2. Why is the innovation or entrepreneur/company successful (or not)? Explain.
3. How do you define success with this invention? Explain.
4. Is this innovation sustainable in the marketplace? Explain.

This written critique should be 1,000 words, Georgia font, 1-inch margins. (Think about 250 words per question.) We are looking for substance more than style, deductive reasoning, and critical thinking, not for the critique to hit a word limit. A full assessment of essentials will be shared with the class via the LMS but you will be assessed on original thinking on all four questions. Please upload all assignments to the LMS as the instructor will not accept any attachment via email.

Final Reflection (10%, Individual):

For the final, all students (undergraduates and graduates) are required to write a 1,000-word reflection, applying the lessons to their own experiences and articulating their future growth. You are required to answer the following the three (3) questions and add context:

1. Before the course begin, you had at least one preconceived notion of the entrepreneurial process. What did you get wrong and why?
2. Which part of the entrepreneurial process is/was the most gratifying and why?
3. If you are pursuing this idea as a company, why? If not, why? Add context and use at least two (4) course concepts. Also, these answers are confidential, so if team dynamic or an idea dispute is a concern, please note or consult me outside of the course setting.

This written critique should be 1,000 words, Georgia font, 1-inch margins. (Think about 300 words per question.) We are looking for substance and style, deductive reasoning, and critical thinking, not for the critique to hit a word limit. A full assessment of essentials will be shared with the class via the LMS but you will be assessed on original thinking on all three questions. Please upload all assignments to the LMS as the instructor will not accept any attachment via email.

Social Enterprise Case Study (15%, Individual):

You will select a social entrepreneurship (brief and main) case study from among 600+ examples on Harvard Business Publishing Education here. (Same as link above in course materials section.) To receive full credit, you must address the following questions and add plenty of substance/context:

1. Why did you choose this example?
2. How does the innovation impact social welfare?
3. What challenges did the entrepreneurs face; how did they overcome them? (Provide 3)
4. What lessons did you take away from the study? (Provide 3)
5. How can you apply them to your own ideas? (Provide 3)

This case study should be at least 1,000 words, Georgia font, 1-inch margins. (Think about 200-250 words per question.) We are looking for substance more than style, deductive reasoning, and critical thinking, not for the critique to hit a word limit. A full assessment of essentials will be shared with the class via the LMS but you will be assessed on original thinking for all five questions. Please upload all assignments to the LMS as the instructor will not accept any attachment via email.

Patent Assessment Assignments (15%, Individual):

Patent Assessment Assignments are individual. We will discuss the value of patents and the inventor’s journey to receive protection of their intellectual property, but what about the invention’s commercial viability? As discussed in class, ask yourself this – who decides the value of the patent? If you said the customer/market, you are correct.

During the semester, the instructor will offer a topic to consider, and each student will then go to Google Patent (or the USPTO database, which is the same as it’s public data), and choose one patented invention aligned with that topic. You will assess the invention’s commercial viability based on the following three criteria:

1. Offer three (3) reasons why this invention is commercially or not commercially viable based on the information shared in class either through discussion or lecture. Devote two or three sentences to each reason.
2. If the invention is commercially viable, describe the market in which it can enter and discuss why in four sentences. If the invention is not commercially viable, describe why the intended market is wrong and discuss why in four sentences.
3. If the invention is commercially viable, describe whether an exclusive or non-exclusive agreement is more advantageous to the entrepreneur in four sentences. If the invention is not commercially viable, describe why the intended exclusive or non-exclusive agreement is wrong and discuss why in four sentences.

You must include the name of the invention, the patent number, link to the patent and at least three comparable inventions to receive full credit. A template will be offered on the LMS.

In total, each assessment should be at least 500 words, Georgia font, 1-inch margins. We are looking for substance more than style, deductive reasoning, and critical thinking, not for the critique to hit a word limit. You will be assessed on these three elements for your grade. The average of three assessment will account for 15 percent of your overall grade for the course. Late assignments will be accepted and be given a 0. Please upload all assignments to the LMS as the instructor will not accept any attachment via email.

Starting a Small Business (50%, Group + Individual):

This is the course (experience!) project in teams of at least two, but no more than four, individuals. I will share my entrepreneurial experience and, when possible, invite guest speakers to share their wisdom. We will cover the practical steps of starting a small business, including conceptualization, registration, pricing, hiring, marketing, digital necessities, and fundraising.

There are three deliverables essential to receive full credit:

1. Write a business/investor plan (10% of 50% overall grade)
2. Complete forms (business name, license, bank account, contracts, etc.) (10% of 50%)
3. Deliver a final presentation that aligns with each step of either the business model canvas (BMC) or lean canvas (LC). (30% of 50%)

You will be assessed on the following:

- Professionalism of all three deliverables
- Quality of all three deliverables
- Thorough completion of all three deliverables
- Case for the value proposition
- Case for the feasibility of the business
- Case for the viability of the business
- Case for the desirability of the business
- Handling of Q and A

Notes:

1. The team will present on one of two designated pitch days at the end of the semester, with each team member presenting their respected part(s) of the company. Critical communication skills and teamwork are essential with any business in any setting. Failure to do so with result in failure for the pitch – conceptually and academically.
2. Final pitches will be 10 minutes with 10 minutes of Q and A. This may be adjusted, depending on the number of companies formed during the course.
3. After each step and team check-in (there are three), you will present your progress and receive feedback.
4. I will be providing the necessary forms during class lectures and provide good/bad examples of final pitch presentation.
5. If it looks like a class project, it will feel like a class project.
6. If it looks like you’re creating a company; it will feel like a company.
7. Teams do not compete; We are a community of innovators helping each other succeed.
