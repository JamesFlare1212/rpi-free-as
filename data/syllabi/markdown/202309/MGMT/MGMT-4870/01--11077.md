## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

In this course you will integrate learning from various functional fields of management to develop your skills in the following areas:

- analyzing business situations and formulating strategy
- implementing strategy and making decisions that enhance a firm’s competitive advantage
- communicating strategic concepts and ideas persuasively to internal and external audiences

At the end of this course you will develop an appreciation of how various elements of an organization fit together to result in superior performance. In addition, you will also be able to identify and formulate viable business and corporate strategies, and differentiate them from those that are weak and uncompetitive.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Learning outcomes:**

- Learn how to conduct in depth industry analysis
- Apply frameworks to understand firm strengths, sources of competitive advantage
- Learn the costs and benefits of different business level strategies
- Analyze the costs and benefits of various firm scope decisions- vertical integration, product diversification, geographic expansion
- Identify the appropriate entry mode for expansion- including alliances, acquisitions
- Analyze different organizational structures and their benefits and disadvantages

**Class Participation**

You will be required to attend classes regularly, and to read the text book chapter and materials assigned for each class. Depending upon how the class progresses, additional articles may be assigned from time to time. Based on your reading, you are then expected to make thoughtful and constructive contributions to the discussions. Students may be called upon randomly to answer questions in class to ensure uniform participation.

An attendance sheet will be circulated at the beginning of every class. It is your responsibility to make sure that you record your attendance. You are allowed a maximum of two absences throughout the semester, after which two percentage points will be deducted for every additional absence.

Note: Attendance alone will not count toward your class participation grade, and the two will be graded separately.

**Quiz I and II (15% each)**

Dates for the two quizzes are indicated in the schedule (13th October, 1st December). These quizzes will cover all topics discussed in class up to that point in time, including cases. An important objective of this course is to help you develop an ability to critically evaluate the material covered. Hence straightforward questions requiring a commitment to memory will not be on the quizzes.

**Case Analysis- Report and Presentation (Group; 12.5%)**

The group case report and presentation account for 12. 5% of the final grade. Students will be earmarked into groups of 3-4 members. Each team will be responsible for preparing a written report and making a formal presentation of their analysis and recommendations. The enclosed schedule indicates the case and the date it is to be presented. Broadly speaking, your presentation will cover the background of the case, the issues identified (see the discussion questions for each case below on page 5), recommendations for the future, and any other points that you think are relevant.

Each team will prepare a report that is no longer than 12 pages, excluding the cover page (please see the guidelines for written reports below on page 3). In addition, each team will make a presentation in class for a maximum of 20-25 minutes (guidelines are also provided below for presentations on page 3). Note that the report is due on the day the case is scheduled to be presented. Late submissions will not be accepted. Please email your report and slides on the day the case is to be presented, ahead of class time.

Note- The cases discussed in class are designed to illustrate specific concepts and decision scenarios. Please therefore do not look up any online material or ‘solutions’ for the case, including ChatGPT. Any attempts to do so will be treated as a serious ethical violation, and in the minimum equivalent to a first offense (see below, and also see ChatGPT policy).

**Workshop Assignment (Group; 7.5%)**

The workshop assignment requires you to develop an insightful presentation on an assigned topic (e.g. the role of culture, a real life example of a successful or failed acquisition). You will be given brief guidelines ahead of the assignment, and your task is to develop a presentation of about 20 mins as a group. The purpose is to not only get you thinking about the topic, but also to inform the rest of the class and enhance their understanding of the subject. It is essential that you develop an in depth analysis and come well prepared to present on the day that the workshop assignment is scheduled, since you will be playing the lead role in these discussions. Please email your workshop slides on the day your workshop is scheduled, ahead of class time

**Strategy Analysis Report (10% Individual, 20% Group)**

The strategy analysis report requires you to select a company with multiple business segments and analyze its performance. The report has an individual component and a group component. Details of the assignment are provided below on pages 6-8.

**Guidelines for Written Reports and Presentations**

Written reports should be prepared in a professional manner, with attention to both content as well as stylistic aspects. Please submit reports formatted with 1-inch margins all around. The report should be double spaced, and the font size should be 11. Your report should use appropriate headings/sub headings to reflect what you are discussing in each section, and to guide the reader throughout. Please proofread your report adequately to ensure there are no grammatical and spelling errors.

Please include page numbers while adhering strictly to the specified page limits. E-mail me your report no later than 10 mins. ahead of class time the day it is due.

The objective of your presentation should be to communicate your ideas clearly, and to generate audience enthusiasm about the topic. The presentation, like the report, should be prepared in a professional manner with minimal typos etc. For the presentations, please email a copy of the slides you intend to use no later than 10 mins. ahead of class time the day it is due.

**Academic Integrity**

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and the Graduate Student Supplement (For 6000 level and above courses) define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in (1) an academic (grade) penalty and (2) reporting to Lally’s Associate Dean of Academic Affairs and either the Dean of Students (for Undergraduates) or the Dean of Graduate Education (for Graduate students).

In this course, the academic penalty for a first offense is 30% of the full course grade, irrespective of the assignment. A second offense will result in failure of the course as noted in Lally’s Three Strikes Policy.

If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

**Chat GPT Policy**

You are encouraged to use AI tools such as ChatGPT to supplement your learning in class. Chat GPT can be a powerful tool for researching industries, firms, and technologies. However it can also be wrong, and in many instances it can be superficial and not in depth enough. Hence you should critically examine and question whatever information it produces.

If you are using ChatGPT, it is your responsibility to cross check the information and ensure it is correct and sufficiently detailed to address the class assignment. ChatGPT may provide a useful starting point and a first draft, but you will need to conduct additional detailed research to meet class requirements.

If you use Chat GPT, please provide a short paragraph at the end of the assignment stating how you used it, along with the prompts you used. Not doing so is a violation of academic integrity policies, and would count in the minimum as one strike. Your paragraph should elaborate on questions such as how well did it work for the particular assignment, what you learnt from interacting with it, and where it was helpful or not helpful.

However note that you are *explicitly prohibited* from using Chat GPT to solve case questions or obtain information pertaining to the cases assigned in class. Doing so, again, constitutes a violation of academy integrity policies, and would count in the minimum as a strike.
