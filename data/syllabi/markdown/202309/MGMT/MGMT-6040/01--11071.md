## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**COURSE DESCRIPTION**

The increased professionalization of the workforce and the high value placed on technology and knowledge work in the global economy emphasize the importance of good workforce management skills for managers and professionals. To be effective, we need to act as "applied" behavioral scientists, that is, we must carefully study our organizations, units, and direct reports and develop an ability to recognize what makes each one tick. In this course more than most others, we need to know not only the conceptual material presented but also how it applies to our work lives and ourselves. People who understand the work environment, know themselves, and can assess their effects on others are more likely to be effective than those who are blind to these dimensions. This class was developed based on Professor Thomas Begley’s syllabus for the same course.

**COURSE STRUCTURE**

All organizations are made up of employees, and so it is important to understand human behavior in order to understand organizational behavior. This class will start by exploring individual psychological principles such as perception, attention, emotions, goals, motivation and how these impact decision-making. We will then build on this foundation to understand group level dynamics and decision-making. We will also discuss organizationally relevant topics such as motivation, leadership, negotiation and diversity.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**GRADING CRITERIA**

**Regular Chapter Quizzes (30%)**

Quizzes will be required for most of the textbook chapters. 1 Quiz per week except for the weeks where there will be exams schedule. Each quiz will be equally weighted. Chapter 1 will be for practice and will not count in grading.

**Midterm and Final Exams (30% combined)**

The exams will test your application of concepts learnt in class. Will be a combination of Multiple Choice Questions and Short Answer Type Questions, with a Mini-Case as well.

**Group Topic Paper and Presentation (20%)**

Each group will be responsible for writing a paper and making a class presentation. Information on both will be provided early in the semester.

I will judge the quality of the group presentations and the class will provide input. Relevant criteria for judging the presentations will include:

(1) Scope - did the presentation cover the relevant material in sufficient breadth and depth?

(2) Clarity - was the explanation of ideas and concepts clear?

(3) Quality of analysis - how well did the group analyze the main issues?

(4) Quality of application – how well did the group connect conceptual material to the topic of the presentation?

(5) Contribution – to what extent did the group increase our understanding of the topic? and

(6) Interest – how well did the group hold our interest?

**Case Discussion Questions/MyLab Simulations (15%)**

You will be assigned cases/mylab simulations from 4-8 times during the course. These cases/simulations will help you understand how to apply concepts learnt in class to practical situations you may encounter at your job.

**Contributions to the Class (10%)** Class participation is an integral aspect of this course. Please read and be prepared to discuss the assigned readings and cases listed on the syllabus for each session. Participation includes active contributions to your group, both during class activities and for the group paper and presentation. Quantity and quality will be considered in evaluating participation. If you are interested in participating but have difficulty initiating it, please let me know so that we can try to ease the process.

**AI POLICY** Use of AI tools, including ChatGPT, is permitted in this course for specific assignments.

Use of AI tools is prohibited when attempting exams/quizzes. My recommendation is to use AI to extract information and generate ideas. To adhere to our scholarly values, students must cite any AI-generated material that informed their work (this includes in-text citations and/or use of quotations, and in your reference list; using different color font to highlight text generated by AI). Where applicable, you must also verify the content generated by AI is true. Do not assume that AI tools are providing factually correct information. Finally, using an AI tool to generate content without proper attribution qualifies as academic dishonesty.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
