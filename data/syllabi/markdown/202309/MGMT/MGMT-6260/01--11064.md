## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides a general overview of the entrepreneurial finance market. We will cover, among many things, how to break down the valuation problem of early-stage ideas, the sources of financing for young businesses and which sources are more vs. less appropriate for different types of young businesses, investment strategy for fundraising, and how contract terms change over a business's life-cycle, potential exit strategies, the inner-workings of the VC industry, and the incentives of the different parties involved in the process.

As such, the primary objective of the course is to provide an understanding of the concepts and institutions involved in entrepreneurial finance and private equity markets, with a focus on VCs. Entrepreneurial finance and VC investing are intrinsically linked, and the course is designed to be broad and comprehensive, with the emphasis being on entrepreneurial finance. In order for an entrepreneur to make the appropriate decisions from a financing perspective and to obtain the most suitable financial contracts, they have to understand the motivations of the various investors that invest in entrepreneurial ventures. Conversely, investors need to understand things from an entrepreneur’s perspective and what incentivizes them in order to evaluate the various investment opportunities with which they are presented. Consequently, the course is designed for students who are interested in either pursuing entrepreneurial activity or financing entrepreneurial ventures or both.

Success in this course is based on the expectation that students will spend a minimum of 10 hours per week in preparation for class (readings, cases/assignments, group projects, preparation for exams, etc.). Given that this is a cross-listed course, graduate students will be held to a higher standard and are required to take on leadership roles in each team dynamic while also attending experiential opportunities throughout the semester for additional rigor.

From a structure standpoint, the course is divided into two approaches – that of the entrepreneur/startup and that of the investor/firm. Even though your final deliverable will be the creation of a new investment firm, it’s essential that you understand both perspectives. For many of you already in the startup space, this will place you in a stronger position to negotiate and value your work. A portion of the investment world embarked on their own startup journeys – some successful and others not so much. Still, both perspectives will be valuable.

Additionally, the course case study readings are segmented based on three clusters – Environment + Relationships; Assessing, Growth + Scale; and Sectors, Models + Ecosystems. These are important to understand the current state of venture capital, options for your final deliverable, and your future opportunities as either founders in a startup or part of an existing or yet-to-be established investment firm.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Upon completion of the course the students are expected to:

1. Understand business valuation in the entrepreneurial context

1. Understand financial contracting in addressing information and incentive problems

1. Understand core elements of financial planning for an entrepreneurial venture

1. Understand the sources of internal and external finance for entrepreneurial ventures

1. Understand the relevant elements of a deal structure

1. Understand venture finance in a portfolio context

1. Understand the economics behinds the venture capital market and deal structure

1. Graduate students apply lessons in leadership and financial decision-making to their own experiences and through additional experiential opportunities

Case Studies Articulation Assignment (20 percent): The HBR coursepack is a compilation of 15 case studies that are divided into three clusters – Environment + Relationships; Assessing, Growth + Scale; and Sectors, Models + Ecosystems. For this deliverable, you will write a short, five-page research paper explaining how elements of each case study will guide the creation of your venture firm. You are to use elements of all 15 case studies and synthesize the information to explain your rationale and cite each. No passage should be more than three lines in length with appropriate attribution (Orzechowski, 2023).

You will be assessed on the following four elements:

Your connectivity and implementation of the case studies found in the Environment + Relationships cluster (25 percent)  
Your connectivity and implementation of the case studies found in the Assessing, Growth + Scale cluster (25 percent)

In total, your deliverable should have 1-inch margins, 1-1/2 line spacing, 12-pt Georgia font, and five full-pages in length (sans citation pages). Please upload all assignments to the LMS as the instructor will not accept any attachment via email.

With entrepreneurship and most readings, the individual only takes what's relevant and all 15 have relevant lessons tied to entrepreneurial finance and your future. This will also guide me in understanding why you chose to develop your investment firm and what you take away in the startup sense, as I continue to work with students with their startups.

In other words, take the best, leave the rest.

Final Investment Portfolio Assessment (20 percent): You will spend time assessing unfunded startups in New York State as part of your final venture capital firm plan. However, you will need a foundational understanding of how to build and manage a portfolio, based on the capital you raise and the availability of startups in the sector of your choice. In turn, your portfolio will serve as the framework for your firm.

On Monday, September 18, we will begin reviewing the available startups defined by key variables that you will use to apply financing techniques and begin building your portfolio. The overall objective of this assessment is to maximize return for you and your investors, and ultimately ensure success of your portfolio companies. A detailed template will be provided on that day. You will be assessed on the following criteria:

- Overall accuracy of your financial modeling, including IRR, investment, and other key variables based on peer review (25 percent)
- Overall methodology explained in assessing your portfolio (25 percent)
- Overall professionalism and thoroughness of the investment memo provided to your key stakeholders/investors with a focus on your top-performing company (25 percent)
- Overall professional and thoroughness of three investment proposal letters (25 percent)

Please note that some firms believe there is an exact science and others believe there is a fair amount of analysis and guesswork included (please see Sept. 5 reading). Extra credit will be given to any team creating an assessment algorithm that will predict the likelihood of success based on the variables provided in the Invest New York dataset.

Success is measured by maximizing return based on the variables you choose outside the customary financial modeling. For example, this can include geographic and demographic data. Please also note that your portfolio will go through a holistic accuracy check for this grade on November 30, however, you will be allowed to reassess/revise financial projections for your final venture capital firm pitch. This holistic assessment will factor into 25 percent of your grade.

Final Venture Capital Firm Plan (40 percent): This is the course (experience!) project in teams of at least two, but no more than three, individuals. Starting on September 25, during our first team meeting, we will review the investment firm criteria and what is expected in the final presentation. This is a culmination of your semester experience. You are founders of the firm, attempting to convince a group of parties to invest and raise capital for your new venture. You will present on the firm's fiscal potential, your desired portfolio, and why this firm is needed. In sum, you will be creating a new venture capital firm.

The team will present on one of two designated pitch days at the end of the semester, with each team member presenting their respected part(s) of the investment firm. Critical communication skills and teamwork are essential with any business in any setting. Failure to do so with result in failure for the pitch – conceptually and academically.

Final pitches will be 10 minutes with 10 minutes of Q and A. This may be adjusted, depending on the number of firms formed during the course. After each step and team check-in (there are three, please see calendar), you will present your progress and receive feedback. I will be providing the necessary forms during class lectures.

If it feels like a class project, it will feel like a class project. If it feels like you’re creating an investment firm; it will feel and look like an investment firm.

You will be assessed on the following:

- Professionalism of the presentation (20 points)
- Incorporation of at least 10 course concepts (20 points)
- Case for the firm's fiscal potential (10 points)
- Your ability to present a portfolio (10 points)
- The accuracy of your portfolio (10 points)
- Argument as to why this firm is needed (10 points)
- Argument as to why I should invest in your fund (10 points)
- Handling of Q and A (10 points)

Student-professor relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that professors have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in (1) an academic (grade) penalty and (2) reporting to Lally’s Associate Dean of Academic Affairs and either the Dean of Students (for Undergraduates) or the Dean of Graduate Education (for Graduate students).

In this course, the academic penalty for a first offense is a grade penalty. A second offense will result in failure of the course as noted in Lally’s Three Strikes Policy.

If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
