## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides students with an understanding of marketing principles and the role of the marketing discipline. The course is intended to help students learn the basic concepts and practices of marketing and to familiarize them with the terminology and techniques for properly framing and analyzing marketing problems. In addition to marketing concepts, processes, and strategy, issues such as the social consequences of marketing are discussed.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The objectives of this course are:

1. To help students gain an understanding of key marketing concepts, terms, and principles
2. To provide insight into the role of marketing within an organization
3. To provide students the opportunity to evaluate, develop and implement marketing strategies
4. To acquaint students with the history and process of marketing research and provide the opportunities to participate in research studies
5. To challenge students to think critically about ethics, diversity and global issues relevant to contemporary marketing
6. To familiarize students with the array of marketing careers that exist and encourage students to consider such opportunities

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate functional knowledge of marketing, including the basic concepts, principles, practices, and process of marketing
- Demonstrate the ability to analyze and evaluate marketing environment, marketing strategies, and the basic elements of marketing mix
- Develop practical solutions to real-world marketing problems, including marketing planning, and communicate it clearly in both oral and written work
- Demonstrate knowledge of current and emerging issues in marketing, such as digital marketing, globalization, data privacy, and inclusivity
- Analytically solve quantitative marketing problems and interpret their implications for marketing decision-making
- Deliver high quality written and oral presentations, using facts, data, and creative visual concepts
- Demonstrate basic understanding of ethics and diversity in the contemporary, data-driven market environment

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance:** You are required to attend in person instruction and punctuality is greatly appreciated. This is helpful for your learning because it provides the opportunity to participate in our many class discussions and activities. Attending group meeting sessions is particularly important as it not only impacts your overall participation and project grades, but is also an opportunity to work directly with the instructor on your final project’s success. Still, students are offered one unexcused absence for which there is no penalty. Beyond that, unexcused absences will be reflected in the participation grade and, as necessary, referral to the appropriate Student Services for further action.

Furthermore, if you are ill, under quarantine for COVID-19, or suspect you are ill, please email me as soon as possible detailing the absence (better with document or Excuse Form from your class dean) and engage the corresponding class remotely via the pertinent LMS materials. You will still be responsible for the material as it relates to exams and other assignments.

**Participation: A healthy classroom is one in which students are engaged and actively share their unique perspectives so that we may all learn from one another’s experiences.** There will be a variety of opportunities for such active participation during every class. Everyone should feel welcome to contribute by offering elaborations, examples, differing viewpoints, or constructive questions. Participation points (12% of grade) are assigned weekly. **Occasionally, I may replace the oral participation requirement with pop quizzes.** Students with excused absences will have an appropriately amended tabulation for the week.

Throughout the semester, we will explore a wide variety of ethical considerations relevant to the material (e.g., diversity of marketing materials, greenwashing, data privacy, etc.). Please come to class with an open, curious mind and compassionate presence, and remain respectful toward viewpoints that may not align with your own.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
