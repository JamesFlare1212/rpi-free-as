## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course provides an opportunity for the development of theorem-proving skills in the field of mathematical analysis. Expansion of a knowledge base comes as a by-product of energy expended in theorem proving and subsequent exposition. Analysis topics included are: sets, functions, the real numbers, cardinality, induction, decimal representations of real numbers, Euclidean spaces, abstract vector spaces, and metric spaces. This is a communication-intensive course. **Prerequisites:** mathematics major and MATH 2010. (from the [Rensselaer Catalog](http://catalog.rpi.edu/))

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is required for quizzes, exams and for full credit on worksheets done during the Wednesday block.

### Other Course Policies:

**Exams:** The material covered on Exams will be announced on LMS when the date of the exam is near. Exams will be given during lecture when the Institute allows it. Exams will include some combination of definitions, true/false, multiple choice, short answer problems and proofs. Exams will be closed book and closed notes and will be timed.

Make-up exams will only be given to students with an [excused absence documented by the Student Success office](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/student-success/excused-absence). **Missing an exam should not be taken lightly.** Thus, students missing an exam due to an excused absence should notify the instructor to make arrangements for a make-up exam ***before the exam begins***. If a student with an excused absence fails to inform the instructor within 24 hours of the exam, it is at the discretion of the instructor to allow the student to take a make-up exam with possibility of a penalty applied. If a student misses an exam due to an unexcused absence (e.g., did not know there was an exam, overslept, etc.), then the student must inform the instructor within 24 hours of the exam. The student may elect to take a make-up exam at a time arranged by the instructor and a 30% penalty will be applied to the score.

**Quizzes:** Quizzes will be 20 minutes in length and will be given on Mondays during lecture. Quizzes will be over material covered the prior week in lecture. They will typically consist of 3-4 questions including definitions, multiple choice/multiple select, short answer and one proof. No make-up quizzes will be given for any reason. Students who miss a quiz due to an [excused absence documented by the Student Success office](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/student-success/excused-absence) will be excused from a quiz. Additionally, up to two (2) quiz scores will be dropped when calculating the overall quiz grade at the end of the semester. To drop a quiz score, a student must have taken a minimum of 8 lecture quizzes, thus if a student misses 3 or more quizzes - excused or unexcused - they must speak with Dr. Kiehl in-person to determine how their quiz average will be determined at the end of the semester.

**Disability Services for Students (DSS):** Students with a memorandum from the [Disability Services for Students (DSS)](http://studenthealth.rpi.edu/disabilityservices) office identifying accommodations for quizzes and exams must present the memorandum to the instructor , ideally by Thursday, September 14. Providing the accommodations often requires advance planning. Students will not be granted accommodations unless a memorandum from DSS has been received. Students with flexible attendance accommodations must discuss in-person with Dr. Kiehl how this will be handled for this course.

**Worksheets:** Worksheets will be distributed at the beginning of Wednesday blocks with an online version available on Gradescope. These worksheets consist of definitions, short problems and proofs, which will be discussed and filled out during lecture. A student’s credit for lecture worksheet discussions will be a combination of participation/attendance in lecture and uploads of (partially)completed worksheets to Gradescope no later than 11:59 PM Wednesday. No late worksheets will be accepted and no make-up worksheets will be allowed. To compensate for this, up to 2 worksheets scores will be dropped at the end of the semester. Students who are unable to attend the Wednesday 9 AM session can upload their worksheet to Gradescope by 11:59 PM Wednesday to receive up to half credit.

**Presentations:** There will be three video presentation assignments made during the semester. Presentations will be assigned in coordination with in class exams during the semester. Slides for presentations will be created using Beamer and recordings of presentations will be done via WebEx. Details regarding problem presentation assignments will be posted on LMS when the presentation dates draw nearer.

**Homework:** There will be 10 homework assignments due on Thursday nights at 11:59 PM. Each homework consist of 2-4 problems that must be done in LaTeX (see below). Homework assignments will be submitted on Gradescope as a compiled pdf file and for some submissions the LaTeX code will be submitted on LMS.

Homework is due at the times posted on LMS. Late homework will receive a 10% deduction of total points for each day, or part thereof, late and will not be accepted after 3 days late (Sundays, 11:59 PM). An **excused absence,** will not automatically result in an adjustment to the late penalty. Late penalties on homework will only be adjusted or waived *at the discretion of the instructor*. This is generally reserved for extended illness and when notification of the absence was done in a timely manner.

At the end of the semester, a student’s homework grade will be calculated out a smaller number than the total number of homework points available. For example, if there are 320 homework points at the end of the semester, the homework grade might be calculated out of 300 points with a cap of 300 so that the max homework grade is 100%. You can assume there will roughly 20 extra homework points available to you throughout the semester.

The rules for collaborating on homework problems with others are as follows:

Before working with anyone, each student must read the **entire** homework problem set and (minimally) do the following

- Look and memorize all relevant definitions and Theorems.
- Make an outline of what needs to be shown.
- Make and attempt at writing the proof.

Once students have satisfied the above criteria, students may seek help and collaborate with others (inside and outside of office hours) on figuring out how to write, refine and improve proofs using the following guidelines:

- Students may get help on problems from an instructor, TA, ALAC provided tutor or another student currently taking the course.
- Students may not get help on problems from anyone outside of the class without explicit permission from the instructor.
- Students are responsible to independently write their submission from their own notes taken during any above interactions.
- Students must include a list of anyone with whom they collaborated (outside of office hours) at the end of each homework assignment. If you have a homework study group, all individuals participating in your study group must list names of participants in the group on every homework.
- LaTeX code and LaTeX files **may not** be copied or shared with other students.
- Submissions with glaring similarities will violate academic integrity policies. The penalties for violating academic integrity policies are shown in a subsequent section on this syllabus.
- Students are responsible for protecting their work and preventing it from being copied by others. In the event of an academic integrity policy violation, all students involved will be held accountable and penalized.
- In order to receive full credit on homework assignments, proofs must be constructed based on the definitions, properties and theorems presented in the course (book and lecture).

**LaTeX:** Learning LaTeX is part of the course. Students must use LaTeX in writing homework. Students will be required to submit the original LaTeX code on LMS for some homework sets. This is to help provide feedback to students about the appropriate ways of writing LaTeX to make it a useful system. In addition, there may be questions about LaTeX syntax on exams. Students should review log files from the LaTeX compiler to make certain that the LaTeX syntax is correct. (LaTeX compilers sometimes overlook incorrect syntax which may produce correct looking output but may eventually lead to intractable difficulties as the document grows.) Sample LaTeX files for homework will be provided for download and usage.

LaTeX is free to download. While the download and installation is time consuming, the advantage of downloading LaTeX is that you can edit files locally on your own machine without connection to the Internet and you can easily control your own source files. Most downloads come with a LaTeX editor, but LaTeX may be edited with any text editor. There is a choice of downloads with two major ones being TexLive and MikTex. Either will suffice for this class.

Alternatively, students may use an online LaTeX editor such as “Overleaf”. While this makes it a little easier to get started, it requires Internet connectivity and it controls your files. Note that the homework rules above explicitly forbid sharing LaTeX files in any fashion and thus prohibit using any of the sharing facilities of online programs such as “Overleaf”.

**Grade appeals:** Changes to exam, quiz or homework grades will be made only if something has been overlooked. Grade appeals will be made on Gradescope. All appeals must be made **during the grade appeal window** specified on Gradescope for the exam, quiz or homework once the grade has been published on Gradescope. Before submitting an appeal please check Gradescope comments on graded assignments.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic integrity:** Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://rpi.app.box.com/s/3f1cl3w1lt1mq8gqla68v6uk3vh1c19f) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams and quizzes must be the student’s own. A student may not change an answer on an exam or quiz before submitting it for an appeal. Additionally you will be asked to “sign” an honor code that indicates your work is your own and you have not given or received any help on exams and quizzes. The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
