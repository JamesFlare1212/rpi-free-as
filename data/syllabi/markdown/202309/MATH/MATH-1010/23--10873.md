## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Functions, limits, continuity, derivatives, implicit differentiation, related rates, maxima and minima, elementary transcendental functions, introduction to definite integral with applications to area and volumes of revolution. (from the [Rensselaer Catalog](http://catalog.rpi.edu/))

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate basic and advanced symbol manipulation skills.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to convert between calculus concepts and their graphical, numerical, and symbolic representations.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to apply calculus concepts to selected problems in science and engineering.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to apply certain fundamental theorems, basic concepts, and rules from calculus to solve symbolic and graphical problems.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to present problems related to the theory of calculus and its applications to science and engineering disciplines.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance:** Students are responsible for all material covered in lectures whether they are present or not. Attendance is expected in lectures, but will only be checked on exam days.

### Other Course Policies:

**Exams:** Details about each exam, including the material to be covered on the exam, will be announced on [LMS](https://lms.rpi.edu/) when the date of the exam is near. There will be four exams given in person during lecture in SAGE 3510. **Students who receive testing accommodations from the** [**Disability Services for Students (DSS)**](http://studenthealth.rpi.edu/disabilityservices) **office will take their exams on Wednesdays at 2–3:50 PM (room TBA) during the week of the regularly scheduled exam.**

If a student has a pre-scheduled absence (e.g. official excused absence to attend a conference, athletic competition) on the day of an exam, then they must notify the instructor via email as soon as they learn of the absence and no later than one week before the regularly scheduled exam. Documentation excusing the student from class at the time of the regularly scheduled exam must be received by the instructor before the day of the exam. This documentation must be either an [excused absence documented by the Student Success office](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/current-students/academic-and-personal-support/requesting-excused-absence) or, if the absence is due to an athletic competition, documentation from a coach. Provided the above conditions are met, the student will then take a conflict exam at a different time.

If a student misses an exam for any other reason (e.g. overslept, was ill on the day of the exam, did not know there was an exam, did not know where the exam was), then the student will receive a zero on the exam. This score will be dropped as the lowest score at the end of the semester. The remaining 3 exam scores plus the final exam score times two will count toward the student’s course grade. If a student misses more than one exam, then they will need to email the instructor within 24 hours of missing the additional exam(s) to make arrangements to discuss options, if any.

Note the policies in this section do not apply to the final exam. See relevant policy under **Final Exam** below.

**Final exam:** The final exam will be cumulative and will be administered during the final exam week (Wednesday Dec 14 — Wednesday Dec 20) determined by the [Office of the Registrar](https://info.rpi.edu/registrar). **All students must take the final exam during the final exam block at the time scheduled by the Office of the Registrar.** Exceptions will only be made if there is a scheduling conflict as defined [here](https://info.rpi.edu/registrar/grades/#FinalExamScheduleandConflicts). The Student Success office will only issue an excuse for a missed final exam for extreme circumstances, such as hospitalization. Therefore, students must not make end-of-semester travel arrangements until the final exam schedule is posted, or should only schedule travel for the time period after the last day of final exams, which is Wednesday, December 20.

**Homework:** Achieve homework assignments will typically be due once or twice weekly on Mondays and Thursdays at 11:59 PM and will be graded automatically by the system. Students may submit these assignments late up to two days after the original deadline, but a penalty will be applied to the score. A 10% penalty will apply to assignments submitted the day after the deadline and a 20% penalty will apply to assignments submitted two days after the deadline. **No other due date extensions will be granted for any reason.** To further compensate for this, several homework assignment scores will be dropped at the end of the semester.

Additional problems will be assigned from the textbook and will be posted on [LMS](https://lms.rpi.edu/). Though these problems will not be collected nor graded, they should not be viewed as optional. Success in the course depends on successful completion of all homework, both Achieve homework and textbook homework. Exam problems are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples. Full solutions to odd-numbered textbook problems can be found on Achieve. When logged in to our Achieve course, view content by assignments and scroll to the bottom of the page. The student solutions manual for chapters 1–6 may be found below No Due Dates. Tip: download and save these files for use in future courses!

**Disability Services for Students (DSS):** Students with a memorandum from the [Disability Services for Students (DSS)](http://studenthealth.rpi.edu/disabilityservices) office identifying accommodations for exams must email the memorandum to the instructor by **Monday, September 11**. Students will not be granted accommodations unless a memorandum from DSS has been received. Providing the accommodations requires advance planning. Students may not be granted accommodations if a memorandum is received on the day of an exam. **Students who receive testing accommodations from the** [**Disability Services for Students (DSS)**](http://studenthealth.rpi.edu/disabilityservices) **office will take their exams on Wednesdays at 2–3:50 PM (room TBA) during the week of the regularly scheduled exam.**

**Classroom decorum:** Students must be on time for class and should refrain from leaving and reentering the classroom during class. If a student has a legitimate reason for arriving late to class or being excused early from class, then he or she should discuss this with the instructor before class. Phones may not be used during class and should be silent. Computers and tablets may not be used for anything other than taking notes. Students who are unable to courteously comply with these policies may be asked to leave the room.

**Visitors:** Visitors are prohibited during all class meetings. Only students who are registered for the class should be present in the classroom. Students may only attend class meetings at the time and place listed on their schedule on SIS.

**Calculator policy:** Calculators will not be allowed on exams. Problems will be designed so that this does not create an issue. Occasionally, a calculator may be required to complete a homework problem, but students should not rely on calculators for simple arithmetic or trigonometric calculations.

**Extra credit:** No extra credit requests will be considered for any reason, so be sure to give your best effort on exams and homework assignments throughout the semester. Any requests for extra credit will be ignored.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic integrity:** Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://info.rpi.edu/sites/default/files/Rensselaer%20Handbook%20of%20Student%20Rights%20&%20Responsibilities%20%C2%BB.pdf) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams and quizzes must be the student’s own. A student may not change an answer on an exam or quiz before submitting it for an appeal. In addition, no calculators, computers, tablets, open books, open notes, phones, headphones, or smartwatches may be used during exams or quizzes. Talking or communicating with other students in any way during exams or quizzes is also prohibited.

The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
