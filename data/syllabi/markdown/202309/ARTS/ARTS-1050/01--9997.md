## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The history of art is expansive and diffuse, encompassing evermore forms of artistic production as modern art historians push back against the limits of the so-called “Western canon.” This class seeks to highlight cross-cultural intersections, and propose associations between distant realms of art production. The course material spans roughly 30,000 BCE to 1550 CE. We will explore the monumental and portable arts of the Paleolithic-Neolithic eras; the empire building of China and Syria; temple constructions of Egypt, Ancient Greece and Byzantium; insular arts as in England, Japan and Rapa Nui; and representations of the nude in European, Asian, and American contexts. Inherently, this class seeks to expose students to a wide range of stylistic and design motifs, through a broad spectrum of production techniques and media.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students will learn to identify and articulate the nuances of art objects with spatial awareness. Consequently, this course will build skills in visual analysis, and will encourage critical reading and interpretation of both objects and scholarly arguments. Though students will be required to memorize artist and artwork names (when available), as well as dates and locations of production, final grades will prioritize the demonstration of interpretative skills through essay-formatted exams, and the final critical analysis essay.
