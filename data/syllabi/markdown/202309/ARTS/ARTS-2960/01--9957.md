## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

From opera arias to indigenous folk tunes to hip-hop, the act of setting words to music has produced some of the world’s most popular and enduring art in the form of songwriting, with many of the world’s greatest songs brought into existence with little more than imagination and a guitar or a beat. This course examines the wide variety of elements present in the creation of a successfully written song, while honing a higher sense of critique when analyzing the work of both successful and obscure songwriters. Song recordings, videos, and lively class discussion frame a closer look at multiple creative techniques for this art form, and students are encouraged to experiment on a grand scale, without fear, when writing eight complete songs over the course of the semester.

Students get positive and useful creative feedback along with opportunities for collaboration, while learning how to make their own effective demonstration recordings. While focusing primarily on aspects of songwriting as a popular and commercial art form, this course can also be seen as an effective study in structure building for creative design forms, in many ways analogous to design forms in architecture, mathematics, chemistry, and computer sciences, where creative collaboration can yield innovative results. Or it can just help you finish that surefire hit you’ve been working on.

The class will be split into two groups (**A- MONDAY, B- THURSDAY)** which allows for all student works to be heard and discussed on weeks that songs will be due. Student song recordings and lyric sheets will be presented weekly via the class Webex space before class time on the due date. Feedback for each song in the group is requested from each group member during the class period, and remotely via Webex chat if absent.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Students personally select a media resource--book, movie, game, media, etc.--that is not necessarily music-based, to use as a reference or an inspiration guide for songwriting throughout the semester.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Student songwriters will create a portfolio of best works, and discover a wealth of strategies for creativity in the study of great music composers, lyricists, and songwriting teams, while becoming more familiar with the enormous body of great (and some not-so-great) works available through streaming and downloading. Students who are more connoisseurs of songwriting than creators can still participate in the songwriting process, and possibly find their appreciation of the artform deepened and enriched by its closer examination, potentially discovering a personal creative voice therein through song production, promotion, and publishing.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Songwriting Elements/Topics: Song History, Demo Recording, Lyrics, Melody, Harmony, Chords, Chord Progression, Phrasing, Rhythm, Song Form, Collaboration, Songs & Society, Publishing, Copyright.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Develop improved understanding of the art of writing songs, as well as the works of great songwriters, past and present.
- Accumulate key techniques for recording, and effectively capture and affix their works to a medium.
- Learn how to effectively collaborate with others on a creative project.
- Improve general writing, presentation, and communication skills.
- Develop techniques for giving and receiving high-quality critique to/from other creatives to achieve positive improvements to works.
- Better understand copyright, publishing, and emerging technologies for song collaboration, promotion, and distribution.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

SONG ANALYSIS PRESENTATION- Students will provide a PowerPoint/Keynote presentation on an analysis of a chosen song on the class Webex space.

SONGS AND SOCIETY PAPER- Students will select a song that had a large effect on society, and write a 1000-word paper analyzing the song, the songwriters, the historical context, and how the song impacted people.

8 SONGS/SONGWRITER PORTFOLIO- Students will meet the eight (8) song deadlines as presented, complete with demonstration recordings and lyrics, over the course of the semester, creating a personal 8-song portfolio of creative work.

PRESENCE AND PARTICIPATION- The success of this class depends on the presence and participation of each student when listening to and critiquing everyone’s work. With this class, we will strive to make a community where quality creative feedback can be freely given and taken. Any student in danger of not receiving these points (aka not participating or responding) will be notified discreetly weeks in advance of assessment, and given every chance to reclaim them.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

The first unexcused absence I’ll let go. Any work due on that date is still expected on time, any missed quizzes score a zero. The second time will make me look askance at you, and wonder why you need to miss this class you signed up for. Third time gets a probationary warning, and the fourth will result in a **half-grade drop off your final grade**, with any further absences another **half-grade drop for each**. Excuses from Student Success are required for illness and extended absences. Communication with the lecturer is key; unexcused absences can be made excused (emergencies, personal difficulties, etc.) with adequate and timely explanation.

### Other Course Policies:

Assignments are due by class time of due date and receive 100 points. Songs submitted by midnight of due date receive a 5-point reduction. Songs and assignments that are late receive a 10-point grade deduction per day.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

If your work is found to be fully plagiarized (words and music both written by someone NOT you) at any time, you will receive a warning from me, and have to re-write the song. You are doing "production" in this instance, when simply re-assembling other's creative work, and should take the recording/production class instead.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

This course requires being able to record simple music demonstration recordings with vocals and accompaniment, and export them as sound files. Phones, tablets, and computers have all the required tools to achieve this, but it is up to the individual songwriter to decide how they will use their available technologies. Some basic recording application learning outside of class may be necessary to achieve suitable quality demos.
