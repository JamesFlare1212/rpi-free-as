## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The purpose of this course is to provide students with a fundamental understanding of audio recording principles and technologies, along with historical and modern audio production techniques, reinforced with hands-on experience in applying that knowledge to the process of recording in a studio. From the analog world of microphones and speakers, to the digital world of DAWs and VSTs, we will explore the process of audio capture, storage, mixdown, and playback in an introductory format. What makes a sound a “good” sound? How do we make sure we are capturing a “good” sound? And can we make a “bad” sound into a “good” one? We explore the concept of audio production, as in: what make someone a “producer” or an audio recording “produced?” Exploring these questions leads to higher level thinking about what audio is capable of when handled skillfully, and how audio principles can be applied to a variety of other disciplines.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Upon completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Demonstrate an understanding of studio ethos and etiquette, and how to set up and tear down an audio recording session safely and efficiently.
- Utilize a fundamental knowledge of analog and digital audio signals, and learn how signal flow operates in a recording studio.
- Proficiently record, edit, and mix audio using a Digital Audio Workstation (DAW), particularly ProTools Ultimate, at beginner to intermediate level.
- Maximize use of the Avid S6 control surface while using ProTools, with appropriate monitoring.
- Identify the properties of different microphones and their ideal placement for desired results.
- Integrate essential concepts of audio engineering, production, mixing, and storage, and using NARAS guidelines for sessions and mixes.
- Achieve Level 3 certification, which allows for more access to the HASS Media Lab, and entry to Sound Recording and Production 2 in the spring.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Introduction to Avid ProTools and using a Digital Audio Workstation (DAW), mono and stereo microphone techniques, microphone properties, basic analog and digital audio concepts, recording most instruments, production vs. engineering, mixing techniques, studio proficiency, mastering and final audio products.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate and understanding of recording studio ethos and etiquette, and be able to set up and tear down an audio recording session safely and efficiently.
- Proficiently record, edit, and mix audio using a digital audio workstation (DAW), prioritizing industry-standard Avid ProTools, at a beginner to intermediate level.
- Identify properties of important microphones, and their ideal placement, positioning, and amplification for desired results.
- Utilize a fundamental knowledge of analog and digital audio signals and their attributes in the creation of audio files, for a variety of formats.
- Integrate essential concepts of audio engineering, production, mixing, mastering, and storage, fulfilling general NARAS guidelines for DAW sessions and mixes.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

LEVEL 1 PROFICIENCY QUIZ

At the outset of the semester, we will make sure you have a clear understanding of general studio etiquette and rules for using the HASS Media Lab for your lab times. This quiz covers the Level 1 guidelines.

THREE (3) HOMEWORKS (5% each, 15% total)

Homeworks are assigned to coincide with reading assignments and lecture content, with up to fifteen questions covering salient points from both.

FOUR (4) LABS (5% each, 20% total)

LAB 1 requires students to import and assemble pre-recorded audio tracks in ProTools.

LAB 2 requires the student to use a portable recording device to do location audio recording.

LAB 3 requires students to make a high-quality recording of an audio event using a single microphone.

LAB 4 requires students to achieve a quality final mix of a 10- to 12-track ProTools session.

PROFICIENCY TEST (ON CAMPUS)-Level 3

Student will demonstrate ability to create a ProTools session, set up and record with microphones, re-amp a track from the DAW, bounce and store a mix, and tear down session efficiently.

MIDTERM EXAM (30%)

Covers salient points of readings and lectures in first half of semester, plus important studio techniques learned.

FINAL LAB (35%)

Student must create and mix eight tracks in ProTools, with four having been recorded by student using microphones and direct boxes. Selection must be at least two minutes long and reasonably well-mixed at a professional volume level. Sessions and mixes must follow NARAS guidelines for deliverables.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

The first unexcused absence I’ll let go. Any work due on that date is still expected on time, any missed quizzes score a zero. The second time will make me look askance at you, and wonder why you need to miss the class without an official excuse. Third time gets a probationary warning, the fourth will result in a half-grade drop off your final grade, with any further absences resulting in a further half-grade drop.

Truthfully, if you are repeatedly absent for whatever reason, I will become very concerned about you, and will do whatever I can to help you rather than just slash away at your grade. Please communicate with me if things are going so badly that this class has become an additional stress, and we can work it out.

An absence can become an excused one by informing me of your very good reason beforehand or, if it’s an emergency, as soon as possible after class with an appropriate reason. It is best for both of us for you to have documentation for the reason of your absence obtained through the Student Success Office.

Activities for other classes cannot be used as reasons or excuses for absences in this class. If you will have a sports-related absence, you must notify me with a list of all classes you expect to miss in advance.

### Other Course Policies:

Labs are due on the dates indicated. **Ten percent of your grade** will be deducted for any lab submitted after the due date and **ten percent for each day late thereafter**, with no lab accepted more than five days after the due date. A missed quiz will result in a score of 0 for that quiz, unless due to an excused absence, in which it is the responsibility of the student to make suitable arrangements with me for making it up.

Class attendance and participation in class activities are required. Students who miss class for extended periods of time without permission or explanation will be reported to the Dean of Students Office or the Department of Public Safety for support and assistance, as needed. Request for accommodations, exceptions, extensions, or incomplete grades due to illnesses or personal emergencies must be supported by written documentation from the Dean of Students or the Dean of Undergraduate Education.
