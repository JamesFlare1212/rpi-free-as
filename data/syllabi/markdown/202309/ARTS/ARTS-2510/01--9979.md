### Additional Information for the Instructor Section:

The more hands-on experience a music educator has in the trenches of the professional performing world, the better equipped that individual is to teach the young artist who has professional aspirations. But that educator must also have the unique teaching skills of being able to best convey that information. Matthew Finck is fully equipped with both.

From the organ bars of Harlem in the 1990s to the Philharmonic concert stages of the 21st Century, Matthew has been on a remarkable journey of musical expression and exploration for more than 20 years. Over the span of his career he has performed and recorded with outstanding artists both inside and outside the jazz world, including Steve Wilson, Dennis Mackrel, Randy Brecker, John Medeski, Don Byron, Heather Masse, Roswell Rudd and renowned Herbie Hancock drummer Mike Clark performing the music of Herbie’s iconic Headhunters band. His enormous versatility in jazz, rock, pop and fusion has led to performances for the Hudson Valley Philharmonic performing with a broad span of diverse artists from Irish tenor Ronan Tynan to Chris Barron of the Spindoctors, and backing notable popular singers of many eras. Matthew has also toured with the traveling Broadway review The Broadway Boys with Jesse Nager (who played the part of Smoky Robinson in the Broadway show Motown). Freelancing in a variety of settings in blues, rock and funk, Matthew currently performs with Jerry Marotta’s Reelin’ in the Years, playing the music of Steely Dan; and with Steve Maglio who has the longest running nightclub show in NYC history. Matthew has led his own funk band Fat Mink since 2005, and is co-leader of the jazz ensemble the Matthew Finck Jonathan Ball Project. He also has composed and performed music for video games since 2001. (For greater detail on Matthew’s outstanding career, click here mattfinck.com

In truth, Matthew’s intensive musical journey began at birth, through the influence of his prominent saxophonist father Ron Finck, the extended family of notable musicians with whom he played, and the future musicians who studied with him – along with major figures in popular entertainment such as Sammy Davis Jr., Milton Berle and Jackie Mason – many of whom were frequent presences in his home. This immersion into the oral traditions of the jazz legacy through direct contact with elders and others in the pursuit of truth through music is an age-old element in the jazz tradition which has been less of an influence in more recent years.

This experience and exposure has been deeply embedded in Matthew as a musician and equally so as an educator, and makes his teaching skills that much more valuable to the young emerging artist. With the combination of vast professional experience in jazz and various forms of popular music, his personal musical virtuosity and the influence of direct contact with the historical legacy, Matthew’s educational offerings are particularly valuable to young artists on the committed path of musical expression and fulfillment in all disciplines.

Based on chapters 4-5 in the text. **Due 10/24** **Essay #1** Build upon what you spoke about in your oral presentations by choosing an idea to focus on. Topics can range from: The role of a specific instrument and how it changed through the decades or a specific instrumentalist or arranger who made a deep impact. You can utilize early jazz precursors, music leading through the big band era, start with its pioneers, the evolution of the instruments and their respective roles, seminal recordings, group improv leading to the focus shifting to the soloist, blues form, etc. Papers should be between **1000-1200 words,** 2-3 pages.

**Oral presentation #2, Due 11/14-11/17**

Choose one album from the list of 30 important albums (handed out in class) to discuss. Listen to the album in its entirety at least 2 x’s all the way through. Speak about what you liked, didn’t like, etc. Were you able to hear the influences of the previous musicians and composers? Evidence of what era it came from? What style of music was it? I highly recommend choosing a recording you like the sound of. Feel free to listen to an album not on the list but please check with me first. It doesn’t have to be something we listened to in class. You can also play excerpts of the recording during your presentation to support something you’re speaking about. **5-8 minutes.**

**Final Essay, due 12/4**

Based on chapters 6-10, your final essay can incorporate topics from the readings, any of the assigned listening and in-class discussions. Speak about a musician of your choice or the styles of Be Bop, Cool Jazz, Hard Bop or Fusion. Use recordings, historic evidence, political influences, etc. For example, one could discuss Miles Davis and his incredible influence in bebop, cool, hard bop, free, fusion, etc. You could also discuss something as broad as a specific instrument and how it changed across the decades. For example, early jazz rhythm section players used a banjo (1920’s) and the acoustic guitar, hollow body electrified guitar (1930-1940), louder amplification and the solid body guitar (late 1960’s-1970’s).

**1200-1500 words**

Please refer to Pages 262-286 in the text, ELEMENTS OF MUSIC for a background on the musical terms we will be using during the semester.

How to listen, what to always listen for:

1.Song form

2.Style (determine era of recording)

3.Instrumentation, listen to each musicians’ part as well as identify what instrument it is, *e.g.* alto saxophone, trombone, etc.

4.Feel (swing, straight 8th, shuffle, etc.)

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will develop the ability to differentiate between major periods of Jazz History by identifying instrumentation, types of improvisation, song form, arrangements, and sound recording quality.
- Students should be able to identify some major contributors to the music by their instrument, musical style, geographical location, and identify sound/video recording we listened to in class.
- Ideally students will develop a connection to the music and be able to discuss any ways they identify with any aspect of 19th and 20th century American improvised music.
