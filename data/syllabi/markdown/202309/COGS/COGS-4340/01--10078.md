## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will understand (a) why meaning-oriented natural language processing is a difficult problem and different ways that the field has tried to solve – or avoid – that problem; (b) how work on natural language processing has changed over time in response to both scientific and extra-scientific factors; and (c) why machine learning – including large language models (LLMS) – will not offer the whole answer to human-level artificial intelligence.
- Students will gain an in-depth understanding of the LEIA program of R&D, sufficient to prepare them to carry out work within this paradigm.
- Students will have a better appreciation for the scientific process as it actually plays out in the world, not as an idealized abstraction.
- Students will improve their oral and written communication skills, including their ability to collaborate with their peers.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

There will be many quizzes to test students' understanding of the readings assigned for homework. There will be two exams. There will be multiple writing assignments to fulfill the communication-intensive portion of the course. There will also be extensive class discussion, which also contributes to the communication-intensive objectives.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

The percentage of the grade that will be due to quizzes on homework readings depends in large part on student performance - i.e., whether they actually do the reading without the need to be checked up on. I discussed this with them during the first class meeting and explained that taking the course required them to buy in to this grading strategy. As a ballpark estimation, I expect that reading-based quizzes might be about 40% of the grade, exams another 40%, and writing and class performance another 20%.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance at every class meeting is required because a large portion of the grade is based on classwork and participation. An excused absence requires a doctor’s note. If you have to miss a scheduled exam, you need to tell me well in advance and I will choose a different day for it.

Assignments missed due to unexcused absences will receive a grade of 0. In addition, unexcused absences will directly penalize the grade as follows:

- 1-2 unexcused absences: no direct penalty but still a score of 0 if there is a quiz or graded classwork on the given day
- 3 unexcused absences: 5 points deducted from the course average
- 4 unexcused absences: 10 points deducted from the course average
- 5 unexcused absences: 15 points deducted from the course average
- 6 or more unexcused absences: a failing grade for the course

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
