## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will provide an overview of the field of linguistics, the study of human language. As linguistics is a relatively diverse and interdisciplinary field (as we will see), we will cover many different topics and sub-fields in an attempt to discover and discuss the various avenues linguistics has to offer. This broad approach will introduce students to a range of language-related topics.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Student grades will be comprised of five components: three exams, participation, and various assignments.

Exam I – 20%

Exam II – 20%

Exam III – 20%

Assignments – 30%

Attendance/Participation – 10%

Each exam will have an in-class component and a take home component. The take home component will be open-note, and the in-class component will not be. The exams are non-cumulative, but concepts from earlier parts of the semester will certainly still be relevant for later parts.

Assignments will appear throughout the semester accompanying class content. These are typically to be completed outside of class as homework.

Attendance/Participation is assigned along the following guideposts: 10 – almost always in class, frequent contributions; 9 – almost always in class, infrequent contributions; 8-7 – often/sometimes in class, frequent/infrequent contributions; 6-1 – varying levels of not really in class very often; 0 – never in class.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

The Rensselaer Handbook defines various forms of academic dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Students should familiarize themselves with this portion of the Handbook and should note that penalties for plagiarism and other forms of cheating can be severe. If you are caught cheating on an exam or an assignment, you will receive a grade of 0 for it and will be reported for academic misconduct.

You may not reproduce, verbally convey, or otherwise reveal the content of any exam to *any* individual at any time.
