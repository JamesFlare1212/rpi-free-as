## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Upon successful completion of the course, students will • recognize and appreciate how the humanities can inform and enrich scientific and technological perspectives
- learn to analyze diverse genres and styles of literary and graphic texts
- become familiar with basic literary terms and recognize them in a given text
- go beyond a surface reading of a text and explore deeper layers of meaning
- consider formal choices made by authors
- examine the influence of cultural and historical contexts
- practice writing academic essays
- participate in a learning community through feedback, encouragement, and accountability
- see how the skills honed in literary analysis are translatable to other contexts

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Assignments**

**Participation (worth 20%)**

Be on time, well prepared, and present in mind throughout the duration of our class meetings. Contribute consistently, actively and meaningfully to our discussion. Often, we will do short writing exercises and group work in class. The spirit of our meeting is collaborative rather than competitive.

- It is your responsibility to have the assigned reading materials with you in class (either paper copy or digital copy on a device other than a cellphone).
- Avoid showing up late or leaving early. Whenever you come to class after I take the roll, it is your responsibility to make sure your presence is recorded by me at the end of the class session.
- Using your cellphone during class, even if only to ‘check the time,’ is prohibited and will impact your participation grade.

Grading: At the end of each class session, I will make a note (either check, check +, or check -) in terms of each student’s contribution to the class. Full credit will receive a student who regularly attends on time, brings the assigned reading to class, is always well-prepared, focused and engaged in our conversation, and who participates actively, positively, and respectfully.

**Introduc****ing** **the Literary Work** **(worth** **10****%****)**

You will lead our class discussion once during the semester. You will adopt the text(s) for the day and close-read 5-7 short passages. There are two parts:

1\) Presentation (speaking time should be between 10 and 15 minutes – excluding the ensuing class discussion which you moderate)

- take us to a relevant quote, contextualize it, read it out loud
- perform a close reading: comment on word use and determine its significance here in this textual moment and for the work as a whole
- explain why each of your examples strikes you as important
- possibly draw connections to other course texts
- prepare five to seven productive, thought-provoking, open-ended, complex discussion questions

2\) Moderation of the Class Discussion (plan for roughly 10 minutes)

- read out your questions, explain or contextualize them
- be prepared to offer your own thoughts as well

The emphasis of this assignment is on quote analysis, and not on plot, author, or historical context. If you choose to include plot, biographical and other contextual information, keep it very brief (2min max).) If you consult other works (books, websites, etc.) to prepare, you must include a list of works cited.

Prepare aesthetically engaging slides to help visualize your close-reading. These slides should allow us to follow the structure of your talk; they should contain the quotes and the major points you want to raise. Don’t read off of your slides. Upload your presentation in .pdf format to LMS *before* our meeting. Come prepared using your own device to connect to the projector.

Pick your slot by the end of week 3. First come/first served. Up to two students can sign up for one slot. You may but don’t have to team up and prepare a joint presentation.

**Two Test****s** **(****each** **worth 1****5****%)**

Tests will be taken in class. They cover the materials of previous class meetings and will include a mix of question types that aim to test a student’s knowledge of these texts and understanding of connections between them. They also ask students to apply the analytical skills we practice in class.

**T****wo** **Short Papers** **(each worth** **20****%)**

Students will write two short papers that ask for their critical and creative engagement with the course materials. They involve drafts and feedback from the instructor and/or peers. Specific prompts will be posted and due dates appear on the course schedule. All first drafts must be substantially revised based on the student’s evolving engagement with the course as well as their peers’ and the instructor’s feedback. Each piece will be evaluated based on *how well* it executes the assignment requirements as well as the extent to which the writer has demonstrated a commitment to the revision process.

Do not:

- submit a first draft of a writing assignment as your final draft
- only make minimal changes
- disregard the comments your reviewers make

**Submission of Assignments**

All written assignments must follow MLA formatting (double-spaced, 1-inch margin on all sides, header, page numbers, 12pt. Times New Roman or comparable font, no extraneous spacing, etc.). Here’s an MLA sample paper: [https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/mla_style/mla_formatting_and_style_guide/mla_sample_paper.html](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/mla_style/mla_formatting_and_style_guide/mla_sample_paper.html).

All written assignments will be submitted via Blackboard LMS. Do not email me your assignment, unless we’ve made such arrangements. Submissions are only acceptable as a .pdf.

Please polish and proofread all typed work before you submit. **You are responsible for ensuring your work is turned in appropriately and legibly. Illegible or blank assignments turned in** **won’t** **count as submitted, so check your submissions.**

**Late Policy**

Deadlines ensure that your work and my work remains manageable. Submit assignments on time. The Presentation “Introducing the Literary Work” must be submitted by the due date in order to receive credit. In extenuating circumstances, I will consider granting a 24-hour extension on final drafts of papers. I will only accept your late draft without penalty if you requested and were granted an extension in advance, that is, before the due date. Late submissions without granted extension will be penalized with -10% per late day. No feedback will be provided for late submissions.

**There won’t be any** **extension on the final draft of our** **last** **paper** **as grades have to be submitted shortly after our last day of class****.**

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Because this class is inherently collaborative and interactive, students should expect to attend *all* class meetings and be on time. I define attendance as being present in body and mind, with the day’s required readings in hand and ready to take notes.

- Over the course of the term, you can use three unexcused absences without penalty. **For every class you miss after the maximum of** **three****, 2%** **will be deducted** **from your final grade**. There’s no need to tell the instructor why you’re missing one of your three allotted absences.
- Missing a considerable amount of time of a particular class meeting (such as arriving more than 15 minutes late, leaving for more than 15 minutes during class, or leaving for more than 15 minutes early) will result in an absence for the day.
- Extenuating circumstances: Absences can be excused in case of illness or emergency. Contact the instructor and show proper documentation.
- Students who miss class for extended periods of time without permission or explanation will be reported to Student Success or Public Safety for support and assistance, as needed. Requests for accommodations, exceptions, extensions, or incomplete grades due to longer illnesses or personal emergencies must be supported by written documentation from the Dean of Students, the Dean for Undergraduate Education, or the appropriate Class Dean.
- If you miss a class, *you* are responsible for anything that you missed. Request notes from your peers. Catch up by studying the material, then approach me with questions (preferably during office hours).
- You will only get a make-up assignment for an excused absence. (For example, if you use one of your three unexcused absences and we happen to do a graded in-class writing exercise, you will fail that assignment.)

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**A****cademic Integrity**  
Student-instructor relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that instructors have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you are responsible for becoming familiar with these. Some examples of plagiarism include copying, paraphrasing, or summarizing without acknowledgment, **using AI-generated content** while pretending it’s your own work, submission of another student's work as one's own, the purchase/use of prepared research or completed papers or projects, and the unacknowledged use of research sources gathered by someone else. Failure to indicate accurately the extent and precise nature of one's reliance on other sources is also a form of plagiarism. Students are responsible for understanding legitimate use of sources, the appropriate ways of acknowledging academic, scholarly, or creative indebtedness.

All assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work completed for this class in particular. In cases where help was received or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. If you have a compelling reason for wanting to use previous work of yours for the class, it must be substantially and fundamentally revised, and you must get approval from the instructor first.

I reserve the right to conduct an ad-hoc oral exam based on a student’s submitted work in case there’s a dispute between instructor and student whether the work was created with the help of ChatGPT.

Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy (for example, plagiarism due to use of ChatGPT) will result in a penalty of a zero for the assignment.

Second offenses will result in a penalty of a zero for the assignment and the potential for disciplinary action by the institution as the student will be reported to the Dean of Students.

Third offenses will result in automatic failure of the course.

If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

For more information, see Student Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs, [https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs), and consult *The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities*.
