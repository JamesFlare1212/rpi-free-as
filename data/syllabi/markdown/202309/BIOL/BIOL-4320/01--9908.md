## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Microbiology is the study of life forms too small to be seen with the naked eye, including Archaea, Bacteria, Viruses, and Protists. These microorganisms (microbes) are the engines of the biogeochemical processes that occur around the world, from the bottoms of the oceans to our guts. In this course, through hypothesis development and the application of experimental methods, you will learn a variety of microbiological methods to culture and explore a diversity of microorganisms.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

You should be able to:

1. Demonstrate an ability to formulate and design experiments based on the scientific method.
2. Analyze and interpret results from a variety of microbiological methods, and apply these methods to new situations.
3. Effectively communicate fundamental concepts of microbiology.
4. Properly prepare and view specimens for examination using microscopy.
5. Use aseptic and selective techniques to enrich for and isolate microorganisms.
6. Use many different methods to identify bacteria.
7. Document and report on experimental protocols, results and conclusions.
8. Practice safe microbiology, using appropriate protective and emergency procedures.
9. Ask testable questions about microorganisms in the environment around us.
10. Demonstrate an ability to communicate and work with others in a laboratory environment.
11. Express yourself through a combination of microbiology and art.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**ACADEMIC INTEGRITY**

Academic integrity is of utmost importance within the scientific and academic community, and it will be diligently monitored and enforced throughout this course. While collaboration is encouraged, it is essential that all final work is an individual's own words and completed independently. Plagiarism and any form of cheating, whether it be on quizzes, exams, reports, or any other assignments, are considered unacceptable. The foundation of student-teacher relationships relies on trust. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions regarding course structure and content, while teachers must trust that the assignments submitted by students are their own work. Engaging in acts that breach this trust undermines the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities offers a comprehensive definition of academic dishonesty (https://publicsafety.rpi.edu/personal-safety/student-handbook). As a Rensselaer student, it is your responsibility to familiarize yourself with and understand these rules. If you have any questions regarding these policies prior to submitting an assignment, please seek clarification. Below is a non-exhaustive list of regulations:

- Your notebook, written responses on assessments, and presentation materials must be composed in your own words. In instances of collaborative assignments, it is crucial to document the contributions made by each student. Plagiarism in any form will not be tolerated.
- The use of websites and services, including but not limited to CHEGG, Quizlet, etc., for completing assignments is considered cheating. This includes the utilization of any Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools.
- Refrain from sharing information about an upcoming exam or quiz with other students who have yet to take it. Likewise, do not seek information about an exam or quiz from students who have not yet taken it.
- If a student is caught violating any of these rules, they will receive a grade of 0 for the corresponding exam or assignment and may be reported to the Dean of Students at the discretion of the instructor. A second incident will result in a final grade of F for the course and may lead to further disciplinary action by the Dean of Students. Assignments involving a breach of academic integrity cannot be excluded from the final grade calculation.
