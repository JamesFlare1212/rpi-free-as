## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

•This laboratory-based course is designed to familiarize new Ph.D. students with a laboratory they are interested in joining for the remainder of their graduate career. Students will be immersed in the day-to-day operations of their lab of choice, culminating in a presentation to the biological sciences department at the end of the rotation.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will investigate the topics and day to day activities in a lab and determine if the lab is a fit for their academic interests. Students will apply what they learn to a research project to investigate a research question in the lab. Students will evaluate the outcomes of this experiment to determine how their data add to the research goals in the lab. Students will demonstrate their understanding of the research by presenting their work in a departmental seminar.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

•Students will be assessed by participation in laboratory research and their presentation at the end of the rotation. For their lab participation, students will be expected to read papers relevant to their project and perform experiments as needed. For their presentation, students will create a presentation in consultation with their selector mentor related to their research project and present this at a departmental seminar. •Student Grade Allotment: •Lab Participation – 70% •Presentation – 30% •Total – 100%

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

•S: The presentation provided background on the scientific question that was raised in the research project. The student dedicated ample time and concentration to the lab component of the research. •U: The presentation had significant flaws and the student did not dedicate ample time and concentration to the lab component of the research.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
