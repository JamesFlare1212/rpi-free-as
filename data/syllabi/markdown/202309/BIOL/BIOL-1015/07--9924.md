## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is an introductory course in Biology. It is intended for undergraduates, both non-majors and majors. It serves as the first course for life science majors and for students interested in the health professions.

Laboratory activities: Students generate hypotheses, and share their ideas on how to test their hypotheses, within interactive in-person mini-groups. Hands-on wet labs include basic cell and molecular biological skills. Other activities include introduction to scientific computing in interactive virtual environments using Python and R.

The goal is for students to gain biological literacy by applying the scientific method. Students will collect various types of biological data, such as whole organismal or cellular or molecular data. Students use quantitative tools, and simulations. Students develop data dexterity, and analytical thinking skills while learning basic laboratory skills. This is a D1 Data Intensive Course.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

3. Students will earn basic wet lab skills.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**The Scientific Method** is applied to understand fundamental concepts in:

- Cell Biology - Mitosis, Meiosis, and the Cell Cycle.
- Molecular Biology - DNA replication, Protein Synthesis, and Regulation.
- Microbiology - Prokaryotic (versus Eukaryotic) cell size, shape & morphology.
- Virology - SarsCov2 genome & spike protein data visualization using PDB.
- Biochemistry - Structures of amino acids, nucleotides, atoms and bonds.
- Genetics - Mendelian Patterns, probabilities, ratios, Chi Square Analysis using corn purple/yellow plump/wrinkled mutant kernels and/or Fruit Flies.
- Ecology, Biodiversity and Conservation - Ecosystems, Biodiversity, Conservation, Epidemiology, eg., R0, vaccine critical threshold, Hardy Weinberg Allele frequency and other calculations.
- Evolution, Phylogeny - Cladistics, Bioinformatics.
- Medicine - Drug/vaccine design and genetic engineering using Simbio’s simulations.
