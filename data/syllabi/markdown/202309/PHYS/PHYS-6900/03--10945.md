## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course offers a series of invited talks related to physics or other disciplines where physics plays a role.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

No textbook. Students will fill out a learning work sheet in each colloquium.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students will learn current physics research and applications from invited speakers. Make connectivity of academic course work with physics research. Ask speakers questions and learn presentation skill from speakers.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The abstract of each colloquium will be announced through e mail a week before the scheduled talk from department staff.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be exposed to research presentations by different invited speakers from diverse disciplines in order to provide a broad view on how research works. Students will learn from different speakers how to present a formal research lecture. Students will be able to interact with different speakers from different institutions about research related to physics.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students must attend all colloquia and fill out work sheet what was learned from each lecture. Students will practice asking questions.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

The grades are S (satisfactory) or U (unsatisfactory).

In order to pass, students must attend all the colloquia and complete the learning work sheet for each colloquium. The learning work sheet will be provided and the filled out sheet be submitted at the end of each colloquium to the instructor.

Students must ask the speaker at least one question in the colloquium series this semester to receive a grade.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students must attend every colloquium. In case of illness, please arrange a memo from RPI health service and send it to the instructor after recovery. For other excuses, please get a pre-approval from the instructor before the scheduled talk.

**Academic Integrity Policy:**

Academic integrity is one of the cornerstones of RPI. Students taking courses at RPI have a right to expect that their work will be evaluated fairly with respect to other students. They have a right to expect that other students will not attempt to enhance their own grades or the grades of their friends by cheating. Teachers have a right to expect that their students are honest and submit work reflecting their own efforts. In an atmosphere of academic integrity, students and teachers are on the same team trying to achieve the same learning objectives.

Collaboration and discussion **are allowed** **and encouraged** in the homework, the project and in-class activities. However, you must write-up your own solution, as the results of synthesizing discussions with others, and not merely copying others’ solutions. On exams, you are on your own and not allowed to discuss anything with your classmates. Thus, don’t copy someone else’s homework, in-class activities, and quizzes and don’t cheat on exams. If I suspect you of either, I’ll ask for an explanation. If your explanation is unsatisfactory, you’ll be given a grade of **0** (zero) and reported to the Dean of Students. If this happens more than once, you will be given an **F** for the course, and the entire matter will be forwarded to the Dean of Students.
