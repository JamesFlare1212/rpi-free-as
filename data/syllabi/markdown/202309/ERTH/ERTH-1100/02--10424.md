## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Read and understand graphical representations of data
- Express some evidence for the theory of plate tectonics
- Understand that rocks record the processes (and events) that operate on Earth
- Describe, analyze and assess the geologic features that impact your daily lives
- Participate as informed citizens in decision-making processes on issues that will affect yourself, your family, your community, country and the world

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Pre-Class and In-class activities:** I will often request that you complete a brief exercise prior to lecture, on your own, as a warm-up for class. This may mean reading an article, watching a video or looking at a dataset. For most pre-class activities, you will complete a short assignment on BlackBoard. These will be due at the beginning of class and available on LMS at least a week in advance.

In class, we will be utilizing a variety of techniques in the classroom to improve your learning and retention. Chiefly, this will include working independently and in small groups on activities, taking quizzes to gage overall classroom understanding, and generating discussion. For some of these activities, points will be awarded for participation/completion and for some, points will be awarded for accuracy. Occasionally, if an assignment is not completed in class, you will be asked to complete it as homework and turn in the next class period.

Because active participation will be a major component of the course, what we do in the class meeting will determine a large portion of your grade. Of course, I understand that sometimes emergencies or other unexpected circumstances arise that make attendance that day impossible. If this is the case, please talk with me as soon as possible so we can make arrangements to get you caught up (this will not apply to non-emergencies-like oversleeping). If you will be absent from a class for a university-sponsored activity, please make arrangements with time – beforehand – regarding any work you might miss. While I will work with you to help you catch up, generally, there will be no make-ups for missed in-class or pre-class activities (either for technical difficulties or for absences). However, the lowest 20% of in-class grades will be dropped at the end of the semester. The dropped scores act as your missing class freebies and can be used for any reason (illness, studying, travel, wild monkeys breaking into your dorm room, binge watching Netflix, etc.).

**Quizzes:** There will be 7 quizzes throughout the semester to check for content understanding and to ensure you are engaging in distributive studying. Each quiz will be administered at the start of class on Tuesdays. Late arrivals will not be given additional time. One make-up will be allowed for quizzes but must be made-up within 48 hours and arranged prior to the quiz. Additionally, the lowest quiz grade will be dropped at the end of the semester. The dates of the quizzes are on the schedule and will not change.

**Preliminary Examinations:** There will be 2 preliminary, cooperative exams throughout the semester. Each exam will cover the topics preceding that exam (**not cumulative**-however, keep in mind that we will be building upon prior knowledge). Make-up must be requested in writing **prior to the exam** and include documentation of extreme hardship.

Exam Part 1: You will independently take the exam (75%) for the first hour. After the hour has elapsed, all exams must be turned in.

Exam Part 2 (optional): You will take a new version of the exam (25%) but can discuss with classmates. No internet/books/notes/phone-a-friend allowed. You will have 45 minutes to take this exam. Each person must hand in their own exam.

- o If you choose not to complete Part 2 OR if your score is higher from Part 1, the Part 1 score will be your grade. Bottom line, taking the cooperative exam will not negatively affect your grade!
- o If you finish Part 1 before the hour is over, you can review notes/internet/etc. as long as you leave the room. Once you turn in your exam and leave, you cannot re-enter the exam room until Part 2 begins.

**Final Examination:** The final examination is comprehensive and will cover all material presented in the course. This exam will be done independently. If your final exam score is higher than the average of the scores on the preliminary exams, this score will replace the latter score provided you took both preliminary examinations.

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

**Late Policy:** If an assignment is due in class, it will be due at the start of class. If it is turned in at the end of class, it is considered late. Assignments will be accepted up to 1 week past the due date. Scores on late assignments will be penalized 10% each day they are late. So, if an assignment is due in class on a Tuesday at 2:00 pm and you turn it in between Tuesday at 2:01 pm and Wednesday at 2:00 pm, you will lose 10%. *After 1 week, the grade becomes a zero.*

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

Based on experience, here are some suggestions for success:

1. Read before and after class. Even if you don’t buy the text, if we are talking about igneous rocks, google igneous rocks and see what you find out. By introducing yourself to key terms, it will make following class easier.
2. If you have a question at any point, ask it! This could be in class, during office hours, an email, slipping a note at the end of class.
3. Focus on concepts. How does information build on each other? How are concepts related to each other?
4. Make sketches as notes!
5. All PowerPoint notes will be posted after class. Instead of focusing on getting down all the words on the slide, focus on key phases, questions, and concepts. Your notes should be in our own words, not mine!
6. Don’t forget to take notes on class discussions, activities and videos

Allow yourself to have fun!
