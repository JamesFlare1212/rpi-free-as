## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is meant to give students the tools to apply geochemical thermodynamic models to research questions of their choosing. The course will focus on calculations of thermodynamic reaction properties, fluid speciation, mineral saturation states, organic transformations and stability, water/rock/organic reactions, and reaction-path modeling. The geochemical models used are based on the Helgeson-Kirkham-Flowers Equations of State originally implemented at SUCPRT92 (Johnson et al., 1992) and speciation and reaction-path models previously developed as the EQ3/6 software package. These fortran-based computer codes have been repackaged in the WORM (Water-Organic-Rock-Microbe) Portal hosted by ASU (worm-portal.asu.edu) using python inside Jupyter Notebooks. The first portion of the semester will include some lecture time to go over the basic geochemical and thermodynamic concepts, and several mini problems to get everyone on board with running the codes. The second portion of the semester will be dedicated to each student’s research project using these tools.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Successful Completion of this course will lead to the following: Through readings, lectures, in class activities, homework, and a research project students should: • Understand the theoretical basis for geochemical reaction property, speciation and reaction path models. • Demonstrate the ability to modify pre-existing notebooks to implement the software packages in the WORM portal to calculate reaction properties; fluid speciation and mineral saturation states, and reaction path models that represent fluid/gas/mineral interactions. • Design in silico experiments to answer specific questions about natural geochemical systems • Design a research project/question that can be explored using these modeling approaches and present results on this near the end of term.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Student progress will be evaluated through in class activities (15%), homework (30%), research proposal (10%), research modeling results and research report (30%), and the final research presentation (15%). No final exam will be given.

*In class activities and homework:* During class meetings and outside of class, students will complete modeling projects as assigned to develop modeling skills and better understanding of geochemical concepts.

*Research proposal:* Students will prepare a short proposal of their research objectives that will be explored using the geochemical modeling approaches discussed in class. Students will proceed with their research project throughout the semester and present their results orally at the end of the semester. Students will also prepare a short research report.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:**

This course will utilize a ***zero-tolerance policy*** for any form of cheating or other academic misconduct. All submitted assignments will be subjected to copying and plagiarism checkers. University policy deals with the problem of academic dishonesty, cheating, and plagiarism. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. No violation will be tolerated in this class. The sanctions provided in this policy will be used to deal with any violations.

Academic dishonesty includes, but is not limited to, copying material from another student or use of written notes during an exam, representing others’ work as your own, changing answers on an exam paper after the paper has been graded, etc. Any student caught cheating will be assigned an F for the course. Furthermore, violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education for undergraduate and graduate students, respectively).

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ***ask*** for clarification.

***A special note on plagiarism.*** Please see the definition of plagiarism given below, as outlined in The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights. To be clear, any time material that is not the students’ own is used, it should be in quotes and contain a citation. If a student paraphrases work from elsewhere, it also requires a citation. Furthermore, even when citations are used, others’ work cannot be used as a student response on an assignment. For example, in this class, if the main conclusions are given in an article, they cannot be used verbatim as a response in the post-discussion evaluation, even if a citation is present.

*“**Plagiarism:** Representing the work or words of another as one’s own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference. For example, using sentences verbatim from a published source in a term paper without appropriate referencing, or presenting as one’s own the detailed argument of a published source, or presenting as one’s own electronically or digitally enhanced graphic representations from any form of media.”* – ACADEMIC INTEGRITY (2014-2016 Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights & Responsibilities, August 2014)
