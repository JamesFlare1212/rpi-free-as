## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course addresses classical and contemporary issues in the theory of money and banking. Topics covered include: financial markets and interest rates, commercial banking, the Federal Reserve System, theories of money demand and money supply, and the theoretical and practical aspects of monetary policy and economic stabilization. Subject matter classifies as macroeconomics, but also draws from microeconomic fundamentals.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Recommended Textbook: *Money, Banking, and The Financial System*, any edition, authors: Hubbard/O’Brien

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- to gain a factual knowledge of money, banking, and the financial system
- to practice interpreting data in the monetary context
- to understand monetary policy and its implications for national and world economies.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

Tests: There will be three tests given on the scheduled dates in the classroom. Test format is on paper, multiple choice. You are allowed to bring one page of notes for use during the test. You must bring your student ID card to the test in order to take the test. **If you do not bring your ID card to the test, you will not be allowed to take the test and your score will be recorded as zero.** Any student who arrives more than 10 minutes late to a test may be denied the test. Prohibited during testing are communication with peers (verbal, written, electronic, other), mobile devices (computers, cell phones, tablets, other), notes, books, hats, and earbuds/pods. There is a zero tolerance policy towards cheating on tests. Cheating includes any means of accessing information, sharing answers, and communication among students. If any incident of cheating on a test is discovered, the involved student(s) will receive a failing grade for the course; see the policy on Academic Integrity, below. **MAKEUP TEST POLICY:** *Makeup tests will only be allowed for absences having an official Rensselaer excuse issued by the Health Center or the Dean of Students, and submitted to the professor by two days after the test. The makeup test is scheduled during the week after the test. If you miss a test and do not have an official Rensselaer excuse, you must take the optional final exam to improve your test average.*

Optional Final Exam: An optional final exam will be offered as a way for students to attempt to improve their grade. The final exam will cover all course material. If you take the final exam, the score you obtain on the final exam will be compared with the average of your tests. You will be given the higher of these scores as your grade for the test portion of the course. Therefore, it cannot hurt you to take the optional final exam. *If you miss a test(s) and do not have a proper excuse (see policy on makeup tests above), then plan on taking the optional final exam to improve your grade.* The Registrar will announce the date of the optional final exam in October, to take place during finals week.

Fed Essay: Students will submit a written analysis of the most recent meeting of the Federal Open Market Committee, to be submitted in LMS by the due date specified; specific instructions for content are posted to LMS and will be explained in class. Late Policy: essays are accepted late only by permission of professor and only if permission is requested within 2 days after the due date; there is a 25% late penalty if professor can accept the late essay. If an excuse can be provided by the Rensselaer Health Center or Dean of Students, then the late penalty will be waived. After grades are posted, you have seven days to appeal the grade.
