## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Attention is directed primarily to variations in the aggregate volume of output, income, and employment. Cyclical fluctuations and long-term economic trends are examined and the interrelations of business and government policies are analyzed. The applicability of economic theory to the problems of business forecasting is discussed.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

“Macroeconomics” by R. Dornbusch, S. Fischer and R. Startz. Irwin-McGraw Hill, 13th Edition.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course provides conceptual, theory and some applications-oriented foundations covering the aggregate economy. Students will be able to show proficiency working with concepts related to:

(a) aggregate measures such as GDP, unemployment etc.;

(b) aggregate demand-supply modeling;

(c) business cycles;

(d) monetary policy, and

(e) fiscal policy.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Homework**

A total of five homework assignments will be given throughout the semester which accounts for 20% of your overall grade. These assignments will be available on LMS and are due at 11:59 pm on the scheduled due dates. You can either type your answers or scan your handwritten results and upload it on LMS (use a PDF version). Make sure your answers are clear and readable in case of choosing a handwritten version; you may receive no credit if the answers are not easy to read. **Late assignments are not accepted (no exceptions)** and will receive a zero grade.

**Quizzes**

During the semester, you will have four quizzes which account for 15% of your overall grade. The quizzes include multiple choice questions and they are all equally weighted. **There are no make-up quizzes** **(no exceptions)**. To mitigate this policy, I will drop the lowest grade, or alternately, you may skip one quiz during the semester.

**Exams**

There are no make-up exams except for health and family issues which would typically be considered as acceptable excuses and are at the discretion of the instructor to decide on a case-by-case basis. In case you can’t make an exam as scheduled, you should let me know **in advance** to get it approved. I will make arrangements for you to take the exam early, depending upon the circumstances of your absence.

**Extra Credit Presentation**

A short presentation (8-10 mins) can be scheduled individually for each student who is interested in receiving up to 3 extra credit points which will be added to the final course grade. Students should analyze a current event in relation to the historical and global context of macroeconomic policies and development. The presentation should represent the student's research into areas that support the focus of the course; it should be analytical and demonstrate critical thinking rather than a recitation of factual information. Make sure to go over some recent data related to your topic as well as the current or future related policies. You can schedule your presentation throughout the semester according to the table on the last page of this document by letting me know a preferred date and topic in class (no emails please!). Make sure not to read from your slides and be ready at the scheduled date of your presentation or otherwise 2 points will be deducted from your final course grade. The last day for determining your presentation date and topic is **Sept 14**.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance & Participation**

As mentioned above, 5% of your overall grade will be accounted for attendance and participation. Attendance will not be taken for each class, but related points will be lost if you are absent when attendance is taken. A sign in sheet will be distributed at the end of SOME classes and leaving the class before having it signed will be recorded as an absence.

The class participation portion of the grade is a subjective evaluation by me of your contribution to the class discussions. You can increase your participation points by coming to class prepared and trying to answer my questions. You should also ask questions to make sure that you understand the material. Ask or comment on other students’ presentations, ask about what is not clear from the reading, or about a homework problem you do not understand. Trying to engage in class discussions will also be considered at the end of the semester in curving close to border grades. You can also make up for your missing attendance points by increasing your participation score.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Dishonesty**

As a future financial professional, your most valuable asset will be your personal integrity. Cases of academic dishonesty are viewed as a serious violation of the University’s student code of conduct. Examples of academic dishonesty include, but are not limited to:

- copying or sharing answers on graded homework assignments
- doing assignments for other students
- including information in written assignments without proper citations

Academic dishonesty will not be tolerated and penalties can include canceling a student's enrollment without a grade or receiving an F for the course. While problems of academic dishonesty rarely come up, feel free to bring them to my attention, and I will work with you to address them appropriately.

**Audio and Video Recording**

Surreptitious or covert video-taping of class or unauthorized audio recording of class is prohibited by law and by Board of Regents policy. This class may be videotaped or audio recorded only with the written permission of the instructor. In order to accommodate students with disabilities, some students may be given permission to record class lectures and discussions. Therefore, students should understand that their comments during class may be recorded.

**Students with Learning Disabilities**

Any student with a learning disability needing academic adjustments is requested to provide me with a letter explaining the necessary accommodations as soon as possible to arrange for timely and appropriate adjustments or accommodations. The letter should come from the Director of Disabilities Services.
