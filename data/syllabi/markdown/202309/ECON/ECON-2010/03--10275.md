## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- To be able to explain to others why economists use mathematics and formal modeling to analyze situations of constrained choice. To be able to use the mathematical tools of algebra and calculus to determine the optimal allocation of resources for a given economic agent’s preferences and constraints (where the agent is either a consumer or producer). To be prepared, in subsequent classes, to approach academic articles in economics and explain the article’s mathematical model and/or implied theoretical concepts to others. To be able to document the limits of economic reasoning and modeling, and clearly explain to others the choices that might separate economic models from the real-world choice situations that they supposedly represent.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

1. **Exams (75 points. Each exam is worth 25 points. Best three count towards grade.)**
   1. Exam 1
   2. Exam 2
   3. Exam 3
   4. Final Exam

The class meeting prior to each exam will be used as a “recap” class and will include a detailed review of problem set solutions. Note that events outside of my control may require that exam dates be altered. I will give you as much notice as possible when/if that happens. If you miss an exam for health reasons, my preferred solution is to re-weight other assignments to account for the missed exam. Other situations are dealt with on a case-by-case basis **and are at the instructor’s discretion**.

2. **Problem Sets (25, 5 points each):** Problem sets will be posted on blackboard along with an associated due date (you will have at least one week to work on the problem set). There will be six or perhaps seven problem sets in total, but I will only count your best five. Each is worth 5 points toward your grade.

**Engagement/Participation (up to 5 *bonus* points):** You are asked to come to class prepared and ready to engage with the material. Maximum points will be earned by those who are fully engaged with the class and the material. One way to show me that you are engaged is to make regular, relevant, and thoughtful contributions to class but that is not the only way! Also, there will be in class exercises that I will collect at the end of class to check on learning, attendance, and engagement.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
