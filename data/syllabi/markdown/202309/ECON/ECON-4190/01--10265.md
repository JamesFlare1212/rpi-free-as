## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course investigates the significance of economic globalization, covering the following topics: international trade and financial flows, technological innovation and intellectual property, technology transfer, national government and transnational corporations, natural resources, health and the environment, impacts on selected industries and countries, and roles of the world trade organization and international monetary fund. The major controversies surrounding globalization are identified, and alternative arguments are evaluated based on available evidence.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- demonstrate knowledge of the major economic theories in international economics.
- use formal economic models to make prediction of the outcomes of trade and government trade policies.
- clearly explain how international economics and globalization affects output, incomes, and welfare.
- demonstrate an understanding of major economic events, government policies, and international institutions that have affected the patterns of international trade and globalization.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Homework**

A total of 8 online homework assignments will be given throughout the semester which accounts for 10% of your overall grade. Each homework is designed to provide students with an opportunity to practice the concepts learned in class. Homework assignments are available on MyEconLab and must be submitted online on the scheduled due date. It is your responsibility to check MyEconLab frequently for any update on the due dates. **Late submissions are not accepted (no exceptions)**. To mitigate this policy, I will drop the lowest homework grade, or alternately, you may skip one online homework during the semester.

**In-Class Group Assignment**

A total of 6 in-class group assignments will be given throughout the semester which accounts for 10% of your overall grade. Group assignments are designed to give students the opportunity to work through the models discussed in class by effectively communicating with classmates. At the end of some lectures, there will be time to work with your group on the questions corresponding to the lecture and I will be there to help you with any question as well. You are responsible for submitting these group assignments on LMS which are due at 11:59 pm on the scheduled due dates. You can either type your answers or scan your handwritten results and upload it on LMS (use a PDF version). Make sure your answers are clear and readable in case of choosing a handwritten version; you may receive no credit if the answers are not easy to read. **Late assignments are not accepted (no exceptions)**.

**Short Assignment Presentation (SAP)**

This course is considered as a Communication Intensive (CI) course and to meet a part of the requirements, you should be able to apply course content to address real or realistic problems through effective communication. SAPs are designed for this purpose and they consist of an essay and a short presentation (10-12 mins) as scheduled individually for each student throughout the semester. Students will analyze a current event in relation to the historical and global context of international economics, related policies, and the role of international organizations. The presentation should represent the student's research into areas that support the focus of the course; it should be analytical and demonstrate critical thinking rather than a recitation of factual information. Make sure to go over some recent data related to your topic as well as the current or future related policies. You can schedule your presentation according to the table on the last page of this document by letting me know a preferred date and topic in class (no emails please!). Make sure not to read from your PowerPoint slides and be ready at the scheduled date of your presentation. The last day for determining your presentation date and topic is **Sep 12**.

You should have your short essay which is mainly a summary of your presentation typed and printed at the time of your presentation. It should consist of **a minimum of 2500 words** and not more than 2800 words (this is about 6 pages, use Times New Roman 12 and line spacing 1.5). Don’t forget to include the references at the end of your essay (references are NOT included in the minimum words requirement). Do not copy and paste anything from any source in your essay, this would be considered as cheating; read the material and write it in your own words.

**Exams**

There are no make-up exams except for health and family issues which would typically be considered as acceptable excuses and are at the discretion of the instructor to decide on a case-by-case basis. In case you can’t make an exam as scheduled, you should let me know **in advance** to get it approved. I will make arrangements for you to take the exam early, depending upon the circumstances of your absence.

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance & Participation**

As mentioned above, 5% of your overall grade will be accounted for attendance and participation. You can also make up for your missing attendance points by increasing your participation score.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Dishonesty**

As a future financial professional, your most valuable asset will be your personal integrity. Cases of academic dishonesty are viewed as a serious violation of the University’s student code of conduct. Examples of academic dishonesty include, but are not limited to:

- copying or sharing answers on graded homework assignments
- including information in written assignments without proper citations

Academic dishonesty will not be tolerated and penalties can include canceling a student's enrollment without a grade or receiving an F for the course. While problems of academic dishonesty rarely come up, feel free to bring them to my attention, and I will work with you to address them appropriately.

**Audio and Video Recording**

Surreptitious or covert videotaping of class or unauthorized audio recording of class is prohibited by law and by Board of Regents policy. This class may be videotaped or audio recorded only with the written permission of the instructor. To accommodate students with disabilities, some students may be given permission to record class lectures and discussions. Therefore, students should understand that their comments during class may be recorded.

**Students with Learning Disabilities**

Any student with a learning disability needing academic adjustments is requested to provide me with a letter explaining the necessary accommodations as soon as possible to arrange for timely and appropriate adjustments or accommodations. The letter should come from the Director of Disabilities Services.
