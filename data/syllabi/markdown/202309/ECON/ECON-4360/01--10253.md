## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

## This course provides an introduction to experimental methods in economics. It teaches you how to test economic theories, as well as introducing you to the experimental methods that are used to investigate questions that may not be possible to study using existing data sets. Students will be introduced to state-of-the-art research methodology in experimental economics. Although not required, it is highly suggested that students take a statistics or econometrics course before taking this course.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Identify and understand the elements of experimental design
- Analyze and critique an experimental design
- Analyze human motivation and behavior across different economic paradigms
- Evaluate existing work on selected topics in behavioral and experimental economics
- Replicate a published experimental paper by running the experiment and analyzing the data, and then produce a replication report

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

***Participation:***

- Your participation grade will be determined by two factors:
  1. *Active participation in class (*50%):
- I will determine your grade based on your participation and your general classroom citizenship. I expect you to show up on time and attend class, participate in and contribute to in-class discussions (by asking questions, providing answers or comments etc), and read the materials that will be assigned. In general, you are expected to join all lectures and be an **active** participant.
- Your participation during **paper presentations** will also impact your grade**.** You are expected to be an **active** participant by asking questions to presenters and sharing your comments.
  2. *Moblab Performance (*50%):
- Your relative earnings in Moblab will determine your grade (out of 10 points). Lowest grade will be a 3 and highest will be a 10.
- I will take your special circumstances into consideration. If your circumstances change, make sure to communicate this to me as soon as you can. I will keep track of your active engagement weekly.

***Paper Presentations:***

- During each of the paper presentation days, we will discuss two papers in class.
- You will find three papers and share them with me; I will choose one. The paper must have been published within the last few years in *Experimental Economics* journal.
- Everyone comes to class having read the abstract, introduction, and conclusion sections of both papers.
- Each of these papers will be presented by 1-3 student(s), depending on the class size. Presenters are responsible for presenting their paper in an engaging manner by asking thoughtful questions and creating a discussion environment if appropriate.
- Students are expected take an active role as attendees by asking presenters questions.

***Grades:*** Your grade will be determined by the quality of paper presentation and your ability to foster an active and engaging discussion environment.

***Quiz:***

- At the **end** of **each** paper presentation, we will randomly determine whether there will be a quiz (with a 50% chance). Each quiz will be 5 min long and contain one or two questions about the paper presented and its in-class discussion.
- No make-up quiz will be given without a university-approved and documented excuse.

***Exams:***

- There will be two essay type closed book exams. Exam dates are indicated on the course calendar.
- No make-up exams will be given without a university-approved and documented excuse. If you know in advance that you have a conflict with a possible exam time, please see me as soon as possible so that we can work out an alternative.

***Referee Report:***

- Guidelines will be shared with you in class and on LMS. Referee report deadline is indicated on the course calendar. Please notice that it is due by **9:30 am** via email.
- Your grade will be determined based on the quality of your report (80%) and your performance during in-class discussion (20%).

***Research Project (ECON 4360 Students):***

You will be put in a group, and each group will be responsible for conducting one replication experiment. All replication experiments will be run using google forms or a similar platform. More detailed instructions to come, but here are the main parts.

1. *Group Formation.* I will provide a list of potential papers for students to choose from. Each student will examine these papers and submit their preference to me. Based on your preferences, I will put you in groups. However, you are also free to form your own groups. There will be a total of 5 groups.
2. *Read the paper carefully*. You will read your assigned paper very carefully and understand their research in detail. You are expected to understand every detail about their experimental design and think about how to implement it on an online platform.
3. *Group Project Meetings*. See course calendar for the exact dates. Before the first meeting, you must have read your paper very carefully. During the first meeting, you will meet with your group members and decide on how to conduct your experiment in class using google forms (or another platform).
4. *Prepare Experimental Materials*. This means that you will have to prepare all the materials that you will use to run your experiment (e.g. google forms). You will add me as a collaborator on your google form and share the screenshots (from the participants’ view not the experimenter’s) in a word document. I will go over your materials and provide feedback. You will revise your materials if needed. You will receive my feedback only twice, so make it count!
5. *Get ready for the experiment*. Incorporate my feedback on your experimental materials and update them. Schedule meetings with me if further discussions are needed.
6. *Conduct the experiment*. Your group will conduct the entire experiment. You will explain the instructions to the class, run the experiment, keep records, collect the data etc.
7. *Organize your data and analyze it*. Organize your data and start analyzing it. You should start working on your final project write-up and presentation slides. Meet with your TA if you need assistance.
8. *Present the replication findings in class*. Present a quick summary of the original paper, their motivation and research question. Then present your own data and compare your findings to theirs. In presenting your data, you will provide summary tables and graphs, calculate useful statistics, perform statistical tests, and discuss how your data compares to the original study. I will provide a template for this presentation.

*Evaluation*. At the end of your presentation, your classmates will evaluate your research. We will also have an in-group evaluation. This means that each group member will evaluate one another. I will incorporate these evaluations to determine your grade from the group project.

9. *Final Project Write up*. You will submit a replication report. I will provide a template for this. This is the final product of your hard work!

**Research Project Grades (ECON 4360 Students):**

Students will earn numerical grades using the following weighting:

*Meeting deadlines and overall group preparedness (10%)*

You are expected to meet all deadlines. You will be penalized for each deadline you miss.

*Quality of Experimental Materials (25%)*

The quality of your experimental materials submitted during the first round and how closely you follow my instructions to revise them during the second round will help me determine your grade.

*Presentation (30%)*

The quality of your presentation and how closely you follow my instructions will help me determine your grade.

*Final Project Write up (35%)*

The overall quality of your replication project write-up, data analysis and presentation, how well you follow my instructions etc. will help me determine your grade.

***Based on my own observations as well as the feedback provided by your group members, I will revise your final grade. Thus, it is possible that not all group members will receive the same grade.***
