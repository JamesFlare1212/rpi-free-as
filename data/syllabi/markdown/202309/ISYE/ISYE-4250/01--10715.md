## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course explores the design and operation of logistics systems in the context of three major design goals: low cost, high throughput, and high levels of service. The course aims to provide students with a basic background in plant layout, materials handling, and storage/distribution systems emphasizing the formulation and application of mathematical models. The course aims for students to gain insights into the applications of engineering design principles as well as analytical and computational modeling as they relate to problems in industrial logistics through frequent assignments and a team project.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Explain facility logistics systems, system components, and component operations
- Analyze flow, space, and activity relationships for layout and material handling alternatives
- Construct models for the analysis and design of facility logistics systems
- Illustrate the techniques and approaches used to design facility logistics systems
- Carry out and present a facility design project by using relevant models and algorithms and applying standards of professional and ethical responsibility

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

Atendance and Lateness: If you are unable to attend class or meet a homework deadline due to illness, injury, or a similar emergency, please get in touch with  
the instructor via email/Webex within 24 hours after the scheduled date to discuss a plan for accommodation and support. If applicable, send the instructor Dean's signed medical excuse. Each student has the opportunity to move the weight of a homework assignment to the weight of its associated exam. For example, if a student is not able to submit the homework assignment on time, then they can elect to have the homework weight transferred to the exam that the homework material is on. This option can be used multiple times throughout the semester as long as the student documents in writing (via an email to the instructor) their intention to transfer homework weight to exam weight BEFORE the homework assignment is due. Late assignments (without arrangements before the assessment is due) will receive a zero unless an emergency arises that is approved by the Student Experience Office.  
Missed exams: Make-up exams will only be given to students who have a valid excuse that the instructor has approved before the due date. Simply leaving a Webex message or sending an email without confirmation is not sufficient. Concerned students must present a note from the Dean of Student Experience Office. There will be no make-up for missing the make-up exam. If you have an emergency, the campus is here to help, please coordinate with the Student Experience Office on the 4th floor of Academy Hall, x8022, se@rpi.edu.  
Re-grade requests: Exams submitted for regrading must not be altered from the original work submitted for grading. All requests must be made in writing via email by attaching a well-phrased note to the original work explaining in full the reason for the request and the points you think you deserve for your error. Requests for a regrade must be submitted no later than the next class meeting after the graded material is available to the class for the request to be considered.  
Tracking performance: Following guidance from the School of Engineering, the Early Warning System (EWS) incorporated in the Self-Service Information System (SIS) will be used when any exam score falls below a passing grade, when any exam is missed regardless of cause, and when cumulative performance jeopardizes successful completion of the course. The goal is to help you in a timely manner.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Collaboration with other students enrolled in the course is permitted on homework assignments, but it is important to ensure that all work is original and not copied from any source, including other students' work. If you worked with other people on a problem, include their names as ``Collaborators" within the specific problem section. It is expected that you present your own solutions in a way that makes sense to you. Plagiarism, or copying the work of others, is a violation of the RPI Community Standard and will not be tolerated. Using the answers of another current or past student, either given in written or verbal form is not allowed. Only permitted reference material or computational aids may be used during an exam if announced as usable. For undergraduate students, the standard punishment for the first offense is a 0 on the assignment and/or a full letter grade reduction on the final semester grade. Students whose violations are more flagrant will receive a higher penalty. Undergraduate students caught a second time will receive an immediate F in the course, regardless of the circumstances.
