## Course Content

### Content Details:

This course delves into human sensory and motor functions, along with principles governing the design of visual and auditory displays, control devices, tools, and workspaces. It assesses the influence of the human-machine interface on workplace efficacy, highlighting how design choices impact well-being, safety, and contentment. Students will explore models depicting humans as information processors, delve into advanced cognitive theories, and gain insights into concepts like situational awareness and responses to high workloads in complex tasks. The course also covers statistical techniques for analyzing dependent data, encompassing ANOVA, ANCOVA, RM-ANOVA, and mixed-effect models.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand/Define the basics of human capabilities, the principles of information processing, and human behavior, as well as performance assessment
- Measure and interpret cognitive states such as situation awareness and mental workload.
- Learn to apply statistical analysis to understand human behavior and performance
- Design human factors studies, including longitudinal research, to assess human performance over both short-term and long-term periods
- Learn to use wearable technologies and immersive environments for behavior assessments.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Student performance will be evaluated based on the in-class activities, exams, homework, articles, projects, and individual and group activities. These assessments will be used to provide feedback on students' performance and to improve them.

### Other Course Policies:

#### **Tests:**

- The students will have **two exams** throughout the semester.
- The second exam covers all concepts throughout the semester.

**Homework Assignments:**

- Throughout the semester exploratory assessment activities will be assigned to reinforce or expand on material that is covered in the lecture.
- **Ten (tentative) homework** sets will be assigned, and these are to be worked on outside of class; The lowest-grade homework assignment will be dropped.
- Students may collaborate on the assignments, meaning students can discuss the assignments, but every student must complete and turn in his/her written solutions in his/her own words (unless otherwise stated in the assignment instructions).
- Students must illustrate their understanding of the material, which means that copying solutions from previous semesters or book solutions verbatim is not allowed and is considered a form of academic dishonesty.
- ***Homework is meant to be a learning tool. If you are having difficulty, find help right away – don’t wait until you fall even further behind!***

**Participation points:**

- Participation will be accumulated throughout the lectures through slido and other in-class activities.
- Videos and podcasts **may be available** prior to class when there is an in-class activity.
- In-class assignments and slido participation weights will vary depending on the effort required of students.

- Students produce a 3-5 page final report.

**Exam Protocol:**

- All exams are open book and notes, the use of calculator is allowed and computers may be allowed.
- All answers must be recorded on the exam answer sheet and full credit is awarded for correct numerical answers as well as a direct answer to the problem presented in the question.
- Little to no credit is awarded for the effort that yields incorrect numerical values.
- The professional world measures performance on correct results, not effort working on the problem or data.

**Exam Regrading Request:**

- Exams submitted for regrading must not be altered from the original work submitted for grading. Altering work for regrading is academic dishonesty and will be treated as such. All requests must be made in writing by attaching a well phrased note to the original work explaining in full the reason for the request and the points you think you deserve for your error. Requests for a regrade must be submitted no later than next class meeting after the material is handed back to the class for the request to be considered.

**Special Testing Accommodations:**

- All students with testing accommodations will be by the instructor and teaching assistant for all tests. Make sure to email your accommodation letter in the beginning of the semester in order to be added to the list.

**End-of-semester grades are not given**.

- Instead, end-of-semester grades are calculated according to a student’s performance on the individual assessment requirements and the weights in this syllabus.
- **Final grades are, final.** Therefore, final grades will not be changed unless a numerical error is made in computing your final grade.
- After final grades have been calculated, I will not respond to emails received requesting a grade change unless it is in response to a numerical calculation error. Please do not ask for extra credit or extra work after final exams have been administered.
