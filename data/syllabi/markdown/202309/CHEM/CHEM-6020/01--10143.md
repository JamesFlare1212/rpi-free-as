## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This advanced graduate level course will focus on the structure, bonding, and reactivity of transition metal coordination compounds. The course will cover group theory, ligand field theory, substitution reactions, photochemistry, catalysis, and redox reactions. Examples from the literature will be included to highlight the current research trends and applications of coordination compounds.  
**Prerequisites/Corequisites:** A working knowledge of inorganic, organic, and physical chemistry is required.  
**When Offered:** Fall odd years

*Tentative* Course Outline:

Intro to Transition Metals and Coordination Compounds

- Periodic trends in TM properties
- Nomenclature of coordination compounds; structures and symmetries of metal complexes, coordination numbers, types of ligands, geometric and optical isomerism
- Structure and Isomers (M&T Chapt. 9)

Symmetry and Group Theory (M&T Chapt. 4)

- Symmetry elements and operations, Point Groups, properties and representations of groups, examples and applications of symmetry to predict chirality and IR/Raman spectra.

Molecular Orbitals (M&T Chapt. 5)

- Symmetry properties of atomic orbitals, LCAO approach to construction of MOs, MO energy level diagrams for large molecules; the group theoretical approach to bonding, SALCs or group orbitals, selected triatomic molecs.

d-Metal Complexes (Coordination Compounds):

- Bonding theories for TM Coordination Compounds; formation constants, the chelate effect, crystal field theory - LFSE, magnetic properties, high/low spin configurations, π bonding effects; square-planar coordination; Jahn-Teller effect; MO diagram for octahedral metal complexes, with and without π-bonding, π-donor and acceptor ligands.
- Bonding (M&T Chapts. 10)
- Electronic Spectra (M&T Chapts. 11)

d-Metal Complexes - Reactions and Mechanisms (M&T Chapts. 12)

- Rates and mechanisms of ligand substn. reacts. - concepts of inertness/lability (kinetic) and (thermodynamic)"stability", correlation of inertness with high LFSE; associative vs. dissociative substitutions; Ligand Substn. Reactns, general terms and concepts; Redox reactions - inner- vs. outer-sphere processes.

Selected Inorganic Research Topics – research articles will be provided

Literature Review Presentations

- Details about literature review presentations and summary write-up will be given out later in the semester.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\) Demonstrate knowledge of how the bonding, symmetry, and structure of transition metal coordination compounds influence their reactivity, stability, redox, and photophysical properties.
- 2\) Demonstrate awareness of current research trends in transition metal coordination compounds, with emphasis a towards electrocatalysis, photochemistry, solar energy conversion, and artificial photosynthesis.
- 3\) Improvement of oral communication skills and literature searches through delivery of a presentation on an assigned research topic related to coordination compounds.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Student assessment for the course material will be determined through several midterm exams and an in-class presentation on a selected research article relating to the course material.
