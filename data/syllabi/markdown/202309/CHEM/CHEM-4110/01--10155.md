## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will introduce advanced instrumental physicochemical methods of chemical analysis as well as instrument design and data capture/processing. Topics covered include atomic and molecular spectroscopy, chromatography, electroanalytical chemistry and measurement basics.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course presents advanced concepts in Instrumental Analysis and Analytical Chemistry and is designed to familiarize undergraduate students with fundamental concepts surrounding analytical instrumentation, which reaches into many areas of the physical sciences. Throughout the semester, the instructor will present historical perspectives and scientific discussion as to *how* and *why* many of the modern analytical methodologies were developed. Students will gain an appreciation for the design and construction of analytical equipment which lead to detection and quantification of analytes, interaction of electromagnetic radiation with chemical species, as well as collection and processing of analytical signals.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate a proficient knowledge of modern analytical instrumentation, its uses and operating principles.
- Distinguish between different measures of performance in analytical measurements such as sensitivity, detectability, accuracy, and precision.
- Explain the principles and underlying processes of analytical techniques and extend this knowledge to explain intrinsic differences in analytical capabilities of the various instrumental methods.
- Effectively handle, process, and interpret large datasets produced by analytical instruments.

**Homework/In-class quizzes:** A combination of ten (10) homework assignments and in-class quizzes will be given throughout the semester, with one assignment covering approximately two lecture periods. Homework assignments will consist of problems from various textbooks and will be posted on the LMS (Blackboard Learn) about one week prior to the due date. **Note**: these homework assignments will NOT be thoroughly graded, but checked for completeness. They are designed to help students prepare for quizzes and exams. Homework assignments should be **hand-written** and submitted to the LMS prior to the due date and time. On certain weeks, unannounced in-class quizzes will be given in lieu of handing in homework assignments. These quizzes could take place at either the beginning or end of lecture. Quiz questions will come from the homework as well as the lecture materials. The instructor reserves the right to alter this homework and quiz policy during the course of the semester. Late homework assignments and make-up quizzes will NOT be allowed without a legitimate excuse.

**Data Collection and Analysis Project (D-CAP):** A topic of emerging importance in chemical and instrumental analysis is the handling of multiple, large datasets. Specifically, there are three important aspects to working with such data:

1. Data collection
2. Processing
3. Interpretation/presentation of findings

In data collection, it is important to ensure the recorded values are accurate and repeatable. Processing of data is important to minimize contributions from noise sources and enhance signal-to-noise. Proper interpretation and presentation of the important findings from large data in a clear, concise manner is essential to disseminate scientific discovery.

To practice these skillsets, groups of 2 students will be expected to devise and carry-out a project that explores and utilizes ***at least two of the three aspects listed above***. Each project will culminate in a presentation (poster or lecture) to the class. In either case, students should clearly present the basis of the project, data analysis approaches used, advantages/disadvantages over other methods, and findings from the project data. Groups and preliminary topic proposals will be due by ***Friday, September 15*** (3rd week of class). Final DCAP proposals will need to be submitted for approval by ***October 3*** (6th week of class). A detailed project status report (including figures and references) will be due ***Friday, November 10*** (11th week of class). The presentations will be due by ***Sunday, Dec. 3***, but can be submitted earlier.

Students are encouraged to base their D-CAP on their own undergraduate research, internship experiences, and/or personal interests. A bigger list of example topics and more specific guidance on the project will provided later in the semester. Some possible project topics include:

- Building a spectrophotometer from the ground up
- Comparison of data smoothing approaches
- Methods for enhanced data visualization
- Multivariate statistical approaches for data grouping
