## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course will provide practical illustrations of some aspects of Inorganic and Physical Chemistry and is related to the theory courses in these areas. Experiments involve individual and partnership exercises; specifics about the experiments assigned are given in a separate manual, but you will find that proper preparation and good planning for managing your time in laboratory are essential. This course also is communications intensive, meaning that a significant portion involves both written reports, which will be a major part of some experiments, and oral presentations. Experimental results in science are of little value if they are not communicated effectively, and skills in doing this are essential for a successful professional career.

The experiments to be done in this course involve some syntheses of inorganic compounds, some instrumental techniques, and some quantitative measurements. Quantitative measurements may be analytical or of physical properties, but their objective is an accurate numerical value. Proper quantitative techniques are essential and must be developed; the measurements made in most synthetic procedures are not accurate enough for real quantitative work. They are qualitative. In quantitative work, weighings must be made on analytical balances to four decimal places; volumes are by volumetric flasks or pipets. Graduated cylinders are only approximate, while the scales on beakers and Erlenmeyer flasks are just rough indicators. Review the techniques you learned in Experimental Chemistry I; read the section “Some Notes on Experimental Procedures” in this manual.

The time scheduled for each experiment allows for completion of the experimental work but not most of the calculations, write-up, or preparation, which must be done on your own time. Because of equipment limitations, it is important to follow the schedule (given separately). Check with the TA’s if you get off-schedule. Students are expected to be present for all scheduled experiments.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. to provide exposure to the practical aspects of some areas of inorganic chemistry 2. to familiarize you with some of the instrumentation and techniques used in the characterization of inorganic compounds 3. to illustrate some aspects of physical chemistry 4. to provide practice in communication in the style of a chemistry professional 5. to provide experience in laboratory team work, time management and organization 6. to provide experience in finding information in the chemical literature

**COVID-19 Related Policies:**

**Masks**: Masks will be worn by all students and faculty in all classrooms/labs, as well as in Institute buildings. Students who violate this policy by not wearing masks will be reported to the Dean of Students and will be requested to leave a classroom and return to their living quarters. Faculty and students will move in and out of classrooms as per the appropriate instructions of the faculty/administration. **In Lab Protocols:** Students are required to wear chemical splash goggles, mask and gloves throughout the lab period. Social distancing rules will be in enforced. Students are expected to clean areas where they will work and all surfaces they were in contact with at the end of the lab period.

**Cleaning of Spaces**: Students are encouraged to clean the surfaces of the chairs/tables/desks they occupy before they sit down and as they prepare to leave. Faculty should advise students to clean personal items with wipes or cloths before and after class.

**Student Health**: Students who are ill, under quarantine for COVID-19, or suspect they are ill will report that to Student Life. Student Life will verify and notify all faculty who have that student. Once notification is made, all faculty will make every reasonable effort to accommodate the student’s absence and will communicate that accommodation directly to the student. Failure to make an appropriate accommodation for a verified or reasonably suspected case of illness may be appealable under the student grade appeal process. Students who need to report an illness should contact the Student Health Center via email or call 518-276-6287.

**Refusal**: Refusal to comply with any appropriate request will be treated as would any classroom disruption and disciplinary actions, and sanctions will be taken through the judicial process outlined in the Student Handbook (request to change the behavior; request to leave the class; dismissal of the class and referral to Student Life and the Dean of Students.).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:**

The relationship between students and faculty is based upon trust and the continued maintenance of this trust is necessary for education to be successful. Students need to trust faculty to make appropriate judgments about the content and structure of the course. Faculty members need to trust that the work turned in by students represents their own effort. Violation of this trust undermines the educational process. In addition, as professionals, you may be designing products upon which lives depend, as a result, there is no tolerance for breach of academic integrity such as cheating, plagiarizing, or inappropriate sharing of laboratories, data or related materials (past or present).

**Students found to use any form of Academic Dishonesty will receive an F in the course.**

Chat-GPT or similar AI software are prohibited. Any use of this AI software will be an automatic failure. Detection software will be employed if work submitted is questionable.
