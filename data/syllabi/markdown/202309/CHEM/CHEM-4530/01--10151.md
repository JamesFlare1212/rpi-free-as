## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

*Modern Techniques in Chemistry* is an Industry-accented lecture-&-lab course geared especially for Chemical Engineering undergraduates at RPI. An ability to confidently wield core chemical knowledge in practical contexts (esp. during Proj.Mngmnt. mtgs.) holds a CHME graduate in good stead with their team, managers, supervisors, employers, suppliers, insurers, legal (incl. patents), & regulators (esp. Enviro. & Safety). The course (*colloq.* “ModTech”) highlights selected topics in deliberately ‘multifaceted’ situations among the following formal areas of Chemistry: Organic, Inorganic, Physical, & Analytical. Its main purpose is for CHME students to strengthen their confidence to use chemical principles (exemplified during some representative lab exercises-&-demonstrations), together with associated lecture material & interrogatively-challenging class-discussions, centering around ‘on-the-job’ competency, ethical leadership responsibilities, & particularly problem-solving skills based on analytical methods & data (esp. supporting/refuting posited explanations/solutions). The contents of the lectures & lab activities reflect typical applications where chemically-informed & chemically ‘intuitive’ thinking is useful in industrial settings, including: synthesis; ‘*work-up*’ procedures; analysis; side-products; interferences; catalysis; separations; extractions; surface chemistry; mesophases (LCs); materials characterization; crystallization; isotropy/anisotropy; ‘batch recovery’; corrosion protection; effluent/exhaust treatments; jobsite/plant trouble-shooting; safety/PPE selection (esp. when supervising juniors/interns/workers); & when discussing a particular sample’s specific analytical requirements with career chemists (*e.g.,* at commercial analytical labs).

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. Gain practical skills in the ‘*hands-on*’ apprentice-like setting-up of varied chemical experiments & apparatus (incl. best-practices for lab safety, & anticipating/recognizing/minimizing potential sources of experimental errors).
2. Able to write-up & present a concise lab-based report in a defensible manner, using correct chemical terminology (esp. chemically-descriptive explanations).
3. Responsibly estimate experimental observational errors, in support of the meaningful ‘downstream’ interpretation of chemical results/data, & the competent wielding of core chemical knowledge/arguments.
4. Interpreting data generated by some typical pieces of analytical instrumentation, along with a working awareness of some of the everyday lab realities/challenges & necessary considerations for obtaining useful & defensible analytical results/data (esp. inherent limitations).
5. Grown confidence in overall chemical competency to ‘*speak up*’ with suggestions/ideas/concerns (esp. during project-management meetings); employing chemical arguments rationally & defensibly to discuss a project’s chemical processes, analytical requirements, & troubleshooting/batch-recovery.
6. Able to ‘winnow’ among online search-results for reliable technical information (esp. chemical systems/reactions/interferences/methods/&c.) by awareness of appropriate & accurate chemical terminology & customary usage (confidently ‘narrowing things down’ being a key modern skill).
7. Working in collaborative lab-partnerships to accomplish practical objectives in allotted lab timeframes.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Lectures & Recitation:** Tuesday & Friday will be 50-minute lectures; recitation will be on Wednesday. The material presented in lecture will expand upon material in weekly readings, the eight core experiments, & various in-lab demonstrations. Excused-absences from lecture should be cleared with the course Instructor. Each student is permitted two (2) unexcused lecture absences; more than two unexcused lecture absences will result in deductions of points from your participation grade at the Instructor’s discretion.

**Exams:** There will be three (3) equally-weighted take-home exams (each being 10% of the Final Grade), which will (like the ‘*real world*’ of problem-solving that awaits you out in Industry) freely draw upon any of the material covered in lecture, in readings, met in exemplar problems on-the-board, or anything covered/discussed in-lab.

**Attendance in Lab is Mandatory.** Chemistry is fundamentally an experimental science which incorporates specialized apparatus (esp. fragile glassware), nuanced sample-handling protocols, as well as both observational & measurement-making skills. Two lab periods per week (M/R for Secn_2; T/F for Secn_1) are for the gaining of practical lab experience & instruction from accomplished practitioners. Experimentally-recorded data is not to be unduly shared (esp. copied); the data that you record from your own experimental set-up is the unique set of data that you’ll then work with in order to generate your final lab report, along with all of your unique observations & the chemical ‘history’ of your experiment (*e.g.,* excursions of temperature, *p*H, aerial oxidative ‘aging,’ drying-out, illumination, etc.). A laboratory notebook must be kept (pen/ink is permissible, but graylead pencil is typically used in-the-field), pre-labs are due on the first day of each new lab (*i.e.,* prior to commencing lab-work), and the summarizing final lab report is due **one week after** the experimental work’s been completed (unless advised otherwise by the Instructor/TA). Your physical attendance in lab is mandatory in order for you to be able to pass this course; any excused-absences from lab must be documented (*e.g.,* a doctor’s note, a sport-coach’s note, etc.) & cleared through the course Instructor in a timely manner.

**Late Reports.** Lab reports are due **one week following** the completion of the experimental work. If a report is turned in late, one (1) point will be deducted for each day that the report is late. After one week’s tardiness, the report will then not be accepted and a zero score will result for that work.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

# Academic Integrity:

The instructional relationship between students & faculty/TAs is based on mutually-respectful trust, upon which genuine education is dependent to be successful. Students trust faculty to make appropriate judgments about the content & structure of the course; Faculty & TAs trust that the work turned-in by each student represents that person’s own honest effort. A violation of this essential trust undermines the educational process.

Breaches of academic integrity include cheating, plagiarism, or the inappropriate sharing of lab write-ups, data, or related materials, either from past cohorts or from current students.

**The penalties of a breach in academic integrity for this class are the following:** 1st-offense: a zero for the work in question, (*i.e.,* report, exam, etc.). 2nd-offense: a zero for the work & a drop in the course letter grade by one whole level (*e.g.,* an A would thuswise become a B). 3rd-offense: a failing grade for the class, without any opportunity to drop the course (thereby being unable to avoid the fail from appearing on the official academic transcript).

The Rensselaer Student Handbook & the Dean of Students Office webpage (http://doso.rpi.edu/update.do?artcenterkey=676) defines various forms of academic dishonesty. Please familiarize yourself with these serious matters, and ask for clarification if you have any questions/concerns about what constitutes academic dishonesty.
