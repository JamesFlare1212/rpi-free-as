## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a hands-on laboratory course for Biomedical Engineering juniors and seniors. It gives the student practical experience with a wide variety of different experimental aspects within the biomedical engineering field. Students should have previously taken a course in human physiology, an engineering laboratory course, and a course in the modeling of dynamic systems. This course provides part of the background for the Biomedical Engineering Design course, which follows it in the curriculum for undergraduate students.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Student Outcomes/Course Educational Objectives**

1. Students who successfully complete the course will be able to demonstrate the operating principles and describe the characteristics of a variety of diagnostic and therapeutic instruments.
2. Students will be able to identify, and subsequently avoid (or minimize) causes for inaccuracy in these instruments.
3. Students will be able to properly display, analyze, and communicate experimental methods, results, and measurement errors through brief, thorough, and well-organized laboratory reports.
4. Students will be able to recognize and avoid conditions that may injure or damage an instrument, the system to which the instrument is applied, or the operator.
5. Students will be able to comprehensively document their activities in a legible manner.
6. Students will be able to identify and utilize resources from outside the laboratory where they can obtain further information regarding laboratory safety, regulations, and standard operating procedures.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Course content/structure**: The course meets on Tuesdays and Fridays for a two-hour interactive lecture, and on Tuesdays/Wednesdays for a three-hour lab period. Initial lectures will introduce the laboratory experiments. There will be one exam following the introductory material. Later lectures will introduce concepts of practical experimental design.

At the beginning of the semester, teams will be formed with 2-3 students to conduct experiments in the laboratory. Students will choose one (out of three) primary track and will be grouped as per their track choice. Laboratory experiments will be offered in 3 tracks. All students will first complete two “common labs”. Common labs will introduce the students to additional concepts or instruments outside of their track. Track related experiments will follow common labs as per each section’s designated calendar found on LMS. Within each track, there will be four experiments, each of which builds on the knowledge gained from previous experiments. The first lab in each track will introduce general laboratory techniques. For the second lab, students will utilize the previously learned techniques to conduct another basic experiment. The third lab (spanning two weeks) will build on all the previous experiments allowing the student to accomplish a more comprehensive experiment. The fourth lab will be designed by the student team. Students must complete two common labs and all four labs within their primary track to receive any grade for this course.

Prior to the lab period, each student must individually prepare for each laboratory by reading the Laboratory Procedure and supplemental notes. Each Laboratory Procedure will be provided to the students via RPILMS. A 5-minute quiz will be administered at the start of the lab through LMS that will cover materials contained in the Laboratory Procedure and Supplementary Information. Students must report to the lab on time to start the lab. Students reporting more than 15 minutes past their lab time will be counted as absent.

At least one student per group must bring their laptop computer to each lab for data collection and display. Every student must bring a bound laboratory notebook to each lab, and a thumb drive to use for data retrieval. Raw data and notes recorded therein will be the basis for the laboratory report. Students will have one week each to complete post lab turn-ins/reports. Students will have five weeks to design, conduct, and complete lab 4 in their primary track.

Laboratory experiments will be conducted as groups and laboratory turn-ins/reports will be completed and submitted as a group. Every student in a lab group must participate in the laboratory procedure and contribute to report preparation. Two times during the semester, students will have the opportunity to evaluate the performance of their fellow teammates. The instructors will provide additional performance evaluation.

If a student misses a lab for a legitimate reason, such as documented illness [documented and verified by the Dean of Students], the student may make up that experiment at a later time. The student will be responsible for conducting the experiment and preparing a laboratory turn-in/report independently. No more than one laboratory exercise may be made up by a single student. If a student misses a lab without a legitimate reason, the lab may not be made up and therefore will be awarded a zero for all its related submissions.

All submissions for the labs will be electronic. Soft copies of lab assignments are due at the *start* of the lab meeting for each section. For example, labs performed on Wednesday afternoon are due at the start of the Wednesday afternoon lab session. Microsoft Word versions of Turn-ins/Reports/Prelabs should be emailed to the instructor **at the start of the lab** to ONLY: **bmed.4010@gmail.com.** Do not submit to course instructors’ emails!!

**Items handed in late will be given zero credit.** Verify if your email has been sent to ensure submission. Late submissions will be awarded ZERO credit. Exceptions are made on case-by case basis at the discretion of the instructor(s). Copy all your team members on all team submissions and all relevant emails to BME team.

In the case of requesting regrades, the student(s) have a one-week period from the time an item is handed back to request a regrade. Attached to the original item, the student(s) must attach a sheet with which item they would like regraded, and why they feel additional credit is due. Note that the entire assignment will be re-checked: where *we* owe *you* points we will give them, but if *you* owe *us* points, we will take them back.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Development of interpersonal and communications skills is critical to all students’ future success. Many of the activities associated with this course are team-based to facilitate this development. However, students must work independently on course assignments designated as “individual”, which include prelab assignments, quizzes, and the midterm exam. Reports prepared by student teams must be original work and not copied, or written in collaboration with other groups or based on other reports.

The Biomedical Engineering Department does not tolerate any act of academic dishonesty. “As a student in BMED 4010, the departmental code of ethics outlined below will be followed.”

“As a member of the Rensselaer Biomedical Engineering Community, I will uphold the ethical conduct befitting a Biomedical Engineer. I will not use knowledge gained from my experiences at Rensselaer for any purpose, which may harm society. My actions will reflect positively on the department, university, and profession. I will respect the faculty, staff, and my peers by not misrepresenting myself through acts of deception. I will not participate in any acts of lying, cheating, or stealing in accordance with the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities. I will conduct research with integrity. I will take pride in my work and continue to promote these principles throughout my tenure at Rensselaer and my career as a Biomedical Engineer.”

**In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work.** In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Penalties for academic dishonesty in this class can range from a grade of zero, “0”, on the assignment of question, to **failure** of the class as a whole. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty that is deemed by the instructor to be appropriate to the infraction. Note that any academic dishonesty will be dealt severely and will be reported to the dean of students. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
