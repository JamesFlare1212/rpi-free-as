### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Course Description: There are several aspects of graduate education that require enrichment and discussion. The purpose of *Discussions in Graduate Research* is to present different aspects for the purpose of fostering graduate student development in different areas of research. These include:

1. Discussion and development of fellowship/candidacy (grant) applications.
2. Discussion and development of a research review manuscript.
3. Discussion and development of a research manuscript.
4. Discussion and development of poster/platform presentations.
5. Discussion of meeting graduation requirements in a timely manner.
6. Discussion of visiting faculty seminars/faculty candidate seminars.
7. Other topics – including but not limited to intellectual property in research, next steps after the Ph.D. (job search), optimizing your research, etc.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grading Criteria: The course is Pass/Not Pass. To receive a pass grade for each semester, please see the appropriate guidelines below:

1. Participation in the activities related to your year/status in the Ph.D. program, as described in the calendar, is required. You are welcomed to attend other sessions.
2. Documentation: submit documents such as advancement, candidacy, and thesis in time, and keep documents (such as the plan of study) updated.
3. Participation/Attendance at departmental seminars (only one unexcused absence allowed per semester) as well as the annual graduate research symposium.
4. Demonstration of progress towards a written scientific paper, submission of a fellowship application, and/or submission of a thesis.

Documentation requirements: year 1 (submit a plan of study); year 2 (advancement), year 3+ (candidacy); for all students, the plan of study should keep updated. Waivers are required for not being able to submit the document for your year. Please refer to the BME graduate student handbook.

Annual Graduate Research Symposium: Typically, year 2 students are required to present a poster, and year 3-5 students are considered for a podium or poster presentation. No requirements for year 1 students.

Fellowship application: Year 1-2 students are required to submit a fellowship application (such as NSF GRFP) in the Fall semester. If unable to do so, as verified by the instructor, the student will complete a literature review. All year 1 student are encouraged to conduct and write a systematic literature review of their field of research. The efforts of seeking fellowship opportunities (such as NIH F31) may continue throughout the PhD study.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Potential Places Where Academic Dishonesty May Take Place In This Course:

1. Signing an attendance sheet for a friend who is missing seminar or discussions in research class.
2. Lying to a professor concerning reasons for missing an exam/assignment.

Since the course requires written work, any instance of plagiarism or copying other’s work word for word without proper citation is academic dishonesty
