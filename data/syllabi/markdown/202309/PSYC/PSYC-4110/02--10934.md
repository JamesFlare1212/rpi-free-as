## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course is intended to make students informed consumers of scientific information and and research methods relevant to human motivation. While there is no one generally accepted comprehensive theory of motivation, there are many findings, concepts and models of behavior that can be very effective for understanding and predicting behavior when used appropriately. Research methods and strategies are available that can help develop useful information for the prediction and understanding of human behavior when reasonable answers are not available.

The course is focussed on providing practice using tools for developing an understanding of specific issues and phenomena. Developing ‘soft skills ‘ related to understanding human motivation is one key to long term career and life success

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

In order to obtain a C in the course

Lecture comment Each individual must submit one paragraph per lecture topic that describes a component of the lecture, a theoretical point, a model of behavior, or an empirical finding, explains it and indicates in a preliminary way how this component can be applied in daily life. In the event that you miss class, submit either an additional paragraph based on a people watching paragraph. Each paragraph should describe what is interesting, why it is interesting, and how it can be useful or insight producing.

Scavenger hunt. Each week submit an interesting a youtube clip, a website, a reference to an article THAT ILLUSTRATES OR EXTENDS THE CURRENT TOPIC

These submissions will be evaluated as acceptable or, if unacceptable the student will have the opportunity to revise and resubmit

In order to obtain a B in the course

submit a report of any in class activities (this is a group presentation with individual contributions identified) 2-3 pages

In addition to the requirements for a C, provide well organized and written summaries of in class group projects and activities. Each student writes a brief summary of their contribution to the group discussion

to obtain an A

Cool projects. In order to obtain an A, In addition to the requirements for a B

each person cooperating with their group will produce a cool project of substantial scope and imagination. The basic topic must be approved before Spring break, including a work schedule with biweekly benchmarks for the submission of parts of the project and an electronic version of the final product must be available to the instructor at least two weeks before the end of the semester. Every time a deadline is missed the amount of work required will be increased.

### Attendance Policy:

When students attend class they are expected to be psychologically present, attentive, and respectful of others. While students are responsible for making the most effective use of their time, each group will have at least one person attending each class.

A major goal of the course is to provide opportunities for interactive learning and active participation. Students can only participate when present and are expected to attend class. Failure to attend class is no excuse or not participating in class based activities. When an individual does not attend class they are responsible for contacting fellow group members and getting caught up on course work. Group members are responsible for supporting each other and keeping each other up to speed.
