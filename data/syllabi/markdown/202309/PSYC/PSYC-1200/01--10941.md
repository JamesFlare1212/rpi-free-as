## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will demonstrate their knowledge of basic psychological concepts, principles, and procedures.
- Students will demonstrate their understanding of the strengths, limitations and appropriate applications of commonly employed behavioral/social science research designs and methodologies.
- Students can gain first-hand experience with psychological research by serving as participants in one or more IRB-approved research projects being conducted by the faculty and graduate students affiliated with Rensselaer’s Cognitive Science Department.

**Homework FAQS**

**Can I get an extension on the homework?**

No extensions on homework are given. The only exceptions to this are: you join the class late or I receive official documentation about an excused absence. See details below about what to do in the case of an absence.

**Can I get partial credit on the homework?**

No partial credit is given. Homework is graded incomplete or complete; only homework fully completed and turned in on time is graded complete (1 point each).

**Exams:** There are 4 exams this semester; there is no final exam. Each exam is graded on a 0-100% scale. Exams will be completed on LMS on the dates indicated in the schedule. Exams are assigned and open for 48 hours to allow some flexibility. Students will have 2 hours to finish each exam (75 multiple choice questions). Once started, students must complete the exams (no stopping and-re-starting). During the exam, students may use the course materials, but may not consult/collaborate with another person—doing so will result in the grade of “0”. Exams will be given through Achieve.

**Exam FAQS**

**What if I don’t take the exam? What if something comes up and I am not able to take the exam?**

Exams will remain available on LMS for 24 hours after their due date, but exams submitted after the due date (late) without an official excuse will receive a 10% grade deduction penalty. Exams can only be taken late in this timeframe (except for extenuating circumstances with official documentation provided to me).

**Can I do a make-up exam?** There are no make-up exams. The only exception to this is when I receive official documentation about an excused absence. See details below about what to do in the case of an absence.

**Attendance and excused absences:** Class attendance is strongly encouraged. However, if you are not feeling well, please do not come to class (join on WebEx as you are able) and seek out medical care as necessary. You will need to let me know *in advance* and provide documentation of a planned excused absence (e.g. memo from your coach, or contact success@rpi.edu for other excused absences) to get extensions on homework or exams. If you have an excused absence for 1 class, you will receive 2 days to complete the work you missed. If you have an excused absence longer than 1 class, you will receive a week after you return to complete the work. If you have been absent because of joining the class late in the semester, you will get a one week extension on the homework you missed.

**Optional extra credit:** Students can earn extra credit for participation in Cognitive Science Department research (see folder on LMS about SONA) or completing activities (e.g. in Achieve or those provided by the professor) at the rate of 0.4 percentage points per hour of documented research participation or activities *up to a limit of 6 hours (2.4 percentage points)*, which will be added directly to the final average. *Extra credit must be completed by Monday April 24 11:59pm.*

***Incomplete Grades*** In the case of an extended excused absence from class, and inability to complete coursework by the end of the semester, an Incomplete may be given. The student needs to contact me to work out the logistics and deadlines for the Incomplete before the last day of class. This is worked out on a case-by-case basis with me.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

***Professor Walf’s I.D.E.A. Statement*** In this classroom, we will acknowledge and aim to respect **i**nclusivity, **d**iversity, **e**quity, and **a**wareness. In our classroom, all students are welcome and will be supported regardless of race/ethnicity, gender identities, gender expressions, sexual orientation, socio-economic status, age, disabilities, religion, regional background, neurodiversity, citizenship status, nationality and the many other diverse identities that we each bring here. Indeed, the diversity that the students bring to this classroom should be viewed as a strength, resource, and benefit.

We will be focusing on the material primarily from a psychological science perspective. We must be aware that science is not always objective and unbiased and historically has not included representation of diverse individuals in the experiments or the researchers themselves. For example, individual differences between human subjects in experiments were often minimized or considered confounds because of the added complexity they may have contributed to the analysis of the data. However, in this classroom, we will strive to integrate a diverse set of experiences for a more comprehensive understanding of the material in this course, despite the potential for added complexity. An important part of a college education is learning about the complexity of various issues. We will also expand our understanding by hearing from diverse voices in the field through our readings, media, and discussions of the material. Moreover, students in this class are encouraged to participate during class meetings and I will make all efforts for students to have an equitable opportunity to contribute and succeed in the supportive learning environment. Our classroom will represent a diversity of individual beliefs, backgrounds, and experiences. We all must listen and show care and respect for every other member of this class even if we do not necessarily agree. Please honor the uniqueness of our classroom community and appreciate the opportunity we have to learn from one another. If you have any questions or concerns, please discuss with me.

**General note on content:** In Psychology, we are often focused on the human experience ─ which can be at times difficult, messy, and even painful just as much as it can be beautiful and joyful.
