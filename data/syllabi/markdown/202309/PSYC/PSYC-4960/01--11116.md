## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course will provide students with a strong foundation in the basic principles and methods of Bayesian statistics and probabilistic modeling. Further, the course will introduce students to state-of-the-art computational methods for Bayesian inference and enable them to apply these methods to challenging real-world problems.

The course will commence with a self-contained introduction to probability theory and the basic logic of Bayesian estimation, model comparison, and decision theory. It will then cover the key differences between Bayesian and non-Bayesian methods, with a focus on the merits of uncertainty quantification and propagation. Participants will learn how to reason about sources of uncertainty and how to interpret the results of probabilistic inference. As the course progresses, we will dive into more advanced topics, such as the mechanics of Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods, hierarchical models, Bayesian meta-analysis, and simulation-based methods.

The course will also include multiple hands-on sessions using the popular R programming language to facilitate practical exposure to the theoretical ideas. In particular, we will cover all steps of principled Bayesian workflows, including checks for prior consistency, computational faithfulness, model sensitivity, and model validation. In the final part, we will discuss a selected set of topics at the frontier of Bayesian research, such as approximate Bayesian computation (ABC), variational inference (VI), and generative neural networks. By the end of the course, students will have a solid understanding of Bayesian principles and methods; they will be able to apply these methods to challenging real-world problems.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Note: The following list and order of topics is tentative and will almost certainly evolve during the couse.

Week 1: Course introduction. Who is who, a conceptual and lightweight introduction to the history and interpretations of probability.

Week 2: Essential probability theory. Basic rules, expectations, graphical models, Monte Carlo estimation, a glimpse into measure theory; first steps with Bayes’ rule.

Week 3: The nuts and bolts of Bayesian parameter estimation. Analytic Bayes, Bayesian updating, conjugate priors.

Week 4: The zoo of Bayesian regressions. Linear models, generalized linear models, prior sensitivity, regularization, brms lab - predicting the Challenger disaster.

Week 5: Markov chain Monte Carlo - A deep dive. The unreasonable effectiveness of stateful sampling. Metropolis-Hastings, Gibbs sampling, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo; typical sets, effective sample size, convergence diagnostics; introduction to Stan and NUTS.

Week 6: The Stan labs. Hands-on applications with Stan; Hackathon: predicting titanic survival rate.

Week 7: Bayesian model comparison I. Prior predictive distributions, the curse of marginalization, Bayes factors, posterior model probabilities; predictive distribution.

Week 8: Bayesian model comparison II: The great debate. Cross-validation and Bayes factors. Should we still choose prediction over explanation?

Week 9: Towards a principled Bayesian workflow I. Domain expertise consistency, prior predictive and prior-pushforward checks, computational faithfulness.

Week 10: Towards a principled Bayesian workflow II. Model sensitivity, generative performance and posterior predictive checks.

Week 11: Hierarchical models I. Understanding shrinkage, normal models, Bayesian meta-analysis, model comparison with hierarchical models.

Week 12: Hierarchical models II. Fitting various multilevel regression models with brms.

Week 13: Advanced topics I. Variational inference, information theory, divergences, inference-as-optimization.

Week 14: Advanced topics II. Approximate Bayesian computation (ABC), simulation-based inference and the future of Bayesian inference.

Final day of classes: Project presentations.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Critical and creative thinking about data analysis and probabilistic modeling in a trans- and interdisciplinary context.
- Ability to apply Bayesian methods, models, and visualization techniques to challenging inference problems using state-of-the-art (SOTA) statistical tools (R, Stan, brms).
- Demonstration of understanding of modern probabilistic modeling as it relates to data analysis in general and the cognitive sciences in particular.
- Ability to set up and perform principled Bayesian workflows, focusing on prior sensitivity, computational faithfulness, model adequacy, and predictive performance.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The final grade in the course will be based on the performance on the five (5) homework assignments and a final project including a presentation. The final project will involve carrying out a principled Bayesian workflow on a topic and data set of own choice and giving a short presentation of the analyses and results. Students will also have the opportunity to complete two (2) bonus writing assignments which can increase the final grade by 10%. The bonus assignments can be used, for example, to make up for a missed assignment or simply to systematize one’s understanding of a particular topic.The homework assignments intend to assess learning outcomes 1, 2, and 4; the bonus writing assignments will assess learning outcomes 1, 2, and 4. The final project and presentation will assess all learning outcomes.
