## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will introduce students to the genre of the literary short story. The primary objectives  
of this course are to write thoughtful and effective short fictions in this genre, engage  
meaningfully in an artistic community of peers, practice listening and nuanced thinking, and  
become more creatively fearless. Through reading and discussion, short writing exercises, craft  
analyses, and participation in a writing workshop, students will learn to recognize diverse  
elements of effective short story writing and be given plenty of opportunities to practice. Every  
student will leave the course with a toolkit of ideas for fiction and three drafted stories.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will demonstrate knowledge of fiction writing in general and the short story in particular by utilizing appropriate terminology to analyze and discuss published fiction.
- Students will demonstrate craft-based reading strategies through annotations, short analytical responses, and discussions.
- Students will establish a consistent creative practice by regularly producing short creative writings and craft analyses.
- Students will demonstrate engagement in the writing process through observing, drafting, peer critique, and revision.
- Students will actively engage in a creative community of peers through participation in workshop, discussion, and potentially, community literary events.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Reading. In this course, you will learn to read like a writer, which is somewhat different than the  
reading you may be used to in English classes. The expectation is that as you engage with these  
texts you are “looking behind the curtain” to determine how the artistic effects were achieved and  
preparing to discuss your theories with others.  
Short Exercises (SEs). These are short creative writing prompts designed to give you practice in  
one particular skill  
Craft Discussion Boards. The purpose of these posts is to help you organize your thoughts  
before class and for us to get discussions started  
Drafts: You will write at least three pieces of fiction this semester: a micro fiction (up to 300-500  
words), a work of flash fiction (500-1000 words), and a short story (1001- 2,500 words).  
Workshop Participation. Instruction in this course follows the guidelines established by the  
Association of Writers and Writing Programs (AWP) for the teaching of creative writing, which  
include a “challenging writers’ workshop” as a hallmark.  
Peer End Comments. You will write peer feedback letters for the flash fiction and short story  
assignments.  
Revision Portfolio: At the end of the term, you will reflect on course learning and include  
detailed revision plans for your flash fiction and your short story based on a thorough reading of  
your peer and instructor end comments. A revision draft is optional for those students wanting  
more feedback.  
Literary Citizenship: All students are encouraged to submit a work of fiction to RPI’s  
McKinney Writing Contest.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Everyone has two “freebie” absences to use for sleep, self-care, or other obligations without point  
penalty or need for an official excuse. Otherwise, I expect that you will come to class every class  
(school athletes see me at the beginning of the term), barring illness. Each subsequent unexcused  
absence beyond the first one will drop your grade by ten points (1%). If you miss more than five  
classes without administrative excusal, you will receive no credit for the course even if you are  
otherwise passing. Three tardies of half hour or more accrue to one absence. Note that graded inclass  
assignments may not be made up due to unexcused absence

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
