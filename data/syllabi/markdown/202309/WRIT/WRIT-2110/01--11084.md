## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Strategic writing is a course that aims to introduce students to a range of genres of writing common at the college level and beyond. We focus on the mechanics of writing as well as how writing fits into a larger situation or context, and is written for a purpose, with a specific audience in mind, by a writer who makes certain rhetorical choices in order to communicate more effectively with a particular audience. Additionally, this class serves as an introduction to writing and researching at the college level, and will teach students how to locate and assess peer-reviewed scholarly sources through the library and through internet searches as well how to incorporate the ideas of others into one’s own writing and how to cite sources in a way that makes it clear which ideas are the student’s own, and which are coming from outside sources. This is a writing-intensive, process-driven class, so students will submit papers in multiple drafts and receive comments from the instructor and from their peers at multiple stages throughout the process.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Learn to write focused, well-organized texts for a variety of academic audiences and rhetorical situations.
- Learn to view writing as a process, to receive constructive feedback from both instructors and peers, and to revise writing through multiple drafts.
- Learn to use the library databases to locate reputable, scholarly sources and to incorporate ideas from these sources into one’s own writing while providing appropriate documentation in the correct citation format.
- Learn to view writing within the context of an ongoing conversation, and learn to make worthwhile original contributions to the types of conversations that typically take place in the academy.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Writing Assignment 1: Literacy Narrative**

A literacy narrative tells the story of how the author acquired a particular literacy or skill. Traditionally, literacy narratives tend to be focused on reading and writing, but we can expand the definition to include skills and literacies of all kinds. An effective literacy narrative will employ many of the conventions of storytelling such as narrative, characterization, and dialogue in order to tell its story. In addition, good personal narrative essays have a strong sense of purpose, and it is clear why an audience would be interested in this narrative. A good place to start is with the skills and literacies involved in your major or career. How did you become interested in them, and what is the story of your learning process? WA 1 rough drafts should be 3.5-4 pages, double spaced, in 12 point Times New Roman, or a similarly sized font, and will be due on Mon 9/11. Final drafts should be at least 5 pages and will be due on Mon 9/18.

**Writing Assignment 2: Analysis of a Persuasive Text**

The student will select a persuasive text on a current issue or debate to analyze. Then, you will write an essay in which you evaluate whether or not the text is persuasive for its intended audience. Some things to think about are: 1) Identify the conversation that the text is joining, comparing and contrasting it to other viewpoints in the same debate. 2) Determine who the audience is and how you can tell. How does the author appeal to the audience’s assumptions or values? Does the author attempt to persuade the audience to adopt or support any viewpoint they would not normally consider? 3) Identify the rhetorical appeals and techniques that the author uses to reach their audience, such as ethos, pathos, logos, and kairos, as well as other rhetorical techniques. 4) Assesses whether or not the argument is effective in persuading its target audience, and provides reasons why the argument is or is not persuasive, and 5) Assesses the strengths and weaknesses of the arguments contained in the article, pointing out any counterarguments the author does not consider, or any ways the argument could be made more effective. Rough drafts should be 3.5-4 pages double spaced, in 12 point Times New Roman, or a similarly sized font, and will be due Mon 10/16. Final drafts should be at least 5-6 pages and will be due on Mon 10/23.

**Writing Assignment 3: Literature Review**

As preparation for their final research paper, students will be responsible for preparing a literature review that provides an overview of 8-10 current, relevant, and reputable sources about their topic. Literature reviews should include a bibliography providing citations for the sources the author will be using in their research paper, and should provide an overview of the major perspectives, themes, and trends on your topic based on the research you have conducted so far. Good literature reviews have a strong sense of purpose and aim to provide an overview of the range of perspectives within an ongoing conversation. Rough drafts of literature reviews should be 3.5-4 pages and will be due Thurs 11/9. Final drafts should be 5-6 pages and will be due on Thurs 11/16.

**Writing Assignment 4: Research Paper**

The semester will culminate with a research paper on a topic of the student’s choice, which will draw on 6-8 peer-reviewed, scholarly sources, and which will aim to make an original contribution to a relevant academic conversation. These papers should be focused narrowly on a specific topic, and should advance a specific thesis or argument about the topic rather than merely summarizing information about the topic. Rough drafts should be at least 4-5 pages, double spaced, in 12 point Times New Roman, or a similarly sized font, and will be due Mon 11/27. Final drafts should be 8-10 pages and will be due on the last day of class, Thurs 12/7.

**Homework and Participation**

This is a process-driven, writing intensive class, and so it is expected that students will attend class regularly and make insightful and relevant contributions to class discussions. For each class session, students are expected to have completed all assignments and assigned readings and be prepared to participate in the class discussion, which will be graded. Homework assignments, rough drafts, revision activities, and in-class group work will also count toward this part of your grade. In general, so long as the student meets the requirements of the assignment, homework assignments, in-class activities, and process work such as rough drafts are graded on a pass/fail basis. Please note that rough drafts are not graded on the same basis as final drafts, and that a rough draft grade should not be taken as indicative of your final draft grade, as rough drafts do not have to be complete and are typically given full credit so long as they meet the page requirement and are on topic for the assignment. However, you will be given written feedback on each draft based on the grading criteria for the assignment, which is intended to help you determine how to revise your draft, and I am available to look at additional drafts or to answer any questions you have about the assignment.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

***ATTENDANCE POLICY***

This is a small group class that places a high level of emphasis on writing as a process and on class discussion, so students are expected to attend class regularly and to actively participate in all class sessions. Student athletes should inform me at the beginning of the semester of any classes they will have to miss during the semester. Other reasons for an excused absence can be submitted through the Office for Student Success. In order to receive credit for class participation for in-class activities, you must either be present in class during that class session, or have a documented excused absence. Otherwise, you will receive no credit for in-class participation or activities for that day.

### Other Course Policies:

***LATE WORK POLICY***

Students are expected to submit work on time and to communicate with me in advance if you need an extension for any assignments. No extensions will be granted once the due date for the assignment has passed. Homework assignments and rough drafts will be due at the beginning of class on the day for which they assigned. After that, no late homework assignments will be accepted. Final drafts of major writing assignments will be due at the beginning of class on the day for which they are assigned. After that, they will be marked down a third of a letter grade for each day they are late (so a B would become a B-, an A- becomes a B+, and so on). If you need an extension for an assignment, contact me before the due date. If you need an extension on a more regular basis, accommodations can be worked out with the Office of Disabled Student Services or with the instructor.

***INCLUSION POLICY***

Our classroom and my office hours are spaces where all are welcome. Please note that discrimination or harassment in any form will not be tolerated in this learning community. Beyond simply avoiding discriminating against others, your presence in this class indicates that you agree to actively promote a welcoming and inclusive learning environment for all, including but not limited to taking the following actions: using inclusive language, such as referring to people by their preferred personal pronouns; understanding that it is your responsibility in class discussions to shape your comments for a diverse audience that may include native and non-native English speakers, people of various genders and sexual orientations, people from various cultural and religious backgrounds, and other identifiers that may differ from your own; listening and not defending when someone from another background takes the time to explain their position; understanding that everyone comes to the classroom with differing levels of access to the dominant culture.

***ACADEMIC INTEGRITY***

All work submitted for this class should be the student’s original work, and should have been written for this class. When students include the ideas of others in their writing, they should make it clear in the text and through in-text citations which ideas and quotes are from outside sources. In cases where group work is assigned, the names of all members in the group must be listed, and the submission must contain only work by those students. Submission of any assignment in violation of this policy will result in a zero on the assignment. A second violation will result in automatic failure of the course and potential disciplinary action by the institution.
