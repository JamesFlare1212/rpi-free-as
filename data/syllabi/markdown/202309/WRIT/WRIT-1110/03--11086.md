## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

WRIT 1110 is an intro level writing class that aims to introduce students to the basics of college level writing as well as a variety of common genres of analytical and persuasive writing. The grade will be based around four major writing assignments plus homework and participation. First, students will be required to visit a place/space and write an analytical essay about how the space is constructed and how it is used by and affects people in that space. Second, students will write an essay on a question of cause and effect, which aims to explain how we know that a specific cause leads to a specific effect and describing the process by which this occurs. Students will be required to draw on secondary research in order to do this effectively. The third writing assignment will ask students to construct a definition for a term whose meaning is in dispute. In order to do this, they will need to look at multiple perspectives on the term in question as well as specific examples that may or may not fit the definition. The final writing assignment will ask students to combine these patterns and write a paper making an argument about the political, social, and/or economic events that the student believes have defined their life. In order to make this case, students will need to draw on secondary research as well as their own personal experiences, and will have to make a convincing argument using cause and effect and any other patterns that may be appropriate. In addition to the major writing assignments, students will be expected to attend class and participate regularly. This is a process-based class, so students will submit a rough draft for each assignment and receive feedback from the instructor as well as several peers.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Learn to write focused, well-organized texts for a variety of academic audiences and rhetorical situations within a variety of analytical and persuasive genres of writing.
- Learn to view writing as a process, to receive constructive feedback from both instructors and peers, and to revise writing through multiple drafts.
- Learn to locate reliable sources from credentialed, reputable authors and institutions and to incorporate ideas from these sources into one’s own writing while providing appropriate documentation in the correct citation format.
- Learn to view writing within the context of an ongoing conversation, and learn to make worthwhile original contributions to the types of conversations that typically take place in the academy.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

In summary, there is first the expectation that you will attend class. In regard to deliverables, there will be **four formal assignments** in addition to maintaining an **in-class guided writing practice** and completion of several **informal assignments** to help prepare you for the formal assignments and to help you stretch, strengthen, and challenge your writing and communication muscles. The primary goal in the formal assignments will be to *improve your audience’s understanding of something they are invited/need/want to understand better*. For all formal assignments, you will have the opportunity to discuss your topic and audience with me and your peer writing group.

**Participation** (10% of final grade)

Showing up to most things in life is the most meaningful; it’s even more meaningful when you are fully attentive, fully engaged, fully present. We are all human, and we all have an infinite number of other things we can (or could) attend to at any given moment. My invitation for the semester is to **be fully present and attentive** in class. *This means silencing your cell phones and keeping your computer away unless it is required for class that day* (which I will communicate to you the class meeting prior). We’ll be “practicing” this at the start of every class by taking a couple minutes to settle in and take inventory of ourselves before we even begin. **This means I will start class promptly to maximize our time together.**

**Attendance** (your presence in the classroom) is an important piece to your success and our classroom community. The work of this course will involve several types of activities in addition to independent formal and informal assignments – class meetings, small group work, peer writing groups, individual conferences, short in-class presentations to name a few.

To “quantify” these invitations and expectations, I am going to base your participation grade on an accumulated points system: ***You can receive a possible 5 points for each class meeting: 1 for timely attendance, 1 for preparedness, 1 for active engagement in daily guided writing practice, 1 for active engagement during in-class activities, 1 for positive contribution to our community.***

If you anticipate missing a class for a **planned event** (e.g., traveling for varsity athletics, job/internship interview) or for a significant period of time (due to medical concerns, personal loss, etc.) you should notify me **at least 2 class sessions in advance** so that we can make plans for how you will make up and submit the work that you will miss. My mantra is “Prior Planning Prevents Poor Progress.” Plan ahead as much as possible, please.

Lastly, if you are unwell and should not (or cannot) attend class for the health and well-being of our classroom community, or if there is a sudden, unexpected event that prevents you from attending class, please email me as soon as possible to arrange for how to make up your missed assignments and class time. *Any coursework or class-time not made up will result in lost points.*

**Guided In-Class Writing Practices**

At the beginning of each class period, we’re going to engage in a writing exercise/practice that encourages us to engage with your inner audience, speaker, and listener. These in-class writings will serve as entry points/sparks/challenges to help stretch your creative process, and prepare you for and potentially help polish your formal assignments. **While I will not be assessing these writing practices on a scheduled basis (or even collecting them), *they* *WILL be a major part of your final reflection assignment.***

**Informal Assignments** (5% of final grade)

Informal assignments will take various forms. They may include submitting a short piece of writing, bringing material that you will use in completing a formal assignment, completing an improvisational activity via email, or short in-class writing assignments. These informal writing assignments will be graded from 1-2 points, depending on the thoughtfulness and quality of what you turn in. If you have room for improvement on one or two of these assignments, your final grade will not be hurt too much.

**Formal Assignments**

**1) Telling a Story** (20% of final grade)

*No one else sees the world the way you do, so no one else can tell the stories that you have to tell. – Charles de Lint*

This assignment requires that you tell a story that you could submit to The Moth Radio Hour storytelling program [https://themoth.org]. It should be a story that gives your audience an *insider’s perspective* on an experience that has shaped who you are/how you understand the world/how you interact with others (or yourself). You’ll tell your story for an audience of people who could benefit from or learn from hearing your story. (In this case, your audience is our classroom community. You may also consider submitting this to the McKinney Writing Contest!) *You will share the “kernel” of your story with me and your peer writing group to receive feedback before you begin to write. In the end, though, you will be the one to decide what story you want to tell depending on your level of comfort, courage, and vulnerability.*

The assignment is also shaped on The Moth’s criteria and expectations. You can learn more about the organization/show on their website, and they offer storytelling tips [https://themoth.org/share-your-story/storytelling-tips-tricks] as well.

*One difference* is that while The Moth does *not* allow you to use notes or read from a paper, I will. However, you should still practice enough in order to just reference your text during your story telling. For this assignment, I’m asking that you first ***write*** the story (but write it as you want to **tell it aloud**) and *then* tell the story during a 2-day Listening Party in class. You will have ample opportunity to “practice” reading your work aloud during peer writing groups; it is also possible to practice your story with a tutor at Comm+D by making an appointment.

**3) Problem/Solution Project** (30% of final grade)

For this project (the meat and potatoes of this class), you’ll be proposing your own project that starts with asking questions about a topic you’re passionate about wanting to change either in school or “real” life. You’ll be choosing your audience for your project based on who will actually be able to make an effective change to answer the question/solve the problem/change the state of things.

We’ll be working through this project in phases in order to strengthen several skills you’ll use throughout your academic and professional endeavors: **researching** using databases, developing **personal archiving** practices, using **citation styles** to give credit to outside sources, **analyzing and evaluating** outside sources as they relate to your major **claims** (points, opinions, thesis), and **synthesizing** those sources. We’ll also be working on using **data** (evidence, facts, statistics, expert testimony, concrete details) to support **warrants** (reasons/answers) based on the **three basic methods** of persuasion—logos (logic), ethos (ethics), and pathos (emotion) to help others understand the value and importance of your project. *You will be required to use between **4-6 outside sources** (including at least ONE multimodal source–images, photographs, sound bites, tables, charts, etc.) for this project. You’ll be required to practice the citation style most used in your major.*

You will also be presenting your project to the class in 3-Minute Thesis style, including a *single, static PowerPoint slide*. The important part of this project is to be able to explain your project clearly to a non-specialized audience in 3 minutes. No more, no less. More information will be provided about this portion of the assignment later.

For your final project, you will be curating a text presentation of how you have individually met the learning outcomes for the course. There will be more details forthcoming in an extended assignment sheet and rubric, but here is the gist:

You’ll choose 10 unique “entries” from your archives (in-class writing practices, informal writing assignments, peer feedback, etc.) to showcase how you, as an individual, have met each of the ten (10) learning outcomes and how you have progressed as a communicator over the course of fifteen weeks. In addition to these entries, you’ll reflect on your individual experience in the course as a whole. You’ll have the opportunity to share how you’ve progressed/grown/evolved as a writer, reader, listener, and speaker (and learner in general!) through suggested prompts and questions.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

This is a small group seminar-style class, so students are expected to attend class regularly and to actively participate in all class sessions. As such, students will get three unexcused absences for the semester with no questions asked. If you are sick, please let me know and stay home. Student athletes should inform me at the beginning of the term regarding any classes they will have to miss during the semester. Other reasons for an excused absence can be submitted through the Office for Student Success. Unexcused absences beyond the second will result in the student’s final grade being lowered by a third of a letter grade (so a B would become a B-, and an A- would become a B+, and so on). More than 6 unexcused absences will result in the student receiving no credit for the course.

### Other Course Policies:

***LATE WORK POLICY***

Students are expected to submit work on time and to communicate with me in advance if you need an extension for any assignments. No extensions will be granted once the due date for the assignment has passed. Homework assignments and rough drafts will be due at the beginning of class on the day for which they assigned. After that, no late homework assignments will be accepted. Final drafts of major writing assignments will be due at the beginning of class on the day for which they are assigned. Starting the following day, they will be marked down a third of a letter grade for each day they are late (so a B would become a B-, an A- becomes a B+, and so on). If you need an extension for an assignment, contact me before the due date. If you need an extension on a more regular basis, accommodations can be worked out with the Office of Disabled Student Services or with the instructor.

***INCLUSION POLICY***

Our classroom and my office hours are spaces where all are welcome. Please note that discrimination or harassment in any form will not be tolerated in this learning community. Beyond simply avoiding discriminating against others, your presence in this class indicates that you agree to actively promote a welcoming and inclusive learning environment for all, including but not limited to taking the following actions: using inclusive language, such as referring to people by their preferred personal pronouns; understanding that it is your responsibility in class discussions to shape your comments for a diverse audience that may include native and non-native English speakers, people of various genders and sexual orientations, people from various cultural and religious backgrounds, and other identifiers that may differ from your own; listening and not defending when someone from another background takes the time to explain their position; understanding that everyone comes to the classroom with differing levels of access to the dominant culture.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

All work submitted for this class should be the student’s original work, and should have been written for this class. When students include the ideas of others in their writing, they should make it clear in the text and through in-text citations which ideas and quotes are from outside sources. In cases where group work is assigned, the names of all members in the group must be listed, and the submission must contain only work by those students. Submission of any assignment in violation of this policy will result in a zero on the assignment. A second violation will result in automatic failure of the course and potential disciplinary action by the institution.
