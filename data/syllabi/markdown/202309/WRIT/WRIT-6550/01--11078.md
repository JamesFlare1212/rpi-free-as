## Course Goals

### Goals:

This class is structured to provide insight into the iterative nature of writing, or in other words, writing as a process. I have designed this process-focused, **workshop-driven** course to help you acquire knowledge and skills to help you develop competitive proposals to support projects in your field of interest. Depending on where you are in your academic career, you might propose an innovative/creative solution for improving life on campus, work on a dissertation or thesis proposal, an internal fellowship application packet, seek grant funding to support scientific research, apply for an external research fellowship, win a place on a panel at a conference or workshop, or pursue funding for a humanities or arts project.

## **Formal Assignments for WRIT 6550**

**1) Field Report (interview)**

You will be required to conduct and write a brief field report and present your findings to the class. You will locate someone who has written the kind of *unsolicited proposal* you are interested in writing for your first major project. Ask them if you can look at a copy of the proposal they wrote. Analyze and breakdown its generic features and baseline logic. You’ll also interview the writer about the a) process, b) outcome, and c) significance. Presentations will cover the selection process, proposal deconstruction/interesting features or requirements, significance for the writer, and reflections on what you learned. In addition to the presentation, you’re required to submit a *brief*, no more than 3-page, write-up.

**2) Unsolicited Proposal[1]**

This *individual project* is meant to help students make significant progress in program requirements or seek internal/external funding sources. Students will be required to submit a proposal plan for how they will successfully complete the proposal. The proposal should be aligned to each student’s current point in their graduate programs.

Examples: HASS, prospectus, practice questions for exams, dissertation chapter.

**3) Solicited Proposal**

This *individual project* is designed to create a clear plan for responding to a Request for Proposals (RFP) or Call for Proposal (CFP) for the purpose of attending a conference, taking part in a workshop, or participating in a research project. You will then craft a persuasive proposal to convince your audience to accept/fund your proposal. You will also create an 8-10 minute presentation to showcase your proposal. Following presentations, you will be required to reflect on your experience writing this proposal.

**4) Final Reflection**

At the end of the semester, you will be required to submit a reflection sharing your overall experience in the course. A more detailed assignment sheet will detail potential prompts and questions to address.

[1] Students taking the 6000-level course may also arrange to complete a substantial solicited proposal for this project depending on their program progress.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

*The* *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* and *The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement* define various forms of academic dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade *must represent your own work*. **If you plagiarize once,** you will receive an **F on the assignment.** If you plagiarize again, you will **fail the entire course** and may be reported to the *Dean of Students* as appropriate. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask me for clarification. The basics: when using information from another source **(including anonymous online sources**) always cite the original text or URL and distinguish it from your own commentary. If you directly cut and paste another person’s language, you need to use it as a **quotation**. *We will talk specifically about citation styles and how to make sure that you are giving “credit” when it is required of you in spirit of scholarly collaboration.* In regard to using AI (e.g. ChatGPT or other Open AI resources): While it may be tempting to use this resource, please note that there is no “check” to ensure the information that you are receiving is correct. Researchers are finding that ChatGPT can provide misinformation. Using this resource is NOT considered *your own work* and **will** be deemed as an instance of academic dishonesty.
