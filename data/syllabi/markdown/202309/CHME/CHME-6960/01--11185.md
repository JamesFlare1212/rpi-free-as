## Course Goals

### Goals:

The goal is to educate the students on major trends of purification of proteins and nucleic acids from biotechnology streams.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Purification of proteins and nucleic acids from biotechnology streams from reading and critiquing peer-reviewed publications, patents, and visiting lectures

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Successfully identify methods for purifying monoclonal antibodies and nucleac acids
- 2\. Prepare and present a 17-minute slide presentation critique the main points of a relevant paper/patent
- 3\. Write and submit an abstract about each presentation that they have given
- 4\. Critique professional reports and presentations from the National Academies (and others)
- 5\. Reflect on their learning at the end the semester via a written submission
- 7\. Assess their work for the potential for submit patent disclosure application

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Measures: Grades will be based on the following:  
Slide performance--depth, creativity and presentation skills  
Abstract critique  
Participation in invited lectures

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

I will grade students based on their reading, reviewing and class presentations - 100% of grade.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.
