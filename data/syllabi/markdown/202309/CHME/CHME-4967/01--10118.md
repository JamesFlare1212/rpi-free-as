## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Sustainable materials play a vital role in forging a future where the use of man-made goods does not necessarily result in detrimental impacts for the natural environment. The plastics economy of today primarily operates in a linear manner, where non-renewable fossil-based petrochemicals are used as feedstocks and waste is discarded without returning value to environmental and manufacturing systems. This course will teach introductory concepts relevant to plastics sustainability and will particularly focus on the role of biotechnology in creating a “circular” plastics economy.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance:** This course uses an active learning approach, where students are experientially involved in the learning process. Studies have shown that actively learning yields improved information retention and comprehension for nearly all students. Each lecture in this course will incorporate numerous in-class activities, group discussions, and peer teaching exercises. Attendance is critical and will be a component of your final course grade. A student must be present for at least 95% of the lecture period for attendance to be counted. Excused absences will be granted on a case-by-case basis, and may require documentation, such as a note from the Student Health Center, etc. If an excused absence is granted, you will be expected to review the lecture, which will be posted online for asynchronous learning.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:** Unless otherwise noted, all assignments are expected to be *individual, original* work. All reference materials (including web materials) used in preparation of assignments must be cited appropriately using footnotes and/or a bibliography. One instance of unacceptable collaboration or plagiarism will result in a reduction of the final course grade by one letter grade (e.g. B to C). A second instance of academic dishonesty will result in failure of the course.

Examples of plagiarism include:

- Using someone else’s words, ideas, art, or other production as your own
- Using someone else’s words, ideas, art, or other production in your production without crediting the source (regardless of whether compensation was exchanged)
- Quoting someone else without putting the quote in quotation marks
- Providing incorrect source information
- Copying or quoting enough ideas or words from someone else so that it makes up the majority of your work, regardless of whether you credit the source or not
- For more information, please see https://www.plagiarism.org/article/what-is-plagiarism

Plagiarism is taken very seriously in this course. Engaging in plagiarism not only robs others of their work, but also has permanent impact on your credibility as a scientist/engineer. Plagiarism can severely affect your academic performance and future career path. In short, it’s just not worth it. If you are uncertain if something qualifies as “plagiarism”, please ask ahead of the assignment’s due date.
