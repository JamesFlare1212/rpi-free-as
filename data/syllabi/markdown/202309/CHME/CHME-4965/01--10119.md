## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides an overview of metabolic engineering, focusing on both experimental techniques and computational tools for performing metabolic engineering. Students will learn about several basic experimental techniques such as DNA cloning and PCR, current methods for performing cellular modifications and will then learn about metabolic balance and metabolic flux analysis. Overview of current used industrial processes based on metabolic engineering and industrial microbiology will also be provided.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

\- Basic molecular biology techniques, such as PCR, DNA cloning, gel electrophoresis.  
\- Basic concepts behind metabolic fluxes and their measurements, such as stoichiometric modeling, metabolic flux analysis and metabolic pathway synthesis.  
\- Applications of metabolic engineering in industrial settings for the production of chemicals.  
\- How to carry out basic literature search and use it to create a review.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

\- Introduction: course objectives and basic concepts.  
\- DNA and DNA modification tools and enzymes.  
\- Transcription and RNA. The concept of the operon  
\- Enzymes and proteins  
\- Review of central metabolic pathways.  
\- Stoichiometric modeling.  
\- Simple metabolic flux analysis using mass balances.  
\- 13C-labeled metabolic flux analysis.  
\- Calculations of yields.  
\- Production of succinic acid.  
-Production of amino acids.  
\- Production of vitamins.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Learn basic cloning techniques
- Learning basic molecular biology techniques, such as PCR, CRISPR, homologous recombination, gel electrophoresis
- Learning how to perform stoichiometric modeling
- Learn how to perform metabolic flux analysis
- Learn examples of industrial applications of metabolic engineering in the production of high value and commodity chemicals.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students will be given regular homework in the first part of the course. In the second part, students will prepare short presentations on the production of different chemicals using metabolic engineering. Students will also be given projects to work on during the semester and will prepare a final report in the form of a proposal and give a presentation at the end of the semester.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grading will be based on homework, presentations and reports.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and the students should make themselves familiar with these. All homework, lab reports and quizzes are expected to be individual work. You will work in groups for your project and may discuss your results with your group or other classmates. Final written reports and oral reports will be done by groups. All reference materials (including web materials) used in preparation of reports must be cited appropriately using notes and/or a bibliography. One instance of unacceptable collaboration or plagiarism will result in a reduction of the final course grade by one letter grade (e.g. B to C). A second instance of academic dishonesty will result in failure of the course.
