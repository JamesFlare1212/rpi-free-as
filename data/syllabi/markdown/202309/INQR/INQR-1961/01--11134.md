## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course bridges multilingual students’ writing skills between high school level writing and college level and professional writing. Through genre-based, audience-centered approaches, the course cultivates communication skills of reading, writing, speaking, and critical thinking on topics such as current events, Englishes used in diverse sociolinguistic contexts globally, cultural diversity, and cross-cultural communication. Students learn structures and organization of academic writing, visual design of professional presentations, and academic writing conventions. The goal of this course is for students to gain communication skills for academic and professional contexts, identify and critically analyze local, regional, and global issues from multiple perspectives and deliver audience-appropriate proposals and problem-solving solutions.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Analyze academic texts in terms of audience, purpose, and genre
- Summarize and critically engage with academic text at the level of ideas
- Understand and use written and spoken academic structures and conventions to produce texts for global academic audiences
- Engage in the processes of academic writing and presentations including reading, outlining, drafting, peer review and editing
- Work collaboratively with classmates in the processes of writing, and presenting
- Find information from appropriate sources, understand these sources and integrate these sources into writing and original ideas through the use of summary, paraphrase, quotation, and reflection
- Practice academic citation conventions and referencing

1. **Reading Analysis and Response Papers** **1****0****%**

Students will summarize, analyze, and reflect on select articles according to the given guidelines.

2. **Paper Proposal and Annotated Bibliography** **15%**

Students will write at least 2 drafts of proposal for their final paper and an annotated bibliography for the paper using good summary techniques and correct APA citation style.

3. **Paper Proposal Presentation- 5%**

Students will present their paper proposal to the class in order to practice presentation techniques and will receive feedback on their proposal from their peers.

4. **Presentation-15%**

Students will give an academic presentation of their final research paper.

5. **Problem-Solution Paper****- 2****5****%**

Students will write a 6 page researched problem-solution paper on a topic approved by the instructor. The students will write 3 drafts of the paper. The first draft will be graded for completion as a homework assignment and will be used for peer review in class. The second and third draft will both be graded and will both contribute to the final score of the paper.

6. **Plagiarism** **and Citation** **Quiz- 5%**

Students will take a quiz related to plagiarism and APA citation in class.

7. **Homework- 1****5****%**

Homework will be assigned on most days in class. At least 200 points worth of homework will be assigned. Students need 150 points in order to receive an A (full marks) in this section of the class. Students will usually be allowed to choose which homework assignments they complete, but a few assignments will be mandatory such as the initial diagnostic assignment and the peer review. Extra credit maybe received in this category by earning more than the necessary 150 points.

8. **Participation- 10%**

Students will be expected to attend and actively participate in class throughout the semester. Students may lose points in this category if they do not attend class or attend class but are unengaged and do not participate. (See attendance policy for specifics).

**Rules for Assignments:**

**LATE POLICY:** Late submission of assignments will be penalized 10% for each day after the due date (i.e., 1-24 hours late is a 10% deduction; 25-48 hours late is a 20% deduction) unless otherwise notified.. An absence is not an excuse for not completing homework. However, PLEASE always email me BEFORE you know you will be absent or if you cannot complete your work by the deadline.

**ABSENCE POLICY FOR IN-CLASS ASSIGNMENTS**: If you are absent on the day of an in-class assignment, such as a quiz, a presentation, or a peer review and your absence is included within the 5 allowed absences, you may be allowed to make up the assignment or complete a similar assignment. If you have missed more than 5 days, you will no longer be allowed to make up in-class assignments.  
**ABSENCE POLICY FOR IN-CLASS PRESENTATIONS:** You must be present in class on the day of your presentation to receive full points on the presentation unless you have talked to me in advance and I have approved your request to present later (requests will usually only be approved in the case of medical or family emergencies). If the request is not approved or you do not talk to me in advance, you will be allowed to present outside of class for a significantly reduced score.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance Policy**

Students are expected to attend on time and actively participate in all classes. As students cannot participate well in class without being present, students will be expected to be physically in class and participate actively to receive full points for participation.

There are a total of 270 participation points; 5 points for each 50 minutes of class (10 points for each class). To receive an A grade in participation (full marks), students must receive at least 220 points. In other words, students can miss 5 classes and still receive a perfect attendance score. These classes can be missed for any reason without any documentation.

If a student has a prolonged illness or other circumstance that would cause them to miss more classes, please see the instructor and special arrangements may be made.

Students arriving after attendance has been called will receive 9 points for the day (if they fully participate while in the class). Students arriving more 10 minutes late – 1 hour 10 minutes late will receive 5 points for the day (if they fully participate while in the class), and students arriving more than 1 hour 10 minutes late will not receive any points that day.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

For plagiarism that is deemed unintentional (poor/lack of citation), the first violation will received a reduced score on the assignment. Further violations will result in a 0 grade for the assignment.

For plagiarism that is deemed intentional (copying someone else’s work), the first violation will result in a 0 grade for the assignment. The second violation results in failure of the course. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

Use of AI

AI programs such as Chat GPT may be used as an aid to your writing in this class for things such as but not limited to the following: help with choosing a topic, creating outlines, finding sources, making reference lists, and editing papers. If you use an AI tool, you should cite that tool and state at the end of your paper how you have used that tool. AI should never be used to write your entire paper or large portions of your paper for you. This will result in a 0 grade for the assignment.
