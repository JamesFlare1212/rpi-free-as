## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Principles of Economics is a one semester survey course in micro- and macroeconomics which has two goals: (1) to introduce a wide variety of concepts and applications in the broad subject of economics, and (2) to function in its special Rensselaer designation as a HASS Inquiry and Data Intensive Course. Economics is the study of our choices. Traditionally, these choices have been framed as how best to employ scarce resources to produce goods and services and distribute them for consumption. To describe these choices we will introduce the concepts of opportunity cost, demand and supply theory, and market structures and consider the role of government in resource allocation choices. Also presented are macroeconomic topics of Gross Domestic Product, economic growth, inflation, unemployment, long-run trends and short-run fluctuations, money, and banking. As a HASS Inquiry course, a foremost objective will be to evaluate multiple diverse perspectives on contemporary and complex global issues and address their implications to social equity. Furthermore, the course is designated as a Data Intensive course and provides opportunity to practice analysis of economic data. The class will meet two times per week for lecture and problem-solving. HASS Inquiry will be described in class with supporting resources posted to LMS.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- to gain a factual knowledge of theories, data, and methods of analysis used by economists
- to evaluate multiple diverse perspectives on contemporary and complex global issues
- to analyze policies with regards to social, cultural, environmental, and economic outcomes
- to consider ethical responsibilities of protecting the public’s health, safety, and welfare
- to practice data analysis within the economic context

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Microeconomics Test, Macroeconomics Test, Final Exam (optional), and HASS Inquiry assignment

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

By enrolling in this course, all students agree to the course policy on academic integrity. Prohibited during testing are communication with peers (verbal, written, electronic, other), mobile devices (computers, cell phones, tablets, other), notes, books, hats, and earbuds/pods, and any means of accessing information, sharing answers, and communication among students. ## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

Tests: There will be two tests given on the scheduled dates in the classroom. Test format is on paper, multiple choice. You are allowed to bring one page of notes for use during the test. You must bring your student ID card to the test in order to take the test. **If you do not bring your ID card to the test, you will not be allowed to take the test and your score will be recorded as zero.** Any student who arrives more than 10 minutes late to a test may be denied the test. Prohibited during testing are communication with peers (verbal, written, electronic, other), mobile devices (computers, cell phones, tablets, other), notes, books, hats, and earbuds/pods. There is a zero tolerance policy towards cheating on tests. Cheating includes any means of accessing information, sharing answers, and communication among students. If any incident of cheating on a test is discovered, the involved student(s) will receive a failing grade for the course; see the policy on Academic Integrity, below.

**MAKEUP TEST POLICY:** *Makeup tests will only be allowed for absences having an official Rensselaer excuse issued by the Health Center or the Dean of Students, and submitted to the professor by two days after the test. The makeup test is scheduled during the week after the test. If you miss a test and do not have an official Rensselaer excuse, you must take the optional final exam to improve your test average.*

Optional Final Exam: An optional final exam will be offered as a way for students to attempt to improve their grade. The final exam will cover all course material. If you take the final exam, the score you obtain on the final exam will be compared with the average of your two tests. You will be awarded the higher of these scores as your grade for the test portion of the course. Therefore, it cannot hurt you to take the optional final exam. *If you miss either the micro or macro test(s) and do not have a proper excuse (see policy on makeup tests above), then plan on taking the optional final exam to improve your grade.* The Registrar will announce the date of the optional final exam in October, to take place during finals week.

HASS Inquiry: This course is identified as a HASS (Humanities, Arts, and Social Sciences) Inquiry course. The purpose of a HAAS Inquiry course is to provide opportunity for the discourse of diverse and divergent ways of thinking about complex global issues. In particular, we strive to be thorough in understanding many perspectives with regards to important issues and the outcomes they create for society, culture, the environment, the economy, and many other realms. Additionally, we must evaluate the ethical responsibilities of protecting the public’s health, safety, and welfare. Instructions for the HASS Inquiry assignments are available on LMS. **There is no makeup assignment for HASS Inquiry; if you miss these assignments, then you will lose credit for this part of the course and it will be impossible to get a final course grade of ‘A’**. The purpose of the HASS Inquiry assignment is to collectively share thoughts and ideas. For this reason, work submitted late, no matter how well-written, is not useful and will not earn credit for HASS Inquiry.
