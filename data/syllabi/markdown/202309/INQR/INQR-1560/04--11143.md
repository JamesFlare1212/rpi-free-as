## Course Goals

### Goals:

1\) You will develop tools for ‘critical media engagement,’ learning how to analyze, interpret and appreciate the capabilities and functions of different media forms. We’ll explore the distinctive features of various media—their inherent strengths or limitations, and their institutional or structural biases. You’ll gain an understanding of the historical and socioeconomic origins of modern media, and their profound impact on contemporary society. A particular emphasis in this version of the class will be on representation in media; here, the concept of ‘representation’ is loosely interpreted, including but not limited to representation of identities (race, ethnicity, gender, sexuality and so on). We’re trying to understand how our ‘re-presenting’ of reality shapes our understanding of it.  
2\) As a complement to these critical tools, we will enhance your ‘reflexive media awareness,’ a term that refers to your processual and dialogic understanding of your relationship to media. In other words, you should gain a deeper understanding of how media shapes us as individuals, as groups and communities, and as cultures.  
3\) You will hone your communicative/generative skills by articulating and refining your thoughts in oral, written, and presentational forms. If you are so inclined, you’ll have the chance to create media works in various formats as a way of engaging with concepts we encounter.You should leave the class able to more fully articulate your experience of media, contextualize it, and empathize with others’ perspectives.  
4\) Finally, an underlying theme of the class is your involvement in a collaborative learning process; the class will be shaped by your interests and prior knowledge as a community, as well as by my disciplinary, personal and pedagogical expertise. Through the use of non-traditional educational strategies, we’ll engage in meta-examinations of the learning process, and we’ll consider how the course itself functions as a ‘medium’ for learning.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

ASSESSMENT: ‘MEDIA POINTS’-BASED ‘GAMEFUL’ GRADING  
Introduction: Grading for this class will unfold a little differently than you’re used to. Using what can be called a ‘gameful’ approach, or an ‘additive grading’ system, your performance in the class is based on the points that you earn, much like in many games (some video games, table-top role-playing games, or what have you). Like in some games, or in many popular films/TV, there’s a ‘clock’ for some assignments, defined by milestones in the semester; many assignments, though, have numerous target dates for them.  
Everyone begins with 0 points. At the start of the semester, there are 540,000+ points available. As you accomplish tasks in the class, you build up points towards your final grade. There are central tasks everyone should complete (like the opening activities, daily readings and daily assignments, and the ‘self-assessments rounds’ connected to those)--activities that allow us to do discussion work in the class. But most major assignments in the class, including the final project, are on a self-selected basis; some are collaborative, some are individual, some are presentations, some are written, and so on. There’s quite a variety (see below). It is up to you to figure out which of the available assignments you will take on in order to build up enough points to get the ‘rank’ (and grade) you want by the end of class.  
Goal of this approach to assessment:  
What I’m looking to do here is to get you to consider a different way to assess and evaluate how to gain skills and knowledge in a class. This is a class about a topic for which there isn’t a single body of knowledge or point of view, so this grading system is meant to encourage personal, active engagement with the material. I want you to be active in your own assessment. The ranks are not traditional grade percentages, but rather levels of achievement in building knowledge of media studies, and in critical and creative thinking. Think of a grading system as a ‘medium’ for your evaluation/assessment; by using an alternate method, I want to put into relief the nature of the assessment systems you generally use. I welcome your feedback on the system I’m using; you’ll have plenty of opportunities to give it.
