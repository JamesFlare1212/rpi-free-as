### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

**Course Overview:**

Why is climate change an issue of justice, and not simply a problem to be solved through new technologies and economic solutions? How is climate justice different from, and a challenge to, mainstream environmental frameworks? This course will introduce students to the theories and concepts of climate justice. Students will investigate the ways in which climate change is entangled with histories of racism, sexism, ableism, and economic disparities and come to understand the role of design methodologies within this framework. Central to this course are issues of equity and social justice, and we will critically investigate the structures of power that cause particular populations to be disproportionally affected by environmental hazards and climate change. Issues we will consider include community-led efforts for safe, clean food and water, an end to resource extraction and air pollution, and re-thinking disaster relief efforts and their impact on historically minoritized communities. We will take a transdisciplinary approach toward climate justice with a particular

emphasis on design practices. The focus will be on the development of communication-intensive projects that blur the boundaries between written, sonic, and visual media and integrate field research to expand our abilities to communicate with, and amplify different audiences. Approaches may include writing, infographics, photography, video, sound, and other mixed-media.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- (1) To have a better appreciation and understanding of how human activity has impacted the climate and its social, economic, cultural and political ramifications;
- (2) Interpret ways in which you can mitigate environmental impact through design;
- (3) Explore how climate change influences people's orientation toward power, authority, community, and freedom;
- (4) Learn how an understanding of the significance of design might influence future attempts to build humane, sustainable, democratic structures to aid responsible human activity;

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Daily comments and observations:**

For every reading session of the class, I ask that you prepare (1) a brief comment of a sentence or two and (2) a question. I will call on people at random to state their observation or question.

**Midterm exams:** There will be one midterm *take home essay* of two to three double spaced pages. Drawing from the weekly readings and class discussions select a topic that resonates with you and directly affects your life and academic pursuits. See schedule below for submission date. There is no “right answer” for the midterm essay. The purpose is to demonstrate *your grasp of readings and other materials in the course along with your ability to address creatively key questions and concepts*.

**Note: The subject of all email sent to me regarding work for this course (including midterm essays) should be CLIMATE JUSTICE.**

**Term project**:

The class will be broken down into teams, ideally 3 students per team, to research and write a paper, about 10 pages double-spaced (with drawings and photos, if appropriate), on a term project covering an aspect of the *social and political dimensions of design as it relates to climate justice.* You’ll propose a topic of special interest to you for study, conduct the research and present your results at the end. Prepare a 10 – 15 minute in class presentation of your term project. Papers to be submitted the last day of class. All team members will receive the *same grade* for the project. [Note: We may need to schedule an extra session for the oral presentations.]

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Your grade will be determined by: Class participation, midterm essay, group term paper and oral presentation. No late work of any kind will be accepted (unless you have a legitimate excuse – illness, job interview, or family emergency). You may miss any two daily sessions without penalty; if you miss more than that, your grade will suffer. All papers, exams and presentations will be graded as follows, using pluses and minuses as needed: Excellent: A; Good: B; Average: C; Very poor: D; Failing: F.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic Honesty: Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the course, and teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own. Any act that violates this trust undermines the educational enterprise. They contradict our very reason for being at Rensselaer. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of academic dishonesty and the procedures for responding to them. You are expected to have reviewed and understand the Handbook. The following additional policies apply to this course:  
For this course, penalties for academic dishonesty, especially in cases involving false representation of work, may well result in your failing the course, with a citation placed in your academic record. While the process for assessing the situation is as outlined in the Rensselaer Handbook, the initial judgment about a violation, and the penalty if any, will be determined at the discretion of the instructors and what they believe to have occurred. If you have any doubts about a situation, especially regarding plagiarism, please check with us first.
