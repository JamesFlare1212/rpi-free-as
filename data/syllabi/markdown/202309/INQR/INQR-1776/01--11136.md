## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The term “American Dream” is familiar, but what role, specifically, does the imaginative play in American literature and culture, past and present? This course focuses on the power of imagination to shape a nation through the analysis of fiction, poetry, film, essays, comics, advertising, and historiographies. Throughout the semester, we will inquire: What is the function of a national imagination? How do literary and cultural narratives shape our contemporary lived experiences? We will use diverse social histories as a means to explore this contemporary experience, focusing on the tension between American dreams and American realities. Requirements include reading/viewing responses, student presentations, and a semester capstone assignment that allows students to choose between a textual or multi-media project.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

## Course Goals / Objectives

- Explore connections between literature, history, and contemporary culture in a seminar setting.
- Enhance appreciation of literary texts as a creative, cultural, emotional, and intellectual medium by which writers describe and make sense of the human condition.
- Sharpen critical and creative thinking, reading, and writing skills.
- Practice team building, small group-dynamics, and interpersonal communication skills by engaging in a learning community.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Utilize rhetorical and narrative analysis as a means to investigate the broad, flexible, and interdisciplinary nature of studies in the humanities.
- Analyze a variety of texts, visual and linguistic, through a particular historical and/or cultural lens.
- Demonstrate the difference between summary, analysis, and synthesis.
- Perform comparative close readings of (synthesize) literary and cultural texts, past and present
- Use interpersonal communication skills through exploration and discussion of complex social, cultural, and intercultural issues.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- Reading Journals
- Literature Review
- Presentation
- Capstone Project: Essay or multimedia

### Attendance Policy:

Five percent of your course grade is an incentive for near perfect attendance. Everyone has one “freebie” absence to use for illness, sleep, self-care, or other obligations without point penalty or need for an official excuse (though this absence *is* calculated into the total number of days missed). Otherwise, I expect that you will come to class every class (school athletes see me if you will have significant conflicts). Each subsequent *unexcused* absence beyond the first one will drop your attendance grade by ten points. If you miss more than five classes **in** **total** you will receive no credit for the ***course even if you are otherwise passing***. Three tardies of half hour or more accrue to one absence.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
