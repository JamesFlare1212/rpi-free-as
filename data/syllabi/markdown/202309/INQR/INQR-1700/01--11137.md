## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

From opera arias to indigenous folk tunes to hip-hop, the act of setting words to music has produced some of the world’s most popular and enduring art in the form of songwriting, with many of the world’s greatest songs brought into existence with little more than imagination and a guitar or a beat. This course examines the wide variety of elements present in the creation of a successfully written song, while honing a higher sense of critique when analyzing the work of both successful and obscure songwriters. Song recordings, videos, and lively class discussion frame a closer look at multiple creative techniques for this art form, and students are encouraged to experiment on a grand scale, without fear, when writing eight complete songs over the course of the semester.

Students get positive and useful creative feedback along with opportunities for collaboration, while learning how to make their own effective demonstration recordings. While focusing primarily on aspects of songwriting as a popular and commercial art form, this course can also be seen as an effective study in structure building for creative design forms, in many ways analogous to design forms in architecture, mathematics, chemistry, and computer sciences, where creative collaboration can yield innovative results. Or it can just help you finish that surefire hit you’ve been working on.

The class will be split into two groups (**A- MONDAY, B- THURSDAY)** which allows for all student works to be heard and discussed on weeks that songs will be due. Student song recordings and lyric sheets will be presented weekly via the class Webex space before class time on the due date. Feedback for each song in the group is requested from each group member during the class period, and remotely via Webex chat if absent.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Students will select their own “textbook”—book, video, movie, game, media—to use as reference and/or songwriting inspiration for the semester. Student will be required to exhibit and explain text selection on Day 2.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Since songwriting means very different things to each individual creator, my goal is simply to provide feedback, guidance, and tools for successful songwriting outcomes. Skill development is entirely at the pace of the writer, and success defined by their personal artistic vision and definition.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Song Critique/History, Making Demos, Lyrics 1 + 2, Melody, Phrasing, Rhythm, Chords, Chord Progressions, Music Genres, Song Form, Collaboration, Songs & Society, Publishing & Copyright, General Songwriting.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Develop an improved understanding of the art of songwriting, and the works of great songwriters, many actually chosen by the students themselves.
- Accumulate key recording techniques to effectively capture and affix their works.
- Collaborate with fellow writers and musicians, learning how to create in teams with others successfully.
- Improve general writing, presentation, and communication skills.
- Generously and insightfully offer high-quality critique to other creative people, and receive it in kind.
- Create a personal portfolio of self-composed song demos, recorded at as high a quality as possible.
- Document recorded works for the U.S. Library of Congress and file copyrights.
- Become more aware of emerging technologies that allow dissemination and monetization of copyrighted works over the Internet, and potential sync licensing for video.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

SONG PRESENTATION (20%, due Day 13/14)- Students will provide a 15-minute analysis and PowerPoint/Keynote presentation on a chosen song to the class. These will be spread out over two class periods with all students (**A + B**) attending.

SONGS AND SOCIETY PAPER (20%, due Day 21)- Students will select a song that has, in their opinion, had some effect on society and history, and produce 1000-word paper focusing on the individualized processes and methods employed by the songwriter in general, a close analysis of the song’s internal elements (lyrics, melody, chord progression, rhythm, form), and the case for its historical significance.

8 SONGS/SONGWRITER PORTFOLIO (45%)- Students will meet the song deadlines as presented, delivering their demo files and lyric sheets to Webex prior to class on the due dates. Songs presented on time with a minimum of 1:30 in length receive 100 points. Songs presented after class on due dates receive a 5-point reduction, and a 10-point reduction for each day late. This grade is determined by an average of eight scores, one for each song.

PRESENCE AND PARTICIPATION (15%)- The success of this class depends on the presence and participation of each student when listening to and critiquing everyone’s work. With this class, we will strive to make a community where quality creative feedback can be freely given and taken. Any student in danger of not receiving these points (aka not participating or responding) will be notified discreetly weeks in advance of assessment, and given every chance to reclaim them.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

20%- Song Presentation: 15-minute PowerPoint/Keynote presentation to full class.  
20%- Songs & Society Paper: 1000-word paper focused on chosen song that had a verifiable effect of society.  
45%- Eight (8) Songs written and demoed in total*, with recorded audio file and lyric document.  
15%- Presence and Participation: being available and engaged as a listener, giving feedback and useful critique to others.  
*-Songs delivered "on time" (by class period of due date) receive 100 points. Songs delivered on due dates after class receive 95 points. Ten points are taken for each day a song is late. Final grade is determined by averaging scores of the eight songs submitted.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

The first unexcused absence I’ll let go. Any work due on that date is still expected on time, any missed quizzes score a zero. The second time will make me look askance at you, and wonder why you need to miss the class without an official excuse. Third time gets a probationary warning, the fourth will result in a half-grade drop off your final grade, with any further absences resulting in a further half-grade drop.

An absence can become an excused one by informing me of your very good reason beforehand or, if it’s an emergency, as soon as possible after class with an appropriate reason. It is best for both of us for you to have documentation for the reason of your absence obtained through the Student Success Office.

### Other Course Policies:

Lateness

If you’re up to five minutes late, you’re OK the first time or two. If you want to make it a habit, we will be having a talk about it at some point. Ten minutes late and later: I consider that a half of an unexcused absence. I won’t be watching the clock that closely though; what constitutes as “ten minutes late” is completely at my personal discretion and how I feel that day. Just don’t be late unless you have a very good reason, please. It is discourteous and disrespectful, a bad habit to develop at this time of your life.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

You must have personal authorship of either the words or music (or both) of your song. Lyric writers are permitted to write to protected music, though it must be cited if not original. Music writers are permitted to use protected lyric material to write to, though it too must be cited. It is not permitted to use both found lyrics and music and claim authorship, and the penalty for this will be a re-do of the song, for less points (to be determined.) Use of AI products such as ChatGPT MUST be cited.
