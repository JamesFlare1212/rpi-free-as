## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This undergraduate course provides a thorough investigation into global water reuse/recycle initiatives, legal frameworks, and treatment technologies. Students will learn about important chemical and biological contaminants present in wastewater and models for health risk analysis. Different tertiary wastewater treatment technologies used to produce high-quality reclaimed water are covered, including membrane softening, disinfection, and advanced oxidation processes. Application areas for reclaimed water such as agricultural irrigation, industrial uses, and groundwater recharge are examined.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The objective of this course is to provide working knowledge about water legislation, water quality requirements, effluent generation, treatment technologies, water conservation, and reuse programs. The course will present a technical analysis of projects for water optimization and water reuse and will introduce treatment methods to achieve different levels of water quality, considering the many different final effluent uses. Each method will be presented and evaluated considering the advantages and disadvantages of its application, as well as recommended practices for each situation.

Throughout the course, students will have specific homework assignments with questions about topics discussed in the classroom as well as specific activities in class to develop projects for certain advanced treatment technologies. This course will also have a final project that synthesizes concepts learned in class. Besides the delivery of a technical report, students will present their findings to the class, which will help with their documentation and communication skills.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate the ability to evaluate different treatment technologies for the removal of wastewater contaminants based on efficiency, cost, regulations, and environmental impacts.
- Compare different advanced technologies for treating wastewater and choose the most adequate ones to remove microbial or chemical contaminants for specific applications.
- Articulate and communicate the benefits of water reclamation and current treatment technologies effectively to a technical and non-technical audience.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Homework should be submitted on the course Gradescope portal at the beginning of the class period on the dates specified on the **Course Calendar**. Thirty percent (30 %) of the assignment grade will be deducted as a late penalty for homework submitted within one week after the due date. Homework submitted ≥ 1 week after the due date will receive a zero grade.

In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work, although students are encouraged to work with each other by collaborating on assignments (see item 2 under *Homework Policy* above). Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy (e.g., one student copying work done by another student or a student copying from a previously developed solution) will result in a grade of zero for that particular assignment. Also, cheating on an exam will result in a grade of zero for that exam and, in accordance with the *Handbook*, notification to the Dean of Students Office of the incident of academic dishonesty.
