## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The graduate-level course will investigate theories and principles that are used for the design and operation of photochemical reactors. Different ultraviolet (UV) emitting sources and UV radiation measurement techniques will be presented. Subsequent lectures will focus on numerical and experimental methods that are used for the characterization and validation of photochemical reactors. Finally, different applications of UV radiation will be discussed including advanced oxidation processes (AOPs), water/wastewater disinfection, and the inactivation of airborne pathogens such as COVID-19. Students will read and reflect on the latest scientific papers on the topics of UV disinfection and photochemical reactions.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students will learn the fundamental principles of photochemistry and photochemical processes that are commonly used. These fundamental principles will be used as a foundation to explore the behavior of photochemical reactors and methods used to evaluate and predict their behavior, including analytical and numerical methods.

Applications of UV radiation, including:

- Disinfection of water and wastewater,
- Disinfection of air and surfaces.
- Chemical transformations by direct photolysis, and
- Photooxidation and photoreduction processes.

The focus of this course will be on fundamental concepts, so as to prompt their application in a wide range of settings, encourage questions and discussion, and promote creativity. Students will develop their critical thinking and communication skills from the evaluation of essential research publications and formal presentations.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Analyze the mechanics and kinetics of photochemical reactions for different reactor models and describe the process of UV generation and detection.
- Apply fluence field models and computational field dynamics to predict the dynamic behavior of photochemical reactors.
- Describe the applications of UV-based photochemical reactors (e.g., photolysis, UV/AOPs) for microbial inactivation and chemical degradation, such as COVID-19 and NDMA, and explain the associated limitations.
