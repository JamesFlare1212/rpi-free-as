## Course Goals

### Goals:

*Fundamentals of Air Pollution:* Purpose of this class: A first, “survey” course in air pollution for the undergraduate major and for any engineer interested in the impacts of civilization on the natural environment and public health by having a fundamental understanding of the atmosphere. This class will present an overview of the field (what is air pollution?) seek to point out the physical and chemical principles that govern the interaction of anthropogenic activity (what people do) and the environment, and show how these principles are used to predict, prevent, and restore the deleterious effects of air pollution.

*The specific areas in which and upon which environmental engineers work include:*

- And airborne environments including outdoor and indoor air, effluent gases from chemical and industrial processes, and gaseous contaminants emitted during the burning of fuels.

*The kinds of pollutants environmental engineers deal with include:*

Flows of material, which may be

- soluble and/or particulate
- inert and/or biological
- infectious and/or hazardous
- concentrated and/or dilute
- natural and/or man-made
- reactive and/or recalcitrant
- volatile, corrosive, explosive, radioactive, infectious, noxious, expensive
- nutrient rich, high-energy, beneficial cheap

### Other Course Policies:

***Homework:*** Homework and written assignments are due in class at the **beginning** of lecture. Class assignments turned in late will be penalized 10% per day or fraction thereof. Exceptions to this rule will be made only by previous consent to the instructor.

***End of Lecture Problem:*** A problem will be presented at the end of each lecture to be solved prior to the end of the lecture. The class will be allowed 10-15 minutes to solve the problem. Lecture notes and the book are allowed during this task. No cell phones or computers (or access to the internet is allowed). The purpose of this is to gently enforce the reading of the book prior to the lecture. The problem can be mathematical, analytical thinking, or concept driven. This will start September 2nd.

***Chemical Project:***

***Undergraduate Students:*** During the semester, individuals will give a presentation on a selected chemical (see the instructor). Each individual will select a contaminant and discuss its environmental significance in air. You should discuss your choice of chemical with the instructor during the first two weeks of classes. This is mandatory and will be part of your grade. Each presentation should describe the compound’s (i) chemical characteristics, (ii) its environmental sources or chemical reactions, (iii) toxicity, (iv) significance, (v) legislation/regulation, (vi) concentration in the air, and (vii) techniques to minimize the chemical. Each presentation should last around 30 minutes including a question period. Grading will be based on 10% (you did what you were suppose to do), 8% (you did most of it correctly), 5% (you tried) and 0% (you did not do it oryour presentation did not last more than 30 minutes). **The presentations have to be done with Prezi.** If you don’t know Prezi, familiarize yourself well before the presentation date.

***Graduate Students:*** During the semester, individual presentations on predetermined and approved subjects will be delivered in the form of oral presentations. You should discuss your choice of topic with the instructor **during the first two weeks of classes.** This is mandatory and will be part of your grade. Each presentation should last at least 45 minutes. Grading will be based on criteria that will be provided beforehand.

***Requirements:*** This course requires an individual performance and collaboration with the instructor. YOU WILL NEED TO READ THE BOOK! Your prompt attendance is required at all lectures and possible field trips. If you cannot attend a scheduled session for some reason, it is requested that you notify the instructor to make appropriate arrangements.

***Learning:*** The learning process is iterative, can be slow, and will be sometimes frustrating. Steady sustained effort is required to make progress. In addition to normal class hours, you are expected to spend about 7 hours each week on the course.

***Computers:*** Computer literacy is a necessity in the engineering profession. This class will stress the use of computer spreadsheets as simple but powerful modeling and analysis tools. Learning by doing is rather easy if you have not had formal training. I have no preferences --- use Lotus, Exel, Quattro, Sigma Plot etc. There is good access to spreadsheets and word processing packages on RCS workstations, on PCs and Macs at various locations around the campus. Please, contact me if you have any problems!!!!

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

***In general:*** Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts, which violate this trust, undermine the educational process. Therefore, in this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. All individual assignments must be done and submitted by each individual student. In cases where teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration and each member receives the same grade. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with the guidelines. Therefore, submission of any assignment that includes plagiarism and/or other forms of cheating is serious violations of the trust between student and teacher, and among other students. Penalties for such violations are severe and will result in a failing (F) grade for the assignment. Repeating violations will result in a failing (F) grade in the course.

***More in detail:*** These statements are intended to cover most, but not all, forms of academic dishonesty. (You should refer to *The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* for detailed information on Institute policy in this regard). You can find the RPI Student Handbook of Rights and Responsibilities online at http://www.rpi.edu/dept/doso/2008-2010RPIStudentHandbook.pdf. Please, pay close attention to the different forms listed.

***Homework:*** Collaboration among the students in the class is permitted and, in fact, encouraged, as described above. Seeking help from other sources is also encouraged. However, the work you submit should be that of yours. Evidence of your merely transcribing another person’s homework will result in a zero grade for the homework assignment in question, **for all parties involved**. A second offense will result in a zero grade for homework for the entire semester **for all parties involved**, with a report made to the Dean of Students Office.

***Exams:*** Collaboration during an exam (exchanging verbal or written information or copying) is, of course, prohibited. Also prohibited is the use of unauthorized materials during an examination. In addition, the use of email (including IM) or the Internet during exams for any purpose is strictly prohibited. All parties involved will receive an academic penalty, which will range from a numerical grade of zero for the exam to a failing grade in the course, deemed by the instructor to be appropriate to the infraction. Submission of an exam for re-grading after having changed an answer will result in a similar academic penalty. All incidents will be reported to the Dean of Students.

***Mobile Devices:*** All mobile devices (cell/smart phones, computers, pagers, etc.) must be stored securely away during lecture and are not be used unless specifically directed otherwise by the instructor. Use of (or **ANY** interaction with) a mobile device during an exam without explicit permission of the instructor will be interpreted as the illicit transfer of exam data, will be considered an act of cheating and will be treated as such.

***Other:*** Outrageous offenses such as arranging for a substitute to take an examination, attempting to obtain examination questions or solutions in advance, or attempting to change a grade record will result in a failing grade for the course and a recommendation to the Dean of Students that the student(s) in question be suspended. Any written assignments that are graded will be checked for cheating and plagiarism. If cheating and/or plagiarism are found, an academic penalty will be given. This penalty will range from a numerical grade of zero for the exam to a failing grade in the course, deemed by the instructor to be appropriate to the infraction.

**NOTE:**

**[1]** **Any** violation will be reported to the Dean of Students using the SoE form. **No exceptions!**

**[2] An academic dishonesty** agreement will be provided first class period and the form is required to be signed by each student.
