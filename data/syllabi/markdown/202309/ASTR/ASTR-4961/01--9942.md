## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Cosmology is the study of the Universe as a whole. In this course we will try to answer the following questions (among others): What are the contents of the Universe? How has the history of the Universe looked until now? How will the Universe evolve from here? How come one location in the Universe is not more special than another? And how come one direction is not more special than another?

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- To be able to derive the Friedman equations employing general relativity.
- Understand how the dynamics of the universe depend on its contents.
- To be able to use methods statistical mechanics to analyse equilibrium processes such as neutrino decoupling in the early universe
- Understand the WIMP miracle.
- Understand the formation process of the light element during Big Bang Nucleosynthesis.
- Understand the problems with standard hot big band cosmology.
- To understand how cosmic inflation solves the above problems.
- To be able analyse different models of cosmic inflation.
- Understand conceptually the theory of reheating.
- To be able to quantitatively analyse the cosmological perturbations that lead to structure formation.
- Understand how the cosmological perturbations originate from inflation.
- Understand how physical processes and quantities determine the shape of the CMB power spectrum.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- 0. There will be a reading assignment in advance of every class period. In order to receive credit for the reading assignment, the student has to submit a question on the topic to the instructor through LMS no later than 9:00 pm the day before the scheduled class time. The question has to be substantial, e.g. ”Can you explain equation XYZ?” will not receive credit. The reading assignment will account for 20% of the final grade.

  1. There will be weekly homework assignments. The homework assignments will be due at the beginning of class on the due date. Homework solutions are to be submitted on paper. In general, late homework will not be accepted. The homework will account for 40% of the final grade.  
     The reading and homework assignments are crucial in mastering the course material. The lowest of each of your homework and reading assignment will be dropped to compute your final grade in the course. Therefore, if you miss one homework assignment/reading question, then this will become the lowest grade that gets dropped. There is one exception to this rule; if a grade of zero is received as a result of academic dishonesty, then that grade will not be dropped. There will be no make-ups or extensions for homework or reading questions.

  2. Term Project:

     In lieu of exams there will be a term project. The students are required to write a review paper (10 pages minimum, excluding references, LATEX, APS format, template will be provided) on a cosmol- ogy topic of their choice. The difficulty level should be comparable to the textbook and contain a calculation, derivation, simulation, etc. The students are expected to work on this throughout the semester. An initial choice of topic must be made by October 6 and a one page proposal is due on October 15. The students are expected to consult with the instructor (early and often) to discuss possible topics, approaches, general progress, and get help with any problems and issues along the way.

     The project is due on the last day of class, December 8. During the last class meeting (December 8) we will have a “gong show” where the students will present their projects to the class in ∼5 minutes. The term project accounts for 40% of the final grade. (Meeting of the deadlines, time management, and progress will also be considered when assigning a grade to the term project, as well as quality of the submitted work and the presentation.)
