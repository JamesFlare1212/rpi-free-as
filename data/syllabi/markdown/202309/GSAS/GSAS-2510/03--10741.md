## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Intro to Game Design is a course that explores the basic theory and practical aspects of game design. This exploration extends to various forms of games and their cultural significance. The core methodology in this class involves crafting playable games through a rapid prototyping and iterative design process. While the course focuses on the creation of non-digital games, discussions about digital games will also take place. The coursework comprises several short assignments along with a final project. Each assignment requires students to write a design statement, explaining the game’s concept, gameplay mechanics, and overall design approach. For the final project, students will select and further develop a project that was previously completed in the class by refining its design and presentation.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

- Cultivate an understanding of what games are, both digital and analogue, as well as their mechanics that contribute to the formation of meaningful player experiences.
- Be familiar with the basic methodologies and conceptual skills of game design, including system and economic design, prototyping, playtesting, design iteration, and critique.
- Develop a collaborative mindset marked by a positive attitude, effective communication abilities, and a receptive mind for engaging with feedback.
- Forge connections between the course concepts and broader contexts, incorporating the dynamics of professional game industries as well as media and culture that are beyond games.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will make playable tabletop games from scratch.
- Students will understand and apply the iterative process of design.
- Students will know how to collect data for improving their games.
- Students will be able to pitch and present their games in front of the public.
- Students will be able to collaborate and communicate in teams.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students' learning outcomes will be assessed based on their projects, presentations, communication, and collaboration.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Plagiarism**  
Plagiarism is defined as representing the work or words of another as one's own through the omission of acknowledgement or reference. Examples include using sentences verbatim from a published source in a term paper without appropriate referencing, or presenting as one's own the detailed argument of a published source, or presenting as one's own electronically or digitally enhanced graphic representations from any form of media.
