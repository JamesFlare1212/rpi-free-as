## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

An introductory course focused on acquiring fundamental arts skills, specifically foundational skills necessary to conceptualize, develop, and/or create visual assets and elements for multimedia applications.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

*Drawing Basics and Video Game Art,* by Chris Solarski

**ISBN** 978-0-8230-9847-7

Additional handouts provided are based on *Rapid Viz*, by Kurt Hanks and Larry Belliston

## Course Goals

### Goals:

To introduce to the student the fundamentals of art that are necessary to successfully conceptualize, develop, and create visual assets and other elements for interactive media; intended to initiate, encourage, and facilitate the acquisition of foundational art skills, which is a lifelong process.  
By the end of the course, the student should have not only a working understanding of these fundamentals, but to achieve a basic level of skill in the following:

- Drawing of Visual Forms; including Line, Shape, Structure, Proportion, Mass, Dimension
- Implementing fundamental applications of Light; including Tone/Value, and basic Color
- Perspective
- Drawing from Life
- Gesture and Anatomy

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Identify the fundamental skills needed to create recognizable, usable, and relevant visual elements
- Draw from visual observation with reasonable accuracy, utilizing perspective, lighting, value, depth, proportion.
- Sketch landscape and architecture on location
- Represent the human figure, with accurate proportions, anatomy, depth and form
- Identify the elements and principles of basic design and create a visual layout that correctly utilizes these elements and principles
- Create a scene from imagination and /or reference using the fundamental skills of line, form, shape, structure, proportion, mass, tone, value, and color.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- Daily sketching and in-class exercises
- Discussions, posts, short responses
- Life studies
- Reproduce master copies of an artist of student choice
- Workbook assignments
- Final project: reimaging art from a game

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.
