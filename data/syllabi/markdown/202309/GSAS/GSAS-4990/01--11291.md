## Course Goals

### Goals:

- Encourage project organization skills.
- Encourage self-motivation and time management.
- Encourage development experience.
- Complete meaningful portfolio piece.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

This is a studio class. Students respond to a brief, and correspond with the instructor through weekly one-to-one sessions to report on progress and plan for the week ahead, and communicate throughout the week to discuss development problems and opportunities to help navigate towards the final project goal.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Learn project management techniques, and the capacity to recalibrate project goals.
- • Learn self-motivation techniques.
- • Learn development techniques related to areas of skill specialty.
- • Learn to accommodate, respond to, and act on feedback.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- 50% of the grade will be awarded halfway through the semester.
- 50% of the grade will be awarded at the end of the semester.

Grading will be awarded through the one-to-one meetings at the appropriate stage in the semester.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

This is a project-based course, and all projects are likely highly distinct and have their own unique features. Grading is achieved through assessment of the work done by specified dates in line with the project objectives, bearing in mind that these projects might be in a variety of formats, of differing degrees of complexity, from differing start-points, with some being solo developer projects, and others being projects for large teams, and many being somewhere in between. Therefore criteria is not set, but tailored specifically to each project.

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
