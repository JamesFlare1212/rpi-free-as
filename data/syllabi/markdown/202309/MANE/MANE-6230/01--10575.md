## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Description**: *Advanced Manufacturing Processes and Systems* (AMPS) is a research project-based lecture/laboratory course that teaches graduate engineering students how to develop new manufacturing processes and systems. During this semester, you will:

- Receive in-depth theoretical and practical knowledge (including individual assignment and team-based laboratory exercise) in four selected topic areas in advanced manufacturing processes/systems including: Micro/nano manufacturing; additive manufacturing; advanced composites and digital manufacturing
- Be organized into one of four student teams that will work on either an advanced manufacturing R&D project for an industrial collaborator or develop a manufacturing lab exercise and
- Write a final report for your team project and present methods, results and conclusions to a review panel made up of course instructors and invited guests from industry.

**Note:** MEng students should particularly take note that this is a research project-centric 6000-level course that will feel different from their usual undergraduate course experience.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Learn the underlying theory, technology, and standard industrial practice for selected advanced manufacturing processes and systems
- 2\. Perform a hands-on laboratory exercise pertaining to the selected advanced manufacturing processes and systems
- 3\. Learn how to organize, lead, and manage advanced manufacturing research and development projects using an effective structured approach
- 4\. Design, analyze, and prototype a new advanced manufacturing process and/or system through a team-based project
- 5\. Demonstrate the new process and/or manufacturing system

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
