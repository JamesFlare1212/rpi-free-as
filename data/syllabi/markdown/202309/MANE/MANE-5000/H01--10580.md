### How to Approach This Course

This course will be conducted using Canvas.

Students should be familiar with its use to obtain and deliver assignments (the first icebreaker activities can be used for testing).

- Each week, a new module is assigned.
- Each module contains:
  - Reading assignments
  - Discussion forum statement to address directly and reply to others once.
  - Conceptual module questions and numerical application problem(s).
- The reading assignment will include:
  - Module class notes,
  - Suggested text chapter.
- To address the discussion forum open statement, a simple one or two sentences, or a short paragraph may be required. It is also required to reply once to others for class participation credit.
- As part of the module assignment, conceptual questions will be provided for each module.
- As part of the module assignment, the homework statement will be provided for each module along with the reference approach for problem solution.
- Two projects or tests will be provided for credit:
  - Mid-semester, and
  - End-of-semester.

**Course Organization**

Online content will be delivered through Canvas system.

Each week of the semester will be given a module. Every module will be of the same format, as follows:

1. Reading assignment (Module class notes and text reading)
2. Discussion statement (to be addressed and replied, with 1 post minimum reply),
3. Assignments (conceptual questions and homework problem(s))

The reading session will include Module class notes with text chapter sessions to further your understanding of the subject.

The Discussion session will include: an address gate which will require answering with simple, one or two sentences, or even a small paragraph; followed by a reply gate to other students, with 1 post reply to minimum for class participation credit. The assignment will include conceptual questions and homework.

Lastly, “Ask the Instructor” folder is an optional discussion board where students can ask questions. Discussions will be open for all students to see and participate.

### Additional Course Description :

### Course Description:

An introductory graduate course in advanced engineering mathematics. This course provides the knowledge necessary to study engineering applications consistent with our physical laws. Topics include: differential equations, linear systems, vector calculus, series solutions, and partial differential equations.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- ### Evaluation Criteria

  **Assignments: Conceptual Questions (20%) and Homework (20%)**

  Conceptual questions and homework will be submitted online via Canvas weekly.

  Both questions and homework answers should be submitted as a pdf document with the following naming convention:
  - **LastName_FirstName_Discussion_X.pdf**
  - **LastName_FirstName_Homework_X.pdf**

  where X stands for module number (i.e., X=1 for module 1, X=2 for module 2 etc.)

  Any scanned documents should be correctly oriented and easily read.

  **Discussion Board (2 address and reply to gates @ 15% each = 30 %)**

  Discussion board interactive questions will be found in the “Discussion” folder. Discussion boards will be used to facilitate student-student communication as well as student-instructor communication. It is expected that every student participates in these group discussions. Your participation in the discussion boards will be a major factor in determining your class participation grade, which is 30% of your final grade. The discussion address gate amounts to 15%; whereas the discussion reply gate amounts to 15%, for a total of 30% as completed class participation. Remember, the reply gate requires 1 post reply to minimum for credit. Participation in discussions may come in many forms. Examples of participation may include, but are not limited to:
  - raising a question
  - answering the question of a classmate
  - explaining a step required to solve the problem.
  - explaining the process for solving the problem
  - commenting on the post of a classmate
  - guiding your classmates to the proper equations or section of the textbook
  - providing a numerical answer to a part of the problem
  - providing an expression necessary for solving the problem
  - providing a schematic to add clarity to the problem.
  - Simple interactions like: “I agree” or similar will not be sufficient and will not be considered as relevant participation in discussion.

  **Rules for posting:**
  - You may not post a complete solution to the problem, even if it contains only variables.
  - Simple interactions like: “I agree” or similar will not be sufficient and will not be considered as relevant participation in discussion.
  - Extra postings which are not related to the subject matter are totally prohibited (examples may include propaganda, personal views/comments not related to the subject matter).

  Discussion boards will be used to facilitate student-student communication and exposure to different perspectives. The format of each discussion board question will vary. In general, responses should adhere to all standard writing, grammar, and punctuation rules. Abbreviations or “lingo” commonly used in texting is not acceptable.

  Discussion board questions will not have a single “correct” answer and may intentionally be left vague (open) to allow for variety in the responses. In some discussion board questions, I will try to better understand how you are thinking about the material. Generally speaking, the instructor will provide the path for the best solution coming from different points of view. Thus, you will not lose points for wrong answers. You will only lose points if you do not put forth a good faith effort or ignore the instructions. Otherwise, I encourage you to think and express yourself freely.

  Your class participation grade will be based on the level and quality of involvement in the class. The goal for the class is that, through participation, students not only have their own learning experience, but can see and be part of the learning experiences of their classmates. Therefore, I would like to see everyone participate in online discussions, promote a positive learning environment, and work together with your classmates. Much of this participation will occur in the discussion boards. In general, if students actively participate in these activities, they will receive 30%. However, I reserve the right to give less than 30% for students who are blatantly apathetic to discussions and/or the group nature of the class.

  **Projects (2 @ 15% each = 30 %)**

  To maximize the effectiveness, two projects will be take-home assignments to be completed per deadlines. These projects are to be written independently. You are free to use your notes, textbook, and class materials.

  **Deadline Penalty****:** The student may incur a 20% credit deduction if the assignments are submitted a week past the deadline. This deduction may increase to 40% after the first week past the deadline. After the 3rd week, the assignment will not be graded (0 credit). This applies to all assignments, projects, and class participation.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity and Rules of Behavior**

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Dean of Students office website defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs). and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. Of course, learning with and collaborating with others in the class is encouraged – work submitted must be completely your own and original.  
Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy may result in a penalty of failure of the assignment or of the course, depending on the severity. A grade of zero will be given on the first assignment where a violation of the policies described above is detected, and a referral to the Dean of Graduate Education. If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of “F” for the course and referral to the Dean. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

**Instructor Note**

I do not expect that we will incur any issues with academic integrity. With that being said, if such issues do arise, the proper measures, laid out in the student handbook and discussed previously, will be taken. Any form of copying other students reports during a take project is prohibited and will result in an F for the test. Violations will be reported immediately to the Dean of Students Office.

**A Special Note Regarding the Use of Solution Manuals**

The use of solution manuals to solve assignments is prohibited. Using solutions manuals is considered plagiarism and/or copying and is in direct violation of the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities, as well as the Graduate Student supplement to the handbook. All assignments showing evidence of the use of solution manuals will receive an F grade and, under my discretion, may also be reported to the Dean of Students Office.
