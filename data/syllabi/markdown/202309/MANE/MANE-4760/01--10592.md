## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

What will you learn?

1. Fundamental thermodynamic and psychrometric processes \&amp; related human physiology, enabling an engineer to design a sustainable indoor environment for human comfort.
2. HVAC Components and Systems – function and performance. Application space covered includes office spaces, Chip Fabs, and institutional buildings such as University, Government, and Healthcare facilities.
3. How to design an HVAC system for an actual office space using industry standard engineering tools and practices

How will you learn?

1. 50% of the class involves lectures, practice problem sets, and related tests enabling a comprehensive understanding of the fundamental processes and system functions.
2. 50% of the class is an interactive team design project. Groups of 4-5 students will be given drawings of an actual building space and be tasked with designing a complete HVAC system. The students will have special access to local HVAC professionals who have volunteered to assist in the design projects.
3. Two special lectures from RPI graduates and current HVAC professionals: 1. HVAC Careers 2. Sustainable Sports Facility Designs \&amp; HVAC Design of the U.S. Open Tennis tournament during the Covid-19 epidemic

What opportunities does the class enable?

1. Sustainability: Impact one of the most energy intensive and CO 2 emitting sources – according to the U.S. DOE building use is association with 66% of all electricity use, 36% of all energy use, and 35% of CO 2 emissions.
2. Career acceleration: 3-6 students each year enter the HVAC industry. Taking the class puts you in a strong position as a potential employee and connects you to firms looking for new hires.
3. Entrepreneur: HVAC professionals often become Engineering Firm Partners – a relatively unique engineering entrepreneurial opportunity.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. The students that finish this course in a satisfactory manner will be able to demonstrate: An ability to formulate and solve basic HVAC engineering problems
2. The students that finish this course in a satisfactory manner will be able to demonstrate: methods for determining HVAC heating and cooling loads as well as size HVAC piping and ducting.
3. The students that finish this course in a satisfactory manner will be able to demonstrate: the knowledge of HVAC heating and cooling systems and associated equipment.
