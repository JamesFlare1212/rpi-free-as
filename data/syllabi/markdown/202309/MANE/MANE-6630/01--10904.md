### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

**Course Description:**

This course is an introduction to the mathematics of analyzing conduction heat transfer. The focus is on the heat diffusion equation and the appropriate methods for solving it. In the first part of this course, we will develop analytical solutions to the heat diffusion equation for steady-state, transient, and multidimensional problems. In the second part, we will study numerical methods for solving more complex heat conduction problems for which analytical solutions are impractical.

## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Objectives as the Instructor:**

The objectives of this course include developing a fundamental understanding of heat transfer by conduction, successfully analyzing complex heat transfer problems by dividing them into simpler subsets, and learning various analytical and computational methods to solve multidimensional and transient diffusion problems. In this course, the students learn to derive the equations governing thermal transport by diffusion using conservation laws. In doing so, the concepts from thermodynamics, heat transfer, and fluid flow are used. Besides, the importance of understanding diffusion transport is emphasized in Cartesian, Cylindrical, and Spherical coordinate systems, allowing students to appropriately select a coordinate system for a given diffusion problem. The course discusses various analytical techniques since the governing equations for heat diffusion can vary significantly based on the given problem, which includes learning Fourier series, Bessel functions, and Legendre polynomials to solve diffusion transport. To address more complex problems and geometries, they learn the applicability of the superposition method and the use of Duhamel’s Theorem for handling time-dependent parameters and discontinuities. Apart from deriving the analytical solution, interpreting the analytical solution using graphs and plots is equally essential. To do this, the students could use MATLAB extensively for calculations involving infinite series and plotting their solutions to gain physical insight into diffusion transport. The course encourages using computational tools for analyzing complex geometries and problems. The tools may include ABAQUS, ANSYS, and COMSOL. The course incorporates research activity in the final project. The students pick a research topic of current interest involving diffusion transport. The course involves writing project proposals, reports, and technical presentations. This comprehensive approach to teaching is expected to prepare the graduate students for a career in industry and academia.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Course Outline:**

*Conduction Heat Transfer* will be taught to cover the topics listed below. In order for all students to develop a good understanding, some topics are discussed in greater detail than the rest. The course may also replace some topics below or add discussion relevant to conduction heat transfer.

1. Introduction to Heat Conduction
   1. Differential Equation of Heat Conduction (Cartesian, Cylindrical, and Spherical)
   2. Initial and Boundary Conditions
   3. Lumped Analysis
   4. Anisotropic Medium
2. Introduction to Analytical Methods for Heat Conduction Problems
   1. Orthogonal Functions
   2. Sturm-Liouville Problem
3. Separation of Variables in the Rectangular Coordinate System
   1. Basic Concepts
   2. Solving a Steady-State Problem in a Rectangular Coordinate System
   3. Method of Superposition
   4. Solving a Transient Problem in a Rectangular Coordinate System
4. Separation of Variables in the Cylindrical Coordinate System
   1. Basic Concepts
   2. Solving a Steady-State Problem in a Cylindrical Coordinate System
   3. Solving a Transient Problem in a Cylindrical Coordinate System
5. Separation of Variables in the Spherical Coordinate System
   1. Basic Concepts
   2. Solving a Steady-State Problem in a Spherical Coordinate System
   3. Solving a Transient Problem in a Spherical Coordinate System
6. Use of Duhamel’s Theorem
   1. Development of Duhamel’s Theorem for Continuous Time-dependent Boundary Conditions
   2. Treatment of Discontinuities
   3. General Statement of Duhamel’s Theorem
   4. Applications of Duhamel’s Theorem
7. Numerical Methods for Solving Heat Conduction Problems
   1. Introduction to Numerical Methods
   2. Using Abaqus, Comsol, and Ansys for Heat Conduction

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Homework (25%):**

Homework is meant to be a learning experience, not a regurgitation of previously learned concepts. Problems sets will elucidate the finer points of applying the fundamental theory that is difficult to convey in lectures, and students should pay close attention to the patterns and concepts that arise in homework that are not discussed in lectures.

**Class Participation (5%):**

Your class participation grade will be based on the level and quality of involvement in the class. The goal of the class is that, through participation, students not only have their own learning experience but can see and be part of the learning experiences of their classmates.

**Exams (2 @ 20% each):**

The exams are to be completed independently of anyone else. You can use notes, textbooks, and any class materials; however, **you may not communicate with anyone else regarding the exam’s content**. This includes coworkers, friends, fellow students, and even other professors. I do not expect that we will incur any issues with academic integrity. If such issues arise, the proper measures in the student handbook will be taken. Any form of communication with others during an exam is prohibited and will result in an F for the test. Both types of violations may be reported immediately.

**Final Project (30%):**

The final project will consist of an in-depth conduction analysis completed with finite element (FEA) software. Students will propose their projects and may use any available FEA software after approval from the instructor. Work on projects will begin during the course. A project proposal (5%) is the first related assignment and will describe the physical problem, background, objectives, and expected results. In order to ensure continued progress, two progress reports (intermediate-5% and final-10%) should be submitted describing the progress (and outlining the remaining steps in the case of the intermediate report). In addition to the final report, students will present their results in a formal presentation (10%). The presentation (15 minutes total) will be graded based on the peer (5%) and instructor (5%) evaluations.
