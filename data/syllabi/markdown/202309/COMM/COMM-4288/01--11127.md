## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- At the end of this course, students should be able to: • Demonstrate knowledge of concepts and theories relevant to communicating health information.
- • Evaluate the appropriateness of a variety of communication strategies for a given topic/issue/population.
- • Identify elements of campaign design important for influencing individual and population health.
- • Evaluate the effectiveness of health campaigns.
- • Demonstrate spoken and written communication competence through the final project.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Course Assignments:**

1. **Reading Responses and Discussions 40%**

Each student will sign up for two readings, write reading responses, and lead the discussions in class. Students will prepare 8-15 slides for each reading response that should *1) provide a brief summary of the article, 2) challenge or support the authors’ methodology, reasoning, or thesis, 3) identify exciting/interesting points raised by the author(s), 4) include two memorable quotes from the paper, and 5) raise two or three thought-provoking questions to prompt the discussions in class.* ***Students should complete the 1st Reading response before the end of 9/25, and the second reading response before the end of 11/27.***

****Please submit your reading response (slides) on LMS Blackboard before noon on your presentation day.***

2. **Module Exercises 30%**

Module exercises will be used to help you understand the topics and prepare the health campaign plan and message designs. These will include, but may not be limited to, writing/speaking assignments, message design, paired assignments, or group assignments. Please be prepared to participate in these activities as well as class discussions. The assignments missed due to absence can't be made up, unless in case of emergency that can be proved by official documents. Please do not ask for an excused absence unless there are exceptional circumstances.

3. **Health Communication Campaign Plan &Presentation 20%**

Each student will write up a health communication campaign plan (slides) including the following components and give a presentation in class:

1. Planning and strategy development: research on a health problem, why a communication program can help, intended audiences, objectives, and communication strategies.
2. Developing materials: Design activities or messages (poster, slides, graphics, or games) and pretest the messages.
3. Implementation: Discuss how you will distribute messages to your intended audiences.
4. Assessments: evaluate your health promotion message and identify refinements.

4. **Attendance/Participation 10%**

Attendance/participation grade is not “free” points automatically given just for showing up. It must be earned. This grade is calculated based on ***the quality and quantity of your responses to discussion questions***, ***your participation in class activities***, and ***your attendance to the class meetings requested by the instructor.***

- A grade: Student always arrives to class on time and stays for the duration of class; is always engaged and attentive throughout the class period; regularly and actively responds to questions and participates in class discussions; never displays disruptive and/or disrespectful behavior during class; and consistently arrives to class prepared to discuss the assigned readings.
- B grade: Student sometimes arrives to class late and/or does not stay for the duration of class; is sometimes disengaged and/or distracted during class period; responds to questions and participates in class discussion, but not regularly; rarely displays disruptive and/or disrespectful behavior during class; and usually arrives to class prepared to discuss the assigned readings.
- C grade: Student often arrives to class late and/or does not stay for the duration of class; does not always listen to others’ ideas and opinions; rarely participates in class discussion; occasionally displays disruptive and/or disrespectful behavior in class; and rarely arrives to class prepared to discuss the assigned readings.
- D grade: Student rarely arrives to class on time and/or stays for the duration of class; often interrupts when other students speak; never participates in class discussion; almost always displays disruptive and/or disrespectful behavior during class; and almost never arrives to class prepared to discuss the assigned readings.
- E/F grade: Student always arrives to class late and/or does not stay for the duration of class; always interrupts when other students speak; shows obvious lack of willingness to participate in class; always displays disruptive and disrespectful behavior during class; and never arrives to class prepared to discuss the assigned readings.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
