## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course explores major approaches to studying––and debates about stopping––the spread of dis/misinformation, “fake news,” and conspiracy theories in media. Topics we will consider include: news gathering and publishing practices in both legacy and digital journalism, including fact checking; public relations and marketing; pre-digital “fake news” and conspiracy theories; propaganda and authoritarianism; and “media manipulation” and dis/misinformation on social media platforms.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Students will learn foundational historical and emerging approaches to propaganda and mis/disinformation research and practice.
- 2\. Students will be able to discuss and to write about how we create and are created by the visual media environment that we inhabit.
- 3\. Students will learn key concepts in the study of visual culture and be able to produce their own visually based project engaging these concepts.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Evaluation (COMM 6961 students, please note the slightly different requirements):**

- **Class Participation (10%)**

Course Participation includes participation in class discussions, exercises, and assignments.

- **Mis/Disinformation Example Responses (20% – 5 total over the course of the semester at 4% each)**

You will find and briefly describe an example of mis/disinformation, whether historical or contemporary, and explain how the readings or lectures for the week help illuminate the example, or how the example supports, complicates, or refutes the readings/lectures. You may only complete one response for credit per week, beginning Week 2. Responses should be about 250 words for COMM 4961 and 500 words for students in COMM 6961.

- **Two Papers (40% total, 20% each – 4-6 pages double spaced)**

Prompts and further details will be provided. You will have the option to propose your own topic (with instructor approval). ***Graduate-level students enrolled in COMM 6961** must write papers of 8-10 pages and must include at least two scholarly sources not listed on the syllabus.

- **Group Project and Presentation (30%)**

Option 1: map a case of mis/disinformation spread

Option 2: design a disinformation campaign

Further details will be provided later in the semester.

***Graduate-level students enrolled in COMM 6961** may either participate in a group project or complete an individually authored final paper of 15 pages addressing either option in consultation with the instructor.

### Attendance Policy:

Because class discussion contributes significantly to the educational goals and outcomes of this course, attendance is a requirement. Four unexcused absences without justification (such as illness, a funeral, athletic event, job interview) after the first week of classes may result in a reduction of the final grade by a full letter. Further additional unexcused absences may result in further letter grade reductions. If you are approaching four unexcused absences, or are experiencing any difficulties with attendance, please don’t hesitate to speak with me. ### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
