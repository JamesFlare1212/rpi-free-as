## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is designed to provide international TAs with communication training so that they are able to present academic information clearly, interact effectively with students, practice classroom management skills, accommodate different learning styles, and understand cross-cultural aspects of teaching and learning.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

*2. Software:* NativeAccent

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate knowledge and competency in pronunciation, spoken grammar, fluency, and listening skills in North American English
- Develop presentation styles that meet the needs of different types of learners
- Explain concepts in their field to listeners without expertise in the area
- Gauge undergraduate students’ (audience) comprehension and respond to questions effectively
- Lead engaging class discussion that contribute to student learning
- Understand cultural differences and how they impact teaching and learning
- Detect elements of speech that make their speech unintelligible to listeners and set goals for ongoing improvement

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Evaluation**

Native Accent 30%

Teaching Demos 30%

Leading a Class Activity 10%

Preparedness and Participation 15%

Homework 15%

1. **Native Accent- 30%**

Each TA’s speaking skills will be different. NativeAccent is a program designed to target students’ individual needs in the areas of English pronunciation, grammar, word stress, and fluency. Your grade in this area will be based on 1) your engagement (how often, and for how much time, you use NativeAccent) and 2) your improvement throughout the semester.

2. **Teaching Demos- 30%**

You will have two teaching demonstrations throughout the semester. These teaching demonstrations will be short mini-presentations to the class, in the style of a lecture you would deliver as a course instructor. The purpose of these teaching demos is to practice your public speaking in a lecture type class, apply the skills we are working on in the course, and build confidence and expertise discussing the content of your subject matter knowledge.

3. **Leading a** **Class** **Activity****- 10%**

You will lead one class activity during the semester. This can be a lab type activity, or a discussion. When you are not the leader, you will be expected to participate in the discussions as a student. The purpose of this class discussion is to practice your ability to lead a student centered class, apply the skills we are working on in the course, and build confidence in interacting with and responding appropriately to students.

4. **Preparedness and Participation- 1****5****%**

Students will be expected to attend class, come prepared for class, and actively participate in class throughout the semester. Students may lose points in this category if they do not attend class or attend class but are unengaged and do not participate.

5. **Homework- 1****5****%**

A few short homework assignments will be given throughout the semester.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance Policy**

Students are expected to attend on time and actively participate in all classes. As students cannot participate well in class without being present, students will be expected to be physically in class and participate actively to receive full points for participation.

There are a total of 200 participation points; 5 points for each 50 minutes of class. To receive an A grade in participation (full marks), students must receive at least 170 points. These classes can be missed for any reason without any documentation.

If a student has a prolonged illness or other circumstance that would cause them to miss more classes, please see the instructor and special arrangements may be made.

Students arriving after attendance has been called will lose 1 point for the day (if they fully participate while in the class). Students arriving more than 10 minutes late – will lose 5 points for the day (if they fully participate while in the class), and students arriving more than 1 hour 10 minutes late will not receive any points that day.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. In cases in which outside sources are used, students are expected to paraphrase, summarize, or quote and cite these sources properly following the citation style introduced in the class. Plagiarism will not be tolerated. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty. If found in violation of the academic honesty policy, students may be subject to two types of penalty. The instructor administers an academic [grade] penalty and the student is reported to the Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education as appropriate. The first violation results in 0 grade for that assignment. The second violation results in failure of the course. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
