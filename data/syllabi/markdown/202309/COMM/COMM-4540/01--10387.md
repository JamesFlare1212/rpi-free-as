## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will give you tools to critically examine the barrage of images we encounter daily and understand how these images affect us emotionally as individuals, how they influence the way we think and understand our reality, and in turn, how we conduct our lives together in the world. We’ll examine a broad range of visual media from photographs, art, television, and film to social media platforms and new media technologies (e.g., YouTube, TikTok and VR). We’ll also spend some time examining how “visuality” is experienced and expressed for those who are blind or visually disabled, and how visual media plays a role in witnessing traumatic events and their political and cultural implications.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Students will be able to analyze visual images in terms of their cultural meaning and affective impact.
- 2\. Students will be able to discuss and to write about how we create and are created by the visual media environment that we inhabit.
- 3\. Students will learn key concepts in the study of visual culture and be able to produce their own visually based project engaging these concepts.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Evaluation:**

- **Class Participation (10%)**

Course Participation includes participation in class discussions, exercises, and assignments.

- **Short Example Responses (15% – 5 total over the course of the semester at 3% each, about 150 words)**

You will find and briefly describe an example, whether historical or contemporary, and explain concepts from the readings and/or lectures for the week help illuminate the example, or how the example supports, complicates, or refutes the concepts. *You may only complete one response for credit per week, beginning Week 2, and may not complete them the same week you complete the Longer Example Response.*

- **One Longer Example Response (5% ­– two opportunities, 1.5-2 pages double spaced)**

For this Longer Example Response, you are to select a concept from either Week 4 or Week 5 and apply it to an example of a visual practice or “text” of your choosing.

The goal of the assignment is to give you the opportunity to practice connecting the concepts to examples in progressively longer and more-focused written arguments.

- **Two Papers (50% total, 25% each – 4-6 pages double spaced)**

Prompts and further details will be provided. You will have the option to propose your own topic (with instructor approval).

- **One Group Project including presentation (20%)**

For this project, you will collaborate in groups of 3-4 to produce a visual text or series of texts highlighting an issue, offering a critique or parody, or presenting a visual representation commenting on lived experiences. Further details will be provided later in the semester.

Because class discussion contributes significantly to the educational goals and outcomes of this course, attendance is a requirement. Four unexcused absences without justification (such as illness, a funeral, athletic event, job interview) after the first week of classes may result in a reduction of the final grade by a full letter. Further additional unexcused absences may result in further letter grade reductions. If you are approaching four unexcused absences, or are experiencing any difficulties with attendance, please don’t hesitate to speak with me. Whatever the reason, you are responsible for talking to a classmate about the work done on the day you miss, for checking the LMS, and for obtaining information regarding any announcements or assignments made in class.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Evaluation:**

- **Class Participation (10%)**

Course Participation includes participation in class discussions, exercises, and assignments.

- **Short Example Responses (15% – 5 total over the course of the semester at 3% each, about 150 words)**

You will find and briefly describe an example, whether historical or contemporary, and explain concepts from the readings and/or lectures for the week help illuminate the example, or how the example supports, complicates, or refutes the concepts. *You may only complete one response for credit per week, beginning Week 2, and may not complete them the same week you complete the Longer Example Response.*

- **One Longer Example Response (5% ­– two opportunities, 1.5-2 pages double spaced)**

For this Longer Example Response, you are to select a concept from either Week 4 or Week 5 and apply it to an example of a visual practice or “text” of your choosing.

The goal of the assignment is to give you the opportunity to practice connecting the concepts to examples in progressively longer and more-focused written arguments.

- **Two Papers (50% total, 25% each – 4-6 pages double spaced)**

Prompts and further details will be provided. You will have the option to propose your own topic (with instructor approval).

**A**

Selects appropriate visual media text(s) and concepts from the course. Provides detailed evidence from the texts that is linked to a robust conceptual argument responding to the prompt. Theoretical concepts are defined, explained, and interwoven into the broader flow of the text and the specific evidence drawn from analyzing primary materials.

Is well-written and has a clear and convincing argument.

**A-**

Fulfills most of the above requirements. May not be as strong in one of the key areas of analytical evidence, theoretical concepts, integrated argument.

May have some mechanical issues that hinder the argument, or non-contextualized use of source materials. May have to connect the dots, but otherwise has a convincing argument.

**B+**

Fulfills some of the above requirements. Paper is not as strong in one or more of the key areas of analytical evidence, theoretical concepts, integrated argument.

Paper may be held back by mechanical issues, or non-contextualized use of source materials. May not have a clear thesis and convincing argument.

**B**

Fulfills some of the above requirements. Paper is not as strong in all of the key areas of analytical evidence, theoretical concepts, integrated argument.

Paper is significantly held back by mechanical issues and non-contextualized use of source materials. May not have a clear thesis and convincing argument.

**B-**

Barely fulfills the above requirements. Paper is significantly lacking in the key areas of analytical evidence, theoretical concepts, integrated argument.

Paper is significantly held back by mechanical issues and non-contextualized use of source materials. May not have a clear thesis and convincing argument.

**C+**

Does not effectively fulfill the above requirements. Paper is significantly lacking in the key areas of analytical evidence, theoretical concepts, integrated argument.

Paper is significantly held back by mechanical issues and non-contextualized use of source materials. May not have a clear thesis and convincing argument.

**C and below**

Does not meet bare minimum assignment requirements (e.g., three course readings).

- **One Group Project including presentation (20%)**

For this project, you will collaborate in groups of 3-4 to produce a visual text or series of texts highlighting an issue, offering a critique or parody, or presenting a visual representation commenting on lived experiences.

This project and presentation are worth 15% of your overall grade. The grade will take into account the quality and impact of the visual texts, as conveyed both by the texts and the explanation in the presentation.
