## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides students an introduction to research in the Communication & Media Studies discipline using quantitative, qualitative, rhetorical, and critical methodologies. It will include practical training in how to perform diverse forms of research as well as an interrogation of the ethical, political, and social dilemmas raised in and by diverse research methodologies. This course is intended to prepare students for producing senior projects and provide training that is valuable for professional careers studying user experience in communication and media.

In this course students will learn how to perform a research project from the initial prospectus through the final draft of a manuscript. Students will become familiar with research projects deploying methods such as: ethnography, conversation and discourse analysis, rhetorical criticism, critical studies, content analysis, survey research, and experimental research. Students will also become familiar with the various theories that are associated with each of these methodologies.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will become familiar with quantitative, qualitative, rhetorical, and critical research methods.
- Students will demonstrate the ability to perform independent research.
- Students will become familiar with the principles of research ethics.
- Students will demonstrate the ability to assess communication and media research.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Progressive Paper (Total 100 Points)** The largest assignment for the semester will be a progressive term paper. This term paper will consist of a series of distinct portions that will be combined to create the final term paper. -Prospectus (10 Points): The first part of the progressive paper will be the prospectus. The prospectus paper will be reviewed to verify that the assignment: 1. Is germane to communication studies. 2. Can be completed in an ethical manner. 3. Can be completed in the relatively limited time available.

-Literature Review (15 Points): The literature review will survey the existing research on the topic as well as the literature relevant to the research methods and techniques used for your project.

-Analysis and Discussion (15 Points): The analysis and discussion paper will be a first draft of the analysis and discussion sections of the paper. This section will present the data collected as a part of this study as well as your interpretations of the data and what it means for other communication scholars.

-Rough Draft Paper (20 Points): The rough draft will combine each of the progressive parts of the paper. This draft will be reviewed to provide feedback intended to improve the final grade.

-Final Draft Paper (40 Points): The final draft paper will be turned in on the last day of class. Because it is due on the day of the final there will be no feedback on this draft.

**CITI Training (20 Points)**

All students will be required to complete CITI training for the course prior to engaging in research. The course is online and will take some time to complete, so be sure to budget accordingly. When you have completed the course email me your results to receive credit. Here is the link with directions for completing the course: https://rpi.app.box.com/s/peta010dc176nhjnxk51b1u2i7xf8c03

Note: This training is recognized as a valuable skill for many employers and when completed can be listed on your resume/CV.

**Reading Quizzes (50 Points)**

Over the course of the semester there will be a series of short answer reading questions. Each one of these questions will be worth either five (5) or ten (10) points depending on the length and significance of the question.

**Article Analysis (30 Points)**

Students will be tasked with collecting and analyzing three articles in an area of interest for them within the field of communication and media. Students will receive up to ten (10) points for each article which is analyzed. Grades will be awarded based on the quality of the writing and analysis of the articles.

**Grades and Grade Disputes**

Grades are determined based on the quality of the work done by the individual student measured by the expectations of the department for students in the course. The grade on each assignment is not a representation of anything other than the work done by the student on the individual assignment. Therefore, the course will not be subject to a curve, or to any other grade modification system. In the event that a question of grades being adjusted they will be rounded to the nearest whole tenth of a point.

Grading errors do happen, and students have a right to dispute grades that seem inappropriate. The process for this is as follows: After receiving the assignment the student shall within one week present to the instructor an argument in writing that articulates why the grade is inappropriate. The student will then arrange a time to meet with the instructor to discuss the grade. Based on the accuracy and quality of the argument presented the instructor will determine what sort of a modification is appropriate.

**Late Assignments**

Late assignments will be accepted with the exception of the final term paper. This paper must be turned in on time in order to receive credit. Other assignments will lose 10% from the final grade on the assignment. For example, a paper that would receive 90% if turned in on time will receive an 80% if it is turned in a day late.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Artificial Intelligence:**

There are an increasingly large number of artificial intelligence tools which some students may be tempted to use for classroom work. Some tools have a long legacy of use in composition, such as spelling and grammar check, which are allowed. More sophisticated tools that have recently been developed, such as ChatGPT, replace student composition, rather than assist in composition. Tools which substitute for student composition are not going to be allowed because they undermine the educational purpose of the class, which includes learning how to compose writing to support original research.

**Academic Honesty:**

Making references to the work of others strengthens your own work by granting you greater authority and by showing that you are part of a discussion located within an intellectual community. When you make references to the ideas of others, it is essential to provide proper attribution and citation. Failing to do so is considered academically dishonest, as is copying or paraphrasing someone else's work. The results of such behavior will lead to consequences ranging from failure on an assignment to failure in the course to dismissal from the university. Please ask if you are in doubt about the use of a citation. Honest mistakes can always be corrected or prevented.

Academic dishonesty is not limited to plagiarism. Other examples of academic dishonesty include cheating on tests or homework, taking an exam or writing a paper for someone else, and selling or uploading unauthorized documents from a class. Talk with your instructor if you have questions regarding academic honesty.

Penalty for such violations of academic honesty will be a zero for the assignment. Students who continue to violate the policy will continue to receive zeros for future assignments and subsequently fail the course.
