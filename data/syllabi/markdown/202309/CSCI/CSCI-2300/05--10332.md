## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course presents fundamental ideas and techniques of modern algorithm design and analysis. After completing this course, students should be able to analyze and design efficient algorithms for a variety of computational problems. Students will learn a variety of algorithm design techniques, how to apply them to different problems, and how to choose which technique should be used for which problem.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

The required course textbook is Algorithms by Dasgupta, Papadimitriou, and Vazirani. See also the textbook errata.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The goal of this course is to provide a strong foundation in algorithms in preparation for jobs in industry or for more advanced courses.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand the correctness of, and analyze the running times of, different algorithms.
- Use different algorithm-design techniques, including, but not limited to, greedy, divide-and-conquer, and dynamic programming techniques, to solve particular problems.
- Model real problems abstractly using the language of graphs and flows.
- Solve problems by reducing to other problems whose solution is known, and show that problems are hard by reducing from other problems.
- Make intelligent decisions about alternative data structures and algorithmic techniques in the context of practical problems, choosing from existing data structures and algorithms or designing your own when necessary.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

There will be three exams and a cumulative final exam, on the dates indicated on the course schedule. You must take the exams during the scheduled times. There will be NO make-up exams (unless the absence is excused by the Student Success Office).

Your exam average will be computed as follows:

- Exam 1: 22%
- Exam 2: 22%
- Exam 3: 22%
- Final Exam: 34%

There will be 10 homework assignments. The lowest homework grade will be dropped. Your homework average will be computed from your grades on the other 9 assignments

The Wednesday recitation period will be used as required office hours. The specific required dates are indicated on the course schedule. You can miss one office hour week without penalty. Please make sure the TA or mentor writes your name down before you leave, so you get credit for attending. Your recitation average will be computed as follows: (number of required recitations attended - 1) / (total number of required recitations)

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

To pass the course, you must do well on the exams and homeworks, and you must attend the required office hours. Specifically, you must meet the following two requirements.

1. Your assignment grade must be at least 70%, where assignment grade = (0.9(homework average)) + (0.1 (recitation average)
2. You must have a passing exam average.

Provided you meet these requirements, your course grade will be equivalent to your exam average.

The following table will be used to convert your course grade to a letter grade.

A = [100,93] A- = [90,93) B+ = [87,90) B = [83,87) B- = [80,83) C+ = [77,80) C = [73,77) C- = [70,73) D+ = [67,70) D = [60,67) F = [0,60)

Individual assignments and exams will not be curved. The course grades may be curved but only to raise the grades.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Every student must formulate and write up his or her homework assignments independently. Looking up solutions and/or copying solutions from another source is not permitted. You may not third-party tools, including generative language models such as ChatGPT, to create, edit, or verify your homework solutions.

You are also responsible for protecting your own homework from being copied. If multiple students turn in problem solutions that are identical, this is cheating, and all students involved will be held accountable.

No collaboration is allowed during exams.

Violation of these policies will be considered a breach of academic integrity. The minimum penalty for any violation is a course letter grade reduction. Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the Dean of Students. If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs.
