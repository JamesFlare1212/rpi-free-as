## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course offers the introduction to network science and review of current research in this field. Classes will interchangeably present chapters from the textbook and the related current research. The emphasis will be on mathematical background of network science: graphs and networks; random networks and various types of scale-free networks; network properties such as assortativity, mobility, and robustness; social networks and communities; and dynamics of processes on networks.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

**Author:** Albert Laszlo Barabasi  
**Title:** *Network Science* **Publisher:** Cambridge University Press, 2016  
On-line version is available at http://barabasi.com/networksciencebook/  
In addition, class notes will be used.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Present and discuss fundamental network science ideas to create models and understand dynamics of networked systems;  
Show how compare, contrast, and describe the similarities and differences of various kinds of networks and processes modeled on networks;  
Give examples of critique and evaluation of the strengths and weaknesses of each of the models and types of networks based on them and these network types performance in diverse network science applications;  
Foster students understanding the principles of applying network science to disciplinary science and design and set up basic models for some specific applications.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

1\. Mathematical background of network science: graphs and networks.  
2\. Random networks and their properties.  
3\. Scale-free networks, small world networks and Barabasi-Alert model.  
4\. Mobility and networks  
5\. Network robustness  
6\. Social networks and communities  
7\. Assortativity of networks  
8\. Dynamic processes on networks

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The students' performance will be measured using three different methods:  
(i) selection of a current research paper for presentation and formulating a plan of an experiment to test the results and methods of the paper,  
(iii) presentation on the selected paper, including slides content, and evaluation of the test experiment with the results and method of the paper,  
(iv) contributions to in-class discussions.  
The presentation and experiment plans will measure the student’s ability to apply concepts of network science to networks analysis.  
The presentation slides and the experiments evaluating paper results and methods will measure student’s ability to prepare summary material based on fundamental scientific concepts and basic research.  
The presentation will measure student’s ability to communicate the concepts and notions related to the research projects in which the student is involved.  
The discussion in class will measure the student’s skills to critically evaluate and objectively assess the presentations of other students.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**An individual planning of the paper presentation and experiments to test methods and results of the paper** (35% of the total grade), followed by an **individual presentation of the selected research paper and results of the test experiments** (55% of the total grade), with **questions and participation in discussions** for at least two student presentations will providing the remaining (10%) of the the total grade.  
**The research papers for presentations** will be listed after the second lecture and the choice of one of them has to by made two weeks later. The final 45 **min in class presentation of the assigned papers and experiments, followed by 5 min discussion** will be scheduled in the last two month of the classes.  
Grade ranges: A 96. A- 91, B 85, B- 80, C 70, C- 60, F <60.
