## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course focuses on the state-of-the-art research on machine learning by exploring advanced research topics and emerging techniques. It includes lectures, invited talks, and students’ presentation. Particularly, students read papers thoughtfully, present them, highlight their strengths and weaknesses, and develop new research ideas. Therefore, this course is intended for students with research experience on machine learning, including graduate and undergraduate students.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The goal of the course is to teach students how to approach research, criticize the existing literature, develop new research ideas, present their research ideas, and learn from the experts in the field.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Providing constructive criticism on others’ research and identifying open problems
- Effective presentation skills
- Collaborating in teams on developing new ideas
- Formulating and writing research ideas

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students are assessed based on the following four items:

- Assignment:  
  Students are divided into multiple groups. Each group is responsible for one research paper. Each group has one week to read a research paper and submit their review by 8:00 am on the class day. The assignment includes the feedback provided on research papers. Students may collaborate on reading the papers but should provide the feedback independently. The feedback includes summary of the paper, strengths, weaknesses, and future research directions.
- Class presentation:  
  During the class, a student presents the paper assigned to him/her as an assignment for review. Students are expected to clearly introduce the problem so that everyone with different research experience can follow, highlight strengths and weaknesses of the paper followed by future research directions. Presentations are evaluated based on understanding of the paper and clarity.
- Class participation:  
  Students are evaluated based on attendance, and participating in the discussion during and after the students’ presentation, as well as the invited talks.
- Project:  
  Students form a group of 3-4. Each group completes a project on a topic of their choice which can be: 1) original research paper; 2) in-depth literature survey with strengths and weaknesses of each paper and future research directions; 3) implementation of a theoretical concept or an experimental work. The topic can be related to students' research (this is encouraged). At the end of semester, students present their project and submit a (5-page maximum) report in the form of a research paper. Also, a preliminary version of the project (introduction and literature review) may be presented during the mid-term.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

A grade of zero will be given on the first assignment where cheating and/or plagiarism are detected. If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of F for the course. Using an artificial intelligence (AI) machine to summarize and review a paper without carefully reading the paper and having enough knowledge about the paper is considered cheating. The same is true for the project, and using any AI tools to help with writing the report is considered cheating.
