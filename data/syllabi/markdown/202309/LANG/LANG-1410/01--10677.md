## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is designed for students who do not have prior knowledge or learning experience in the Mandarin Chinese language. It will provide students with fundamental skills in listening, speaking, reading, and writing in Chinese. Students will have opportunity to participate in interactive communication during class as well as through the Chinese Mentor Program meetings and the Conversation Corner & Mandarin Club (CCMC) activities. Together with the instructor, students will create and enjoy a friendly, supportive, and collaborative Chinese learning community in which everyone can study at ease, support each other, and grow to reach their own maximum potential.

In addition to developing language skills, this course will provide students with the opportunity to deepen their comprehension of Chinese society, people, history, tradition, and culture through a variety of interactive mediums by going beyond the limitations of the textbook. These include,

\* Exploration of topics related to Chinese people’s everyday lives and ways of thinking

\* Exposure to selected Chinese classical poems, folk songs, tongue twisters, or cross-talk art

\* Presentation on Chinese culture

\* Discussion of Chinese sayings or idioms

\* Discovery of the origin and evolution of Chinese characters

\* Watching relevant video clips

\* Enjoying 1-2 Chinese movies

## Course Goals

### Goals:

After successfully completing this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to：

1. Understand and use Mandarin Pinyin (including initials, finals, and tones)
2. Recognize and write around 150 Chinese characters
3. Read and use about 300 words (with their collocations)
4. Communicate for the following topics (see "content details")

## Course Content

### Content Details:

- 1\) Exchange greetings (including how Chinese address one another with their proper titles and how they call a person by his/her full name)
- 2\) Express and reply to appreciation and apology
- 3\) Say goodbye to people
- 4\) Introduce oneself or others (including recognizing Chinese names, and foreign names in Chinese)
- 5\) Ask about someone’s nationality
- 6\) Talk about family members and pets, numbers and proper measure words for different nouns in Chinese, ask and answer questions pertaining to one’s age, profession and language ability
- 7\) Express time, dates, days of the week, and months (including understanding Chinese Lunar and Solar calendars)
- 8\) Talk about daily life schedules (do what at what time)
- 9\) Make an appointment; talk about some sports and hobbies (like or dislike)
- 10\) Wish someone “Happy Birthday”
- 11\) Select and give a gift which is culturally appropriate in a particular context
- 12\) Inquire and express preferences for food and drink
- 13\) Discuss various dishes and their flavors
- 14\) Order at a Chinese restaurant, offer to pay for a meal

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

1. ATTENDANCE

- (1) Attendance is mandatory. Learning a language is very different from learning in other domains, which may be done by reading the materials yourself. Every language practice class directly builds upon the previous ones. Any absence means the loss of an invaluable opportunity to participate in group exercises and practice communicating with others, and this loss will continue to negatively affect future classes. In other words, if you miss one class, you actually lose much more than the two-hour period itself.
- (2) Because life happens, you will have two absences that will not count against you (“free pass”). Although the first two absences do not count negatively toward a student’s grade, the third absence becomes costly. Upon the third absence, the final grade will be dropped from A to B, B to C, C to D, or D to F. In other words, students having SIX absences (excused or unexcused) for the entire semester will result in an automatic grade of F for the course.

Students can go to Chinese mentoring sessions to make up for any unavoidable absences. Every three mentoring sessions in excess of the required hours may be used to make up for one absence. The maximum number of absences that can be made up this way is THREE. In some extremely rare situations, if students must have five or more absences and all of them have institute-approved “excused” letters (NOT the combination of “excused” and “unexcused”), they will be given UNLIMITED opportunities to re-earn the attendance points by attending the mentoring sessions.

- (3) Please be punctual. Arriving late for class interrupts the learning process for everyone and should be avoided as much as possible. Every three times a student is marked late counts as one absence.

### Other Course Policies:

2. ACTIVE PARTICIPATION

- (1) All students are strongly encouraged/expected to actively participate during class. This class strives to achieve a comprehensive development of students’ language skills, cultural appreciation, and communicative competence. Therefore, active participation in vocabulary and grammar exercises, role plays, simulations, class discussions, and activities is vitally important.
- (2) Classroom interactions are meant to be enriching and collaborative. Students will be expected to have prepared for the new vocabulary, narrative, and dialogue in each lesson. Class participation should provide some evidence that you have prepared beforehand.
- (3) Students are encouraged and highly appreciated to keep the lines of communication open. I will always stay in the WebEx meeting room and warmly welcome you to stay after class to chat with me or set up a separate appointment.
- (4) Regardless of the virtual instruction model and all kinds of difficulties, I have been putting forth my utmost effort to hold all classes in real time and promote active participation and communicative interaction among students. They expressed great appreciation about our class being “like a family” and “always friendly and inviting”, as well as my “willingness to go the extra mile to answer any questions or concerns that students had”. In this warm family, no one worries if they make a mistake. Any type of participation is highly appreciated. 3. ASSIGNMENTS

- (1) Completion of all assignments on or before the due date.

It is the instructor’s belief that students are responsible adults and are to be treated as such. Students will receive full credit ONLY for assignments submitted in advance or on time. Students will STILL be able to receive 50% of the score for the assignments and quizzes made up throughout the semester.

If you do submit work late AND you want credit, you are responsible for keeping track of it. During the LAST WEEK of the semester, please provide me with ONE complete list of the late work you submitted and the date it was submitted. Without this list, you may not receive credit for late submissions.

- (2) Important exercises in both the textbook and workbook will be assigned with some additional work related (if necessary).
- (3) Presentations on Chinese language and culture need to be well prepared and well revised. All students are expected to use what they have learned in the class (vocabulary, grammar, expression, and cultural information) and are very much encouraged to search for and try out some new things.
- (4) Listening exercises, words used in sentences, interviewing native Chinese speakers, news report in Chinese, authentic reading, and/or composition writing may be assigned.
- (5) Reading Fluency Check and Dialogue Performance

Students are expected to fluently read the dialogues (characters, not pinyin) in each lesson. Reading fluency will be checked and graded for each lesson (8 times during the semester). Students are also expected to apply what they have learned from each unit to create and perform/act out their own dialogues without reading from scripts. Dialogue performance will be due for only two out of four units covered. Scores from both reading fluency check and dialogue performance will be counted as the speaking evaluation for this course.

5. CHINESE MENTOR PROGRAM (virtual)

It is required for every student to meet with one (any) of our Chinese mentors for a minimum of six (6) hours total during the entire semester (accounting for 6 points in the final grade). Mentoring sessions start from the second week and end in the second to last week of the semester (mornings, afternoons, evenings, or even weekends, per request). Please start early and plan well.

Students who meet with our Chinese mentors for more than 6 hours per semester may use that time to make up absences or earn extra credit. Every 3 hours of mentoring session may be used to make up one class missed. If students have full attendance, they can earn 0.2 points (extra credit) for each additional hour, which will be added to their final grade.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
