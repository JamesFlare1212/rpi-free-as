## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Introduction to modeling and control of dynamic chemical processes. Topics include the development of first-principles models, linearization and state space form, input/output (transfer function) form, design and tuning of PID controllers, model-based control, frequency response for robustness analysis, case studies in multivariable control, numerical analysis, and simulation.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Bequette, B.W. Process Control: Modeling, Design and Simulation, Prentice Hall (2003)

## Course Goals

### Goals:

After completing this course, students should (at a minimum)...

1\) be able to formulate differential equations describing unsteady-state mass and energy balances to describe chemical process dynamics.

2\) be able to solve systems of ODEs and plot results using MATLAB.

3\) be able to linearize ODEs and formulate state-space models.

4\) be able to recognize systems with 1st or 2nd order responses to step inputs.

5\) be able to develop empirical transfer function models to fit data from step response functions.

6\) be able to formulate feedback and IMC control strategies for chemical processes.

7\) be able to apply MATLAB/SIMULINK to simulate chemical process dynamics and control schemes.

8\) be able to perform a Routh stability analysis to find controller tuning parameter values that cause the closed-loop system to lose stability.

9\) be able to design a PID controller using the IMC-based PID design procedure.

10\) exhibit basic knowledge of modern topics in process control.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

- 1 Incentives for process control, fundamental models (Chapters 1 and 2).
- 2 Fundamental models, state space models, transfer functions, empirical models (Chapters 2, 3 and 4).
- 3 Introduction to feedback control (Chapters 4 and 5).
- 4 PID controller tuning (Chapter 6).
- 5 Internal Model Control (IMC) (Chapter 8).
- 6 IMC-based PID tuning (Chapter 9).

Selected topics from:

- 7 Cascade control (Chapter 10).
- 8 Feedforward control, PID enhancements (Chapter 11).
- 9 Split-range, selective, and over-ride strategies (Chapter 12).
- 10 Control loop interaction, multivariable control (Chapters 13 and 14).
- 11 Multi-variable control, plant-wide control (Chapters 14 and 15).
- 12 Model predictive control (MPC).
