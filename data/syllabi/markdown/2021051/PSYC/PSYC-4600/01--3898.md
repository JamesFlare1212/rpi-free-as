## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

## Perception and thought are considered in terms of processes represented in the brain. The localization and lateralization of function are examined, drawing upon research on the behavioral effects of brain damage as well as brain-imaging studies and other approaches. Examples of topics include object recognition, memory, language, emotion, spatial ability, and motor processes.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Students will integrate information about how the brain functions into their existing knowledge base and expand this to gain a greater understanding of the brain regions involved in specific cognitive functions.
- 2\. Some of the specific topical areas that students will demonstrate being able to describe the function and the brain region(s) involved are: attention, sensory processing, movement, memory, cognitive control, symbolic representations, and emotion.
- 3\. Students will be able to apply their understanding of Cognitive Neuroscience to analyze and interpret data reported in the primary literature as well as popular press, and communicate their perspectives.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Participation and attendance (25%):** Attendance and active engagement in class is mandatory. Students will receive an average grade for attendance and participation. Participation includes working in groups on activities during class time, contributing to class discussions and polls, and/or completing synchronous and asynchronous participation challenges or reading responses (on Top Hat).

**Quizzes (20%):** Quizzes will be given for each of our chapter readings after we complete them. Quizzes are take home and will be due a week after they are released on LMS. Please see schedule on last page. These quizzes will be multiple-choice, true/false, or fill-in-the-blank questions. Students will be expected to answer question associated with assigned readings and the lecture materials from the previous lecture(s). No make-up quizzes will be permitted. I will drop the lowest quiz grade for each student when calculating the average quiz grade.

**Group presentation (15%):** Students will be required to work with their group to choose an article for all to read, critique and discuss and will prepare a presentation for our in-class discussion of that paper.

**Journal Article Critiques (10%):** We will read 5 journal article readings throughout the semester; articles will be chosen by student groups. Students are expected to read and write critiques of 5 primary literature articles related to the topics discussed in class. Each critique is due on the day that the article will be discussed by 11:59 pm (see schedule). For class sessions indicated, students must write a critique or reflection on the journal article listed. These should be brief (1 page, single-spaced), explicitly include student’s thoughts about the work, and submitted on LMS.

**Reflection papers (30%):** Students will complete three short reflection papers this semester related to each of our three modules. Papers should be 2-3 pages in length, double-spaced, 12-point font.

**\*\* Any of these above assignments that are worked on outside of class that will be graded need to be** **submitted via the LMS link by 11:59 pm EST on its due date to be considered on time. See lateness policy below for more details.\*\***

*Professor Walf's I.D.E.A. Statement* In this classroom, we will acknowledge and aim to respect inclusivity, diversity, equity, and awareness. In our classroom, all students are welcome and will be supported regardless of race/ethnicity, gender identities, gender expressions, sexual orientation, socio-economic status, age, disabilities, religion, regional background, neurodiversity, citizenship status, nationality and the many other diverse identities that we each bring here. Indeed, the diversity that the students bring to this classroom should be viewed as a strength, resource, and benefit.

We will be focusing on the material a (neuro)science perspective. We must be aware that science is not always objective and unbiased and historically has not included representation of diverse individuals in the experiments or the researchers themselves. For example, individual differences between human subjects in experiments were often minimized or considered confounds because of the added complexity they may have contributed to the analysis of the data. However, in this classroom, we will strive to integrate a diverse set of experiences for a more comprehensive understanding of the material in this course, despite the potential for added complexity. An important part of a college education is learning about the complexity of various issues. We will also expand our understanding by hearing from diverse voices in the field through our readings and discussions of the material. Moreover, students in this class are encouraged to participate during class meetings and all efforts will be made for students have an equitable opportunity to contribute and succeed in a supportive learning environment. Our classroom will represent a diversity of individual beliefs, backgrounds, and experiences. We all must listen and show respect for every other member of this class even if we do not necessarily agree. Please honor the uniqueness of our classroom community and appreciate the opportunity we have to learn from one another. If you have any questions or concerns, please discuss with me.
