## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- On successful completion of this course students will be able to: • to select simplifying assumptions in analyzing a complex thermal system. •
- • to analyze heat transfer in thermal systems by identifying heat transfer modes–convection (forced/natural, internal/external), conduction (transient and steady-state) and radiation using the appropriate equations to evaluate them.
- to analyze thermal systems by applying conservation of mass and energy.
- • to analyze the performance of heat exchangers and design heat exchangers given a set of specifications.

**Study Groups, Homeworks and Projects (for extra credit)**

Learning can be enhanced by working with someone else. I suggest that you form a study group to aid your understanding of the material. Difficulties on homework can be minimized if you discuss a problem with someone else. However, homework must be your own individual work. Copying of someone else’s homework solutions is cheating.

Homework solutions must show all steps and be presented in an organized and clear manner. For grading purpose all students must create and account and upload their homework on gradescope. ONLY NEATLY WRITTEN HOMEWORK WILL BE GRADED! Each student is expected to make a serious effort at completing all homework assignments and has individual responsibility for learning the material. LATE HOMEWORK WILL NOT BE ACCEPTED!

Teams of two students could be formed to work on ONE mini-project for extra credit. In special cases a miniproject can be worked on individually. The design and analysis of the project is expected to be original. However, interested students should read background info in previous weeks to understand the system they have to design for, and establish requirements and specifications (system size and cooling/heating loads).

The miniproject analysis and design will be presented via webex to the instructor or the TA. As part of this presentation, each student will be asked questions related to the project, but also related to the material covered in the lectures prior to the project. Two examples of mini-projects are below. Each mini-project presentation is expected to last 30 min with 15 min for project presentation and 15 min for questions. The extra credit award is up to 10% total.

**Mini-project 1:** design a cooling system for electric car battery pack. Articles and sites you may want to research when designing your system:

https://www.dober.com/electric-vehicle-cooling-systems#electric_vehicle_cooling_systems

**Mini-project 2:** design a solar water heating system for your house or for an average US residence. Articles and sites you may want to research when designing your system:

https://www.energystar.gov/products/water_heaters/water_heater_solar/how_it_works
