## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

A course emphasizing advances concepts and methods from Calculus. Topics include multivariable integral theorems (Green’s, divergence, Stokes’, Reynolds transport), extrema of multivariable functions (including Taylor’s theorem and Lagrange multipliers), calculus of variations (Euler-Lagrange equations, constraints principle of least action), and Cartesian tensors (calculus, invariants, representations).

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Vector Calculus, Sixth ed., by Jerrold E. Marsden and Anthony Tromba, Freeman, 2012.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students should demonstrate a conception and practical understanding of multivariate functions and their rates of change.
- Students should be able to compute extrema of multivariate functions.
- Students should demonstrate a conceptual understanding of integrals of multivariate functions along paths and over surfaces and volumes.
- Students should demonstrate a conceptual understanding of integral theorems and their practical applications.
- Students should be able to compute extrema of functionals.
- Students should demonstrate an elementary understanding of Cartesian tensors.

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

**Lectures:** Lectures will be held via Webex during the assigned lecture times listed for our course. To attend, log in to LMS, click on Webex: Meetings & Office hours in the left sidebar, then find the the session you wish to attend under Virtual Meetings. Recordings will be posted on LMS.

**Exams:** Details regarding Exams #1 and #2 will be announced on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/) when the date of the exam is near. Exams will be given on LMS during the scheduled lecture time. Exceptions will only be made if there is a legitimate time zone conflict. Exams may include some combination of true/false, multiple choice, and manually graded questions where all your work must be shown.

Make-up exams will only be given to students with an [excused absence documented by the Student Success office](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/student-success/excused-absence). Missing an exam should not be taken lightly. Thus, students missing an exam due to an excused absence should notify the instructor to make arrangements for a make-up exam before the exam begins whenever that is possible.

Students may use their textbook and notes during exams and quizzes, but the use of online resources outside of LMS is prohibited. Communicating with other people in any way during exams about the content of the exam is prohibited.

**Homework:** Homework will be assigned for each covered section of the textbook and will be posted on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/). Homework assignments are due on Wednesdays at 11:59 PM. The first assignment is due on June 2nd. After the due date, late assignments for each week will be accepted until the following Sunday at 11:59 PM and a 25% penalty will be applied to the score. After that, no late homework will be accepted.

While you are encouraged to discuss homework problems with other students, all work submitted must be your own.

**Quizzes:** A few scheduled quizzes will be given. Those may include some combination of true/false, multiple choice, or other automatically graded type of questions, as well as manually graded questions where all your work must be shown. Quizzes will be posted on LMS and will be timed.

**Time Zone Conflicts:** Students with legitimate time zone conflicts with the lecture times or quiz times may opt to take exams/quizzes at an alternate time. Time zone information will be collected in the beginning of classes so arrangements can be made in advance.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://rpi.app.box.com/s/3f1cl3w1lt1mq8gqla68v6uk3vh1c19f) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams and homework must be the student’s own. Students may use their textbook and notes during exams, but the use of online resources outside of LMS is prohibited. Communicating with other people in any way during exams about the content of the exam is prohibited.

The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
