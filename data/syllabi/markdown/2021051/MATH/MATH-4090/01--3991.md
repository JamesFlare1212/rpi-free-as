## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course description:** The course provides an opportunity for the development of theorem-proving skills in the field of mathematical analysis. Expansion of a knowledge base comes as a by-product of energy expended in theorem proving and subsequent exposition. Analysis topics included are: sets, functions, the real numbers, cardinality, induction, decimal representations of real numbers, Euclidean spaces, ab-stract vector spaces, and metric spaces. This is a communication-intensive course.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Analysis With and Introduction to Proof, Fifth ed., by Steven R. Lay, Pearson, 2014.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate

- read mathematical proofs and verbally communicate how each sentence contributes to deducing the assertion,
- solve problems in analysis,
- write high quality mathematical proofs, and
- write LaTeX syntax for mathematics and mathematical equations.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

### Other Course Policies:

**Homeworks:** Homework is due at the times posted on LMS. Late homework will receive a 10% deduction of total points for each day, or part thereof, late and will not be accepted after graded homework has been returned. Homework handed in late due to an **excused absence** will not incur a penalty assuming notification of the absence was done in a timely manner.

**Lecture Quizzes:** Will be administered on LMS and will be based on the content of the lecture posted that day. Lecture quizzes will need to be completed not later than Tuesday night for Monday lecture and Friday night for Thursday lecture.

For each student some quiz scores may be dropped at the end of the semester. Only students who miss a quiz due to an [excused absence documented by the Student Success office](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/student-success/excused-absence) may be excused from a quiz. It is not possible to give make-up quizzes for any reason.

**Exams:** The material covered on Exams will be announced on LMS when the date of the exam is near. Exams will be given on LMS. Exams will include some combination of true/false, multiple choice, and other LMS graded question types and file upload, manually graded questions where work must be shown. Exams will be timed.

Make-up exams will only be given to students with an [excused absence documented by the Student Success office](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/student-success/excused-absence). **Missing an exam should not be taken lightly.** Thus, students missing an exam due to an excused absence should notify the instructor to make arrangements for a make-up exam before the exam begins. If a student misses an exam due to an unexcused absence (e.g., did not realize due date of exam, etc.), then the student must inform the instructor within 24 hours of the exam. The student may elect to take a make-up exam at a time arranged by the instructor and a 25% penalty will be applied to the score. Excused absences where students either fail to notify the instructor or make arrangements to make up the missed exam in a timely manner may also receive a penalty.

**Grade appeals:** Changes to exam, quiz or homework grades will be made only if something has been overlooked. All appeals must be made within 1 week of the time of the exam, quiz or homework grade is made available on LMS. Please check LMS comments on graded assignments in a timely manner.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic integrity:** Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://rpi.app.box.com/s/3f1cl3w1lt1mq8gqla68v6uk3vh1c19f) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted for this class must be the student’s own. A student may not change an answer on anything before submitting it for an appeal. Additionally you will be asked to “sign” an honor code that indicates your work is your own and you have not given or received any help on exams. Students must follow the guidelines under the **Homework** section for all homeworks submitted for the course. The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
