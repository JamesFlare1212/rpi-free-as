## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course introduces students to the practice of graphic design with a variety of hands-on projects. Studies include the principles and theories of two-dimensional design and the integration of form, color, type, image, and content. Students will develop a heightened visual awareness and an understanding of how design influences meaning. The course emphasizes strategic and critical thinking in the solving of graphic design problems for the purposes of professional communication across a range of media platforms. In addition, students will study the basic functions and integral properties in the following applications: Adobe Illustrator, InDesign, Photoshop, and AfterEffects. These applications will be used to create solutions for various graphic design projects.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 01\. Understand and explain visual communication and graphic design concepts and terms
- 02\. Use design thinking, techniques and theories through guided projects and readings to visually solve communication problems
- 03\. Understand how design thinking and the utilization of graphic design principles can be applied to solving marketing, engineering and other design application problems
- 04\. Learn the basics of the following design software while creating documents for all class projects: Adobe Illustrator, Adobe InDesign, Adobe Photoshop, Adobe After Effects
- 05\. Develop and demonstrate team working skills through group interaction, critique, problem solving and verbal expression

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**evaluation criteria**

**Project assignments 75%**

***Aesthetics 25%*** Does the project have strong aesthetic and formal qualities?

***Conceptual Framework 25%*** Are the concepts or ideas behind the project original, thoughtful?

***Comp Work 25%*** How far did you take the work and explore the concept? How many comps or variations did you make?

***Technical Skill 25%*** Does the project demonstrate a mastery of the skills and techniques addressed with good production?

***Formal Focus*** **+ Readings (20%)**

***Formal Focus*** **Paper (5%)**

Rensselaer uses the QPA scale and applying the percentage the project is worth.

A=4.0, A-=3.65, B+=3.3, B=3.0, B-=2.7, C+=2.3, C=2.0, etc. Your average for the course must be at least 3.72 to receive an A.

**A/A-:** Excellent This is usually work done by a highly motivated student who works both beyond the required classroom assignments, to augment their own learning, and regularly reaches beyond the expectations of each assignment. Exceptionally skilled and dedicated, the A student takes extra time and effort to create work that is truly outstanding. In order to earn an A in the course, a student must turn every assignment in on time, adhere to the one absence rule, and create work that demonstrates exceptional design ability.

**B+/B:** Very Good/Good This is usually work done by a highly motivated student meeting all of the performance criteria as set forth by the assignments. Exceptionally skilled and dedicated, the B student takes extra time and effort to create work that is truly outstanding. The work will show strong technical and conceptual skills. Work may not be handed in on time or is incomplete during critique. The student may have had more than one absence. The work shows better than average design sensitivity.

**C:** Good/Satisfactory This work is average, it may even be excellent but it is not handed in on time. Work has fulfilled the requirements of the project, but may lack technical proficiency and/or visual interest and thoughtful and imaginative resolution. Work may not be consistently handed in on time or presented in critique.

Students at mid-term (or anytime) can contact me if they want to confirm their grades, absences, and latenesses to date to see if any penalties have occurred. Grades are posted on LMS in a timely fashion soon after projects are due, so students should have no surprises. Your final grade will be determined by translating the letter grade for each project into a numerical equivalent based on the QPA scale and applying the percentage the project is worth.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic dishonesty**

All assignments in this class must represent the student’s own work. Academic dishonesty consists of plagiarism and copyright infringement, i.e., representing the work of others as your own through deliberate omission of acknowledgments or references.

Plagiarism and copyright infringement include copying text, graphics, photos, sound, music, video, animations, user interface designs, etc. created by others and submitting that work as your own. You may discuss your assignments with others and solicit feedback from others to improve your work. If you have questions concerning intellectual property rights don’t hesitate to discuss those questions with me. If you have questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

Student penalties for plagiarism and cheating can be quite harsh. The Rensselaer Handbook defines various forms of academic dishonesty and procedure for responding to them. Students are responsible for familiarizing themselves with these policies.

Plagiarism or any academic dishonesty will result in a grade of ‘F’ for the assignment, with no chance for resubmission. A second incidence of academic dishonesty will result in an “F” for the course. Students will also be required to write a letter of admission to the professor of this course and submit a copy to the Dean of H&SS, and the Chair of the Dept. of Communication & Media.
