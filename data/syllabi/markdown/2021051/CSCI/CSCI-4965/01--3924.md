## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The class will explore current AI topics through reading, writing, programming, and exploring some of the classic fiction that has former people's (mis)perceptions of machine intelligence. This course will give students an appreciation on how to separate fiction from fact, and how to critically evaluate the impact current and upcoming AI topics will have on society.

A hands on exposure to AI is one of the goals of the class, but the programming will be primarily through the use of existing tools. Python notebooks will be used. Time will be provided during class time to work on the labs, although you will be need to complete and submit them outside class time.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course has two primary goals:

- Students will be able to better understand the reality vs. the hype around artificial intelligence.
- Students will get hands on experience programming a simple AI program from scratch and using existing code/tools online.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The course will include selected readings and videos, and will cover topics including: AI in reality v. (science) fiction and help students to understand, through lecture and hands on labs, about some of the key technologies of use in AI today, especially the use of 'deep learning' for vision and language, and an introduction to 'reinforcement learning' and agent-progamming for games.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- The student will be able to understand and apply good heuristics for determining whether news reporting, popular literature and even academic publications are realistically reporting the results of the performance of AI systems.
- \<br>The student will be able to use PyTorch to design and run deep learning programs. The student will also be able to appropriately use convolutional techniques for vision, recurrent and attention mechanisms for natural language processing, and extend pre-trained systems for language tasks and other uses.\<br>

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Homeworks will largely focus on the understanding of the general issues involved in understanding and evaluating AI systems, as well as the ethical issues that arise from the widescale use of AI in practice.  
Labs will focus on the understanding of the coding of AI programs focused on vision, language, and games.  
The project will focus on students' abilities to put this all together.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Homeworks and assignments must be in the students' own words, but students are allowed (nay, encouraged!) to discuss the concepts and issues required. Lab policy may vary based on the labs, individual labs will specify their rules.  
Some assignments will allow group work, as long as the group is identified. The project will require group work (students working remotely may be able to team with other remote students in similar time zones depending on final class roster).  
Some assignments and labs may allow or require the use of code found on the Web or elsewhere. In those cases, students should identify the code used.  
Submission of any assignment/lab that is in violation of this policy will result in a grade of zero for that assignment/lab. Multiple infractions will result in a grade of F in the course and/or reporting to campus as a violation of the academic integrity policy.

We have 4 programming labs in this course, and we allow 2 out of the 4 labs to be submitted no later than 3 days after the deadline without any penalty. For other delayed lab submissions, there will be a 20% deduction of the lab grade for each late day.  
If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
