## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Physical processes governing occurrence and distribution of precipitation, infiltration, evaporation, and surface water runoff. Hydrology, mechanics of flow. Statistical hydrology, unit hydrograph theory, and watershed modeling. Floodplain hydrology and open channel hydraulics. Urban hydrology, hydraulics of storm sewers, and design of detention structures for flood control. Design project using ACE HEC-HMS flood hydrograph package.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The objective of this course is to develop a working knowledge of the basic principles of hydraulics and hydrology. Students will do this by building on the knowledge gained through fluids (ENGR 2250/ CIVL 2250). Upon successful completion of the course, students will have a basic understanding of open channel flow, precipitation and runoff, and flow and flood routing through natural and manmade catchments. Having successfully completed this course, students will have the necessary skills to take senior-level design courses (ENVE 4180/CIVL 4920).

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Model physical systems (channels, rivers, streams, catchments, watersheds) by employing principles of conservation of mass, energy, and momentum to develop equations describing their behavior.
- Solve these equations via concepts of specific energy to determine characteristics (flow, runoff) and compute a variety of flow and water surface elevation scenarios and observe these in laboratory experiments.
- Develop skills in computer modelling of these systems using commercial software (HEC-HMS).

**HOMEWORK REQUIREMENTS:**

As an engineer and a professional, your work will often be read and scrutinized by others. In some instances, your work could be a legal document or a piece of evidence in a court of law. It is your responsibility to present your work in a legible, methodical, and logical manner. The following format is required in this course (see example on next page). The intent of the required format is to encourage you to present your work in a professional manner.

**1)** ACCURACY is essential in all engineering work. Accuracy is dependent upon the absence of errors. Clear work, neatly and systematically arranged, will assist in reducing errors to a minimum.

**2)** APPROXIMATIONS are justified when precision is impossible or unnecessary. Approximations are based upon good judgment, not upon guesses.

**3)** SYSTEMATIC PRESENTATION reduces errors and increases the informational and engineering value of the work performed (see requirements below).

**4)** CLEARNESS AND NEATNESS are the result of clear thinking and they are essential if the work is to be valuable and intelligible to others (see requirements below).

**SYSTEMATIC PRESENTATION:**

**1)** Use engineering “ruled” paper (8 ½ X 11 in.) with a clean left edge (i.e., no perforations). This paper is available at the bookstore. Use only one side of each sheet of paper. Each problem should begin at the top of a new page with the problem # and text problem # shown.

**2)** The upper margin of the first page of your homework should show your name, the homework assignment number, the date the homework is turned in, and the page number. The upper margin of the first page of each problem should show the problem number and page number. Other pages should include the page number. The page number should be given in the upper right corner in the format A/B where A is the current page number and B is the total number of pages in your homework. A staple in the top left corner should be used to attach all pages of your homework.

**3)** Computations should be arranged in logical order and each step recorded in full (other engineers should be able to easily follow your work). The order of presentation of work should be as shown below. Any work that does not substantially follow this order of presentation will receive no credit.

\- Given data and diagrams

\- Required results

----------------------------------- (use a line to clearly separate the information that is given to you from the work that you perform)

\- Calculations and diagrams

\- Final answer (boxed in)

**4)** Do not make short-cuts. Perform one operation at a time and make a complete record of it. Only experienced engineers can safely take short-cuts. An exception may be made for repetitive or tabular computations, in which case it is enough to provide a complete record of a sample calculation.

**5)** Final results should be clearly indicated by *boxing* them in.

**6)** The correct units should be provided for intermediate results and final answers. A result is of no value if units are not provided. Note that careful consideration of units will often prevent mistakes.

**CLEARNESS AND NEATNESS:**

**1)** Figures and letters must be neat and large enough to be easily read.

**2)** All work must be done in pencil. Pencil marks must be clear-cut and black so that they are easily readable.

**3)** Erasing must be clean and thorough. Smudgy, dirty work is not acceptable.
