## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

## Grading Criteria

**[A]: Truly Outstanding**

- presents well-articulated, well-structured analysis of relevant issues
- develops arguments with *specific* points and relevant examples
- articulates a clear position on the issues at hand
- demonstrates superior command of standard written English, with few or no errors

**[A-/B+/B]: Strong**

- presents a generally thoughtful, well-ordered analysis of relevant issues
- develops arguments with *specific* points and relevant examples
- presents a clear position on the issues at hand
- demonstrates facility with standard written English, but may contain minor errors and/or awkward constructions

**[B-]: Satisfactory**

- presents a competent analysis of the relevant issues, with some flaws
- develops arguments with relevant but insufficient and/or vague reasons and examples
- presents some issues with organization and/or focus
- demonstrates sufficient control of English language to express ideas with acceptable clarity, but may contain errors

**[C+/C/C-]: Seriously Flawed**

Exhibits one or more of the following major problems:

- unclear or seriously limited in addressing main issues
- provides few, if any, relevant reasons or examples to support arguments
- poorly focused and/or organized
- serious problems with control of English language, interfering with clarity of and expression of meaning

**[D and Below]: Fundamentally Deficient**

Exhibits one or more of the following major problems:

- provides little evidence of understanding the issues
- shows little or no evidence of the ability to develop an organized response (including extreme brevity)
- severe issues with control of English language, interfering profoundly with clarity and expression of meaning

***Missed and Late Assignments***

Missing a deadline is a stressful experience. Sometimes it’s just the result of a one-off timing issue, but sometimes it’s the result of something more persistent—struggling with the material, life circumstances, mental state—and the source of trouble is not always obvious from the inside. Student in this situation often blame themselves and feel like they could just complete the assignment the next day if they do what they need to get done, but sometimes the next day isn’t any easier, and the stress snowballs as late penalties accumulate.

If you find yourself in this situation, I can help. If you are going to miss a deadline:

1. Email me whatever you have before the deadline.
2. Stay in contact with me until you finish. We will make a plan to get you back on track in the course

## *Academic Integrity*

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty. In the case of major acts of plagiarism (e.g., text copied verbatim from an unidentified source) the student will receive a grade of F for the course. In the case of minor acts of plagiarism (e.g., referencing the findings of others without appropriate citations) the student will fail the assignment and receive a reduction of final course grade by one letter grade. If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
