### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Private Instrumental / Vocal Instruction – Elissa Harrigan

Arts 2962

SYLLABUS

**Objective:** Using classical vocal training based on each student's ability, coaching of musical selections, and performance opportunities (when applicable), students should be able to demonstrate improved singing and musical ability. For beginner students they will need to learn how to read music and sight sing. These students should be able to demonstrate increased ability to sight read exercises that are assigned each week and in a scheduled final exam of sight singing of unrehearsed exercises. Sight singing training will utilize the series “Sing at First Sight” by Andy Beck. Students are also required to learn how to sing a major and chromatic scale with solfeggio as well as being able to recognize intervals in a major scale.

**Instructional time:** This course is taken for 1 credit, graded A-F or Pass/Fail. One credit = 750 instructional minutes. It can be 15 50-minute lessons plus final or 12 60-minute lessons plus final. You will be expected to memorize two songs for your final

**Grading:** 60% lessons 40% final. Final – 2 songs 35% scales and sight reading/singing - 5%

**Unexcused absences:** 2 lowers grade to B, 3 lowers grade to C, 4 lowers grade to D, 5 is automatic failure. Tardiness: when tardiness equals 30 minutes it will count as one unexcused absence. Attendance deductions are applied first. d. Further deductions: 5-10 point deduction for not fulfilling seminar attendance and singing requirement. See 4d below. 5–10 point deduction for poor preparation for lessons. e. Final performance: Grade based on overall performance, preparation and memory work, general improvement or lack of improvement over the semester.

**Course requirements:** Attendance at lessons on a weekly basis plus a final. Lessons will consist of vocal technique training and vocal repertoire development with sight singing and ear training, It is expected that you will practice a minimum of 2 hours of formal practice per week. You will sing songs that you are preparing in your lesson and receive constructive criticism.

**Absences, Excuses, Make-ups:**

1. Sick Leave for Instructor: Your instructor may need to be absent due to sickness. I will make every effort to make up these lessons. I will send an email and/or text when I will be absent.
2. Make Up Lessons: In this studio there will be no scheduled make up lessons for unexcused absences. You will be allowed **two** make up lessons for excused absences if instructor was given more than 8 hour notice. Make up lessons are typically offered at the end of the semester. It is your decision whether you take the make up lessons that you are offered. There is no negative result from not taking make up lessons.
3. Excused absence: an absence because of illness, family emergency, class conflict, or other acceptable reason AND is preceded by communication to the teacher in advance of the student's lesson. (Phone call, voice mail, email, or text are acceptable) Unexcused absence: an absence for a reason that is not considered adequate (oversleeping, studying, forgetting lesson, going home for the holidays early, etc.) AND/OR a lesson that is missed and no contact is made in advance of the scheduled lesson time.
4. Sickness: If you are sick with something contagious DO NOT COME TO YOUR LESSON, call, text, or email in advance to cancel. If you attend your lesson while sick, I reserve the right to cancel the lesson and send you home. This protects your voice and my health. Take notes during lessons or immediately after your lesson. Performing arts students are expected to build a repertoire notebook.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
