## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

In History of Hip-Hop, students will examine the development of hip-hop music and culture through an in-depth survey of its cultural artifacts, its changing political valence, and its place in broader American society. In order to understand the innovations of hip-hop culture, students will interrogate the economic, racial, societal, and artistic conditions that led to its rise and track its evolution from the B-boys and B-girls of the Bronx in the 70’s through its surge to national popularity in the 80’s, the contentious culture wars around violence and misogyny in gangsta rap in the 90’s, and its status as a global pop culture hegemon and vehicle for radical creativity, self-expression, and protest today. Through listening assignments, scholarly and journalistic readings, and written responses and critiques, students will gain a deep knowledge and understanding of the most significant musical genre of the last forty years. This course satisfies the communication intensive requirement.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Analyze the social, historical, and political contexts of hip-hop music and culture, with a focus on understanding its communicative potential and its ability to articulate individual and group identities.
- Form and articulate clear and well-informed arguments through weekly listening and journaling assignments, formal written responses, and verbal dialogue.
- Write with clarity, precision, and intellectual rigor about popular music and broad cultural, technological, social, and political trends.
- Identify how extra-linguistic signs (sound, style, persona, etc.) can enhance interpersonal communication and community formation.
- Analyze specific communication situations, including audiences and purposes for effective communication.
- Communicate effectively in weekly journals, reading responses, analysis papers, in-class discussion, and oral presentations.
- Identify and apply appropriate genre and design conventions, such as language, images, graphics, typography, organization, and tone.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Class Participation:** In order to take full advantage of the opportunities offered by this course, active and enthusiastic participation in class discussions and activities is necessary. Students are encouraged to ask questions, express opinions, and support similar participation from their peers.

**Quizzes:** Four in-class quizzes are given throughout the semester. These quizzes will test familiarity with the people, groups, terms, texts, and music covered in class as well as the ability to articulate and support arguments.

**Listening Journals:** There will be listenings assigned with each class. On a weekly basis, students will be asked to reflect on these listenings in short journal posts.

**In-Class Workshops and Responses:** In order to work on the application of learned knowledge and skills, there will be in-class projects and activities like “drop the needle” or primary document examination. Other than assigned readings and listenings, there is no extra out-of-class work required for these grades.

**Reading Responses and Analysis Papers:** There will be two reading responses and two analysis papers throughout the term. Reading responses will ask students to demonstrate their ability to summarize and critically engage with scholarly writing on hip-hop. Analysis papers will ask students to perform formal, stylistic, and cultural analysis of specific musical, poetic, or other artistic texts.

**Midterm Paper**: The midterm will ask students to answer one of a set of given prompts which address broad questions of hip-hop’s artistic development, cultural impact, or social/political significance.

**Final Project**: The culminating project of this course will ask students to complete either a research/analysis project or a substantial creative project. The specifics of the project’s topic and focus will be worked out between the student and the instructor. The final project grade includes a proposal, first draft, oral presentation, and final draft.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
