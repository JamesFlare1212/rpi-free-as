## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Basic symbol manipulation skills pertaining to integration.
- Ability to state and explain basic Calculus definitions and theorems and their applications.
- Ability to describe curves parametrically and apply Calculus concepts and techniques to curves described in this way.
- Ability to express curves via polar equations and to solve Calculus problems in the polar coordinate system.
- Ability to apply Calculus to selected problems in Science, Engineering and Mathematics.
- Ability to use convergence tests to analyze the behavior of infinite series.
- Ability to represent functions using power series and solve problems using these representations.
- Ability to describe lines and planes in space in equation form and solve problems using these representations.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are responsible for all material covered in class and recitation, so attendance of every lecture and recitation session is strongly recommended and encouraged. If a student is absent, they should get the class notes and/or recitation examples posted each week on LMS. Students must also submit the in-class problems found at the end of each set of notes and complete the Achieve homework on time regardless of absence. There are no makeups for these assignments for any reason.

Attendance of midterms and quizzes is mandatory, and absence may result in a grade of zero. Students are responsible for knowing the dates and contacting the instructor PRIOR to the quiz or midterm in case of an emergency.

Attendance of Wednesday block is optional. It will be used to go over extra practice problems, answer questions, review for the midterms, and administer makeups. The only time a makeup will be given is during the closest Wednesday block.

### Other Course Policies:

Quizzes: These will be administered during recitation in accordance with the class schedule. Quiz questions will resemble problems from homework, lectures, and previous recitations. Solutions to Achieve problems are available three days after the due date. Lectures and recitation problems are posted on LMS.

Midterms: These will be administered during lecture time in accordance with the class schedule. Midterm information and review sheet will be posted on LMS prior to each midterm. Our TA(s) will go over the review sheet in recitation and/or the Wednesday Block.

Makeups: Missed quizzes and midterms may only be made up if a student has notified the instructor prior to the exam. The quiz or midterm may only be made up during the Wednesday block that follows that quiz or midterm or receive a grade of 0. The makeups will cover the same material; however, they may be made more challenging due to the additional time the students have to prepare.

Homework: Achieve homework assignments will be due twice weekly on Tuesdays and Fridays at 11:59 PM and will be graded automatically by the system. Students may submit these assignments late up to two days after the original deadline, but a penalty will be applied to the score. A 10% penalty will apply to assignments submitted the day after the deadline and a 20% penalty will apply to assignments submitted two days after the deadline. No other due date extensions will be granted for any reason.

In-class problems are assigned each lecture and must be submitted on Gradescope, with access through LMS. They will be due twice weekly on Thursdays and Sundays at 11:59pm. Please verify submission to make sure it goes through. Late submissions will not be accepted for any reason. However, you can email me the picture before the deadline if you experience technical difficulties.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on quizzes and midterms must be the student’s own. In addition, no calculators, computers, tablets, open books, open notes, phones, headphones, or smartwatches may be used during exams. Looking at someone else’s paper, talking or communicating with other students in any way during quizzes and midterms is also prohibited. The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the quiz or midterm. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

Calculator Policy

Calculators will not be allowed on exams. Problems will be designed so that this does not create an issue. Occasionally, a calculator may be required to complete a homework problem, but students should not rely on calculators for simple arithmetic or trigonometric calculations.

Classroom Decorum

Phones may not be used during class and should be silent. Computers and tablets may not be used for anything other than taking notes. Students who are unable to courteously comply with these policies must note that they are probably missing most of the lecture, including practice questions, common errors or hints that may be given about graded assignments, quizzes, or midterms. The pace of the course is fast and it is easy to miss a concept if you're working on something else.

Health and Safety Protocols

Students are required to follow the health and safety protocols of RPI, which are subject to change throughout the semester. Please be courteous to others when feeling sick. Consider wearing a mask, covering your face when sneezing and/or coughing, and washing your hands more frequently.

Visitors

Visitors are prohibited during all class meetings. Only students who are registered for the class should be present in the classroom. Students may only attend class meetings at the time and place listed on their schedule on SIS unless cleared by the instructor.

THERE ARE NO EXTRA CREDIT ASSIGNMENTS GIVEN AT THE END OF THE SEMESTER. All course credit comes from the assignments, quizzes, in-class problems, and midterms assigned and administered regularly throughout the semester. Successful completion of the class indicates an in-depth understanding of the content AND the ability to correctly and precisely communicate this understanding by solving relevant problems using symbolic representation. If you cannot do the actual math, you cannot pass the course.

Getting help: don’t hesitate to ask and get help. You can ask math questions during class, recitation, Wednesday block and office hours of the instructor. If those do not work for you, please don’t hesitate to email either of our TAs to set up a time to meet for tutoring. You can also use email to ask questions. It helps to be as precise as possible and to have a picture of the work you have tried. For example, if you ask a homework question by email, please attach a picture of your work so that I may check it to provide better and faster assistance. There is also tutoring and support offered by ALAC.
