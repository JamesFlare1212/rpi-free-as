## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Directional derivatives, maxima and minima, double integrals, line integrals, div and curl, and Green’s Theorem; matrix algebra and systems of linear equations, vectors and linear transformations in R^n, eigenvectors and eigenvalues, applications in engineering and science.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Calculus: Early Transcendentals, 4th Edition (Rogawski, et al)  
Introduction toLinear Algebra, 5th Edition (Johnson, et al)

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to

- solve optimization problems,
- compute double integrals,
- apply fundamental theorems to compute line integrals,
- solve systems of linear equations,
- find bases for certain subspaces, and
- find the characteristic equation, eigenvalues, and eigenvectors of a matrix and diagonalize a matrix.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will learn derivatives and integrals of functions of multiple variables
- Students will learn vector analysis and Green's Theorem
- Students will learn Gaussian elimination
- Students will learn to compute eigenvalues and eigenvectors of matrices

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Weekly quizzes given in Lecture, and 4 in-class exams. Exam 1 covers the first half of the Multivariable Calculus content and Exam 2 covers all Multivariable Calculus content (including material on Exam 1). Exam 3 covers the first half of the Matrix Algebra content and Exam 4 covers all Matrix Algebra content (including material on Exam 3).

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are responsible for all material covered in lectures whether they are present or not. Attendance is expected in lectures, but is only required whenever there is a quiz.

### Other Course Policies:

**Exams:** The material covered on each exam will be announced on LMS when the date of the exam is near. The dates for Exams 1–4 are listed in **Grading** above. Exam 1 covers the first half of the Multivariable Calculus content and Exam 2 covers all Multivariable Calculus content (including material on Exam 1). Exam 3 covers the first half of the Matrix Algebra content and Exam 4 covers all Matrix Algebra content (including material on Exam 3). This course has **no final exam**.

**Missing an exam should not be taken lightly.** If a student has a pre-scheduled absence (e.g. an athletic competition, official excused absence to attend a conference) on the day of an exam, then they must notify the instructor via email as soon as they learn of the absence and no later than one week before the regularly scheduled exam. Documentation excusing the student from class at the time of the regularly scheduled exam must be received by the instructor before the day of the exam. This documentation must be either an [excused absence documented by the Dean of Students Office (DOSO)](https://doso.rpi.edu/student-support/excused-absence) or, if the absence is due to an athletic competition, documentation from a coach. Provided the above conditions are met, the student will then take a conflict exam at a different time.

Students who miss an exam due to an unforeseen absence (e.g. illness on the day of the exam) must make every effort to notify the instructor **before the exam begins** to avoid grade penalties.

- If a student misses an exam due to an unforeseen absence and notifies the instructor before the exam begins, then the student is eligible to take a make-up exam without penalty.
- If a student misses an exam due to an unforeseen absence and notifies the instructor after the exam begins but before 11:59 PM on the day of their scheduled exam, then the student is eligible to take a make-up exam and a 20% penalty will be applied to the make-up exam score. A higher penalty may be applied to a student with repeated late notifications for missing exams.
- If a student misses an exam due to an unforeseen absence and **fails to notify the instructor before 11:59 PM on the day of their scheduled exam, then the student is** **not** **eligible to take the make-up exam and receives a score of 0 on the exam.**

The make-up exam may differ in format from the regularly scheduled exam. Only students eligible to take the make-up exam (see conditions above) will be given a make-up exam. The make-up exam must be taken within one week of the missed exam. Details regarding the make-up exam will be sent to eligible students the day after the missed exam. There are no make-up exams for the make-up exam.

**Quizzes:** Quizzes will be given typically once a week during lecture on Tuesdays. Material to be covered on each quiz will be posted by the previous Friday in the Homework & Quizzes tab in the Quiz Schedule on [LMS](https://lms.rpi.edu/). Students must take quizzes during the lecture time that is listed on their schedule on SIS. **No make-up quizzes will be given for any reason.** To compensate for this, two (2) in-person quiz scores will be dropped at the end of the semester (and one (1) quiz score for students taking half courses). Students who miss a quiz due to pre-scheduled absence (e.g. official [excused absence documented by the Dean of Students Office (DOSO)](https://doso.rpi.edu/student-support/excused-absence) to attend a conference, athletic competition with documentation from a coach) may be excused from a quiz if they provide documentation to the instructor prior to the missed quiz. A student with two (2) or more pre-scheduled excused absences must contact the instructor before the second excused absence to discuss how their quiz average will be calculated for the course.

**Homework:** Homework will be assigned regularly from the textbooks. Though these problems will not be collected nor graded, they should not be viewed as optional. Success in the course depends on successful completion of all homework. Exam and quiz problems are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples.

**Calculator policy:** Calculators will not be allowed on exams or quizzes. Problems will be designed so that this does not create an issue. Occasionally, a calculator may be required to complete a homework problem, but students should not rely on calculators for simple arithmetic or trigonometric calculations.

**Access and Neurodiverse Student Success (ANSS):** Students with a memorandum from the [Access and Neurodiverse Student Success](https://studenthealth.rpi.edu/list-services/disability-student-services) office identifying accommodations for exams must [email](mailto:browne10@rpi.edu) the memorandum to the instructor by **Monday, January 26**. Students will not be granted accommodations unless a memorandum from ANSS has been received. Providing the accommodations requires advance planning. Students may not be granted accommodations if a memorandum is received on the day of an exam.

**Gradescope:** Gradescope will be used for grading and returning all quizzes and exams. At the start of the semester, you should receive an email that you have been added to the course. You will also be able to access Gradescope by clicking on the Gradescope tab on LMS.

**Grade Appeals:** If upon reviewing the graders’ feedback and posted solutions for an exam a student feels that their work was graded incorrectly, then the student may submit a grading appeal directly on Gradescope. Specific dates will be given for regrade request periods for exams at the time that scores are released. Regrade requests for quizzes will also be available on Gradescope for one week after the quiz scores are released or until the last day of classes, whichever comes first. You may only appeal scores based on factual criteria (e.g. a correct answer was marked wrong, or incorrect rubric item checked in Gradescope). Grade appeals may not be used to request more partial credit or for disagreement with the point values on the rubric.

**Classroom Decorum:** Please adhere to the following during meetings for this course.

- Students must be on time for class and should refrain from leaving and reentering the classroom during class. If a student has a legitimate reason for arriving late to class or being excused early from class, then they should discuss this with the instructor or teaching assistant before class.
- Phones may not be used during class and should be silent.
- Computers and tablets may not be used for anything other than taking notes.
- Visitors are prohibited during all class meetings. Only students who are registered for the class should be present in the classroom.

Students who are unable to courteously comply with these policies may be asked to leave the room.

**Extra credit:** No extra credit opportunities will be available during the course, so be sure to give your best effort on exams and quizzes throughout the semester. Any requests for extra credit will be ignored.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://info.rpi.edu/sites/default/files/Rensselaer%20Handbook%20of%20Student%20Rights%20&%20Responsibilities%20%C2%BB.pdf) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams and quizzes must be the student’s own. A student may not change an answer on an exam or quiz before submitting it for an appeal. In addition, no calculators, computers, tablets, open books, open notes, phones, headphones, or smartwatches may be used during exams or quizzes. Talking or communicating with other students in any way during exams or quizzes is also prohibited. The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
