## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Given observations of complex phenomena, can we extract information about the underlying laws and principles? Can we use data to improve incomplete models of real-world processes? This course will teach students how to use data to develop and refine models by integrating mathematical tools from classical applied mathematics, probability and statistics, and numerical analysis. Progressing systematically from linear equilibrium phenomena to nonlinear time-dependent processes, students will study the limitations of black-blox prediction algorithms, data-driven paradigms for nonlinear and stochastic modeling, the impact of noisy/corrupted data, and the histeretic influence of unresolved variables. Techniques will be illustrated through both elementary and advanced examples from physics, engineering, biology, and network science.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

1. Learning structured linear models from data
   1. Low-rank model recovery
   2. Sparse model recovery
   3. Other select structures (convolutions, conservation laws)
   4. Noisy data and stability of the recovery problem
   5. Explicit and implicit regularization techniques
2. Learning nonlinear models from data
   1. 1D Warm Up: interpolation, regression, and extrapolation
   2. Dictionary embedding and the “kernel trick”
   3. Kernel interpolation and regression
3. Incorporating uncertainty
   1. Gaussian processes (GP) and GP regression
   2. Optimal uncertainty quantification
4. Operator learning
   1. Linear algebra in infinite-dimensions
   2. Learning Green’s functions of elliptic partial differential equations
   3. The Random Feature model
5. Learning dynamical models from data
   1. Reduced-order models for time-dependent systems
   2. The multiverse of Dynamic Mode Decompositions (DMD)
   3. The Koopman operator formalism and data-driven spectral analysis
   4. Data-driven approaches to external forcing and linear response
   5. Models with memory and the role of unresolved variables

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Reasoning about models constructed from real-world data
- Selecting algorithms to construct mathematical models from real-world data
- Analyzing structured models (low-rank and sparse linear models, nonlinear manifolds, autonomous time-dependent processes, influence of external forcing and unresolved variables) and the "recovery" problem, including the quantifiable characteristics of the data that influence existence, uniqueness, and well-posedeness (stability).
- Analyzing algorithms for recovering and approximating structured models from data, including recovery guarantees, stability of recovery, and approximation properties.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

4 homeworks (1D approximation theory, Linear models, Nonlinear models, Time-dependent models) approximately 2-3 weeks apart, Final project on topic of student's choosing (due April 22), in-class presentation on final report topic (April 24 / 27).
