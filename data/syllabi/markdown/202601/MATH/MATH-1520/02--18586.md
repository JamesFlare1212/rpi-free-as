## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course introduces some mathematical methods which have been widely adopted in management and economics. Topics include the introductory linear algebra, and other analytical techniques needed for further study in probability, statistics, and operations research.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to demonstrate the ability to solve linear systems using Echelon Method and Gauss-Jordan elimination.
- Students will be able to demonstrate the knowledge of matrix algebra, e.g. addition, subtraction, and multiplication of matrices.
- Students will be able to demonstrate the ability to compute the inverse of a non-singular matrix and use it to solve a linear system.
- Students will be able to demonstrate the knowledge of linear programming by using graphical and simplex methods.
- Students will be able to understand the principles of game theory and find the Nash equilibrium.
- Students will be able to understand the principles of building a neural network.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are responsible for all material covered in class so attendance of every lecture and recitation session is strongly recommended and encouraged. If a student is absent, they should get the class notes posted each week on LMS, submit their in-class problem(s) and keep up with the homework assignments on Pearson by the regular deadline.

Attendance of recitations and exams is mandatory, and an absence may result in a grade of zero. Students must notify their TA prior to missing recitation and their instructor prior to missing an exam with a valid excuse.

### Other Course Policies:

Exams: Exam problems will consist of problems for which the students must show all their work, as well as multiple-choice, multiple-select, true/false, and fill-in-the-blank questions. Exam problems are frequently similar to homework assignments, lecture examples, and problems from recitations.

Makeups: Missed exams may only be made up if a student has notified the instructor prior to the exam with a valid excuse. Exams must be made up within a week of the date the exam was given unless a medical excuse can be provided. The 3rd and last exam will be given on the last day of class; therefore, a makeup exam will be given only in rare cases of emergency.

Homework: Pearson homework assignments will be due twice weekly on Tuesdays and Fridays at 11:59 PM and will be graded automatically by the system. Students may submit these assignments late up to three days after the original deadline, but a 50% penalty will be applied to the score of the questions submitted after the due date. No other due date extensions will be granted for any reason. To further compensate for this, the lowest homework assignment score will be dropped at the end of the semester.

In-class problems are assigned each lecture and must be submitted on Gradescope, with access through LMS. They will be due twice weekly on Thursdays and Sundays at 11:59pm. Please verify submission to make sure it goes through. Late submissions will not be accepted for any reason. However, you can email me the picture before the deadline if you experience technical difficulties.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams must be the student’s own. In addition, no computers, tablets, open books, open notes, phones, headphones, or smartwatches may be used during exams. Looking at another student’s exam, talking or communicating with other students in any way during exams is also prohibited. The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the exam. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.

Students are required to have a calculator for this course to use on exams, in class, and in recitation. Mobile devices will not be allowed on exams to use as calculators. If a student does not have a calculator for an exam, one will NOT be provided.

THERE ARE NO EXTRA CREDIT ASSIGNMENTS GIVEN AT THE END OF THE SEMESTER. All course credit comes from the assignments, participation, and exams assigned and administered regularly throughout the semester. The successful completion of the class indicates an in-depth understanding of the content AND the ability to correctly and precisely communicate this understanding by solving relevant problems using symbolic representation.
