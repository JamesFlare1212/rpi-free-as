## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course description:** This course focuses on the mathematical and computational fundamentals as well as the applications of classical finite element methods and discontinuous Galerkin methods. The methods are presented through their formulations, matrix assembly for implementation, discrete spaces and approximation theories, stability, and error estimates. They will be applied to numerically solve elliptic/parabolic equations, convection-diffusion equations, and kinetic transport equations in one and higher dimensions. Galerkin-projection based reduced order models may also be discussed.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- To understand the mathematics behinds the classical finite element methods and discontinuous Galerkin methods, as well as some related methodologies and concepts: formulation, matrices assembly for implementation, discrete spaces, approximation theory, stability, error estimates
- To carry out standard mathematical/numerical analysis and derivations
- To implement and to evaluate numerical methods for one- and two-dimensional problems for various PDE models; To extract mathematically meaningful information from numerical observations

### Other Course Policies:

**Policy on collaboration and on use of external sources (e.g. papers, books, non-AI products):** You are encouraged to discuss homework problems with others, or to refer to online or library references. However, the work you submit for grading must be written by yourself and represents your own understanding on the subjects. If your work involves significant help from other people, reference books or papers, explicitly state this in your report to avoid penalty in grade.  
**Policy on using AI tools:** For a course (like ours) focused on the mathematical and computational foundations of numerical algorithms, it is essential for us to think through the algorithm formulation and derivation mathematically, implement an algorithm numerically via computer programming, and analyze the findings and observations and finally connect back to theoretical results.  
Using artificial intelligence (AI) tools

- to complete the analytical or theoretical part of your homework report, or
- to write your findings, observations, discussions and summaries related to the numerical components of your work,

is not allowed and will be treated as a violation of the Academic Integrity Policy. Limited AI assistance is allowed solely for improving non-mathematical writing clarity (e.g., grammar or phrasing), not for generating content.  
Building computer codes from scratch is crucial to your learning and genuine mastery of the subject. Using AI tools as code-vending-machines is not allowed and will be treated as a violation of the Academic Integrity Policy. Appropriate use of AI tools is permitted when they assist rather than replace you and your work, such as helping to debug code or explore optimization ideas to improve code structures or performance.  
If AI tools are used, one needs to explicitly state, e.g., which/how AI tools are used in your report.
