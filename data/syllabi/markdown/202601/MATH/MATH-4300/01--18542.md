## Course Content

### Content Details:

An introduction to the theory and applications of complex variables. Topics include  
complex numbers, complex functions and analyticity, elementary functions, complex integration, series  
representation, residue theory, conformal mappings, transforms in applied mathematics.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- A student successfully completing the course will be able to 1) carry out standard mathematical analysis and derivation for complex variables,
- 2\) develop analytical reasoning skills and gain practice in formulating logical arguments,
- 3\) gain some familiarity with how complex variables provide connections between geometry and analysis,
- and apply complex variable techniques to solve certain kinds of problems in the applied sciences.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

1. Your course grade is determined by your performance in homework assignments, exams, in-class work, designed based on the Course Objectives and with the following distribution: homework (32%), exams (22.5%+22.5% for midterms, 25% for the final), and in-class work sessions (5%)
2. How is your in-class work graded? Suppose n\_{total} sets of in-class work are collected, and you submit n\_{submitted} sets, you will receive k% for your in-class work, provided n\_{submitted}/n\_{total} in ((k-1)/5, k/5], with k=1, 2, 3, 4,5.
3. The grading scale is  
   92-100 (A), 89-91(A-), 86-88(B+), 83-85(B), 80-82(B-), 76-79(C+)  
   73-75(C), 70-72(C-), 60-69(D+), 50-59(D), <50(F)
4. Graduate students are graded in the same way for scores of 70% and above, but they will receive a grade of F for scores between 0-69%
5. Your course grade can be enhanced, i.e., with up to 5 percentage points awarded, by your insightful and thoughtful input and questions, or your active and substantive contribution to discussions and to the collective learning.

### Other Course Policies:

**Policy on Late Submission:**  
Each homework is due by a specified time date. Homework reports must be submitted as single documents on LMS/gradescope. Late submission will result in reduction in grade of  
5%: on the same day of deadline, at most up to 1/3 of a day (i.e. 8 hours); 10%: up to one day; 20%: up to two days  
No late homework is accepted after two days, or after reference solutions are posted on LMS, whichever comes first. Any request for extension without penalty needs to be made at least **24 hours**  
before the deadline.  
**Exams:**

- No books, notes, crib/cheat sheet, or any electronic or other devices are permitted. No collaboration of any kind is allowed. Any violation will result in a grade of zero for the exam, and a report will also be made to the Dean of Students Office.
- Missing an exam without a legitimate excuse will result in a grade of zero and can not be made up. If you have an excuse, you should contact the instructor as soon as possible.
- A student who is substantially late to an exam without advance approval may be denied the opportunity to take the exam. Being substantially late means being half an hour late (or longer) for a midterm exam, and an hour late (or longer) for the final exam.
- **Reminder:** *Make yourself familiar with various institute deadlines (e.g. W grade, P/NC etc). You can discuss with the instructor if any concern arises regarding the exam dates especially when the actual dates are announced in class.*

**Questions regarding the grading** of assignments and exams must be discussed with the instructor *within two days* from your reports being available (an email notification will be sent to the class). For homework assignments, regrade request can be submitted within a two-day time window.  
**On Email Correspondence:** Any email to the instructor is expected to consist of the greeting, the text of the email, and the name of the sender.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Policy on Collaboration and on Use of External Sources (e.g. papers, books, AI tools):** You are encouraged to discuss homework problems with other students or work in groups, or to refer to online or library reference. However, the work you submit must be written by yourself and represent your own independent understanding on the subjects. If your work involves significant help from other people, online sources, reference books or papers, it is required that you **explicitly state** this in your report to avoid penalty in grade.  
It is strictly forbidden **to copy any part** of the homework solutions from others (including classmates, members in your study group, books, online solutions etc), or to use artificial intelligence (AI) tools (including AI writing tools) to complete your homework assignments. Such behavior will be considered a violation of the Academic Integrity Policy, and will result in **no grade for the entire homework set** (of all involved individuals if there is any), and a report will also be made to the Dean of Students Office.  
Students may be called for interviews with the instructor to verify that their work represents their own thinking and understanding in order to keep the originally assigned grade.
