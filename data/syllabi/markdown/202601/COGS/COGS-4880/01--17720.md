## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This class will address some of the central scientific (not technological!) problems and issues in understanding human cognition and modeling it using digital computers.  
The class will be devoted to the requirements for and approaches to modeling human capacities of perception, reasoning, decision-making and action in artificial agents that perceive, interpret the results of perception, think, communicate, act and are capable of explaining all of the above.

We will also address methodological issues related to developing cognitive systems as well as selected societal and philosophical issues related to this area of research and development.

The class will combine lectures and reading-oriented classwork.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course will familiarize students with issues related to the development of intentional artificial intelligent agents. It will help the students to appreciate the challenges faced by intelligent agents that are intended to serve as full-fledged members of human-AI teams and thus tasked with efficiently collaborating and fluently conversing with people. It will prepare students for further study in the areas of general computational cognitive modeling, cognitive robotics and intelligent agent implementation as well as natural language understanding. This course is not devoted to building practical skills. It will concentrate on learning and understanding new concepts, theories, methods and knowledge content.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Students will have sufficient breadth and depth of knowledge of agent construction to pursue scholarship in related areas. • Students will have a deep appreciation for the challenges involved in building human-level intelligent agents. • Students will understand why computational cognitive modeling is a difficult problem and different ways that the field has tried to solve – or avoid – that problem. • Students will understand how work on computational cognitive modeling has evolved over time in response to both scientific and extra-scientific (e.g., technological) factors. • Students will understand why generative AI will not offer the entire answer to human-level artificial intelligence. • Students will gain an in-depth understanding of the program of R&D on language-endowed intelligent agents and cognitive robotics, sufficient to prepare them to carry out work within this paradigm. • Students will have a better appreciation for the scientific process as it actually plays out in the world, not as an idealized abstraction. • Students will gain practice in learning through reading and discussion rather than primarily through lecture, which is important for their professional development.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
