## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

How do people navigate along a crowded sidewalk, walk over rocky terrain, or drive along a winding road? How do skilled athletes, musicians, and dancers move with such efficiency and precision? What goes wrong when the ability to perceive or move is impaired, and how can these abilities be restored? Why is it so challenging to build machines that can perform the same activities most of us carry out effortlessly? This course is an advanced undergraduate-/graduate-level survey of the science of perception and action. It is designed for students in the psychological and cognitive sciences, as well as those in other fields who want to better understand how people perceive and interact with the world. Students will explore how perceptual and motor systems work together to support everyday activities and skilled performance, how impairments affect these abilities, and how this knowledge informs applications in robotics, human–machine interaction, VR/AR, games, and the training and rehabilitation of motor and sports skills.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

There is no textbook for this course. The reading material for each class will be divided into background reading and reading for class discussion. For the most part, background reading will be optional or recommended but not required. It is provided in case you want to follow up on one of the lecture topics. Occasionally, background reading will be mandatory to fill in gaps in the lecture. This will be stated in class. The reading for class discussion will include one target article, which everyone should read, and one or two additional papers, each of which will be read and presented by one student. Occasionally, you will be asked to watch a video of a talk rather than or in addition to reading a paper. All required reading material will be available on LMS.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- LO1: Students who successfully complete this course will be able to critically evaluate, synthesize, and conduct research in perception and action, integrating findings from multiple studies and/or original empirical or computational work into coherent theoretical accounts.
- LO2: Students who successfully complete this course will be able to explain how perceptual and motor systems work, including the major theories, key empirical findings, and ongoing theoretical debates in the field, and use this knowledge to motivate research questions or models.
- LO3: Students who successfully complete this course will be able to apply concepts and methods from perception and action research to design, implement, and interpret a small-scale research project addressing a meaningful scientific or practical problem.
- LO4: Students who successfully complete this course will be able to communicate research ideas and outcomes effectively, presenting original or synthesized work clearly and persuasively to both scientific and general audiences.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are expected to attend all classes in person and to participate in class discussions. There is no option to attend class remotely. Each new topic will be introduced during lecture; therefore, students who miss class will need to spend significantly more time outside of class to keep up with the material.

In addition, students cannot earn points toward their participation grade (which constitutes 10% of the final grade) if they do not attend class. If a student misses class on a day when a quiz is given or when they are scheduled to give a presentation, they will receive no credit for that quiz or presentation. Quizzes and presentations cannot be made up at a later time.

If a student must miss class for a legitimate reason, they should contact the instructor in advance by email or in person during office hours whenever possible. Students who miss more than one week of classes due to illness will be required to provide a doctor’s note or other supporting documentation.

### Other Course Policies:

**AI POLICY**  
For paper presentations, the use of AI is limited to clarifying terminology, concepts, or methods after you read the paper yourself. You may experiment with having AI generate practice questions to check your understanding but bear in mind that the AI interpretation may not be accurate. You are responsible for any errors, misinterpretations, or hallucinations produced by AI tools. You may not use AI to summarize the paper instead of reading it or to generate content for presentation slides or talking points. All content must reflect your own understanding of the assigned paper. You must be able to answer detailed questions about the paper without relying on AI.  
For the research project, you may use AI to help brainstorm or sharpen research questions, search for relevant papers, debug code, and provide feedback on the clarity of content that you generate for presentations. You may not use AI to design your experiment, model, or reanalysis of data. You must be able to explain and justify all choices that you make about your project.  
If you use AI to prepare your presentations or assist you in your research project, you must include a note about how it was used. If you are unsure about whether a particular form of AI use is acceptable, ask the instructor in advance.  
**EMAIL**  
For the purposes of this course, email should be used as a tool for scheduling a time to meet with me if office hours conflict with your schedule. Please include times during which you are available to meet and a brief description of the purpose of the meeting. In general, I will not respond to emails asking for further explanation of material covered in class or the reading material, or for clarification on assignments. This policy encourages you to ask questions about these matters during class time and to seek help by meeting with me in person, which is far more effective than trying to get help via email.

**USE OF ELECTRONIC DEVICES**  
Use of laptops, cell phones, and other electronic devices during lectures is strongly discouraged, even for the purposes of note-taking. These devices interfere with learning and can be a distraction to other members of the class. Recent research suggests that students learn more effectively when they take notes by hand using paper and pencil (or possibly, a tablet with a stylus) than on a laptop. Laptops and tablets may be used during class discussions for viewing the reading materials.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

**COURSE FORMAT**  
The format of this course will be a combination of lecture and class discussion. In general, the first ~45 minutes of each class will be set aside for student presentations and class discussions of reading material related to the topic of the lecture from the previous class. The remainder of the class period will be used to introduce the next topic on the syllabus (via lecture).  
**QUIZZES**  
Quizzes will comprise multiple choice questions and will assess your understanding of the material covered in each unit of the course. Quizzes will be given at the very beginning of six of the classes, so it is important that you arrive on time. If you do not complete a quiz before the window closes, you will receive a zero for that quiz. Quizzes may not be rescheduled (no exceptions) but your lowest quiz grade will be dropped. During quizzes, you may refer to one sheet of hand-written notes that you prepared. You may not refer to other notes, research articles, or the internet.  
**RESEARCH PROJECT**  
Students will work individually to complete a research project on some topic of their interest related to perception and action. The project could involve a literature review, small-scale experiment or demo, a model, or an analysis of an existing data set. Others options will be considered but require instructor approval. Some class time will be set aside for students to work on their projects, but most of the work will be done outside of class. Each student will give a presentation on their idea for the project, a progress report, and a final presentation.
