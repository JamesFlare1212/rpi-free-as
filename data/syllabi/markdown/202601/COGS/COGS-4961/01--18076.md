## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is designed for students who are already working on a project or research task which involves the use of AI technologies (or in some cases, for students not yet involved in a project but ready to join others in an ongoing or new one). AI alignment, defined as “the process of encoding human values and goals into AI models to make them as helpful, safe and reliable as possible,” applies to any project in which an AI system is either working with, working for, or producing results intended for human interaction. This course will be a combination of student presentations of ongoing research, lectures (including visiting lecturers), and other interaction among those who are working on their projects.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

## Course Goals

### Goals:

AI systems, especially LLMs, are not intelligent and are not human -- although often terms that are used to describe human intelligence get improperly used when talking about AI (intention, conciousness, etc). By the end of this course, students will better understand these differences and how they can better align the outputs of their programs with the abilities of humans for a stronger and more ethical interaction.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The course will meet on Mondays and Thursdays in room AE 217. After the first couple of weeks, one session will be "traditional class" and the other a working lab session. At first students will be required to attend the lab sessions, but later on project groups will be able to determine whether to meet at these times or on their own -- however, at least the amount of time of a class session, per week, will be expected of lab groups.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to discuss Ai technologies in appropriate forms, avoiding, where possible, "suitcase words" (a sub-outcome will be students being able to define and identify suitcase words)
- Students will be able to present the ongoing work in their projects, to both make suggestions to others (constructively) and to take constructive criticism in turn.
- Ongoing student projects, those not completed during the term (which is not a requirement) will be able to express their objectives, status, future work, and issues to be addressed. Those finishing a project (within defined bounds) will be able to explain what it does, how it works, and how it aligns with human cognitive needs.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students will be judged based on group presentations of their projects in specific sections (goals, status, outcomes to date, etc) with guidance as to what is to be projected. All students in a project will be expected to contribute.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

The course grade will be determined by participation in the projects and outcomes, including a short final demo and poster. The determination will not be made based on completion or success of the project, but by how it had progressed, met the objectives of the course, and identified future issues.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are expected to attend classes - if they can't, they are expected to inform the instructor (by email or webex) beforehand. For project presentation dates, participation is required, so students unable to make such a date will need to work ith the other students in their own, or other, project(s) to determine a date.

### Other Course Policies:

There are no exams in this course. Student progress will be measured by how the presentations, projects, and demos evolve over the course of the term.  
All students in a project will receive the same grade, except in exceptional circumstances to be discussed with the professor in advance.  
The course is intended to dovetail well with the RPAI student club -- club attendance is not required, but it is encouraged. In addition, there may be some joint speakers who will either present during class (with club members invited) or present during club meeting hours, with students in the class "strongly" encouraged to attend.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

This being a project oriented course, it is obviously the case that students in a project will be expected to work together. In addition, students will be encouraged (and rewarded) for sharing techniques, coding tricks, development approaches or other such with other students. Use of code found on the web, in githubs, Hugging Face or other open source code repositories. Using code that has IP limitations, or requesting IP limitations for projects (for example, if students are considering potential entrepreneurial activities related to their project) must be cleared with the instructor in advance.

Use of AI in code generation or project presentations is allowed, and in fact encouraged, with the proviso that what system and how it is used is revealed in presentation, demonstration or other submissions.
