## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Description**

In this class, students will write on topics from their major discipline and investigate the kinds of research texts that professionals in their field produce. The course will emphasize research writing as an ongoing conversation with other researchers. Students will (1) use discipline-specific library databases, (2) identify genre conventions from their major disciplines, (3) develop literature reviews to identify “gaps” for research questions, and (4) write research proposals. In addition, they will develop effective note-taking and research skills and learn strategies for effective prose style.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Objectives**

In this class, students will:

- Analyze genre conventions for research writing in their field of study and articulate how those conventions respond to specific audiences and purposes. This will be demonstrated through a Genre Analysis Paper, in-class activities, reading comprehension questions, and a final reflection.
- Apply genre conventions appropriate to their field of research to strategically communicate to an academic audience in several common formats related to research writing. This will be demonstrated through a literature review, research proposal, and a research presentation.
- Recognize research as an ongoing conversation and describe academic conversations in their major fields of study. This will be demonstrated through a Literature Review, Research Proposal, and a Research Presentation. In-class activities and reading comprehension questions scaffold students into these assignments.
- Organize academic sources into a literature review for an academic audience. This will be demonstrated through a Literature Review, Research Proposal, and a Research Presentation.
- Develop research questions and construct a research proposal for an academic audience to explore questions pertinent to their academic field of study. This will be demonstrated through a Literature Review, Research Proposal, and a Research Presentation. In-class activities and reading comprehension questions scaffold students into these assignments.

**Reading Comprehension Questions (15 Due x 8 points=120 points/12%. Graded on complete/incomplete**). These are out-of-class activities in which you will respond to questions about course readings or apply concepts that we learned about in class, such as finding sources. They are **150-words,** and they are due ***before*** class because we will engage with your answers in class. Rather than having you write responses to everything in every reading, these are targeted questions and activities to ensure that you understand key concepts and they are meant to scaffold you into larger assignments. These are not graded on grammar or correctness. However, **you must respond all parts of the question for full points**.

**In-Class Activities (10 Due x 6 points=60 points/6%. Graded on complete/incomplete**). These are in-class activities like quick-writes and other informal activities that are meant to scaffold you into your major assignments. The portals to turn in these in-class activities will open during class time and close by the end of class time. **You must be in class to receive points for in-class activities**. The in-class practice on citation will be graded on correctness. With that one exception, the other in-class activities are not graded on grammar or correctness. If you complete the assignment fully, you will receive full points.

**Peer Reviews** **(3 total x 40 points each=120 points/12%. Graded on complete/incomplete).**

For peer reviews, you will bring in nearly complete drafts of your upcoming assignments. These are meant for you to practice getting feedback from actual readers (your peers) and myself. These are also meant for you to practice putting yourself in the position of the reader. Answer the peer review questions for your peers and write 3-5 things you will change for the final draft. **You must be in class to receive points for peer review.**

**Percipio: Scholarship as conversation Module: (21.5 points) (due by 11:59 PM on Feb. 23)**

Scholarship as Conversation Tutorial. Turn in 1) screenshot of your score and 2) your Certificate due. When you have finished the tutorial, DOWNLOAD the certificate and take a screenshot of your score to turn in.

**Genre Analysis Paper (183 points, 18.3%).**

In this major assignment, you will locate **2** academic scholarly, peer-reviewed research articles that use **empirical methods** from a journal in your field and conduct a genre analysis of the articles. Broadly, the paper will answer the question: What key features characterize academic research writing in your field? How do these features fulfil the purpose of academic articles in these journals and how do they meet academic audiences’ expectations? The paper will analyze global organization of articles, content included in articles, and sentence-structure elements. It will be in APA format and be at least **1,000** words (before citations).

**Literature Review (183 points, 18.3%).**

In this major assignment, you will **write a literature review** about a topic in your field that leads to a research question which you could feasibly answer in this field. It will include at least **8 scholarly**, peer-reviewed sources, and it will be about **1,500 words** long (before citations). Rather than simply summarizing previous studies, the literature review will put scholars into conversation with each other and use clear topic sentences to outline themes and debates.

**Research Proposal (210 points, 21%).**

In this major assignment, you will write a research proposal. You may build from your literature review and research question in your previous assignment. The rest of the proposal will address methodology (optional), methods, and the significance of the research. Since this is *not* a methods class, I will *not* be looking to see if your methods are accurate or appropriate for your field. Instead, I am looking to see that you have a well-developed research plan that you can write about for an educated audience. It will include at least **8 scholarly**, peer-reviewed sources, and it will be about **2,000 words** long (before citations).

**Research Colloquium Presentation (80 points, 8%).**

The research colloquium presentation is an opportunity for you to share with your peers your research proposal. The presentation will be 8-10 minutes long. You will need to shorten your literature review and focus on ***your*** proposed research. You will also turn in a PowerPoint **before class** with this presentation. You should exhibit basic presentation skills, including speaking loud enough, organizing your speech, and basic slide design. You will sign up for one of two days to present, to give everyone enough time to present. However, everyone’s PowerPoints will be due on the ***first*** day of presentations by class time to ensure that the people who have later presentation days are not unfairly privileged.

**Final Reflection (10 points, 1%).**

The final reflection will be completed in-class as your final exam. As with in-class assignments, the portal to turn in the in-class activities will open during class time and close by the end of class time. **You must be in class to receive points for in-class activities**. It will not graded on grammar or correctness. If you complete the assignment fully, you will receive full points.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity Statement**

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs.

RPI’s Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities states:

“If found responsible for committing academic dishonesty, a student may be subject to one or both types of penalties: **an academic (grade) penalty administered by the professor and/or disciplinary action through the Rensselaer judicial process described in this handbook**.

Academic dishonesty is a violation of the Grounds for Disciplinary Action as described in this handbook. A student may be subject to any of the following types of disciplinary action should disciplinary action be pursued by the instructor: disciplinary warning; disciplinary probation; disciplinary suspension, expulsion and/or alternative actions as agreed on by the student and hearing officer. It should be noted that no student who allegedly commits academic dishonesty will be able to drop or change the grade option for the course in question and is not eligible to request an F examination for the course. The definitions and examples presented below are a sampling of types of academic dishonesty and are not to be construed as an exhaustive or exclusive list. The academic integrity policy applies to all students, undergraduate and graduate, and to scholarly pursuits and research. Additionally, attempts to commit academic dishonesty or to assist in the commission or attempt of such an act are also violations of this policy.”

You can read more about the judicial process here: https://www.rpi.edu/dept/doso/resources/policies/Fillable2015-2016Version2AcademicIntegrityBrochure.pdf

For this class, I differentiate between misuse of sources and intentional plagiarism. The extent of the infraction will determine my response. For example, minor misuse of sources may result in you having to rewrite the paper and take a late penalty. Papers purchased online may result in an F in the class and disciplinary action through the RPI judicial process.

For clarity, all of your papers should have (1) a works cited page at the end of a paper ***and*** (2) in-text citations. Materials that you **must cite in-text** include: (1) Quotations, paraphrases, and summaries of words, ideas, and opinions of others, (2) Visual and statistical material and computer programming code derived from others (3) Facts not widely known and arguable claims (4) Help from others.

As an upper-division class, you are expected to know how to cite sources. For this class, you may choose between MLA or APA. You can read more about how to use both through the OWL Purdue. If you have any other questions about citations, I suggest you take your paper to COMM+D (see below).

You may not use **copy and paste language from Artificial Intelligence, such as ChatGPT, DALL-E, etc. into your assignments, nor may you use fictious sources. Both will result in an automatic 0 for the assignment, and you may *not* make up these assignments**.

Since ChatGPT frequently makes up information, **if statements in your paper are false, there will be a penalty, up to and including a 0 for the assignment**.

To avoid situations where there is concern about whether you have used ChatGPT, ***I highly recommend keeping time-stamped drafts of your assignments to demonstrate that you have drafted the paper***. An easy way to do to this would be to use Google docs or another cloud storage service that can restore previous versions of your document

**Grading Reporting, Missed Work, and Late Work Policy**

Grades will be reported via Blackboard, and most assignments will have a portion submitted in learning management system. Some work will be completed in class, especially for in-class assignments for participation. All major assignments are due by the announced date and time except in the event of a medical or personal emergency. **For late work for major assignments, 10% (one letter grade) will be deducted for each 24-hour period as soon as the deadline has passed. This means, if the assignment was due at 10AM and you turn it in at 10:01AM, it will be considered late. I will continue to deduct 10% from the assignment for every 24-hour period until a grade of F is reached.** Some minor assignments cannot be made up. You must be in class to receive credit for peer reviews, and they must be turned in on time to avoid negatively impacting your peer.
