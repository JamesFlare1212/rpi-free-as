## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The novel in stories is a flexible, hybrid form that bridges the gap between short stories and longer narratives such as the novella and the novel. In this advanced fiction course, will study numerous outstanding examples of the genre, grappling with the goals and techniques of the short story form as well as the driving forces and structural devices that comprise the novel. Craft-oriented readings will accompany the primary literature. Assignments include creative writing, craft analysis, and peer critiques. Students will leave the course with several stories/chapters, a novel synopsis, and a complete outline for a book-length work of fiction.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Students will demonstrate knowledge of fiction writing in general and the short story in particular by utilizing appropriate terminology to analyze and discuss published fiction
- • Students will differentiate between craft techniques of the novel and that of the short story.
- • Students will demonstrate knowledge of the history, craft, and breadth of the novel in stories as a hybrid genre.
- • Students will demonstrate craft-based reading strategies through annotations, short analytical responses, and discussions.
- • Students will demonstrate intermediate skills in drafting, peer critique, and revision.
- • Students will actively engage in a creative community of peers through participation in workshop and discussion.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Reading.** Reading should be an active endeavor, and your preparedness and additions to discussions will be evaluated as class participation.

**Craft Analyses Worksheets (4):** For each category of assigned texts, you will complete a worksheet template that asks you to analyze what strategies the text uses to link a collection of short stories in novelistic ways. On discussion day, you will help facilitate the discussion of that text alongside a cohort of peers.

**Peer End Comments.** You will write peer feedback letters that take an editorial stance. Feedback is most helpful if it makes specific points. It's least helpful when it deals in generalizations.

**Novel Portfolio:** You will begin with several preparatory worksheets to help you design your book. At the end of the term, you will develop these into a complete outline for a novel in stories, including a book synopsis, table of contents, and a brief description of each story. Your portfolio will also include the three stories written/revised in this course, and optionally, additional original stories you’ve previously written.

**Craft Presentation:** You will read an article of your choice on fictional craft, present it to the class, and lead a writing exercise designed to practice this craft element.

**Drafts:** You will write two new stories for this course. “New” is defined as any story that has not yet been workshopped. Students may then choose between revising a story workshopped in a previous class or writing a third new story.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

This is a small seminar class, the success of which is entirely dependent on active student presence and participation. Everyone has two “freebie” absences to use for sleep, self-care, or other obligations, such as interviews or conferences, without point penalty. Therefore, I do not routinely distinguish between excused and unexcused absences. I expect that you will come to class (school athletes see me at the beginning of the term). Each absence beyond the second one will drop your grade by ten points (1%). If you miss more than five classes, you will receive no credit for the course even if you are otherwise passing. Note that three tardies of half hour or more accrue to one absence.

### Other Course Policies:

Students who are within 4 points of the next final grade step and have 0-1 absences will be awarded 4 additional points.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
