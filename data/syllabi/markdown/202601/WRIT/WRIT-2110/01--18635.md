## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course helps students learn to write effectively, convincingly, and appropriately for a variety of situations, tasks, and goals. Each week you will be presented with a different rhetorical strategy to master. We will engage each strategy by examining works crafted by esteemed writers who utilize specific modes of writing. You will practice and hone these strategies through assignments to be completed both in and outside of class. Collaborative discussion and small group work will be essential, assuring that you receive adequate feedback and that you implement your strategies in ways that convince and/or communicate meaning to an audience or team. In completing oral and written assignments, you will demonstrate competence in engaging one or more strategies, assuring you are prepared to write effectively and carefully throughout your college career and beyond.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Comprehend and Inquire: Show an understanding and recognition of a plethora of effective means of strategic modes and be able to respond through meaningful critical inquiry, formulating both questions and answers.
- Assess different writing tasks and plan your approach: Craft written texts for a variety of different situations, with attention to the preparation process which may include free-writing, brainstorming, assessing sources, outlining, drafting, and revising according to peer and instructor feedback. Learn to take criticism and revise for a variety of concerns, not limited to sophistication, clarity, and style.
- Engage diverse writing modes: Learn to identify and implement specific writing strategies to effectively communicate your ideas and/or findings to an audience.
- Organize your written and oral work: Present and compose texts that effectively argue, compare, define, describe or analyze in a thorough, convincing manner, and in a way that considers tone, voice, genre, and style. Overall, learn to communicate effectively and appropriately, in a way that is appropriate for the situation and audience.
- Create your own texts/arguments and hone your scholarly voice: Through practice, class work, assignments, and reflection, you will hone your written and oral performance, understanding how to approach assignments, gather and analyze research materials and/or evidence, synthesize materials, and finally composing your written texts using one or more rhetorical strategies with confidence.
- Explain your strategies: Reflect on your own writing in a way that shows an understanding of your topic, anticipating questions, concerns, or issues that may arise from an audience, group, or team.
- Engage diverse writing modes: Learn to identify and implement specific writing strategies to effectively communicate your ideas and/or findings to an audience.

**Strategy Practice: due most Mondays:** On certain weeks when we learn a new writing strategy, I will present a task in class. These tasks must be completed by the following Monday before the beginning of class, typed, and turned in on LMS. ***Late entries will receive up to 50% credit but will not be accepted after a week beyond the due date. The rubric for strategy practice assignments is copied below:***

2 points: The task demonstrates exceptional written work that is clear, concise, and shows critical thinking, with minimal to no grammar mistakes or typos. The task is completely finished.

1 point: The task is only partially complete, and/or needs more work to reach adequate or excellent style, level of critical thinking, and full development. This score also applies to work that is well-developed but is difficult to understand due to typos, grammar issues, and/or poor diction.

0 points: The task is lacking in style, development, grammar, and sophistication. This score may also apply to a task that does not follow directions properly or completely.

**Mini Essay:** This will be a short critical writing assignment (2-3 pages) that explores one’s experience of an environmental concern, theme, or detail, relating to a text from class. Major assignments (such as the mini essay, the course research paper, or the oral presentation) will receive a **grade deduction of 10% per day when turned in after the due date.**

**Course Research Paper:** The capstone writing project for the course will be a research paper where you analyze a question, a concern, or an issue related to our course theme, perform research and synthesize your findings, form an opinion, and argue a specific point (6-8 pages). You will write to participate in a conversation regarding ecocriticism as it relates to your particular subtopic within the field. Major assignments (such as the mini essay, the course research paper, or the oral presentation) will receive a **grade deduction of 10% per day when turned in after a due date.**

**Preparation Assignments:** A research paper cannot be completed well in a day or two. Thus, the preparation assignments ensure you are well-prepared and pacing yourself, progressing on the steps needed to build throughout the semester to the finished product. Preparation assignments should be typed and turned in on LMS. ***Late entries will receive up to 50% credit but will not be accepted after a week beyond the due date nor after the final paper due date has passed.***

**Oral Presentation:** The presentation will be based on your final paper research. You will present your findings to your peers and persuade them of your argument. (Students must be prepared to present on the first day of presentations although not all will be able to present on the first day.) **Not being ready to present will be counted as lateness and will receive a 10% grade deduction for class days attended unprepared.**

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance Policy: Attendance is mandatory** as well as an important factor for your success in this course. All students are granted **four absences** without penalty, no questions asked. This does not mean that you should plan on missing four classes. Missing classes means that you do not benefit from valuable class discussions and lectures, placing your learning experience at a disadvantage. Rather, the absence allowance offers students leniency in case of illness or personal matters that arise unexpectedly. Missing beyond four absences will result in a loss of your attendance points as listed in the grade breakdown (6% of your final grade). **Missing more than seven classes will result in failure of the course.** If you miss more than seven classes, you should drop or withdraw from the course to avoid a failing grade on your transcript. If you withdraw from the course, I suggest meeting with your academic advisor to discuss your next steps.

If you add the class after the semester begins, your missed classes will not count as absences toward your quota, but you will need to contact me to complete makeup assignments to offset your missed class content in addition to making up any regular classwork that the class was assigned before you added the course. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:** If you are not yet aware at this point in your college career, academic dishonesty, including plagiarism, intentional and unintentional, is a serious offense at the college level and can result in your dismissal from the university. **All submitted work must be your own.** Words or ideas from any source (including but not limited to lecture, print, or digital mediums) must be properly cited. Changing phrases from a source and putting them in your own words also needs to be cited.

**Written work generated by AI and turned in as your own also counts as plagiarism.** This includes Chat GPT. Do not use ChatGPT or any other AI program to summarize reading, correct grammar, generate ideas, formulate or generate text, or reword your own work. **You are not permitted to use AI for this course in any capacity.** No one may complete in part or in entirety any of your class assignments besides you. Plagiarism will be reported to the Office of Judicial Affairs and will lead to a failing grade on the assignment in question or the course.
