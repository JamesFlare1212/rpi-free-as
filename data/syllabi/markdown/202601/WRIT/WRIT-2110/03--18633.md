## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Analyze academic texts in order to cultivate an ability to recognize and adapt to the features of different genres and purposes in writing
- Understand and use written and spoken academic structures and conventions to produce texts for academic audiences
- Successfully engage in the processes of academic writing and presentations including reading, outlining, drafting, peer review and editing
- Find information from appropriate sources and integrate it into writing
- Successfully implement academic citation conventions and referencing

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

In-class participation and in-class writing assignments: 10%

Short Essay 15%

Weekly Strategy Practice: 15%

Preparation Assignments: 10%

Oral Presentation: 10%

Course Research Paper: 40%

Participation: There is a participation grade for this course and to receive full credit for each class meeting, you must be prepared for class. Specifically, to get full participation points each class you will need to:

-have your course materials ready: Pen and paper are required, as well as a copy of that week’s assigned readings, whether in digital or hard copy.

-be prepared for class having read all assigned readings.

-share verbally if I ask for comments on in-class work from everyone.

-turn in any in-class written assignments that I ask to collect at the end of class.

-not spend class time chatting, working on anything not related to this class, or using technology such as laptop or phone for personal or recreational reasons.

Short Essay: This will be a short critical writing assignment that explores one’s experience of a problem, idea, concept, question, phenomenon, or issue.

Course Research Paper: The capstone writing project for the course will be a research paper where you analyze an issue, perform research and synthesize your findings, form an opinion, and argue a specific point. You will write to participate in a conversation regarding a relevant question or issue.

Weekly Strategy Practice: due most Fridays: Almost every week I will present a task in class. These must be completed by the end of the week (on Fridays), typed, and turned in on LMS.

Preparation Assignments: due most Thursdays: A research paper cannot be completed well in a day or two. Thus, the preparation assignments will assure you are well-prepared and pacing yourself, progressing on the steps needed to build throughout the semester to the finished product. Preparation assignments should be typed and turned in on LMS.

Oral Presentation: The presentation will be based on your final paper research. You will present your findings to your peers, persuading them of your argument. (Students must be prepared to present on the first day of presentations although not all will be able to present on the first day.) Not being ready to present will be counted as lateness.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are expected to attend on time and actively participate in all classes. As students cannot participate well in class without being present, students will be expected to be physically in class and participate actively to receive full points for participation.

5 points will be given for each 50 minutes of class. Students may miss 40 points (400 minutes of class) and receive an A grade in participation (full marks). These classes can be missed for any reason without any documentation; these points include both excused and unexcused absences.

If you have a prolonged illness or other circumstance that would cause you to miss more classes, please see the instructor and special arrangements may be made.

Students arriving after attendance has been called will lose 1 point for the day (if they fully participate while in the class). Students arriving more than 15 minutes late – will lose 5 points for the day (if they fully participate while in the class), and students arriving more than 1 hour 15 minutes late will not receive any points that day.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

For plagiarism that is deemed unintentional (poor/lack of citation), the first violation will received a reduced score on the assignment. Further violations will result in a 0 grade for that assignment.

For plagiarism that is deemed intentional (copying someone else’s work), the first violation will result in a 0 grade for that assignment. The second violation results in failure of the course. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

Use of AI

AI programs such as Chat GPT may be used as an aid to your writing in this class for things such as but not limited to the following: help with choosing a topic, creating outlines, finding sources, making reference lists, and editing papers. If you use an AI tool, you should cite that tool and state at the end of your paper how you have used that tool. AI should never be used to write or translate your entire paper or large portions of your paper for you. This will result in a 0 grade for the assignment.
