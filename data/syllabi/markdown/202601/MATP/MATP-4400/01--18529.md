## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Description:** This course will center on applications of basic data analysis techniques such as data visualization, classification, clustering, and ridge regression. A case study approach will be used to provide immediate immersion into the problem of understanding high-dimensional data. The course will develop the necessary mathematics to understand and execute the techniques. Each student will execute a mini-data analysis project on a real-world data problem such as from engineering, medicine, and business. (From the Rensselaer Catalog 2025-2026.)

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Extract structure from large scale data using techniques such as principal component analysis and linear discriminant analysis,
- Derive information from data by interpreting and visualizing this extracted structure,
- Present the derived information in a professional way understandable by others,
- Demonstrate knowledge of the mathematical scaffolding needed for expressing and analyzing data problems,
- Demonstrate knowledge of basic linear algebra, and
- Pursue interests in high-dimensional mathematics and data analysis techniques applied to problems in science, engineering, and business using cyberinfrastructure.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Homework:** About every two weeks, there will be written homework assigned. Each of these assignments will be of equal weight. Homework will be submitted via Gradescope and will be due on Fridays at 11:59 AM. Late submissions will be subject to penalties in accordance with the late policy described below. (See Absences & Late Policies).

**Labs:** On most weeks, there will be computer labs assigned. The labs have a Pre-Lab with an associated quiz to be done by Wednesdays at 9:00 AM. The lab may have both in-class and out-of-class components. The in-class components will be done in the Wednesday sessions. The labs will be submitted online through Gradescope and are usually due on Tuesday by 11:59 AM. Later in the semester, students will work on group lab projects which will have both individual and group components contributing to the grade. The group projects will require group presentations.

**Exams:** Exams will be given in person, during lecture. Exams are given on paper and no electronic devices or calculators are allowed. Details about each exam will be announced on LMS when the date of the exam is near. The dates for Exams 1 and 2 are listed below.

**Missing an exam should not be taken lightly.** If a student has a pre-scheduled absence (e.g. an athletic competition, official excused absence to attend a conference) on the day of an exam, then they must notify the instructor via email as soon as they learn of the absence and no later than one week before the regularly scheduled exam. Documentation excusing the student from class at the time of the regularly scheduled exam must be received by the instructor before the day of the exam. This documentation must be either an excused absence documented by the Dean of Students Office (DOSO) or, if the absence is due to an athletic competition, documentation from a coach. Provided the above conditions are met, the student will then take a conflict exam at a different time.

Students who miss an exam due to an unforeseen absence (e.g. illness on the day of the exam) must make every effort to notify the instructor **before the exam begins** to avoid grade penalties. Make-up exam eligibility and penalties are as follows:

- If a student misses an exam due to an unforeseen absence and notifies the instructor before the exam begins, then the student is eligible to take a make-up exam without penalty.
- If a student misses an exam due to an unforeseen absence and notifies the instructor after the exam begins but before 11:59 PM on the day of their scheduled exam, then the student is eligible to take a make-up exam and a 20% penalty will be applied to the make-up exam score. A higher penalty may be applied to a student with repeated late notifications for missing exams.
- If a student misses an exam due to an unforeseen absence and **fails to notify the instructor before 11:59 PM on the day of their scheduled exam, then the student is not eligible to take the make-up exam and receives a score of 0 on the exam.**

The make-up exam may differ in format from the regularly scheduled exam and must be taken within one week of the regularly scheduled exam. Details regarding the date/time/location of the make-up exam will be sent to eligible students the day after the missed exam. There are no make-up exams for the make-up exam.

**Final Project:** The last few weeks of class will be devoted to a final project. There will be both individual and group components contributing to the grade. During the final exam slot, there will be a mini-conference where each group will present their results, and then the group will answer questions.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

All the learning outcomes listed above will be assessed through the following:

- Homework worth 20%
- Labs and Projects worth 35%
- Online quizzes worth a total of 15%
- 2 equally weighted in-class exams worth a total of 30% (15% each)

Letter grades will be assigned according to the following scheme:

A 93–100; A- 90–92; B+ 87–89; B 83–86; B– 80–82; C+ 77–79; C 73–76; C– 70–72; D+ 65–69; D 55–64; F < 55

- The cut-offs are the minimum overall course grade needed to guarantee the associated letter grade without rounding.

**Grade Appeals:** If upon reviewing the graders’ feedback and posted solutions for an exam a student feels that their work was graded incorrectly, then the student may submit a grading appeal directly on Gradescope. Specific dates will be given for regrade request periods for exams at the time that scores are released. Regrade requests for homework/labs/projects will also be available on Gradescope for one week after the scores are released or until the last day of classes, whichever comes first. You may only appeal scores based on factual criteria (e.g. a correct answer was marked wrong, incorrect rubric item checked in Gradescope). Grade appeals should not be used to request more partial credit based or for disagreement with the point values on the rubric.

Exams, labs, and quizzes are to be done individually. Students are allowed to work together on projects. You must ask the instructors for permission before you get help on the homework or the projects from someone outside or from a website outside of the class. Use of websites like Chegg is strictly forbidden. More information that discusses appropriate and inappropriate collaboration within class is provided on LMS.

First violations on homeworks/labs will result in a grade of 0 on the material and a report to the Dean of Students. Subsequent infractions as well as cheating on exams will result in failure of the class and the policies for Academic Dishonesty of Rensselaer will be rigorously followed.

**Artificial Intelligence (AI):** Generative AI tools use is not permitted. All assignments (homework/labs/projects/quizzes) must be fully prepared by the student. IDM is an introductory course teaching the core principles of data analytics. Developing strong competencies in the skills associated with this course, including student-based brainstorming, code writing, and presentation of results, will prepare you for success in future courses, performing research, and ultimately, a competitive career. Therefore, the use of generative AI tools such as ChatGPT, Bing Chat, Claude, Google Bard, or any other automated assistance to complete any aspect of assignments for this course are not permitted and will be treated as plagiarism. First violations of using Generative AI on any assignment will result in a grade of 0. Subsequent infractions can result in failure of the class for Academic Integrity violations. If you have questions about what constitutes a violation of this statement, please contact the instructors.
