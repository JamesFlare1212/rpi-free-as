## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Learn procedural shader workflows
- Create rigs for quadrupeds, vehicles, and non-bipeds
- Create tools for procedural, generative, and advanced art demands
- Create real-time VFX/Animation/Lighting/Styling Workflows
- Optimize pipelines, asset workflow, and generate technical documentation for large scale pipelines

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Assessments**

- Assignment 1: *Substance Designer (Organic)*
  - **Due: end of week 3**
    - Students will be creating 3 parameterized organic materials in Substance Designer.1 of which will be assigned, and another will be given a theme.

- Assignment 2: *Substance Designer (Synthetic)*
  - **Due: end of week 5**
    - Students will be creating 3 parameterized synthetic materials in Substance Designer. 1 of which will be assigned, and another will be given a theme.

- Assignment 3: *Substance Designer (Animated)*
  - **Due: end of week 7**
    - Students will be creating 3 parameterized animated (organic or synthetic) materials in Substance Designer. 1 of which will be assigned, and another will be given a theme.

- Assignment 4: *Unreal Shader*
  - **Due: end of week 9**
    - Students will be building a parameterized shader with custom channels, tiling, movement, and other FX.

- Assignment 5: *Advanced Rig*
  - **Due: end of week 11**
    - Students will be building a rig for a non bipedal mesh like a quadruped vehicle, monster. The rig should have driven keys, blend shapes, and baked deformers.

- Assignment 6: *Art Tool*
  - **Due: end of week 13**
    - Students will be building a generative, or procedural art tool that facilitates a large scale art task like spawning buildings, complex lighting, etc.

- Assignment 7: *Real Time VFX*
  - **Due: end of week 15**

- Students will be building a parameterized VFX in conjunction with a shader and model used for a real time VFX in Unreal’s Niagara.

- Final Projects
  - **Due: end of semester**
    - The project will vary depending on the student and game.
    - Shaders, rigging, VFX, tools, etc. are all within the scope of this final project.
  - Submission: Various

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Grading**Grading standards for this class are high. This is one of the upper levels game electives, not to mention a course in one of the most competitive fields in the current job market.

A’s will denote work that is currently representative of industry level practices and quality.

B’s mean the student is rendering work just on the cusp of transitioning into professional quality, but still has visible denotations of student work.

C’s translate to student work that is clearly rendered by a student hand and eye. The effort and time is present, but the quality level is not on the cusp of being valuable to the game industry.

D’s & F’s when students are not only delivering work that is not industry level, but also lacks the effort of a committed student, and has failed to include the required amount of assets, techniques, and expectations of each milestone.

**Grade Weights**

- Assignment 1: 10%
- Assignment 2: 10%
- Assignment 3: 15%
- Assignment 4: 10%
- Assignment 5: 10%
- Assignment 6: 10%
- Assignment 7: 10%
- Final Projects: 15%
- Professionalism: 10%

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Policies**

**Late Work** ***-*** There is no late work in this class. Assignments are extensive and take time to complete. Getting behind only means you will have less time to complete the next assignment, etc. Part of the work is being able to complete it in the time given, which is already generous. Submit early and often, all assignments are open for unlimited submissions. Do not be tempted by midnight submissions, assume something will go wrong.

**Professionalism** **-** This is your attendance, preparation, and participation in the class. Have the necessary software installed, ask questions, engage with your classmates, show up on time.

**Purposeful Ambiguity** **-** Creative fields can be frustrating when feedback is not specific, but it is an unfortunate part of the industry. You must acclimate yourself to working with limited feedback at times. The instructor will always step in with more directed feedback or paint over work when progress has stalled, but be prepared to deal with times where you must creatively work through ambiguity.

**Sensitive Topics** **-** Like with any creative/artistic course there may be a discussion of sensitive topics. These topics will always be discussed neutrally, and from an academic perspective. The instructor will always do their best to handle them elegantly and use them as learning opportunities.

**AI & Plagiarism** - AI usage is considered plagiarism and will be treated like any other plagiarism case and will be reported to the University for disciplinary action**.**
