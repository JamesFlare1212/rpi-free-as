## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

**Course Content:**

This course will use an Iterative development model, introducing project management and production processes and Agile software development methods.

Lectures and tutorials will be adjusted based on the course projects, and may include topics such as 3D art asset pipeline; texturing, rigging, and animation; camera control paradigms; networked multiplayer; navmesh and AI agents; level design; sound design; virtual reality and immersive environments; and other relevant topics.

**Game Pitch Parameters**

You will be working on a single game the entire semester in GD2.  
It must be a video game (no board games).  
2D or 3D  
Unity or Unreal  
C++ or Blueprints is allowed  
External assets – SFX and animations are okay, no models, textures, code, or music.  
Games should be around 5 – 10 minutes, but that is loose depending on the genre (roguelikes get much longer)  
There must be tutorialization  
There must be debug controls (level skipping, health immunity, resetting, etc.)  
After the Vertical Slice there should be 5 – 10 external playtests conducted a week, and documented.  
There should be analytics running internally.

Gold is Game Fest April, Release is end of semester.

Pitches will be first day of class. You may pitch multiple games, you may team pitch, and you may pitch from multiple teams as well.

Pitches should be about 5 – 10 minutes, and resources for those are in Game Dev 1’s Blackboard.  
The game does not need controller support, but that is encouraged.  
Does not need narrative.  
On Vertical Slice, Alpha, Gold, and Release you need cooked, packaged, and built executables.  
Projects are now subject to (self) cancellation if predefined deadlines are not met, team issues arise, or if the game is simply not interesting and the team feels the project isn’t worth continuing. Cancelled teams are broken up and go to continuing games.  
There are no subject matter or genre criteria or guidelines.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate effective team collaboration
- Apply game development techniques
- Demonstrate project and time management skills to build a complete game
- Demonstrate effective written, spoken, and electronic communication
- Analyze and critique peer work

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Grading Criteria:**

All submissions are evaluated as a combination of the overall submission and individual work. Each milestone deliverable will include an executable build and review of current project files in Perforce, as well as the Knowledge Repository (Basecamp) and project tracking tools..

Individual effort is assessed through 3 components:

- Logged time that is an exported PDF of your assesed and estimated time in Notion (make sure to filter out all other team members, and only submit the current sprint)
- Personal Summary including all cut work, completed work, and WIPs submitted as a PDF or portfolio blog
- Teammate Evaluation (no submission) your teammates will anonymously evaluate your performance

Grades will be delivered in LMS.

Milestone Deliverable 1 (First Playable): 18% (Prototypes/Art Direction/Tests/Audio Profiles)

Milestone Deliverable 2 (Vertical Slice): 18% (All elements present, UI, Animation, Gameplay, Audio, Modelling, etc.)

Milestone Deliverable 3 (Gold): 18% (Gamefest)

Milestone Deliverable 4 (Final): 18% (Release)

Upon each deliverable the project will be assessed as a whole for the following (28%):

- state (where is the project in comparison to industry standards)
- expectations upon delivery (is the project meeting the deliverables put forth in the design document)
- implementation of critique
- playtesting (is the projeect undergoing playtests, are they documented, and being implemented)
- debugging
- tutorialization
- analytics

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:**

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that

teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses

they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their

own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer

Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement

define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar

with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the

student’s own work.

Collaboration: As game development is a fundamentally collaborative practice, work is assessed as a combination of the team effort and the student’s individual effort.

Use of third-party assets: All code and assets must represent the student’s own work. Engine standard assets are allowed. 3rd party assets are allowed for animation and SFX. Code examples may be used as reference, but any substantial code not written by the student must be clearly designated.

Derivative work: work that derives from existing works such as collage, remix, or derivation must demonstrate significant transformation on the part of the student to receive credit as original work.

Use of AI content generators: AI is not allowed in any form and will be considered plagiarism.

Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy may result in a penalty of a grade of zero for the assignment.

Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of

Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate

students, respectively)
