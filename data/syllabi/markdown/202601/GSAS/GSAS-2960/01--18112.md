## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

As we experience the world with all available senses, it’s hard to escape the importance of audio cues to our perception of reality. When things don’t quite “sound right” in an environment it can trigger subconscious alarms, but when everything sounds the “way it should” we can find ourselves more comfortable in the space. Commercially available media—video, cinema, games, music, audiovisual installations, etc.—take full advantage of the effective emotional power of audio cues, utilizing **sound design** to enhance visual information and storytelling. This course focuses on creating best practices and workflows for designing all variety of recorded and synthesized non-musical sounds. These processes will require the student to employ sound **analysis**, **imagination**, and creation **technique** for success, with the result being a personal library of bespoke useful sounds for use with visual media and audio performances or installations. At semesters end, we will explore how these sounds can then be integrated into visual and experiential media: film/TV, Wwise, and Unity.

With classes located in the HASS Media Studio (DCC174), lectures on aspects of audio and sound design techniques will alternate with in-studio analysis and recording sessions, as well as remote recording and soundwalks around the RPI campus. Students will get studio training early and given a proficiency allowing them unsupervised access to the HASS Media Studio, and will also be recording in locations around campus, using personal gear or equipment from the HASS Equipment Room, Sage 1121. All students will require a Digital Audio Workstation (DAW) of their choice on their computer (ProTools is not a necessity, though it is available on our studio drive) for audio file editing and a good-quality set of headphones, NOT earbuds. An audio interface, dedicated media hard drive, and a decent pair of near-field speakers are highly encouraged for those who decide to go further into this field, though they are not required.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students completing this course will have demonstrable ability in and knowledge of analog and digital audio recording techniques, microphone use, audio editing in a DAW, sound repair and encoding, and creating/cataloging useful recorded sounds. Through analysis, imagination, and technique, students will be able to design all necessary sounds for visual media using samples, field recordings, environmental sounds, Foley audio, modular and granular synthesis, and audio effects, using them correctly for best results within their chosen media. Students will also create an immersive sound installation or time-based performance accompanied or unaccompanied by visual media, allowing their personally created sounds to present an artistic vision or tell a story. These design skills can be utilized in commercial video and game design to help enhance and elevate the immersive experience in these art forms. Many designers in these fields often look at their audio needs late in the process, reaching for online open-source sound libraries…students completing this course will leave with a wide range of bespoke samples of a noticeably higher sound quality to use or share with others.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate essential and optimal microphone and recording techniques for the creation of high-quality audio.
- Utilize a variety of different synthesizer technologies—modular, FM, granular, additive/subtractive modes, wavetables—to achieve both innovative and natural sonic solutions for sound effects.
- Work seamlessly between audio and video environments while focusing on the necessity and effectiveness of the audio components.
- Create a catalog of audio sound files that meet industry standards and are of commercial release quality.
- Utilize the Universal Catalog System (UCS) to accurately and professionally name and categorize sound effect files.
- Effectively record sonic environments on location, as well as impulse responses or reverberant spaces for convolution reverbs.
- Imagine and develop Foley techniques to accompany video shorts and replace whatever audio is present effectively.
- Design an immersive audio environment that presents an artistic idea or tells a story.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

If you need to miss this class for personal reasons, I’m good for up to three (3) unexcused absences; it’s your money, your life. If you find yourself needing to miss classes for medical reasons, then **it is best for both of us for you to have documentation for the reason of your absence obtained through the Student Success Office.** An unexcused absence can (on occasion) become an excused one by informing me of your extremely good reason beforehand or, if it’s an emergency, as soon as possible after the class. **Activities for other classes cannot be used as excuses for absences in this class.** If you have a sports-related absence, you must notify me with a list of all classes you expect to miss in advance and be prepared to do any necessary make-up work.

Your third unexcused absence will trigger a warning from me, and each unexcused absence following will result in a **half-grade reduction in your final grade.**

### Other Course Policies:

You should have been provided adequate time to get to my class on time by RPI, and DCC is in the center of campus. Sustained extreme tardiness will result in escalating discussions and eventually grade degradation if not curtailed or adequately excused. If I don’t see you when I take attendance (5-10 minutes into class) I may mark you absent, and that won’t be my fault, it will be on you. **Please don’t be late if you can help it**.

Labs are due on the dates indicated. **Ten percent of your lab grade** will be deducted for any lab submitted after the due date and **ten percent for each day late thereafter**, with no lab accepted more than five days after the due date.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:
