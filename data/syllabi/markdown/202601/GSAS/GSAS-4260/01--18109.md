## Course Goals

### Goals:

Our work together will focus on developing an ongoing design practice based in play and collaboration.

## Skills

### **Play**

*It is impossible to understand games without playing them. We will play a variety in and out of class.*

### **Observ****e**

In order to develop our design sensibilities, we must train ourselves to carefully observe experiences of gameplay, both our own and others'

### **Analyze**

*As we play games, we will break them down into the components of their narrative design*

### **Hack**

*No design emerges from nothing. We must tinker, hack, and repurpose existing designs if we are to make anything at all*

### **Design**

*At some indefinable point, we reconfigure existing elements enough that we have designed something new*

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

## Homework and Log

### Weekly Homework

- Given in class on Tuesdays
- Due the following Tuesday
- Varied work, such as playing and analyzing a game, taking notes on a reading, recording ethnographic observations, or writing a guided reflection.

### Course Log

You will keep a log of the course in a composition book. The course log is comparable to a lab notebook in a laboratory science course, but the content includes **all** aspects of the course

- Each week should have a minimum of 4 entries:

- Two Class Session Notes

- Class session notes should, at a minimum, log the basic events of the session
- Additionally, they should contain your ongoing thinking based on the session

- One Homework note

- Most homework assignments will involve taking notes or making reflections in your log

- At least one other reflections and notes

- At least one other note containing thinking related to the course

## Reverse Engineering Project

Using a provided template, you will write a 4-8 page essay analyzing the narrative design of a selected game.

### Deliverables

- Game selection (January 23)
- First draft (February 27, returned by March 13)
- Final draft (April 10)

## Narrative Design Project

You will design and publish a short-form narrative-driven analog game.

### Deliverables

- Initial concept (February 6)
- Design document (March 13)
- Ashcan edition (March 27)
- Final game (April 24)

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

- Roll will be called at the beginning of each class
- Attendance points are based on the Zombie Scale of Classroom Participation

- **4 points:** Fully present or skilled enough at performing "fully present student" to seem fully present
- **3 points:** Tardy (i.e., arriving after roll has been called, but otherwise present)
- **2 points:** Extremely tardy (more than 15 minutes after class start), notably unprepared, distracted, sleeping, etc.
- **1 point:** Technically in the room, but non-participatory or virtually leaving the room via technology

## Analog Classroom

Unless otherwise stated, in-class use of electronics is restricted unless necessary as an accommodation. This policy helps us all to be more fully present to each other and reinforces the embodied learning goals of the course.

## Communication

Course-related communication with the instructor should be conducted via e-mail or in-person. The instructor will be available by e-mail during standard work hours, but will not check or respond to e-mails outside those hours.

## Hard Copy Submission

**Submit all assignments in hard copy, either in class or to the instructor's mailbox in West Hall 101.**

Assignments will specify handwritten or typed submission. Use the following specifications:

- Student name and assignment title in upper right of first page
- Stapled
- Follow the APA Style Guide
- Additionally, for typed submissions:

- 12 pt, Times New Roman or equivalent (e.g., Liberation Serif)
- Double-spaced, 1” margins
- Last name and page number in header, upper right

Assignments that don't meet all specifications will have 5% deducted from their grade. (90%-->85%) Those that fail to follow multiple specifications will have 10% deducted from their grade. (90%-->80%)

## Deadlines

Assignments are due by the end of the noted class period, unless otherwise stated. Submission by the end of the following class period will be accepted at half credit. Submission by the end of the second class period after the deadline will be accepted at one-quarter credit. No submissions will be accepted more than one week after the deadline.

## AI

Any use of AI to assist with work related to the course, whether as a brainstorming aid, editing, content generation, or any other usage must be discussed with the instructor *prior* to utilization. While the expectation of the course is to conduct all work without the use of AI, some limited exceptions may be granted. Use of AI without prior approval will be considered a violation of academic integrity.
