## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course surveys several art-making methods utilized in games. Modelling, texturing, lighting, and other workflows are covered in order to immerse students in the breadth of artistry present in games. This course focuses on environment art creation but the skills learned are applicable to many different pipelines and techniques. For example, vehicle artists and environment artists are often working in the same programs and workflows, but only the subject matter differs.

Students will be working on a single environment over the course of the semester, and that environment only needs to be readable from 1 camera angle. It can be stylized or PBR. It should have some foliage element and some architectural element. There should be some sort of focal point, and students should have plans for the other principles of environment art structured as well. There are several other requirements in the assignment students should consider as well.

Students begin by concepting and designing a singular environment while learning the fundamentals and theory behind world building, level design, but most importantly environment art.

From there students begin modelling assets and content while preparing their models for UV’s and any retopologizing that needs to be done.

Next students move into a various stages of texturing, from single asset painting in Substance Painter to more procedurally oriented texturing in Designer. We also go over some more hand painted texturing workflows similar to Blizzard or Riot games.

Students then move into a VFX phase where they go over the basic functionality of Unreal’s Niagara and Cascade system for particles.

Penultimately students go over the theory and practice of lighting a space. Elements of cinematography and photography are combined with the technical demands of lighting real time spaces.

Lastly students go through post processing and rendering in order to put the final touches on their environments and make them play ready.

Students will be diving deeper into some of the more technical aspects of game art creation when dealing with shaders and technical art. While artists focus primarily on creative mediums, they do need to be able to do some light programming. Students should have some basic understanding of programming concepts as they will be utilized in shader creation and technical art assignments.

The final outcome of this course should be an understanding and familiarity of the different artistries of environment art and students will leave the course with 1 polished portfolio piece for modelling, texturing, or environment art.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- create game environments
- utilize game environment pipelines for modelling, texturing, lighting, and rendering

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Assessments

- - Assignment 1: *Reference Package*
    - **Due: end of week 2**
      - Students will need to submit their reference package, asset list, and environment sketches/plan.
  - Assignment 2: *Greybox*
    - **Due: end of week 3**
      - Environment should be blocked and scaled out in Unreal.
  - Assignment 3: *External Assets*
    - **Due: end of week 4**
      - Students can utilize external assets for up to 2 flora variations (grass, flowers), 2 larger foliage variations (trees, bushes), and 5 generic assets (tables, barrels, chairs, etc.)
  - Assignment 4: *Modelling Pass 01 (Foundations + Trim Sheet)*
    - **Due: end of week 5**
      - Students should have their trim sheets, textured and placed on walls, floors, and trims in their environments. The ground, landscape, walls, and floors should be in the scene and UV’d, but may not be textured yet.
  - Assignment 5: *Modelling Pass 02 + Texturing (Foliage)*
    - **Due: end of week 6**
      - All grass, trees, and flora should be placed and textured in scene whether procedurally or by hand.
  - Assignment 6: *Modelling Pass 03 + Texturing (Props)*
    - **Due: end of week 8**
      - At least 15 props should be modelled, UV’d, Textured, and set dressed to the scene.
  - Assignment 7: *Texturing 01 (Foundations)*
    - **Due: end of week 10**
      - All architecture, landscapes, and structural elements should be textured with atleast 1 instance of vertex painting.
  - Assignment 8: *Modelling + Texturing Pass (Focal point)*
    - **Due: end of week 11**
      - Students will generate one larger more elaborated prop to act as a focal point for the scene. There should be a Zbrush component to the piece.
  - Assignment 9: *Lighting + Post Process*
    - **Due: end of week 12**
      - The scene should be lit, have a post process volume to control the look, and a LUT.
  - Assignment 10: *VFX, Decals, + Animation*
    - **Due: end of week 13**
      - Students will need to have 1 Niagara system, 1 decal, and 1 animation (simulation or rigged).
  - Assignment 11: *Final Shots + Video*
    - **Due: end of semester**
      - Students should include any critique revisions, finish up untextured assets, and shoot their environment from multiple angles. Untextured and include prop shots.
