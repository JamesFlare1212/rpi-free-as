## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Learn familiarity with the broad history of the field of historical simulations.
- ● Learn analytical tools to facilitate study of board games as historiographic expression.
- Learn familiarity with selected historical topics.
- Demonstrate an ability for theoretical thinking with regards to game mechanics.
- Demonstrate an ability to test selected games and to evaluate them as game design within the context of historical simulation.
- Demonstrate an ability to analyze historical subject matter in fusion with game designs and to articulate this analysis.
- Demonstrate the ability to create their own historical informed playable prototypes.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Student work is assessed through presentations (Assignments 1, 2), through seminar participation (covered through 20% of the participation and attitude part of the grade), through the creation of a playable prototype (Assignment 3), and through a critical analysis (Assignment 4).

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

### Criteria Details:

**Assignment Details**

**Assignment #1:** Identify an historical topic of interest to you. Give a 10-15 minute (maximum) presentation on this topic - with a view to making a game of some kind on it later this semester, although I don’t want any game details at this point. I just want to hear about the history.

**Delivery Date:** TBD

**Final Grade Weight:** 10%

*Tip: choose a small, discrete and/or less well/known topic. This will make it easier for you to tackle the other assignments this semester.*

**Assignment #2:** Give a 10-15 minute (maximum) presentation on a high level design pitch for a game on an historical topic. The assumption is this will be the same topic you presented on previously. If this is not the case (you have changed your mind), be sure to begin your presentation with a high-level summary of the history you are exploring.

This pitch should include:

1. Game Title
2. High Concept: what is its historical setting/topic?
3. Player count: how many players is it for?
4. Play duration: roughly how long does the game take to play?
5. Complexity: how difficult is the game to play?
6. Actors and Factors: who are the historical actors (who do players play as, what emotional space are you attempting to put your players into through these historical actors, and what are the other historical factors included in your focus)?
7. Scope and Scale: what is the scope and scale of your game’s focus?
8. Ends, Ways, and Means: what are they for your game?
9. Sequence of Play: what does a turn look like?

**Delivery Date:** TBD

**Final Grade Weight:** 20%

*Tip: don’t default assume your game must be competitive. Perhaps it is cooperative or semi-cooperative. Let the history inform you.*

**Assignment #3:** Create a playable prototype we will play in class. Any player count is acceptable (including solitaire) and any play duration, but we are going to play in class (in teams and/or with observers if necessary) and it should be possible to teach and play a meaningful portion of the game within 100 minutes.

**Delivery Date:** TBD

**Final Grade Weight:** 50% (undergraduate), 20% (graduate)

**Assignment #4:** (Graduate students only). Write a 4,000-5,000 word paper critical analysis of your game. Use the four rhetorical lenses to examine your game: Ludic Rhetoric, Aesthetic Rhetoric, Discursive Rhetoric, Experiential Rhetoric.

**Delivery Date:** TBD

**Final Grade Weight:** 30% (graduate only)

Note 20% of final grades are calculated through attendance, attitude, and participation.
