## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

**Required Course Text:** *Astrobiology: A Very Short Introduction,* 1st Edition (2014) by David Catling (required). This book is a very short, simple read to get everyone caught up on the basics of astrobiology. It is currently available online for <$15. Everyone in the class is ***required*** to have read this book by the beginning of the second class meeting and be prepared to discuss.

**Other suggested reading material (on reserve in the library or on the LMS site)**

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Course Goals / Objectives** This is an ***interactive*** readings and seminar course in which we will discuss current topics in astrobiology and the origins of life through the view of earth sciences, chemistry, biology, and astrophysics. Each week will consist either of a guest speaker or discussion. Students are required to come prepared to seminar having read assigned papers, and fully participate in Q&A and discussion. The goal of the course is to provide broad exposure to current topics in Astrobiology, and hone reading, discussion, and synthesis skills.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity** This course will utilize a ***zero-tolerance policy*** for any form of cheating or other academic misconduct. All submitted assignments will be subjected to copying and plagiarism checkers. University policy deals with the problem of academic dishonesty, cheating, and plagiarism. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. No violation will be tolerated in this class. The sanctions provided in this policy will be used to deal with any violations.

Academic dishonesty includes, but is not limited to, copying material from another student or use of written notes during an exam, representing others’ work as your own, changing answers on an exam paper after the paper has been graded, etc. Any student caught cheating will be assigned an F for the course. Furthermore, violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education for undergraduate and graduate students, respectively).

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ***ask*** for clarification.

***A special note on plagiarism.*** Please see the definition of plagiarism given below, as outlined in The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights. To be clear, any time material that is not the students’ own is used, it should be in quotes and contain a citation. If a student paraphrases work from elsewhere, it also requires a citation. Furthermore, even when citations are used, others’ work cannot be used as a student response on an assignment. For example, in this class, if the main conclusions are given in an article, they cannot be used verbatim as a response in the post-discussion evaluation, even if a citation is present.

*“**Plagiarism** Representing the work or words of another as one’s own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference. For example, using sentences verbatim from a published source in a term paper without appropriate referencing, or presenting as one’s own the detailed argument of a published source, or presenting as one’s own electronically or digitally enhanced graphic representations from any form of media.”* – ACADEMIC INTEGRITY (2014-2016 Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights & Responsibilities, August 2014)
