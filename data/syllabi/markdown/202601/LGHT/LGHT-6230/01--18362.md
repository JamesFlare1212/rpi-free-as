## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The *Advanced* *Lighting Design* course is a graduate course with seminar, workshop and studio elements to deepen the skillset needed to design illumination schemes for various built contexts, and to develop and detail lighting proposals.

Lighting Design practice is evolving together with the technologies used to assess, plan, implement, and control lighting in the built environment. This course builds on lighting design fundamentals (from the pre-requisite course) and explores how research findings from various disciplines can inform and integrate with the lighting design of the built environments. Research in many scientific disciplines, to protect and advance the world we live in, addresses topics that are related to lighting. In this class, we will learn about such topics (e.g., sensory perception, environmental light pollution, sleep and alertness,…) to incorporate, interpret and address them creatively in sustainable lighting design projects. Readings and research presentations will be part of the course as well as individual and group design assignments, related mock-ups, and project presentations.

Students will learn about and use advanced digital tools and methods to evaluate, design and modify the lighting in selected environmental contexts both daytime and after-dark. The aim of this course is to prepare, inspire, and empower students to test and discuss connections between lighting-related research and application, to develop skills to collaborate on projects in interdisciplinary and impactful ways, and to explore opportunities, arguments and implications of research-enhanced lighting design for practice.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

At the conclusion of this course, it is expected that students will have a better understanding of lighting design processes, techniques and requirements as well as related research topics, and be able to integrate and explore research findings in lighting proposals.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- At the conclusion of the course, students will be able to establish lighting objectives based on selected research findings and related assessment techniques.
- At the conclusion of the course, students will be able to develop lighting design proposals, based on objectives, that integrate concepts from research, technology, art and design.
- At the conclusion of the course, students will be able to demonstrate skills to test, assess and communicate the benefits of the lighting design proposal (various audiences) using appropriate tools.
- At the conclusion of the course, students will be able to produce information on environmental, societal, and economic implications (including maintenance considerations) for the design proposal.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Student assessment will be based on a review of their performance on individual and group work, presentations, and other class assignments. The above learning outcomes will be assessed through visual and written reports, quizzes, in-class presentations and participation, simulations and calculations, hands-on mock-ups and group presentation to project stakeholders outside the institution. Preparation, quality of work, and student participation in class discussions are considered for the final grade.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

The course consists of several assessment activities that will be graded individually and contribute to the overall grade as follows: group design project presentations and documentation (35%), individual design project presentations and documentation (25%), quizzes (15%), homework (15%), class participation (10%).  
A cumulative midterm evaluation will be provided.  
Your final grade for the course is accumulative, and will be one of: A, A-, B+, B, B-, C+, C, C-, or F. You may appeal any progress grades given by discussing your concerns first with the course instructor, Program Director and ultimately with the Head of Graduate Education and the Graduate Education Committee. Full attendance and participation in class discussions are essential parts of Class Participation. Late assignments turned in one day late will be penalized a full letter grade (e.g., an A- will be given a B-). Assignments later than one day will be assigned a grade of F unless a valid excuse is presented; if there is an emergency please discuss with the instructor.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are expected to attend all classes, however, there is no need for a medical excuse to be provided for absence of up to a week (five days). Students missing more than a week of classes due to illness will be required to provide a doctor’s note or other supporting documentation to the Student Success Office. The Class Dean will communicate the receipt of the note (with expected end date) to the relevant faculty. Students are responsible for completing any work that they might miss – including assignments, quizzes, labs, exams, etc. – due to illness, quarantine/isolation, or other circumstances. When possible, students should notify the instructor in advance of an absence. The instructor will make every effort to work with and provide reasonable modifications for students who cannot attend class due to illness to complete assignments and exams. Students should keep in mind that virtual learning or class recording may not be possible. Students who adhere to these processes will not be penalized per the attendance policy (class participation) for the course. Students are expected to maintain punctuality and to participate actively in all class discussions, studio and laboratory sessions, and project reviews. They are expected to come to class with all required reading and assignments completed. On time attendance at all scheduled meetings is mandatory. Late assignments will only be accepted for valid reasons as described above. Each unexcused absence will result in a grade reduction. An unsatisfactory record of attendance may result in a failing grade, and/or dismissal from the program. Students are expected to maintain punctuality, and late arrivals may be recorded as absences.
