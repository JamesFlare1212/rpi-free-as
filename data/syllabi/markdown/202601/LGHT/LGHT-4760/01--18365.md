## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The *Lighting Workshop* is an upper-level undergraduate and graduate research and design studio course, integrating scholarship, technology. design, policy, entrepreneurship, and communication in a project-specific context. The course includes multiple topics selected to emphasize scholarship and information synthesis; utilize a variety of written and verbal presentation techniques; develop skills in design, applications, and visualization software; and require both teamwork and individual efforts. The *Lighting Workshop* emphasizes studio and seminar work supplemented with lecture, class discussions, and individual and group research, design, writing, and reading assignments. Students will gain skills, in-depth knowledge, and hands-on experience while working on group and individual projects. The aim of this course is to prepare students to take on leadership roles and face real-world challenges, especially as related to illumination in the built environment.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

In this course, students in LGHT-4760 will be able to:

1. Develop creative solutions to lighting-related problems by understanding relevant technology, societal, policy, cultural, environmental, or economic issues.
2. Evaluate the integration of research and design to meet the requirements of a given lighting application.
3. Demonstrate effective oral, written, and graphic communication skills through in-person presentations, computer visualizations, and other types of presentations.
4. Demonstrate effective project management skills, including task and milestone planning, and fulfilling deliverables.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- Participation (5%): This course requires discussion and participation from all students and the Workshop environment requires that all parties learn from each other. Students are required to participate in discussions, be up to date on assignments, and give constructive critiques of each other’s work and presentations. Students are expected to be at all sessions, alert and on time. Students must read all materials provided prior to specific classes. Active participation, leadership in Workshop activities, and studio citizenship will contribute to this portion of the grade.
- Charrettes (5%): Several charrettes, or brainstorming activities, will be assigned throughout the semester to develop ideas for art, entrepreneurship, designs, and technologies. Brief in-class presentations, emphasizing communication skills and concept development will be required.
- Projects (90%): Projects encourage experiences in creatively advancing the effective use of light. The Projects Menu below lists project choices that may be done individually or in groups of any size. Projects with multiple students will have proportionally higher expectations in the evaluation.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are expected to attend all classes, however, there is no need for a medical excuse to be provided for absence of up to a week (five days). Students missing more than a week of classes due to illness will be required to provide a doctor’s note or other supporting documentation to the Student Success Office. The Class Dean will communicate the receipt of the note (with expected end date) to the relevant faculty. Students are responsible for completing any work that they might miss – including assignments, quizzes, labs, exams, etc. – due to illness, quarantine/isolation, or other circumstances. When possible, students should notify the instructor in advance of an absence. The instructor will make every effort to work with and provide reasonable modifications for students who cannot attend class due to illness to complete assignments and exams. Students should keep in mind that virtual learning or class recording may not be possible. Students who adhere to these processes will not be penalized per the attendance policy (class participation) for the course. Students are expected to maintain punctuality and to participate actively in all class discussions, studio and laboratory sessions, and project reviews. They are expected to come to class with all required reading and assignments completed. On time attendance at all scheduled meetings is mandatory. Late assignments will only be accepted for valid reasons as described above. An unsatisfactory record of attendance may result in a failing grade, and/or dismissal from the program. Students are expected to maintain punctuality, and late arrivals may be recorded as absences.
