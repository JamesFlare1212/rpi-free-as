## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

*Technology at Work:* *Reconfiguring Labor Relations* examines how computing configures and mediates paid and unpaid labor relations at different sites: the factory, the warehouse, the road, the home, and the platform. The course starts with an introduction and a discussion about the state of labor today. After that, each week we will discuss an instance (an ethnography or a historical work) of this nexus between technology, labor and situated practices: the future, de-skilling, efficiency, globalization, and time management. The course is loosely organized to represent different moments on a supply chain; we move from labor in the factory to labor on the road and then on labor in the office or the home office.

Through in-class discussions of academic work and other take-home assignments (class facilitation, memos, and a 12-page final paper or a grant proposal), we will articulate the different ways in which technology and labor interact in society. Each week in class, we will discuss one instance of the relations between technology and labor. Our discussions will have two focus points. First, we will pay attention to when and how specific technological configurations — on the platform, in the home, in the factory, and on the road — distribute power between managers and workers. Besides power, key topics that will be transversal to our discussion are gender, race, and capitalism. A second focus point in this course are the different approaches to the study of labor as well as feminist and decolonial critiques.

Weekly memos will help us connect the readings to current events, personal experiences, and your dissertation topic. These shorts (1000 w) written memos are the space for tryout ideas and connections and get comments. These ideas and connections will be the starting point of your final paper or grant proposal. In the final paper or grant proposal, you will have the possibility to apply labor as an analytical lens to examine their dissertation topics.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

By the end of the course students will successfully:

- Articulate how computing configurates and mediates paid and unpaid labor relations at different sites: the factory, the platform, the home, the warehouse, and the road.

- Identify and compare different theoretical frameworks in the study of labor and technology and put them in conversation with other Science and Technology Studies (STS) approaches.

- Apply these theoretical frameworks to analyze other settings and contexts including current events.

- Apply labor as an analytical lens to examine an aspect of their own dissertation topics in 10-pages paper (map a literature review, research questions, analysis and conclusions).

- Use labor as an analytical lens to draft a grant proposal (map a literature review, research questions, preliminary analysis and research plan) connected to their dissertation topics.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

This is a graduate seminar that aims at discussing complete works. I do not expect that you have extensive knowledge on topics around labor and technology. I do expect you to have some knowledge about STS perspectives on technology. This knowledge will help you develop appropriate skills and knowledge for the study of labor throughout the semester. I also expect you to come to class prepared, pay close attention to the details in this syllabus, and be aware of what each week holds. Please take a careful read of this syllabus and keep it easily accessible for reference throughout the semester. Finally, I expect, however, is that you treat every peer, instructor, and concept we encounter with respect and curiosity. You are encouraged to ask questions and clarifications on issues while being aware of where these questions may be coming from.
