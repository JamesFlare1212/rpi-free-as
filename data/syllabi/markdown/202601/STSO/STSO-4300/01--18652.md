## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is required for [and designed for] students majoring in STS or Sustainability Studies. Students should take it in their sophomore year to best prepare for Arch internships and leverage the expertise they are developing through their undergraduate studies. Students will investigate the evolving terrain of career pathways at the intersection of science, technology, sustainability, and social change, learn job search strategies, and network with career role models.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Investigate and evaluate the roles of diverse organizations and domains in which work for sustainability and social change takes place.
- Identify and evaluate the diverse skills and forms of expertise that are needed to address key social challenges related to science, technology, health, and the environment.
- Describe their personal strengths, values, and priorities in order to pursue appropriate and desirable career pathways.
- Effectively use a variety of sources to investigate employment opportunities.
- Skillfully prepare job applications, cover letters, resumes, and other career development materials.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

# Academic Integrity

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty, and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty. If found in violation of the academic honesty policy, students may be subject to two types of penalty. The instructor administers an academic grade penalty, and the student is reported to the Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education as appropriate. The first violation results in 0 grade for that assignment. The second violation results in failure of the course.

Plagiarism includes purchasing term papers; copying or handing in the writing of another student (current or former); using sentences verbatim from a published source without appropriate referencing (when in doubt, cite the source); presenting as one’s own the detailed argument of a published source; and presenting as one’s own the text produced by an AI tool such as ChatGPT (see below for more on this). “Recycling” papers written in other courses is also forbidden. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

# Using Artificial Intelligence

ChatGPT and other AI tools can make many tasks easier, but they can also be a hindrance to learning and personal development. In this course, **I expect you to be honest and original**, not only because that is the ethical thing to do, but also because I want you to learn and grow to your full potential. Don’t forget that ChatGPT is a language model, not a factual model. You will *not be successful* if you attempt to use AI to (1) fact check; (2) write a paper; (3) or replace critical thinking.

Additionally, it is worth considering the environmental impact of AI. Each ChatGPT query consumes energy at a rate ten times that for an ordinary Google search [source: https://theconversation.com/ai-supercharges-data-center-energy-use-straining-the-grid-and-slowing-sustainability-efforts-232697]. The growing use of AI strains our power grid and hampers efforts to reduce fossil fuel use.

There are some circumstances where using AI may be reasonable. For example, ChatGPT can give feedback on your writing, helping you to improve your grammar. This is especially helpful when English is not your first language. If you have found other creative ways to use AI to assist with your learning, feel free to come to my office hours to discuss it with me. **If you use an AI assistant like ChatGPT to help with any part of your assignment, you must include a footnote that explains how you used this tool.**
