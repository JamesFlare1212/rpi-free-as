## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

DIS Studio C focuses on the co-production of products, organizations, and problem-solving strategies. Students will explore how to facilitate communication across multiple disciplinary and audience modalities, including stakeholders, designers, and users. Projects and course discussions will train students to translate social scientific and engineering problem-solving judgments and to evaluate how complex problems come to be defined. This course is required for DIS students who are dual majoring with the School of Engineering.

This course will focus on designing in the smart city. Students will work in groups of three. In these groups, they will undertake three interconnected projects. The first project involves creating a “smart” device. The second project will concentrate on assessing and advertising this product. The third project is about mobilizing policy to enhance the product's success. Throughout these projects, we will discuss the ethical and social aspects of corporate practices and technology more broadly.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

- Successfully identify labor configurations and business strategies as designs’ byproducts and connect them to STS themes.
- Facilitate communication across multiple disciplinary and audience modalities, including stakeholders, designers, and users via prototypes, demos, and visualizations.
- Communicating effectively social scientific and engineering problem-solving judgments behind each step taken in the design process via written (findings reports), oral (presentations), and visual (demos) means.
- Evaluate how complex problems come to be defined in policy via the production of environmental impact assessment and product life cycle documentation,
- Producing written work that accurately summarizes and dialogues with concepts from the field of Science & Technology Studies to discuss the intersections between design and society.
- Discussing the ethical and social aspects of corporate practices and technology more broadly.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Your Observations are worth 6 points.** This is a pass/fail/excel assignment. You will receive zero if it is not submitted, 3 points if it is submitted with minimal effort in presentation, or 6 points if effort was applied.

**Your slide deck is graded out of 2 points,** based on quality of visual communication (1 point), and tidiness of work (1 point).

**Your presentation is graded on a scale of 5 points.** The presentation should last approximately 10 minutes. It should present all the slides in the deck and successfully facilitate 5 minutes of Q&A.

**Your prototype will be graded on a scale of 3 points.** Your prototype must focus on enacting as faithfully as possible a specific task, feature or interaction of the product. This prototype will be used in two usability tests. Consequently, it must be sturdy. Additionally, your group should have a repair protocol.  
Refer the syllabus for more information.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic Integrity

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments students submit are their own work. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty, and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments submitted for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that violates this policy will result in a penalty. If found in violation of the academic honesty policy, students may be subject to two types of penalties. The instructor administers an academic grade penalty, and the student is reported to the Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education as appropriate. The first violation results in a 0 grade for that assignment. The second violation results in failure of the course. If you have any questions regarding this policy before submitting an assignment, please request clarification.
