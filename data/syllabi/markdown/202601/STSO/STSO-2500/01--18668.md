## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Recognize the role storytelling plays in history and appreciate the political and social factors that shape historical storytelling.
- Analyze and interpret historical sources.
- Generate questions and construct narratives about the past.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

*Journals Challenge Prompts:*

- Reflecting on your experience. What was it like to study these materials? How would you describe your experience? How did it make you feel?
- Highlighting quotes you found meaningful or significant.
- Identifying and explaining vocabulary, keywords, or concepts that seem significant.
- Keeping track of questions to discuss in class, topics to explore, and ideas to wrestle with.

*Other Endeavors*: These endeavors are intended to help us better comprehend the material, connect with our curiosities, and generate meaningful questions…

- Primary Source Projects
  - historical map interpretation
  - exploratory historical newspaper research project
- Presentations

*Final Reflections*: At the end of the semester, you’ll be asked to submit a portfolio of your work as well as a final reflection on your experience in the course.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

*Assessment*: There’s no standardized grading rubric for this course. But if you complete the required presentations and assignments and make a good-faith effort, you can choose your own grade at the end of the semester. If you don’t, we’ll need to meet and talk about it. Outside that, I’ll work to generate feedback (but not a letter grade) intended to cultivate a constructive dialogue rather than simply evaluate your efforts. Coming to class regularly, completing the reading and assignments, and participating in our discussions will afford you the most substantive benefits from this course.

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
