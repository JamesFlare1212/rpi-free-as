## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course combines demand, derived from consumer preferences, and supply, based on firms’ production functions, to establish market prices for goods and services. Calculus-based techniques are used to minimize costs and maximize utility and profits across differing industry structures. Product pricing strategies are examined. The course also provides an introduction to topics such as the pricing of stocks and bonds, game theory, positive and negative externalities, asymmetric information, and behavioral economics.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This is an intermediate course in microeconomic theory that elaborates on modeling at the calculus level. Building on foundational concepts, we’ll examine advanced mathematical models of economic behavior, specifically consumer and firm behavior. This includes decision-making processes of individuals and firms that may respond to changing constraints (budgets, costs, market power, etc). Economic interpretation of equations and graphs is emphasized throughout. Success in the class not only depends on knowledge of facts, but also producing novel insights through the models we encounter.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

- Introduction: Math review and principles of economics review (Chapter 2)
- Consumer choice and demand theory (Chapters 3–4)
- Theory of the firm (Chapters 6–7)
- Perfect competition (Chapter 8)
- Monopolies (Chapter 11)
- Game theory, monopolistic competition, oligopolies (Chapters 13–14)
- Consumer choice under uncertainty (Chapter 16)
- Adverse Selection and Moral Hazard (Chapters 18-19)

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- demonstrate the principles of microeconomics through agents acting in their best interests.
- understand utility representation of preferences via indifference curves.
- combine the firm’s production, costs, and market power to derive their profit function.
- impart economic reasoning and intuition to mathematical expressions.
- extract demand and supply based on utility maximization and profit maximization.
- differentiate between market structures from competitive markets to monopolies.
- recognize the role of uncertainty and risk in decision making.
- utilize game theory to analyze firm behavior and other situations of asymmetric information.

- **Grade Appeals:** For exams/quizzes, you will be allowed 2 days after receiving your grade to make an appeal for a regrade on a single problem per exam/quiz. A successful appeal will be one that displays merit for erroneous or misinterpreted marks. A successful appeal will also result in being rewarded an additional appeal following the same rules as the prior appeal.

- **Make up:** Make ups will not be granted except under extraordinary circumstances. Please contact me for more information.

### MyLabs Online Homework Policies

- **Lowest two homework scores dropped:** You homework grade will be determined from your 10 best scores (2% each).
- **Late Homework:** If the student misses a homework deadline, they will be allowed to submit before 10 PM EDT that day with a ``late'' penalty of 10% on the remaining problems. If the student does not submit before 10 PM, they will be graded solely on problems completed and the remainder will not receive credit. Please make an effort to attempt any problems since you will receive credit on any correct responses.

- **Picky Online Homework:** The online homework system is useful for instant feedback, but sometimes can be picky with how you respond. If you feel the system misinterpreted or erroneously did not give credit, please contact me with supporting evidence. I reserve the right to make corrections on your homework grading.
