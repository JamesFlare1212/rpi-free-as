## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course examines how the U.S. labor market works, addressing topics of labor supply and labor demand, theories of wage determination, employment benefits and regulations, how wage differentials develop and evolve, and the growing role of technology, automation, and artificial intelligence. We will examine the effects of education and skills training on wages and employment prospects, including the effects of training in Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics. Additionally, we will pay special attention to the effects of discrimination, using economic theory and empirical methods to help us understand how gender, racial, and sexual minority discrimination affect earnings and employment.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Student Learning Goals:** By the end of the semester students will be able to:

1. **Analyze Labor Market Mechanisms:** Students will understand the foundational theories of labor supply, demand, and equilibrium, and will be able to evaluate how market structures affect wages, employment, and policy outcomes.
2. **Evaluate Wage Differentials:** Students will be able to assess how factors like education, experience, productivity, and job characteristics contribute to differences in wages and employment across workers.
3. **Apply Data Analysis Skills:** Students will develop proficiency in interpreting empirical methods to understand labor market outcomes, including hypothesis testing, regression analysis, and causal research designs.
4. **Connect Theory to Policy:** Students will be able to use economic models to critically analyze or predict the impacts of current or future labor market policies, such as minimum wages, taxes, and workplace regulations, on workers and employers.
5. **Investigate Labor Market Disparities:** Students will be able to examine how various factors, including discrimination, influence wage gaps and employment differences, supported by theoretical and empirical evidence.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- By the end of the semester students will be able to: Explain the central features of the US labor market to others, including how they impact employment conditions, wages, and benefits.
- Explain to others what drives differences in earnings among workers, including the effects of education, experience, and effort.
- Define and explain discrimination to others, including how discrimination differs from prejudice and the differences between statistical and taste-based discrimination.
- Describe the extent of disparities in outcomes in the United States to others, including disparities by gender, race, sexual orientation, national origin, age, health behaviors, and disability status.
- Explain the key research methods used to identify differences in labor market outcomes between groups, including uncovering evidence of discrimination in market/economic settings.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

1. **Problem Sets (20 points, five at 4 points each)**

There will be six problem sets that will require you to work on various questions relating to economic theory, econometric/statistical issues, or to provide short written answers. I will count your best five “problem set” scores towards your grade (3 points each). These are graded as “check, check minus”, with 4 points for a “check” and 2 points for a “check minus.”

2. **Exams (70 points = two exams at 35 points each, see exam dates in course outline)**
   1. The first exam will focus on neoclassical labor market theory and *traditional* explanations (preferences, human capital, compensating differentials) for wage differences in the economy.
   2. The second exam will focus on discrimination in the labor market.

3. **Quizzes (10 points)**

We will have at least six short quizzes (2 pts each) based on assigned readings (I will count your best five scores). These will be graded as check/check minus, earning 2 or 1 points as appropriate. If you miss the quiz **for any reason**, your score on that quiz will be 0. Note that there will be more than five quizzes so there are no “retakes.”

4. **Attendance & Participation (5 bonus points)**

You are expected to attend class on time, remain engaged, and avoid distractions such as phone use or unrelated activities during class. Excessive lateness (>15 mins late) or distracted/disruptive behavior (laptop or phone use unrelated to class, for example) will result in being marked as absent and may negatively impact your grade. I begin tracking attendance after the add/drop deadline.
