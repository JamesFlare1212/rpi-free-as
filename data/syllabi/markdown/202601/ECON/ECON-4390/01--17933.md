## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a communication-intensive course that combines simplified economic theory with policy-relevant literature to explore the intersection of energy systems and societal values. Specifically, we will closely examine the fossil fuel industry, regulating emissions from oil and gas operations, the structure of U.S. electricity markets, renewable electricity generation, and the role of climate policy.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Filippini, M., and Srinivasan, S. An Introduction to Energy Economics and Policy.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

(1) Describe the economic and societal impacts of using markets to supply and allocate energy.  
(2) Recognize and explain market failures that warrant energy policy interventions.  
(3) Evaluate the benefits and costs of different energy policies.  
(4) Engage in meaningful and productive discussions on energy policy issues.  
(5) Communicate key concepts in energy and environmental economics effectively through oral presentation and written analysis.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Every week, students complete in-class worksheets on economic models of energy industry and regulation.  
Students present papers every week on energy policy, and engage in in-depth discussions on the assigned papers. Students also complete in-class reading quizzes to assess their understanding of the papers.  
There are four homework assignments to ensure students understand models covered in class.  
There are 2 in-class exams that test the students' knowledge on both the models and policy applications from papers.  
Students submit a final paper on an energy policy issue of choice in three stages where the instructor provides detailed feedback.
