## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course introduces mathematical and statistical methods that are central to contemporary research in the psychological sciences. Students will develop practical skills in probability theory, statistical modeling, linear algebra, optimization, and machine learning, with an emphasis on how these tools are used to analyze behavioral, cognitive, and neural data. The course prioritizes conceptual understanding and applied problem solving over formal mathematical proofs, equipping students to interpret, implement, and critically evaluate quantitative models used in current research. Students will implement models and analyses using the R programming language and modern data science workflows.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Student who complete this course will be able to: (1) Develop reproducible data analyses and visualizations in R by writing well structured code, using modern data science tools, and producing documented analyses suitable for scientific communication.
- (2) Apply core principles of probability theory and statistical modeling to characterize uncertainty, formulate likelihoods, estimate model parameters, and evaluate model fit in psychological and cognitive science data.
- (3) Formulate, estimate, and compare Bayesian models using numerical optimization, simulation based inference, and probabilistic programming tools, with attention to assumptions, overfitting, and model selection.
- (4) Use linear algebra and optimization concepts to understand and implement modern modeling approaches including linear models, dimensionality reduction, dynamical systems, and machine learning methods relevant to neural, cognitive, and behavioral data.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work.
