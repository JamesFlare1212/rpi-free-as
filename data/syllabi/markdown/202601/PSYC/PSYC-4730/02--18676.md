## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Students are invited to apply positivity practices to their own lives, contexts, and experiences to ensure understanding of the material from the book and from class. From your textbook authors: The text examines how positive psychology applies to stressors and health within such traditional research areas as developmental, clinical, personality, motivational, social, and behavioral psychology. Furthermore, the text offers perspectives on positive emotional states, research and theory on positive traits, coverage of positive institutions, and a look at the future of positive psychology.

In class journaling and exercises will reinforce the concepts taught in lecture.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Cheaven & Feldman (2022). *The Science and Application of Positive Psychology*. Cambridge University Press.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Understanding the Science of Well-being – Students will learn the fundamental theories, research, and applications of positive psychology, including concepts like happiness, resilience, strengths, and mindfulness.
- • Applying Positive Interventions – Students will develop practical skills to implement evidence-based strategies for enhancing well-being, such as gratitude practices, strength-based development, and fostering positive relationships.
- • Cultivating a Growth Mindset – Students will explore how to shift perspectives towards personal and professional growth, developing habits that promote resilience, optimism, and a fulfilling life.

Academic Integrity

Student-teacher relationships are built on mutual respect and trust. Students must be able to trust that their teachers have made responsible decisions about the structure and content of the course and that they are conscientiously making their best effort to help students learn. Teachers must be able to trust that students do their work conscientiously and honestly, making their best effort to learn. Acts that violate this mutual respect and trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Students Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. Your collaborative project should reflect an equivalent amount of effort by each group member.

I believe very strongly in academic integrity. If you are caught cheating on a quiz (e.g., talking, signaling, etc) or submit an assignment that someone has already submitted in a previous semester, you will receive a zero. You will be reported and a notation will be made in your file or you will be expelled.

Academic Integrity

Student-teacher relationships are built on mutual respect and trust. Students must be able to trust instructors have made responsible decisions about the structure and content of the course and that they are conscientiously making their best effort to help students learn. Teachers must be able to trust that students do their work conscientiously and honestly, making their best effort to learn. Acts that violate this mutual respect and trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Students Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work and collaborative projects should reflect an equivalent effort by each member.

I believe very strongly in academic integrity. If you are caught cheating on a quiz (e.g., talking, signaling, etc) or submit an assignment that someone has already submitted in a previous semester, you will receive a zero. I do report academic dishonesty.

***Please Take Note…***

It is likely that at some point a topic may arise that makes someone uncomfortable. If someone in class voices their discomfort, please respect their wish to move on to another topic. Know that I may not always be able to move on right away though. I want to make sure that I have discussed ways to work through the challenge. Having said that, it is also likely that topics arise in class that are important to talk about so that we learn how to work through challenges in life. For example, I will be talking about my experience with cancer. Talking about cancer may make some students uncomfortable. Talking about sensitive topics such as cancer is important so that we can learn how to think about those challenges and work through them. The first semester I shared with the students that I was a breast cancer survivor, a student came up after class and said, “Prof. Traver, I’m a cancer survivor too, and I want to learn how to think like you.” We both cried and hugged and laughed.

Bottom line: yes, we may discuss things that some may find uncomfortable. If we don’t discuss them, we are avoiding those things. When we avoid things, we don’t develop the skills to handle the emotions, and the situation could escalate. This class is to help prepare you for crises and challenges in life, so that you can deal with them, or even recover more quickly from them. With that being said, if a topic or discussion or question in class is too much for you at this point in time, step away and come back when you are able. We will not judge because we do not know what any one person is going through at that point in time.

***About Late Assignments***

Assignments are due with a time stamp on LMS by 8pm on the due date. This is a class on well-being and strategies we employ to maintain or even improve well-being. So the 8pm deadline is to help cultivate healthy sleeping practices. There is also a 5 day grace period with no penalty to submit an assignment (the exception is the On-Campus Activities assignment and the Well-Being Journal). After 5 days, the assignment will no longer be available to students and you will not get any extensions (unless you have extenuating circumstances…then PLEASE, PLEASE, PLEASE come talk to me so we can work something out).
