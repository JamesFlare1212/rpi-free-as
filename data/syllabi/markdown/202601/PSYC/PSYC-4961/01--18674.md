## Course Goals

### Goals:

*Students who successfully complete this course will be able to:*

- Analyze human memory processes at an advanced level
- Recognize how different studies in the literature of empirical and theoretical research increased our scientific knowledge of memory processes
- Express knowledge and informed opinions on the field of human memory in both written and verbal form
- Apply this knowledge in a class project

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Course Assessment Measures:**

***Assignments***

*AI Writing tools are not permitted for any stage or phase of work in this class. If you use these tools, your actions would be considered academically dishonest and a violation of the Academic Integrity Policy.*

Reading Responses:

Reading responses will consist of your thoughts, reactions, and/or questions to readings and class discussions, which you will submit on a Discussion Board on LMS by 1:00 pm on class day (or before). ***These responses must be your original thoughts or questions! You are smarter than AI :)***

Your reading responses may be as long (or short) as you wish (a few sentences or a question, a few paragraphs or two pages), but it must show evidence that you read and thought about at least a part of the assigned reading. In order to get credit for reading responses, you have to submit at least 7 reading responses throughout the semester.

Group Experimental Project:

Students will form groups of two or three to carry out a research project. For the project, with the instructor’s help and approval, the groups will design and conduct a pilot experiment on a certain memory process. The instructor will help each group choose a topic and design their experiment, possibly creating novel versions of tasks from classic experiments. In the end of the semester, you will report and interpret your results in an 8-minute conference-style presentation.

Group Article Presentation:

Before the pilot experiment is conducted, each project group will present and lead class discussion on a research article, which will be related to the project.

***Exams***

Two Exams (50%; 25% each): There will be two exams during the semester consisting of open-ended questions on materials (e.g. lectures, readings from text and journal articles) covered in class up to the exam date. Practice questions will be posted on LMS one week before the exam date. The exam will consist of six of the practice questions, four of which you will answer for full credit and the remaining two for bonus points.

*The exams will be closed books, but you will be allowed to use one two-sided sheet with notes, which will be collected with the exams. No calculation or communication devices will be allowed during the exam.*

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
