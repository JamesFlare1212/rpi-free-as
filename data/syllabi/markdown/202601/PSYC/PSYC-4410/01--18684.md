## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students who successfully complete this course will be able to:  
• Identify fundamental puzzles in perception and compare and contrast various solutions.  
• Demonstrate an informed understanding of the basic physiological and neural mechanisms that underlie perceptual systems.  
• Describe the psychological processes that support basic perceptual abilities, such as object recognition, color perception, space perception, and motion perception.  
• Demonstrate an informed understanding of how perceptual systems support routine, everyday activities.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Quizzes & Exams  
LMS Quizzes (30%): There will be six LMS quizzes consisting of multiple-choice or true/false questions covering assigned readings and the lecture materials that were covered in the previous weeks (not cumulative). They will be available on Blackboard for 48 hours and you will have unlimited attempts. The attempt with the highest grade will be kept in the gradebook. There are no make-ups or extensions for LMS quizzes. The instructor will drop the lowest quiz grade for each student at the end of the semester, so the LMS quiz portion of the course grade will be based on five quizzes (%6 each).  
In-class short quizzes (10%): There will be recurring in-class short quizzes consisting of three to five questions. There will be a total of around 10 in-class short quizzes and taking 5 of these will be sufficient, regardless of the correctness of your answers, to earn the full assigned credit of 10% of the course grade.  
Two Exams (60%; 30% each): There will be two non-cumulative exams during the semester consisting of multiple-choice and open-ended short answer questions. Questions will be on materials (e.g. lectures, readings from text, exercises) covered in class up to the exam date. The exams will be closed books, but you will be allowed to use one two-sided sheet with notes, which will be collected with the exams. No calculation or communication devices will be allowed during the exam.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
