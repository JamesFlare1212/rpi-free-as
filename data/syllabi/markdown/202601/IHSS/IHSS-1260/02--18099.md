## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This undergraduate course is an introduction to ethical frameworks and business law. Ethical approaches such as utilitarianism, rights, justice and virtue ethics will be discussed. Legal frameworks currently being applied in the business environment in the United States will be discussed.

The course looks to engage with moral and legal challenges that businesses currently face and may face in the future, such as maintaining a safe workplace, respecting employee rights and responsibilities, addressing threats to data privacy and security, issues pertaining to climate justice and environmental sustainability, and issues arising due to employee surveillance mediated through digitization among others.

Readings include Harvard business cases and media articles that highlight the societal benefits and societal costs that businesses may perpetuate. Students use case studies and readings as tools to understand and critique current business practices in the realm of globalization, marketing, employee well-being, automation and the adoption of AI, and the impact of businesses on the environment.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. Become familiar with different ethical and moral frameworks for decision making
2. Understand the evolution of business ethics and the development of business laws
3. Develop an understanding of the effects of law, government, society, technology and ethics on business creation and operation.
4. Practice how to identify and resolve ethical tradeoffs in professional, and business investment decision making contexts.
5. Develop critical understanding of current business ethics issues: AI ethics, Intellectual Property Protection etc. by reading and analyzing challenging cases
6. Developing oral, written and presentation communication skills via written assignments and group projects.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**CASE REFLECTIONS:**

**Cases can be purchased as a single coursepack ($57.40 for 10 cases and additional readings) here:**

**https://hbsp.harvard.edu/import/1376674**

The case reflection should be between 300-400 words. Although you may use AI tools to refine your writing, DO NOT use AI tools to summarize the cases (it makes mistakes!) and DO NOT use it to complete the assignment. For each case reflection, you will need to disclose the extent of AI use. Use the following structure:

**Opening (2–3 sentences)** Identify the central ethical dilemma in the case *in your own words*. Do not spend too much time summarizing the case. Instead, focus on the one decision point you found most ethically complex.

**Personal engagement (3-4 sentences)** What was your initial reaction to the case? How did it connect (or conflict) with your prior views on business ethics?

**Analytical application (4-5 sentences)** Apply at least **two ethical frameworks** we’ve discussed (e.g., utilitarianism, rights and duties, justice, virtue ethics, care). How would each framework evaluate the dilemma? Be specific about *who is affected, what the harms/benefits are, or what rights/duties are at stake*.

**Concluding reflection (2–3 sentences)** Where do you land after weighing these perspectives? What open question(s) remain for you about how managers should navigate such dilemmas?

**CASE DISCUSSION (GROUP ASSIGNMENT) –** Each of the 9 cases will be assigned to a group to present. You will be briefed by the instructor on how to prepare the case discussion. This will involve reading the case deeply, identifying the ethical dilemmas in the case, identifying stakeholders, finding resolutions and conducting some kind of follow up analysis. These presentations will take place on Thursdays.

**ATTENDANCE AND PARTICIPATION:** This grade is based on your attendance (frequency of coming to the lectures on time), listening actively and participating in class discussions.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

- During the course of the semester, we will read and discuss case studies to that highlight important ethical issues in business. You may be asked to submit written answers to questions based on the case. These case response due dates will be indicated on LMS.
- There will be 9 Harvard business cases throughout the semester, which will be presented as a group, for which individual reflections have to be submitted and, which will account for 35% of the grade. The case presentations (group level assignment) will account for 15% of your grade.
- Case reflections are due prior to class on Thursday when the case will be discussed.
- There will be two exams which will include multiple-choice questions and short-answer type questions. These exams will account for 40% of the grade (20% each)
- Participation and discussion is crucial to the success of this course. A primary objective of this course is to learn to analyze cases and appreciate nuance. Attendance and discussion will account for 10% of your grade.

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

**AI POLICY**

Use of AI tools, including ChatGPT, is permitted in this course for specific purposes. You may use these tools to conduct research(although you must verify the information these tools provide you) or to proofread and improve the quality of your writing.

Use of AI tools is prohibited when attempting exams/quizzes. My recommendation is to use AI to extract information and generate ideas. To adhere to our scholarly values, students must cite any AI-generated material that informed their work (this includes in-text citations and/or use of quotations, and in your reference list; using different color font to highlight text generated by AI). Where applicable, you must also verify the content generated by AI is true. Do not assume that AI tools are providing factually correct information. Finally, using an AI tool to generate content without proper attribution qualifies as academic dishonesty.
