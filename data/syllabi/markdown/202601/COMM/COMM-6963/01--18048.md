## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

How do our social relations as users or makers of digital media intertwine with the business practices, policies, and ever evolving products of consumer technology and media companies? And how do they connect to the broader economic system? These are questions about the political economy of digital media, and to try to answer them, this co-listed undergraduate and graduate course examines major approaches to describing, analyzing, and attempting to change it. By the mid-point of this course, you will have a grasp of the historical origins of the digital economy and will be able trace these origins into the contemporary landscape. By the end of the course, you will understand the competing emerging frameworks on political economy of digital media and will be able to apply these frameworks within your own original arguments or case studies of digital media companies.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to critically evaluate major historical and contemporary approaches to the political economy of media and technology, assessing their assumptions, analytical strengths, and limitations.
- Students will be able to analyze media and technology industries across historical periods, situating media technology companies and products within longer trajectories of capitalism, labor, regulation, and cultural production.
- Students will develop theoretically informed analyses of case studies that move beyond description to advance clear analytical claims about political-economic dynamics, drawing on course readings and independent scholarly research.
- Students will produce original written and oral arguments that engage scholarly debates and articulate normative, regulatory, or political implications of digital capitalism and its alternatives.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Assessments and Evaluation for COMM 6963:**

- **Class Participation (10%)** Course Participation includes participation in class discussions, exercises, and assignments.
- **Weekly Short Reading Responses (10% -- complete 10 total over the course of the semester beginning Week 2 at 1% each, about 250 words)**
- **Mini Case Study Responses (15% -- complete 3 total over the course of the semester beginning Week 2 at 5% each, about 400-600 words)**
  - o Each response will be based on an in-class case study discussion and should be approximately 400-600 words. Responses should apply relevant course concepts and readings to analyze the political-economic dynamics of the case. **Mini Case Study Responses are due within** **48 hours** **of the class session in which the case is discussed.** See assignment for further guidelines.
- **First Paper (15% – 6-8 pages double spaced)**
  - o Prompts and further details will be provided. You will have the option to propose your own topic (with instructor approval). 6000-level students are required to find and use at least two scholarly sources not listed on the syllabus through their own independent research.
- **Second Paper (15 % – 6-8 pages double spaced)**
  - o Prompts and further details will be provided. You will have the option to propose your own topic (with instructor approval). 6000-level students are required to find and use at least two scholarly sources not listed on the syllabus through their own independent research.
- **Individual Case Study or Policy Brief Presentation (15%, 10-12 minutes with brief Q&A)**
  - o Individual Case Study or Policy Brief Presentations will focus on a digital media or technology company, platform, policy, or infrastructure relevant to the course. The presentation should apply political economy frameworks discussed in the course to analyze the case, situating it within its historical, economic, and regulatory context. Presentations should **move beyond description** to offer a clear analytical claim about how the case illuminates broader dynamics of digital capitalism and its historical trajectory. Further details will be provided later in the course.
- **Individual Case Study Report or Policy Brief (20%, 2,500-3,000 words, due April 24 same day as presentation)**
  - o Comm 6963 students will also submit a Case Study Report or Policy Brief that expands on the in-class presentation. Further details will be provided later in the course.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance:**

Because class discussion contributes significantly to the educational goals and outcomes of this course, attendance is a requirement. Four unexcused absences without justification (such as illness, a funeral, athletic event, job interview) after the first week of classes may result in a reduction of the final grade by a full letter. Further additional unexcused absences may result in further letter grade reductions. If you are approaching four unexcused absences, or are experiencing any difficulties with attendance, please don’t hesitate to speak with me. Whatever the reason, you are responsible for talking to a classmate about the work done on the day you miss, for checking the LMS, and for obtaining information regarding any announcements or assignments made in class.

Please be on time for class. If you are late, you are responsible for correcting the attendance record at the end of class. If there is a pattern of lateness, it will be counted as 1 or more unexcused absences, depending on the specifics.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
