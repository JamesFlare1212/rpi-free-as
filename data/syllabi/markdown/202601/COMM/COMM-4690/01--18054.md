## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course covers theory, research, and practice in interactive, multimedia interface design. Topics include visual perception, information architecture, rapid prototyping, usability testing, cross-cultural design, interactive data visualization, augmented reality, and intellectual property.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course will focus on the design of user interfaces for interactive, multimedia computer programs. The class will present theoretical and technical issues in user interface design.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Lectures and discussions will include topics such as:

- information architecture for interactive computing
- effective screen design for user interfaces
- cultural dimensions of interface design
- usability testing
- intellectual property
- data visualization

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to critically analyze different types of data visualizations and interfaces designs.
- Students will be able to define the audience and the objectives for an interactive program and apply visual design, data visualization, and HCI research to meet those objectives. Using this information, they will be able to develop a design document, wireframes, and a storyboard.
- Students will be able to develop an interactive prototype.
- Students will be able to test the prototype, evaluate the results, and propose revisions for the interface design.
- For the data visualization project, students will be able to: o effectively communicate about problems/issues in this field in which data is a relevant tool, including writing about, presenting on, and visualizing data; o critically evaluate the pros/cons of different types of data visualizations to determine the most effective ways to visualize specific data relationships; and o create effective data visualizations by identifying the audience and objectives, deciding what data relationships meet the objectives, and determining the most effective interactive data visualizations to show specific data relationships.
- Students will be able to analyze peer projects and provide constructive feedback on the projects during class discussions.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Class attendance is required and will comprise 10% of your final grade. You may miss up to three classes without penalty and receive an A for class attendance. However, if you miss more than three classes, you will receive an F for that part of your final grade.

**Late Penalty**: I will take attendance during the first 10 minutes of class. **If you are more than ten minutes late for class, you will be marked “late.” If you are late for class three times, it will count as an unexcused absence.**

It is important that you arrive on time for class. We have class presentations and group work in this class and arriving late can be disruptive and not fair to your group. Also, at the start of class, I often cover important information, including upcoming assignments and deadlines.

**Projects**

Assignments and required class workshops/exercises are described below.

Details for each assignment will be covered in class lectures and handouts that will be distributed and posted on LMS. **The Analysis of Interactive Programs and Data Visualization assignments are individual projects. All other projects are team projects**. **Due dates are listed in the class schedule.**

**Analysis of Interactive Programs** **(Individual Project) 10% of final grade**

You will analyze the strengths and weaknesses of two websites and/or interactive programs, **one of which should be an interactive data visualization**. Address the points outlined in the assignment handout.

C**lass presentations are required. If you fail to do a presentation, your grade for this project will be dropped one letter grade.**

**Data Visualization Project (Individual Project) 15% of final grade**

You will use interface design principles and data visualization research to create an interactive design to visualize data relationships and analyze feedback on the design. You will select the data to analyze, the audience and objectives for the data visualization and use data design research and theory to determine the best ways to visualize the data using specific charts, data filters, and interaction that allows the user to compare different relationships.

You will work as a team to define the problem and specific data relationships to visualize. **Each member of the team will submit a separate paper. There will be individual grades.**

This project will involve a class presentation and a written paper. A handout with more details will be distributed later in the semester.

**Class presentations are required. If you fail to do a presentation, your grade for this project will be dropped one letter grade.**

**Design and Development of Interactive Program (Team Project)**

**There are three stages; each stage is 20% of final grade.**

You will have an opportunity to develop an interactive, multimedia program on a topic of your choice. The project that will be divided into three stages: 1) Design Document, 2) Prototype, and 3) Usability Testing.

Each stage of the project will be graded separately, and the grades will be weighted as follows in your final grade:

**Design Document 20% of final grade**

**Prototype 20% of final grade**

**Usability Testing 20% of final grade**

**There will be class presentations for each of these three stages in the project.**

**Class presentations (with participation from all the members of the team) are required.**

**If you fail to do a presentation, your grade for this project will be dropped one letter grade.**

**Required Class Exercise: Peer Review of Prototypes 5% of final grade**

You will have an opportunity to review all the prototype programs (except the one you created) and provide written comments for the teams to use, so they can make final revisions before they begin the usability testing.

**Grade** You complete the peer review of the prototypes. A  
You do not complete the peer review of the prototypes. F

**Progress Reviews**

There will be three progress reviews to show your progress with the development of the prototype and the materials for the usability testing of the prototype. The dates for the progress reviews are listed on the last page of the class schedule. Each member of the team must participate in these progress reviews.

**Final Grade**

To summarize, you final grade will be determined as follows:

Class Attendance 10%

Analysis of Interactive Programs 10%

Data Visualization Project 15%

Design and Development of Interactive Program

Design Document 20%  
Prototype 20%  
Usability Testing 20%

Required Class Exercise 5%

Your final grade will be determined by translating each letter grade into a numerical equivalent based on the following scale: A=4.0, A-=3.67, B+=3.3, B=3.0, B-=2.67, C+=2.3, C=2.0, etc. Your average for the course must be a 4.0 to receive an A.
