## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course explores theories and practices of adaptation across media formats and genres. We’ll consider what happens when we adapt a novel into a film, or produce a TV show inspired by a video game, or create a board game from a comic, and so on. This course has a particular focus on what happens in the transmedial adaptation processes between ludic and narrative experiences–i.e., between stories and game (or other interactive formats). We’ll trace some of the historical trends in adaptation studies, and use various conceptual frameworks to closely examine adapted media and games in connection with their source materials. Throughout the class and in their final projects, students will have opportunities to undertake critical analyses of adaptations and/or to produce their own adaptations in a medium of their choosing.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Articulate foundational terms, concepts, and theories from the field of adaptations studies.
- Analyze case studies of adaptations so that they gain insights into the ways that cultural texts are adapted across various media formats and cultural contexts.
- Compare and contrast their own and their peer’s application of the analytical processes through dialogic/collaborative presentations on a set of interrelated adapted texts.
- Produce and present to the class an extended analysis of a self-selected adapted work, or produce a theoretically-informed creative adaptation of an extent work.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

ADDITIVE ASSESSMENT:

Rather than an exercise in knowledge absorption, this course should feel like a process of exploration, articulation, reflection, and re-articulation. You’ll be carefully considering concepts in connection with concrete examples, putting yourself in dialogue with past scholars and with each other through the media and games we’re examining, and discovering new perspectives as you create your own work (analyses or adaptations of your own devising). Assessment in this class will be an attempt to measure your depth of engagement with and understanding of the material.

We will use an ‘additive assessment’ strategy, where everyone starts with zero points and then accumulates points based on assignments and activities; there are 155+ points possible. You’ll be evaluated on your depth of engagement with the material. You won’t be graded on whether or not you agree with my perspective on the materials; you’ll be graded on whether or not you’ve thoroughly thought it through. If you follow the guidelines for each activity and genuinely work towards thinking through and applying the concepts, you’ll get most or all of the points for the assignments.

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

Paper Formatting

For essays, please properly use academic formats, and correctly cite references. As a standard for this class, MLA format will be used. If you have a personal/professional reason for wanting to use a different format—APA, Chicago, etc.—I am perfectly open to that, as long as it’s a standard format. The Purdue University’s OWL website is an excellent resource for both MLA and APA formats and general academic writing practices: \<https://owl.english.purdue.edu/owl/>

HOWEVER…For certain of your writings—such visual essay final projects, personal reflections or non-traditional essays, or prototypes—you will be welcome to use a non-traditional format. Make sure that it makes sense for the project that you are doing, and discuss it with me if you have questions.

Use of Images or Audio/Video in Assignments

In this class, of course you should include images on any assignments and papers. If you’re using stills from motion pictures or audio, in your references you should give a citation for the media text itself (not the website where you found it), and when possible give a minute/second notation (just as you would give page numbers for a book) so that someone can find the cited material in the original text.

Late assignments\*/\*\*

As a general rule, you’ll lose 10% or 1 point (whichever is greater) per day if something is late.

*NOTE: I am always open to discussing extenuating circumstances, but will be much more lenient if you communicate with me promptly and professionally about what those circumstances are/were.

Revisions of Assignments

If you feel that the points weren’t as high as you think you could have achieved on an assignment, we can make arrangements for a resubmission to improve the points attribution of that assignment. Note that this will NOT be possible for the final projects due to time constraints on grading.

Lateness and Early Departure

Please make every effort to be punctual and stay through the duration of each class; if you occasionally must leave early, I understand. However, repeatedly arriving late or leaving early will count against you (see the attendance policy) by .5 points, at my discretion, unless we’ve made arrangements ahead of time or unless there are demonstrable extenuating circumstances.
