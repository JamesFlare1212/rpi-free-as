## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Through a series of projects, you will have the opportunity to learn about how organizations are structured (both formally and informally), how they do what they do, and how they measure and optimize performance. Using your own experience combined with interesting and informative learning resources, you will study the climate in which you currently operate and then apply methods to understand and optimize performance of your team.  
Credits Allocated: 3 semester hours

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Enhance their decision-making capacities – the process of using relevant contextualized information to evaluate and respond to a problem, decision, or research question.
- Understand techniques to manage people and teams – effective use of personal capabilities to facilitate achievement of goals with individuals, teams, and work units.
- Effective strategy implementation methods and resources – focus and align resources to achieve established organization’s goals and strategy.
- Communicate more effectively – communication with purpose and clarity to present ideas in written and oral formats.
- Optimize performance of their teams – make decisions to improve organizational performance given the upstream, internal and downstream ecosystems.
- Measure and evaluate performance – evaluate the organization’s performance using financial and internal analysis techniques to recommend improvements.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

## Academic Integrity

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Dean of Students office website defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty in the [RPI Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement.](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs) Please make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. Of course, learning with and collaborating with others in the class is encouraged – work submitted must be completely your own and original. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy may result in a penalty of failure of the assignment or of the course, depending on the severity. A grade of zero will be given on the first assignment where a violation of the policies described above is detected, and a referral to the Dean of Graduate Education. If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of “F” for the course and referral to the Dean. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask your faculty mentor for clarification.
