### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

The experiments in this course have been designed to represent the basic process by which a biotechnology product is developed and manufactured. You will create a recombinant organism that produces a protein product of interest. You will grow this organism and then extract, purify, and assay the protein product. In this way, you will get a feel for the workflow of biotechnology and biomanufacturing. The protein product that we will be producing in this course is b-galactosidase (also referred to as LacZ). This is an enzyme that catalyzes the hydrolysis of lactose to glucose and galactose. Indeed, low levels of this enzyme in humans give rise to lactose intolerance, and it is used commercially in lactaid and other milk products that have low lactose levels. It is also commonly used as a reporter gene in many molecular biology studies, so its gene is widely available in a variety of cloning systems. In addition, several artificial substrates for the enzyme exist which yield colored products; hence, there are relatively easy assays for its presence and activity.

This lab class incorporates many assays in which quantitative data is collected and analyzed. You will obtain hands-on experience in generating sets of data, as well as gain a realistic understanding of concepts such as dimensional analysis, experimental error, standard curves and linear ranges, and data fitting.

In this class, you will also learn how to prepare for laboratory experiments through watching tutorial videos and completing pre-lab assignments. These pre-labs help ensure that you understand (prior to starting the experiment) the technical concepts involved in each experiment, the materials and supplies needed, and the relevant safety concerns.

The main assignments in this class are designed to mimic an industry setting. As such, concise, accurate, and professional communication skills are required. Many aspects of the experimental setup will be performed by the TAs. However, you are ultimately expected to take ownership and responsibility for understanding how to conduct your lab experiments, analyze your results, and present your findings.

You will notice that we are using many premade reagents and kits. While this is not standard practice in university research laboratories (although it is becoming more common), it is fairly common in industry. Purchased reagents and kits offer better consistency and reliability but are relatively expensive. Furthermore, many buffers and reagents in this class will be prepared ahead of time by the TAs. You are still expected to know the contents of all reagents, solutions, buffers, and kits used in class.

Working in biotechnology and biomanufacturing requires a strong understanding of the nature of biological systems; this is best undertaken by direct laboratory experience. The systems in biochemical engineering work are quite distinct from almost any other in engineering, chemical or otherwise. This uniqueness is founded on the fact that biochemical engineering systems are usually living systems (i.e., often involving microorganisms that live, metabolize nutrients, produce waste and by-products, and even die). Even when microorganisms are not strictly involved, as in enzyme engineering, the protein catalysts one works with still require special treatment, unlike the treatment of more classical chemicals. The special features of biochemical engineering systems cannot be intuitively appreciated except by hands-on experience.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Lectures:** ***All students are expected to attend lectures in person*** to allow for an interactive learning experience, except for those who have been approved to be remote for medical or other reasons. Quizzes or other graded activities may be administered during the lecture. If a student is unable to attend the live in-person lecture (e.g., for illness, job interview, required quarantine, other emergencies), the instructors must be notified as soon as possible, and an excused absence may be granted. These students are expected to watch the livestreamed lecture on Webex or the archived footage posted on LMS.

**Labs:** Hands-on learning is a crucial aspect of this laboratory course. ***Thus, in-person attendance during your assigned lab sessions is required***. If a student is unable to attend lab for excusable reasons (e.g., illness, job interview, required quarantine, other emergencies), the instructors must be notified as far in advance as possible to obtain an excused absence, and arrangements should be made for the other group member to perform the lab alone. ***More than one unexcused absence in lab will result in failing the course*.**

***Special Note for Spring 2026:*** Please be respectful to the health of you and your classmates. If you develop COVID-19 symptoms or another serious illness, please do not come to lecture/lab in person. Notify Prof. Zha immediately and you will be granted an excused absence. You will be expected to participate remotely with your partner’s lab session, watch the recorded lecture video, and/or the TAs may perform your lab tasks for you.

### Other Course Policies:

**Teams:** Students will form teams of 2 at the beginning of the semester and will work in that team for the entirety of the course.  
**Notebooks:** Lab notebooks will be maintained electronically using OneNote, which is available to all students as part of the Office 365 software suite. (See https://itssc.rpi.edu/hc/en-us/articles/360010037611-Office-365-and-OneDrive-RPI-Access-Terms-and-Conditions) for installation information. Note that you can install OneNote on your own laptop and run it locally or you can run it in the cloud on OneDrive @ RPI. We recommend that you download this software as soon as possible and become familiar with its basic operation. When attending lab, it is recommended that students bring their laptop so that notes can be taken in real-time. Disposable keyboard covers will be provided so that you may work with your laptop with gloves on within the lab. Pre-lab assignments and lab notebooks will be submitted via Blackboard for periodic evaluation by generating PDFs from your OneNote pages. Lab notebooks will be submitted for grading in the middle and at the end of the semester.

**Preparation for Lab Work and Pre-labs:** Students should watch all relevant tutorials and lab videos and complete the pre-lab for the experiments of the week by **8 PM the evening prior to your lab session**. This is an important exercise for preparing to conduct experiments accurately and safely. **Pre-labs must be submitted as individual assignments**.

**Memos:** Each group will write three memos throughout the semester. These memos are prepared and submitted as a team. The memos are graded on a 10-point scale. The rubric and a sample memo will be available on LMS. They will be due by 5 PM on the dates listed in the syllabus. Each team will receive the option to revise and resubmit one memo during the semester for a re-grade. *Late memos will receive 10% off per day late and may be submitted up to one week late.*

**Work Contribution Statement:** For each memo, students will individually submit a statement describing the contributions of each team member (including themselves) to the preparation and submission of the memo. These statements are to ensure equitable contribution and effective teamwork. Team members are encouraged to come to a consensus, but each student must write their own statement and submit individually.

**Quizzes and Short Assignments:** Short quizzes will occur occasionally during lecture, typically at the start of class. They will cover material that you should have learned prior to the lecture and your understanding of previous experiments. ***Make-up quizzes will not be offered for any reason***, so it is critical that you contact the instructor ahead of time if you need an excused absence. Your lowest quiz grade of the semester will be dropped. In addition, there may be occasional short assignments and worksheets to help you prepare for lab sessions and to reinforce material discussed in lecture and during lab.

**Final presentation:** The final oral presentation will be done jointly with your teammate. Detailed guidelines for the presentations will be made available near the end of the semester.

**Final report:** The final report will be completed as a team. It should be in the form of a standard academic report with an abstract, introduction, methods and materials, results, discussion, and conclusions.

*In all oral and written presentations, proper use of the English language and effective communication plays a significant role in your grade. Moreover, in oral presentations, the quality of your presentation materials will also weigh into your grade. Remember that these should be considered like reports that you would present to your supervisor, clients, and colleagues in industry. Therefore, the highest level of professionalism is expected.

**AI Use for Preparing Assignments:** AI platforms, such as ChatGPT, can be a useful tool for professional technical writing. They can also be a demise to your learning and a significant detriment to your grades. AI can help you gather information, proofread your reports, improve your use of professional and concise language, and stimulate ideas. **However, AI cannot think for you.** It cannot perform your experiments for you. It cannot make observations in lab for you, connect those observations with outcomes/data, or critically think about why YOUR experiment did or did not work. AI does not know how to evaluate information supported by years of peer-reviewed science vs speculative pseudoscience with little support or logic. AI can sometimes hallucinate, creating fake references and information, so anything gathered from AI needs to be independently verified. AI cannot think on-the-spot for you in the lab, and you (not ChatGPT) will bear the consequences of lab accidents.

While usage of AI tools is not strictly forbidden in this course, you must proceed with caution if you choose to use AI. You should avoid using AI tools in preparing your pre-labs. Verify any information you obtain from AI with a trustworthy expert source (such as your TAs, instructors, a reputable textbook, or information from the manufacturer of kits/reagents/equipment used in lab). **You must append an AI Usage statement at the end of memos and the final report which describes how (if) you used AI in preparing the assignment.**

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:** All quizzes, worksheets, pre-labs, contribution statements, and lab notebooks are expected to be individual work. As described above, memos, final written reports, and oral reports will be completed in teams. You may discuss or share your laboratory results with other classmates; however, the writing must be your team’s. All reference materials (including web materials) used in preparation of reports must be cited appropriately using footnotes and/or a bibliography. ## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

**Laboratory Safety**

- Safety glasses, lab coats, gloves, long pants, close toed shoes, and laptop keyboard covers are mandatory at all times in the laboratory. Lab coats, gloves and keyboard covers are provided. Long hair must be tied back. If you do not have proper attire on, you will be told to leave the laboratory and receive no credit for the experiment. There will be no exceptions!
- **Do not wear contact lenses in the laboratory.**
- Additional safety procedures will be covered during the first laboratory period/orientation.
- Specific safety precautions are included within the laboratory procedures and proper use of any equipment will be demonstrated in class.
- *Always ask the instructor or TA if you have any questions or concerns about the proper handling of chemicals and biological materials or the proper operation of equipment.*
- Materials safety data sheets (MSDS) for chemicals and reagents used are provided in the lab.
- An eye wash station, safety shower and fire extinguisher are located next to each entrance to the lab.
- No food or drinks are allowed in the lab.

**Laboratory Waste Procedures**

- All materials that have touched live biological samples (*E. coli*, yeast, other cells) must be disposed of as biohazard waste – including disposable gloves.
- The instructor or TA will dispose of liquid cell culture waste. Petri dishes may be placed in the red biohazard waste container.
- All sharps (razor blades, needles) should be placed in red “Sharps” containers.
- All pipet tips should be placed in the “Biohazard Benchtop Keepers” provided to each group.
- Additional disposal instructions for waste generated in the lab will be provided by the TA or instructor. *Always ask if you have any questions or concerns about the proper disposal of laboratory waste.*
