## Course Goals

### Goals:

The purpose of this course is to introduce undergraduate students to the theory and practice of chemical reactor analysis and design. From the course learning, students are expected to develop an ability to constitute a set of organized questions to solve the chemical reactor design projects and to find the solutions for the questions. The MATLAB package will be used, and many of the homework assignments will require computer-based simulations. The course is often taught in a studio format, combining lectures, discussions and computer-based simulations in a single laptop-based classroom. Thus, students should plan on bringing their laptop computers to each class.  
An agreement for a new student version of MATLAB should be completed and a new license installed:

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate an understanding of the modeling, design and operation of batch stirred tank reactors, continuous stirred tank reactors (CSTR), and plug-fow reactors (PFR), and compare the performance characteristics of the different reactor types.
- Demonstrate an understanding of multiple steady-states, reactor stability, and runaways.
- Demonstrate an understanding of the safety implications of reactor design.
- Use computer packages (such as MATLAB) to solve reactor models.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Homework and exam will be used for the course learning outcome assessment.  
TENTATIVE EXAM SCHEDULE  
Mid-term exams will be held during regular class hours. Closed book and notes. There will be no make-up quizzes without a written excuse from the health center or the dean of students.  
Mid-Term Exam #1: 10 AM EST, February 17, 2026, Tuesday  
Mid-Term Exam #2: 10 AM EST, March 30, 2026, Monday  
Final Exam: Date/time will be available or discussed during the semester.

All exams are closed-book and closed-notes. Only a pen/pencil, an eraser, a scientific calculator, and a ruler are allowed during the exam.

Graded homework and exams will be returned in class.  
HOMEWORK ASSIGNMENTS  
Homework will be uploaded to the LMS. The majority of the homework assignments and projects will be performed in groups of **THREE** students; each student is responsible for understanding the entire assignment. Homework is due at the beginning of class. Late homework, including homework turned in during or after class, will be accepted, with a penalty of 25% for each day late (even 1 minute late). Although an assignment may have several problems, often a few questions will be selected at random for grading.  
Groups may discuss the problems with other groups, but are not allowed to share solutions (MATLAB m-files, etc.). Violations will be handled in accordance with the student handbook.  
Note about Showing Work: In this class, the process of getting to an answer is as important (if not more) than the actual answer. Therefore, you must show and justify all of your work to receive credit. If you do not show your work, you will receive zero credit (even if the answer is correct). Follow the following guidelines for full credits:

- Only single-sided hard-copy homework on letter-sized paper will be accepted. Electronic copies or homework on non-letter-sized papers, including papers with spiral-binding parts, will be returned without review.
- Your name and RIN must be at the top of the first page of homework. If not, your homework will be returned without review.
- Only properly stapled homework will be accepted. Non-stapled or paper-clipped homework will be returned without review.
- Your answer must be in the order of the questions. If your answer is not in the order of the questions of the given homework problem set, your homework will be returned without review.
- Your answer must be legible, and your work should be in the order of the logical developments for your final answer. You must provide a reasonable explanation regarding the development of your answer, as shown in the sample problems in the textbook or homework keys. Non-legible homework or answers without logical justifications will receive no points or a significant penalty.
- Late homework will receive a 25% penalty to the score for each day it is late.
- Note that well-prepared reports are a critical component in your career development.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Grade Appeal  
A student may appeal a grade by attaching a memo to the specific exams or homework assignment that is being appealed. This memo must state the specific reason(s) for the regrade. A verbal appeal is not accepted. The student has one week after the grades have been assigned to contest the grade. The regrading process will be based on proper physical and chemical implication(s) of the answer to the given question. Be professional in the appeal process.  
Use of Electronic Devices  
All mobile devices (cell/smartphones, computers, pagers, etc.) must be stored securely away during lecture hours and are not be used unless the use of a device is specifically directed by the instructor. Use of (or ANY interaction with) a mobile device during an exam without an explicit permission of the instructor will be interpreted as the illicit transfer of exam data and will be considered an act of cheating and will be treated as such.
