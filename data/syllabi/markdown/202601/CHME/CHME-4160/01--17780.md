## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The purpose of this course is to provide senior chemical engineering students with hands on experience operating and analyzing common chemical engineering unit operations. The unit operations covered in this laboratory course build on the theoretical material covered in previous and concurrent chemical engineering courses. Emphasis is placed on planning and executing the experiments, analyzing data and dissemination of results. Courses at RPI are generally expected to require 2-3 hours of outside work for each credit hour. You should expect, therefore, to commit at least 6 hours/week outside the classroom for this course.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Each student laboratory team will complete three experiments from among the following topics: Interfacial Forces / Spin-Coating, Polymerization, Virtual Reality: Chemical Reactor Design -or- Process Control, and Rheology. Each student laboratory team will also complete a semester-long laboratory design project in which students will design a maze-like separation device for fluidic particle separations, simulate its performance *in silico*, and experimentally validate its performance using a fluidic apparatus.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Each of the three experiments will contribute equally to the final grade.

Experiment Reports (3): 60 % of course grade; 20% each; Foreman Report submission required for each.

Laboratory Design Project (3 memos): 30 % of course grade; points are split evenly over memos. Effort and quality of deliverables will be factored into the memo grades.

Instructor’s Discretion (Lab safety, participation in discussions & experimentation): 10 % of course grade

## Course Calendar

### Calendar Details:

We will meet Monday morning for lecture approximately once every three to four weeks to discuss progress on the laboratory design project. Students will perform approximately one physical experiment per month. Students are expected to schedule sessions as needed for the laboratory design project.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance**  
The regular lab hours are listed as above, but some experiments may run over, depending on the students’ level of preparation and any problems encountered with the procedure. All students within a group are required to stay in the lab until the experiment and cleanup are completed. Students who need to leave early should obtain prior written approval from the instructor, or a grade deduction may result.  
**Laboratory Teams**

The unit operations laboratory experiments in this course require teamwork for successful completion. Laboratory teams will consist of three people, though some teams of two people may be needed. Students are free to choose their own groups during the first week of class. Students who are unable to form a group will be assigned to a group by the instructor.

### Other Course Policies:

Laboratory manuals (electronically posted on LMS) and basic personal protective equipment (safety gear) are required for this course. It is important that you bring your safety gear to each lab session. You are not allowed in the lab without safety glasses and a lab coat. In addition, we are required to wear nitrile gloves when working with chemicals.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Post-Experimental Procedures**

Running a laboratory safely and efficiently requires organization and adherence to safety guidelines. The TAs will make sure that you have all material, supplies and equipment ready and available to you before you begin the experiments. Your job will be to ensure that the materials, supplies and equipment are returned back to the location in which you found them before beginning your experiment. All chemicals, waste materials, and used PPE (e.g., gloves) must be discarded in the appropriate waste containers.

**Academic Integrity: Laboratory Reports**  
Evidence of collaboration, plagiarism, or falsification of laboratory data will be dealt with according to the Student Handbook. Consulting “back work”, meaning graded or ungraded laboratory reports from past years, is considered cheating and is strictly forbidden. Laboratory reports showing evidence of copying, paraphrasing, or adapting writing or calculations from previous years’ students, no matter how much or little work was borrowed, will be grounds for a grade of zero on the report in question for the entire group. Fair warning!
