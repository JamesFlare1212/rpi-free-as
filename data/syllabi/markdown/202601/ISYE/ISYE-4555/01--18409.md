## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Explain major ethical concepts and theories relevant to engineering practice.
- 2\. Analyze how moral philosophies and values influence individual and organizational ethical decision-making in engineering contexts.
- 3\. Interpret and apply the fundamental canons of professional engineering ethics to real-world scenarios.
- 4\. Evaluate the challenges involved in interpreting and applying engineering codes of ethics in complex situations.
- 5\. Critically assess diverse perspectives in ethical dilemmas and formulate reasoned arguments that respect differing viewpoints.
- 6\. Analyze the “problem of many hands” by identifying relevant stakeholders and assessing their roles in engineering decision-making.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity Matters:** Student-teacher relationships are built on mutual respect and trust. Students must be able to trust that their teachers have made responsible decisions about the structure and content of the course, and that they are conscientiously making their best effort to help students learn. Teachers must be able to trust that students do their work conscientiously and honestly, making their best effort to learn. Acts that violate this mutual respect and trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Students Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty, and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work.

**Policy on Collaboration and Cheating/Plagiarism**: On the first assignment where cheating or plagiarism is detected, a grade of zero will be given and it will be reported to the Dean of Students. If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of F for the course. If a student has any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

Please watch the following video – https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WKZhNT9TfbE

**Policy on the Use of AI:** Students may use AI as part of their research and preparation for assignments, or as a text editor, but **text that is submitted must be written by the student**. For example, students may use AI to generate ideas, questions, or summaries that they then revise, expand, or cite properly. Students should also be aware of the potential benefits and limitations of using AI as a tool for learning and research. AI systems can provide helpful information or suggestions, but they are not always reliable or accurate. Students should critically evaluate the sources, methods, and outputs of AI systems. Violations of this policy will be treated as academic misconduct. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, or if you are unsure whether a particular use of AI is acceptable, please do not hesitate to ask for clarification.
