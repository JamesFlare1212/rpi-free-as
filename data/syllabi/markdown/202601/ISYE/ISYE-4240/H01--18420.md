## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is meant to provide you with skills, tools, and perspectives for engineering project management. I want you to be able to directly apply what we learn to your work now and in the future. The focus of the course is two class projects that will provide you with tools that you might apply at work. One project will ask you to use a predictive project development approach, while the second will ask you to use an adaptive (agile) project development approach.  
**Credits Allocated:** 3 semester hours  
**Synchronous Sessions**

As part of the RPI at Work model, this course offers synchronous sessions to provide you with the opportunity to connect with your instructor-mentor and classmates in a live setting. Please refer to the RPI*Studio* to view synchronous session dates and Zoom links.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- RPI at Work Section Learning Outcome 1: Utilize predictive project development processes and tools (e.g., requirements traceability matrix, work breakdown structure, Gantt chart) to develop a project plan.
- RPI at Work Section Learning Outcome 2: Describe the limitations and strengths of predictive project management and adaptive (agile) project management.
- RPI at Work Section Learning Outcome 3: Utilize adaptive project development processes and tools (e.g., product backlog, product roadmap, release plans, sprint plans) to develop a project plan.
- RPI at Work Section Learning Outcome 4: Understand the “triple constraints” (scope, time, & cost) in planning and executing projects.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

## Academic Integrity

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Dean of Students office website defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty in the [RPI Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement.](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs) Please make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. Of course, learning with and collaborating with others in the class is encouraged – work submitted must be completely your own and original. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy may result in a penalty of failure of the assignment or of the course, depending on the severity. A grade of zero will be given on the first assignment where a violation of the policies described above is detected, and a referral to the Dean of Graduate Education. If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of “F” for the course and referral to the Dean. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask your faculty mentor for clarification.
