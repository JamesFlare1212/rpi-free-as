## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

From universities to multinational corporations, people will encounter daily the necessity of communicating across cultural borders. This course gives students a better understanding of what it means to communicate effectively across these cultural divides in today’s globalized society. Students will learn both principles of intercultural communication and the fundamentals of academic writing and presentations with a strong focus on audience and context. Writing skills and intercultural communication principles will be brought together in short written assignments or projects and a research paper and presentation.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand basic principles intercultural communication and critically engage with these principles in oral and written assignments
- Successfully use written and spoken academic structures and conventions to produce texts for global academic audiences
- Engage in the processes of academic writing and presentations including reading, outlining, drafting, peer review and editing
- Work collaboratively with classmates in the processes of writing, and presenting
- Find and cite information from appropriate sources, understand these sources and integrate these sources into writing

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- **Analysis and Response Papers 20%**

Students will summarize, analyze, and/or reflect on select articles, videos, or events according to the given guidelines.

1. Paper Proposal and Annotated Bibliography 15%

Students will write at least 2 drafts (1st draft 5%; 2nd draft 10%) of proposal for their final paper and an annotated bibliography for the paper using good summary techniques and correct APA citation style.

1. Paper Proposal Presentation- 5%

Students will present their paper proposal to the class in order to practice presentation techniques and will receive feedback on their proposal from their peers.

1. Presentation-15%

Students will give an academic presentation of their final research paper.

1. Researched Paper- 25%

Students will write a 6 page researched paper on a topic approved by the instructor. The students will write 3 drafts of the paper. The first draft will be graded for completion as a homework assignment and will be used for peer review in class. The second (5%) and third draft (20%) will both be graded and will both contribute to the final score of the paper.

1. Homework- 10%

Short weekly assignments will be given throughout the semester.

1. Participation and Preparation- 10%

Students will be expected to attend and actively participate in class throughout the semester. Students are also expected to come to class having read the assigned materials. Students may lose points in this category if they do not attend class, attend class but are disengaged and do not participate, or have not read material prior to class.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance Policy

Students are expected to attend on time and actively participate in all classes. As students cannot participate well in class without being present, students will be expected to be physically in class and participate actively to receive full points for participation.

5 participation points are given for each 50 minutes of class (10 points for each class). Students can miss a total of 40 points and still receive a perfect attendance score. These classes can be missed for any reason without any documentation.

If a student has a prolonged illness or other circumstance that would cause them to miss more classes, please see the instructor and special arrangements may be made.

Students arriving after attendance has been called will receive 9 points for the day (if they fully participate while in the class). Students arriving more than 10 minutes late but less than 1 hour and 10 minutes late will receive 5 points for the day (if they fully participate while in the class), and students arriving more than 1 hour 10 minutes late will not receive any points that day.

### Other Course Policies:

Rules for Assignments:

LATE POLICY: Late submission of assignments will be penalized 10% for each day after the due date (i.e., 1-24 hours late is a 10% deduction; 25-48 hours late is a 20% deduction) unless otherwise notified. An absence is not an excuse for not completing homework. However, PLEASE always email me BEFORE you know you will be absent or if you cannot complete your work by the deadline.

ABSENCE POLICY FOR IN-CLASS ASSIGNMENTS: If you are absent on the day of an in-class assignment, such as a quiz or a peer review and your absence is included within the 5 allowed absences, you may be allowed to make up the assignment or complete a similar assignment. If you have missed more than 5 days, you will no longer be allowed to make up in-class assignments.

ABSENCE POLICY FOR IN-CLASS PRESENTATIONS: You must be present in class on the day of your presentation to receive full points on the presentation unless you have talked to me in advance and I have approved your request to present later (requests will usually only be approved in the case of medical or family emergencies). If the request is not approved or you do not talk to me in advance, you will be allowed to present outside of class for a significantly reduced score.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

For plagiarism that is deemed unintentional (poor/lack of citation), the first violation will received a reduced score on the assignment. Further violations will result in a 0 grade for that assignment.

For plagiarism that is deemed intentional (copying someone else’s work), the first violation will result in a 0 grade for that assignment. The second violation results in failure of the course. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
