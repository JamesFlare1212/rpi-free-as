## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This class is an introduction to communication and media studies. As a survey of approaches to addressing the cultural impact of mass media and popular culture (including television, film, music, radio, games, print, the internet, social media, and so on) this course aims to increase your media literacy through analysis of specific media texts and discussion of broad topics such as commercialization, ideology, and representation. The course will serve as a ‘sampler plate’ of approaches to media studies for students from all majors, and as a first step/core course for majors and minors in Communication, Media, and Design. As an inquiry class, we will primarily consider the functions media play in shaping our individual and social identities. We’ll think through how they work on each of us individually, as well as on groups of us collectively, and we’ll take action towards becoming more engaged in media processes.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1\) You will develop tools for ‘critical media engagement,’ learning how to analyze, interpret and appreciate the capabilities and functions of different media forms. We’ll explore the distinctive features of various media—their inherent strengths or limitations, and their institutional or structural biases. You’ll gain an understanding of the historical and socioeconomic origins of modern media, and their profound impact on contemporary society. A particular emphasis in this version of the class will be on representation in media; here, the concept of ‘representation’ is loosely interpreted, including but not limited to representation of identities (race, ethnicity, gender, sexuality and so on). We’re trying to understand how our ‘re-presenting’ of reality shapes our understanding of it.

2\) As a complement to these critical tools, we will enhance your ‘reflexive media awareness,’ a term that refers to your processual and dialogic understanding of your relationship to media. In other words, you should gain a deeper understanding of how media shapes us as individuals, as groups and communities, and as cultures.

3\) You will hone your communicative/generative skills by articulating and refining your thoughts in oral, written, and presentational forms. If you are so inclined, you’ll have the chance to create media works in various formats as a way of engaging with concepts we encounter.You should leave the class able to more fully articulate your experience of media, contextualize it, and empathize with others’ perspectives.

4\) Finally, an underlying theme of the class is your involvement in a collaborative learning process; the class will be shaped by your interests and prior knowledge as a community, as well as by my disciplinary, personal and pedagogical expertise. Through the use of non-traditional educational strategies, we’ll engage in meta-examinations of the learning process, and we’ll consider how the course itself functions as a ‘medium’ for learning.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

ASSESSMENT: ‘MEDIA ENGAGEMENT POINTS’-BASED ADDITIVE GRADING

INTRODUCTION: Assessment/Grading for this class will unfold a little differently than some of you are used to. Using what can be called an ‘additive’ or a ‘gameful’ system, your performance in the class is based on the points that you earn–much like in many games (some video games, table-top role-playing games, or what have you). Also like in some games, or in many popular films/TV, there’s a ‘clock’ for some assignments, defined by milestones in the semester–some flexible, some fixed.

Everyone begins with 0 points. At the start of the semester, there are 380,000+ points available. As you accomplish tasks in the class, you build up points towards your final grade. There are central tasks everyone should complete (like the opening activities and essay, daily readings and daily assignments, and the final course reflection)--activities that allow us to do discussion work in the class. But many major assignments in the class, including the final projects, are on a self-selected basis. You choose which assessments you will do in order to build points up to the grade level that you want. However, also know that if you want an A in the class, you will need numerous activities from all categories of assignments, and you will likely need to do well on the final project.

Goal of this approach to assessment: What I’m looking to do here is to get you to consider a different way to assess and evaluate how to gain skills and knowledge in a class. I’m not so much grading your work, but trying to measure your level of engagement. This is a class about a topic for which there isn’t a single body of knowledge or point of view, so this grading system is meant to encourage personal, active engagement with the material. So while we will discuss and debate your positions, and the quality of your thinking, I’m giving you points based on the degree of engagement, rather than whether your viewpoints are ‘correct’ or not. Think of a grading system as a ‘medium’ for your evaluation/assessment; by using an alternate method, I want to put into relief the assessment systems classes generally use. I welcome your feedback on the system I’m using; you’ll have plenty of opportunities to give it.
