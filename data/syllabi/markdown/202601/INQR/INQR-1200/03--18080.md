## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a one semester survey course in micro- and macroeconomics with two goals: (1) to introduce a wide variety of concepts and applications in the broad subject of economics, and (2) to function in its special Rensselaer designation as a HASS Inquiry and Data Intensive Course. This course approaches economics as a pluralistic and critical social science, emphasizing the role of institutions, historical context, and competing perspectives. Topics covered include but are not limited to demand and supply theory, market structures, the role of government in resource allocation choices, gross domestic product, economic growth, inflation, and monetary institutions. As a HASS Inquiry course, a foremost objective will be to evaluate multiple diverse perspectives on contemporary and complex global issues and address their implications to social equity. Furthermore, the course is designated as a Data Intensive course and provides opportunity to practice analysis of economic data.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand the assumptions of the economic models used in contemporary literature within the discipline.
- Interrogate the assumptions of these models in the context of policy issues using empirical findings.
- Analyze socio-economic issues accounting for diverse points of view, particularly of those who have been historically deprived of economic or political power.
- Engage in meaningful discourse on economic policies proposed or enacted in the U.S. and elsewhere.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1\. Homework and Exams  
There are 4 homework assignments to test the understanding of economic models covered in class, along with two exams.  
2\. Readings and Discussions  
To contextualize these theoretical concepts, students work in small groups (typically groups of four) to deliver a concise, 20-minute presentation on an assigned reading that discusses pressing socio-economic issues in the U.S. and around the world. Following the presentation, the presenting group leads a class discussion of the assigned reading by posing questions and actively engaging with classmates’ comments.  
3\. In-Class Journal Entries on Readings and Presentations  
After the presentations and the discussion, each student submits a 100-300 word journal entry evaluating how effectively the presentation summarizes and engages with the assigned reading. This reflection is written during class at the end of the session, and submitted on LMS. Reflections are graded based on the clarity and cogency of the analysis, as well as a demonstrated understanding of both the presentation and the reading.  
4\. Final Reflection  
Each student writes a short final reflection on the economic concepts they learned throughout the semester, relating them to a policy issue of interest.
