## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Many firms consider their people to be their most important asset, since everything within an organization gets done through its people. No matter whether it is a product or service, the organization’s people are the ones who invent, produce, sell, and deliver. This course is about the management of that asset and is designed to provide students with an overview of how employees and managers successfully utilize human resources to gain competitive advantage. Students will learn the theories and practices involved in the management of human resources, including strategic alignment, recruitment, selection, performance appraisal, compensation and reward systems, benefits, career development, and equal employment opportunity. The emphasis will be on understanding and applying these concepts to help students succeed in the wide-range of positions and organizations in which students may eventually find themselves. To help students prepare for their career and achieve success in organizational life, this course provides students an overview of how to manage people by viewing them as resources that must be carefully managed if the organization is to survive and prosper. It short, this course is about managing talent and people – it is not about the human resources department nor is it meant to prepare individuals to work in that area. Rather, its focus is on managers in their respective disciplines (e.g. IT department, Marketing department, Accounting department, etc.) and how they must help support and design systems to best manage their company’s most valuable asset, its people.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Class Contribution (10%):** In complex business situations there is often more than one correct answer. As a result, we will discuss your ideas, points of view, and approaches for solving problems posed in class. Participation in class discussions is therefore important to fully understand and integrate the material. In this course a portion of your grade will therefore depend on your active participation in class. This includes active participation in discussions, cases, exercises, presentations by peers and guest speakers, etc. Obviously, the thoroughness of your preparation and the ability to participate in class are intertwined.

You can participate in many ways: respond to a question, advance the discussion by commenting on what someone says, take the direction of the discussion in an insightful new direction, illustrate with an example, make a connection to the supplemental readings, bring in an additional reading that relates to the current material, etc. You are highly encouraged to attend our synchronous discussions and participate actively, so that you can learn and integrate the material.

The quality of your participation is more important than the quantity. I use the following criteria to judge the quality of your participation:

- Is the comment accurate, incisive, and insightful? Does it combine the facts from the readings, the course concepts, and critical thinking to cut to the heart of the matter?

- Does it advance the discussion in a way that enhances our understanding of the problems and their solutions (as opposed to being repetitive or to rehashing facts without purpose)?

- Does it demonstrate a willingness to test new ideas or to challenge constructively the ideas that are on the floor?

- Is the position supported by well-reasoned argument?

During class sessions, I will rely on both volunteer participation and cold calls. I do not cold call to embarrass you or to catch those that have not kept up with the reading. Rather, I cold call to help you learn to reason critically, think on your feet, and to articulate your conclusions and arguments verbally in a convincing way on short notice. These skills are paramount to business success.

**Exams (30%):** There will be two exams for this course covering each half of the course material presented in the textbook, each worth 15% of your final grade. Everything discussed in class is fair game for the exams, including lecture material, textbook, readings, handouts, case/exercise discussions, etc.

**Course Project (25%):** This semester the course project will be focused on developing a “State of Artificial Intelligence Use in Human Resources” white paper/guidance document as a class. The Addendum after the Course Schedule provides a more complete description of this group-based, semester-long project, and it will be discussed in class more thoroughly. Additional grading criteria will also be provided.

**Manager or HR Expert Interview (5%):** Each student will interview a manager or HR expert to better understand what are the most difficult aspects of his/her job. A write-up and description of the manager’s most difficult challenges will be due as noted on the schedule. You will also use this interview as a part of your part of the AI in HR project.

**Wildcard Homework (5%):** The instructor reserves the right to assign any number of off-the-cuff assignments throughout the semester. Students will always be given ample time to complete these assignments, depending on breadth and depth, and the total of these random assignment will be no more than 5% of your final grade.

**Pearson MyLab Homework (25%):** During the semester, there will be homework assigned using the Pearson’s MyLab online platform. This will be in the form of chapter quizzes (due for completion before the class time period when those chapters are discussed) and other assignments as noted in the syllabus and assigned via the LMS.

As in the business world, all assignments are expected on the due date; late assignments will not be accepted. The lowest two graded homework assignments will be dropped for final grading to account for missed assignments due to illness or forgetfulness. These assignments are open book/resources—so there’s no reason not to get close to full credit for this portion of the grading.

A final note about grading:

Strong written and oral skills greatly facilitate success in the business world. Those individuals who can effectively communicate their ideas will have an advantage over those that cannot. As such, the evaluation of all assignments center on clarity of thought, conciseness of expression and thoroughness of research.
