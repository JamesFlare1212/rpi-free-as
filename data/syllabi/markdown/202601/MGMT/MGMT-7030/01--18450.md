### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

On completing the course, it is expected that students would have learned and would be able to demonstrate an understanding of the following.

1. The purpose and process of managing the overall business enterprise
2. The content of business strategies and corporate strategies
3. Ways by which one evaluates whether the strategies are likely to succeed
4. The processes by which the strategies are formulated
5. How one evaluates whether strategies can be implemented successfully

Skills objectives

In addition to knowledge objectives, the course will provide an opportunity to develop or apply the following skills:

1. *Analytical skills: synthesize functional knowledge to develop skills pertaining to achieving and sustaining superior performance*
2. *Group work skills: work on conceptual, ill structured problems as a team to come up with solutions*
3. *Business writing skills:* *communicate qualitative and quantitative ideas effectively*
4. *Live presentation skills: verbally communicate abstract ideas to functional specialists and implementers of solutions*

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Policies**

***Attendance and makeup***

Attendance (and participation in class discussions) is expected from all students. In the interest of being fair to every student, assignments will not be accepted late. Also, make-up tests will not be given if you miss a scheduled test (except under extraordinary circumstances).

***Academic integrity***

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own performance. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty, and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty. If found in violation of the academic honesty policy, students may be subject to two types of penalty. The instructor administers an academic [grade] penalty, and the student is reported to the Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education as appropriate. The first violation results in 0 grade for that assignment. The second violation results in failure of the course. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

***Collaborative work and plagiarism***

All assignments and tests, except ones explicitly identified as group assignments, should be your own work. You cannot collaborate with anyone else in completing these assignments and tests.

For papers or presentation projects, if you have to use arguments or conclusions from experts or sources using their own words, you can do so in a limited fashion (not more than a couple of sentences) but only after clearly identifying these as direct quotes (by enclosing the direct quotes within double quotation marks) and providing a reference to where the quotes were taken from (e.g., Author, year, publication title, page #). If your work is found to be plagiarized, you will receive a grade of zero for the assignment in the first such instance. If there is a subsequent infraction, you will receive an F for the course.

For group projects, you should collaborate only with members of your group and not with students in other groups.

***Electronic device use***

Please switch off electronic devices (cell phones, laptops, iPads, etc.) during class. You can use these only when I ask you to do so.
