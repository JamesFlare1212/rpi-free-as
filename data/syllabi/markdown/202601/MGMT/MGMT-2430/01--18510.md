## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Marketing is a dynamic and interdisciplinary field that combines science and art to shape consumer behavior and drive business success. This course provides students with a comprehensive introduction to marketing principles, focusing not only on core concepts and practices but also on the ethical, societal, and environmental impacts of marketing decisions. Students will explore the role of marketing in addressing global challenges, cultural diversity, and sustainability, while also learning how to critically evaluate marketing strategies and their broader implications. Through case studies, real-world examples, and interactive assignments, this course emphasizes the power of marketing to influence society and create meaningful change.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**STUDENT LEARNING OUTCOMES:**

Upon successful completion of the course, students will be able to:

1. Demonstrate functional knowledge of marketing, including the basic concepts, principles, practices, and processes such as the 4Ps, consumer journey, branding, and customer relationship management (CRM).
2. Demonstrate the ability to analyze and evaluate the marketing environment, marketing strategies, and frameworks such as SWOT analysis, BCG Matrix, and Porter’s Five Forces.
3. Develop practical solutions to real-world marketing problems, including marketing planning, and communicate them clearly in both oral and written work.
4. Demonstrate the knowledge of the current and emerging issues in marketing, such as digital marketing, social media marketing, globalization, and ethical data practices.
5. Analytically solve quantitative marketing problems, interpret their implications for marketing decision-making, and apply relevant analytical tools effectively.
6. Deliver high-quality oral presentations using both facts and data, supported by clear visualizations.
7. Demonstrate a basic understanding of the marketing research process, including data collection and analysis.
8. Demonstrate an understanding of the ethical, societal, and environmental implications of marketing, including diversity considerations.
9. Critically evaluate global and cultural factors in marketing, recognizing their impact on strategy and decision-making.
10. Understand how marketing can address societal challenges, such as sustainability and social equity, through promotion design and stakeholder engagement.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work.
