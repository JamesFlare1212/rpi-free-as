## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. Ability to differentiate between types of risk (market, credit, operational, business, strategic, etc.) and measures of risk.
2. Apply the terminology, concepts, and tools available for risk management.
3. Implement risk management frameworks for specific issues and risk types (market, credit, interest-rate, liquidity, and operational).
4. Apply the concepts underlying insurance and strategy risk for management using insurance
5. Understand the various risks in managing emerging technologies.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Identify, classify, and critically evaluate major categories of financial and enterprise risk—including market, credit, liquidity, operational, strategic, and emerging technology risks—using established risk management frameworks and regulatory perspectives
- 2\. Apply quantitative risk measurement techniques (e.g., Value-at-Risk, stress testing, credit migration models, asset-liability management) to real-world financial data in order to assess, compare, and manage risk exposures across institutions and markets
- 3\. Design and implement risk management strategies and instruments—including hedging, securitization, insurance, and derivatives—to mitigate specific risk types while accounting for institutional constraints and trade-offs
- 4\. Utilize computational and statistical tools (e.g., Python, R, Excel) to conduct data-driven risk analysis, model validation, and scenario evaluation consistent with professional financial risk management standards
- 5\. Assess and manage risks associated with emerging technologies and financial innovations, such as FinTech, big data, machine learning, blockchain, and cybersecurity, integrating model risk and operational considerations into enterprise risk management decisions

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
