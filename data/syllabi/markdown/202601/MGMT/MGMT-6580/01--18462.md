## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is designed to help students develop an understanding of the strategies and practices involved in marketing technologically oriented products and services. Students will learn where these strategies differ from marketing of non-technical products/services and appreciate how and why they vary. Students will learn how to critically analyze marketing problems, develop strategies, and design a comprehensive marketing plan. Through case discussions, interactive assignments, and a major group project, the course emphasizes key skills such as teamwork, communication, and critical thinking. Students will also explore the broader role of marketing in addressing global challenges, cultural diversity, and sustainability, while reflecting on the ethical and societal implications of marketing decisions. By the end of the course, students will be equipped not only with foundational marketing knowledge but also with the professional skills necessary to evaluate , develop and present marketing strategies and plans in diverse contexts.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Upon successful completion of the course, students will be able to:

1. Demonstrate functional knowledge of marketing concepts for high-tech products, principles, and practices, including strategic marketing planning, understanding high-tech consumer behavior, how to conduct marketing research, and the 4Ps (Product, Price, Place, Promotion).
2. Analyze and evaluate the marketing environment using strategic frameworks such as SWOT, BCG Matrix, Porter’s Five Forces.
3. Understand the customers of high-tech products. This includes their decision-making journey, different categories/stages of adopters and the basics of segmentation, targeting and positioning.
4. Demonstrate an understanding of the marketing research process in guiding marketing strategies.
5. Apply marketing mix decisions—product, pricing, place, and promotion—by using appropriate models and analytical tools to support decision-making.
6. Evaluate the ethical, societal, and environmental implications of marketing decisions, including issues of diversity, sustainability, and social responsibility.
7. Develop and present practical marketing solutions through case analyses and marketing plan projects, demonstrating clear communication in both written and oral forms.
8. Deliver professional-quality oral presentations supported by structured arguments, data, and clear visualizations.

## COURSE ASSESSMENT MEASURES

1. **Exams (35% total):** There will be two exams in this course, and each exam will be non-cumulative. The material assessed will be drawn directly from what we cover in lectures as well as key ideas and themes that come up during class discussions. If you require accommodations, please make sure to share that information by **23rd January** so arrangements can be made in a timely way. Make-up exams will be evaluated on a case-by-case basis and may be subject to up to a **50% penalty**, depending on the circumstances.
2. **Case Studies Written Assignment (15% total):** There will be **9 groups of 4 people each**. Each group will be required to complete an extensive analysis of **two cases****.** The cases you submit will be different from those discussed in class and must be completed as a **written submission****.** For each case, you should:

(1) summarize the situation the focal company is examining,

(2) provide a market analysis using relevant frameworks (e.g., SWOT, BCG, Porter’s five forces etc.),

(3) answer the case questions provided in the document uploaded on the LMS, and

(4) discuss general recommendations to address the problems faced by the focal company by applying concepts covered in class.

The deadline is **April 13 at 6:00 PM****.**

Please note:

1\) you must use **only the information contained in the case**. The company in the case might have taken decisions later (after the period covered in the case), but those decisions are not the purview of the case analysis.

2\) Only use those concepts and frameworks which are appropriate for the case. You do not need to use all the concepts and frameworks which we discuss in the lectures.

**III. Marketing Plan (10% + 30%):** The marketing plan project will be completed in **9 groups of 4 people each** (the same groups as the case studies). One of the major goals of this course is to develop the ability to write a **professional marketing plan** for your ideas one that demonstrates a solid understanding of the concepts covered in the course and, ideally, has the potential to be implemented with a relatively high likelihood of success. For this project, you will act like a **start-up seeking seed funding**. The project has **two components**: (1) a plan presentation and (2) a plan report. The **plan presentation (10%)** will be evaluated by both peers and the instructor and may be up to **30 minutes** long. The presentation includes three parts: the **product idea** (note: the idea itself is not evaluated), the **market analysis (15%)****,** and the **marketing strategy (15%)****.** The marketing plan report must be submitted after the presentation, with a deadline of **May 6 at 6:00 PM****.**

1. **Individual Ethics Assignment (5% total):** A short written assignment will allow students to apply course concepts individually. The assignment will be 5-6 pages (double spaced). the deadline will be 12th March ,6 PM
2. **Attendance (5% total):** All class sessions are mandatory unless otherwise specified, and all students must be present for group presentation sessions. Students are expected to arrive on time; anyone who is more than 10 minutes late will be marked absent. However, if the instructor is more than 10 minutes late, everyone will be marked present. Approved accommodations will be treated as a “present” and include the following: health-related absences for up to five sessions (you must email within one week of the missed class), athlete absences on game days, and other circumstances supported by a dean’s note indicating an “excused absence.” Attendance grades are assigned as follows:

**90% or higher attendance** (three or fewer missed sessions) earns a **5**;

**80–90%** (four to five missed sessions) earns a **4**;

**70–80%** (six to seven missed sessions) earns a **3**; and

**Below 70%** (more than seven missed sessions) earns a **0**.

Students can also earn **3 extra credits** for **100% attendance**.

**TEAMWORK: ADDRESSING CONTRIBUTION ISSUES**

You are expected to work with your team members and contribute your fair share. By the end of semester, if there is a group member who has not contributed and the rest of the group members agree, the group can request in writing to have that individual penalized on the assessment. The entire team will be required to meet with me so that I can determine if any penalty should be imposed on the individual. Please make sure to note that it is up to the aggrieved team members to show documented proof that the individual did not adequately participate (i.e. attendance records, meeting notes etc.).

**No plagiarism is accepted. Any instance of plagiarism will get ZERO on the component.**

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
