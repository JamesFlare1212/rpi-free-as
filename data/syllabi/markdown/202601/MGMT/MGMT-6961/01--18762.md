## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

This is a seminar class, and as such there will be no required texts. As pointed out above, the readings and discussions will be based on journal articles and working papers which are listed below.

**Recommended Reading**

I strongly recommend that each of you get a hold of a general MBA level textbook. Examples include, Corporate Finance by Berk and DeMarzo; Corporate Finance by Brealey, Myers and Allen; Corporate Finance by Ross, Westerfield and Jaffe.

Most, and more than likely all of you will be doing empirical work. I should point out that corporate finance research is overwhelmingly empirical in nature. This means that you have to be comfortable with and have a sound knowledge of econometrics. Here is a list of some books that will be helpful:

Angrist, Joshua and Jorn Pischke, 2009, Mostly Harmless Econometrics, Princeton Publishing.

Scott Cunningham, Causal Inference The Mixtape. Available online for free.

Jeffrey Wooldridge, Introductory Econometrics: A Modern Approach

Jeffrey Wooldridge, Econometric Analysis of Cross Section and Panel Data.

The first of the Wooldridge textbooks is very approachable and is essentially a text that is helpful to see how estimations are carried out and also for interpretation of results. The second book is more of a reference book and as you go through your career it is more likely to become more important.

As researchers we are writers, thus becoming good writers is vital for success as a researcher. Here are several suggestions of books and a video that will be helpful:

William Thomson, 2001, A Guide for the Young Economist, The MIT Press.

Deidre McCloskey - Economical Writing

1. Zinsser - On Writing Well.

Miller, Jane E., 2013, The Chicago Guide to Writing about Multivariate Analysis, Second Edition, The University of Chicago Press.

In addition – Here is a video that can be obtained from You Tube that is helpful: **The Craft of Writing Effectively - Prof Larry McEnerney, U of Chicago**

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

***Presentations 20% of your grade:***

I expect you to come to class prepared to participate in class discussions having carefully read and thought about the assigned papers. Each student will be making several presentations during the semester. A typical presentation will run for about 30 minutes, depending on the level of discussion. These presentations will be like an extended conference presentation. The presentation will be power point slides or some equivalent format. While I do not want you to spend an inordinate amount of time in preparing the slides, the ability to organize a set of ideas in a presentation (conference or otherwise) will be crucial to your success as an academic. Thus, these presentations will serve as an opportunity to develop your presentation skills and to begin discovering your natural presentation style. Ultimately, it is up to you to decide how important it is to you in being an outstanding presenter. These summaries should be approximately 150 words and should be uploaded on LMS by 6:00, on the Monday before Tuesday’s class.

Here are some questions to consider/focus on in preparing your presentation and summaries (and non-presenters in preparing for class discussions):

- What is the paper about? That is, what is the big picture?
- What is the research question and why is it important?
- What is the theoretical framework, if any, that is used to motivate the research question and hypotheses?
- What are the main concerns, limitations, and weaknesses? How does the paper’s empirical methods attempt to mitigate these?
- How well is the research design tied to the research question? How accurately do the empirical proxies capture the underlying economic constructs of interest? To what extent is the research design capable of distinguishing between alternative hypotheses?
- What are the main results? How do the authors interpret them? How do you interpret them?
- What do we learn from the paper that we did not already know?
- What could be improved or extended?

***Paper Summaries- 15% of grade***

Students are also required to provide summaries of the papers that will be presented/discussed in class. These summaries should be approximately 150 words and should be uploaded on LMS by 6:00, on the Monday before Tuesday’s class.

***Term Project: 65% of your final grade***

The major proportion of your grade will be based on a class project. This project is a draft of a research paper that you will have written to the best of your ability. The objective is for you to have a paper which you can work on and based on feedback from me, your colleagues, and other professors, turn it into a paper that is ready for submission to one of the better finance conferences. The accomplishment of this task requires significant time commitment. However, this is well worth it as you will be well on your way to having a completed paper. Below is a detailed outline of deliverables which will help you in completing the task at hand. Failure to satisfy these deliverables will negatively impact your grade. The steps outlined are the typical steps that are required to write a paper. Specifically, students will be required to identify a research question, specify hypotheses which will be tested, obtain the data that will be used to test the hypotheses and present the empirical results clearly in tables. An important aspect of papers in the corporate finance area is that of establishing a causal relationship within the issue being examined. Thus, students are required to clearly articulate an identification strategy. Presentation of the draft of the paper will be presented at the end of the semester.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Class Attendance and Conduct:** We are all adults and are fully aware of the importance of class attendance and being fully invested in the class. It is expected that you will attend all the classes unless for some unforeseen circumstance you are not able to attend. Please let me know in advance if you will not be able to attend class on a particular day. It is also expected that you will come to class prepared and be a full participant in the discussions. The more you invest in the class the more you will get out of it.

**Generative AI:**

Chat-GPT and other generative AI engines have become more important as each day passes by. And can play an important role in the class project. However, this role should be primarily at the early stages. Having AI play an important role in the project is not acceptable. The project should be based on your work and not that of some AI engine. A significant amount of reliance on AI and claiming that it is your work is unethical and will importantly stifle your ability/development to do original research. Be circumspect in your use of any such assistance, limiting yourself to a minimum amount of using it to improve your writing, programming etc. Significantly relying on AI and claiming that it is your work is dishonest and could lead to academic honesty violations.
