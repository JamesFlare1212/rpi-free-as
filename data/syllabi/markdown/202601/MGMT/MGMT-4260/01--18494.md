## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Description:**

This course is designed to strengthen students’ ability to correctly analyze, interpret, and evaluate financial statements and their accompanying disclosures. The course is aimed at anyone whose career might involve working with accounting data. Discussed throughout the semester will be an in-depth review of financial statements and financial ratios, including industry average comparisons, trend analysis, and investment analysis, as well as the use of financial information sources. The course assumes that students have a basic knowledge of accounting. The focus is on integrating key concepts and applying them to financial decision-making, including interpreting and analyzing real-life company annual reports and 10-K forms.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Course Objectives:**

- Devel Build competency in interpreting and evaluating financial statements and related disclosures for decision-making.
- Develop proficiency in applying financial statement analysis tools (common-size, trend, ratio, and benchmarking) to assess liquidity, solvency, profitability, and investor-focused performance.
- Strengthen skills in using Excel to organize financial data and perform accurate, efficient analysis.
- Apply concepts to real-company annual reports and SEC filings (10-K) and communicate findings clearly.

**Learning Outcomes:**

- Interpret the balance sheet, income statement, and statement of cash flows, including key disclosures, to explain a firm’s financial position and performance.
- Perform common-size, trend, ratio, and peer/industry benchmarking analyses to evaluate liquidity, solvency, profitability, and investor-relevant measures.
- Extract and structure data from annual reports and 10-Ks, analyze it in Excel, and produce clearly presented results.

Synthesize quantitative analysis and disclosure insights into concise written and/or oral conclusions supported by evidence.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Assignments:**

The most effective way to learn accounting is through consistent practice. The assignments in this course are designed to engage you in hands-on learning. You are expected to complete all chapters and related assignments according to the Course Schedule. **All assignments are due by 11:59 PM ET on their respective due dates.** Late submissions will not be accepted. If accepted under extenuating circumstances, late work will receive a significant grade penalty.

**Homework:** Homework assignments are completed online and are not timed. You are allowed up to three attempts, with the highest score counting toward your final grade. Each attempt will offer a new set of problems to provide essential practice in mastering key accounting concepts. To maximize your success in this course, I recommend the following approach for each homework submission:

- Review the feedback provided after each submission to identify any mistakes.
- For problems answered incorrectly, revisit the corresponding sections of the chapter to strengthen your understanding.
- Once you have clarified the concept or technique, reattempt the same problem and ensure that you can arrive at the correct answer on your own.
- If the concept remains unclear, seek assistance from your instructor without delay.

**Cases:** All cases must be completed using **Excel** and submitted as an Excel file through the LMS. No other file formats will be accepted. After submitting your file, ensure that it is readable by downloading and reviewing it. If there are any issues, make the necessary changes and resubmit the file. These cases are designed to apply key course concepts to real-world business scenarios while enhancing your proficiency with Excel. A substantial portion of your Case grade will be based on the accurate application of Excel commands, formulas, and functions. Students being able to troubleshoot and fix their mistakes on their own is one of the major goals of these cases.

**Attendance Quizzes:** Each chapter may feature one or more attendance quizzes, aimed at assessing your comprehension of the material and recording class attendance. These quizzes contribute significantly to your overall grade, making it essential to perform well. Attendance quizzes can only be submitted by students physically present in class. Submitting an attendance quiz without attending will receive -20 points for that assignment, and it will be considered academic dishonesty. In the case of a missed attendance quiz, a documented excuse (e.g., a doctor’s note or institutional documentation) is required to arrange a make-up.

**Exams:** The Exams are timed assessments administered in class. Accounting concepts build on top of each other. As such all exams will be cumulative because you need the foundational concepts from previous chapters to be able to answer questions from later chapters. Any form of collaboration or cheating during the exams is strictly prohibited and will be considered academic dishonesty. Sharing or discussing exam content or answers, whether directly or indirectly, with students who have not yet taken the exam also constitutes academic dishonesty. In the event of a missed exam, a documented excuse (e.g., a doctor’s note or institutional documentation) is required for the date of the exam to arrange a make-up. Please note that **make-up** exams will differ from the original and will be significantly more challenging. Do not leave for vacation without taking the Exam; make-up exams will not be allowed to accommodate early travel plans.

**Final Project and Presentations:** The final is a team project consisting of a written report and a class presentation on a company assigned to your team. The project will utilize all the concepts learned in this course. This is an Excel-based group project and presentation; all team members must contribute significantly in order to score well. Individual grades will depend on the Team Evaluations Rubric.
