### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

Information technologies (IT) are fast changing the business of business. Managers in today's corporate environment cannot be effective without knowing the role of IT and its impacts in terms of transformations on the organizational, process, market, and global enterprise levels. Simultaneously, firms are harnessing the power of big data, artificial intelligence, and breakthrough technologies such as ChatGPT to make important business decisions.  
At a conceptual level, this course is designed to make the students knowledgeable of the fundamentals underlying the technological and strategic use of modern information systems. While some of the effort will be devoted to hands-on work with some business software, the major emphasis will be on the understanding the managerial and strategic aspects of information technology. We will discuss how IT and big data impact business strategy and enable new business models.  
I hope that at the end of this course, the students will know how IT and AI affect business strategy and transforms organizations and industries.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. *Terminology*: The student will be able to explain the meaning of terms used to describe common techniques and concepts in business information systems.
2. *Advanced Information Systems Concepts*: The student will be able to describe the ways in which computers are and will be used in business and management. These concepts include telecommunications, electronic commerce, business analytics, and future directions of information systems.
3. *Business Strategy and Systems Development*: The students will be involved in a small-scale IT project and experience how it is to work in a team setting. They will not need to do programming in this course, but will evaluate a business lifecycle for their product.
4. *Behavioral and Organizational Issues*: The student will be able to identify and suggest appropriate responses to managerial and organizational issues stemming from development, implementation, and use of computer-based information systems.
5. *Social and Ethical Issues*: The student will understand the major social and ethical issues involved in the development and use of information technology.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Details of grading criteria**

1. **Team Term Project and Presentation (25% of your grade):**

   This course places a lot of emphasis on experiential learning through a team term project. I shall invite industry partners for feedback on the term project; I shall provide more details of this project on LMS and in-class.

   You would need to develop a PowerPoint or Alternative Software (e.g., Prezi, PDF) Presentation, which would be the primary deliverable for the project. Each team will prepare a ~12-15 minutes presentation (I shall provide more specific guidelines later depending on the final class size and number of groups).

   At least 24 hours before your team’s presentation, post your presentation file on LMS in the folder that instructor will create for group projects. Your PPT/alternative format presentation file must contain slide(s) listing all the references/ resources/back up materials that you used for your research towards the end. Try to make slides self-explanatory and focus on key issues in class. Make sure that slides or handouts are readable if printed.

   Please email me your presentation before class on the day of your presentation.

   The project will be graded using the following criteria:
   - Soundness of idea: **novelty of ideas and realism of execution** (implementation plan, and some back of the envelope calculations for financials)
   - Managerial relevance: how well you have addressed the business and strategic issues including use of course concepts and what you are learning in other courses
   - Quality of the presentation: originality and creativity of your work, readability and self-explanatory nature of slides, appropriate mix of content and white space, appropriate use of multimedia/ video, use of tables/charts/graphs, quality of written/oral communication, completeness and diversity of references etc.
   - Ability to do critical analysis and prioritize/ present the most important issues within the space/time limit.
   - Following is the project grading rubric:
     - Project idea and app design-10%
     - Strategic analysis (Use only one framework. For instance, if you have Porter's Five Forces, do not use SWOT) - 15%
     - Identifying target market - 10%
     - Competitive analysis - 15%
     - Financial analysis (expected revenue and cost structure, taking into account demand, competition, revenue model, etc.) - 25%
     - Marketing plan - 25%

   If anything is not clear on this project assignment, please bring your questions to class so that all of us are on the same page with respect to what we discuss and decide.
   2. **Share and Tell (5% of your grade):**

   A Google Document will be made available for signing up for a Share and Tell session with schedule relying on a first come-first serve basis. If you do miss your Share and Tell session without a valid excuse, it may be difficult to reschedule and you may face a penalty. Please email me if you have any questions.

   Make a brief presentation (max 3 minutes, Google Slides/PowerPoint are good options) on a topic that relates to one of the course themes. This could be (1) a recent case study based on your research at CIO.com, CIOInsight.com, Computerworld.com, InformationWeek.com etc., (2) some item currently in news (from New York Times, Wall Street Journal, The Economist etc.), (3) an item on which you have relevant IT or managerial experience, (4) any idea relevant to this course from a book or article that you read recently, (5) some new App or YouTube video that relates to course themes broadly interpreted. You need to upload the link and/or reference article on Compass under the specific time slot. Please email me any supporting material in class before your presentation.

   You must articulate how your topic relates to one of the course themes.
   3. **Exams and pop-up quizzes (45% of your grade):**

   There is one mid-term exam that will provide an opportunity for you to demonstrate your mastery of theoretical concepts and vocabulary presented in lectures and readings. Active class participation will help improve your chances of doing better in the exams and quizzes. The mid-term exam will cover material from the beginning of course through the last lecture prior to the exam date (may change under the discretion of the instructor and you will be informed prior to the exam). The exam will be administered on LMS. There is **no** end-term exam.

   There will also be in-class (online) weekly pop-up quizzes. These will focus on the topic from the previous week. Given the class schedule, most quizzes will be on Tuesday and will be administered on LMS. I also drop your worst quiz score (e.g., if there are 10 quizzes, I will use the best 9 out of 10 quizzes in your score calculation). ***If you miss one, there won’t be any “make up” pop up quizzes*** unless you can convince the Student Experience office and me of the ***extreme circumstances***.
   4. **Assignments/Case Submissions (15% of your grade):**

   *Written Analyses*: Where specified, you will submit written analyses for designated cases/readings throughout the term. The analyses will be responses to the specific questions provided by the instructor and will be posted on LMS, with due dates specified. Please note that I \*will not\* send you any reminders about when an assignment is due.

   The submissions should not be more than one and a half (single spaced) pages long and *should* address the questions posed. In case you are late in submitting the assignment, a typical penalty would be docking of some points off the assignment. For instance, if you submit a 10 point assignment a day late, I would dock 2 points and grade it out of 8 points; if you submit it two days late, I would dock 4 points and grade it out of 6 points, and so on. Submissions late by 5 days or more would receive 0 points. Please email me if you have any questions.
   5. **Class Participation (10% of your grade):**

   I expect everyone to come to class after reading all the assigned articles and cases and contribute to the discussions by offering your unique perspectives and reacting to that of others in a thoughtful manner. I might cold call – so please make sure that you have something to say if I ask you to summarize an article or case in your words or to respond to someone else’s summary, question, or comment. This is a communications intensive class; therefore, class activities are built in for intense team discussions and in-class presentations.  
   I also encourage class participation via LMS. If you cannot make it to a class that involves an article reading, you should post your viewpoint about the article in the discussion board of the class before that class to partially make up for your absence.
