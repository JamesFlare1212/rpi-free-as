## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- This is an introductory course in business management. Consequently, the idea is to provide a survey of various aspects of business and management. Specifically, the objective for the course is to help students develop a basic understanding of the following. 1. The US business system. 2. How businesses are managed and organized. 3. The importance of small businesses and some aspects of running a small business. 4. The importance of employee motivation and some aspects of managing people in a business. 5. Some of the basic principles of marketing. 6. Accounting and other information systems in business. 7. Some financial aspects of business. In addition to the above knowledge objectives, the course will also provide an opportunity to develop one’s analytical, writing, group work, and presentation skills.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Policies**

***Attendance and makeups***

Attendance (and participation in class discussions) is expected from all students. If you miss class, you are expected to obtain class notes and copies of handouts from a classmate. In the interest of being fair to every student, assignments will not be accepted late. Also, make-up tests will not be given if you miss a scheduled test (except under extraordinary circumstances).

***Group work and group grades***

There are many group projects in the course, and I want to keep the same group membership for all the projects. However, despite your best efforts at vetting group members, and communicating actively throughout the duration of the projects, you may find that a member does not discharge his/her responsibilities. If this were to happen, I need to hear about this as soon as possible. If you cannot resolve the issue within your group, I will convene a meeting of all of you in the group to seek a solution. Remedies though are limited. You may have a chance to swap the group member that is not performing his/her tasks for one from another group. Note that this remedy will not be easy, as you and I will need to find another group and that group’s member willing to make the trade. So, to the extent possible work hard to make your initial selection well, and make sure you do all you can to communicate and keep your group members performing at their best.

***Academic integrity***

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own performance. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty, and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty. If found in violation of the academic honesty policy, students may be subject to two types of penalty. The instructor administers an academic [grade] penalty, and the student is reported to the Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education as appropriate. The first violation results in 0 grade for that assignment. The second violation results in failure of the course. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

***Collaborative work and plagiarism***

All assignments and tests, except ones explicitly identified in the syllabus as group assignments, should be your own work. You cannot collaborate with anyone else in completing these assignments and tests.

For papers or presentation projects if you must use arguments or conclusions from experts or sources using their own words, you can do so in a limited fashion (not more than a couple of sentences) but only after clearly identifying these as direct quotes (by enclosing the direct quotes within double quotation marks) and providing a reference to where the quotes were taken from (e.g., Author, year, publication title, page #). If your work is found to be plagiarized, you will receive a grade of zero for the assignment in the first instance. If there is a subsequent infraction, you will receive an F for the course.

For group projects, you should collaborate only with members of your group and not with students in other groups.

***Electronic device use***

Please switch off electronic devices (cell phones, laptops, iPads, etc.) during class. You can use these only when I ask you to do so.
