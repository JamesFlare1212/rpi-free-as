## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

## COURSE DETAILS AND RELATION TO THE JAMES FUND:

The James Fund at RPI is a student-managed fund run through the Rensselaer Finance Club (RFC) at the Lally School of Management (“Lally”) in which students are provided the opportunity to gain experience in asset management and investing. The Fund was made possible by an initial donation from Lally alumnus Dr. Frank James of James Investment Research, Inc. with the goal for students to attain functional knowledge of investment strategies and portfolio management by managing and maintaining a fund that consistently outperforms a diversified stock/bond portfolio.

As of January 1, 2026 the fund had about $250K in capital. The fund has its own Investment Policy Statement (IPS) and publishes regular Portfolio Allocation Updates (PAU) and is structured similarly to a real investment fund (e.g., an asset manager or hedge fund).

The Fund is managed by an investing committee (“Committee”) consisting of student fund managers. The Committee is comprised of i) Chief Investment Officer(s) and ii) Portfolio Manager(s). The Committee is supported by other student fund managers as detailed later in the IPS. The Fund’s trades will be executed by the Chief Investment Officer(s), who will also have final discretion over any trades the Fund decides to enact, subject to approval by the RPI CFO or their designee. The Fund is supervised by the Club’s Faculty Advisor. Professor, and Lally School Associate Dean, Brian Clark, serves in an advisory role to the student fund managers. The Fund is also informally advised by an industry advisor who serves as the chair of the Fund’s Alumni Advisory Board. The Fund reports periodically to RPI’s Financial Analyst (Michael Sharp), RPI’s Division of Finance, and an Alumni Advisory Board consisting of RPI alumni with relevant experience in the financial services and securities industries.

While the course will be run in conjunction with the James Fund and several students will likely be active members of the James Fund, being an active member of the James Fund is not required; nor are James Fund members required to take this course. However, students in the James Fund can use work they are doing for the James Fund for credit in this course, provided that the outcomes and work align appropriately. For example, all students will be writing and presenting investment pitches in largely the same format that the students in the James Fund carry out their pitches. In this case, given the almost 1:1 overlap in the deliverables, it is acceptable for James Fund students to use their pitches as part of this course.

Moreover, all students will be part of an investment team. For example, the James Fund students will manage their fund as outlined in the fund’s IPC and charter. Other teams will be broken up into teams with an identical initial asset allocation as the James Fund but create their own IPS and investment strategies throughout the semester. The teams will be broken up into functional areas similar to a real hedge fund (e.g., leadership, risk, research, analysts, etc.).

## CLASS STRUCTURE:

The course meets on Mondays from 6:00 – 8:50 pm in Pittsburgh 5114. Typical classes will start with an initial meeting with the whole class and then teams (and sub-groups) will break out and work together. Presentations on deliverables will occur regularly and the audience will be the relevant class participants. For example, when investment pitches are given, all investment teams and CIOs will be in the audience. The instructor’s role in the meetings will be to represent a Board of Directors or Investment Advisory Council – essentially providing high-level governance and strategic guidance. Students can expect that the instructor will provide real-time feedback that is challenging and meant to be constructive criticism.

Student attendance and engagement is mandatory. Students are expected to behave professionally at all times as would be required in a real fund – after all, real capital is at risk with the James Fund.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

***Required****:*

1. Access to statistical software such as Excel, Python, and/or R.
2. Active Bloomberg Terminal account (free, to be setup during the first week of classes).
3. Active FactSet membership (free, to be setup during the first week of classes).
4. Students not in the James Fund will set up Interactive Brokers paper trading account or Stocktrak account access (TBD) under the instructions provided by no later than the second class. This will be verified by the instructor by the end of class. This account access will allow you to view the portfolio, track investments, follow potential ideas, identify trends, and participate in the course with your peers. This is a full team effort.

***Recommended****:*

1. Pitch the Perfect Investment: The Essential Guide to Winning on Wall Street by Paul Sonkin and Paul Johnson (Wiley Finance)
2. Exchange-Traded Funds and the New Dynamics of Investing by Ananth N. Madhavan (Oxford University Press). Since the fund can only invest in ETFs, this is essential reading for all student fund managers.
3. Quantitative Equity Portfolio Management: An Active Approach to Portfolio Construction and Management by Ludwig B. Chincarini, CFA and Daehwan Kim (McGraw-Hill)
4. Quantitative Technical Analysis: An Integrated Approach to Trading System Development and Trading Management by Howard B. Bandy (Blue Owl Press)
5. Quantitative Trading with R: Understanding Mathematical and Computational Tools from a Quant’s Perspective by Harry Georgakopolous (Palgrave Macmillan)
6. Python for Finance: Analyze Big Financial Data by Yves Hilpisch (O’Reilly)
7. Algorithmic Trading & DMA: An Introduction to Direct Access Trading Strategies, by Barry Johnson (4Myeloma Press)
8. Online Data and Information Resources
9. CFA Readings: This course will rely on supplemental material, in the form of the Chartered Financial Analyst Program Readings, for a number of themes and topics. This information will offer additional perspective for those in need of clarity or those who hold a desire to pursue mastery of the topics.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Investment Policy Statement (ALL) Construct, interpret, and evaluate an institutional-quality Investment Policy Statement (IPS) by defining investment objectives, constraints, governance, and benchmarks, and apply the IPS consistently to portfolio decisions throughout the semester while adapting investment strategies to evolving market conditions.
- 2\. Investment Research (ALL) Analyze and synthesize professional-grade investment research by evaluating business fundamentals, valuation, and risk factors, and interpret research outputs to support portfolio decision-making.
- 3\. Investment Research Leadership (MGMT 6420) Lead and oversee advanced analytical research efforts by designing, critiquing, and refining complex technical analyses, and mentoring team members in best-practice research methodologies.
- 4\. Risk Measurement & Portfolio Analytics (ALL) Apply, analyze, and interpret quantitative risk measures and portfolio analytics to assess total portfolio risk, factor exposures, and downside scenarios, and evaluate their implications for asset allocation and security selection.
- 5\. Risk Leadership (MGMT 6420) Direct and evaluate advanced risk analyses by integrating multi-factor models, stress testing, and scenario analysis into portfolio-level recommendations.
- 6\. Investment Pitches (ALL) Develop, defend, and deliver professional investment pitches by evaluating expected return, risk, and portfolio fit, and justifying recommendations before an investment committee using clear, evidence-based arguments.
- 7\. Fiduciary Duty & Compliance (ALL) Evaluate and apply fiduciary duties and compliance requirements by assessing investment decisions for consistency with governance standards, regulatory constraints, and institutional oversight.
- 8\. Ethics (ALL) Apply and evaluate ethical standards consistent with the CFA Institute Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct by analyzing ethical dilemmas, resolving conflicts of interest, and demonstrating ethical decision-making in both coursework and fund operations.
- 9\. Leadership Development (MGMT 6420 only) Demonstrate and assess leadership competencies required for professional success in finance by leading investment teams, coordinating research and presentations, communicating with stakeholders, and mentoring undergraduate students in technical analysis and professional conduct.
