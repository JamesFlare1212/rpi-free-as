## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The primary objective of this course is to provide the financial engineering or business analytics students with applied quantitative skills in the areas of artificial intelligence and machine learning for finance applications. The course material will involve hands-on use of real data and focus on various AI/ML algorithms common in the finance industry. The course will cover supervised learning and unsupervised algorithms and applications including natural language processing. R will be the primary computing language used in this course. However, students will be permitted to use Python for course projects.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1\. Ability to understand and implement core AI/ML algorithms widely used in the finance industry including clustering methods, tree-based models, support vector machines, ensemble techniques (e.g., boosting, random forests, bagging), deep learning, and natural language processing.  
2\. Ability to apply these AI/ML techniques to real-world financial datasets, such as trading assets, credit data, and unstructured data.  
3\. Understanding of challenges in AI/ML applications, including model interpretability, overfitting, and model risk.  
4\. Ability to clearly communicate AI/ML concepts and results to diverse audiences.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

1. Course Projects (50%)

There will be two major course projects assigned throughout the semester. The projects will be done in groups. The size of the groups will depend on enrollment. Written project reports will be due approximately during typical mid-term and final exam times. Each group will also present at least one of their projects. All group members may not receive the exact same grade. Individual grades will be related to each student’s contribution to the project and their presentation performance. The peer evaluation may be used. Course Projects includes:

1. Midterm Project (20%)
2. Final Project (30%)

2. Assignments (40%)

Assignments will be given during class and posted online through LMS every 2-3 weeks (4-6 total). They may include readings, research, and quantitative exercises. Unless explicitly stated otherwise by the instructor, all homework must be completed individually. Students are strongly encouraged to discuss the material with classmates and the instructor; however, all implementation, analysis, and written work must be completed independently. You will have one week to complete each assignment. The lowest one grade will be dropped. **Late submissions within one week will receive a 50% deduction from the earned grade.** No late submission more than one week will be accepted.

3. In-Class Quiz (10%)

There will be short, in-person only quizzes in the first class meeting of every week. Quizzes are graded on participation and completion, not correctness. You will not lose points for a wrong answer. Two quizzes will be dropped from your grade to accommodate situations beyond control where you cannot attend.

### Other Course Policies:

**AI TOOLS USE POLICY**

The use of AI-based tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Gemini, DeepSeek) is permitted only as a learning aid in this course and must be used responsibly. Students may use such tools for tasks such as brainstorming, debugging, or exploring alternative approaches. However, all submitted work must reflect the student’s own understanding and original effort.
