## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course examines how to plan, execute, and evaluate integrated marketing communications (IMC) across channels. Topics include IMC fundamentals; consumer behavior and persuasion; source, message, and channel effects; creative strategy; objectives and budgeting; media planning and evaluation; digital media; sales promotion; and measurement of communication effectiveness, among others. Students build foundational knowledge and analytical thinking through lectures, develop practical ability through applied assignments, and strengthen comprehensive capability in researching, reasoning, and communicating through presentations.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Belch, George E., & Michael A. Belch. Advertising and Promotion: An Integrated Marketing Communications Perspective (12th ed. or newer).

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Master core IMC knowledge.
- Connect insights from external readings to core IMC knowledge to strengthen your understanding of the subject.
- Find, evaluate, and synthesize information through research.
- Communicate clearly, logically, and insightfully in presentations.
- Analyze real advertising and promotional examples.
- Create simple advertising/campaign outputs.
- Practice critical thinking by asking high-quality questions and reasoning from first principles.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Exams**

- MGMT 4450: Two closed-book exams assessing your understanding of key course topics.
- MGMT 6540: Two closed-book exams assessing your understanding of key course topics and strategic IMC thinking skills.

**Assignments**

Four completion-based thinking/analyzing/practice assignments that extend course concepts and strengthen your critical thinking, analytical reasoning, and practical application skills.

**Reading Presentations & Discussion Leads**

Each student will present twice during the semester in a group of 2–3 students based on assigned readings. These presentations develop and assess your ability to research, synthesize key ideas, and communicate clearly and effectively.

**IMC Audit & Presentation (MGMT 6540 only)**

A comprehensive project that evaluates your research, communication, and strategic IMC thinking.

**Class Contributions**

You will practice critical thinking through class interaction.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
