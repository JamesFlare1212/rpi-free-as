## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course focuses on designing and evaluating financial portfolios that help organizations manage risk-return trade-offs. The course covers portfolio management, risk management, asset pricing and hedging, and provides the necessary quantitative background to students in the growing field of asset management. The course is primarily concerned with the “buy-side” firms consisting of asset managers, hedge funds, and other firms that buy or sell securities on behalf of their clients. To be successful, students need to develop research and analytical skills, the ability to identify investment opportunities, an understanding of global market trends, the ability to create timely and high-quality reports for investment decisions, financial modeling and Excel skills, to name a few.

Asset management is a broad subject, and the students are expected to have a basic finance background as indicated in the above course-prerequisites. In order to provide useful treatment of the relevant topics, it is necessary to stress fundamentals and to explore topics at a technical level. The course comprises four major sections: the first section covers introduction to investments, cash flows, discount rates, bonds and equities, and concepts of risk aversion. In the second, topics such as modern portfolio theory, extensions of mean-variance portfolios, leverage and liquidity cost-controlled selections, risk factors and fund alpha creation are discussed. The third covers large asset sets and sparse portfolio selection, estimation errors, index tracking and beta-controlled portfolios and their properties. In the final section, advanced asset management strategies are discussed, covering asset screening using fundamental financial data, firm efficiency analysis and asset management under changing macroeconomic conditions.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Expose students to the knowledge and skills required to select and employ basic tools for financial analysis of varying assets for investment purposes.
- Foster an understanding of different types of assets, including stocks and bonds, as well as options and derivatives, and their risk characteristics to aid analysis in investment asset selection.
- Develop insights on risk management and portfolio diversification, leverage control, and develop an understanding of risk control measures to be able to evaluate and support varying objectives of asset management to suit applications.
- Understand and practice basic concepts of reporting that require portfolio stress testing, risk adjustment, and out-of-sample analysis to organize documents and presentations of quantitative fund management.

## Course Calendar

### Calendar Details:

Jan 13-16: Introduction to assets, markets, investments

Financial securities, markets, traders, stocks and bonds, options and derivatives, valuation and pricing of cash flows, cost of capital

Jan 20-23: Review of financial modeling

Linear and nonlinear optimization, fixed income models, bond prices and sensitivity to interest rates, term structure, bond portfolios, spreadsheet modeling

Jan 27-30: Risk-averse portfolio optimization

Risk aversion and management, quantification of risk, portfolio statistics, equity portfolio diversification, coherent and convex risk measures, stochastic dominance

Feb 3-6: MV portfolio theory and implications

Capital Asset Pricing Model, asset and portfolio betas, beta-controlled portfolios, risk factors and factor regression, fund alpha

Feb 10-13: Extending MV portfolios to Index-tracking portfolios

Active vs. passive funds, low vs. high-dimensional asset sets, estimation errors, risk mitigation

Feb 17: (NO CLASS)

Feb 20: Homework 1 DUE by midnight, by electronic submission at LMS

Feb 20-24: Sparse portfolio selection

Reasons for sparsity, cardinality constraints, lasso and ridge-type controls in high dimensional portfolio selection

Feb 27: Quiz 1 and review

Mar 02-06: (NO CLASS) Spring break

Mar 10: Exam (in-class)

Mar 13: Introduction to course projects, teams, and discussion

Mar 17-20: Leverage-controlled high-dimensional portfolios

Data-driven portfolio selection and validation, in-sample training and out-of-sample testing

Mar 24-27: Market impact and liquidity modeling

Transaction cost optimized portfolio selection, market microstructure and modeling market impact, solving impact-controlled MV models

Mar 31: Quiz 2

Relative firm efficiency, DEA and SFA analysis, tests for firm strength-return correlation analysis, asset-screening

Apr 14-17: Economic regimes and estimation using HMM

Hidden Markov Models, economic indicators, sector rotation to counter economic regime changes

Apr 21: Homework 2 DUE by midnight, by electronic submission at LMS Website

Portfolio strategies optimized to economic regimes, automated selection

Apr 28: Final Project presentations (in-class)
