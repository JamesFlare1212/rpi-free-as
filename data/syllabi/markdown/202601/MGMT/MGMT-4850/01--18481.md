## Course Goals

### Goals:

The increased professionalization of the workforce and high value placed on technology and knowledge work in the global economy emphasize the importance of good workforce management skills for managers and professionals. To be effective, we need to act as "applied" behavioral scientists, that is, we must carefully study our organizations, units, and direct reports and develop an ability to recognize what makes each one tick. In this course more than most others, we need to know not only the conceptual material presented but also how it applies to our work lives and ourselves. People who understand the work environment, know themselves, and can assess their effects on others are more likely to be effective than those who are blind to these dimensions.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

This undergraduate course provides an overview of basic processes in human behavior that influence the effectiveness of individuals, groups, and organizations. Its focus is on understanding what happens during interpersonal interactions in work situations, and what can be done to make employees more effective. Topics covered include organizational socialization, motivation, decision-making, team dynamics, virtual teams, influence, and conflict management. Numerous exercises and case analyses are used in class to help provide students with insights into these processes.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Exam 1 (10%) / 2 (15%) / 3 (25%)**

Exam 1 will consist of 20 multiple choice questions spanning all material covered up to and on January 30. Exam 2 will consist of 30 multiple choice questions spanning all material covered *after* Exam 1. Exam 3 will consist of 50 multiple choice questions spanning all material covered *after* Exam 2. Exams are meant to assess your ability to identify and understand the class content. Information in both lectures and assigned reading will be included in exams. Lecture slides will be posted on Blackboard to aid you in studying.

**Quizzes (10%)**

I will distribute brief quizzes, at random, to gauge learning within a specific subject area we cover. I will drop the lowest quiz grade.

**Group Activities (20%)**

Every class with a lecture on and between January 12 and April 14 will contain activities that requires you to apply course concepts to solve a problem, analyze a situation, and/or engage in discussion/debate. These activities are meant to apply concepts taught during the lecture portion of the class to streamline the learning process and stimulate engagement. Activity performance will be assessed at the group level based on either completion vs. noncompletion, or through a set of measures which I provide access to in the syllabus. I will drop the lowest group activity grade. If you do not attend class, you will not receive credit for your group’s activity performance. Group activities are often experiential and thus cannot be made up. Groups will be randomly assigned each class.

**Group Presentation (20%)**

Your presentation group will be given a unique organizational issue that must be critically analyzed using class concepts. Groups will each have 10 minutes to give their presentations (see grading measures in the syllabus).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute is committed to the principles of intellectual honesty and integrity. All members of the RPI community are expected to maintain complete honesty in all academic work, presenting only that which is their own work in tests and assignments.

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and the Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a grade of zero on the first assignment where a violation is detected, and the infraction will be reported to the Associate Dean for Academic Affairs. The Lally School has a Three Strikes Policy. A second instance of cheating by the same student in any course will result in the student receiving an F for that course. A third instance of cheating will result in expulsion from the program.

Plagiarism is always a violation. For any assignment, I will tell you in advance what the expectations are for individual versus group work.

If you have any questions regarding what is acceptable related to any aspect of course work, please contact me before the situation becomes problematic.

In this course, the academic penalty for a first offense is a zero for the quiz, assignment, or test in question. A second offense will result in failure of the course as noted in Lally’s Three Strikes Policy.
