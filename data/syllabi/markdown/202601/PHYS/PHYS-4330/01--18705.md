## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Particle and rigid body dynamics using Newtonian, Lagrangian, and

Hamiltonian methods. Motion of particle systems. Central force motion. Rotating coordinate

systems. Rigid body motion using the inertia tensor and Euler angles. Coupled systems and nor-

mal coordinates. Introduction to continuum mechanics and the mechanics of deformable media.

Introduction to Hamiltonian Mechanics, including proof and applications of Liouville’s Theorem.

Formalism of Special Relativity.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

By the end of the course, and upon satisfactory completion of all HW assignments and exams,

the students will have an elementary understanding of Classical Mechanics and its applications.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Analyze single and multiple particle mechanics in the Newtonian framework.
- To be able to analyze systems using inertial as well as non-inertial reference frames.
- Understand the Principle of Least Action and how it leads to the Lagrangian formalism.
- To be able to use Noether’s theorem and general symmetry considerations to gain invaluable insight into physical systems.
- To be able to analyze the physics of rotating bodies.
- Understand the impact of of the Hamiltonian formulation of mechanics to all of physics, especially Quantum Mechanics.
- Distinguish and compare the methods of Newton, Lagrange, and Hamilton.
- Develop Einstein’s theory of Special Relativity and it’s impact on physics.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Reading Assignments:

There will be a reading assignment in advance of every class period. In order to receive credit

for the reading assignment, the student has to submit a question on the topic to the instructor

through LMS no later than 6:00 pm the day before the scheduled class time. The question has to

be substantial, e.g. ”Can you explain equation XYZ?” will not receive credit. The lowest reading

assignment score will be dropped to compute your final grade in the course. Therefore, if you can

miss one reading assignment without a penalty. The reading assignment will account for 20% of

the final grade.

Homework:

The homework assignments and respective due dates and times will be posted on LMS. In general,

late homework will not be accepted. The homework will account for 10% of the final grade.

Exams:

There will be three exams during the semester to test your progress and mastering of the material.

The exams will be given during class time. Please see the class calendar for dates. There is no final

exam. In calculating the exam average the two exams with the highest scores will each count as

two exams, while the exam with the lowest score will count as a single exam. So the exam average

will be calculated from a total of 5 scores. The exam average accounts for 70% of your final

grade.

If a student misses an exam due to extenuating circumstances communicated to the instructor prior

to the start of an exam, a make up oral exam will be given.
