## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is designed to be an introduction to optics and the manipulation of light on length scales shorter than the radiation wavelength. The course will explore concepts of light localization and light-matter interactions on the nanoscale, non-linear optics, Fourier optics, the generation of coherent light by lasers. Students will be introduced to the field, its theoretical framework, and the experimental methods that are used within it. Additionally, students will learn to critically read current scientific literature, write about their research, and give a science presentation.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Determine how tightly light can be focused, both in free space and close to a material.
- Understand how elementary dipoles radiate in a nanoscale environment.
- Demonstrate understanding of modern microscopy techniques that allow imaging beyond the diffraction limit.
- Explain the basic components of the laser and the conditions required for lasing.
- Examine plasmonic effects at metal-dielectric interfaces.
- Use the concepts of nonlinear susceptibility to predict the frequencies generated by nonlinear optical processes and understand the theory of second harmonic generation.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own.
