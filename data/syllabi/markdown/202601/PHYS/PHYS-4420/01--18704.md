### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

**Course Description:**

**TOPICS:**

1. **Classical Thermodynamcs**: conditions for equilibrium and phase equilibria, Laws of Thermodynamics.
2. **Foundations of Statistical Mechanics –** Classical and quantum statistics (Boltzmann, Fermi-Dirac, Bose-Einstein).

**Course Objectives:** The purpose of this course is to introduce the foundations of thermodynamics and statistical mechanics and to lead students to attain a working knowledge and to get familiar with the use of these techniques to describe diverse physical systems. Examples discussed in class will include applications to gases, solids and liquids. The class emphasizes developing problem-solving skills at the advanced undergraduate level.

**Honor code:** The course does not measure your ability to search the web. Any instance of copying problems that you have not solved on your own or in groups will constitute dishonesty. Dishonesty is punishable with an F in the course. Working in groups and consultation with other students is allowed, but each one should hand in their own work. I may surprise you with a homework problem in the exam and assume that you can solve it quickly.

**Academic Integrity Policy:**

Academic integrity is one of the cornerstones of RPI. Students taking courses at RPI have a right to expect that their work will be evaluated fairly with respect to other students. They have a right to expect that other students will not attempt to enhance their own grades or the grades of their friends by cheating. Professors have a right to expect that their students are honest and submit work reflecting their own efforts. In an atmosphere of academic integrity, students and professors are on the same team trying to achieve the same learning objectives.

Collaboration and discussion **are allowed** **and encouraged** in the homeworks and in-class activities. However, you must write-up your own solution, as the results of synthesizing discussions with others, and not merely copying others’ solutions. On exams, you are on your own and not allowed to discuss anything with your classmates. Thus, don’t copy someone else’s homework, in-class activities, and don’t cheat on exams. If I suspect you of either, I’ll ask for an explanation. If your explanation is unsatisfactory, you’ll be given a grade of **0** (zero) and reported to the Dean of Students. If this happens more than once, you will be given an **F** for the course, and the entire matter will be forwarded to the Dean of Students.
