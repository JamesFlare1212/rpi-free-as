## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will explore the structure, dynamics, and content of galaxies such as the Milky Way, as well as stellar and Galactic evolution. Students will learn methods to gather information about stars, the Milky Way galaxy in particular, and galaxies in general. Students will master the mathematical foundations that will enable more complex calculations of the interactions of stars, gas, and dark matter in galaxies.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

**Required Text:** Galaxies in the Universe, by Sparke and Gallagher, second edition

**Other Resources:** Dynamics and Astrophysics of Galaxies, by Jo Bovy (https://galaxiesbook.org/), Galactic Dynamics, Binney and Tremaine, second edition

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- (1) Students will be able to describe the Milky Way galaxy and the matter of which it is composed.
- (2) Students will be able to identify stellar types in spectra and photometry, and calculate the effects of reddening from dust.
- (3) Students will be able to describe the stellar populations in the Milky Way, including the stellar densities and luminosity functions.
- (4) Students will be able to calculate parallaxes and proper motion, create and interpret reduced proper motion diagrams.
- (5) Students will be able to make simple calculation of the dynamics of stars in the Milky Way, including orbits, spiral density waves, bars/bulges, tidal disruption and virialization.
- (6) Students will be able to describe the chemodynamical evolution of baryonic matter in the Milky Way.
- (7) Students will analyze current literature related to course topics, and discuss them with each other.
- Students will create lectures and a related homework problem on a special topic and present it to the class. (ASTR 6960, PHYS 6961 only)
- Student will evaluate each others the solutions to their assigned homework problems and analyze the effectiveness of the problem in promoting student learning. (ASTR 6960, PHYS 6961 only)

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Homework: Homework will be due about every two weeks, seven assignments in total. Students are encouraged to discuss homework assignments, but the work that is turned in must be the student’s original work. Submitted homework must clearly show the steps leading up to each answer. If you feel that you have received a grade in error, see the graduate TA first. The instructor will resolve grading disagreements only after an attempt has been made to resolve the issue with the TA.

Class Participation: We will have literature reviews about once per week. For each review, one person will be chosen to present a paper related to the topic that we are discussing in class. Half a course point will be awarded for active and thoughtful participation in the discussion of each paper, up to a maximum of six points. Four points (four percent of the course grade) will be based on the quality and thoroughness of the review of the assigned literature review.

Course Instruction (ASTR 6960, PHYS 6961 only): Students will prepare a half hour lecture on an extended course topic, along with a homework problem that will be distributed to the class. Each student will meet with Prof. Newberg before the presentation to get feedback on the lecture. Students will create a solution and grading rubric for the problem, and grade the homework problem for each student in the class. The lecture notes and graded homework will be turned in to Prof. Newberg for review. The grade for course lecture will be based on the quality of the lecture materials (4 points), the quality of the presentation itself (2 points), the quality of the homework problem (at an appropriate level, promote learning of the lecture material, 2 points), grading of the homework problem (good rubric, fairly graded, 2 points)

Exams: The exams will test the students’ mastery of the homework and important derivations in the lectures and textbook. The goal is to ask typical Galactic structure questions that might be asked on a qualifying exam in astronomy graduate school. Students will be allowed to use a calculator up to the level of a TI Inspire (nothing in the memory), but no notes or books. Sample questions will be distributed ahead of the exams to facilitate studying. Exam questions will be selected from the list of sample questions. The final exam is comprehensive; the questions will be chosen from the exam questions for each of the midterm exams and additional questions from the remainder of the class. Grading questions on exams should be resolved with the instructor.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students who cheat will receive a failing grade for the course. Cheating includes: copying, or allowing another student to copy, a test or assignment; altering a graded test or assignment before requesting re-grading; copying submitted solutions from published solution manuals or previous years; using materials other than those that are allowed on an exam; misrepresenting the work of another group or individual as your own. It is considered cheating to help another student cheat.

The environment in which students operate has many sources for solutions to homework and projects, including AI chatbots, online information, Chegg, student-organized archives, classmates, and other people. It is okay for to consult any of these sources for ideas, information, and to check your work. However, the work you actually turn in must be 100% your own. You must show how and why each step in the homework solution is done, and you must be writing them from your head and not by copying from somewhere else. A good test for you to determine whether you are following the course rules is to ask yourself whether your actions promote learning, whether you are learning the information and skills that the course is designed to teach you, and whether everything that you turn in is your own work.
