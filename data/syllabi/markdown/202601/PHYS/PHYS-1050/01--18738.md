## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a one semester overview of physics fundamentals with an emphasis on concepts and applications. Topics will include mechanics, including equilibrium and statics, fluids, oscillations, and waves; basics of thermodynamics and heat flow; electricity and magnetism; electromagnetic radiation and optics; and elements of atomic and nuclear physics. The class style will be a combination of lecture and interactive learning through in-class activities and discussions

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

## Course Goals

### Goals:

At the end of this course, the student should gain insight into various fundamental physics concepts and applications that are important for understanding how reality works and how we utilize these aspects of physics for various practical applications.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- At the end of this course, students will be able to describe core physics concepts and their real-world applications to a non-technical audience using audience-appropriate analogies and language.
- Through the completion of this course, students will be able to design and deliver a presentation that explains a physical phenomenon to a non-technical audience, using appropriate visuals, which is relevant to their field of study or interests.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Student attendance will be gauged through engaging and completing the in-class activities. The activities will consist of short research questions and experiments done mostly through online simulations to get a general idea for physical phenomena. Exams will consist of two parts with the first being the take-home scripts, which will be given at least ten days before the in-class exam portion. For these scripts, you will have to model the back and forth between your role as a scientist, real estate agent, or business person to convince your audience that your idea is worth proceeding with and using physics to justify your proposals. For the second part, there will be two in-class exams, which will consist of 40 multiple choice questions each related to the topics discussed in lecture. Lastly, there will be a Special Topic Project, which will be your chance to explore a topic you find the most interesting and you will write an abstract, research paper, and a presentation on that topic.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

It is expected for you to attend all classes to make the most out of your education. 35% of your grade will come from class participation which will be accounted for through in-class activities and discussions and staying in class until we have completed the class. In general, the structure of the class will be about 35-45 minutes of lecture with most of the rest of class consisting of an activity and discussion. The instructor will lead the activity and students should interact with each other to answer the questions in the activity. Also, if possible, it is highly encouraged to not make a habit of being late to class because if you miss lecture at the beginning of class, it will likely make answering questions on the activities more difficult.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.
