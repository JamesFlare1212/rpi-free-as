## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is a hands-on introduction to the primary building blocks of music and musicianship in a 21st-century context. The course will explore acoustics and psychoacoustics, rhythm, pitch, harmony, melody, timbre, improvisation, composition, and music notation through engaged listening practices and creative projects. No formal musical background is expected.

Students will be expected to sing, clap, play, and otherwise make noise.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Through active engagement and completion of course activities, you will be able to:

● understand the fundamental concepts of acoustics and auditory perception

● articulate ways in which acoustics and auditory perception interact to form the basis for a variety of musical traditions and practices

● gain fluency in critical listening skills and have a language for articulating what you hear

● understand and analyze musical notation and express musical concepts eﬀectively on your own and with others

● gain an understanding of the variety of roles musical notation can be used to accomplish musical goals

● learn to comfortably interact with others in musical contexts, both spontaneously and with direction.

● gain an appreciation for the great variety of contemporary musical practices, including the acoustic, electronic, and electroacoustic realms

● get familiar with the fundamentals of Western musical practices so that they may progress on to Music and Sound I

● gain keyboard fluency

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Homework assignments will include listening & reading with written responses, analysis, and worksheets. Homework will usually be assigned on Friday, and due the following Friday at the beginning of class.

The majority of your written response takes place in your Sound Journal. This is a weekly writing of 300-500 words. Each week, you will be given a question, idea, sound file, or other item, which you will use as the source of your journal entry. Journal entries will include subjective responses, aural analysis, memory reflection, and other orientations.

The aural drills portion of the homework will be conducted through musicca software. Each student will create a linked musicca account. New musicianship modules (rhythm, interval identification, singing, and dictation) will be posted each week. You will practice these throughout the week and track your progress.

Each unit will culminate in a collaborative composition/improvisation project with a small group of your classmates. Projects may include an in-class performance or presentation. The final creative project may be a short composition, a harmonic or theoretical proposition, a piece of software, a radio show, or another proposal with the approval of the instructor.
