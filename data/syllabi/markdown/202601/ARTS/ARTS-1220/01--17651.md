## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Fundamentals of 2D Design will introduce you to basic concepts about pictorial space, composition, light and color in the two dimensional visual arts. It is my hope that by looking at strategies of pictorial design and representation in art and trying to exercise some of those strategies in simple compositions, you will develop the means for expressing visual ideas effectively. Weekly hands-on homework projects, using analog media, will be complemented by in-class slide presentations, videos, discussions and critiques.

On Tuesday sessions the instructor will lecture on course topics, and time will be used to discuss readings and to present additional images, texts and video to augment the readings. Examples of pictorial design concepts will be drawn from both historical and contemporary art.

Homework assignments will be issued each Tuesday to be due and reviewed the following Friday. Students will also gather three found images relating to the weekly theme to present to the class each Tuesday. It is essential that you keep up with reading and homework assignments so that you can participate fully in reviews and discussions. (See “Work Wheel diagram”)

Homework assignments are to be executed in a consistent format in order to facilitate the production of a well-designed, high quality portfolio at the end of the semester. Weekly assignments will usually consist of a series of contrasting images that compare various design approaches described in the readings. Design assignments will be completed in a style of pure abstraction, with no recognizable images allowed. Everyone is required to post and submit work at weekly critiques and participate in discussions. *NOTE: students are NOT allowed to use color until the class begins color studies.*

Work submitted must be ORIGINAL work, designed by the student, as opposed to appropriated images from the internet or other sources. Original works may include some appropriated materials, such as collage elements, but those sources must be sufficiently manipulated so as to create an original design.

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance Policy**

After teaching at the college level for more than a decade, I can attest that attendance is the most definitive indicator of whether a student will succeed or fail at a course. I urge you to attend each and every class session whenever possible, realizing that sometimes illness or emergencies may prevent your attendance. Lectures will not be archived, although I encourage students to share notes. Inevitably, missing class will mean missing discussions, questions, important demonstrations and the intellectual community of the classroom. It is your responsibility to familiarize yourself of the content of any class period you miss.

While in class, I ask you be engaged in the classroom community. A student who is excessively texting or scrolling social media, arrives more than 15 minutes late, or sleeps in class will be marked absent.

You receive 100 points for your attendance over the semester. If you miss a class because of illness, please email Nathan **the day of your absence** to possibly receive credit for the missed class. More than two consecutive absences OR three absences over the course of two consecutive weeks will require an official excuse from health services or your class dean. Retroactive excuses are only allowed from health services or your class dean.
