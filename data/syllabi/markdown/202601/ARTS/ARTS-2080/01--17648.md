## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Creative and Research Development (Major Project — 50%) Demonstrate advanced creative agency by producing an original work or research project that reflects a personal artistic voice within contemporary improvisation practices. Apply relevant methodologies (creative, analytical, historical, or technological) to develop a substantial project from concept to completion. Integrate theoretical, historical, or contextual knowledge into creative or research outcomes appropriate to professional contemporary music practice. Communicate artistic or scholarly ideas effectively, whether through performance, composition, multimedia work, or written research.
- Rehearsal Preparation and Professionalism (15%) Prepare effectively for rehearsals and collaborative sessions, demonstrating reliability, punctuality, and consistent technical readiness. Contribute constructively to ensemble processes, showing leadership, flexibility, and responsiveness in group improvisation settings. Exhibit professional standards of conduct expected in contemporary music environments.
- Reflective Journaling (15%) Critically analyze listening examples from diverse improvisational traditions, identifying techniques, structures, and aesthetic approaches. Reflect on personal artistic growth, documenting evolving strategies, challenges, and insights related to improvisation. Articulate connections between listening, practice, and creative decision‑making.
- Mid‑Semester Project Review (10%) Present a clear and coherent project plan, articulating artistic or research goals, influences, and intended outcomes. Demonstrate meaningful progress toward the major project through drafts, sketches, recordings, or research findings. Respond constructively to feedback, adjusting artistic or scholarly approaches as needed.
- Attendance and Participation (10%) Engage actively in class activities, including improvisation workshops, discussions, and collaborative exercises. Support a positive learning environment through attentive listening, respectful dialogue, and consistent participation. Demonstrate curiosity and openness in exploring unfamiliar musical practices and creative processes.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Assessment Structure**

**50% — Component 1: Major Project**

Students choose **one** of the following pathways:

**Option A: Creative Project**

A substantial original work demonstrating advanced creative practice in contemporary improvisation. Formats may include:

- Performance (solo or ensemble)
- Composition or structured improvisation framework
- Recording or production project
- Installation or multimedia work
- Technology‑based system or instrument design

**Option B: Research Project**

A written project integrating historical, analytical, and theoretical perspectives on improvisation. May include:

- Analytical study of improvisational practices
- Historical or cultural research
- Theoretical exploration of improvisation systems
- Critical engagement with contemporary repertoire or methodologies

**15% — Rehearsal Preparation and Professionalism**

Assessment of:

- Preparedness for rehearsals and workshops
- Reliability, punctuality, and communication
- Contribution to ensemble processes
- Professional conduct in collaborative settings

**15% — Reflective Journaling**

Ongoing written or recorded reflections demonstrating:

- Critical listening and analysis of improvisational works
- Self‑assessment of artistic development
- Documentation of creative processes
- Engagement with course materials and listening assignments

**10% — Mid‑Semester Project Review**

A checkpoint evaluating:

- Progress toward the major project
- Clarity of artistic or research direction
- Ability to articulate goals and next steps
- Responsiveness to feedback

**10% — Attendance and Participation**

Evaluation of:

- Consistent attendance
- Active engagement in class activities
- Contribution to discussions, workshops, and group improvisations

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic dishonesty is, by definition, considered a flagrant offense to the education process. It is taken seriously by students, faculty, and Rensselaer and will be addressed in an effective manner. If a student is found in violation of academic dishonesty policy (for example submitting another student's assignment for your own), your grade will be submitted as "F" for that assignment and the incident will be reported to the Dean of Students Office. For the full formal policy, please see http://doso.rpi.edu/update.doc
