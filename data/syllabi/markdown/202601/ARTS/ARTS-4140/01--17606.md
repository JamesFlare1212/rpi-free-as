### Other LMS Information:

Queer Ecologies is an undergraduate and graduate arts course that uses art to think about our eco-futures. We will look at the disruptive nature of Cartesian thinking and binary positioning in relation to sexuality and ecologies. “Queering” is a means to refuse this binary thinking and consider expansive interdisciplinary practices that have evolved from LGBTQ+ and feminist theory, decolonial thinking, extinction and death studies, environmental justice, critiques of science and big pharma, biopolitics, bioethics, biology, science fiction, aesthetics, art and play.

Queer Ecologies “examine[s] the ways in which sex and nature are understood in light of multiple trajectories of power and matter.” – Heather Davis

The class will stress philosophy, theory, science and creative actions to understand and create space to “listen” to the voices within environmental politics. We will use examples of ecological and biological art projects to spur our conversations and look at ideas through the lens of art processes. This class will be playful and explorative. Open to all levels and abilities – all welcome.

*Queering what counts as nature is my categorical imperative. Queering specific normalized categories is not for the easy frisson of transgression, but for the hope for livable worlds.*

– Donna Haraway, “A Game of Cat’s Cradle”

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Communicate knowledge of current theory around “queer ecology,” including queer, and LGBTQ+ issues, and new ecological art and science practices through written and visual assignments.
- • Analyze and ask ethical questions concerning queer approaches to ecological issues.
- • Formulate opinions about ethical practices and the queering of issues in relations to the human body and “nature” developed through readings, discussions, class workshops.
- • Conduct research and write about contemporary new queer ecological art and science practices.
- • Envision and create artwork around queer ecological and sustainable practices.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Outcome Assessment Measures: Students are assessed based on: 1. their understanding of materials and concepts presented in class. 2. Assessment happens through the varied assignments (from mask making to papers, from historical research to imaginary future speculation, and even through responses to readings), and class participation (which is key). 3. In the course we engage in discussions and conversations about readings and assignments. 4. The students are evaluated based on their expression of and use of ideas around shifting and complex concepts regarding gender, sex and our perceptions of nature and science. Students Assignments -**

**- Readings** **- Readings and Class Participation: 25% of final grade**

There will be weekly reading assignments required for each week’s class. Students will be asked to lead 1-2 class discussions about readings and demonstrate a working knowledge of the readings. Readings will be provided digitally as PDFs on a shared Google drive. Please write brief journal entries about the articles (minimum 14 entries – ideally one per week).

**- Assignments #1-5 - 75% of grade****:**

**Hands-on exercises & projects** There are five art and research projects aimed at giving you a chance to use what we're learning in class. All text, graphics, and other materials posted on a website/portfolio must be your original work, or posted with permission of the copyright owner, or using Creative Commons copyright indicators (http://creativecommons.org/license/).

- **Assignment # 1 – Microbial Worldlings (10%)**– With materials distributed in class pls find a microbial world that you would like to explore. Take photos of the progress of the microbial life on your agar plates. Present a story about the results with in-class presentation.

- **Assignment # 2 - Performing Queer Animal/Plant** **(10%)**– Bring materials to class to create a queer plant or animal mask/costume. You will perform your chosen creature wearing your mask/costume.

- **Assignment # 3 –Indigenous Territories** **(20%)**– Research the original indigenous group of your homeland. Whose land are we living on? What is the history of this particular indigenous group and are they still living in the area? Draw maps of these territories. What might your homeland be like if Indigenous communities still thrived and oversaw the land where you grew up? What ecologies would be thriving as well? Research a way to honor and pay tribute to the original people of that land. You will present your findings to the class and include maps and listing of ecologies. History of the original indigenous peoples and also what today might look like if things had not changed as they did. Be as imaginative as possible – based off your research. What is you tribute?
- Students will present a power point or slideshow to the class. 4000 level students will submit a 1-2 page write up of your research findings and proposals.

- **Assignment # 4 –****Feral Ecology Assignment** **(10%)**– This assignment asks you to spend one hour in a queer and/or feral ecological space (broadly interpreted) and to make observations, such as a cruising site, abandoned lot, forest, or another site. You should follow Anna Tsing’s methodology of the “arts of noticing,” asking you to really sit with a space and inquire into what is there and what relations it produces. You should take notes, make drawings, or, if possible, take photographs during your time in the space.
- Afterwards write a 1-2 page response to your experiences, drawing upon course material. You will present your findings to the class in a power point/slide show.

- **Assignment # 5 – Final project “Queer Ecologies for Your Community: or how to motivate your community” (25%)–** *What can we do to encourage change of attitudes and belief systems to align with the tenants of Queer Ecologies?* *How can we take actions that work towards equity, racial, social and queer eco-justice and rights? What would you like to contribute as inspiration towards these ends?*
- Students will write a **final research paper** and make a **final art project** (with accompanying written research references) based on a “queer ecologies” topic of your choice drawing upon readings from the class (plus other sources). You may make your project comment upon our current moment. You will present a power point, artwork, ritual or performance relating your topic to the class. As well you will submit a paper summarizing your research. Minimum word count: 1500 words.

**GRADING**

**Your projects will be graded on three major criteria:**

- **Technique:** How well you use and employ knowledge from class. Do you demonstrate mastery of the skills covered in class?
- **Effectiveness**: Overall strength of work, how well it conveys intent. Does the work accomplish what it sets out to do? Is it contextualized by your research?
- **Ambition:** You will be rewarded for taking risks, ethical considerations, and artistic uniqueness. Is the project challenging, or attempting something extraordinary?

**Late Policy:** If students know they will not be able to make deadlines for assignments, they should let Professor High know of their conflicts. Extensions will be granted when needed.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty. If found in violation of the academic honesty policy, students may be subject to penalty. The instructor administers an academic (grade) penalty. The first violation results in 0 grade for that assignment. The second violation results in failure of the course. Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the Dean of Students for undergraduate students, or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively.
