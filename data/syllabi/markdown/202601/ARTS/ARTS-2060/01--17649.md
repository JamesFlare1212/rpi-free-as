## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- In this course the student will develop their creative ability and depth of expression using digital imaging tools. Students will gain knowledge of animation history and its role in the art world, including experimental and underground animation. Students will apply technical/aesthetic knowledge in the completion of a series of animation projects. Students will gain experience in critique process by presenting their work in class. Students will learn the fundamental vocabulary of animation, such as spacing and timing, anticipation/reaction, squash and stretch, motion cycles, exaggeration, and staging. Students will develop technical skills in animation and video production techniques using appropriate software.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Grading**

Studio projects are graded by both **concept** and **execution**.

A strong concept may be original, expressive, personal, political, novel, clever, or unexpected.

A good execution will be whatever is necessary to convey the artist’s concept. The artist should strive for clarity, without heavy-handedness.

Even in the case of botched execution or unsuccessful concept, a student may earn extra points(even an A) for taking a daring risk in their work. In this case, **the noble failure**, the class will use this daring risk as a moment to reflect on both the successes and failures in the work, and why elements may still be worthwhile and/or meaningful.

After a critique, projects may be reworked and resubmitted. A resubmitted project may receive a high grade, but will never be assigned a lower grade than the original version.

**Late submissions WILL NOT be accepted.** Students not submitting work on time will be given ZERO points for that assignment. In rare cases, extensions may be granted, at the instructor’s discretion. Extensions should be requested as far in advance to the due date as possible. Any medical conditions that may interrupt assignment submissions should be communicated to the instructor as soon as possible, and extensions may be granted at the instructor’s discretion.

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance Policy**

After teaching at the college level for more than a decade, I can attest that attendance is the most definitive indicator of whether a student will succeed or fail at a course. I urge you to attend each and every class session whenever possible, realizing that sometimes illness or emergencies may prevent your attendance. Lectures will not be archived, although I encourage students to share notes. Inevitably, missing class will mean missing discussions, questions, important demonstrations and the intellectual community of the classroom. It is your responsibility to familiarize yourself of the content of any class period you miss.

While in class, I ask you be engaged in the classroom community. A student who is excessively texting or scrolling social media, arrives more than 15 minutes late, or sleeps in class will be marked absent.

You receive 100 points for your attendance over the semester. If you miss a class because of illness, please email Nathan **the day of your absence** to possibly receive credit for the missed class. More than two consecutive absences OR three absences over the course of two consecutive weeks will require an official excuse from health services or your class dean. Retroactive excuses are only allowed from health services or your class dean.
