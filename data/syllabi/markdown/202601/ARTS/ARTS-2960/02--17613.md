## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides an introduction to the shared principles and practices of musical composition across a range of styles, including concert music, songwriting, jazz, film and game scoring, electronic, and experimental forms. Students will explore fundamental techniques for creating melody and harmony, developing musical material, shaping texture and balance, and organizing musical structure. Through creative projects, analytical study, and in-class discussion, students will strengthen their compositional skills by exploring a variety of strategies and styles, cultivating an individual artistic voice. Open to students of all levels and musical backgrounds. Ability to read Western music notation recommended.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Gain a broad knowledge of diverse contemporary compositional practices.
- Be able to use and articulate basic compositional techniques and strategies.
- Students will expand their abilities to develop musical ideas.
- Gain proficiency with a variety of methodologies of musical analysis.
- Gain awareness of a variety of notational and analytic strategies.
- Gain practical knowledge of the interfaces between technology and creative practice.
- Gain experience developing their own unique creative voice.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- **Participation:** This course relies heavily on in-class discussion and group projects.
- **Sound Journals:** @eekly reflective writings on a given listening- or composition-related topic. They range from creative exercises to analytic topics to thought experiments. Each journal has a 350 word minimum.
- **Composition Exercises:** A series of exercises, focused on developing specific techniques, strategies, and harmonic considerations will be assigned over the course of the semester.
- **Presentation:** Each student will give 15-minute presentation on a piece of music of their choice.
- **Final Project:** Each student will develop a short final project of their own choosing.
