## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

An intermediate hands-on studio course in 3D computer animation. Students will complete several small assignments that are designed to encourage creativity, develop a further skill with the tools and techniques of animation.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The goal of this course is to develop a solid foundation of the principles and practice of 3D animation and gain fluency with industry-standard 3D animation software.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate proficiency in animation techniques, vocabularies and relevant technologies
- Demonstrate effective use of animation principles such as timing, spacing, overlap, arcs, weight, force
- Demonstrate development of original creative expression in 3D animation
- Critique peer artworks in a creative community and and evaluate their earlier creative work.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

3D animation is a combination of artistry, technical methods, animation craft, and - most importantly - *practice!* Becoming a good animator is about practice, and being self-reflective in your practice. Animation is also about finding inspiration: from other animations, from other media, and from the world around you; and about participating in a creative community.

- Short Animation Projects: 50% total
  - Project 1: Chain Reaction
  - Project 2: Microscopic World
  - Project 3: Body Mechanics: Short Action or Walking animation
  - Project 4: Pantomime acting shot
- Final Animation Project: 25%
- In-class exercises: short technical exercises, typically completed in class: 15%
- Attendance and Participation: 10%

# Grading Criteria

Short animation projects are graded on:

- Animation technique and effective use of animation principles
- Completeness
- Adherence to technical specifications
- Presentation at critiques
- File submission

The final project is graded on the same criteria, as well as Creativity and originality.

**Assignment submissions and due dates**

- Project files will be submitted in Perforce, and will be presented in class in group critiques. Successful file submission and participation in critiques are both required to receive full credit for the assignment.

**Partial Credit, Late Assignments, and Revisions**

- Projects that are not completed should still be presented during critiques and submitted

**Attendance and participation:**

- This is a studio course, and success is heavily dependent on making consistent, effective use of class time to animate. Students are expected to attend class, work on the projects for the course in class, and participate in critiques both by presenting their own work and by giving thoughtful feedback.
- Up to three unexcused absences are allowed without penalty.

**Assignment revisions**

- Short Animation Projects may each be revised and resubmitted once for an improved grade.
