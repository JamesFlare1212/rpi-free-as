## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course studies mechatronics at a theoretical and practical level with a balance of theory/analysis and hardware implementation. Emphasis is placed on physical understanding rather than on mathematical formalities. A case-study, problem-solving approach, with hardware demonstrations, either on video or in class, and hardware lab exercises, is used throughout the course. This course covers mechatronic system design, modeling and analysis of dynamic physical systems, control sensors and actuators, analog and digital control electronics, continuous controller design and digital implementation, interfacing sensors and actuators to a microcomputer/microcontroller, and real-time programming for control. Throughout the coverage the focus is kept on the role of each of these areas in the overall design process and how these key areas are integrated into a successful mechatronic system design.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

- Understand the importance of the integration of modeling and controls in the design of mechatronic systems.
- Understand the dynamic system investigation process and be able to apply it to a variety of dynamic physical systems.
- Understand the importance of physical and mathematical modeling (both from first principles and using system identification experimental techniques) in mechatronic system design
- Understand and be able to model various nonlinear and parasitic effects in real dynamic systems: backlash, time delay, saturation, Coulomb friction, unmodeled resonances.
- Be able to develop a hierarchy of physical models for a dynamic system, from a truth model to a design model, and understand the appropriate use of this hierarchy of models.
- Become proficient in the use of MATLAB/Simulink and other software to model and analyze nonlinear and linear mechatronic systems.
- Have an awareness of the key elements of a measurement system and the basic performance specifications and models of a variety of analog and digital mechatronic sensors.
- Understand the characteristics and models of various electromechanical actuators (brushed dc motor, brushless dc motor, and stepper motor) and hydraulic and pneumatic actuators.
- Understand analog and digital circuits and components and semiconductor electronics as they apply to mechatronic systems.
- Understand stability (absolute and relative) and performance (command following, disturbance rejection, robustness) as it applies to feedback control systems.
- Understand and be able to apply, with the use of the MATLAB Controls Toolbox and other software, various control system design techniques: open-loop feedforward control, classical feedback control (root-locus and frequency response), and state-space control.
- Have an awareness of industrial motion control: types of controllers (PID-type control modes and variations), tuning of controllers, and position/velocity control loops with encoders/ resolvers.
- Understand the digital implementation of control and basic digital control design techniques.
- Become proficient in the use of MATLAB/Simulink/Controls Toolbox and other software to design and analyze analog controllers and verify their digital implementation.
- Be able to apply all these skills to the design of a mechatronic system.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance at *all* classes is mandatory and participation in class is strongly encouraged. Preparation for class is essential, as each session will be conducted with an interactive, applied, mentoring approach. Students are encouraged to ask as many questions as they want to (and more)!

**No late assignments will be accepted without consultation with the instructor *prior* to the due date for the assignment**

### Other Course Policies:

***Homework*****:**

Students will have 1 to 2 weeks to complete each homework problem set. They are to be *individually* and professionally done and submitted before class - **no exceptions**. ***Collaborative discussion (Not Copying!) on homework assignments is permitted and encouraged***. MATLAB (including Simulink and the Control System Toolbox) and other software will be used extensively in the solution of these assignments. ***Completing and understanding the problem sets are vital to be able to succeed on the Midterms!***

***Labs******:***

Labs will involve the use of hardware and/or software and will be completed **individually**. Instructions for preparing lab submissions will be provided on Blackboard. Format may vary based on the nature of the lab activity. Initial labs will focus on system components and later labs on prototyping level hardware + system design.

***Project******:***

The final project will be an extension of the balancing system lab. Students will choose an additional feature or functionality to design and implement on the balancing system. Each student must submit a brief project proposal, according to provided guidelines. This will allow the instructor to provide feedback to refine the scope and plan. Students are expected to work both in and out of class to make progress on their project. Students will present their results in person to the instructor, according to provided guidelines.

***Midterm:***

Two “open-book, open computer” exams given during class hours. Instructor will give detailed guidelines prior to midterms on permitted information sources. With the rapid evolution of artificial intelligence, the instructor reserves the right to restrict certain resources in the interest of fairness. There will not be a final exam for this course.

***In Class Exercises******:***

There will be in-class exercises throughout the semester. In addition, take-home exercises or deadline extensions may be included to practice more exhaustive concepts. In-class exercises cannot be made up; attendance is mandatory.

***Computer Usage******:***

**Matlab R2025b** with Simulink and the Control System Toolbox will be used extensively throughout the course. Installation guidance and tutorials will be made available at the beginning of the semester on Blackboard. Other software/hardware may be used for both measurement/control, as well as for analysis and control design.
