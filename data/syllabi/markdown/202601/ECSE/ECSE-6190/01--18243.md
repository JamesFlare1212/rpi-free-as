## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Apply the student’s knowledge of power engineering to the solution of power system stability problems by computer methods. Treats matrix techniques and solutions to sets of non-linear algebraic equations, power flow analysis, network admittance building, numerical integration, models and simulation of power systems and power apparatus, stability, and control design.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Chow, J.H., and Sanchez-Gasca, J.J. “Power System Modeling, Computation and Control,” Wiley, 2019.  
Milano, Federico. Power System Modelling and Scripting, Springer-Verlag, London, 2010.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Upon successful completion, students will be able to:

1. Analyze and reflect on the static and dynamic operation of a power system.
2. Derive and evaluate dynamic models of synchronous machines, excitation systems, and their controls.
3. Apply and contrast various numerical techniques to simulate large-scale power systems.
4. Design basic power system damping controls using power system stabilizers (PSS)
5. Develop software scripts using course tools to perform dynamic simulation studies.
6. Synthesize and validate a computational solution for a complex power system stability problem, justifying the choice of models and methods.
7. Critically evaluate the results from different software tools and articulate findings in a technical report of publishable quality.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

- Week 1-2: Overview & Review of Power System Fundamentals (Per-unit, Transformers, Generators, Lines)
- Week 3-4: Power Flow Analysis (Formulation, Solution Methods, Voltage Stability)
- Week 5-6: Power System Dynamics & Simulation (Integration Methods, Transient Stability)
- Week 7-8: Linear Analysis & Small-Signal Stability (Linearization, Oscillatory Modes)
- Week 9-10: Synchronous Machine Modeling (Detailed Models and Parameters)
- Week 11-12: Excitation Systems and Controls (AVR Models)
- Week 13-14: Power System Stabilizer (PSS) Design & Project Work
- Finals Week: Final Project Due

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- In-Class Hands-on Computer Workshops and Assignments: Several in-class hands-on computer workshops will be carried out. Each of these workshops consist of computer modeling exercises where you will follow step-by-step instructions from the teacher and verify you can build and simulate power system models with Modelica, using the Dymola or OpenModelica software, as well as other tools. The workshop will have a corresponding assignment, where you will report on your workshop results and perform a few additional tasks to validate your learnings.
- Homework: Assignments will consist of solving quantitative problems by pen and paper, performing computer modeling and simulation exercises, and programming tasks using the course software tools.
- Final Project: Students will work individually on a project to demonstrate proficiency in the course learning outcomes. The project will involve developing simulation models and building software routines to solve a problem assigned by the instructor or a topic relevant to the student's own research (with instructor’s approval).
