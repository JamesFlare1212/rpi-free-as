## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course introduces methods to develop numerical and algorithmic techniques necessary for mobile robots to navigate and coordinate. Topics include mobile robot kinematics and dynamics, control, localization, mapping, path planning, and multi-robot systems.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Students who complete this course satisfactorily will be able to understand and apply the following major topics:  
• Wheeled robot kinematics, dynamics and control  
• State estimation  
• Localization  
• Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM)  
• Path Planning: Bug Algorithms, potential fields, search and sampling-based approaches  
• Aerial robot dynamics and control  
• Multi-robot coordination and planning

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- use sensors and landmarks for state estimation, localization, and mapping
- find a feasible path and trajectory for a mobile robot
- design coordination for multi-robot systems
- formulate a real world mobile robot problem and develop solution methods
- analyze mobile robot (wheeled and aerial) dynamics and control

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Course deliverables will consist of homework assignments, short quizzes, written reports for lab assignments, a final project report and presentation.

### Other Course Policies:

**Lab Etiquette**  
Lab access is necessary for the satisfactory completion of the projects, which accounts for a good percentage of your overall grade. Managing shared workspaces and resources is an important skill for all professionals. Students will be held to high standards with regard to cleanliness, professionalism, and consideration. The following are some of the rules that students need to follow:  
• Access: Card access will be given to JEC 5312 to work on your lab assignments.  
• Robot Charging: The robot runs on Lithium-Ion batteries. Such batteries pose some safety concerns, so please read this section thoroughly. Do not leave devices unattended while charging, it is a fire safety concern. We can help each other by charging robots not in use when in the lab. If a robot needs charging and no one is around to monitor it, please notify the instructor and the teaching assistants. Please turn off the robot before charging, and do not turn it on again before unplugging the charging cable.  
• Food and Drink: Food and drink (except for water in a bottle) are not allowed in the lab space. The equipment is sensitive.  
• Cleanliness: You should move things back to where they are before you leave the lab and keep the lab clean.  
Any concerns regarding lab etiquette should be brought to the attention of the instructor as soon as possible. If the lab etiquette violations become a problem (e.g., if the robots left charging, if there is damage from spilled food or drink, etc.), lab privileges can be revoked resulting in getting zero for lab assignments  
**Late Submissions**  
Because there might be unexpected events (e.g., work or family commitments) there is 0% penalty for 48 hours (no questions asked) for late submissions of the following types of assignments: homework, lab assignment reports, final project proposal and report. After this period students would get 0 credits. You can not get bonus points if you submit late. Quizzes and final presentations should be submitted on the posted due date, after which students would get 0 credits. If you are unable to complete the assignment on time due to sickness, communicate this to the instructor before the posted due date (not the late submission date) so the necessary extensions  
can be granted.  
**Grade Appeals**  
Requests for regrade must be made (in writing) within 72 hours of the grade being communicated to the student. Regrade requests for final projects need to be done within 4 hours. Any concerns regarding overall course performance must be communicated to the instructor as soon as possible. Final grades will not be modified once published in SIS unless there is a demonstrable error made by the instructor (i.e., a calculation error).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Specifically, in this course, quiz submissions must be performed individually and must represent the student's own work. In homework submissions, high level discussions with other classmates are allowed, as long as they are properly identified during the submission. Direct copying or code sharing is not allowed. Lab assignments and projects will be performed in groups. In cases where external help was received or given in these assignments, a note should be included to indicate the collaboration.  
Usage of materials (books, internet sites, etc.) outside the listed course resources should be cited. Evidence of academic dishonesty will result in a zero for the associate assignment and a report to the Dean of Students (and potentially to the student's department) for possible additional actions.  
**Note on use of Generative AI:** Students may use generative AI tools (such as ChatGPT) as part of their research and preparation for assignments, or as a text editor, but the text that is submitted must be written by the student. For example, students may use AI to generate ideas and questions, perform debugging that they then revise, expand, and cite properly. Students are only allowed to use these tools when collaboration is allowed. Hence, students are not allowed to use such tools for quizzes. Students should also be aware that the outputs of these tools are not always reliable or accurate. Any use of such tools and the extent of the use should be acknowledged and cited properly (i.e., the name and the version of the AI model needs to be indicated). Violations of this policy will be treated as academic misconduct. If you have any questions about this policy or if you are unsure whether a particular use of AI is acceptable, please do not hesitate to ask for clarification.
