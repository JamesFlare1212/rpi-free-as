## Course Goals

### Goals:

This pilot, introductory course, co-taught with a team of leading industry experts, will provide insight into key fabrication technologies that enable the semiconductor manufacturing to produce integrated circuits with more functionality, less power consumption at a similar cost as before.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Semiconductor manufacturing is a highly complex endeavor, drawing on many engineering disciplines including Chemical, Mechanical, Electrical, Material, Industrial, Physics, and Computer Science. This pilot, introductory course, co-taught with a team of leading industry experts, will provide insight into key fabrication technologies that enable the semiconductor manufacturing to produce computer chips with astonishing increase in performance and functionality and reduction in energy, size and cost. It is targeted towards advanced undergraduates and early graduate students. There will be a tour of GlobalFoundries Fab8 in Malta New York where students can see how all of these elements come together in one of the world most advanced manufacturing facility.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Explain the fabrication processes and technologies for state-of-the-art computer chips, as well as the issues and solutions related to the fabrication yield/failures and the reliability of the chips.
- Demonstrate an ability to understand several aspects of computer chip design for manufacturing, including those related to memory, high power, radio frequency and silicon photonics
- Carry out research on selected topics, and gain experience on technical presentation and report.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- (i) Pre-lecture questionnaire (PLQ) for students to get prepared for the lecture.
- (ii) Homework (HW) assignments in the form of multiple choices and short answers to help students review and learn the lecture material.
- (iii) Project presentation and report; one submission per group of 5 students. Students will present their projects during the class hours, so they will learn from peers.
  1. Reports from undergraduate students may be based on lecture topics and/or closely related literature to summarize the key learning, provide feedback or questions, identify the critical innovations, and figure out the future challenges, as well as the potential solutions.
  2. Extra requirement for reports from graduate students is that it should be at a level of research report with in-depth analysis on chosen research papers published in top conferences/journals.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
