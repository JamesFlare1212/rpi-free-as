## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course introduces the principles, technologies, and system architectures of optical interconnects that enable high-bandwidth, energy-efficient, low-latency links in next-generation artificial intelligence (AI) and high-performance computing (HPC) systems. Students will explore device- to system-level integration of photonics and electronics, including optical communication fundamentals, high-speed lasers, modulators and photodetectors, photonic integrated circuits (PICs), high-speed transceivers, and co-packaged optics (CPO). Industrial case studies and a design project provide experiential learning aligned with state-of-the-art technology trends in semiconductor photonics.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

1. Understand the motivation, physical principles, and architectural drivers for optical interconnects in AI and HPC systems.
2. Analyze the performance and limitations of optical transceivers, and photonic integrated circuits used in short-reach communication links.
3. Compare monolithic and heterogeneous integration technologies for realizing photonics-electronics systems-on-chip.
4. Develop a conceptual or preliminary design for a next-generation optical interconnect system or component.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

| **Week** | **Topic** |
| --- | --- |
| **Week 1** | **Introduction to Optical Interconnects and AI Data Centers**: Motivation for optical interconnects; data bottlenecks in AI/HPC; comparison with electrical interconnects; energy and bandwidth scaling; overview of industry trends |
| **Week 2** | **Optical Communication Fundamentals**: Light modulation, transmission, and detection; basic link budget; BER and eye diagrams; overview of NRZ, PAM4, and coherent schemes |
| **Week 3** | **Laser Sources for Optical Interconnects**: Edge-emitting and surface-emitting lasers (DFB, VCSEL, micro-LED); modulation bandwidth, noise, and power scaling |
| **Week 4** | **Photodetectors and Receivers**: PIN, APD, and heterojunction photodiodes; TIA design basics; noise-equivalent power and responsivity |
| **Week 5** | **Silicon Photonics Basics**: SOI platform, waveguides, couplers, MZMs, ring modulators; foundry processes; current challenges |
| **Week 6** | **Monolithic Photonics Integration**: III-V epitaxy growth technologies using MOCVD and MBE; epitaxy growth and regrowth; quantum well intermixing |
| **Week 7** | **Heterogeneous Photonics Integration:** III–V-on-silicon integration; wafer bonding; micro-transfer printing; GaN, InP, and Ge-on-Si integration approaches. |
| **Week 8** | Midterm Exam + Industry Guest Lecture |
| **Week 9** | **Co-Packaged Optics (CPO):** CPO architecture and design; thermal and electrical co-design; optical-electrical co-simulation |
| **Week 10** | **VCSEL and Micro-LED Arrays for Parallel Optical Interconnects**: High-density emitters; integration with CMOS drivers; multi-channel optical interconnect architectures |
| **Week 11** | **Packaging, Coupling, and Fiber Attach Technologies**: Passive/active alignment; fiber arrays; micro-optics; thermal management; reliability testing |
| **Week 12** | **Optical Networks-on-Chip and AI Accelerator Integration:** Optical NoC architectures; optical memory access; photonic tensor cores |
| **Week 13** | **Emerging Trends and Research Directions** |
| **Week 14** | S**tudent Project Presentations and Course Review**: Final presentations: concept designs for next-gen optical interconnects |

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

1. Explain the key physical principles underlying optical interconnect technologies, including light modulation, transmission, and detection in short-reach data links.
2. Analyze the performance of optical communication systems using link budgets, bandwidth and energy efficiency metrics, and evaluate trade-offs between optical and electrical interconnects.
3. Design a basic optical interconnect link or subsystem (e.g., VCSEL-based or silicon-photonics-based link) that meets given data-rate and power-budget specifications.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Course assessment will be done through homework or assignments, one midterm exam, and a final exam or project. Homework/Assignments will consist of either in quantitative or analytical problems. The mid-term will be open book, open notes, and time-limited, and will be conducted during the class hours. For the final project, the students can work individually or in teams to apply one of the techniques learnt in the course to a real-life application. The final project demo/presentation will be held in the last week of classes. The details of the grading policy are provided below.

## Grading policy for 4962

1. Homework / Assignments 40%
2. One midterm exam 30%
3. Final project (demo/presentation) 30%

**Homework:** Homework problems will be regularly assigned. These problems are required to be done individually in a professional manner (neatness and thoroughness count!). Graded homework will be returned in class approximately one week after they are due. Collaboration in the solution of the homework problems is permitted and is indeed encouraged if it enhances the learning process, but mere copying of the solution from another student is not allowed. In general, no late assignments will be accepted. However, extensions may be requested in writing prior to the assignment due date and evaluated on a case-by-case basis.

**Project:** Course projects will be performed individually and involve choosing a research paper/topic to investigate in depth. Students may choose from a list of recommended topics or propose own custom projects, subject to instructor approval. Project should involve a detailed analysis of the chosen topic.

**Exam:** There will be one midterm exam, but no final exam. In all exams, students are allowed to bring computer, calculator, cell phone, Internet access, smart watches are allowed in exams.
