## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Development and application of Maxwell’s equations in free space and within materials. Introduction to vector calculus and computer-aided analysis and design methods in electromagnetics. Applications include calculation of lumped circuit elements from field theory, plane wave propagation in various materials, and reflection from boundaries. Transmission line concepts, Smith charts, and other design tools for distributed circuits.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Fawwaz T. Ulaby and Umberto Ravaioli, Fundamentals of Applied Electromagnetics, Prentice Hall 8th Edition, 2020.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. an ability to obtain solutions to electrostatic and magnetostatic fields for typical configurations of materials and sources
- 2\. an ability to determine the capacitance of simple practical systems of conductors
- 3\. an ability to determine the self and mutual inductance of simple practical current carrying systems
- 4\. an ability to apply the basic principles of electromagnetic motors and generators
- 5\. an ability to determine the transmission of power by low loss TEM transmission lines from a simple source to a passive load
- 6\. an ability to determine the reflection and transmission of TEM waves for uniform plane waves incident on planar material boundaries for low loss or conducting media Course Assessment Measures

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

*10 Homework Assignments* (15%) – submission through Gradescope

Homework assignments will be posted on Gradescope and are usually due a week after they are assigned, at 11:59pm on the designated due date. Homework assignments that are submitted up to a maximum of 2 days late are subject to a penalty of 10% of the total possible points for the assignment per day late. Up-to-date assignment due dates can be found on Gradescope and the course calendar document on LMS. The lowest two homework grades will be dropped when calculating the final grade.

*3 Mid-Term Exams* (35%)

There will be three exams during the semester, covering three of the four main units. The fourth unit will only be covered in the cumulative final exam. All exams will be closed book, but students will be given one or more 8.5" x 11" crib sheet(s) that contain information relevant to the topics on that particular exam. Students are expected to use the equations provided on the crib sheet to solve the exam problems. Students who use equations or approaches to solving exam problems from sources other than the crib sheet or course material should be prepared to explain their methods to the instructor to receive credit.

Students may leave the exam room to use the restroom during an exam, but must first ask the instructor to do so and must also leave all electronic devices in the exam room. Leaving the exam room with an electronic device will be considered a breach of the course’s exam policy and is subject to consequences outlined “Collaboration and Academic Dishonesty”.

*28 In-Class Exercises* (8%) – submission through Gradescope

During most class meetings, exercises on lecture topics will be assigned to be completed during class time and submitted on Gradescope. Penalties for assignments submitted after the due date are similar to those for homework assignments.

*5 Studio Sessions* (6%) – submission through Gradescope

On Wednesdays, a Studio Session is held. On some days, this consists of a short one-week activity that is related to the current lecture material and followed by a short mini-report. These sessions are intended to help you apply and integrate the skills taught in the class as well as to implement them in an engineering design setting. Studio session assignments that are submitted up to a maximum of 2 days late are subject to a penalty of 10% of the total possible points for the assignment per day late.

*2 Design Projects* (12%) – submission through Gradescope

Two topics of the Studio Sessions will be devoted to multi-week design projects that are followed by more detailed lab reports. Design projects that are submitted up to a maximum of 2 days late are subject to a penalty of 10% of the total possible points for the assignment per day late.

*Final Exam* (20%)

The final exam will be a cumulative assessment of the course material and will take place during finals week.

*Class Participation & Attendance* (4%)

Five percent of your grade will be based on participation and attendance. This will include lecture and Studio Session attendance and your active participation during class, including answering occasional concepts-based questions in lecture. This assessment will be based on the observations of the TAs, the instructors and your fellow students.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. A violation of Academic Integrity policy is, by definition, considered a flagrant offense to the educational process. It is taken seriously by students, faculty, and Rensselaer and will be addressed in an effective manner. If found responsible for committing academic dishonesty, the involved student(s) will be reported to the Dean of Students Office and may be subject to one or both types of penalties: an academic (grade) penalty administered by the professor, which could include failure for the course, and/or disciplinary action through the Rensselaer judicial process described in this handbook. Three relevant academic integrity violations to emphasize include:

Collaboration: Collaboration is defined as deliberately facilitating an act of academic dishonesty in any way or form; for example, allowing another student to observe an exam paper or allowing another student to “recycle” one’s old term paper or using one another’s work in a paper or lab report without citing it as another’s work.

Copying: Copying is defined as obtaining information pertaining to a graded exercise by deliberately observing the paper of another student; for example, noting which alternative a neighboring student has circled on a multiple-choice exam.

Plagiarism: Plagiarism is defined as representing the work or words of another as one’s own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference. Examples include using sentences verbatim from a published source in a term paper without appropriate referencing or presenting as one’s own the detailed argument of a published source, or presenting as one’s own electronically or digitally enhanced graphic representations from any form of media.

**Policy on Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) Tools**

Artificial intelligence (AI) tools can be useful in helping you gain different perspectives on the course’s content by providing information that supplements what is already provided as part of the course materials. The general policy for the use of AI tools in this course is that AI tools *may* be used for gathering information and locating new sources of information (you must directly cite the sources that were used), but ***may not*** be used to generate any content that is submitted by a student as part of an assignment. Every element of all assignments you submit for this course must be fully prepared by you, and consequently, the use of generative AI tools for any part of work that you submit will be treated as plagiarism. If you have any questions, please contact me.
