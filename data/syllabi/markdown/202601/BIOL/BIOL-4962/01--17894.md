## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course explores computational methods and their applications in various domains of biology and medicine. Aimed at undergraduate and graduate students, the course covers topics such as molecular docking, computational immunology, biomedical ontologies, knowledge graphs, image analysis, natural language processing, precision medicine, electronic health records, systems pharmacology, molecular dynamics simulations, data science, pharmacogenomics, and biological LLMs. Each session includes theoretical lectures and practical exercises that apply computational tools to real-world biological and clinical data.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • analyze and interpret diverse types of biological and clinical data using computational tools and techniques
- • apply natural language processing methods to extract and process information from biomedical literature and electronic health records
- • demonstrate proficiency in molecular docking, molecular dynamics simulations, and computational drug discovery methods
- • design and implement machine learning models, including encoders and decoders, for analyzing complex biological datasets
- • perform pathway analysis, functional enrichment, and network-based studies to understand biological processes and disease mechanisms
- • utilize CRISPR and other genome editing technologies to design experiments and predict off-target effects computationally
- • manage and analyze large-scale biological data using big data analytics and cloud computing resources
- • design and conduct independent computational biology research projects, including the formulation of hypotheses, data analysis, and interpretation of results
- • evaluate the ethical, legal, and social implications of computational biology research and data management practices

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
