## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The goal of this course is to gain practical experience with cellular and molecular biology through hands-on experimental techniques. The laboratory exercises are designed to illustrate current concepts in cellular and molecular biology. This is the laboratory course that accompanies the lecture BIOL-2120.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

None – laboratory manuals will be proved as PDFs on BIOL-2125 Lab LMS. A physical lab notebook will **not** be needed, but you **MUST bring your laptop to lab** to record that day's work in your digital notebook.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. The student will learn to successfully perform common techniques currently used in prokaryotic and eukaryotic cell biology research.
- 2\. The student will generate, analyze, and interpret data from experimental techniques.
- 3\. The student will demonstrate proper documentation of laboratory work by keeping a laboratory notebook.
- 4\. The student will demonstrate effective communication through the oral presentation of their work.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**ASSESSMENTS**

- **Pre-lab Questions**
  - ­ Pre-lab questions can be found on BIOL-2125 Lab LMS. These questions will also appear in your lab manual, but they **must be submitted to LMS for a grade**. These assignments must be submitted **before you meet for that week's lab**. Therefore, the due dates will be specific for each lab section.
- **Quizzes**
  - ­ There will be 3 lab quizzes held during the semester. All of these will be held on BIOL-2125 Lab LMS on predetermined dates and will be open for a period of time that should accommodate all students. These quizzes will not be held in person. Each quiz is timed**. Group work is NOT allowed on quizzes and the quizzes are NOT open book**. On the first academic integrity violation of any kind on a quiz, at a **minimum**, you will receive a 0 for the quiz, which cannot be dropped as the lowest quiz grade. In other words, of the two (out of three) quiz grades that are used to calculate your quiz average at the end of the semester, **one of the quiz grades will be a 0** and the other grade will be the highest of the two remaining quiz grades. A second offence, and in some cases even a first offence, will result in an **F for the course** and additional penalties described in the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities.
- **Notebook Checks**
  - ­ All students will keep a lab notebook. Your lab notebook will be provided to you as a Google Docs file that you can edit; physical notebooks will not be used. You **MUST bring your laptop to all in-person labs** for the purposes of recording that day's work. Notebook checks will be conducted during the weeks listed in the *Lab Syllabus*. You must export your Google Docs notebook as a PDF file and submit it to BIOL-2125 Lab LMS. More detailed instructions are given on LMS. Therefore, the due dates will be specific for each lab section, unless otherwise noted.

- **Student Presentation**
  - ­ Students will work as pairs during the semester; you will give a presentation at the end of the semester with your lab partner. The presentation will be based on the data that you collected during one of our lab sessions. A PDF version of your presentation slides must be submitted on LMS. The presentation will be **in-person** at the end of the semester.
- **Lab Citizenship**
  - ­ Good lab citizenship includes being on time, not eating or drinking in lab, proper sharing of instruments, maintaining a clean work space, following instructions, and social distancing. Violation of these rules or any rule listed under the *Lab safety* section will result in **loss of Lab Citizenship points**.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**ATTENDANCE POLICIES**

- Attendance is **REQUIRED** for all labs. **Unexcused absences are NOT allowed** - missing a lab will result in a **deduction of** **10 %** from your lab grade.
- If you miss a lab, you ***MUST*** obtain a Dean’s excuse. Students who need to report an illness should contact the Student Health Center via email or call 518-276-6287. Then, you must **submit this documentation to Dr. Bonocora by email and also notify your section instructor by email.**
- For any excused absence from lab, your lab work must still be made up. If you foresee yourself missing a lab, contact your lab TA and instructor well ahead of time. For any absence, it is your responsibility to coordinate a make-up time. Failure to make up lab work will result in the same penalty as missing the lab. When a make-up lab is not feasible (e.g., when quarantining), an online make-up lab will be offered.

### Other Course Policies:

**PRECAUTIONS FOR COVID-19 AND OTHER INFECTIOUS DISEASES**

- **Masks:** Wearing masks is encouraged but not required. In many cases, you will be working very close to other students for extended periods of time, so respiratory disease transmission is likely if you are unmasked. Cloth masks are not sufficient; you must at wear a surgical-style mask at the very least or, preferably, a N95/KN95 mask. Please wear your mask properly so it covers **both your mouth and nose**.
- **Cleaning of Spaces:** Students are required to disinfect their work surfaces in the lab **before** they begin work and **after** they have finished for the day. 70% ethanol will be provided for disinfection. Disinfecting lab chairs and stools is also encouraged. This is a standard lab policy in most biology labs.
- **Student Health: :** If you are ill, suspect you are ill, test positive for COVID-19 within the last 48 hours, or under quarantine for COVID-19, report this to the Student Health Center at (518) 276-6287 if you have not done so already.
- Refusal to comply with a reasonable request to limit the spread of disease will be treated as a classroom disruption and disciplinary actions and sanctions will be taken through the judicial process of the Student Handbook. This entails: request to change the behavior, request to leave the room, dismissal and referral to Student Life and the Dean of Students.

**LAB SAFETY**

- Follow all rules that your lab TA and instructor issue.
- Report any spills or accidents to your TA or instructor immediately.
- Do not engage in any activities that will endanger yourself or those around you.
- **Eating and drinking are not allowed in lab**. Do not bring water bottles to the lab; if you do, you will be asked to leave the bottle in the hallway.
- Wear clothing that entirely covers the legs and torso.
- You must wear shoes that **enclose the entire foot**; footwear like sandals and crocs are not allowed.
- Book bags, backpacks, and other personal items must be stored out of the way to avoid making your personal belongings a tripping hazard.
- **Wear gloves** when interacting with any laboratory equipment and **change gloves often**. You will be provided gloves.
- **You must wear safety glasses or goggles** at all times during lab except when using microscopes.
- When finished, you must clean your work area and return it to the condition you found it in; **disinfect your work areas with 70% ethanol**.
- Clean and disinfect eye pieces of microscopes with ethanol before and after use.
- Dispose of **biohazardous material**, **chemical hazards**, **glass**, and **metal sharps** in the designated locations.
- Violation of any rules stated here, in your lab manual, or verbally by your lab TA or instructor will impact your **Lab Citizenship grade**, and major violations can have you removed from the lab.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**GRADING POLICIES & ACADEMIC DISHONESTY**

- **Appealing a Grade**
  - ­ If you feel that the grade you received on an assessment is not justified, contact your section TA or instructor for clarification on your grade. If the issue is not resolved by your conversation with the TA, you can appeal your grade to Dr. Bonocora. You must submit justification ***in writing*** explaining the reasons why you believe your grade should be changed. You should submit your justification via email, and be sure to CC your TA/section instructor on this email.
- **Late Assessments**
  - ­ Late quizzes and presentations will **NOT** be accepted. Late pre-labs and notebook checks may be accepted for reduced credit: -10% after the first day, -20% after the second day, and not accepted after that.
- **Academic Integrity**
  - ­ Your notebook, all written answers on assessments, and your presentation must be in your own words. Plagiarism of any kind will NOT be tolerated.
  - ­ Collaborating with students on non-group activities like quizzes and pre-labs is NOT allowed.
  - ­ Do not pass out information about a quiz that other students have yet to take, and similarly, do not request information from other students about a quiz you have not yet taken.
  - ­ For online assignments/quizzes the use of websites and services including, but not limited to, CHEGG, Quizlet, etc… or AI assistance such as ChatGPT for the assignment is considered cheating.
  - ­ If a student is found to be violating any rules listed here or listed in the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities (https://rpi.app.box.com/s/p1b39vybm914b5cao4csrgl84bfrrh2l), The instructors reserve the right to issue a score of **0 for the assessment and an** **F for your course grade!** These policies also apply to students who assist others in cheating. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assessment, please ask for clarification.
  - ­ Any act of academic dishonesty will be **reported to the Dean of Students**. Institutional consequences for academic dishonesty are spelled out in the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities. This handbook also defines various forms of academic dishonesty; you should make yourself familiar with these.
