## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is an introductory course in cell structure and function. It is intended for Biology and Biomedical Engineering majors and covers such topics as chemical composition of cells, molecular structure function of organelles, membrane transport, and the biochemistry underlying cellular behavior including cancer cells.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand and properly use scientific vocabulary common for cell and molecular biology.
- Demonstrate understanding of prokaryotic and eukaryotic cell structure and function.
- Describe the structure and function of the biomolecules found in all living organisms.
- Describe the synthesis of DNA, RNA, and proteins.
- Explain the processes involved with gene expression.
- Explain the processes by which animals acquire nutrients and eliminate wastes.
- Explain the structure and function of membranes and intracellular compartments.
- Link modern advances in cell and molecular biology to a better understanding of diseases.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

4 tests, 1 cumulative final exam, and homework are used to assess students. Reading the textbook, coming to lecture and asking questions, and doing the homework gives students the best learning experience.

**DESCRIPTION OF ASSESSMENTS**

Cheating on any of the following will result in severe penalties; see *Course Policies* § *Academic Integrity*.

- **Exams**
  - ­ There will be **five exams**: four unit exams and a comprehensive final exam. All exams are equally weighted. The exam with the **lowest score will be dropped** at the end of the semester. Thus, the final exam can be treated as optional – if you are satisfied with your course grade and choose to skip the final exam, a score of 0 will appear for this exam on LMS and dropped as your lowest exam grade.
  - ­ Exams will consist of a combination of multiple choice and essay questions.
  - ­ Exams are administered through LMS **in-person**; you MUST bring your **fully charged laptop** to class and connect to the RPI network via WiFi to take the exam. **YOU CANNOT USE A VPN** when connecting to the RPI network; any exams that appear to be submitted from outside the RPI network or from any location other than the proctored testing space will receive a 0. **You must have WebEx running with your camera active during the entire exam.** You can use a USB webcam if your laptop doesn't have a built-in webcam.
  - ­ Exams are timed; you will have **60 mins for Unit Exams** and **90** **mins for the Final Exam**. You must have a stable WiFi connection to receive your full time.
  - ­ If you have testing accommodations, you must inform Dr. Klein at least **two weeks** **before** the first exam is held. You must have the office of Disability Services for Students (DSS) email a **PDF** of your accommodations memorandum to Dr. Klein (see *Accommodations for Students with Disabilities*).

The format of a make-up exam may **differ significantly** from the standard exams. Make-up exams **cannot be given more than 1 week** past the original exam date. No makeup exam can be given for the Final Exam.
- **Homework Assignments**
  - ­ Pre-class and post-class homework assignments for each lecture will be administered via **SmartWork**. A link to these assignments can be found on LMS.
  - ­ Pre-class assignments should be completed before the corresponding lecture, but there is no penalty if these assignments are completed after the lecture so long as they are submitted before the due date. You are responsible for submitting assignments in a timely manner; **late assignments CANNOT be accepted under any circumstances**. Due dates are shown on the SmartWork website.
  - ­ Assignments are weighted by the number of points available in each assignment; some assignments will have more questions or multi-part questions that are worth more points. E.g., an assignment worth 16 points is weighted exactly double an assignment worth 8 points. All assignments will be averaged together based on this weighting and reported to LMS as a rolling average.
- **Extra Credit**
  - ­ Post-class discussions will be held on LMS under the heading “Extra Credit Discussions.” Only meaningful contributions will be rewarded with extra credit at the end the semester. - ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

- ­ All assessments for this course are done electronically, either through LMS for exams and extra credit discussion or through SmartWork for homework assignments. The nature of online assessments can temp some students to cheat, abusing the trust placed in students by Dr. Klein and your TA’s. Cheating is considered to include, but is not limited to, the following:
  - » Collaborating with other students on exam and homework questions,
  - » Passing exam answers or information about exam questions to or from other students,
  - » Making transcripts or copies of exams,
  - » Submitting answers to free-response questions in words other than your own (i.e., plagiarism),
  - » Accessing any materials outside of the exam for help with exam questions; this includes your textbook, lecture slides, websites, and AI like ChatGPT,
  - » Defeating or attempting to defeat timed exam lockouts on LMS to gain extra time,
  - » Attempting to take an in-person exam in any location other than the proctored exam space.
- ­ If a student is found to be cheating, Dr. Klein reserves the right to issue a score of **0 for the assessment** and an **F for your course grade**. These policies also apply to students who assist others in cheating. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assessment, please ask for clarification.
- ­ Any act of academic dishonesty will be **reported to the Dean of Students**. Institutional consequences for academic dishonesty are spelled out in the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities (https://rpi.app.box.com/s/3f1cl3w1lt1mq8gqla68v6uk3vh1c19f). This handbook also defines various forms of academic dishonesty; you should make yourself familiar with these.
- ­ In the unlikely event an exam must be held remotely, the exam will differ significantly from an in-person exam and is considered an “open-book” assessment where your textbook and lecture slides can be used to help answer questions. However, this does not mean you can copy answers from your textbook or anywhere else or get help from anyone else; all answers must be **in your own words**.
