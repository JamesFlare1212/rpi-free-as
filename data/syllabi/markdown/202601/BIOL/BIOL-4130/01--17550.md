## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Syllabus: BIOL 4130 Biomedical Research

**Spring Semester 2026 – 6 credit hours**

**Wednesdays 9-11 AM: didactic portion at AMC (MS 103 EXCEPT WHERE NOTED!)**

**Expectation of 12 hrs/week in lab**

This course is ‘Communication Intensive’ according to Institute definitions and is the ‘Culminating Experience’ for students in the Physician Scientist combined degree program.

**Course Directors**

Sarah McCallum, PhD – Associate Professor Dana Crawford, PhD – Associate Professor

Office: ME 508 Albany Medical College Office: J411 Albany Medical College

Telephone: 518 330-6977 (cell) Telephone: 518-262-6652 (office)

E-mail: mccalls@amc.edu Email: crawfod@amc.edu

**Texts**

- Students should read: *A Funny Thing Happened on the Way to Stockholm* by Dr Robert Lefkowitz **Due 14 Jan 2026 (Available as used copy or Amazon Kindle; ebooks**

##### **Component 1: Something of Interest** (Due: Individually according to Class Schedule; Due Dates: varied. **MAKEUP OPTIONS – MUST DISCUSS WITH DR. McCALLUM**

Discuss a topic of interest to the medical/research community in a 20 minute presentation with 10 minutes of discussion/Q&A. Presentations should include discussion of research related to the topic (NOT the student’s own project). See attached Description for further details.

*Desired Learning Outcomes: Students will be more aware of issues in the field of medicine. Students will develop mutual respect and consideration of widely disparate views of current issues. Students will be proficient at making professional presentations.*

Assessment: See the attached grading rubric (SOI).

**Component 2: Literature Review Assignment (Due Feb 11th)**

This exercise will involve both a literature review in the field relating to student’s project, identification of 1-2 seminal works from this field, and a written (3-5 pages, single spaced) analysis of how more current research in the field relates to the original work.

*Desired Learning Outcomes*: *demonstrated use of Pubmed or Web of Science and Endnote; critical analysis of relevant work in student’s field.*

Assessment: see the attached grading rubric (literature review)

**Component 3: Significance section of your research project (Feb 27th, March 11, March 18)**

3.1 Written Significance/Innovation section of final proposal. Students will prepare the Significance and Innovation section of the Research Proposal (see Component 5). Approximately one page in length, the section should also contain a bibliography of sources cited. The bibliography must be generated by EndNote and EndNote library sent. (**Written document due: Friday Feb 27 at 10pm)**

3.2 Oral presentation. Students explain the significance of their research project and present a brief (5 minute) overview **in class** on March 11 or March 18.

*Desired Learning Outcome**: Students will engage their research mentor in detailed discussions of the significance of their project. Students will demonstrate knowledge of their project. Students will describe the clinical and basic science significance of their project and articulate how their project fits in to the overall goals of their mentor’s laboratory research. Students will be able to explain the significance of the project that other students are doing.*

Assessment: See the attached rubric. Written section will be assessed, and feedback provided, but not graded.

**Component 4: Hypothesis and Specific Aims** (**Due: March 30**)

Students will state (and project) their hypothesis and specific aims in class and the class will critique it. Discussion will focus on the clarity and testability of the hypothesis and aims.

*Desired Learning Outcome: Each student will have a clear and testable hypothesis and be able to analyze the hypothesis and Aims of other students.*

Assessment will be by peer feedback

**Component 5: Research Proposal** (**Due April 27)**

Students will submit a research proposal in the form of an NIH grant application, using PHS398 forms and guidelines.

*Desired Learning Outcome: Students will generate a proposal that will guide their research throughout the summer. Students will utilize the form and content of an NIH-style research proposal.*

*Assessment: Full proposal is graded, for this course, as a submission from an undergraduate student. Assessment will be according the process described for NIH proposals. As a part of the Summer of Research and associated activities, Students will also get detailed feedback in the form of an NIH-style Summary Statement.*

**Component 6: Research**

The didactic portion of this class meets 9:00 AM to 11:00 AM on Wednesdays in MS 103 (occasionally by Webex or in a different classroom- see **Schedule**). Students will spend the rest of Wednesday (to 5:00 pm), and Tuesdays and/or or Fridays in the laboratory, to equate to 12 hours of laboratory research per week. Students’ research efforts will be evaluated twice during the semester by their mentor using the attached rubric.

*Desired Learning Outcome: Students will become familiar with the mentor’s laboratory and be up-to-speed to begin the summer session. Students will take ownership of their research project. Students will learn techniques, procedures, and whatever the mentor deems appropriate for the amount of time available, all preparatory to engaging in research full-time in the summer.*

Assessment: See the attached mentor evaluation rubric.

**Component 7: Professional Development: Preparing for Medical School** (Lectures, class participation required)

A series of topics designed to help students prepare for their imminent future.

Topics include:

- Book club – review of *A Funny Thing Happened on the Way to Stockholm*
- Literature searching/citations using EndNote- Traci Tosh, AMC Library
- Publishing and authorship
- Transitioning to Albany Medical College/MDDR (led by medical students)

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Demonstrate effective written and oral communication of biomedical research. 2. Develop and articulate a research question or hypothesis (with mentor support) addressing a significant problem in biomedical research. 3. Describe and prepare the key components of an NIH style grant proposal including Specific Aims, Significance, Innovation and Approach 4. Learn how to give and receive peer review feedback

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1. Demonstrate effective written and oral communication of biomedical research.
2. Develop and articulate a research question or hypothesis (with mentor support) addressing a significant problem in biomedical research.
3. Describe and prepare the key components of an NIH style grant proposal including Specific Aims, Significance, Innovation and Approach
4. Learn how to give and receive peer review feedback

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

20% Research (evaluated by mentors at mid-semester and end of semester)

30% Presentations (Spec Aims, Hypothesis, Significance, Something of Interest) and Class Participation

30% Research proposal

20% Literature review exercise and paper
