## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Measurement and characterization of earthquake ground motion and structural response in time and frequency domains. Linear and nonlinear response history and response spectrum analysis of structures subject to earthquake loading. Seismic analysis and design of structural systems using building codes and design specifications.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Dynamics of Structures: Theory and Applications to Earthquake Engineering, A.K. Chopra, Sixth Edition, Pearson, 2023. ISBN-13: 9780137602520

## Course Content

### Content Details:

| 1 | Measuring earthquake ground motion and structural response. |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | Characterization of earthquake ground motion and structural response in both the time and frequency domain; Use of SeismoSoft for ground motion characterization. |
| 3 | Response history and response spectrum analysis of linear SDOF systems subjected to earthquakes; Elastic design response spectrum; Seismic response analysis of linear SDOF systems using MATLAB and SAP2000. |
| 4 | Response history and response spectrum analysis of nonlinear SDOF systems subjected to earthquakes; Inelastic design response spectrum; Seismic response analysis of nonlinear SDOF systems using MATLAB and SAP2000. |
| 5 | Equation of motion for MDOF systems subjected to earthquakes. |
| 6 | Modal response history (exact) and response spectrum (approximate) analysis of linear MDOF systems subjected to earthquake ground motion; Seismic response analysis of linear MDOF systems using SAP2000. |
| 7 | Direct response history analysis of MDOF linear and nonlinear systems subjected to earthquake ground motion. Seismic response analysis of nonlinear MDOF systems using SAP2000. |
| 8 | Earthquake response of inelastic buildings. |
| 9 | Simplified seismic analysis in building codes (equivalent lateral force procedure for regular buildings; accounting for ductility and overstrength; predicting inelastic displacement demand; evaluation of P-delta effect) |
| 10 | Steel-framed building configurations for effective earthquake resistance. |
| 11 | Design of steel special moment frames for seismic resistance. |
| 12 | Introduction to seismic design for multiple hazard levels and multiple performance levels (i.e., performance-based seismic design); Introduction to pushover analysis for seismic performance assessment. |

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Describe how earthquake ground motion and structural response are measured and characterized.
- Determine the response of structures subjected to earthquakes using various methods of analysis, including code-based procedures.
- Use commercial software for seismic response analysis.
- Describe how structural systems resist seismic load.
- Design components of a steel framing system to resist seismic load.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Full participation in each class is likely to be strongly correlated with course grades. Class participation will include students presenting homework solutions to the class and engaging in discussions about the homework.  
Students are expected to attend all classes. If a student does not attend class, it is the responsibility of the student to notify the instructor in advance and to provide justification. Every student is allowed a maximum of two automatically-excused absences for medical reasons, as long as the instructor was notified in advance and justification was provided. Students missing more than two classes due to illness are required to provide a doctor’s note or other supporting documentation to the Student Success Office. The Dean of Students will communicate the receipt of the note (with expected end date) to the instructor. For all absences that are not due to medical reasons, students can request an excused absence from the instructor by providing a valid reason and supporting documentation to the instructor. In such cases, and based on the provided information, the instructor will determine whether the student will be excused from class.

### Other Course Policies:

Missing the final exam will result in a grade of zero unless permission is granted before the scheduled exam time. If a valid reason exists that prevents a student from taking the final exam, it is the responsibility of the student to submit a request to make up the final exam to the Dean of Students. If the Dean approves their request, the grade of the student for the course would then become NE (Not Examined) and they would have to take a make-up final exam next semester by a specified date.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational process.  
The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and the Graduate Student Supplement to Handbook define various forms of academic dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. These documents are available through the RPInfo website: https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs. Students should be aware that the penalties for academic dishonesty, as described in these documents, can be quite severe.  
In this class, all solutions that are developed by a student must represent the student’s own work, although, after attempting to solve problems on their own, students are encouraged to discuss their work with other classmates to help each other arrive at the correct solution or to check their solution (see item #2 under Homework Policy). Development of a solution that is in violation of this policy (e.g., one student copying work done by another student or a student copying from a previously developed solution including a solution from a previous semester or a solution that is contained within a solution manual) will result in a course grade reduction of up to one full letter grade (e.g., from B to C). In addition, subsequent work done by the student will be scrutinized to evaluate potential cheating. Finally, the Dean of Students, who maintains records of academic dishonesty, will be notified. Additional disciplinary actions may be taken by the Dean of Students.  
In this class, all exams must represent the student’s own work. If cheating occurs during an exam, the student will receive a grade of zero for that exam. In addition, and in accordance with the Handbook, the Dean of Students Office will be notified of the incident of academic dishonesty. Additional disciplinary actions may be taken by the Dean of Students. In addition, all mobile/electronic devices (cell/smart phones, computers, etc.) must be stored securely away during an exam and are not to be used unless specifically directed otherwise by the instructor. Any use of, or interaction with, a mobile/electronic device during an exam without explicit permission of the instructor will be interpreted as the illicit transfer of exam data and thus will be considered an act of cheating.
