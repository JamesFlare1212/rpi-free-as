## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Open-ended design project in which students work in teams. Oral presentations and written reports cover alternates considered, design assumptions, cost, safety, and feasibility. This is a writing-intensive course.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The primary motivation of this course is for students to learn about the processes of civil engineering design through working on a practical design problem. The course is intended to integrate skills learned over the entire undergraduate curriculum and not necessarily to learn new engineering techniques. The course is intended to improve students’ skills in project planning, teamwork, problem solving, communication, and presentation. This course is not only required as part of the civil engineering curriculum, but it is also the only course in the civil engineering curriculum which fulfills the School of Engineering communications intensive course requirement.

The specific objectives and outcomes of the course are as follows. Students will:

- Further develop design skills, especially the application of engineering standards and strengthen problem solving skills
- Develop written and oral communication skills, in particular the ability to communicate technical material to a variety of audiences in a variety of media
- Work effectively in an environment where they work in multi-disciplinary teams to integrate prior course work and skills to solve problems taken from a "real world" context
- Be able to incorporate and apply current technology used in the practice of their discipline
- Understand their professional and ethical responsibility

In the course, students will be assessed according to the following ABET outcomes:

- an ability to identify, formulate, and solve complex engineering problems by applying principles of engineering, science, and mathematics
- an ability to apply engineering design to produce solutions that meet specified needs with consideration of public health, safety, and welfare, as well as global, cultural, social, environmental, and economic factors
- an ability to communicate effectively with a range of audiences
- an ability to recognize ethical and professional responsibilities in engineering situations and make informed judgments, which must consider the impact of engineering solutions in global, economic, environmental, and societal contexts
- an ability to function effectively on a team whose members together provide leadership, create a collaborative and inclusive environment, establish goals, plan tasks, and meet objectives
- an ability to acquire and apply new knowledge as needed, using appropriate learning strategies

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Students will collectively work on developing a site to be determined. There will be about 7 “building teams” of 6 members each (4 structural, 2 geotechnical students) to work on a specific building.

There will be 3 “non-building teams” that will assist and coordinate with the building teams. The transportation team will work on site design, circulation, parking, trip generation and traffic flow. There will be two transportation sub-teams – one for physical design and the other for systems design such as traffic engineering, highway capacity, etc. The water resources team will work on site drainage and water supply.

Teams will be assigned by the instructors on the basis of skill distribution.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Apply civil engineering design principles and standards to develop integrated, realistic solutions to an open-ended, real-world infrastructure project considering safety, constructability, cost, and societal context.
- Function effectively on multidisciplinary teams by planning work, coordinating tasks, assuming leadership and support roles, and delivering project objectives within schedule and quality constraints.
- Communicate technical information professionally and effectively through written reports, oral presentations, posters, and meetings tailored to diverse audiences including engineers, faculty, and external practitioners.
- Incorporate modern engineering tools, data, and technologies appropriate to professional civil engineering practice in the analysis, design, and evaluation of systems and components.
- Demonstrate professional and ethical responsibility by exercising sound engineering judgment, engaging with public processes, and reflecting on the broader environmental, economic, and societal impacts of engineering decisions.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Student attainment of the course learning outcomes will be assessed using the following measures:

1. **Engineering Design and Problem Solving**
   - Project Proposal
   - Midterm Report
   - Final Report
   - Specialty Design Assignments
   - Evaluation of calculations, design criteria tables, and compliance with applicable codes and standards
2. **Teamwork and Project Management**
   - Weekly Project Reports and Meeting Minutes
   - Instructor and TA observations during progress meetings
   - Mid-semester and final individual and group peer assessments
   - Professional Practice grade
3. **Written Communication**
   - Project Proposal
   - Weekly Progress Reports
   - Midterm Report (individually authored sections)
   - Final Report (individually authored sections)
   - Public Hearing Report
4. **Oral and Visual Communication**
   - Midterm Oral Presentation
   - Final Oral Presentation
   - Final Poster Presentation
   - Informal progress briefings with instructors and mentors
5. **Use of Modern Engineering Tools and Technology**
   - Application of analysis software, design aids, and project management tools documented in reports and reviewed during presentations
6. **Professional and Ethical Responsibility**
   - Professional Practice assessment
   - Public Hearing assignment
   - Adherence to academic integrity policies
   - Quality, professionalism, and accountability demonstrated across all submissions and interactions

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students are reminded: "RPI expects the highest standards of honesty and integrity in the academic performance of its students. Any act of scholastic dishonesty is regarded as a serious offense, which may result in expulsion. RPI defines scholastic dishonesty as submission of false records of academic achievement; cheating on assignments or examinations; plagiarizing; altering, forging, or misusing an academic record; taking, acquiring, or using test materials without faculty permission; acting alone or in cooperation with another to obtain dishonestly grades, honors, awards, or professional endorsement. Aiding and abetting an act of scholastic dishonesty is also considered a serious offense."

The course project is assigned to student teams collectively; however, there will be individual assessments of performance, particularly in the area of communication. The level of effort among all team members should be comparable. Students who are not active participants in their group as characterized by things such as consistently missing team meetings, not adhering to deadlines, etc. may be considered in violation of academic integrity standards. Students will be required to assess the performance and contribution of individual team members in their projects.
