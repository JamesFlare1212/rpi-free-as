## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Water Resources Engineering provides a foundational treatment of the design of systems to control the quantity, timing, and distribution of water to meet the needs of human habitation and the environment within the Civil and Environmental Engineering curriculum at Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute. Topics covered include the hydrological cycle, design criteria for water distribution systems and water storage reservoirs, pump performance, applied pipe flow analysis, and pipe networks. Open channel hydraulics is also strongly featured, including the performance of hydraulic control structures such as weirs, bumps, and contractions. Theory is strongly complemented with experimental laboratory sessions. Contemporary and social and environmental issues are introduced at various points throughout the term including equity issues related to infrastructure, green infrastructure, and climate change.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Describe and analyze the major components, operating principles, and performance criteria of engineered water resources systems, including water distribution systems, open-channel flow systems, and hydraulic structures.
- 2\. Explain how social, economic, environmental, and regulatory considerations influence water resources engineering decisions and system performance.
- 3\. Develop models of water resources systems (pipes, pumps, pipe networks, and channels) by applying principles of conservation of mass, energy, and momentum.
- 4\. Apply concepts of specific energy, gradually varied flow, and conservation principles to compute flow rates, water surface elevations, and system responses, and to interpret corresponding laboratory observations.
- 5\. Use industry-standard computational tools to simulate water resources systems and evaluate system behavior under varying operating and boundary conditions.
- 6\. Formulate and solve open-ended water resources engineering problems by selecting appropriate physical models, assumptions, and governing equations, and by evaluating the accuracy and limitations of the resulting solutions. (ABET 3.1)
- 7\. Communicate water resources analyses and design decisions effectively through professional-quality written reports, figures, and tables appropriate for both technical and non-technical audiences. (ABET 3.3)
- 8\. Evaluate water resources engineering solutions in the context of ethical responsibility and societal, environmental, and economic impacts, and justify recommendations using engineering evidence. (ABET 3.4)
- 9\. Function effectively on an engineering team by contributing to planning, task execution, and decision-making, and by reflecting on team and individual performance. (ABET 3.5)

**Homework Presentation:** Layout specifications:

1. Include a clear header on the first page with: name, homework number, date, and page numbers. Number pages in the format A/B (e.g., 2/6).
2. Use ruled engineering paper (physical or digital). Start each problem on a new page.
3. Show work in a logical order so another engineer can follow it. Use this structure:

o Given data / diagram

o Required results

o ------- (separator line)

o Calculations / diagram

o Final answer (boxed)

4. Show steps (no big jumps). For repetitive calculations, one fully worked sample plus a table is acceptable.
5. Box final answers and include correct units on intermediate and results.
