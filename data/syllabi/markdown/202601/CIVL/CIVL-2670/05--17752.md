## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Introduction to the elastic behavior of structural components. Analysis of statically determinate systems. Deflection calculations by virtual work and elastic load methods. Analysis of simple statically indeterminate structures. Influence lines. Interaction of structural components. Typical structural engineering loads.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The objective of this course is to develop a working knowledge of the basic principles of structural behavior, modeling, and analysis. Students will do this by building on the knowledge gained through statics (ENGR 1100) and strength of materials (ENGR 2530).

Students will gain a basic understanding of:

- structural elements and systems
- load paths and anticipated structural behavior
- typical structural loads
- commercial structural analysis tools

Upon successful completion of this course, students will have the necessary skills to take advanced structural engineering courses such as Matrix Structural Analysis (CIVL 4440), Steel Design (CIVL 4070), Concrete Design (CIVL 4080), and graduate courses such as Structural Dynamics (CIVL 6450), Wind Engineering (CIVL 6390), and Earthquake Engineering (CIVL 6490).

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Sketch deformed shapes of determinate and indeterminate structures and compute or estimate internal force and moment distributions
- Compute (by hand and via software) external reactions, internal forces, and deflections for statically determinate structures
- Construct unit influence lines for statically determinate structures and utilize them to predict maximum load effects on structures
- Predict the response form of structural elements: axial (tension/compression) and/or shear and bending
- Explain the importance of and determine load paths through a structure
- Perform simple experiments using responsible data management practices
- Develop simple experimental procedures in a laboratory setting
- Critique the role of civil engineers in society

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Participation requires, at a minimum, consistent attendance at both lecture and lab sessions. Participation may be demonstrated through engagement in in-class discussions or via submission (**on Gradescope**) of in-class assignments/unannounced quizzes (~10-15 minutes). There will be no make-up for assignments during a class session.A grade of zero will be given for each missing in-class work. Also, students are encouraged to attend the instructor’s and/or TA’s office hours as needed. Please silence all cell phones and refrain from using laptops during class unless otherwise requested.

### Other Course Policies:

## Laboratory Sessions

Schedule

Unless directed otherwise, students are expected to arrive promptly in the Structures Laboratory (JEC 1042F) during their assigned sessions for lab. These sessions will be used for experimentation with structural models. Students must wear closed-toed shoes and proper PPE while in the lab.

Lab Reports

Three sessions will be dedicated to laboratory experiments. Unless directed otherwise, all students are required to record their own data and submit individual lab reports. Copying or falsifying data will be considered a breach of academic integrity and subject to appropriate consequences. The reports are due at the start of the lab session *on the week shown in the syllabus schedule*, typically one to two weeks after the experiment.

## Homework Policies

**Presentation**

Submitted scanned homework (**on Gradescope**) should be presented in a professional format (i.e., legible work that is presented in a logical order such that it can be readily followed). See detailed "Homework Requirements" posted on LMS. If a problem is illegible or is not presented in a logical order that can be readily followed or does not substantially follow the guidelines in the "Homework Requirements", the problem will be regarded as not completed and will be assigned a grade of zero.

**Lateness**

Homework submissions are typically due at the end of the day (11.59 pm) on Fridays of the week. Late homework will be penalized by **10% each day and will not be accepted after three days**. Students who experience any emergency preventing them from submitting the homework on time should contact the instructor.

**Grading**

Homework assignments are intended for students to practice the skills necessary for successful understanding of the course content. On each homework assignment, some problems will be graded for accuracy while others will only be graded for completeness. The distinction between these problems will not be announced ahead of time. Students are strongly encouraged to complete *all* problems on each assignment.

**Collaboration**

Students are *strongly* encouraged to solve the homework problems through collaboration with classmates (after trying the problems themselves); however, each student must turn in their own work. Copying among students is not acceptable, and if it occurs, all students involved will be assigned a grade of zero for the assignment.
