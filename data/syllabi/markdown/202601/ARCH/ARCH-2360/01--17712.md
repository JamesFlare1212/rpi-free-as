## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

An exploration of the fundamental principles of human physiology as it relates to the built environment through the perspective of thermal and luminous comfort, building energy systems and indoor air and material quality. Emphasis is on bioclimatic and psychrometric climate analysis and its relationship to architectural design, understanding the energy exchange between body in space, the natural meaning of enclosures, and nonstructural materials and systems. The focus is on passive heating, cooling, and daylighting systems and their design. Exercises include vital sign analysis of existing spaces (thermal, air, luminous), forming hypotheses of building performance, using scientific instrumentation, tenant survey techniques, and physical modeling and simulation techniques related to daylighting and shading techniques.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Outcome 1 - Simulating Environmental Principles in Architecture: Students will demonstrate broad knowledge of the principles, methodologies, and terminology of ecology and sustainability as they apply to architectural design. Additional CLOs for Graduate students: In addition to the outcome described above, graduate students will produce iterative models benchmarking a performance goal and demonstrating improvements when compared to the baseline model
- Outcome 2 - Design Application and Representation: Students will be able to integrate environmental principles and passive systems introduced in course lecture materials and assignments into their studio design work through text, schematic drawings/diagrams, and other media. Additional CLOs for Graduate students: In addition to the outcome described above, graduate students will prepare a written narrative describing the application of passive design strategies and associated tradeoff decisions
- Outcome 3 - Thermodynamics in Buildings: Students will be able to identify and apply fundamental principles of thermodynamics and and how they affect architectural design and building performance including passive conservation, thermal mass, controlling solar heat gain and passive cooling/ventilation
- Outcome 4 - Daylighting and Shading Principles: Students will be able to identify and apply fundamental principles of daylighting and architectural shading and and how they affect architectural design and building performance.
- Outcome 5 - Humand Comfort: Students will be able to identify and apply fundamental principles of human comfort including quantifiable metrics, their relationship to comfort thresholds and passive strategies to improve occupant comfort
- Outcome 6 - Building Health: Students will be able to identify and apply fundamental principles of material and indoor air hygeine as they relate to occupants and greater environmental impacts.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Assignments, Quizzes and formal exams will be issued as per the course schedule and count towards the final class grade as per the course learning outcome matrix. Below is a summary of the intent of each assessment measure:

**Assignments:** Consist of software-specific analysis exercises based on software tutorials provided in class and on posted in LMS. Individual assignment briefs will be provided for each assignment with specific deliverables and grading criteria.

**Quizzes:** Consist of 5 multiple-choice, true/false or multiple answer questions based on lecture content outlined in the schedule. All quizzes will be taken and graded through LMS.

**Exams:** Final Exam will be issued consisting of 25 multiple-choice, true/false or multiple answer questions. The exams are based on cumulative semester content.

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance will be taken during each class and counts towards your final class grade. Please see the Assessment Measures section for assessment breakdown. More than three unexcused absences will result in a failing grade for the course.

To qualify as an excused absence, the student should contact their section instructor prior to the missed class or shortly after with the reason for their absence. It is up to the discretion of the instructor to accept or deny the excuse. Notes from the Student Health center or doctor are not required.

All lecture slides and tutorial recordings will be posted to the LMS and it is the student’s responsibility to review all content in preparation for course assessments.

Attendance grades will be issued based on the following metrics:

0 absences 100%

1 absence 95%

2 absences 85%

3 absences 65%

3+ absences (F) course failure

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
