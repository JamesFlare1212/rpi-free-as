## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The seminar pursues the difficult theorization of what is architecture through the unfolding and discussion of the following subjects.  
000 State of things  
001 Several against (and one toward)  
002 Vitalist materialism (the organization of material life)  
003 Absolute indifference (architecture is like the sea)  
004 What is the virtual?  
005 Consistency  
006 The machinic  
007 Constructivism  
008 A self-superseding method  
009 Over-determinacy as determinable engine of indeterminacy  
010 Convolution versus revolution  
011 Systematization of sameness and of difference  
012 Regimes of difference  
013 Kinds and degrees  
014 Variables, variations, variabilities, varieties  
015 Ranges and thresholds  
016 Material as sets of restrictions  
017 Singularity and beyond  
018 Generality through intense specificity (sublime)  
019 Ever complexifying complexity  
020 Complex synthesis and oxymoronic simplicity  
021 Conservativism and containment of potential  
022 Models as systems with a form  
023 Idealizations versus idealities  
024 The architectural model (models versus projects)  
025 Worlds of their own  
026 Models (architecture as medium of thought)  
027 Order, multiplicity and singularity  
028 The grace of abstract figures and the unfathomability of oxymoronic concepts  
029 Construction of values and the making of sense  
030 Routines, rituals, machinations, myths  
031 A joyful approach to culture  
032 Tempestuous professionalism  
Classes consist of lectures and debates. Work consists of the participation through questions during the seminars and of the writing of four quotes each class.  
Final submission is a 2500-word essay unfolding them.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The procedure will produce a mosaic of elemental projects out of a chosen historical architectural material progressively mixed with 90 other built or unbuilt projects, which will be selected from an extensive list of canonical projects of the past five centuries. Through the use of blending tools, divergent directions of the chosen project will be recognized, sharpened, singularized, and synthesized conceptually, thus expanding the spectrum of architecture culture by means of versions of a historical canon. The course will work in four stages: 01 investigation and construction of an archive of images, 02 description of the material and formulation of a general hypothesis, 03 development and specification of blends, 04 definition of ideas, rewriting of hypothesis, and final presentation. These stages will mutually redefine each other as they unfold along the duration of the seminar.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 01 Research 01a_Ability to use/develop specialized knowledge Students enter in direct interaction with a panorama of contemporary architectural theory, from post-modernism to the present times. This is assured by their active attendance to a series of lectures at each class, which includes a presentation by the seminar professor, followed by a debate articulated by the construction of questions. An anthology of quotes out of those lectures and debates, the introduction of relevant bibliography and iconography in real time, and complementary readings enable students to augment their disciplinary baggage and develop a consistent disciplinary position.
- 01 Research 01b_Ability to use / develop broad and integrative knowledge Students are challenged to bring into the discussion personal experiences coming both from their design studios and seminars and from real-life experiences, mostly related to various forms of art (particularly visual arts, music, and cinema) and architecture, which articulate the problems introduced by the debates with a larger contemporary cultural frame. The expansion of the bibliography and iconography to other cultural forms in real time, and the introduction of complementary sources from other artistic disciplines enable students to augment their cultural baggage and mature their cultural position.
- 02 Intellectual skills 02a_Ability to think architecture with theoretical maturity Students are conceptually nurtured to develop an ability to ground design decisions on a hands-on approach to architecture theory as a quotidian material. This approach is articulated by visually and conceptually engaging with the theoretical knowledge embedded in contemporary architectural projects and writings.
- 02 Intellectual skills 02b_Ability to think architecture with communicative fluency Students are challenged to fluidly communicate with (and be influenced by) different interlocutors at different stages of the course, from architectural theoreticians through their writings to student colleagues in a speculative discussion.
- 03 Execution 03a_Seminar classes Students demonstrate the ability to implement the learning outcomes through an iterative process that repeats the construction of questions in real time and the construction of an anthology of quotes. These articulate their process of intellectual growth and configure the scaffolding of an essay on contemporary architecture culture.
- 03 Execution 03b_Final Essay Students demonstrate the ability to implement the learning outcomes through the writing of a 2500-word essay on contemporary disciplinary culture. Essays are expected to not simply illustrate a certain topic in general terms, but to construct the reasoning behind a specific position regarding a concrete problem, in which they take active part.

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance**

Attendance to classes is required at all class meetings during the semester and part of the final assessment. Note that according to the Institute Policy, two (2) unexcused absences from class will result in the lowering of the grade by one (1) letter. Four (4) unexcused absences from class during the term result in an automatic F. Whether an absence (health, immediate family death, officially approved field / sport trip, etc.) is excused or unexcused is determined by the Students Experience Office (4th Floor Academy Hall, x8022, se@rpi.edu), upon presentation of required documentation, according to established policies and procedures (see http://se.rpi.edu/policies/ea/).

Students are required to work in the Seminar space during Seminar hours and have the required materials and basic tools to develop the assignments. Work includes participation in classes, pinups, presentations, and discussions. Failure to work in class with undivided attention and disruptive behavior (tardiness, leaving early, lack of participation, general socializing, etc.), as well working on assignments from other classes during class, will count as absences.

**Excused Absence policy**

There is no need for a medical excuse to be provided for absences of up to a week. Students missing more than a week of classes due to illness will be required to provide a doctor’s note or other supporting documentation to the Student Success Office. The Class Dean will communicate the receipt of the note (with expected end date) to the relevant faculty. Students are responsible for completing any work that they might miss – including assignments, quizzes, labs, exams, etc. – due to illness, quarantine/isolation, or other circumstances.

Students who adhere to these processes should not be penalized per the attendance policy for the course and as detailed in the course syllabus. Instructors should make every effort to work with and provide reasonable modifications for students who cannot attend class due to illness to complete assignments and exams. It is recognized that virtual learning may not be possible in some in-person laboratory and performing arts courses.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Discussions and pinups**

Students are regularly required to present questions as a formal routine. Presentations and questions are a critical part of the architectural education. They are opportunities to judge the work and critically evaluate the study process. It may be appropriate at points in the semester to evaluate only selected works to convey information relevant to the entire class. In this situation, it is important for each student to witness and participate in the pinup process. Even if his/her project is not reviewed, the criticism and discussions inform the evaluation of his/her progress. Failure to attend one or more pin-ups for the full class period may result in a grade deduction.

**Critiques**

The Seminar environment involves two-way communication. The role of the Seminar professor is to evolve and strengthen the student’s design process. Every time the student meets with the faculty, he/she must have new iterations of work based on previous conversations. If the student has not produced new work, the faculty reserves the right to skip the critique and continue with other students who have produced new material.

**Timely Completion of Work**

All work must be submitted for pinups, critiques and/or discussions on the dates under each assessment measure. However, final assessment of work does not occur until after the final review and the final submission. Students must respond to feedback received from the Seminar professor and the guest jurors to refine and develop work. Students are encouraged to attend faculty office hours and tutoring sessions to help plan the completion of assignments at different Seminar phases.

**Archiving**

At the conclusion of the course, all students are required to submit all final digital works to Publications. Publications assign a liaison to the Seminar. All students are responsible for submitting work to the liaison. In addition, selected physical material is collected and stored between semesters so that it may be photographed or scanned by Publications. Grades for the semester are not released until this material is received.

**Environmental Hazards**

The use of spray adhesives, paints, aerosols, and other potentially toxic materials is not permitted in or on the building. These materials can be harmful to your health, the health of others, and the environment. Epoxies and resins, acrylic and other materials or process that create dangerous off-gassing cannot be used in the building.

When using adhesives and other potentially toxic materials, follow the user guidelines. If it smells bad, it is bad. It is unacceptable to subject others to it as it may pose serious health risks. If it does not smell bad, it still may be bad. Read and follow the user guidelines. Your responsibility is not only to your own health, but also for the health of others in the environment.

**Academic Integrity**

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process.

**Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism**

Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust merits a failing grade in this course and the violator will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.

Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of the course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student. Any misrepresentation of work is evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student results in a failing grade for the course.
