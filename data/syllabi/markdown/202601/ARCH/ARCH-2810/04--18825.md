## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Building upon the skills acquired in Architectural Design Studio 1 (AD1), Architectural Design Studio 2 (AD2) introduces students to supplementary means and methods of working spatially and abstractly relative to place, scale, material awareness, and interiority.

The course is organized in two phases. In *Phase I - Parti and Concept,* students will focus on generating a conceptual foundation for this semester’s project in the form of an organizational diagram or *parti*. This phase will begin with a series of generative design studies, formulated by the section critic, that will run parallel to an investigation of the project site. This phase will privilege plan and section making, not as final deliverables, rather, as critical design tools by which one develops an architectural proposition. In *Phase II – Design Development,* students will further develop their projects at a higher degree of resolution. This portion of the semester will place an emphasis on the representation of the interaction between spatial, material, and environmental phenomena within the project.

The discipline of architecture is uniquely broad and can be conceived across multiple scales: a global and environmental scale, the scale of the city, infrastructure, buildings, and even the smallest everyday object. However large or small the intervention is, architects must learn to negotiate between, and contend with, fundamental aspects of material, spatial, and environmental phenomena.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Preliminary Design Research and Analysis

Phase I will begin with a speculative design study that runs concurrently with an investigation of the project site.

*Assessment Measure #1: Research and Analysis*

\- Research Summary- 250-word summary of the site’s historical significance, cultural impact, and contextual relevance.

\- Existing Site Analysis Diagram(s) – A diagram(s) outlining existing conditions of the site.

*Assessment Measure #2 - Generative Design Studies*

\- A series of speculative drawings, diagrams, and models guiding the development of the formal and conceptual aspects of the project. Each section critic will determine the scale and format of the material produced.

*Assessment #6 - Site Documentation*

\- Existing Site Plan (Scale @ 1/16” = 1’- 0”) – A conventional site plan accurately delineating the existing conditions of the site with all elements drawn to scale. This drawing should include, but not limited to the following information:

\- Orientation to North (North arrow)

\- Existing site circulation: roads, paths, sidewalks, etc.

\- Contour lines of existing topography

\- Vegetation, i.e., trees/ tree lines, grass, plants

\- Existing roads

\- Existing Infrastructural elements

Parti

In this exercise, students will begin to translate the formal language developed in their generative design studies into a clear organizational diagram or *parti.* The *parti* can be understood as an abstract tool, or conceptual device, that allows the designer to navigate the various complexities associated with the design process. The development of the *parti* involves the organization of volume(s), interior spatial organization, circulation, entry/exit sequences, and the distribution of structure. While typically conceived in plan, *partis* are also often described in section. Ultimately, the material developed in this exercise will lead to a singular diagram that effectively and succinctly outlines the proposed scheme. The process by which students will arrive at an effective diagram involves a set of iterative studies via orthographically projected floor plans and sections.

*Assessment Measure #3 – Parti Diagram*

\- Parti Diagram (Scale TBD) – A clear organizational diagram conceived in plan and/or section outlining the organization of volume(s), interior spatial organization, entry/exit sequences, and circulation.

*Assessment Measure #7: Orthographic Drawings*

\- 2 Floor Plans (Scale TBD)

\- 1 Short Section (Scale TBD)

\- 1 Long Section (Scale TBD)

\- 1 Elevation (Scale TBD)

Site/ Schematic Model

A physical model constructed with precision and care using multiple materials including but not limited to task board, 3D printing, acrylic, and basswood. This model will represent the general scheme of the intervention relative to the river and topography at either the “north” or “south” site location. The model will be evaluated based on the following:

\- Use of multiple materials such as task board, 3D printing, acrylic, basswood, etc.

\- Clarity of Parti/ scheme

\- Topography/ contours

\- Craft

*Assessment Measure #8 – Site/ Schematic Model*

\- Physical model - constructed at 1/16” = 1’-0”

Project Manifesto

A manifesto is a concise declaration of intent; it is an argument for a position taken. Write a manifesto for the project describing the following:

\- an attitude towards the aestheticization of the project

\- a brief description of the *parti*

\- the intended use or program

\- how humans and/or non-human occupants move through the project

\- a brief description of the flow and containment of water throughout the project

\- any other aspects of the project deemed most important.

*Assessment Measure #4 – Project Manifesto*

\- A 300-word text graphically incorporated into final presentation boards.

Three – Dimensional Imagery

Two high quality rendered views of the project are required: one eye level view from the exterior and one interior perspective. Test and save multiple camera options for each view. Once views have been selected, add detail to the three-dimensional model in areas visible in the views. Develop a material and texture palette and an overall aesthetic that complements and reinforces conceptual ideas of the project. Do not default to a conventional sunny day, blue sky, green vegetation aesthetic. Final image production must start at least three weeks before the final review and be developed iteratively.

*Assessment Measure #5 – Three - Dimensional Imagery*

\- 1 Interior Rendering

\- 1 Exterior Rendering

Sectional Chunk Model

A physical model constructed with precision and care using multiple materials including but not limited to task board, 3D printing, CNC milling, acrylic, and basswood. The model must be well supported with a supporting base element and necessary framing elements. The model must explore and represent the following:

\- a volumetric condition of the project and its relationship to the river and topography

\- a three-dimensional cutaway of the envelope to reveal interior spatial development

\- accurate scale figures

\- location of at least one daylighting element

\- use of multiple materials

*Assessment Measure #9 - Sectional Chunk Model*

\- Physical model – (Scale TBD)

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance

Attendance is required at all class meetings throughout the semester. Two (2) absences from class will result in the lowering of the grade by one (1) letter. Four (4) absences from class during the term result in an automatic F. For medical absences of up to a week, formal excuses are not required, and students must work directly with faculty to arrange missed work. If illness causes more than a week of absences, students must provide a doctor’s note or documentation to the Student Success Office, and the Class Dean will inform faculty. COVID-19 and flu cases follow this same process. Students are responsible for completing any work that they might miss due to illness, quarantine/isolation, or other circumstances. The instructor will make every effort to work with and provide reasonable modifications for students who cannot attend class due to illness to complete assignments and exams. In some cases, virtual learning may not be possible. Ultimately, it is the responsibility of the student(s) who miss class to contact the instructor so they may agree upon a plan of action for making up missed assignments.

Students are required to work in studio during studio hours and have the required materials and basic tools to develop the assignments. Work includes participation in site visits, design and development, pinups, presentations, discussions and field trips. Failure to work in class with undivided attention and disruptive behavior (tardiness, leaving early, lack of participation, general socializing, etc.), as well working on assignments from other classes during class, will count as absences.

Failure to fully engage in class or display disruptive behavior (such as tardiness, leaving early, lack of participation, or socializing) will be considered absences, as will working on assignments from other classes during class time.

Late assignments

Late assignments will be subject to a grading penalty and may not be accepted without the above-mentioned legitimate excuses.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

“The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process. Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust will merit a failing grade in this course and the violator(s) will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.”

Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism

Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of this course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student. Any miss-representation of work shall be evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student will result in a failing grade for the course.
