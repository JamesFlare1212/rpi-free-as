## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Catalog Course Description:**

This design studio continues to develop and refine the studio project from ARCH 4820. In this course “students develop the ability to make design decisions within architectural projects while demonstrating integration of building envelope systems and assemblies, structural systems, environmental control systems, life safety systems, and the measurable outcomes of building performance.” NAAB (SC.6 Design Integration). This course is required of all fourth-year architecture undergraduates in the B.Arch. program. Any student receiving a second ‘D’ in any design studio must repeat the second design studio and receive a grade of ‘C’ or higher to proceed to the next design studio.

CDS2 is a technology-based design studio emphasizing the materialization and making of architectural design projects. The integration of building code requirements for fire protection, life safety, accessibility, building environmental systems, structure, construction, and materiality is central to the effective achievement of design intent. Students become aware of how these issues affect and inform design decisions. Students will learn to integrate technology, systems, and materials in the comprehensive resolution of building design and gain exposure to construction documents and design documentation. Construction practice observations and site visits are an integral part of the studio. Students must co-register for ARCH 4550 Professional Practice II, and ARCH 4325 Building Integration II which intend to compliment the content covered in CDS2 including, but not limited to regulatory, life safety, environmental considerations, Mechanical, Electrical, and Plumbing integration, structure systems and integration, construction systems and assemblies, facade technologies, codes, regulatory processes, contract documentation, building design and cost control, and construction administration.

**Comprehensive Design Studio Sequence Overview:**

Comprehensive Design Studio 1 (CDS1) and Comprehensive Design Studio 2 (CDS2) form a two part sequence for the production of a comprehensive architectural design in the core curriculum of the professional degree program. These studios recognize the dilemma of achieving design synthesis and building integration, including technological, material, regulatory, and environmental needs, all with a quality of resolution within the limits of any given academic semester’s time frame. Thus, with these studios organized as a two-part sequence, students work through specific stages of development in the architectural design process from schematic and design development through building systems integration, construction systems development, environmental response and performance, with resolved regulatory requirements that approach the level of detailed development and integrated thinking demanded for construction and documentation. To ensure that each student achieves competency with respect to identified learning areas as outlined in Student Criteria 6 (SC6), each student will develop and deliver a single project in which they are the primary author. Collaboration plays a critical role in CDS2 projects. Some students will be paired with the Bedford Chair and engineering students while everyone will engage with consultants and design professionals who will offer feedback and critique in the form of weekly workshops, reference addendums, and/or presentations and reviews. By integrating both internal and external critics into design and theory discussions, students will be exposed to a broad array of perspectives from a professional practice (design and engineering) perspective as well as from stakeholder communities.

However, the process of developing a design for construction - one in which enclosure, environmental systems, structure, and building codes are well considered in a design scheme - does not inevitably ensure the resultant building scheme will reach a level of synthesis that could be considered a work of architecture. Conversely, the development of an elegant, provocative architectural scheme does not represent the end of the architect’s responsibility to development. Ultimately, authorship incorporates all of these systems to achieve the status of a synthetic work of architecture. It is, arguably, a tall order for any design studio or sequence within a professional degree program. To this end, it is both the project and the process that seeks to provide an arena for the student’s ability to develop a synthetic work of architecture. In summary, architecture is a technical art, where both art informs the technical and the technical informs the art. The Comprehensive Design Studios seek to resolve this reality with the aim of producing a synthetic work of architecture.

In order to pursue these larger goals, this studio presents a wider array of issues that need to be addressed at all stages of design development. Rather than thinking of design, then program, then environment, then site, then structure, etcetera, as distinctly sequential and compartmentalized issues, this studio sequence leads towards a holistic approach where students confront a wider range of design issues throughout the process, with iterative layers of development. Fundamentally, this iterative process of visiting and re-visiting critical issues and drivers in the architectural design provides the opportunity for students to develop specific skills through narrative, drawings, diagrams, and models that capture the process of integrative design and attain a project approaching a holistic architectural response. By developing a pedagogical process and building a project through an iterative approach, these two studios, taken together as a sequence, will develop the fundamental abilities of students to attain both synthesis and integration in the context of a comprehensive design studio project.

**Building Performance in the Context of CDS2:**

Buildings are increasingly complex assemblies that must perform many specific technical, aesthetic, and social tasks. The design development phase requires an architect to integrate those aspects of the building enterprise materializing in a construction that conforms to codes and other performance requirements. Responses can range from passive strategies which mitigate the requirement for resource consumptive systems; to complex intelligent systems that make the building, its spaces, surface, and systems responsive to integrated systems that fold the two together by employing form, materials, and systems to serve multiple integrated functions. Though the studio is focused on the integration of building systems including codes, means of egress, structure, envelope, storm water drainage, flood control, HVAC (heating ventilation, air conditioning), lighting and site development, the properties and aesthetics of materials and construction are equally important considerations, particularly with regard to how they inform and/or influence design decisions. We will focus on materials, composition, and detail as they relate to and support design intent and ambitions. Texture, color, pattern, finish and effect have a significant place and will be specifically addressed in a selected and strategic focus area of development.

The studio’s emphasis is in refining and making of a building using processes through which designs continue to gain conceptual strength and clarity over time. Technical resolution and understanding of the interrelationship between systems, components, performance, and design are central to success in this course. During the design development phase, it is expected that systems integration and detailed development of your project support and enlarge ideas and intentions. Design is always an act of opening up a line of questioning; even when the goal is to converge and specify in order to actualize.

**Site & Program**

Given the relationship between CDS1 and CDS2, questions of program and site are initially established in the CDS1 portion of the studio sequence. It is expected that a project advancing from CDS1 into CDS2 will have established a mature understanding of the site conditions and programmatic requirements of the project. It is additionally expected that the project will have achieved an overall comprehensive design strategy along with a clear set of concepts and ideas for the design of a public, multi-story building of approximately 53,000 square feet of programmed space and located in and serving an urban context. Although many of the deliverables of CDS2 will require further development of the site and program as part of the design process, under no circumstances will alternate sites or complete reassignment of program be permitted.

**Academic Integrity**

Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that their teacher has made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process.

In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, teamwork was allowed, or AI was used, notation on the assignment should appropriately indicate such collaboration. Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work, all violate academic integrity. ## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- BUILDING ENVELOPE SYSTEMS AND ASSEMBLIES: Demonstration of integrated building envelope systems in a comprehensive architectural project.. Design solutions must exhibit a resolution of building systems based on sound building science approaches, contemporary manufacturing methods, and conceptually clear approaches to construction and assembly.
- STRUCTURAL SYSTEMS: Demonstration of the primary structural system in a comprehensive architectural project in relation to material and construction systems (for example: steel, wood, masonry, concrete), construction sequencing, and resolvable approaches to the major structural load paths in a architectural design.
- ENVIRONMENTAL CONTROL SYSTEMS: Demonstration of integrated passive and active environmental control systems. Integrated Active systems include distribution of mechanical conditioning and dedicated outdoor air systems addressing indoor air quality. Integrated passive systems include mixed mode conditioning, incorporation of thermal mass performance (as appropriate to the design) and daylighting control.
- LIFE SAFETY SYSTEMS: Demonstration of sufficient means of egress and life safety provisions as based on first principles, universal standards, provisions for universal access (accessibility) and governing national and local codes as appropriate.
- MEASURABLE BUILDING PERFORMANCE: Demonstration of measurable building performance with respect to daylight as a driver of design in an integrated architectural project.
- DETAIL DESIGN AND INTEGRATION – Demonstrate the ability to develop integrated architectural project that is linked to the organization of the buildings program, site, building, environmental and constructions systems. Representations include diagrams, models, interior and exterior renderings, plans, sections and architectural details.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Phase I: The Detail**

Development of a section ‘chunk’ and associated detail wall section / partial elevation demonstrating architectural design as integral to the concept and parti of the project. Chunk and drawings must demonstrate the building’s structural system, building envelope and assemblies, material systems, and environmental strategies as coordinated with your studio instructor.

Deliverables:

- Axonometric cut-away section ‘chunk’ drawing from foundation through the roof; approximately 20’Lx20’Wx60’H of the building. Chunk should encompass a ‘knuckle’ of the building that includes the long span space and its transition to a short/ traditional span.
- Partial Elevation of the facade of the ‘chunk’
- Partial Detailed Section drawing of the ‘chunk’
- Five key section details including

1. Building to ground (section)
2. & 3. Typical slab to exterior wall (section & plan)
3. Exterior Wall to roof/parapet (section)
4. Exterior wall at the corner (plan)

- Concept / parti description and supporting diagrams illustrating how your concept is achieved at the scale of the chunk and at the scale of the massing

**Phase II: Concept Refinement**

This phase will take the small scale development and refinement from phase I and work its achievements through the project at a holistic level. This will include project development in the plans, sections, axons, diagrams, visualizations, written descriptions and oral presentations addressing the design concepts as solidified in phase I and outlining strategies to be further developed for the six course learning outcomes. This phase will also require students to assemble a scale model of the axon chunk that they developed in Phase I.

Deliverables:

- Site Plan (1/32” = 1’-0”)
- All project Floor Plans (3/32” = 1’-0”)
- One Building Section East/West Through the site (3/32” = 1’-0”)
- One Building Section North/South through the site & creek (3/32” = 1’-0”)
- Building Elevations for each facade (north, south, east, and west) (3/32” = 1’-0”)
- Reflected Ceiling Plans (RCP’s) for all floors (3/32” = 1’-0”)
- Roof Plan (3/32” = 1’-0”)
- One Aerial Rendering
- Two Exterior Renderings
- Two Interior Renderings
- ½”= 1’-0” physical chunk model representing the axonometric detail chunk drawing from phase I

**Phase III: Penultimate Design Development**

Following the midreview, students will continue to develop their project, focusing on specific aspects of the project as outlined below. This process is intended to be iterative and therefore a back and forth between areas of focus will be required. All deliverables as presented at mid-term review are required for the penultimate review, with further development, revisions, and refinements. Additionally, students will complete all technical book sections and visualizations as outlined below.

SC6.1 Building Envelope Systems and Assemblies

Technical poster / book presentation explaining the integrated design of a building envelope system including the material systems, construction systems, and qualities of the assembly’s thermal performance. Key representations will include axonometric drawings, wall sections, plans, and diagrams that will demonstrate the integration of building envelope systems and assemblies.

SC6.2 Structural Systems

Technical poster / book presentation addressing the primary structural system of the architectural design with the key concepts of load path and lateral force resolution developed with identification of construction systems and materiality. Key representations include axonometric drawings, highlighted plans and sections, sequencing diagrams, and load path diagrams which demonstrate the integration of structural systems as integral to the architectural design.

SC6.3 Environmental Control Systems

Technical poster / book presentation addressing active and passive systems envisioned as integral to the fundamental design approach of the architectural proposal. Key representations include axonometric drawings, highlighted plans and sections, system organizational and flow diagrams which will demonstrate the integration of environmental control systems as central to the architectural design.

SC6.4 Life Safety Systems

Technical poster/ book presentation of the building’s spatial organization and floor areas with respect to life safety systems, including awareness of building codes and with provisions for universal access. Key representations including highlighted floor plans and sections, distance and egress sequencing diagrams, will demonstrate the integration of life safety systems as integral to the architectural design. Drawings will also include references to the integration of fire suppression technologies and awareness of smoke containment strategies as required.

SC6.5 Measurable Building Performance

Technical poster / book presentation of the building’s daylighting performance as integral to the architectural design. Key representations including analysis of floor plans and sections with respect to measured daylight will demonstrate the integration of key concepts of daylight distribution in relation to the spatial and programmatic organization of the architectural design.

**Phase IV: The Final**

All drawing requirements as outlined above, with further development, revisions, and refinements. Additionally, students will be required to produce a new architectural model (3/32” = 1’-0”) which must include a landscape design and fits on the shared studio section site model. Students are expected to print and bind a minimum of two copies of the final technical booklet. Submissions of final review boards and booklet are due to the instructor on the final review date.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is required at all class meetings during the semester. Students are expected to arrive prepared and ready to use the class period for development of their projects. In the event that you must miss class, please notify your instructor at your soonest convenience. Formal excuses are not required for medical absences for up to a week of missed classes. Students may contact their faculty directly to discuss completing missed assignments and exams. Students missing more than a week of classes due to illness will be required to provide a doctor’s note or other supporting documentation to the Dean of Students Office. Your Student Support Dean will communicate the receipt of the note (with expected end date) to the relevant faculty.

For information on institute policies on absences including prescheduled absences, unforeseen absences, absences that require documentation, and information on how to request absence from class, please refer to the following webpage: https://doso.rpi.edu/student-support/excused-absence

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that their teacher has made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process.

In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, teamwork was allowed, or AI was used, notation on the assignment should appropriately indicate such collaboration. Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work, all violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust will merit a failing grade in this course and the violator(s) will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and the Graduate Student Supplement to the Handbook defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit RPI’s website for more information on the Academic Integrity Policy [here](https://doso.rpi.edu/student-rights-responsibilities-and-conduct).
