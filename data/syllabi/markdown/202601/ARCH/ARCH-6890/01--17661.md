## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course goals are an essential component of the Architectural Acoustics Program. Architectural Acoustic Design generally addresses the needs of human inhabitants. Building of the Applied Psychoacoustic class, this course focuses on how the built and electronic environments were created to address these needs from practical and cultural standpoints. The approach is somewhat opposite to Architectural Acoustics Practice that stems from the built environment.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

- After taking this course, the successfully participating students will have learned the fundamental terms and concepts of Aural Architecture according to Barry Blesser and the World Soundscape Project by Schafer, Truax, and Westerkamp.
- The students will understand the principles of sound design, its application to the design of acoustics spaces.
- The students will know the technological concepts for the design of virtual acoustic spaces (virtual environments, auditory displays, and studio technology in general).
- The students will have deepened his/her knowledge in psychoacoustics to master the above goals.
- The students will understand how psychophysical and statistical methods can be used to evaluate acoustic designs and establish design criteria.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

- Aural Architecture & Sound Quality
- Multimodal Perception
- Loudspeaker-based Displays: WFS, Ambisonics
- Sound Scapes & Sound Art
- Music Genre and Period Specific Concert venues
- Advanced binaural hearing, Head-based Auditory Displays
- Studio Technology (microphones, amplifiers and mixing consoles, effect processors and perceptual audio data compression)
- Advanced Digital Signal Processing, Interactive Virtual Environments
- Ecological vs. Laboratory Methods, Cognitive Music Perception
- Computational Auditory Models, Provisions for Hearing-Impaired People

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. After taking this course, the successfully participating students will have learned the fundamental terms and concepts of Aural Architecture according to Barry Blesser and the World Soundscape Project by Schafer, Truax, and Westerkamp.
- 2\. They will understand the principles of sound design, its application to the design of acoustics spaces, and its history.
- 3\. They will know the technological concepts for the design of virtual acoustic spaces (virtual environments, auditory displays, and studio technology in general).
- 4\. They will have deepened their knowledge in psychoacoustics to master the above goals.
- 5\. They will understand how psychophysical and statistical methods can be used to evaluate acoustic designs and establish design criteria.
- Scientific Writing: The course participants can analyze and write scientific aural architecture related texts, including proper lab reports, using field specific terminology.
- Cultural Understanding: They can write summaries of cultural stream and trends that informed aural architecture over time by understanding how music was formed acoustically, electroacoustically and digitally and the role of acoustic and electronic environments for various genres.
- Psychoacoustics: They can design simple psychoacoustic experiments to optimize acoustic spaces following best practices and using adequate terminology. They can perform statistical tests to interpret the results of listening experiments.
- Perform Auralizations: Students can use state-of-art methods like ray-tracing to (re-)create the sound of acoustical venues and bring them to life with culturally adequate sound files and/or live processing. They will learn the room acoustical principles, programming in Matlab, and digital signal processing to do so. Students who take the undergraduate version of this course can substitute this course goal.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Learning Outcomes will be measured using the following deliverables:  
Midterm and Final Project: Application of knowledge for practical questions  
Quizzes, and 2 Exams: Factual knowledge in aural architecture and related fields.  
Homework Assignments: Applied knowledge in signal processing, psychophysical and statistical methods and programming skills.  
Students may substitute assignments in discussion with the instructors if feasible and all learning outcomes are met.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students are expected to hand in their own work, but consulting other students about homework assignments and projects is fine as long as the student understand their assignment and not blindly copy the work of others. Students are encouraged to reference significant help from others, e.g., as personal communication. Students may use Deep Learning methods is fine for fact finding but such methods should not be used to write substantial text or assignments as the goal is to learn the class material. For the take-home exams, deep learning tools and the consultation with others is not allowed, but all other tools may be used. These exams build on trust and I hope we can continue to test students on this basis as it is very convenient. I encourage the use of plagiarism checkers for larger assignments to avoid accidental errors. Assignments, were students intentionally plagiarized material will be downgraded or graded with zero points. Class participants may consult me if they are unsure which tools and approaches are acceptable for class work.
