## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Students will work with non-profits, city officials, and local residents to document changed incurred as a result of the 2021 Gowanus rezoning. Students will design graphic systems to communicate to multiple audiences, and propose ways the rezoning can have a maximally beneficial outcome for all residents.  
Relationship to Concurrent Seminars  
The B.Arch cirriculum at CASE is designed to have tight integration with concurrent seminars. Studio work will overlap most notably with two seminars, Systems Prototyping and Materials Systems and Production. In these courses students will conduct analysis and detailed designwork on topics directly related to their studio work. Work produced in these seminars will be expected to augment and support studio work, and visa versa. Students should expect to incorporate work from these seminars into their studio presentations.  
Group Work  
We will work in groups during the research and design phases of this studio. Group work is essential to your development as a student and as a professional. If you have concerns about group work, please discuss with me directly. During the semester we will also work with project partners such as personnel from the Cathedral of All Saints, the project structural engineer, and historic preservation architect. It is essential that we use all of these parties as a resource and interact with them in a professional manner.  
Analysis and Visualization  
Students will be introduced to several softwares this semester that will enable advanced material anaylsis and data visualization. These tools will be are emblematic of the unique attributes of a semester spent studying at CASE.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Outcome 1: Research 1a abilty to use / develop specialized knowledge - Students will have the ability to utilize analyze, track, and compare completion of points of agreement between the City of New York and residents of the Gowanus neighborhood. 1b abilty to use / develop broad and integrative knowledge - Students will have the ability to create visualizations of changes brought as a result of the 2021 Gowanus rezoning for multiple audiences.
- Outcome 2: Intellectual Design Skills 2a ability to design - the ability to design data visualizations that communicate changes in the built environment clearly and effectively. 2b. ability to design using information resources - the ability to conduct a precedent analysis of other large scale urban rezoning projects. 2c ability to design engaging diverse perspectives - ability to develop engage city officials and local residents in regards to the 2021 Gowanus rezoning. 2d. ability to design with ethical reasoning - ability to reconcile promised Points of Agreement with realized accomplishments. 2e. ability to design with quantitative fluency - ability to record data that describes development in the Gowanus neighborhood, and informs design proposals that can benefit existing residents of the neighborhood. 2f. ability to design with communicative fluency - ability to communicate clearly using terminology associated with the Gowanus rezoning, and in a way that is accessible to all audiences.
- Outcome 3: Collaboration 3a ability to collaborate within a team - this covers all aspects of the studio. Students will be asked to collaborate in pairs and as an entire studio. 3b ability to collaborate within the studio culture - problem solving, efficiency and producing presentations and proposing solution as a collective.
- Outcome 4: Civic and Global Engagement Ability to engage the disciplinary realm and the profession - the abillity to situate a design inquiry within a context of large urban scale rezonings.
- Outcome 5: Design Execution Ability to design a project that incorporates multiple stakeholders, research interests, quantitative analysis as well as personal preference. Ability to evaluate designwork by calculating expected performance outcomes. Ability to clearly articulate a project through drawing and verbal presentation.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism:  
Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of this course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student. Any miss-representation of work shall be evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction  
by the student will result in a failing grade for the course.
