## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides consultation with experts to prepare students with the necessary leadership skills for collaborative practice through lectures and skill building workshops. This course will provide access to professional disciplines typically engaged in the building design process as consultants, including sustainable practice, structural, mechanical, environmental, and high-performance facade design to support the integration of of technical knowledge and to promote innovation in current and future best practices, and stewardship of resources within architecture. As such, this class cannot be taken without first passing the pre-requisite courses, Construction Systems (CS) and Environmental and Ecological Systems (EES).

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This three-hour class balances short, focused lectures and breakout group sessions where students will work directly with faculty to integrate technical concepts into their comprehensive design studio project. Some of the class content will occur in a virtual classroom when software analysis and measurable building performance is demonstrated. Lecture slides and virtual recordings will be posted to the LMS for reference.

Course learning outcomes will be assessed through tech boards that align with Comprehensive Design Studio 2. As this course is a co-requisitie to ARCH 4830_Comprehensive Design Studio 2, Assignment deliverables will support the development of the final studio project.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Outcome 1 Building Enclosure Systems and Assemblies Integration: Students will demonstrate the ability to coordinate, interpret and communicate the input of an enclosure specialist to identify critical building enclosure details, recognize basic building science, weatherization and energy performance principles and apply the concepts to develope an acceptable series of transition details.
- Outcome 2 Structural Systems Integration: Students will demonstrate the ability to coordinate, interpret and communicate the input of a structural specialist to identify the basic structural concept and layout for a building design and its critical characteristics, details and components.
- Outcome 3 Environmental Control Systems Integration: Students will demonstrate the ability to coordinate, interpret and communicate the input of a mechanical specialist to identify the basic concept and layout for the design of a building's heating, cooling, ventilation, fire suppression and plumbing systems.
- Outcome 4 Measurable Building Performance: Students will demonstrate the ability to coordinate, interpret and communicate the input of a daylighting specialist to develop a lighting concept along with measurable evidence through a documented iterative design process.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

## The assignments listed below are intended to support the parallel work that is being completed in Comprehensive Design II. Graduate students will use work from an approved studio course when CDII is not an available option. The assignment descriptions found in the Comprehensive Design II studio should supercede all details found below, though new grading rubrics and evaluation criteria will be established for this course. Please note that SC6.4 Life Systems is not in the scope of this course.

Assignment 1 (SC6.1) Building Envelope Systems and Assemblies

Technical Book explaining the integrated design of a building enclosure system including the material systems, construction systems and qualities of the assembly’s thermal performance. Key representation will include axonometric drawings, wall sections, plans and diagrams that will demonstrate the integration of building envelope systems and assemblies.

Assignment 2 (SC6.2) Structural Systems

Technical Book addressing the primary structural system of the architectural design with the key concepts of laod path and lateral force resolution developed with identification of construction systems and materiality. Key respresentation includes axonometric drawings, highlighted plans and sections, sequencing diagrams, and load path diagrams which demonstrate the integration of structural systems as integral to the architectural design.

Assignment 3 (SC6.3) Environmental Control Systems

Technical book addressing active and passive systems envisioned as integral to the fundamental design approach of the architectural proposal. Key represetntations include axonometric drawings, highlighted plans and sections, system organizational and flow diagrams which will demonstrate the integration of environmental control systems as central to the architectural design.

Assignment 4 (SC6.5) Measurable Building Performance

Technical book presentation of the building’s daylighting performance as integral to the architectural design. Key representations including analysis of floor plans and sections with respect to measured daylighting will demonstrate the integration of key concepts of daylight distribution in relation to the spatial and programmatic organization of the architectural design.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance will be taken at the beginning and end of each class and counts towards the final class grade. Attendance will be taken using a self check-in process requiring a computer or a cell phone. Please see the Assessment Measures section for assessment breakdown. More than three unexcused absences will result in a failing grade for the course.

To qualify as an excused absence, the student should contact their section instructor prior to the missed class or shortly after with the reason for their absence. It is up to the discretion of the instructor to accept or deny the excuse. Notes from the Student Health center or doctor are not required.

Attendance grades will be issued based on the following metrics:

0 absences 100%

1 absence 95%

2 absences 85%

3 absences 65%

3+ absences (F) course failure

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
