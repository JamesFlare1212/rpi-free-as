## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Architectural Design tackles the problem of designing housing in downtown Troy, or more specifically *multi-unit collective* housing, in which some aspect of the domestic realm is shared among multiple domestic spheres. While many aspects of our personal and professional lives are increasingly communicated into collective spheres, a majority of our domestic spaces are structured by the prevailing American ethic of individual property, individual building. This ethic has manifested itself predominately in suburbs developed since World War II, and has now become such a fixture of a particular type of consumer-driven lifestyle that it is being exported to other countries, other cultures.  
Clearly this is not a sustainable future as the world becomes increasingly urbanized and more extreme pressures are placed on the Earth’s resources. So as a typology, multi-unit collective housing must be understood as a global cultural necessity due its relative efficiency in terms of quantity of land, building materials, and energy required to construct and operate each housing unit. Designing multi-unit collective housing must also be approached as a way of introducing strategies for equitability and affordability to a housing market that in many cases exacerbates socio-economic disparities in American culture.

The semester-long project in this studio will be to design a mixed-use, multi-unit housing project on an urban site in downtown Troy, NY at the northwest corner of Congress and Third Streets. The site is currently occupied by a small fast food franchise building, which for the purposes of this studio will be removed, leaving the entire parcel bound by Third St. to the east, Congress St. to the south, Franklin Alley to the west, and existing buildings to the north, free for a new building.

In 2018 The City of Troy released a Comprehensive Plan for ongoing development in the city:

*https://www.troyny.gov/DocumentCenter/View/324/Realize-Troy---Comprehensive-Plan-2018-PDF?bidId=*

This plan describes the historic downtown zone, which includes the studio site at Congress and Third Streets, as “an architecturally rich, walkable district with many boutiques and restaurants, and a successful Farmer’s Market.” It notes that adding housing units in the historic downtown zone “will enhance safety, support activity beyond the workday and would better position the city to attract continued investment job and population growth. Over time, and through population growth, the downtown could attract new neighborhood-serving amenities such as a small, well-stocked grocery store.” As a response to the City of Troy Comprehensive Plan, this studio will design a building containing 30 housing units and street level retail in the urban context of downtown Troy.

The studio will address housing design at range of scales from the small, intimate scale of quotidian domestics rituals, to the scale of the building, its various components, and its site. There will be structured moments in the semester when work will jump from one scale to another to effectively develop the project at the scale of the entire building and its site, and at the scale of details within an individual housing unit. To ensure that projects are thoroughly developed at a range of scales, students will work in teams of two. However, each student will be responsible for specified deliverables individually as noted in the descriptions of assessment measures that follow.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**COURSE COMPONENTS, SEQUENCE AND DELIVERABLES**

1. **Precedent Documentation and Analysis**

Each student will be assigned one precedent project to analyze individually. The precedents will be chosen from a list of built housing projects on urban sites. Each student will acquire comprehensive knowledge of the complexities of their assigned project and produce a presentation to share with the entire studio. The goal of precedent analysis is to expose every student to a range of design strategies for urban multi-unit housing.

Before making the analysis, each student will produce a series of drawings that comprehensively document the precedent project and can subsequently be used as the basis for analytical diagrams. These drawings will include plans, sections, elevations, and any other drawings required to fully describe the project. They must be constructed from scratch in two dimensions. Images of drawings scanned from books and collected online may be used as underlays for these drawings, but the drawings must not be tracings of images. Rationalized dimensions, organizational principles, and architectural alignments must be implemented in a consistent and precise manner to CONSTRUCT the drawings rather than TRACE them. Develop clear, consistent, legible graphics for all drawings. Use the Rhino layer template provided to organize drawings. **These documentary drawings must be output directly from Rhino, do not use Illustrator.** Scale and format of drawings to be determined with section critic’s guidance. Include north arrow and graphic scale.

From completed documentary drawings students will develop a series of analytical diagrams that describe the functioning, organization, and relation of the project to its surrounding site. Analytical diagrams must include the following:

- \- clearly and precisely drawn documentary drawings with clearly calibrated line weights used as underlays for analytical diagrams
- \- parti
- \- overall organizational diagram(s) in plan and section
- \- orientation to north and solar exposure
- \- relationships between private and public programs
- \- horizontal and vertical circulation in relation to each other
- \- structural system
- \- site circulation and relations
- \- repetition and variation in unit types
- \- overall building dimensions (in feet and inches)
- \- unit, corridor, and stair dimensions (in feet and inches)
- \- gross square footage
- \- net square footage

Scale and format of diagrams to be determined with section critic’s guidance.

*Assessment Measure #1 (Individual) - Precedent Documentation and Analysis (Due 1/17):*

A set of constructed two-dimensional drawings comprehensively documenting a housing precedent project, serving as underlays for analytical diagrams.

A series of notated analytical diagrams addressing issues listed above. Scale and format of diagrams to be determined with section critic’s guidance.

2. **Site Analysis**

In order to understand the context in which the project will be located, students will make an analysis of the surrounding site. Like many small cities in the United States, Troy has undergone several transformations in its history from colonial beginnings to rapid industrial development spurred by rail and water transportation infrastructure, to a loss of manufacturing and disinvestment, to a current period of some redevelopment. New architectural projects are part of the changing nature of the city, and they must be designed with an awareness of context. New buildings do not need to be visually similar to existing buildings around it, but they must be designed in response to other aspects of context. Each studio section must assemble a site analysis incorporating the following elements. Students will be individually responsible for one of the following tasks (selected tasks may be broken into smaller parts):

- \- Photographic documentation of building elevations on Third between State & Ferry, and Congress between Second and Church
- \- Horizontal and vertical datums governing the architecture along Third and Congress
- \- Access points to buildings along Third and Congress
- \- Distribution of public and private programs along Third and Congress
- \- A map of transportation infrastructure in the city of Troy and surrounding region
- \- A map of public programs in a three-block radius of the site
- \- A three-dimensional model of a three-block radius around the site
- \- Solar exposure analysis of the site
- \- FEMA flood map analysis of site

Working individually, every student must produce their own zoning analysis of the site.

*Assessment Measure #2 (Individual) – Site Analysis (Due 2/3):*

Portion of the studio’s group site analysis and individual zoning analysis.

3. **Generative Drawing and Modeling**

An abstract design exercise will generate formal and conceptual languages for the project. Generative work must consist of physical modeling and drawing. Physical models must be spatial, multi-material assemblies i.e., they must consist of at least three parts that fit together, are made of different materials, and define interior and exterior spaces. Generative models must be developed and discussed in terms of their potential to define a parti diagram for the project.

Beyond these general requirements, specifics of this generative exercise will be determined by each section instructor individually.

*Assessment Measure #3 (Individual) – Generative Drawing and Modeling (Due 2/3):*

A set of speculative physical models and drawings that form the basis of a formal/conceptual design language, and a parti diagram for the project. Scale and format of drawings to be determined with section critic’s guidance.

4. **Programming and Massing**

The primary goal of this semester is to design a mixed-use building containing housing, street-level retail, and parking at the northwest corner of Congress St. and Third St. in Troy, NY. The project must be resolved in terms of planning, egress, structure, and envelope design. It must respond effectively to all aspects of the site analysis performed earlier in the semester. The project must incorporate the following primary program elements:

- \- 10 studio apartments
- \- 10 one-bedroom apartments
- \- 10 two-bedroom apartments
- \- Private and/or public outdoor space
- \- Ground level retail
- \- 15 off-street parking spaces
- \- Underground storage, mechanical, and/or parking spaces

Based on a collaborative discussion of the generative work, each student is required to develop an annotated graphic document that specifies square foot areas of all program elements and demonstrates how these areas are configured on site in accordance with zoning regulations, horizontal and vertical circulation, and egress requirements. This document must specify:

- \- configuration of horizontal and vertical circulation
- \- egress routes from housing units all public spaces including parking
- \- type, size, and distribution of housing units
- \- size and distribution any ancillary spaces deemed necessary and/or desirable for the project (lobby, mail room, gym, etc.)
- \- location(s) of outdoor space
- \- overall project square footage
- \- ratio of built area to open space
- \- how the project meets zoning regulations

Each student must then individually develop a physical massing model based on the annotated graphic document.

Based on the individually developed graphic document and physical massing model, students must then come together to develop collaborative versions of the graphic document and massing model. The collaborative work will serve as a ‘final’ version of the programming and massing exercise and will be the basis of further project development.

*Assessment Measure #4A (Individual) – Programming and Massing (Due at Mid-Review):*

One annotated graphic document specifying all aspects of program described above, developed individually. One physical massing model(s) at 1/16” scale, developed individually.

*Assessment Measure #4B (Collaborative) – Programming and Massing (Due at Mid-Review):*

One annotated graphic document specifying all aspects of program described above, developed collaboratively. One physical massing model at 1/16” scale, developed collaboratively.

5. **Floor Plans, Sections, and Elevations**

Building on skills developed last semester in AD3, two-dimensional orthographic projections (plans, sections, and elevations) drawn at 1:1 scale will continue to be used instrumentally in design. Similar to last semester, these drawings MUST:

- \- be built up from scratch – a three-dimensional model may be used as a reference underlay to help produce the drawing, however it must not be included in the drawing
- \- make use of the layer structure template provided
- \- used to explore and evaluate design decisions in relation to city context and solar exposure
- \- bring together aspects of the precedent analysis and generative drawings with design decisions made through explorations of massing, programming, and context
- \- show an accurately designed parking lot with accessible parking spaces
- \- show a resolved configuration of different types of housing units including how units aggregate, relate to structural and plumbing systems, light and air requirements, circulation, and egress
- \- describe all components of architecture at accurate scale
- \- describe accurate floor, wall, and roof assemblies and thicknesses
- \- demonstrate how the project incorporates strategies for equitability and affordability
- \- be graphically clear through effective use of line weight and line type

Refer to Architectural Graphic Standards as well as references file provided to the studio for information regarding architectural drawing standards.

Refer to *Architect's Role in Creating Equitable Communities* and *Accessibility Requirements for Buildings* in the bibliography below for information regarding strategies for equitability and affordability.

In a similar way to how a project is organized in a professional office, each student will be responsible for specified drawings (or portions of drawings) individually. Each team must work with their section instructor to determine how the work will be split between the team members. Team meetings and discussions must occur on a continual basis so that the project effectively refers to and further develops decisions made collaboratively in the previous phase of the project, and the final drawings represent a cohesive building.

*Assessment Measure #5A (Individual) – Building Floorplans, Sections, and Elevations (due at Final Review):*

The team is responsible for four floor plans minimum, two sections minimum, and two elevations (along Congress St. and Third St.) at 3/16” scale. Determine with section leader which drawings each team member is responsible for (i.e., 2 plans, one section, one elevation). **Output drawings directly from Rhino rather than using Illustrator to assign graphics.**

*Assessment Measure #5B (Collaborative) – Building Floorplans, Sections, and Elevations (due at Final Review):*

The team will be assessed for how well individually developed drawings are integrated into a cohesive and consistent design.

6. **Unit Design and Detail Chunk Model**

Architecture always operates across a wide range of scales, from the global scale of the project and its site to the local scale of how individual parts of a building are put together, and everything in between. In this phase of the project, shift design efforts from large-scale organization and figuration of the building to a smaller-scale design of individual housing units. Consider the following in designing housing units:

- \- entry sequence
- \- how apertures are placed in exterior and interior walls/roof/floors of the building, for access to light, air, and emergency rescue
- \- relationship of private and public spaces within the unit
- \- sectional qualities of the unit
- \- accurate floor, wall, and roof assemblies
- \- accessibility within the unit

Each student is responsible for designing and drawing at least one unit type individually, working with their teammate to show how multiple unit types come together in a larger aggregate. These units must be accessible, or they must be design so they can be converted to be accessible.

Building on skills developed in building the tectonic detail last semester in AD3, explore a portion of the building in a large-scale detail chunk model. While chunk models in AD3 were at 1/4” scale, AD4 models will be built at 1/2” scale and will thus incorporate greater detail. This model must explore and show the following:

- \- detailed design of residential and public interior space in relation to exterior of building
- \- detailed exterior envelope development
- \- relationship of structure and envelope
- \- accurate floor, wall, and roof assemblies

This model will not show full height of the building, it must be a chunk extracted from 2-3 floors of the building at any selected height. It must show how individually designed units are integrated into a cohesive and consistent design. Model materials to be determined with section leader’s guidance. Team members must work collaboratively to produce this model.

*Assessment Measure #6A (Individual) – Unit Design Drawings (Due at Final Review):*

Plans and sections of at least one unit type at 1/2” scale.

*Assessment Measure #6B (Collaborative) – Chunk Model (Due at Final Review 4/17):*

Chunk model at 1/2” scale. Integration of individually designed units into a cohesive and consistent design.

7. **Three-Dimensional Imagery**

Four rendered views of the project are required: one aerial view, one eye level view of building exterior, interior view within the building, and one other view chosen to represent a particularly important aspect of the project. Test and save multiple camera options for each view. Once views have been selected, add detail to the three-dimensional model in areas visible in the views. Research rendered images and find a reference example(s) that references an appropriate aesthetic for the project. Develop a material and texture palette, a technique for handling context, and an overall aesthetic that complements and reinforces conceptual ideas of the project. Consider how an image conveys a vision of realism rather than being realistic, and how it is hyperreal rather than photoreal.

Images must be rendered in Rhino VRay and post-processed in Photoshop rather than output in highly deterministic visualization software such as Lumion, Sketchup, etc. Consider what aspects of image creation will be handled in Rhino vs. Photoshop. Refer to lecture given on digital imagery in AD3, refer to image making techniques and process introduced in Digital Constructs 3 and 4.

*Assessment Measure #7 (Individual) – Three-Dimensional Imagery (Due at Final Review):*

Each student is responsible for two of the four required rendered views. Although developed individually, the rendered views must be visually consistent with each other. Size and format of images to be determined with section leader’s guidance.

8. **Project Manifesto**

A manifesto is a concise, incisive declaration of intent; it is an argument for a position taken. Write a manifesto for the project describing the following:

- \- a clearly articulated thesis for the project
- \- how the project addresses lessons learned in precedent analysis
- \- how housing and public programs respond to context and site analysis
- \- how the project incorporates strategies for equitability and affordability
- \- the aesthetic approach of the project

*Assessment Measure #9 (Collaborative) – Project manifesto (Due at Final Review):*

300-word text graphically incorporated into final presentation boards.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Parti - Ability to create a clear, organized design concept and building massing, informed by a well-defined design language, which responds to the physical, social, and environmental context of an urban building site, as well as zoning regulations controlling bulk of the building on site. (Demonstrated by assessment measures #1, 2, 4, 6).
- Environmental - Ability to diagram yearly/hourly sun path on a given site and orient a building in relation to the sun. Ability to show how apertures are used for daylighting, and how sun shading devices are used on different building exposures of the building. (Demonstrated by assessment measures #1, 5, 6).
- Program - Ability to generate and organize an architectural program that responds to the needs of diverse stakeholders within the community of downtown Troy. Ability to calculate gross square footage, net square footage, and floor area ratio (FAR) of a building. Ability to design and organize different types of housing units, and public and private space in a mixed-use housing building. (Demonstrated by assessment measures #1, 2, 4, 5, 6).
- Technical - Ability to scale and dimension spaces (such as bedrooms, bathrooms, stairs, corridors, etc.) and architectural assemblies (thicknesses of walls, floors, and roofs and elements that make them up) accurately. Ability to create an accurate zoning diagram for a building site based on reading and understanding a municipal zoning code. (Demonstrated by assessment measures #1, 2, 5, 6).
- Systems - Ability to organize building systems including circulation, services (routes for plumbing and electrical), structure, and daylight in relation to each other and programmed spaces. (Demonstrated by assessment measures #1, 4, 5, 6).
- Synthesis - Ability to demonstrate how components of an architectural project relate and are synthesized across scales. This includes showing, how the scale of a building relates to the scale of the city, how the scale of a housing unit relates to the scale of the building, and how the scale of a building components (column, beam, stair, etc.) relate to the scale of the building, as well as how environmental factors, including daylighting and solar shading, are incorporated into the design language of the project. (Demonstrated by assessment measures #1, 4, 5, 6, 8).

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is required at all class meetings during the semester. **Two (2) unexcused absences from class will result in the lowering of the grade by one (1) letter. Four (4) unexcused absences from class during the term result in an automatic F.** Whether an absence (health, immediate family death, officially approved field / sport trip, etc.) is excused or unexcused is determined by the Students Experience Office (4th Floor Academy Hall, x8022, se@rpi.edu), upon presentation of required documentation, according to established policies and procedures (see http:// http://se.rpi.edu/policies/ea/).

Students are required to work in studio during studio hours, and have the required materials and basic tools to develop the assignments. Work includes participation in site visits, design and development, pinups, presentations, discussions and field trips. Failure to work in class with undivided attention and disruptive behavior (tardiness, leaving early, lack of participation, general socializing, etc.), as well working on assignments from other classes during class, will count as absences.

All work must be submitted for review and discussion on the dates noted under each assessment measure. However, final assessment of work will not occur until the final review. Students must respond to feedback received from the section instructor and midterm jury to refine and develop work.

For students with an ADHD diagnosis, extra time applies only to exams, and quizzes completed in class. It does not apply to projects or labs where the students are able to plan more time outside of class to complete assignments. Note that a studio class such as AD4 consists solely of project-based assignments. Students are encouraged to attend faculty office hours and tutoring sessions to help plan the completion of studio, lab or project-based assignments.

### Other Course Policies:

**Faculty Presentations**

Each AD4/GAD2 faculty member will give a lecture a covering major architectural topics being newly introduced or reinforced this semester including:

- \- Diagramming
- \- Housing typologies: building scale
- \- Introduction to building, accessibility, and zoning codes / Equitable housing strategies
- \- Urban site planning and parking
- \- Details of domestic space
- \- Polemics of drawing

**Digital Constructs 4 (ARCH 2550) / Grad. Digital Constructs 2 (ARCH 5170)**

Digital Constructs 4 / Grad. Digital Constructs 2 is a co-requisite to AD4 / GAD2, taking place on Wednesdays. Students will attend their DC4 section either 2-4pm or 4-6pm and will attend studio during the other half of the 2-6pm time period. Digital sessions will introduce new, and reinforce known, tools and techniques that support the development of studio projects.

It is the student’s responsibility to sign up for the correct studio section and corresponding Digital Constructs 4 sections. Failure to do so will may result in not receiving a grade for one or both courses.

**Environmental and Ecological Systems (ARCH 2360)**

Selected work produced from the Precedent Documentation and Analysis, and Unit Design and Chunk Model may serve as a basis for solar exposure analysis done in Environmental and Ecological Systems (EES), which is taken concurrently to AD4. Students will not be directly assessed on this solar exposure analysis in AD4, however, drawings and models produced in AD4 will serve as a basis for the analysis performed in EES.

**Pin-ups**

Periodically students will be required to present their work publicly in a formal review process. These presentations are a critical part of the architectural education. It is an opportunity for the student and the faculty to judge the project and critically evaluate the process of design. It may be appropriate at points in the semester to evaluate only selected projects in order to convey information relevant to the entire class. In this situation it is important for each student to witness and participate in the review process. Even if your project is not being reviewed, the criticism and discussion should inform the evaluation of your own progress and design.

Failure to attend one or more pin-ups for the full class period may result in a grade deduction.

**Desk Critiques**

The design studio environment involves two-way communication. The role of the studio critic is to evolve and strengthen the student’s design process. Every time the student meets with the faculty, he or she must have new iterations of work based on previous conversations. If the student has not produced new work, the faculty reserves the right to skip the critique and continue with other students who have produced new material.

**SoA Lectures**

Attendance is MANDATORY for AD4 students at all School of Architecture’s lectures. Unless otherwise noted, lectures begin at 6:00pm at EMPAC. Attendance at SoA lectures will be recorded by the section instructor. Failure to attend lectures will result in a grade deduction.

**Archiving**

At the conclusion of the course, all students will be required to submit all final digital works to Publications. Publications will assign a liaison to the second year studio; all students will be responsible for submitting work to the liaison. In addition, selected physical material will be collected and stored between semesters so that it may be photographed or scanned by Publications. Grades for the semester will not be released until this material is received.

**Environmental Hazards**

THE USE OF SPRAY ADHESIVES, PAINTS AND AEROSOLS IS NOT PERMITTED IN OR ON THE GREENE BUILDING. If you must use these materials, use the JEC high bay spraybooth or use these materials outside away from windows and doors and against a backdrop to protect lifeforms and building materials from overspray.

THESE MATERIALS CAN BE HARMFUL TO YOUR HEALTH, THE HEALTH OF OTHERS, AND THE ENVIRONMENT. FOLLOW ALL DIRECTIONS ON THE LABELS. Epoxies and resins shall not be used in the Greene building and if used, should comply with all procedural safeguards and not returned to the Greene Building prior to being adequately off-gassed. Acrylic and other materials, used in the laser cutter or any other process that will create dangerous off-gassing must be adequately off-gassed in the shop off-gassing chamber. This material must be left in the off-gassing chamber for the appropriate time as listed or taken from the Greene Building until the proper time has passed. If any student is caught violating this policy the following may occur:

1. The student may be charged for the cost of cleaning / repairing any damage to the building and its surroundings,
2. The material being worked on will not be included in the review of that project. It will be disqualified from consideration in the evaluation of the project.

WHEN USING ADHESIVES AND OTHER POTENTIALLY TOXIC MATERIALS, FOLLOW THE USER GUIDELINES. IF IT SMELLS BAD - IT IS BAD. IT IS UNACCEPTABLE TO SUBJECT OTHERS TO IT AS IT MAY POSE SERIOUS HEALTH RISKS. IF IT DOESN’T SMELL BAD IT STILL MAY BE BAD. READ AND FOLLOW THE USER GUIDELINES. YOUR RESPONSIBILITY IS NOT ONLY TO YOUR OWN HEALTH, BUT ALSO FOR THE HEALTH OF OTHERS IN THE ENVIRONMENT.
