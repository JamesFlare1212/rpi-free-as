## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

### Course Description

This course introduces the methods upon which finite elements are built. Methods covered include the construction of weak forms, the discretization of the weak forms including covering the local/global linkage, construction of element shape functions, element mapping and numerical integration. The course will also cover the application of finite elements methods employing software tools. This will include open-source tools for the efficient implementation of finite elements and commercial software commonly applied in industry.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

- Strong form and weak form of boundary value problem in 1-D (heat conduction)
- Finite element analysis: Galerkin approximation for 1-D Problems
- Finite element method for 2-D Problems (heat conduction and linear elasticity)
- Finite element analysis for time dependent problems (heat conduction)
- Triangular and rectangular isoparametric elements for 2-D problems using Lagrange shape functions
- Numerical integration
- Introduction to other shape function construction methods (p-version and isogeometric)
- Finite element error estimates, derivative (stress) recovery methods
- Introduction to plate finite elements
- Introduction to finite elements for fluid flow problems

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Describe, discuss and explain the fundamental theoretical basis of the finite element method (variational formulation, finite element basis functions, solutions of systems of algebraic equations).
- Demonstrate skill in the application of the method together with existing finite element software for the determination of approximate solutions to meaningful, realistic engineering problems
- Critically examine the results of finite element computations and provide sound, authoritative judgement regarding their validity and reliability.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

By the end of this course, students should be able to

- Describe, discuss and explain the fundamental theoretical basis of the finite element method, finite element basis functions, etc.
- Demonstrate skill in the application of the method together with existing finite element software for the determination of approximate solutions to meaningful, realistic engineering problems.
- Critically examine the results of finite element computations and provide sound judgement regarding their validity and reliability.

# Collaboration and academic integrity:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that the students turn in are their own. Acts which violate this trust, undermine the educational process. Students are expected to conduct themselves in a professional manner at all times.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In addition, attempts to commit dishonesty, or to assist in the commission or attempt of such act, are also violations of the academic dishonesty policy. If found in violation of the academic dishonesty policy, students may be subject to two types of penalties. The instructor administers an academic (grade) penalty, and students may also enter the institute judicial process and be subject to such additional sanctions as: warning, probation, suspension, expulsion, and alternative actions as defined in the current *Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities.* The following specific collaboration rules apply to this class:

1. Students are encouraged to collaborate in the solution of homework and practicum problems but **submit independent solutions that are NOT copies of each other**. If a student is found in violation of this policy, **a grade of zero will be awarded for the first violation and a grade of ‘F’ in the course for the second violation**. The same policy applies if any unfair means, including receiving help from online sources or solutions from prior years, have been resorted to.
2. **Collaboration during quizzes or receiving help from external or online sources is strictly prohibited**. **Any violation will result in a grade of ‘F’ in the course.**

If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
