## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This laboratory course offers a culminating experimental experience in the field of thermal and fluids engineering for MANE students. The theoretical background gained in previous courses is now consolidated and expanded by hands-on experiments and analysis of thermal and fluids systems. This lab course is intended to familiarize students with a variety of thermodynamic, heat transfer, and fluid flow phenomena in the context of the operation of major pieces of equipment. Both fundamental concepts as well as practical/applied ideas will be explored.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

There are no required texts for this class.

- *Introduction to Thermal and Fluids Engineering by Kaminski and Jensen* is the most recommended reference.
- The Library also contains the following books which may be used to reinforce fundamentals of heat transfer, thermodynamics and fluid mechanics: *Fundamentals of Thermal-Fluid Sciences* by Çengel and Turner, *Brief Introduction to Fluid Mechanics* by Young, Munson and Okiishi, *Fundamentals of Engineering Thermodynamics* by Moran and Shapiro, *Fundamentals of Heat and Mass Transfer* by Incropera and DeWitt, and *Fluid Mechanics* by White. In addition, you may find the book by Figliola & Beasley (*Theory and Design for Mechanical Measurements* 2nd Edition, 1995, Wiley) to be a useful reference.
- Materials specific to the four experiments performed in this class will be provided through handouts and lecture notes posted on LMS. Extensive handouts have been prepared on the first three experiments. These handouts include an introduction to the project, theory, specific objectives, equipment description and operating instructions, and lab report requirements. Another handout has been prepared to provide information on uncertainties in experimental data. It should be studied as carefully as the ones on individual experiments.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The topics to be covered will be a combination of new material specific to this lab course and material covered in previous courses.

The specific objectives of the course are:

1. To demonstrate thermodynamic, heat transfer, and fluid flow principles;
2. To integrate theory and practice through the operation of experiments and through the acquisition and analysis of experimental data;
3. To familiarize students with complex, realistic systems and the interactions among thermodynamics, heat transfer, and fluid flow;
4. To introduce some new concepts and techniques in the three topic areas;
5. To develop abilities to understand, experiment with, and analyze thermal and fluids systems, including analysis and decision making in the face of experimental uncertainty;
6. To exercise technical communication skills in a variety of modes.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

You will work together in teams, and there are a total of four laboratory experiences. Three weeks will be spent on each laboratory experience. You start preparations a week ahead of a scheduled experiment by reviewing the theoretical background and studying the experimental setup and procedures. You will be given a prelab assignment to complete, which will be due the day you perform the experiment. Each laboratory experience consists of two experimental investigations. You will be given a homework assignment of analyzing the data from your experiments and preparing your results. You may collaborate with your group members on this, but you will be asked to submit your own work individually. One week after you conducted the experiment, you and your group will meet in a studio-style discussion with your section’s instructor to review your results and talk about details of the lab. Your “Results Homework” is due at the start of this discussion session, and you should come prepared to (individually) explain how you obtained the values you submit. The rest of the discussion session time is available to you as a group to begin writing an extensive laboratory report which will be due after another week. The laboratory reports will be similar to technical reports produced by practicing engineers. It is recommended that you take advantage of the time during the discussion session to organize who will work on which parts of the report and to ask your instructor questions about the lab. Your reports will be graded as a group. Your prelabs, homeworks, and reports will be evaluated by your lab section instructor.

The experiments (in the order in which they will be performed) and the general concepts associated with each are listed below:

1. Hydraulic flow loop -- instrument calibration, friction factors (CII 5118)
2. Wind tunnel -- field concepts including velocity field, hydraulic boundary layers, transition to turbulence, heat transfer coefficients, multimode heat transfer (CII 5222)
3. Air conditioning system -- refrigeration cycles, psychrometrics, heat transfer, energy balances, refrigerant properties (JEC 2303)
4. Student Designed Lab on Critical Heat Flux -- convective heat transfer in a liquid, material science (JEC 2303). You will design your own experiment to determine the critical heat flux of fluids, determine the heat transfer coefficient, and visualize boiling bubble dynamics. You will have the option of using a pool boiling heat transfer module and its associated supplies or other supplies may be available (ask instructors). You will submit your plan in place of a prelab and will give an oral report, i.e., a presentation, instead of a written report. (Your presentation should cover all the same categories of content that would have been in a report. Your presentation slides must be submitted for grading.) There is no homework for Lab 4.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

In this course, academic dishonesty – cheating – can take the following forms.

- Submission of a lab report that, except for short phrases or properly referenced material (e.g., quotes and figures from the lab manual, etc.), consists of any content not created by and original to the report authors listed. Plagiarism may be penalized according to the severity and deliberateness of the act, from reduction of report grade to failure of the course and referral to the Dean of Students for dismissal from Rensselaer.
- Taking the final with any assistance from another person, regardless of who they are, or the medium of communication for assistance (talk, phone, texting, etc.).
- Using any materials that are not explicitly permitted during the final.

Mobile Devices: During the final all mobile devices (e.g., cell/smart phones, pagers, cameras, etc.) must be stored securely away and are not to be used unless specifically directed otherwise by the instructor. The use of, or any interaction with, a mobile device during a test without explicit permission of the instructor will be interpreted as the illicit transfer of data, which will be considered an act of cheating and treated as such.

The penalty for violating these policies is at the discretion of any or all members of the instructional team. In general, the penalty for any violation is failure of the course and referral to the Dean of Students

Collaboration: You are expected to collaborate with the members of your laboratory group with respect to the conduct of your experiments and the preparation of your reports. However, as stated above, your report, including the experimental data and observations described therein, must be those of your own group. In rare cases (e.g., lab equipment failure) your instructor may provide you with experimental data to use as if you had obtained it yourselves; this will be considered “your data”, regardless of the actual source.
