## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This introductory undergraduate to graduate course provides the knowledge necessary to model engineering applications consistent with fluid dynamic physical laws. Discretization models include overview of finite difference, finite element, and finite volume discretization approaches, physical problem classification, discretization techniques, and numerical guidelines. Applications include incompressible and compressible flows, laminar and turbulence flows, with introduction to computational fluid dynamics turbulence modeling, followed by boundary layer, and parabolized flow simplifications.

**Credits Allocated:** 3 semester hours

### Evaluation Criteria

**Assignments: Conceptual Questions (20%) and Homework (20%)**

Conceptual questions and homework will be submitted online via Canvas weekly. Both questions and homework answers should be submitted as a pdf document with the following naming convention:

***LastName_FirstName_Discussion_X.pdf***

***LastName_FirstName_Homework_X.pdf***

where X stands for module number (i.e., X=1 for module 1, X=2 for module 2 etc.) Any scanned documents should be correctly oriented and easily read.

**Class Participation Discussions Board (Address and Reply gates @ 15% each = 30 %)**

Class participation discussion board, interactive questions will be found in the “Discussion” folder in Canvas. Discussion boards will be used to facilitate student-student communication as well as student-instructor communication. It is expected that every student participates in these group discussions. Your participation in the discussion boards will be a major factor in determining your class participation grade, which is 30% of your final grade.

Participation in discussions may come in many forms. Examples of participation may include, but are not limited to:

- raising a question
- answering the question of a classmate
- explaining a step required to solve the problem
- explaining the process for solving the problem
- commenting on the post of a classmate
- guiding your classmates to the proper equations or section of the textbook
- providing a numerical answer to a part of the problem
- providing an expression necessary for solving the problem
- providing a schematic to add clarity to the problem
- Simple interactions like: “I agree” or similar will not be sufficient and will not be considered as relevant participation in the discussion

Rules for posting:

- You may not post a complete solution to the problem, even if it contains only variables.
- Simple interactions like: “I agree” or similar will not be sufficient and will not be considered as relevant participation in the discussion.
- Extra postings which are not related to the subject matter are totally prohibited (examples may include propaganda, personal views/comments not related to the subject matter).

Discussion boards will be used to facilitate student-student communication and exposure to different perspectives. The format of each discussion board question will vary. My expectations for your responses (content and length) will be explained for each discussion board. In general, responses should adhere to all standard writing, grammar, and punctuation rules. Abbreviations or “lingo” commonly used in texting is not acceptable.

Discussion board questions will not have a single “correct” answer and may intentionally be left vague (open) to allow for variety in the responses. In some discussion board questions, I will try to better understand how you are thinking about the material. Generally speaking, the instructor will provide the path for the best solution coming from different points of view. Thus, you will not lose points for wrong answers. You will only lose points if you do not put forth a good-faith effort or ignore the instructions. Otherwise, I encourage you to think and express yourself freely.

Your class participation grade will be based on the level and quality of involvement in the class. The goal for the class is that, through participation, students not only have their own learning experience, but can see and be part of the learning experiences of their classmates. Therefore, I would like to see everyone participate in online discussions, promote a positive learning environment, and work together with your classmates. Much of this participation will occur in the discussion boards, pseudo-office hours, and/or face-to-face sessions. In general, if students actively participate in these activities, they will receive 30%. However, I reserve the right to give less than 30% for students who are blatantly apathetic to discussions and/or the group nature of the class.

**Projects (2 @ 15% each = 30%)**

I would like to maximize the effectiveness of our interactions. Therefore, tests will not be given during these times. Instead, there will be two projects this semester. These projects may be interactive with other students, but the final report is an individual effort that will be graded individually.
