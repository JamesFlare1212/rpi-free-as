## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Intrinsic to engineering design are modeling and analysis, which this course introduces in the context of mechanics: the action of forces on material systems. The first half focuses on undeformable bodies in static equilibrium. The second half introduces the concepts of stress, traction, and force/displacement boundary conditions to model the mechanics of deformable bodies and structures in static equilibrium. Computational thinking, including vector and matrix algebra, will be fundamental to how we analyze our mechanics models of real-world systems.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

*Engineering Mechanics: Statics*, 15th Edition, R.C. Hibbeler, Pearson, 2022.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Topics covered:**

- Vector decomposition, vector algebra, unit vector, dot product, vector along a line, vectors in spherical and cylindrical coordinate systems
- Particle equilibrium in 2D/3D
- Cross product, moment of a force; moment of forces about axes; intuitive approach to moment of a force
- Force types (body and contact), distributed force, concentrated force equivalents
- Free body diagram; body equilibrium
- Friction, friction in machines
- Distributed loads
- Traction in pins, pinned joint model in free body diagrams; traction in fixed supports, fixed joint model in free body diagrams, roller joints; different joint models
- Machines/frames in 2D/3D
- Internal force, traction, stress
- Stress transformation, principal stresses/directions (eigenvalue analysis)
- Matrix structural analysis, simple truss

**Topics covered in the Computational Laboratory part**

- Lists and arrays and vectors and matrices as computational constructs
- Matrix algebra – operations and properties
- Linear systems and their solution
- Centroids of lamina and solids
- Moments of area
- Eigenvalues and eigenvectors
- Rotation of axes and reference frames

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- For mechanical systems (particle and undeformable bodies) in static equilibrium with a variety of boundary conditions: model the problem, solve the problem, and interpret the results.
- Visualize a model using free body diagrams and explain assumptions in modeling forces and various boundary conditions (e.g., joints, friction, distributed loads).
- Understand basic force/displacement boundary conditions and how they are implemented in mechanical systems’ models.
- Use simple hand calculations and robust computational tools to solve engineering problems using vector and matrix algebra.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
