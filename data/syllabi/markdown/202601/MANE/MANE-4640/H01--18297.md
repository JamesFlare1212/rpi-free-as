### How to Approach This Course

The Canvas course shell contains most of the course materials students will need and will be available at the start of the course. However, the shell will be continuously updated through the semester and you must make it a regular habit to log in and explore the contents thoroughly and to interact regularly, both with your instructor and with your classmates. Key components of a blended format course include live, interactive, video-streamed (synchronous) and pre-recorded sessions, assigned readings and other supporting online content, computer mediated activities and office hours. Please note that in the blended format, the LMS course shell is the lynchpin of the teaching-learning process (i.e. a 21st century version of the Classroom) and that the synchronous sessions are most successful and useful when they focus on the discussion of particularly troublesome or difficult concepts that may be hindering the student’s learning experience.

**Successful learning in this format requires strong and continuous student commitment, engagement and dedication. My role is to be ready to help you achieve your learning goals. Active participation in computer mediated activities, synchronous activities and the production, discussion and timely achievement and submission of the required milestones are all critical for success in this class.**

*Course Organization*

Course content is available online through the Canvas LMS. The course shell is organized around three key Milestones that must be achieved in order to demonstrate the student’s grasp of the subject. Each Milestone involves the development of specific abilities. Learning materials designed to assist this process are provided for each Milestone. Each milestone involves the following elements:

1. A collection of curated video recordings.
2. A collection of selected reading materials.
3. A collection of example computer programs.

*Course Methodology*

Students will learn how to analyze a manufacturing process by developing mathematical models using fundamental principles of mechanical engineering, science of materials and industrial engineering.

To achieve the stated learning objective, the course is being offered in **project format** where students select a specific manufacturing process from among the list of key topics at the outset of the course, make gradual inroads in their understanding of the intricacies of the selected process through computer experimentation and produce three cumulative Milestone reports describing their progress at specific dates through the semester. **Students MUST share their progress regularly with the entire class through the semester by actively participating in the Live Interactive Sessions.**

*What does it mean that the course is being offered in Project Format?*

In a Project Format course the achievement of learning objectives is demonstrated by successful completion of a semester-long, individual term project. Although there are live interactive sessions, there are no homework assignments or exams and learning is demonstrated through active participation in the live interactive sessions and particularly by completion of three project reports (Milestones) that successfully combine, blend and integrate the key course topics with the selected project topic. Students receive full credit only when the successful seamless integration of course topics and the project topic is clearly demonstrated. These are rather challenging and are most successfully completed when the student and the instructor work closely together through the semester during regular one-on-one interactions scheduled on an as-needed basis.

RPI has long being recognized world-wide as a technological institution pioneering innovative teaching and learning approaches. The Project Format courses represent a current trend in learning experimentation in that tradition.

In the Project Format all students must select a project topic area by the first day of class and declare it in writing in their Icebreaker report. Students must select one project topic based on their own personal and professional interests, delve into the selected topic to produce high quality partial progress reports and a final report of their work in accordance with the scheduled Milestone deadlines.

*The Importance of Continuous Communication*

Continuous communication with your instructor is the key for success in this course. In conventional courses (live or online), students sit passively listening to lectures, transcribing the instructor notes and providing answers to homework and exam questions; i.e. the communication is mostly one-directional. In most current online course offerings, students access the course materials through a LMS and also answer some questions to earn credit, i.e. students are mostly on their own. In contrast, in this course you are expected to interact extensively with your instructor through frequent, individualized one-on-one meetings.

Meetings do not take place at fixed days/times, instead, they are held at the student’s convenience and upon request, either by the student or by the instructor. While the course also includes a few live sessions where you are strongly encouraged to participate, much mentoring and learning in this course takes place during the one-on-one sessions. Please feel free to contact me anytime with your request for a one-on-one meeting. I will also be contacting you through your official RPI e-mail address asking you to get back to me to arrange a one-on-one meeting. **It is required that you check your official RPI email address at least once daily for those requests as well as for other important messages regarding this class.**

## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Review of basic aspects of manufacturing engineering including driving forces, quality attributes, tolerances, etc. Examination of basic principles of mechanics, engineering materials, analysis of both bulk-forming (forging, extrusion, rolling, etc.) and sheet-forming processes, metal cutting, and other related manufacturing processes. The role of computers in manufacturing is emphasized.

The course may contain all or some of the following key topics depending on the project topic choices made by the students:

1. Solidification Processing
2. Deformation Processing
3. Machining Processing
4. Heat Treatment Processing
5. Powder Particle Processing
6. Additive Manufacturing
7. Manufacturing Logistics

1. **Credits Allocated:** 3 semester hours

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

**Icebreaker**

Students shall complete the “Icebreaker” and submit the requested deliverable no later than the first day of classes. The icebreaker assignment consists of the following FOUR items:

- A brief written bio-sketch (~ 100 words long in PDF format).
- A computer screen capture proving that you have learned to access the required software for this class and it is ready for use.
- A signed statement indicating that you have carefully and thoroughly read through this course syllabus and are aware and in agreement with the information and instructions contained therein.
- The specific topic area selected for your term project.

**Milestone Reports**

Students shall demonstrate their grasp of the subject by creating three Milestone reports. Each Milestone will report on progress made on the analysis of their selected manufacturing process. Please note that reports are CUMULATIVE. Detailed constructive feedback will be provided after submission of Milestones 1 and 2 reports. Students will be expected to act on the feedback provided by reviewing and editing their reports as appropriate in preparation for the following report. Deadlines for these reports are listed in the [*Course Summary* area in our Rensselaer*Studio* digital classroom](https://rpi.instructure.com/) online.

- Milestone 3

Milestone reports must demonstrate mastery of the course material through its application to the student’s chosen term project topic. Reports must be carefully edited, well-written, persuasive, typeset documents in order answer to earn full credit. All milestone reports MUST be submitted with a cover page containing your name and the milestone number and in PDF format (see Report Template provided).

While Milestones 1 and 2 shall describe partial progress, Milestone 3 will be a comprehensive document that, while focusing on the most complex version of the student’s chosen topic, must also incorporate material from the reports produced for Milestones 1 and 2.
