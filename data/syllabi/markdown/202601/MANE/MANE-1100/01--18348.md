## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This Engineering Exploration Elective is a survey of current trends, future technologies, and active research in nuclear engineering, and how you can be a part of these advancements. Both nuclear power production and non-power applications of nuclear science and technology are included. Topics may include next generation nuclear power plants, environmental impacts of nuclear power, radiation protection, nuclear policy and regulation, and other topics of current interest. This course is graded Satisfactory/Unsatisfactory.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach and how student work is evaluated, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.  
In this class, all assignments must be significantly representative of your own work unless it is specifically stated that multiple authors are permitted. As an individual you will submit your own work with your own substantial and significant individual content and style.  
In cases where group work is allowed, a notation on the assignment should clearly identify all collaborators and, if requested or if you choose to, their individual contributions.  
That said, I encourage you to collaborate in this course to maximize your learning and to broaden your experiences in how one might apply what you learn to solving challenging problems. Nonetheless, at the end of the collaborative process you must have your own copy of your deliverable in which you identify your collaboration (e.g., in comments and/or references clearly indicated in the assignment). Prior to submission you should review and revise your deliverable to match your unique style and approach to organizing and communicating your information.  
Specific to this course, academic dishonesty can take the following forms.  
1\) Submission of a deliverable that, except for short phrases or properly referenced material, consists of any content not created by and original to you.  
2\) Indicating someone as “present” (e.g., on an attendance sheet or submitting an in-class exercise) who is not present in class at the time the indication is made.  
3\) Collaboration is encouraged. However, assisting someone else with work where they intend to turn in the work as if it was done on their own will itself be considered a serious violation of academic honesty. Give credit for any assistance received, and have sufficient content of your own making according to the expectations of the deliverable.  
4\) You may use (so-called) Artificial Intelligence tools as a form of collaboration. As we will experience in this course, when used well it can be a strong asset for your success. But see #1, above: if what you submit is not substantially created by you and original to you, you have committed academic dishonesty.  
Submission of a deliverable that is in violation of this policy will result in a grade of zero on the assignment for the first offense. If there is a subsequent infraction the student fails the course and is referred to the Dean of Students with a recommendation for suspension.  
If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting a deliverable please ask for clarification.  
If you are ever concerned about passing the course please talk to me as soon as possible. I will work with you as I would anyone and everyone in class to help you help yourself. A student’s dishonesty in this course might be motivated by greed or an inflated sense of entitlement, but it should never be motivated by fear.
