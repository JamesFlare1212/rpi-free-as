## Course Content

### Content Details:

This course covers a variety of topics in nuclear engineering materials utilized in different nuclear environments and materials selection for nuclear applications; Engineering materials for light water reactors including fuels, cladding, structural materials, waste form materials and materials for next generation nuclear reactors. Specific topics include crystal structure of materials; atomic bonds; defects in solids; thermodynamics (phase diagram); kinetic processes in materials diffusion; phase transformation; mechanical behavior of materials; corrosion; radiation effects on materials due to neutrons and charged particles; properties of zircalloy; and materials for next generation nuclear reactors.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Apply fundamental knowledge of radiation interaction with matters to solve nuclear materials problems
- Identify the fundamental principles of engineering materials for nuclear applications, challenges and opportunities for advanced nuclear energy systems
- Develop the skills using modern engineering tools to evaluate radiation interaction with materials
- Explain/describe the importance of kinetics and thermodynamics on materials phenomena under radiation environments

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own performance. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

Discussion and interaction among students are encouraged; however, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Evidence of any student merely transcribing another student's work will be grounded zero for the particular homework. Subsequent violation of this policy will result in a penalty of a grade of F given for failure in the course and also further disciplinary action as outlined in the Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities.
