## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The objective of this course is to introduce the concepts in fluid mechanics topics viz. Fundamental concepts on fluids, Fluid Properties, Fluid Statics, Kinematics of fluids, Fluid flow and classification, Control volume, Conservation equations, Differential treatment of fluid flow, Dimensional analysis, Inviscid and viscous flow, Flow inside pipes, Boundary layer flow, and Fundamentals of compressible flow.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Textbook:  
No textbook required. Lecture notes will be posted on the course website/LMS. The notes are prepared from several books and predominantly from the reference texts listed below.  
Reference books:

- 1\) Fluid Mechanics Fundamentals and Applications, Yunus A. Cengel, John M. Cimbala, McGraw-Hill
- 2\) Fluid Mechanics, Frank M. White, McGraw-Hill
- 3\) Introduction to Fluid Mechanics, Robert W. Fox, Alan T. McDonald, John W. Mitchell, John Wiley & Sons
- 4\) Fluid Mechanics, R. C. Hibbeler, Pearson.

Notes for students who prefer a textbook: Cengel is a good, easy-to-understand book with sufficiently detailed explanations for derivations. Fox and McDonald book is concise and bit heavy sometimes for undergraduates. Hibbeler helps fast readers, with just enough information. White is overall a good book in all the above aspects.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The objective of this course is to introduce the concepts in fluid mechanics topics viz. Fundamental concepts on fluids, Fluid Properties, Fluid Statics, Kinematics of fluids, Fluid flow and classification, Control volume, Conservation equations, Differential treatment of fluid flow, Dimensional analysis, Inviscid and viscous flow, Boundary layer flow and a few applications: Flow inside pipes, Flow around surfaces etc.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The following topics will be covered in this course.

- Fundamental concepts of Fluid Mechanics
  - o Viscosity, properties of fluids, classification of flows
- Fluid statics
  - o Pressure, buoyancy
  - o Hydrostatic forces on surfaces
- Basic equations in integral form
  - o Control volume
  - o Conservation laws
  - o Reynolds transport theorem
  - o Bernoulli’s equation
- Basic equations in differential form
  - o Momentum, energy, mass equations
  - o Navier Stokes equation
- Incompressible inviscid flow
- Dimension analysis
- Boundary layer flow
- Laminar and turbulent flow
- Applications (optional)
  - o Flow inside pipes
  - o Flow around surfaces

# Class exercises

Class exercises (ICEs) will be assigned at the end of many (if not most) lectures to provide insight into the contents and reinforce the understanding of the covered topics. Students can interact with the instructional team to complete the ICEs within the class duration. The **lowest five** of the ICE scores will be **dropped** from the calculation of final grades**.** The dropped scores cover all absences including excused absence, illness, family-related absence, sports related absence etc. For classes with no assigned ICEs (for possible reasons like lack of time etc.), full score will be assigned to all students. **Students must submit their own submission and be prepared to submit a hard (paper) version of the ICE if required by the instructional team**. **Any communication regarding missed ICEs will not be entertained.** Students with accommodation(s) are encouraged to communicate with the instructional team regarding any concerns on ICEs at any time during the course.

# HOMEWORK assignments

Homework submission needs to be clear and legible. Please write clearly starting your assumptions, appropriate expressions before starting your calculations. In this course, collaboration in the form of discussion of approach to the problems is permitted to a degree. It is expected that each student’s work will be their own, in both form and content. This means that even if students collaborated on working out problems in the homework assignment, the final work that is handed in must not be a clone of another student’s work. It is good practice to list the people that you collaborated with. Please refer to the schedule (at the end of this document) for the due dates of the homework assignments. The **lowest** of the homework scores will be dropped from the calculation of final grades. The dropped score(s) cover all absences including excused absence, illness, family-related absence, sports related absence etc.

# Tests

Two closed-book in-person tests will be administered in the classroom and during the class timings for a duration of about 60 minutes. The lectures applicable for each test are mentioned in the course schedule (see below). Scientific calculator and a **single** A4/letter sized **self-written crib sheet (both sides)** will be permitted for the tests. The crib sheet needs to be attached to the submitted exam. The crib sheet **must contain only equations with small subtitles and must not have any figures, explanations, nor worked examples**. Dates for the tests shall be found in the course schedule (at the end of this document). Grading challenges for tests will be addressed within two weeks after they are returned. Students with accommodation(s) shall contact the instructor within the first two weeks of the start of the course.

**Test makeup:** There will be one makeup test at the end of the semester for those who seek absence on any of the two tests due to reasons beyond their control, which includes illness, family-related absence, sports related absence etc. Students need to inform the instructor about their absence prior to the end of the class on the missed test day. Students who happen to miss both the tests (considered a rather unlikely scenario) are required to make up one of them on the makeup test day and discuss with the instructor regarding the overall test score. No class for other students on the makeup test day.

# FINAL EXAM:

All the course contents covered are applicable for the final exam. All students are required to attend the final exam. No makeup will be conducted, except for course conflict on the exam duration. In case a student missing the final exam, the instructor will evaluate the reason and circumstances of the absence and may calculate a cumulative score replacing the final exam score based on the student’s performance in all the tests, all ICE submissions and all homework submissions – including the dropped scores. **Two** papers of A4/letter size **self-written crib sheet (both sides)** will be permitted for the final exam. The crib sheet **must contain only equations with small subtitles and must not have any figures, explanations, nor worked examples**.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

# Collaboration and academic integrity:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that the students turn in are their own. Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational process. Students are expected to always conduct themselves in a professional manner.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In addition, attempts to commit dishonesty, or to assist in the commission or attempt of such act, are also violations of the academic dishonesty policy. If found in violation of the academic dishonesty policy, students may be subject to two types of penalties. The instructor administers an academic (grade) penalty, and students may also enter the institute judicial process and be subject to such additional sanctions as: warning, probation, suspension, expulsion, and alternative actions as defined in the current *Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities.* The following specific collaboration rules apply to this class:

1. Students are encouraged to collaborate in the solution of homework and practicum problems but **submit independent solutions that are NOT copies of each other**. If a student is found in violation of this policy, **a grade of zero will be awarded for the first violation and a grade of ‘F’ in the course for the second violation**. The same policy applies if any unfair means, including receiving help from online sources or solutions from prior years, have been resorted to.
2. **Collaboration during tests or receiving help from external or online sources is strictly prohibited**. **Any violation will result in a grade of ‘F’ in the course.**

Mobile Devices Policy for ***Resident*** Lecture: All mobile devices (cell/smart phones, tabs, etc.) must be stored securely away during lecture and are not to be used unless specifically directed otherwise by the instructor. Use of (or **ANY** interaction with) a mobile device during an exam without explicit permission of the instructor will be interpreted as the illicit transfer of exam data, will be considered an act of cheating and will be treated as such.

If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
