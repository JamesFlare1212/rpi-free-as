## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Topics**: An introduction to airplanes and flight mechanics; inviscid and irrotational flow: incompressible potential flow theory; thin airfoil theory; finite wing theory; compressible flow and shock waves; rocket-nozzle theory; subsonic compressible flow over airfoils: linear theory; linearized supersonic flow; effects of viscosity: boundary layer theory applied to airfoils, flow separation and stall.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

*Fundamentals of Aerodynamics*, Sixth edition, by John D. Anderson, McGraw Hill, Boston, 2017. Please note, 4th or 5th editions are fine as long as you ensure that the problems are the same.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

To gain a fundamental understanding of aerodynamics principles pertaining to inviscid incompressible flow, inviscid compressible flow, and viscous flow with and without compressibility.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\) Ability to perform basic calculations of Flight Mechanics for steady (unaccelerated) flight.
- Ability to perform standard atmosphere calculations for the Troposphere and Tropopause regions.
- Ability to estimate non-standard day conditions in the Troposphere based on modified linear temperature calculations.
- Ability to calculate flow field quantities including Stream Function, Velocity Potential, and Vorticity.
- Ability to perform calculations of Circulation based on its definition and/or Stokes' Theorem.
- Ability to perform Potential Flow calculations using superposition of solutions.
- Ability to perform calculations for potential flow associated with a cylinder in uniform flow, with and without circulation.
- Ability to perform calculations of lift-per-unit-span based on Kutta-Joukowski Theorem.
- Ability to perform calculations based on Thin-Airfoil Theory for both symmetrical and cambered airfoils.
- Ability to perform induced flow calculations based on Biot-Savart Law.
- Ability to perform wing aerodynamics calculations based on Prandtl Lifting-Line Theory.
- Ability to perform calculations of isentropic flows.
- Ability to perform calculations of normal and oblique shock waves.
- Ability to perform calculations of Prandtl-Meyer expansion fans.
- Ability to perform quasi-one-dimensional flow calculations in choked and unchocked converging-diverging ducts, including cases with shock waves inside or outside the duct.
- Ability to perform calculations of compressible, subsonic flow using linear theory.
- Ability to perform calculations associated with Critical Mach number for aircraft, based on linear theory.
- Ability to perform calculations of supersonic flow based on linear theory.
- Ability to perform calculations of viscous flow in fully-developed systems, without dissipation.
- Ability to perform calculations of viscous flow in fully-developed systems, with dissipation.
- Ability to use Blasius Boundary Layer results to compute details of laminar flow on a flat plate.
- Ability to use empirical results to compute details of turbulent flow on a flat plate.
- Ability to perform simple calculations pertaining to temperature distribution and associated heat transfer in Couette flow, with and without dissipation.
- Ability to perform flow calculations on plannar wedges using the Falkner-Skan solution.
- Ability to perform basic estimates of flow separation based on the Falkner-Skan solution.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments which students turn-in are their own. Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational process. The *Rensselaer Handbook* defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Students should familiarize themselves with this portion of the *Handbook* and should note that the penalties for plagiarism and other forms of cheating can be quite harsh. While collaborative or group work will be encouraged in many instances, there will also be times when individual work is required, such as during the tests and the final exam. While collaboration may be helpful when thinking about a particular homework or computer assignment, check with the instructor or the TA about when an assignment is meant to be collaborative vs. individual.
