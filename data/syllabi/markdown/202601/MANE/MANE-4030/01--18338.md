## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Introduction to the design of mechanical components and integrated assemblies. Loads, stresses, and strains. Failure phenomena and material selection. Mechanical components including shafts, couplings, bearings, gears, springs, clutches, brakes, screws and fasteners, and bonded joints. (4 credit hours)

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

*Mechanical Design of Machine Elements and Machines, Second Edition*, by Jack A. Collins, Henry Busby, and George Staab, John Wiley & Sons, 2010. ISBN: 9780470413036.

*Shigley’s Mechanical Engineering Design, Eleventh Edition*, by Richard G. Budynas and J. Keith Nisbet, McGraw-Hill Education, 2020. ISBN: 9780073398211.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course is designed to introduce the student to the design process through a thorough study of mechanical design. Problem definition and solution requiring both analysis and optimization are emphasized, while studying both the fundamentals of mechanical design and specific mechanical components.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Demonstrate an ability to apply knowledge of mathematics, science and engineering to analyze the stress and deflection in a variety of machine elements; Predict failure in machine elements subjected to static, cyclic, and impact loads; Design and analyze a variety of commonly used machine elements.
- • Demonstrate an ability to identify, formulate, and solve engineering problems involving stress and deflections in machine elements, identification of potential failure modes, and performing the appropriate analysis.
- • Demonstrate an ability to use the techniques, skills, and modern engineering tools necessary for engineering practice such as drawing free-body-diagrams, relating loading conditions to stresses, identifying potential failure modes and appropriate analysis techniques.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Exams:** There will be three in-class exams, all of which will count towards the final grade. There will also be an optional cumulative final exam during final exam week (or last week of classes during summer). During exams, you are permitted printouts of posted class notes, including homework solutions, and a physical copy of the textbook (Mechanical Design of Machine Elements and Machines, Second Edition, by Collins, Busby, and Staab and/or *Shigley’s Mechanical Engineering Design, Eleventh Edition*, by Richard G. Budynas and J. Keith Nisbet) or printouts of pages from the textbook, a pocket calculator, and a straight edge. You are not permitted to use any other electronic devices such as laptops, tablets, phones, smartwatches, etc. You are also not permitted to bring printouts of solutions to any previous exams. Students are encouraged to create summary crib-sheets. The exams will emphasize problem solving. The nature of the topics in this class is cumulative so students are expected to be familiar with fundamental concepts taught in the earlier part of the semester which will be required for exams held in the later part of the semester. Solutions of some of the in-class exams may be posted on LMS. If the optional final exam is not taken, the three in-class exams will count 25% (lowest exam score), 30%, and 30% towards the overall score. If the final exam is taken, the three in-class exams will be scaled to count as 50% of the overall score, and the final exam will count as 35% of the overall score.

The Final Exam will serve as a make-up exam for any missed exams. If a student misses any of the three in-class exams for any reason, the student must take the Final Exam, and the score on the Final Exam will count as both the Final Exam and the missed exam. For example, if a student misses one in-class exam, the Final Exam will count for 49.7% of the overall score.

**In-class assignments:** In-class assignment problems will be assigned on most classes and will be due by end of class time. The problems will be of varied nature and difficulty and will be based on the material covered up to that point; there may be multiple in-class assignments on the same class. It is essential that students do the in-class assignment problems and discuss their work with the instructor. In-class assignments and course activities will involve problem solving and computational or digital work, so students are required to bring a laptop to class.

**Homework:** Homework problems will be assigned weekly. Homework assignments will be posted on LMS. You are expected to spend 2-8 hours outside of class each week doing work associated with this class, and the size of the homework and reading assignments reflects this. Most weeks, homework will be collected and graded. There may be more homework assignments then listed in the posted schedule at the beginning of the semester. Submission of homework assignments is via Gradescope only. Homework assignment submissions are due by 9 PM on the day listed. Late homework will be accepted with a penalty of 10% off that assignment for each 8 hours late. The graded homework, after dropping the lowest score, will count for 15% of the overall grade. The homework grading may not be detailed, so you should always check your solutions against the posted homework solutions. *It is essential that students do the problems and check their work against posted solutions to ensure that they understand the material.*
