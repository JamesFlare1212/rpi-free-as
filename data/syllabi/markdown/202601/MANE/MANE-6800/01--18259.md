## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Summary:** *Manufacturing Systems Integration* focuses on the details of production systems – an organized collection of people, equipment, and procedures located within a facility for the purpose of manufacturing discrete products for sale – and how all aspects of such systems are integrated (via the intelligent use of computer control and automation) to efficiently accomplish the manufacturing operations of a company. Topics are drawn from the fields of manufacturing, industrial, mechanical and computer systems engineering along with business management.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Textbook: Groover, M.P., *Automation, Production Systems, and Computer-Integrated Manufacturing*, 5th Edition, Pearson Education, Inc., New York, 2019 (ISBN-10: 0133499618 • ISBN-13: 9780134605463).

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Course Objectives:** At the conclusion of this course, a student should know:

- The realities of modern manufacturing
- The high-level details about the international supply chain and different types of manufacturing systems and their components, how such systems are modeled, and metrics used to characterize them
- Technical details about automation (including robotics & cobotics), industrial control (analog and discrete) and security, computer numerical control, material handling, material and product storage, and automatic identification and data capture; how these technologies are applied to manufacturing systems; and how to quantify their performance to make system-level design decisions
- How to design and quantify the performance of manual and automated manufacturing (processes) and assembly systems, cellular manufacturing systems, and flexible manufacturing lines, and
- How to implement and quantify the performance of different types of quality control systems.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.
