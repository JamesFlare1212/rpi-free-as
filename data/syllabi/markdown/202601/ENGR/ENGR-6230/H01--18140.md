### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

### How to Approach This Course

So, the course is based on one project where you will be submitting a phase for each of the DMAIC framework – here is the approach:

We will have six synchronous sessions, in which we discuss and scope the approach. They are:

1. **Project Kick Off:** conducted during the first week of class to discuss project selection and course layout.
2. **Project set up & Minitab Part 1:** goes through the participative approach, DICE, and Pre-Mortem. Introduces the students to Minitab as the statistical tool for this course.
3. **Minitab Part 2**: go through Q&A on how the Minitab reports are run and interpreted.
4. **Leadership & Minitab Part 3:** goes through Conflict Resolution methodology, Capability Analysis, and Hypothesis testing.
5. **PDCA Action Log w/Benefits**: review with live examples of how to extract the benefits from a successful PDCA action.
6. **Lessons Learned & Final Presentation:** goes through Lessons Learned, Minitab comparison, and Do’s / Don’ts for the Final presentation.

The digital classroom will walk you through a series of steps – the approach – and provide you with the learning resources you will need right when you need them. In addition, you will be able to access the LSS Library and Minitab Instructions throughout the semester. You will see that most of your deliverables will have past students’ examples embedded as videos.

I am available to assist you at any point where and when you need – Zoom, text, phone, or in person if that suits you. Just reach out and we will schedule something mutually convenient.

As you progress through, you will prepare a series of “deliverables” – artifacts that show your work to that point. You will submit these deliverables in the digital classroom.

I will evaluate your deliverables and give you feedback – feedback you can incorporate for your final presentation.

When you have completed your phase, I will evaluate it according to the pre-established rubric (which you can see in the classroom before you submit). If your project meets the criteria, you can move to the next phase.

When you are done with all the phases and you have received feedback that your work has met the rubric criteria, you will consolidate all the phases into one cohesive presentation which will be your Final.

My role in the course is to serve as your Mentor as you work through the project. Rensselaer hires us to be Mentors based on our academic background as well as our years of being a practitioner in the field – and I still continue to actively practice in the profession as a full-time endeavor.  
Therefore my role is to be your Mentor – to be your guide, to give you feedback, to be there wherever you have questions or need assistance. We can schedule individual times to meet and discuss, we can do it during a HW session, we can do whatever is needed so that you have what you need, and where you need it, to successfully master the course.  
If you are to receive a zero for your deliverable because it failed to meet the rubric criteria and thus, you require to resubmit, you will lose 2 points. For lateness in submitting your deliverables, I will deduct 1 point per day. If you contact me prior to the due date, informing me of the possible lateness, you will not receive any deductions in your grade. The reason for the grading deducts is to ensure you are progressing in your journey within the allotted timeline of this course. It is vital you complete the work sequentially and as per the instructions so to avoid “waste” in rework. I want you to always be submitting your best work – work that you feel strongly not only meets the criteria but also is a deliverable you would proudly submit to your manager at work. When this occurs, the focus is no longer on the “grade” but on your mastery.  
When a student goes above and beyond the outlined criteria and submits top-quality work in respect to the other students, then I will be posting their work in the discussion board for everyone to view. In addition, the student will receive a bonus point to the overall grade of this course.

There will be supplemental deliverables that are going to be optional for you to complete. If you successfully complete them as per the outlined criteria, then you can be rewarded with up to 5 additional bonus points going towards your overall deliverables percentage.  
Finally, I know that you are a working professional with many draws to your time and energy. I also know how big of a commitment it is to work on a graduate degree while employed – I did it myself. So, please, the key to success is communication. If you need additional time for your deliverable, please let me know. We can work on a workaround together. If you are struggling, do not wait until you are at the end of the phase, reach out – we can schedule a mutual time to discuss your issue and get back on track. DMAIC projects on general take three to nine months to complete. If you have a commitment and cannot attend a synchronous session, let me know – I will be recording and posting all sessions including the weekly “Open Forum” so you can partake at a later time. In any case, let me know – DMAIC projects are usually completed within a three-to-nine-month time frame. In this course we are going through a formula that ensures you can do it in thirteen weeks as long as you keep with the pace.

Please take a look at this syllabus document and plan ahead for the synchronous sessions and the project milestones as laid out. Remember, I am here to mentor and guide you through this journey, so take advantage in developing your mastery in DMAIC projects. I hope you have an enlightened and outstanding semester. And most of all, I hope you improve your workplace!

Welcome to the start of your Lean Six Sigma journey! This course will introduce you to Lean Six Sigma philosophy. The goal is to use the “Show by Doing” mentality making you AMBASSADORS for change. It is having you grasp the paradigm shift from “Increasing Quality at work means higher costs” to “Increasing Quality means reducing costs.”  
This course will be hands-on where you will be selecting the problem you want to solve at your workplace. The objective is to apply the Lean Six Sigma methodologies at your workplace to learn how you can improve your environment.

## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Builds the main perspectives of Lean Six Sigma including quality improvements, waste reduction, and best practices that ultimately lead to fewer defects and increased efficiency. Topics covered include the principles, qualitative methods, process improvements, and problem-solving techniques for improving existing business processes. The emphasis is on the Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, and Control (DMAIC) phases of Lean Six Sigma.
