## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Data summary and presentations; probability; discrete and continuous probability distributions; descriptive statistics; confidence intervals; hypothesis testing; regression analysis and introduction into machine learning; use of computer applications in statistical analysis. The examples and exercises strongly emphasize engineering applications and the use of R language.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Uwe Kruger & Xun Wang. Modeling and Analysis of Uncertainty - A Theoretical Foundation and Practical Guide to Engineering Probability, Statistics and Regression Analysis. ISBN: 978-1-60797-993-7. Linus Learning, 2023.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course teaches students

- An appreciation for uncertainty in the application of engineering models,
- The difference between probability and statistics,
- Discrete and continuous distribution functions,
- Marginal and conditional distributions,
- Descriptive statistics,
- Sample distributions and point estimates,
- Confidence intervals,
- Hypothesis testing.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Students will be able to:

- Solve for the probability and conditional probability of events in various scenarios;
- Obtain and graph information about random variables and variable sets;
- Identify variable distributions correctly and apply various discrete and continuous distribution functions;
- Construct and interpret one-sample and two-sample confidence intervals;
- Formulate hypotheses tests and perform the tests using the method of critical region, p-value, and confidence interval.

## Grading Criteria

- **Tests and final exam (80%):** There are three tests on Wednesdays (10 – 11:30 am) and a final exam. They are weighed as: test 1 – 15%, test 2 – 15%, test 3 – 20%, and final exam – 30%. Each of the three tests is 1.5 hours long and the final exam is 2 hours long. All tests are cumulative but with emphasis on the material since the last test. The final exam includes the entire semester. They are all close-book, calculator-free, and in-person. Relevant formulas and statistical tables will be provided.

One of the essential skills for an engineer is being able to clearly communicate their ideas and process in problem solving. Therefore, the majority of points are allotted to proper showing of work on tests and projects. Examples of sufficient, detailed work shown can be found on LINUS LMS past test solutions.

- **Projects (10%):** There are two projects based on R, each counting for 5% of overall grade. Late submissions receive a reduction of 25% per day from points earned.
- **Quizzes (10%):** There are 9 quizzes taken either in person or remotely via LINUS LMS (https://mau.linuslearning.net/) on Wednesdays (10 – 10:30 am).

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).
