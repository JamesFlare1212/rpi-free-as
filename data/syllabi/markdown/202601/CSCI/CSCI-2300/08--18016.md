## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

There is no official textbook for the course, but a primary resource for you (and the instructor!) is Introduction to the Design and Analysis of Algorithms by Anany Levitin. Other useful texts include Introduction to Algorithms by Cormen, Leiserson, Rivest, and Stein; Algorithms by Dasgupta, Papadimitriou, and Vazirani; and the definitive multi-volume tome The Art of Computer Programming by Knuth.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Learning goals:  
Students will learn a variety of algorithms for solving common problems in computer science (e.g. searching, sorting).

Students will be able to use the tools and vocabulary of complexity theory to prove the correctness of algorithms, analyze their efficiency, and select the most appropriate one from several candidate solutions.

Students will become familiar with several methods for constructing algorithms, including greedy approaches, divide-and-conquer, and dynamic programming.

Students will be able to use all of the above techniques to produce algorithmic solutions to novel problems.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

We are scheduled for 28 lectures, 9 labs, and 3 exams during the term, plus a final exam. You are expected to attend each class period; however, we all have things that are higher priority than this course. To account for this, you are permitted to miss three lectures with no impact on your course grade; after this, each absence reduces your overall course score by 0.5%. Similarly, you may miss one lab; after that, each missed lab is a 2% deduction. A quick e-mail informing me of your absence is courteous but is not required. In the event that external factors causes a more lengthy absence, this policy may be waived so long as the student promptly informs me so that we can come to a reasonable alternative arrangement.

The beginning and end of a class period are times when attention is highest; disrupting these is detrimental to your classmates. You are strongly encouraged to arrive on time and to stay until the end of class. (For labs, you must either complete the task or stay until the end of the period to obtain credit for attending.)

Students may not use their “free” absences on exam days. In the event of an emergency absence on an exam day, a makeup exam will be given. Other exam absences may, at the sole discretion of the instructor, result in a zero for that exam or in a makeup exam with a grade penalty of up to 25%.

### Other Course Policies:

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

For this course: Any violation of academic integrity is grounds for immediate failure of the course. If you have any doubts about whether something is permitted, ASK FIRST.

Specific academic integrity policies:  
• Submitting responses or files copied from another student or any other source (even if later modified) is never allowed.  
• Students are explicitly barred from using Chegg, StackOverflow, and similar “answers” sites for their work on this course.  
• Students are explicitly barred from using ChatGPT or any other large-language model or AI system for any activity related to this course.  
• Absolutely no collaboration is allowed during exams.  
• When completing homework assignments, students are encouraged to collaborate with and seek help from the instructor, TA, mentors, and classmates. However, each student must submit their own assignment, and in doing so, they affirm that the work is their own. Remember: directly providing answers is not help, it’s cheating.  
• A student whose work is copied by another student will also be subject to discipline under the academic integrity policy. You are responsible for ensuring that your work is not copied by another student.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

Take care of yourselves and each other.

This is a challenging course at a challenging university. It is very likely to cause you stress at times during the term, and if it doesn’t, some other course probably will. Some stress is fine – it can lead us to perform better than we might have otherwise, sometimes better than we knew we could. But it doesn’t work unless you are in a position to handle it.

This means you need to eat, you need to sleep, you need to exercise, you need to shower, and you need to take time to be social and have fun. No college course is worth sacrificing your health over.

If you start to feel overwhelmed, there are many resources available to help you on campus: the Health Center, the Counseling Center, your friends and classmates, and your instructor. Don’t suffer in silence. If you notice that another student appears to be struggling, reach out! Sometimes all it takes is someone noticing.

Finally, if you have some issue (on or off campus) that is affecting your class performance, please let me know – I can be very flexible as long as you’re communicating with me. I want you to be both successful and happy, and I will do what I can to facilitate that.
