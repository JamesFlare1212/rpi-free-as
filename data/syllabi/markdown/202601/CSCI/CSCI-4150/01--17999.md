## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course provides a foundational introduction to the principles and techniques of Artificial Intelligence (AI), with an emphasis on computational approaches to decision-making, reasoning, and learning in complex environments. The course is designed to:

- Introduce the fundamental concepts and paradigms underlying modern AI systems, including search, planning, probabilistic reasoning, and reinforcement learning.
- Develop an understanding of how intelligent agents perceive, reason about, and act within uncertain and dynamic environments.
- Provide students with hands-on experience in implementing core AI algorithms and evaluating their performance in practical problem-solving settings.
- Explore the trade-offs between different AI methodologies in terms of optimality, efficiency, and scalability.
- Foster the ability to formulate real-world problems as computational decision-making tasks and design appropriate algorithmic solutions.
- Examine the broader impacts and limitations of AI technologies, including ethical considerations and responsible deployment.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

This course covers the foundational methodologies used in the design and implementation of intelligent agents. Topics include:

- Introduction to Artificial Intelligence and Intelligent Agents
- Problem Formulation and State-Space Search
- Uninformed Search Methods (e.g., Breadth-First Search, Depth-First Search)
- Informed Search and Heuristic Design (e.g., A*)
- Adversarial Search and Game Playing (e.g., Minimax, Alpha-Beta Pruning)
- Constraint Satisfaction Problems
- Knowledge Representation and Logical Reasoning
- Decision Making Under Uncertainty
- Probabilistic Models and Bayesian Networks
- Markov Decision Processes
- Reinforcement Learning (e.g., Value Iteration, Policy Iteration, Q-Learning)
- Ethical and Societal Implications of Artificial Intelligence

Students will gain practical experience through programming assignments that involve implementing and evaluating core AI algorithms in simulated environments.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Student achievement of the course learning outcomes will be assessed through a combination of the following components:

- **Programming Assignments:** Students will implement core AI algorithms such as search methods, adversarial decision-making techniques, and reinforcement learning approaches in simulated environments. These assignments evaluate students’ ability to translate theoretical concepts into functional computational solutions.
- **Written Homework Assignments:** Analytical problem sets will assess students’ understanding of key AI concepts, including problem formulation, heuristic design, probabilistic reasoning, and decision-making under uncertainty.
- **Midterm Examination:** The midterm exam evaluates students’ comprehension of foundational AI principles and their ability to apply algorithmic techniques to structured problem-solving tasks.
- **Final Examination:** The final exam assesses cumulative understanding of the full range of course topics, including probabilistic models and reinforcement learning.

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students must work independently on all course assignments. You may consult other members of the class on the assignments, but you must submit your own work. For instance you may discuss general approaches to solving a problem, but you must implement the solution on your own (similarity detection software may be used). Anytime you borrow material from the web or elsewhere, you must acknowledge the source. Copying and pasting from published sources or the internet is considered plagiarism and is not acceptable. Plagiarized work will receive an automatic grade of zero.

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Submission of any assignment that is in violation with these policies will result in a penalty that is deemed by the instructor to be appropriate to the infraction ranging from a grade of zero on the assignment in question, to failure of the class as a whole. The student will also be reported to the Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education as appropriate. Note that academic dishonesty will be dealt with severely and will be reported to the Dean of Students. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
