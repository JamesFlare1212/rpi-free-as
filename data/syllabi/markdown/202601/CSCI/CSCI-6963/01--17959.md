## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This seminar course explores the emerging field of distributed quantum computing, where information is processed and shared across interconnected quantum systems. The course will cover three broad areas: quantum networks and their applications; distributing quantum programs, and hybrid quantum-classical computing. We will work together to develop a deep understanding of this content through just-in-time lectures on foundational topics, class presentations and discussions of research papers; and student-led tutorials. Students will also create a research or survey project of their choosing.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Describe the fundamental building blocks of quantum networks and distributed quantum applications.
- Critically evaluate research publications on quantum networks and distributed quantum computing.
- Develop and deliver presentations on research publications.
- Design and implement software for quantum computers and simulators.
- Develop and execute a research or survey project on distributed quantum computing.
- Identify research gaps in distributed quantum computing and propose new ideas.
- Explain how distributed quantum computing impacts their own research topics.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The assessment measures are: paper presentations, creation of tutorial, class participation and worksheets, and a research project.

## Grading Criteria

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Paper presentations*:* You may use resources beyond the paper, including Generative AI. You are responsible for ensuring the correctness of all of the resources you use. You must create your own presentation; you may integrate these external resources as appropriate. If you use someone else’s work (figures, research publications, slides, etc.) you must (1) indicate how this work was used, and (2) acknowledge this work in a bibliography slide.

Tutorials: You may use any resources that you find useful. IBM has many good tutorials that you can use as a starting point. Your tutorial cannot be an exact copy of any existing tutorial; it should be customized for this course. As with paper presentations if you use someone else’s work (figures, research publications, slides, etc.) you must (1) indicate how this work was used, and (2) acknowledge this work in a bibliography slide.

Research projects: Details on references and attributions will be discussed when the project is assigned. We will determine the policy on the use of Generative AI as a class. All students will be expected to adhere to this policy.

Violation of these policies will be considered a breach of academic integrity and will result in a grade penalty ranging from a reduction of a single letter grade through receiving an F in the course, depending on the severity. Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the Dean of Students. If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
