## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Modern ML formulates goals as optimization problems and applies suitable algorithms. The first part of the course introduces convex optimization for analyzing deterministic and randomized optimization methods; the second part introduces a selection of modern ML architectures. Homeworks give hands-on algorithmic experience and assess understanding of theoretical aspects. A project gives the students experience with proper research methodology, critically reading the research literature, and effective technical communication.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Formulate ML tasks as optimization problems.
- Apply convex analysis and optimality conditions.
- Implement first-order and stochastic solvers.
- Discuss trade-offs among solvers.
- Implement deep-learning architectures and explain inductive biases
- Apply ML to real problems and evaluate performance.
- Analyze and adapt their communication to the technical contexts of machine learning and optimization, clearly identifying the goals, assumptions, and methodological approaches of optimization algorithms and ML architectures.
- Communicate effectively in course-relevant genres, including algorithmic write-ups, empirical experiment summaries, technical reports, and technical presentations.
- Explain optimization concepts and machine-learning methods clearly and succinctly, including the formulation of learning problems, interpretation of model behavior, and justification of algorithmic and architectural design choices.
- Identify and apply appropriate conventions for technical communication in ML and optimization, including the use of mathematical notation, pseudocode, convergence plots, learning-curve visualizations, tables of experimental results, and well-structured written and oral exposition.
- Evaluate strong convexity, smoothness, and nonconvexity in algorithm design.
- Critically evaluate ML literature and communicate conclusions.
- Deduce performance guarantees (convergence and computational cost) for stochastic optimization algorithms.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students complete six homeworks, a project, and a midterm and final exam.  
Students must complete an original research project addressing a substantial theoretical or applied question in ML and/or optimization; work must show novelty beyond replication or tuning. All projects include reproducible code in a public repo, a report in ICLR-style format, and a 20minute presentation. Projects are also assessed on depth of analysis, literature contextualization, and strength of theoretical or empirical contribution.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Homework Format and Verification: Each homework will include a Code Portion (implementation and experiments) and, when appropriate, a Theory Portion (derivations/proofs). A statement of authorship must be included: “I completed this work without assistance from AI tools.” Oral spot-checks and individual explanations may be required to verify authorship. When significant gaps are observed between the understanding exhibited in person and that in submitted work, penalties may include reduction of the score on the assignment up to a failing grade in the course.  
Strict AI Prohibition: Use of AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini, Claude, etc.) to generate or debug code, proofs, derivations, text, figures, or analysis is strictly prohibited. The instructor may orally examine any student to confirm authorship. Violations will receive a course grade of F.
