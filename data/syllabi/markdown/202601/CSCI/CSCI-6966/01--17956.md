## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

(Modified from Jim Orlin's Network Optimization Class at MIT):  
Network flow problems form a subclass of linear programming problems with applications to transportation, logistics, manufacturing, computer science, project management, and finance, as well as a number of other domains. This subject will survey some of the applications of network optimization and focus on key special cases of network optimization problems, including the following: the shortest path problem, the maximum flow problem, and the minimum cost flow problem. We will also consider other network optimization problems.  
The goals of the course are the following:

- To present students with knowledge of the state-of-the-art in the theory and practice of solving network optimization problems.
- To provide students with a rigorous analysis of network optimization algorithms.
- To help each student develop his or her own intuition about algorithm development and algorithm analysis.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1 {Perform Problem Transformations/Reductions}: Students will learn to connect related problems in network design and how to transform one problem into another rigorously. As an example, students learn how to reduce problems like airline scheduling and image segmentation to minimum cut problems, which enables standard minimum cut algorithms to be used to for these applications.
- 2 {Derive Optimality Conditions}: Students will use fundamentals from graph theory and optimization to prove necessary (and often sufficient) conditions for a solution to be optimal.
- 3 {Design Algorithms}: Students will learn to design algorithms to provably enforce optimality conditions to solve key problems in network flows.
- 4 {Analyze Run-Time of Algorithms}: Students will learn to quantify the efficiency of algorithms. This will enable students to identify different types of algorithms to use based on problem settings/parameters, e.g., the preferred algorithm may depend on the density of a network. Analysis includes amortized analysis and techniques that rely on knowledge of data structures.
- 5 {Design and Implement Metaheuristics}: Metaheuristics can be powerful tools for identifying good solutions. Students will focus on understanding the basic design of metaheuristics and how to use them to benchmark the performance of more problem-specific approaches.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

I care that every student has the opportunity to learn.  
To do well in this class, you should capitalize on that opportunity -- participate during class time, re-watch videos, complete practice problems, review notes, attend office hours and ask questions.  
Whether you seize that opportunity is entirely up to you.  
As such, I do not take attendance.  
If life ever conflicts with this schedule, then I encourage you to contact me.  
My primary requirement is that you \textbf{you shall not do anything to remove the learning opportunity from other students}, e.g., snoring/drooling while sleeping, talking on the phone, consuming loud/smelly food (in person) etc.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
