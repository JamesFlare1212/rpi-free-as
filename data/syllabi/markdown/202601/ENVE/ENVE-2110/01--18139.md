## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The application of basic principles and equations dealing with water, air, solid and hazardous wastes; material and energy balances, and chemical and biochemical cycles. Topics include water resources, water quality and pollution, air quality and pollution, solid and hazardous wastes, and environmental legislation.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The objective of this course is to introduce students to the field of environmental engineering. Students will learn about different physical and chemical principles, including material & energy balance and reaction laws. In addition, they will learn about the ecotoxicity of different pollutants and applicable environmental laws and regulations. Students will gain an understanding of different engineered systems used to maintain a clean environment including wastewater treatment processes, air pollution control, and radiation safety systems.

Lab experiments and modeling software (**ChemLab Standard software**, freely available to students) will be used throughout the course for a hands-on learning experience. Furthermore, a scheduled field visit to a local water treatment plant and guest lectures by practicing engineers and public officials will give students real-world knowledge of the environmental engineering industry.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Assess the environmental quality of ecosystems by identifying pollutants, calculating the concentration of pollutants in the atmosphere, and comparing them to federal and state regulations. Additionally, students will be able to use environmental quality data to assess human health risks and toxicity to the ecosystem.
- Apply physical, chemical, and biological laws and principles to determine the fate and transport behavior of different pollutants in the environment.
- Use material and energy balance equations to determine the flow rates and concentration of solids and other water constituents in different streams. Identify and describe the unit treatment processes used to produce safe drinking water and clean domestic and industrial wastewater

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* (found at [www.rpi.edu/dept/doso/handbook.html](http://www.rpi.edu/dept/doso/handbook.html)) defines various forms of academic dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Students should familiarize themselves with this portion of the *Handbook* and should note that the penalties for academic dishonesty can be quite severe.

In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work, although students are encouraged to work with each other by collaborating on assignments (see item 2 under *Homework Policy* above). Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy (e.g., one student copying work done by another student or a student copying from a previously developed solution) will result in a grade of zero for that particular assignment. Also, cheating on an exam will result in a grade of zero for that exam and, in accordance with the *Handbook*, notification to the Dean of Students Office of the incident of academic dishonesty.
