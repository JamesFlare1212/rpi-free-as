## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course looks investigates Earth’s climate and its surface environment from a geologist’s perspective. Students will develop an understanding of the atmospheric, oceanographic, geological, and biological systems that interact to modulate the climate. Focus will be on the study of modern coastal and river processes in the context of our region’s glacial history.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Describe and compare how wind, water, ice and biological processes shape the Earth’s surface.
- Explain the components of Earth’s climate system and evaluate how atmospheric, oceanographic, geological and biological factors influence the climate.
- Apply scientific reasoning and geologic principles to interpret real-world environmental and climate related problems
- Assess how geologic processes affect human environments and daily life, and articulate ways humans influence the Earth’s surface and climate.
- Interpret landscapes-particularly coastal, river and glacial environments-and communicate how they formed and may change in the future

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Tasks**: Throughout the semester there will be 13 short mini-assignments (i.e. Tasks) where you will have the opportunity to apply what we are learning to a problem or to dig into a topic in more depth. These will be posted on LMS at least a week in advance.

**In-class engagement:** Research shows that regular engagement with material through the semester increases retention and leads to better grades. In class, we will be utilizing a variety of techniques in the classroom to improve your learning and retention. Chiefly, this will include working independently and in small groups on activities, taking surveys to gage overall classroom understanding, and generating discussion. For some of these activities, points will be awarded for participation/completion and for some, points will be awarded for accuracy. In‑class engagement earns ~5 points per week through participation in activities, surveys, group work, and discussions. Engagement points cannot be made up, because the activities depend on real-time class participation. However, there will be more than a total of 75 points available throughout the semester so that missing a class will not penalize you.

**Quizzes:** There will be 8 quizzes throughout the semester to check for content understanding and to ensure you are engaging in distributive studying. Each quiz will be administered at the start of class on Ts. Late arrivals will not be given additional time. One make-up will be allowed for quizzes but must be made-up within 48 hours and arranged prior to the quiz. The dates of the quizzes are on the schedule and will not change.

**Journal Entries:** The geology journal is designed to help you be reflective about what you are learning in class and connect course material to your everyday life. Note-these are not a summary of class topics! You can complete up to 5 journal entries throughout the semester.

**Case Study Assignment**: Earth’s surface environments—coasts, rivers, glacial landscapes—are dynamic systems shaped by interactions among wind, water, ice, biology, and human activity. This assignment asks you to investigate a real-world example of a landscape undergoing measurable change and to apply the concepts learned in class to explain how and why that change is occurring. You may choose either a coastal or river environment from the list provided on LMS.

**Exams:** There will be 3 exams throughout the semester. Each exam will cover the topics preceding that exam (**not cumulative**-however, keep in mind that we will be building upon prior knowledge). There are **no make-up exams** unless you have a **documented, legitimate reason** discussed **with me in advance** whenever possible.

Legitimate reasons typically include: Illness with documentation, Religious observances, University‑approved travel (e.g., athletics) and Family emergency. The final decision on what qualifies as legitimate is at my discretion, but I follow RPI’s academic policies when making these determinations
