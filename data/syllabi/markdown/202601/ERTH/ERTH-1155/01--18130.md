**Weekly Group Assignments:** Each week you will have to complete an in-class discussion assignment. These assignments will be due in class and submitted as a group and will be based on the movie or media we view as a group on Monday of that week. I will drop the lowest group assignment grade. In addition to the weekly movie/etc. that we watch as a class, you will need to watch short, informative videos that describe important Earth Systems concepts and serve as background information. This background must be reviewed outside of class before Thursday’s discussion. The group discussion outcome can be in bulleted or paragraph form, but memes are also accepted/encouraged.

**Earth Science Meme Challenge:** Individual in class submissions covering any Earth Science topic. The sky is the limit, be creative!

**Mid-Term and Final Projects:** There will be two interactive media projects that you will complete in small groups (~5 people). You will produce a video with a narrative that tells a story involving accurate depictions of Earth science concepts that advance the storyline. Plot etc. are completely independent, but must depict accurate science.

Basic guidelines are:

- Create a short work of science fiction (NOT science fantasy);
- OR create multiple shorter TikTok style shorts.

## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

From disaster movies to sci-fi thrillers, pop culture frequently brings Earth Science into the spotlight**,** but how accurate are these portrayals? This course explores the intersection of Earth Science and media, critically analyzing how topics like natural disasters, climate change, space exploration, and geologic phenomena are represented in films, television, video games, and literature. Through case studies of popular works such as The Day After Tomorrow, Dante’s Peak, Interstellar, The Core, Don't Look Up, etc. students will compare fictional depictions with real scientific principles. We will look at the literature underpinning the science in these works and examine how entertainment shapes public perception, influences policy discussions, and sometimes spreads misinformation.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Critically evaluate the scientific accuracy of Earth Science portrayals in media.
- Understand fundamental Earth Science concepts and their real-world implications.
- Explore the role of media in shaping public attitudes toward environmental and geoscience issues.
- Develop communication skills to engage in informed discussions about science in popular culture.
- The student will be able to demonstrate effective communication through the written and oral presentation of their work.
