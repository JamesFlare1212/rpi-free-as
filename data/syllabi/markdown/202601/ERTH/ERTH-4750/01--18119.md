## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate proficiency and independence in a suite of GIS skills and use those skills for analysis and problem-solving in a variety of fields.
- Download, prepare, and troubleshoot data for analysis using a wide variety of data sources, not just work with prepared data that are given to them.
- Develop accurate workflows to plan GIS analyses and apply what they have learned to different but related problems.
- Develop a personal GIS portfolio and resource collection that will be useful to them in the future as they encounter problems that can be tackled using GIS either in future courses or in a job.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Weekly Maps:** Each week you will make a map, finalize in the QGIS Print Layout view, and submit via Gradescope. All maps must be in PDF format and will be graded on the analysis done in addition to the aesthetics and design of the map (see rubric in LMS). All maps must include metadata (title, legend, N arrow, scale, basemap, and a text box with name, date, and data source). *Full points on map analysis, you must go beyond the basic requirements for the weekly map*. A good map can be easily understood on its own, without any additional context or you there to explain it. Maps will be due in Gradescope at 11:59pm Sunday nights.

**GIS Portfolio:** This is not just a collection of your weekly maps! Your GIS portfolio will live in a shared Google Drive (link in LMS) and is where you will organize your notes, workflows, and print layouts of your weekly maps. Compiling and updating your portfolio is a semester-long endeavor where you will document what you learned using GIS so that in the future you can easily reference all of the resources, material, and examples you have worked through previously.

**Final Project and Poster**: You will choose a question that mapping and spatial analysis can be used to answer. Your project must include geospatial analysis to draw new conclusions – Simply making a map or set of maps is not acceptable. Additionally, you cannot simply repeat one of the class exercises in a new area or with a different set of data. You will need to find and manipulate your own geospatial data and prepare a poster that includes a high-quality map, text, and additional elements that explain the problem, analysis, and results. Your project can be on any topic you find interesting but must include a new, meaningful map that you produced. *You are required to attend the final poster session to pass this class.*
