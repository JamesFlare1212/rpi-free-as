## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Phase 1: Literature Analysis and Question Development**

You will analyze primary literature from leading Earth Science journals, learning to evaluate various research approaches in geoscience. Through guided discussions and workshops, you will develop skills in:

- Reading and annotating geoscience papers from journals such as Geology, Nature Geoscience, and Journal of Geophysical Research
- Identifying different research methodologies in Earth Sciences (field studies, laboratory experiments, numerical modeling, remote sensing)
- Recognizing gaps in our understanding of Earth systems
- Formulating answerable research questions in geoscience

**Phase 2: Independent Research**

You will conduct independent literature reviews focused on their research questions within their chosen Earth Science subdiscipline. This phase includes:

- Individual consultations with the instructor
- Peer review workshops
- Development of analytical frameworks for synthesizing multiple lines of evidence

**Phase 3: Synthesis and Presentation**

You will produce an **original research paper** that develops and defends a clear argument using evidence synthesized from published Earth science studies. Deliverables include

- A comprehensive research paper
- A research poster following AGU/GSA format guidelines
- Participation in an undergraduate research symposium

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Analyze primary geoscience literature to identify key research methodologies, findings and limitations across field, laboratory and modeling studies
- Compare and contrast findings across multiple Earth Science studies to identify patterns in Earth Systems and processes.
- Design focused research questions that address identified gaps in geoscience literature
- Synthesize findings from multiple subdisciplines to construct evidence-based arguments about Earth processes
- Deliver professional presentations that effectively communicate complex Earth Science concepts

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Weekly Reading Journals:** These short, structured reflections help you stay engaged with weekly readings and track your developing understanding of Earth science research. Journals are designed to take no more than 45–60 minutes and prepare you for in‑class discussions.

**Research question development and outline:** In this assignment, you will refine an answerable, well‑justified research question that emerges from your early reading. You will also submit a working outline for your research paper, including planned themes. This milestone ensures your project has a clear direction and that you are building a logical structure for your final paper. You’ll receive instructor and peer feedback to guide your next steps.

**Annotated Bibliography:** Your annotated bibliography will document the core set of peer‑reviewed papers that form the foundation of your topic. Annotations briefly summarize the purpose, methods, key findings, and relevance of each source to your research question. This assignment helps you build a well‑organized evidence base and ensures that your final paper is grounded in high‑quality scientific literature.

**Poster Presentation:** You will design and present a scientific poster following AGU/GSA formatting guidelines. The poster should clearly communicate your research question, key themes from the literature, major findings, and their broader significance. This assignment develops your ability to communicate complex scientific ideas visually and verbally. You will receive feedback during a practice session before presenting at the Undergraduate Research Symposium.

**Final Research Paper:** The final paper is an ***original research project grounded entirely in the analysis and synthesis of published Earth science literature***. Instead of collecting new data, you will develop and answer your own research question by integrating evidence from existing studies. Your paper should present an argument, support that argument with data and interpretations drawn from the literature, and contribute a new perspective to an active area of Earth science research. This assignment goes beyond summarizing articles: you will evaluate methodologies, compare findings across studies, analyze patterns and discrepancies, and build a coherent interpretation of the processes or systems relevant to your question. The final product should resemble a research‑style manuscript and include an introduction, clear objectives, evidence‑based analysis, figures or conceptual models, and a well‑supported conclusion.

**Class participation and peer feedback:** Active participation is central to the success of this seminar‑style course. You will contribute to discussions, ask questions, offer insights, and engage respectfully with your peers’ ideas. Throughout the semester, you will also provide structured peer feedback on proposals, outlines, and poster drafts. These contributions support a collaborative learning environment and strengthen your own understanding of the literature.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty of loss of credit for the assignment for the first offense and subsequent offenses can result in receiving a failing grade for the course. If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

**Use of AI**: AI tools can be helpful for brainstorming or clarifying ideas, and you are welcome to use them in that limited way. However, all written work, analyses, interpretations, and figures you submit must be **your own original thinking and your own writing**. Submitting AI‑generated text is a form of **academic dishonesty**, and assignments containing such material will receive a **zero**. Please also remember that AI tools often produce inaccurate or oversimplified scientific explanations. Relying on them can undermine your ability to develop the analytical skills this course is designed to strengthen. If you are unsure whether a particular use of AI is acceptable, please ask.
