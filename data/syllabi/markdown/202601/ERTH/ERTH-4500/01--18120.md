## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Identify and describe the major components of Earth’s climate system (atmosphere, hydrosphere, cryosphere, lithosphere, biosphere) and explain how they interact to regulate climate.
- Analyze different types of paleoclimate proxies (e.g., ice cores, sediments, isotopes) and interpret the climate information they provide.
- Evaluate the roles of natural Earth system processes and human activities in driving past, present, and projected future climate change using scientific evidence
- Critically assess climate related media, reports, and public communications by identifying claims, evaluating evidence, and detecting common misconceptions or logical fallacies
- Communicate climate science concepts and findings clearly and accurately to both scientific and non scientific audiences through written, visual, and/or oral formats

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Problem Sets**: There will be 9 problem sets/assignments you will complete during the course of the semester. The purpose of these is to explore and reinforce material covered in class. You will generally have ~1 each week and will have 1 week to complete each. The lowest one will be dropped at the end of the semester.

**Quizzes:** There will be 4 quizzes throughout the semester. Each quiz will cover the topics preceding that exam (**not cumulative**-however, keep in mind that we will be building upon prior knowledge). Quiz dates are on the schedule and dates will not change.

**Journal Entries:** The climate journal is designed to help you be reflective about what you are learning in class and connect course material to your everyday life. These will include both directed entries and independent entries. You will need to bring your journal to class for directed entries. Journals will be collected randomly, but you can expect a collection every 2 weeks. ***There should be at least 1 independent entry/week.*** Additional information on journals is available on LMS

**Communicating Climate**: Scientists must be able to communicate effectively with the public. This assignment helps you practice translating complex ideas into accessible content. You will choose from making a video, infographic, popular science article, or another medium of your choice (approved by Dr. Cadieux).

**Use of AI**: AI tools can be helpful for brainstorming or clarifying ideas, and you are welcome to use them in that limited way. Submitting AI‑generated text is a form of **academic dishonesty**, and assignments containing such material will receive a **zero**. Please also remember that AI tools often produce inaccurate or oversimplified scientific explanations. Relying on them can undermine your ability to develop the analytical skills this course is designed to strengthen. If you are unsure whether a particular use of AI is acceptable, please ask.
