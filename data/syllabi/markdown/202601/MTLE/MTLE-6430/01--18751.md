## Course Goals

### Goals:

This graduate level course aims to impart a fundamental understanding of the physical bases of microanalytical and microscopy techniques used to characterize the chemical and structural features of surfaces and thin layers of materials. Techniques include electron- and ion-beam spectroscopies, depth profiling, and X-ray and electron-diffraction, electron microscopy, and surface scanning probe microscopy. We review the physical principles, salient details of probe-beam and data-collection instrumentation, and key concepts germane to data analysis and interpretation through real-life examples. The advantages and limitations of the techniques will be elucidated in order to enable technique selection and data interpretation.

### Other Course Policies:

Students are expected to come prepared to class by reading the relevant sections in the text book prior to the lectures.  
Homework assignments combined with in-class discussions on problems and will be crucial means through which we will learn about various aspects of a variety of materials characterization techniques this semester. Attempts to HW assignments should be *neatly* presented with clear descriptions of procedures and reasoning behind the steps followed to arrive at the answer.
