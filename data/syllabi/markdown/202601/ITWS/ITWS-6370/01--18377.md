## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course covers foundational models for information security, policies, business continuity and disaster recovery, authentication, access control, database security, assurance, auditing, and intrusion detection. It devotes substantial time to the IT and business cases for security as well as secure coding practices. Students will develop a solid foundation upon which to build an information security career in the subfield of their choosing. Cryptography is not covered since it is a focus of other courses.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will demonstrate knowledge of the theoretical foundations of information security, analyzing that knowledge to interpret best practices and develop solutions to improve community security practices.
- Students will demonstrate knowledge of a broad range of issues that must be addressed to design and build secure systems, assessing, evaluating, and critiquing real-world arguments and products to form recommendations for everyday people.
- Students will demonstrate knowledge of common programming problems that are often exploited by viruses, worms, and other malware, developing their own malware and applying that malware to real-world software and situations.
- Students will be able to investigate and present topics in information system security at a level so that other students obtain an understanding of the key issues, breaking down arguments and drawing conclusions based on peer-reviewed research into a topic of their choice to construct their own arguments about the state-of-the-art in cybersecurity.
- Students (only in 6370) will earn an industry-standard Department of Defense-approved cybersecurity certification, to enable students to produce their own cybersecurity trainings and develop pedagogical techniques to teach the next generation of cybersecurity students.
- Students (only in 6370) will participate in a “flipped classroom” approach in which they are responsible for one course lecture and related learning materials, as cybersecurity professionals are continually called upon to educate everyday people on the necessity of cybersecurity, this tasks sees students in 6370 go far beyond learning outcome 1 and develop leadership skills in this emerging important task for cybersecurity professionals.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

CTF tournament – All students will participate in the National Cyber League season, consisting of an individual tournament and a team tournament (20%, 15% for students in 6370).

Coding Project – project in which students (in groups) will work to implement their own malware (25%, 15% for students in 6370)

Presentation -- there will be a 30-minute presentation in which you (in a small group) will explore a security topic not covered in class (20%). Students in 6370 will be expected to provide a 1-hour presentation (15% for students in 6370).

Security/Privacy Assessments -- One (individual) written assessment of 8-10 pages with 6-8 sources on one new technology and its security and privacy consideration (25%). Students in 6370 will write a 10-15 page paper with 8-12 sources (15% for students in 6370).

Lecture – Students in 6370 will participate in a “flipped classroom” approach in which they will be responsible for one lecture and associated learning materials (15% for students in 6370)

Attendance/Participation -- Each class begins with an approximately 20 minute discussion of the day’s security news. Students are expected to come to class having read some interesting, timely security news, prepared to discuss it and put it into broader conversation with other students’ news items and news items previously discussed (10%)

Security certification – Each student in 6370 will earn an industry-standard cybersecurity certification offered by ISC2 (or CompTIA). This certification is a DoD 8570 Baseline Certification at the IAT Level II and CSSP Infrastructure Support (15%).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Cheating and plagiarism guidelines:  
The studio exercises must be completed by each student independently unless otherwise specified. You may discuss your approach to deriving the answers with your fellow classmates, but you must write and defend your own answers in your own words. Copied answers will be grounds for disciplinary actions. See the academic integrity statement in the course syllabus.  
The coding project must be completed by each student independently. Students may not peruse GitHub or other code hosting sites for solutions to the coding project.  
Students must write their assessment papers individually and in their own words. Plagiarism occurs when students fail to properly cite their research sources in the paper.  
Students must produce their presentations solely in their groups without consulting other students. Plagiarism occurs when a presentation group fails to properly cite their research sources in the presentation.  
Cheating and plagiarism policy:  
First offense: Failure for the course

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

Some of the techniques learned in this course can be used for malicious purposes. These techniques are taught because they are necessary for a complete understanding of information security. Using such techniques for malicious purposes may be a crime, depending on context. Using such techniques against any RPI machines, including but not limited to other students’ machines, the shared course server, or any RPI computers, are grounds for immediate failure of the course.
