## Course Goals

### Goals:

The primary goal of this course is to help you better understand key issues in HCI and develop as a more informed and empowered technological consumer and technological content creator.

- Build a UX portfolio.
- Explore HCI testing and evaluation processes from both a scientific and heuristic perspective using real world case studies.
- Understand key issues in human and computer interactions.
- Communicate with a variety of *real audiences* in different written and spoken generic forms.
- Achieve a *variety of purposes* – provide perspective on an experience, showcase your experiences and skills, and propose a creative, innovative solution to positively impact the world.
- Practice creating and making effective oral presentations and being attentive and aware designers.
- Become active and close readers of challenging texts within the field of HCI.
- Become more aware of the choices you and others are making with technology.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- These goals translate into more specific learning outcomes. You’ll be expected to: • Analyze technological artifacts. Identify specific ways technological mediation impacts knowledge, needs, emotions, values, and experiences that are both individual and social-cultural in origin. • Engage clients. Learn to more deeply see and listen to your future client’s needs more deeply while guiding them to more successful outcomes. • Explain your ideas in ways that reflect a thorough understanding of the subject and your audience’s needs, knowledge, values, etc. by anticipating and answering the audience’s likely questions and elaborating appropriately (i.e. to consider things your audience takes as “givens”). • Explain appropriate technology, not only in your design processes but in the kinds of questions you answer or avoid, and the kinds of information you choose to include and exclude. • Practice and hone your speaking and listening communication skills in both one-on-one, small group, and large group settings. • Reflect on how you want technology to function. • Begin to establish a toolkit of “practices” that you can take with you for designing your own content and analyzing the work of others. • Create a portfolio level final project with team members.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The deliverables for this course are outlined below. Together, they are designed to assess your engagement with course materials, collaboration with peers, and ability to apply Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) and user experience (UX) principles in practice.

1. **Reading Reflections – 10% (100 points)**Over the course of the semester, you will respond to **Medium articles posted by Dr. Dowdall**. These reflections are intended to demonstrate your understanding of the readings and your ability to critically engage with course concepts. The Discord forum is meant to facilitate informal discussion and conversation. Your TA Ed Moris will be responding to your posts as well. Medium articles will be posted throughout the semester, and you are required to submit **one reading reflection per sprint**, for a total of **five reflections**. Posts will be graded by the TA on the **last day of each sprint**, which serves as the due date. While only one formal response per sprint is required, responding thoughtfully to other students’ posts will be considered when calculating participation.
2. **Research Proposal – 20% (200 points)**The research proposal is a **group assignment** that outlines your plan for conducting a UX study. This proposal should clearly explain how your group intends to contribute to the research process. It must include an overview of your chosen research methods, a project timeline, technical requirements, and any initial constraints or limitations. The proposal should be concise, approximately **five double-spaced pages**, and demonstrate an introductory understanding of the UX research methods discussed in class and from the readings.
3. **Presentation – 20% (200 points)**Each group will deliver a professional presentation that summarizes the findings of their UX study and highlights key insights and recommendations. Presentation length will depend on the number of groups and topics covered. Presentations should clearly explain your design process, showcase your work in Figma/Adobe/other, and discuss how your ideas evolved. If a beta version of your project is complete, a walkthrough of the main features should be included. This presentation serves as your final opportunity to present your work and receive feedback. Please note that the **project is due before the presentation** to ensure it is complete; however, if changes are requested, the report may be revised and resubmitted.
4. **User Experience Portfolio – 40% (400 points)**The User Experience Study is the **major group project** and the largest component of the course grade. The final report should be approximately **20 pages total**, with an estimated contribution of about **five pages per group member**, not exceeding **10 pages per member**. Responsibilities for writing, design, and testing should be distributed by the group’s project lead, who will be elected by the group. However, all group members share responsibility for the fair distribution of tasks. Reports will be evaluated based on the effective use of iterative UX methods, creativity, innovation, and overall quality. The primary audience for the report will be the client, and content should be tailored accordingly. While length is less important than quality, graphs and figures count toward the page total (references do not). Improper or excessive use of images, tables, or figures without meaningful contribution may result in a lower grade.
5. **Participation – 10% (100 points)**Participation is based on your overall engagement throughout the semester. This includes attending class, contributing to discussions, actively participating in group work, and engaging with course activities both in and outside of class. You can guarantee your grade by showing up to class, working with your group, participating in the research process, and responding to your fellow students on discord.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**GRADING**

To help you achieve the goals and outcomes for this course, I will introduce you to research and theory about how technology is thought about and used. Based on the understanding of the theoretical background that supports this course, you’ll learn strategies you can use to communicate the concepts effectively with your audience for any communication practice—oral, written, or visual—in any situation, both in classrooms and in your career. These strategies are not algorithms or recipes that can be followed blindly: They are actions and choices people routinely take and make—sometimes consciously, habitually, and/or intuitively—in communicating effectively. In your assignments, therefore, you’re expected to:

- Demonstrate critical thinking concerning technology.
- Organize communication about your theories and ideas coherently and cohesively.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

*The* *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* and *The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement*define various forms of academic dishonesty, and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade *must represent your own work*. If you plagiarize once, you will receive an F on the assignment. If you plagiarize again, you will fail the entire course and may be reported to the *Dean of Students* as appropriate. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask me for clarification. The basics: when using information from another source (including anonymous online sources) always cite the original text or URL and distinguish it from your own commentary. If you directly cut and paste another person’s language, you need to use it as a quotation. *We will talk specifically about citation styles and how to make sure that you are giving “credit” when it is required of you in spirit of scholarly collaboration.* Regarding using AI (e.g. ChatGPT or other Open AI resources): I am ok with you using AI for editing and even helping articulate your ideas in clear ways. Meaning that it ok to ask, “can you help me make this clearer” or “can you check my grammar.” I mean what’s the difference between that and spell check. Do not use it to come up with ideas, write your essay, or outline the project. The processes are important for your thinking and I care deeply about these aspects of your work. Remember in the end, thinking is what makes us human and our work valuable.
