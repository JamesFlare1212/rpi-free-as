## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The hallmark of human-computer interaction (HCI) is the understanding of human behavior. The course starts with fundamental concepts related to human behavior through the lens of HCI, human-centered design, design thinking, user experience (UX) and user interface (UI). These concepts are applied experientially throughout the semester.

Research design, persona development, information architecture, low and high-fidelity prototyping are used to develop a final product using software called Figma (collaborative interface design tool). The course long project is the culmination of material covered throughout the course and can be used as a portfolio item to showcase classwork for an interview or other employment situation.

**Course Catalog (2022-2023):** An introduction to the current theories, methods, and issues in human-computer interaction. Theory and research along with practical application are discussed within the context of organizational impact. The course provides the knowledge of HCI systems and research used for the implementation of safe, quick, and useable interactive technologies.

##### Team-based Learning

This course will use a modified Team-Based-Learning (TBL) format ([www.teambasedlearning.org)](http://www.teambasedlearning.org/) grounded in team research. These instructional methods aim to help develop your 21st century workplace professional skills and will be done in a way that will hold individuals accountable for preparing for team activities and projects.

While, in some circumstances, group work can be structured unfairly, such that some students end up doing all the work while everyone shares in the credit and grade, several factors will prevent that from happening in this class. First, many lectures will be

preceded by one or more preparatory assignment, for which each individual will be accountable, thus ensuring that individual team members are each prepared to contribute to the team effort. Second, each individual’s contribution to teamwork will be assessed by his or her teammates throughout the semester at the end of each round.

**Phase 1** – **Preparation**: You will complete readings and/or activities for each topic assigned.

**Phase 2** – **Quizzes**. The quizzes (generally 10 multiple-choice questions) measure your comprehension of the assigned readings/activities and will help you to learn the material needed to begin applying the material in phase 3. Once the full test period (individual and group) is over, we may give a short mini lecture to clarify concepts that are not well understood as evidenced by the individual test scores. The ultimate purpose of phase 2 is to ensure that you and your teammates have sufficient foundational knowledge to begin learning how to apply and use the concepts in phase 3. Quizzes are open book and based on the assigned readings/activities and most will be given before the reading material is covered in lecture.

- - **Individual quizzes** – You will individually complete a multiple-choice test based on the readings/assignment.
  - **Team quizzes** – In the class following the individual quiz due date, the same multiple-choice quiz is re-taken with your team. You will use a scratch off card to answer the ten questions. Your team is awarded **10 points** if you uncover the correct answer on your first try, **6 points** for second try, **3 points** for third try, and **1 point** for fourth try.

**Phase 3** - **In-Class Activities Designed to Apply the Reading Materials**: You and your team will use the foundational knowledge acquired in the first two phases to complete projects and other activities.

##### Group Project

Much of the learning in this course revolves around the experiential learning of the concepts presented. Teams will be formed consisting of 4 – 5 students the second week of classes. Students can pick their own teams, and we will assist. The course will be designed so that most of the teamwork can be done in class.

##### Team Semester Design Challenge

Design a digital product experience that appeals to college students and makes it safe to find the ideal roommate. Design the experience from the perspective of a person who is looking for a roommate as well as the one who is looking for a place to live. We will ask you to identify pain points in the “finding/keeping a good roommate” journey and to find ways to solve those pain points.

##### Individual Project

Developing a professional presence is critical to your job search and professional development. For this project you will create a professional presence to showcase your coursework, internships if you have had them and any other projects of interest.
