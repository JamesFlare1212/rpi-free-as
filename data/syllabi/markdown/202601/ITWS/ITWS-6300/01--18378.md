## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course introduces engineering and science students to the ***business issues that are critical in turning new technology into market-winning products***. Students should take this course if they desire to ***maximize their effectiveness as technologists*** by understanding the business, customer, competitive and financial considerations that impact the work involved in bringing new products to market and making them successful.

This course investigates business-related considerations in successfully commercializing new technology. It exposes technologists to *five critical business issues an existing enterprise faces in creating successful products/services* and investigates *how these issues impact technical work*: Most importantly, commercial business is a *competition*, and success requires beating the companies who are competing for “your” customers. In the business environment, the most successful engineers and scientists are attuned to these considerations, and they pursue their work in an effective partnership with their colleagues who have business/profit responsibilities.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

**Required text**: an instructor-designed “electronic casebook” hosted on the Harvard Business School Publishing website will be electronically-accessed. Students can access and pay for (~$75; credit or debit card) the casebook via an internet link provided to the class.

**Homework**: Each week, questions will be assigned based on the readings. (Class Session Topics and weekly readings are listed in this syllabus.) Usually, the student will be asked to analyze aspects of a business situation/problem, and then to develop an opinion and/or recommendation. ***Each week, each student must submit brief written answers to the assigned questions, PRIOR to class.*** Homework submissions will be graded *acceptable* or *unacceptable*, and will be returned with comments *only at the request of the student*, since homework will be discussed in class.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

# Overview of Course Content

1. **Profit & Loss (income statement) basic** - understanding the factors that determine how a business makes money; what defines a successful product.
2. **Business plans** - the key plan aspects that maximize the probability of creating a successful business or product
3. **Customer/market analysi**s - identifying target markets and designing to customer and competitive requirement.
4. **Beating the competitio**n - developing competitive advantage and deciding on product differentiation
5. **Product decisions** - selecting product markets; dealing with business-technical tradeoffs in defining products
6. **The marketing function in technically-oriented enterprise** - what it does, and its relationship to technical work; internet-based marketing
7. **Sales and sales channe**ls - the various ways products are sold, and how to choose between them; how selling method affects product/service design
8. **Development and Operation**s - effective new product development processes; meeting market windows and designing for cost, reliability, and serviceability; basic principles and challenges in company operations
9. **Product Management** - successfully developing products, going to market, and managing for growth and profitability
10. **People** - the role of technologists in business decisions; effective collaboration with business managers and colleagues.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Upon successful completion, ALL STUDENTS will have a rudimentary capability to: 1. analyze a company’s financial performance and assess its opportunities and challenges
- 2\. assess the barriers to success in a particular market and select the appropriate target customers
- 3\. identify a company’s competitive advantages by performing a comprehensive analysis of its direct and indirect competition
- 4\. define the “whole product” and decide on appropriate product differentiators
- 5\. select appropriate sales/distribution channels and promotional methods
- 6\. integrate the course learnings to determine a plan for specification of a new product
- 7\. identify best practices in new product development and understand the challenges in integrating the marketing, and operations
- 8\. understand how technical people interact with marketing, sales and operations personnel, and how they contribute to resolution of business issues
- 9\. understand the ways in which business issues can and should impact technical work.
- 10\. Additionally, 6000 Level Students will analyze a current (case-based) business, addressing the opportunities and challenges for that company, and then develop an achievable plan for future economic growth.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Class sessions will be a combination of instructor-led presentation, interactive class discussion and group analysis of real businesses and business situations (case studies). Assignments will largely involve analyzing real-world business problems, understanding the business/technology interplay, and identifying resolutions and courses of action. A majority of the learning in this course will occur during class discussion/dialogue about the weekly readings and associated assignment questions (readings include discourses on business concepts, opinion-based pieces on business best-practices, and “cases” that describe specific business situations faced by real companies). Accordingly, ***completing the weekly readings, answering the assigned homework questions, attending class regularly, and participating actively in class discussions are all required for successful completion of the course*.**
