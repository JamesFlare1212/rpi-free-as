## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to i) Derive models for biomedical systems using first principles; ii) Identify key components of models and what needs to be specified for their solution; iii) Formulate numerical solutions methods for biomedical models; iv) Implement and simulate models in a commercial software package; v) Estimate parameters of models describing biological systems

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Exams:** There will be a total of three exams. Approximate dates are after 4 weeks, 10 weeks, and during the scheduled time of the final; exact dates will be discussed in class approximately 1-2 weeks before an exam is given. The plan is to give exams during class time to avoid scheduling conflicts, but this is potentially subject to change. Closed notes/book. Bringing a laptop to exams is mandatory as many exam problems require MATLAB for their solution.  
There will be no make-up exams. If one exam is missed with an excused absence, then the final will count for double its usual percentage. If an exam is missed unexcused then this will count as a zero score on the exam. If two exams are missed, then the class needs to be retaken.

**Homework:** Homeworks are the best way to study for the exams and as such, 10 homework sets will be uploaded on LMS. Homeworks do not have to be handed in and will not be graded, however, we will be happy to discuss homework related questions during office hours.

**Quizzes:** There will be 5-7 unannounced quizzes given throughout the semester. The quizzes will be short (10 minutes) and the material will cover concepts from the last lecture or two. Some quizzes may require computation and as such, bringing your laptop to each class is highly recommended. There will be no make-up for missed quizzes; instead, the quiz with the lowest score will be thrown out (a zero if a quiz was missed) when calculating the quiz average.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.
