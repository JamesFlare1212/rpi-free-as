## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The purpose of this course is to bring in researchers in Biomedical Engineering and related areas to share their latest research and gain knowledge in specific areas within Biomedical Engineering. Seminars are intended to foster professional skills for biomedical engineers. Through these seminars by nationally and internationally recognized experts in their fields, students will learn about current research areas and key problems being studied.

Biomedical Engineering doctoral students are required to attend all Biomedical Engineering seminars (irrespective of enrolling in the seminar course). **Students registered for either the graduate or undergraduate sections are required to attend all seminars and submit the following assignments (described below) to pass the course.**

**Time and place:** Selected Thursday’s from 2-3 pm CBIS Auditorium. Please show up before 2 pm to both sign in and ensure you get a seat.

**Schedule:** The tentative schedule will be posted at https://bme.rpi.edu/seminars but may change due to weather impacting travel. Seminars may be added during the term.

*Potential Places Where Academic Dishonesty May Take Place in This Course:*

1. Signing an attendance sheet for a friend who is missing a seminar.
2. Lying to a professor or falsifying an excuse concerning reasons for missing a seminar.
3. Since the course requires written work, any instance of plagiarism or copying parts of other’s work without proper citation is academic dishonesty and results in failing the course.

**Institute-wide Information and Guidelines**

**Academic Integrity:** The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own performance. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

**Course Policy on Collaboration and Cheating/Plagiarism**: Plagiarism involves using another’s work and claiming it as one’s own. Cheating includes plagiarism, and includes additionally, but is not limited to, using or facilitating another to use work-product for a graded deliverable which is misrepresented as to authorship of the solution/ concept/ approach/ answer, or involves a breach of rules stated as part of the protocol for the assignment or exam. Collaboration on assignments, quizzes or exams is permitted only when explicitly described as permitted or required by the instructor for specific occurrences. The student will receive at least a full grade penalty, up to and including an F for the course, the full deduction being at the instructor’s discretion.

Mobile Devices: All mobile devices (cell/smart phones, computers, pagers, etc.) must be stored securely away during lecture and are not to be used unless specifically directed otherwise by the instructor. Use of (or **ANY** interaction with) a mobile device during an exam without explicit permission of the instructor will be interpreted as the illicit transfer of exam data, will be considered an act of cheating and will be treated as such.
