## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will cover the fundamentals of basic immunobiology and then describe engineering strategies to modulate, control, and exploit immune responses to improve human health. Through lectures, literature reviews, and group projects we will understand the principles of innate and adaptive immunology, explore the immunologist’s toolkit to measure and assess immune cells and responses, and study new engineering approaches to modulate the immune system that have developed immunotherapies against cancer, autoimmunity, and other diseases.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Student learning outcomes for undergraduate section (BMED-4520)**

1. Differentiate between the innate and adaptive immune systems by applying concepts of evolution, structure, function, and comparing immune cell types across tissues in health and disease.
2. Discuss different methods to analyze the immune system, which tools are best suited to measure immune responses, and design experimental tools to probe immune function.
3. Analyze immunoengineering approaches to treat disease, give examples of successful therapies that modulate the immune system to treat disease, compare strengths and limitations of these approaches.
4. Apply engineering principles to modulate the immune system, including designing immunoengineering approaches and identifying unmet clinical needs.

**Additional student learning outcome for graduate section (BMED-6520)**

5. Integrate knowledge of immunology to develop a comprehensive research plan and grant application to develop new immunoengineering approaches to treat disease.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Plagiarism involves using another’s work and claiming it as one’s own. Cheating includes plagiarism, and includes additionally, but is not limited to, using or facilitating another to use work-product for a graded deliverable which is misrepresented as to authorship of the solution/ concept/ approach/ answer, or involves a breach of rules stated as part of the protocol for the assignment or exam. Collaboration on assignments, quizzes or exams is permitted only when explicitly described as permitted or required by the instructor for specific occurrences. The student will receive at least a full grade penalty, up to and including an F for the course, the full deduction being at the instructor’s discretion.

Potential Places Where Academic Dishonesty May Take Place In This Course:

1. **Academic Fraud:** The alteration of documentation relating to the grading process. For example, changing exam solutions to negotiate for a higher grade or tampering with an instructor’s grade book.
2. **Collaboration:** Deliberately facilitating an act of academic dishonesty in any way or form. For example, allowing another student to observe an exam paper or allowing another student to “recycle” one’s old term paper or using one another’s work in a paper or lab report without citing it as another’s work.
3. **Copying:** Obtaining information pertaining to a graded exercise by deliberately observing the paper of another student.
4. **Cribbing:** Use or attempted use of prohibited materials, information, or study aids in an academic exercise. For example, using an unauthorized formal sheet during an exam.
5. **Fabrication:** Unauthorized falsification or invention of any information in an academic exercise. For example, use of “bought or “ready-made” term papers, or falsifying lab records or reports.
6. **Plagiarism:** Representing the work or words of another as one’s own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference. For example, using sentences verbatim from a published source in a term paper without appropriate referencing, or presenting as one’s own the detailed argument of a published source, or presenting as one’s own electronically or digitally enhanced graphic representations from any form of media.
7. **Sabotage:** Destruction of another student’s work. For example, destroying a model, lab experiment, computer program, or term paper developed by another student.
8. **Substitution:** Utilizing a proxy, or acting as a proxy, in any academic exercise. For example, taking an exam for another student or having a homework assignment done by someone else.

**Exams:** Midterm and final examinations will be given in this course. The date of the midterm exam is specified in the syllabus. The final exam is comprehensive and mandatory. Exam grades may be challenged in writing, submitted to the instructor within one week after the exam is returned. Exam grades or challenges will not be discussed via email. A missed final exam will result in a grade of zero unless permission is granted before the scheduled exam time. Permission will only be granted in cases of serious illness or family emergency. In such cases, the final exam will be rescheduled for an alternative date and time, most likely toward the end of the final exam period. Missing both the scheduled final and the makeup will result either in (i) scaling your grade based on the other exams taken during the semester, or (ii) assigning an Incomplete (I), at the instructor’s discretion on a case-by-case basis. No makeup will be given for the midterm exam; instead, if the midterm is missed for an approved reason, the final exam grade will substitute for the missing midterm exam grade.

**Electronic devices:** mobile devices (cell/smart phones, computers, pagers, etc.) must be stored securely away during lecture and are not to be used unless specifically directed otherwise by the instructor. Use of (or ANY interaction with) a mobile device during an exam without explicit permission of the instructor will be interpreted as the illicit transfer of exam data, will be considered an act of cheating and will be treated as such.
