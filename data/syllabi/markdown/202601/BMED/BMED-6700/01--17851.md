## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is also open to all students at Rensselaer, particularly those supported by NIH grants. This 14-week course includes lectures, discussion sessions, readings, and public forums (see the Syllabus below). Predoctoral Training Program (T32) faculty and external ethicists are invited to participate in the modules. The diverse array of research areas in which the Training Program faculty focuses will provide an enriched student experience. Valuable real-world topics representing challenges that the students will face in the academic and industrial workplaces will be presented. The results of these discussions will be included in the assessment of the course learning objectives. Topics discussed in the course will include the acquisition and use of human tissues or medical data, the use of vertebrate animals in research, and conflicts of interest arising from industrial collaborations. Students will benefit from this experience by understanding how ethical challenges may arise in real research programs.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course helps the student develop an understanding of the factors involved in facing and solving ethical dilemmas and allows full discussion of various perspectives and strategies that can be used in ethical decision-making. The goal of the course is to provide broad exposure to the students in the Responsible Conduct for Research while staying updated on the latest developments and case studies involving research conduct. A key component of this course is active teamwork among students with diverse backgrounds, and the acquisition of new tools for understanding and motivating others. All current and past trainees of NIH training grant will participate in this course with other interested students, particularly those funded by the NIH grants.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Lecture Topics**

Ethics and compliance concepts in research: historical perspective and philosophy; personal vs. societal beliefs

Institutional responsibility: University/Institutional process regarding allegations; conflicts of Interest and conflict of commitments

How rise of AI (and Chat GPT) impacts biomedical research

Environmental and societal impacts of scientific research

How the research works – proposing research, data acquisition and laboratory tools

Publication and dissemination of findings; authorship; data management, sharing and ownership;

Peer review and grantsmanship; fraud/research misconduct and policies for handling misconduct

Animal use in research and education: animal use, sex and age as biological variables

Interpersonal responsibility and scientific research ethics: mentor/trainee responsibilities and relationships

No Class - President's Day

Common methods of managing conflicts of Interest/commitments within academia/public-private partnerships (individual/institutional);

Genetic technologies: recombinant DNA research, transgenics; gene therapy

Animal welfare, and “rights”; activism and liberal movements; animal uses: pharma production in animals: product testing

Human cell research: Henrietta Lacks' cells; Stem Cells: Embryonic, adult, iPSCs etc (Reference IRB)

Ethical considerations of genetically modified organisms

Bias and Fairness in clinical trials and observational studies

Human participation in research: regulations and training; informed consent (Tuskegee); inclusions of age/sex/ race; (Reference IRB)

Varia: organ donation and organ sales (human and animal issues); bioart: GMO Foods

Academic freedom: modern ethical challenges related to medicine and technology

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

Student projects will involve independent research to identify and present (oral and written reports) case studies on specific topics cover by this course and listed below. Your presentation and report should include a brief summary of key concepts presented and discussed in the class followed by their use in analyzing and presenting case studies reported in the media or from your own research. The project report is limited to a maximum of two pages (excludes references and appendix containing reference images (such as examples of image manipulation).

Project topics listed below. Within two weeks from the course's start date, indicate your choices (#1 to #10) by selecting the topic listed below on the discussion page. Efforts will be made to provide your top choice while balancing and covering all the topics. The topics will be allocated in order of the responses received.

1. Case studies on modern ethical challenges related to medicine and technology.
2. Case studies on research system (mentor-mentee relationships, authorship, plagiarism, international controls).
3. Case studies/types on peer review and grantsmanship; fraud/research misconduct
4. Case studies/Examples of academia/public-private partnerships
5. Case studies on use of animals in research
6. Case Studies on use of humans in research
7. Case studies on human stem cell research
8. Case studies or use on Genetic technologies
9. Case studies on organ donation and organ sales
10. Case studies on bioart and academic freedom

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
