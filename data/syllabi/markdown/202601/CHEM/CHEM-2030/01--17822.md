## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**CHEM 2030 - Inorganic Chemistry I**

Descriptive chemistry of the elements. Properties, structures, and typical reactions of the elements of the periodic table and their compounds; basic principles of inorganic chemistry.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

*Weller, **Inorganic Chemistry***, 7th Edition.

Oxford University Press ISBN: 9780198768128

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The chief objectives of this course are to build knowledge and practical understanding regarding the chemical elements and their “inorganic” compounds.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

1. **Introduction to Inorganic Chemistry (Chapter 1)**
   1. Introduction and Review
   2. The origin of the elements and elemental abundances
      1. What is inorganic chemistry? What is an “element”? Synthesis of the elements, their cosmic and terrestrial abundances, isotopes and atomic weight.

1. **The Periodic Table & Atomic Structure (Chapters 1 & 9)**
   1. Its Origins and Basis (Chapter 1)
      1. Early efforts to systematize observations regarding elemental properties; recognition of patterns in elemental atomic masses, “grouping” of elements according to physical properties and chemistry, culminating in Mendeleev's "periodic table".
   2. Basis for the periodic table in the electronic structure of atoms; filling of atomic orbitals; development of "periods" and "groups"; meaning of: "valence electrons", "valence - or - oxidation state", "isoelectronic species".
   3. Periodic Trends in Atomic Properties (Chapter 9)
      1. Electronic screening and effective nuclear charge; trends in ionization energies, ionic and atomic radii, electron affinities; concept of electronegativity and its periodic variation.
      2. Overall dependence of elemental properties and chemistry on location in the periodic table; elemental forms: metals, non-metals, and semimetals; physical and chemical behavior within the periods and groups; oxidation states of main group elements.

- **Molecular Structure and Bonding (Chapters 2 & 3)**
  1. Overview of "bonding" in inorganic compounds, metallic, covalent, ionic, Van der Waals, etc.; relation to form and properties of the elements and their compounds; initial focus on covalent bonding in molecules.
  2. Lewis concept; orbital hybridization; VSEPR theory; MO theory (Chapter 2)
  3. An Introduction to Molecular Symmetry (Chapter 3)
     1. Symmetry operations, elements, and point groups

1. **Structure and Bonding of Ionic Solids** **(Chapters 4)**
   1. Structure and bonding of simle solids; concept of Lattice enthalpy; Born-Haber cycle for ionic solids; radius ratios and crystal structure; close-packed structures as a starting point for consideration of crystal structures of ionic solids; important structure types; thermal stability and solubility of ionic solids with complex anions, dependence on r-/r+.

1. **Acid-Base Concepts (Chapter 5)**
   1. Water, H-Bonding, Acids and Bases
   2. Water as a solvent, other solvents; the "hydrogen bond", in water, ice and in biology; dielectric constant; aqueous solutions: heats of hydration of ions, hydrolysis; protonic acids and bases (Chapter 5)
   3. Lewis acids and bases; effect of inductive, resonance and steric interactions on relative strengths; "hard" and "soft" acids and bases (HSAB theory) (Chapter 4)

1. **Redox Chemistry (Chapter 6)**
   1. Oxidation and Reduction
   2. Determining oxidation numbers from Lewis structures, periodic variation of oxidation nos., balancing redox equations, half-cells, the Nernst equation, electrode potentials as thermodynamic functions, Latimer, Frost, Pourbaix and Ellingham diagrams, disproportionation.

- **The d-Transition Metal Elements (Chapter 19)**
  1. d-Transition Metals
  2. A survey of these elements (all metals) and their most important oxidation states, electronic structures.

- **Coordination Chemistry (Chapters 7 & 20)**
  1. Transition metal complexes, their formulas (nomenclature), structures (coordination nos.), basic properties (Lewis acid-base complexes); ligands: typical examples, bi-, tri- etc. dendate - the chelate effect; stereoisomerism; coordination equilibria; coordn. complexes and the HSAB concept. (Chapter 7)
  2. Electronic structure and bonding in transition metal complexes, Crystal Field/Ligand Field theory – for Oh and Td complexes; high spin and low spin configurations. (Chapter 20)

1. **Main Group Chemistry (Chapters 10-17)**
   1. Hydrogen and Hydrides (Chapter 10)
      1. Hydrogen and its isotopes - production, chemistry and use; types of "hydrides" and their properties.
   2. Group 1 & 2 - The Alkali and Alkaline Earth Elements (Chapters 11 & 12)
      1. Trends within these Groups; isolation of the elements and their properties; the ions and their properties, complexes, organometallic compounds, the ions in biology.
   3. Group 13 (Chapters 13)
      1. Boron and Its Compounds
      2. Property trends within Group 13 - comparison with Group 3; elemental boron and borides; boron hydrides, carboranes, and cluster formation in inorganic chemistry in general; boron oxide and hydroxide, borates.

- The Rest of Group 13 - Al --> Tl

1. The elements and their properties; chief oxidation states - the "inert pair effect"; their ions and significant compounds - their structures, bonding and properties.

1. Group 14 (Chapters 14)
   1. Carbon - What Organic Chemistry Leaves Out
   2. The forms of elemental carbon - diamond, graphite and the "third form", "fullerenes" - structure, bonding, properties; the oxides (CO and CO2), carbonate and bicarbonate ions.

- The Heavier Group 14 Elements - Si --> Pb

14. Elemental properties, a chemical view of doping in Si; silica and silicates, structures and properties, aluminosilicates, zeolites, the silicones; the rest of Group 14.

1. Group 15 (Chapters 15)
   1. Nitrogen
   2. Trends within this Group; elemental nitrogen (the thermodynamic sink in N chemistry) - Nitrogen "fixation" - in biology and in industry; ammonia and its properties; the nitrogen oxides (NOx).

- Group 15 - P --> Bi

1. The elements - their structures and properties; key compounds and their important features - the halides, oxides and oxyacids - particularly of P.

1. Group 16 (Chapters 16)
   1. Oxygen and Oxides
   2. Trends in Group 16; properties of elemental oxygen and ozone; the "oxidative" ability of O2 à oxides (covalent and ionic).

- Sulfur, Sellenium, and Tellurium

1. The structures and properties of the elements (particularly S) and their key compounds (and oxidation states): H2S and sulfides; the oxides of S, their properties and chemistry.

1. Group 17 (Chapters 17)
   1. The Halogens
   2. Trends within Group 17; isolation and properties of the elements; key compounds, especially the hydrides, halides and oxyhalides (including nomenclature).

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\) Describe the electronic structure of the elements and explain how it relates to the arrangement and utility of the periodic table. Correlate the chemical and physical properties of elements and their compounds with their positions in the periodic table.
- 2\) Compare and contrast different theories to describe the bonding and electronic structure of inorganic compounds. Utilize knowledge of bonding to predict the structures, physical properties, and general reactivity of elementals and inorganic compounds.
- 3\) Interpret how the structure and bonding in transition metal coordination compounds relates to their physical properties, including magnetics and perceived color.
- 4\) Categorize and characterize reactions of inorganic compounds as belonging to one or more categories including redox, acid-base, coordination, etc., and predict the relative equilibrium of those reactions.
