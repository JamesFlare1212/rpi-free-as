## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is the third part of a three-semester sequence aimed at cultivating theory, analysis, and musicianship skills that will enhance students’ ability to understand and create music in a diverse range of styles and media. Each unit is focused around a general technical principle which is explicated and explored through written work, analysis, ear-training, improvisation, performance, and creative practice. Topics include chromatic harmony, tonicization/modulation, altered dominants, and advanced forms. Students will also engage in expanded listening exercises, helping them to develop and articulate their own approaches and responses to a variety of musical and sonic experiences drawn from contemporary music and sound art.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

- All course texts and materials will be provided by the instructor. Students are required to have a pencil and blank manuscript paper to use every class.
- Notated creative projects should be completed using notation software. The best notation programs are Sibelius and Dorico, but free programs like MuseScore (https://musescore.org/en) are also perfectly acceptable.
- A simple digital audio program will be necessary for some of the creative projects. A reasonably good free option is Audacity (https://www.audacityteam.org/).
- For extra musicianship practice, students can use programs like Earmaster (https://www.earmaster.com/).
- We will use MIDI keyboards in class. In order to make use of the keyboards, it is necessary to have some kind of program that can take MIDI input and produce sound. A free program that can be used for this purpose is bitKlavier (https://bitklavier.com/)

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to dictate chromatoic melodies, counterpoint, and harmonic progressions.
- Students will be able to analyze chromatic counterpoint, chord progressions, part-writing, and advanced forms.
- Students will be able to improvise and compose chromatic counterpoint, chord progressions, and multi-part harmonic realizations.
- Students will be able to perform notated chromatic counterpoint, chord progressions, and part-writing.
- Students will develop a fluency in critical listening skills and a vocabulary for articulating what they hear.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Class Participation:** This course depends on engaged and open-minded participation in all class activities, including lectures, discussions, workshops, performances, and listening exercises.

**Written Homework:** There are two written homework assignments given each unit. These will be checked in class the day they are due and are graded based on effort.

**Listening Responses**: Throughout the semester, there will be seven listening exercises, each of which will be accompanied by a written response to be turned in through LMS.

**In-Class Written and Dictation Exams**: Each unit, students will take an in-class exam to demonstrate their grasp of the unit’s basic theoretical concepts followed immediately by a dictation exam.

**Creative Projects:** Creative work provides students the opportunity to apply theoretical concepts and skills to their own creative practice. Given the scope of this class, these projects will be simple creative tasks that will be evaluated through a clearly defined set of criteria. If students want to develop their creative practice beyond these projects, they are welcome to share their work in office hours.

**Performance Projects:** For each unit, there will be an assigned performance project which will provide students with an opportunity to apply the relevant topic to their ongoing practice of musical performance.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance:** Students are expected to attend all class meetings. Over the course of a semester, many unexpected conflicts and distractions can arise. If a student must miss a class meeting for any reason, they should provide the instructor with advance notice and arrange for an opportunity to catch up on missed work and material. Students may miss two classes without any penalty. Beyond that, each unexcused absence will incur a penalty of 2.5% to the final course grade.

### Other Course Policies:

**Email:** During the workweek, I will answer all course-related email within 24 hours of receipt.

**Late Work:** This course moves quickly through material and each unit’s work builds upon previously introduced topics. In order to stay on top of the course topics, it is essential that students do their work and turn it in on time. Late work can be turned in up to two weeks after it is due with a penalty of 5% per day it is late. If a student is aware of extenuating circumstances that will require them to miss an assignment’s due date, they should communicate in advance to avoid the late penalty.

**Devices:** Electronic devices, including phones, laptops, tablets, and smart watches, are ubiquitous in contemporary society. In furtherance of the learning goals of this course and in tandem with a robust participation in classroom activities, their use is encouraged. However, if at any point a student’s use of electronic devices becomes counterproductive to their learning or distracting to their fellow students, they will be asked to discontinue use and could face penalties to their participation grade.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
