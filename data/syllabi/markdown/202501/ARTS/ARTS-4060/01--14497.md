## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course explores computer animation with a focus on procedural systems and special effects. Specific areas of focus include particles, pyrotechnics, fluids and rigid body simulations. Lectures, discussion, and exposure to contemporary work will enable students to develop skills in this rapidly evolving field.

While this course explores 3D visual effects, we will be doing so in a “procedural” manner. This means that instead of making an animation, environment or sculptural form as single “one off” form from scratch, we will learn how to **construct forms so that the form might be changed, repurposed or reanimated in accordance with the instructions that we encode into it.**

This means that we will be learning SideFX software’s Houdini. While this program has a deep history in the field of visual effects, it is becoming increasingly important for a much broader range of purposes, in and outside of the entertainment industry. This is due to the high degree of control, flexibility and speed that it can bring to the people that are comfortable with it. In other words, learning Houdini will allow you to do more things in less time, to have greater control over them and to accomplish more tasks with fewer resources. But, most importantly, **since this software is an increasingly pervasive part of our media landscape, learning it will give you the tools to more thoroughly deconstruct what you see in the world around you.**

However, Houdini is a difficult program to learn. Full stop. It relies on an understanding of how 3D geometry functions in an abstract sense. **This means that you’ll be using math, basic coding and script writing to build parts of our animations**. But, I speak from experience when I say (as a person with a non-math, non-coding background), that learning these process is as exhilarating and mind-expanding as it can be frustrating.

Visual effects is a huge field with a rich history, but it is my goal to make sure that you leave this course with a deep enough understanding of some fundamental approaches to its study. With this foundation, your individual pursuits will be a less-intimidating and more exciting journey.

**RECOMMENDED RESOURCES**

SIDEFX TUTORIALS

SideFX’s own tutorials page is very good and contains a searchable database of (mostly) free tutorials that range from beginner to advanced levels.

*https://www.sidefx.com/tutorials/*

*SIDEFX WEBINARS*

SideFX’s Talk/Webinars page is also an excellent place to look for more in-depth videos on specific tools/topics. These are typically more advanced, but are still fun to watch, even if you don’t understand everything they’re talking about.

*https://www.sidefx.com/learn/talks/*

*CGWIKI/Matt Estela*

This is my all time favorite resource, created by artist Matt Estela. This was originally created by him, for himself, as a set of notes. But, as time has gone on, it has become a valuable resource to many Houdini artists, myself included. If you’re looking for a way to break down a complex concept or process into a quick paragraph of text, this is THE place.

*https://tokeru.com/cgwiki/*

ENTAGMA

Entagma (artists Manuel Casasola and Moritz Schwind) have been producing excellent

video tutorials that explore Houdini in a fun, experimental way for years. Their videos

are typically short and to the point. They strike a great balance between being friendly

to beginners while simultaneously pushing the boundaries of the software.

*https://entagma.com/*

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**HOW THIS CLASS WILL WORK**

The challenge with learning anything that is as technically sophisticated as Houdini (or 3D animation in general) is to learn what is necessary while still leaving room for creativity and experimentation. In other words, how do we learn a TON of new technical stuff and not get lost in the details and have fun?

For this course, we will create a series of projects. Each project will be based on a specific technique and set of tools. I will demonstrate through tutorial videos and lectures how these tools work. It will then be your job to create your own animation using these tools. Sometimes, the projects will be very short and you will simply submit a Houdini file or project folder to our Blackboard page. Others will be lengthier and the final product will be a fully rendered animation, where you will submit a link to a Youtube or Vimeo containing your work.

These projects, along with attendance and participation will constitute your grade.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand the creation and distribution of procedural geometry.
- Learn the fundamentals of Houdini’s SOPs, DOPs and LOPs contexts.
- Render animations, create still images and USD files using the Solaris environment.
- Explore the basics of dynamic simulations, including smoke, fire, fluids, particles, soft and rigid bodies.
- Create animation without keyframes, using scripts, expressions and VEX code.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**GRADING**

- This course is primarily project based. This means that you will be working across multiple class sessions on a project. 70% of your final grade will be the average grade of these projects. The remainder will be based on in-class participation. Therefore, while a large percentage of your grade is based on the outcome of your work, attendance and participation is critical.
- Criteria for project grading will include, but is not limited to, technical proficiency, creativity, craftsmanship, and presentation.
- Completed projects are due at the beginning of class on the stated date and time.
- Assignments not completed on due date/time will be lowered one letter grade.
- Participation includes being prepared to work on projects in class, participating in discussions and critiques, and being open to constructive suggestions from the instructor and classmates.
- Students have the option to redo or revise any project for an improved grade.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

### ADDITIONAL ACADEMIC INTEGRITY POLICIES

While deciding who the "author" is in artistic production can be a thorny process, in this class, all work must be made WHOLLY by you. If there are any exceptions to this rule, I will inform you in advance.  
This means:  
\* If a model is required for a project, **you and you alone** will be doing that modeling.  
\* If a rig needs to be constructed, **you and you alone** will be the one constructing it.  
\* If a script needs to be written, **you and you alone** will write that script.  
The first infraction against this policy will result in failure of the project.  
The second infraction will result in failure of the course and a report to the Office of the Dean.
