## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

***Rethinking Documentary*** is a production course investigating documentary or non-fiction film/video. Taking a broad look at what defines "documentary" media, this course will incorporate criticism with production. Looking at such questions as truth versus fiction, personal responsibility, community involvement, the efficacy of video intervention, the ethics of representation and the authority of mass media, as well as aesthetic formal considerations, students will be asked to produce a range of media artworks questioning conventional documentary styles, experimenting with diverse techniques to realize their own unique style.

Alongside this production, students will view and theorize about traditional documentary works including ethnographic films, cinema verité, propaganda films, but also "home movies," reality TV, tabloid news, auto-biographic and activist videos, experimental film, video, social media, gaming and new media. Students will concentrate on developing the skills to create and identify paradigms and cinematic structures in documentary works; the knowledge then will be transferred to their own personal style and production.

The course’s main emphasis is the completion of two short video sketches, followed by one major project. Class time will be divided between critiques of student projects (both in progress and finished pieces), and screening of films and videos by independent artists, visits to EMPAC’ Film/Video, Time-Based Visual Art Fall ‘24 series, along with discussions of readings and different approaches to the documentary production. Students will concentrate on developing the skills to create and identify paradigms and cinematic structures in documentary works, articulating *their own personal voice* and vision in documentary practice.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Learning Outcomes:**

***Students who successfully complete this course at the 4000 level will be able to:***

- Develop the critical and media literacy skills to identify and analyze paradigms and cinematic structures in documentary works and "non-fiction" experimental media art
- Incorporate knowledge from cinematic structures into their own personal style and production.
- Learn to balance aesthetic and technical video production with the study of history/theory of independent/alternative film/video art-making
- Develop expertise to create documentaries, through idea development, production and post-production

***Students who successfully complete this course at the 6000 level will be able to:***

- Demonstrate a complex understanding of critical and media literacy skills to analyze and write about paradigms and cinematic structures in documentary works and "non-fiction" experimental media art
- Incorporate advanced knowledge from cinematic structures into their own personal style and production that demonstrates artistic and professional excellence
- Produce advanced quality documentary art which demonstrates aesthetic and technical skills (particularly digital video production) with the study of history/theory of independent/alternative film/video art-making
- Demonstrate an advanced depth of expertise to create documentaries, from idea development to production to post-production

**Learning Assessments**

***Students at the 4000 level will be assessed to evaluate whether learning outcomes have been achieved in the following ways:***

- All projects must be presented before the entire class, from the beginning of the process through the final work, for critique and assessment of how knowledge learned is effectively incorporated into personal production
- All assignments are graded individually, assessing aesthetic, technical and professional accomplishment

***Students at the 6000 level will be assessed to evaluate whether learning outcomes have been achieved in the following ways:***

- All projects must be presented before the entire class, from the beginning of the process through the final work, demonstrating an advanced level of ability to engage in critique and achieving personal production with showcases artistic and professional excellence
- All assignments are graded individually, assessing advanced level of aesthetic, technical and professional production expertise
- Student must demonstrate independence in critical thinking and artistic production

**Class Assignments:**

**PRODUCTION**:

*A focus on developing the craft of conceiving and creating documentary and non-fiction projects. Video assignments will be completed outside of class time.*

- **Video Sketch: Magic of the Moment**

*Length 1-3 minutes. Due: Thursday, 1/25*

Create a video poem, an intimate portrait of a person or a place, a poetic experience. Use expressionistic techniques to film this piece. Introduce us to the person/place, by creating a mood. Do not rely on interviews, voice overs or additional music. Witness, eavesdrop, observe and listen deeply, discover the action, sound and magic of the moment. Use an off-the-hip shooting style, work with diegetic sound and image.

- **Video Sketch: Call to Action**

*Length 1-3 min. Due: Thursday, 2/12*

A few options here:

- Activate your audience through your own self-expression. Examine a topic in an open-ended a way, to promote the audience to experience personally, become curious, and even evocatively motivate them to action. You can incorporate an interview or two if desired.
- Persuade, Promote, Interrogate, Intervene: pick a topic which you are passionate about and which you would like to produce a change, such as environmental issues, anti-racism, LGBTQ rights, or something important to you that you believe needs to change. Find a way to speak about it in a convincing manner: produce a public service announcement (PSA) and express a very strong point of view.

*"Artwork should encourage inquiry, offer space for judgment and provide tools for evaluation and further action -- in short, encourage an active response... In a culture that valorizes consumption -- and the disposable culture responsive to that imperative -- it may well be crucial for documentarians to consider the stakes of an intervention: to challenge and activate audiences even in the process of instruction or entertainment. In this regard, analysis remains the documentarist's most crucial support." "Towards a Poetics of Documentary" by Michael Renov, Theorizing Documentary, Ed. Michael Renov, AFI Film Readers, Routledge, 1993, p. 31-32.*

*Students may integrate the first two assignments into a final project.*
