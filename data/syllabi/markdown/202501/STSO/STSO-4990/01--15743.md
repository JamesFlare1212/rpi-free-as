## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The Senior Project Seminar is the culminating academic experience designed to integrate and apply knowledge and skills acquired throughout the STS & Sustainability Majors. This course provides a structured environment for students to develop, complete, and present a major research project or thesis on a topic of their choosing. Students will engage in independent inquiry and analysis while benefiting from peer collaboration and faculty mentorship.

The seminar emphasizes the iterative nature of the research and writing process, guiding students through topic selection, literature review, methodological design, data collection, analysis, and synthesis. It also fosters critical thinking, professional communication, and ethical research practices. Students will have the flexibility to tailor their projects to their academic interests and career goals, culminating in either a traditional written thesis or an alternative project that showcases their findings in a creative or applied format, such as a report, multimedia artifact, or community engagement project.

Weekly workshops and discussions will focus on refining research questions, developing arguments, managing large-scale projects, and presenting findings effectively to diverse audiences. By the end of the course, students will have produced a professional-quality deliverable and presented their work in an academic setting, preparing them for graduate studies, professional careers, or other post-graduate endeavors.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

By the end of the course, students will:

1. Conduct original research using appropriate methodologies.
2. Analyze and synthesize information effectively.
3. Develop a comprehensive written thesis or equivalent project.
4. Present findings in professional written and oral formats.
5. Engage in constructive peer review and refine work based on feedback.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Conduct original research using appropriate methodologies.
- 2\. Analyze and synthesize information effectively.
- 3\. Develop a comprehensive written thesis or equivalent project.
- 4\. Present findings in professional written and oral formats.
- 5\. Engage in constructive peer review and refine work based on feedback.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1. **Weekly Reflections (10%):** When we have assigned readings, you are expected to do the reading in advance and write a brief (around 300 words) reflection on what you learned from the text and how you plan to apply it to your own writing. You will print and bring these reflections to the class.
2. **Data Collection Report (15%)**: Students will conduct research based on their approved proposal and document the process. This includes methods used, challenges encountered, and preliminary findings. Data collection report should focus on the data you have collected so far. Hardcopy *Due: Week 4.*
3. **Introduction, Abstract, and Conclusion Draft (10%)**: A draft of the thesis introduction, outlining the research scope, significance, and methods, along with a conclusion synthesizing the key findings and implications. Hardcopy *Due: Week 4.*
4. **Making a Good Argument (10%)**: Students will construct a strong argument based on their analysis, supported by evidence and aligned with their research question. This assignment includes peer review and refinement. Hardcopy *Due: Week 5.*
5. **Research Significance (5%)**: Write a paragraph or two explaining why your research topic is worth studying. Hardcopy *Due: Week 6.*
6. **Findings (10%)**: A written analysis of the collected data, highlighting key themes, trends, or findings. This draft will be reviewed in class workshops to refine analytical approaches. Hardcopy *Due: Week 10.*
7. **Data Analysis/Discussion (10%):** A written analysis of the collected data, highlighting key themes, trends, or findings. This draft will be reviewed in class workshops to refine analytical approaches. Hardcopy *Due: Week 11.*
8. **Oral Presentation (10%)**: Students will deliver a professional presentation of their research findings to the class, including visuals or multimedia as appropriate. Presentations will be peer-reviewed for clarity and engagement. Hardcopy *Due: Week 15.*
9. **Final Project/Thesis (20%)**: A complete and polished project or thesis, incorporating all revised sections and feedback. Students may submit a traditional written thesis or an alternative format approved by the instructor. Hardcopy *Due: Week 16.*

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
