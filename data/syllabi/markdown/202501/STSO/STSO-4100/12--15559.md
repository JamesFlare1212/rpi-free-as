## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course focuses on increasing students’ knowledge concerning the impact of technical and non-technical issues on the viability of engineering designs and solutions. The non-technical issues we will consider include the cognitive and physical strengths and limitations of people in the chain spanning from product/equipment designers/manufacturers to end users, as well as economic, environmental, cultural, political, ethical, health and safety, and societal influences. During the course, we will read and discuss articles and case studies in which the solution pursued did not have the desired effect or led to disaster. Possible explanations include a failure to take into account the environmental, economic, socio-cultural, and/or political issues associated with the technology’s ultimate usage. In addition, the resulting impact of the disaster/event on society will be examined.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will improve their verbal and written communication skills through interactive discussions, presentations, project work, and essays. They will effectively share analyses and findings with diverse audiences while collaborating with peers to address complex engineering challenges.
- Students will demonstrate their understanding of the global impact of engineering solutions by critically evaluating real-world case studies. They will identify and assess the technical, ethical, economic, environmental, political and socio-cultural factors involved, and propose viable design improvements or alternatives.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**general expectations**

There are 7 steps to success in this course that will help you grow academically and professionally:

**1.** **Be engaged with learning**

Among other things this course represents human behavior at work, so students are expected to hold themselves to professional expectations of behavior – You’ve signed up for an In-person course with a scheduled class time. Take copious and detailed notes. Review readings, assignments you’ve completed, and notes before each class so everything is fresh in your mind.

**2.** **A. Be on time and**

We will get started promptly. Arriving late is both disruptive and disrespectful to classmates who are attempting to participate in their learning process and also affects your own ability to participate in and learn from the entire class session. Plus, being on time to meetings is just really good practice for being in the professional world. So, be awake, prepared, and ready to focus and participate.

**B. Be in class**

You are expected to attend each class regularly and be fully prepared. Failure to attend and participate regularly decreases your grade. I will be taking attendance and expect that you will be present and engage in team activities on WebEx:

You will lose points:

• If you miss more than 1 class without prior notice

• If you attend regularly but do not engage

• If you provide minimal feedback to your peers

If you neither attend regularly nor participate, your participation grade will be 25 points or less. As in business, you will receive 1 personal day for prior notice absence. You may have a trip coming up or wish to study for another exam. If I do not hear from you, I will assume you are taking the class off as one of your personal days. PLEASE NOTE: This day may not be on the day of your team

**3.** **Be proactive if you’re absent**

You are responsible for making up what you’ve missed. Powerpoints, handouts, and other material will be on LMS. You are responsible for getting that material from LMS, reviewing it in detail, and taking detailed notes. It is necessary that you complete these steps and take every effort to learn the material you missed before you meet with me (meeting with me is optional).

**4.** **Practice effective communication**

Check your email and the WebEx Teams chat space every day. I will be posting things and emailing students regularly. Email is also the preferred method for you to contact me outside of class. I will usually respond to your email within 24 hours. My emails to you will be professional; your emails to me should also be professional (relevant, brief, on-topic, with proper spelling and grammar, and with appropriate “business-style” salutations and closings). Do not send me an email asking a question about course policies, an assignment, or scheduling until you have checked whether the information is available in this syllabus, and on LMS.

**5.** **Do your work on time**

Assignments are due on the date indicated in the syllabus. Any changes to deadlines (these are rare and would only give you more, not less, time) are announced in class and via email/Webex Teams Chat. Deadlines are real at work, so they’re real here too. No late work will be accepted.

Most assignments will state that you should hand it in via LMS. A few assignments might state that you should email them to me as an attached file instead. Using Microsoft Office is expected (e.g., Word, Excel, and PowerPoint). Links to a shared document site will not be acceptable ways to deliver assignments.

**6.** **Pay attention to writing skills**

Written communication skills are an essential part of the engineering business world. Students are expected to rely heavily on assessment rubrics for each assignment to understand standards for writing assignments. In addition to those standards, assignments/papers must follow the following professional format:

12-point font, Double spaced, 1” margins, and APA style for all in-text citations as well as reference lists. For guidance in proper referencing of sources, as well as how to avoid plagiarism, consult the most recent editions of the following works: (https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/apa_style/apa_formatting_and_style_guide/general_format.html OR https://www.apastyle.org). Since formatting is part of the professional world, it matters here too. Grade deductions will be taken for violations of these expectations. Format expectations for specific assignments will be discussed during the semester and will follow conventions in industry.

**7.** **Expect change**

Any changes will be announced in class and via email/Webex Teams Chat. Regardless of absence, students are responsible for adhering to the information contained in the syllabus and for being up-to-date regarding announcements about course activities, assignments, the schedule, & deadlines.

**Please remember: The general rule of thumb regarding college studying is, that for each class, students should spend approximately 2-3 hours of study time for each hour that they spend in class.**
