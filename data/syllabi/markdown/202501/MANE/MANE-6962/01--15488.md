## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course will cover past and future scientific space missions ranging from  
CubeSats (e.g., ASTERIA), through formation-flying and medium-size missions  
(e.g., GRACE), and onto flagship missions (e.g., the James Webb Space  
Telescope). Throughout the seminar, the student will learn to translate scientific  
goals into engineering requirements. The students will research specific missions,  
quantitatively analyze them, and present their findings in class.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

- Analyzing and interpreting the objectives of space instruments.
- Translating mission objectives into quantifiable engineering requirements and constraints for space mission development.
- Performing quantitative analyses of space missions/instruments.
- Developing and delivering clear, well-researched presentations.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

No Course Learning Outcomes are available for this course.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students will be assessed through two project-based assignments:

1. **Project 1: Zero-Order Calculation** – Students select a space instrument and perform a fundamental first-principles analysis to determine key performance parameters.
2. **Project 2: First-Order Calculation & Simulations** – Building on Project 1, students refine their analysis by incorporating higher-order effects and, if applicable, simulations.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

### **Criteria Details**

**Grade Breakdown:**

- **Project 1:** – 30%
- **Project 2:** – 50%
- **Attendance** – 20%

Each project will be graded based on technical accuracy, depth of analysis, justification of assumptions, clarity of communication, and completeness of results.

**Handling Poor Performance:** If a student demonstrates significant improvement in **Project 2**, their score may be weighted more heavily to reflect growth in understanding.

**Grade Appeals:** Students may appeal grades by submitting a written request within **one week** of receiving feedback, outlining specific concerns.

**Mid-Semester Assessment:**

- By mid-semester, students will receive individual feedback based on **Project 1** results, which will indicate their standing and areas for improvement.
- Additional check-ins and office hours will be available for students seeking guidance.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Collaborative discussions and group work are encouraged for general concept development and problem-solving approaches. However, each student is responsible for independently conducting their own analysis, calculations, and written reports.

**Plagiarism and Cheating Penalties:**

- **Submitting an external report or someone else’s work as your own will result in an automatic grade of F for the course.**
- **For other academic integrity violations (e.g., uncredited use of external sources), the first offense will result in a warning, and a second offense will result in a grade of F for the course.**

If students are unsure about what constitutes plagiarism or improper use of external sources, they should seek clarification before submission.
