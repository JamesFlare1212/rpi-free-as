## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is the second of a two-semester sequence for seniors intended to be a “capstone” design project where students have the opportunity to utilize the broad range of their undergraduate experience in an interdisciplinary design project. Projects are selected to provide interaction between nuclear engineering and engineering physics majors to provide exposure to cross-fertilization of ideas and team interaction, which simulates anticipated future professional experience. The product of each design project is a comprehensive report or design proposal having both global and detail completeness. Under some circumstances, the project may involve development of cost information necessary to effect construction and may actually involve construction and commissioning of the designed apparatus. Students will demonstrate the ability to relevant skills for: problem definition and requirement analysis, design specifications, consideration of alternative solutions, constraints, risk, use of appropriate standards, presentation skills, and consideration of health, safety, environments, social, economic, etc. issues as appropriate. This is a communication-intensive and writing-intensive course.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

Final project will be judged by faculty and students, the best team will win the Max Yeater award.

A Typical Week

110 minutes of class Fridays 2:00-3:50 pm for one or more of the following:

- student presentations (status reports / progress reports / design reviews)
- faculty presentations on the engineering design process specific to the nuclear engineering ecosystem, technical communication, and engineering impact analysis (i.e., considering engineering solutions in global, economic, environmental, and societal contexts)
- class discussions on (possibly common, sometimes unique) design challenges teams are confronting
- individual team meetings at a time everyone can attend
- formal student team oral presentations (evaluated by faculty)
- submission of formal written reports (evaluated by faculty)
