## Course Content

### Content Details:

1. **Vector and tensor calculus**

- Definitions and tensor operations, Tensor decomposition, Principal directions and principal values. Polar and other decompositions.
- Divergence, gradient and curl operations,
- Integral theorems: Gauss, Green and Stokes,
- Metric of space

2\. **Finite strain kinematics**

- Referentials
- Deformation tensors
- Variation of volume and area elements during deformation
- Reduction to small deformation theory
- Rate of deformation and various time derivatives
- Invariant tensors and invariant functions of vectors

3\. **Principles of mechanics and introduction of the notion of stress**

- Techniques of calculus of variations
- Hamilton variational principle
- Lagrangian mechanics, Euler-Lagrange equations
- Conservation principles and definition of force
- Conservation principles – forms adequate for continuum mechanics
- Tractions, Cauchy principle
- Cauchy stress tensor
- Balance equations in spatial coordinates
- Piola-Kirchoff stresses
- Balance equations in material coordinates
- Energy and work conjugate stresses
- Principle of virtual power
- Other conservation laws. Noether’s theorem. Path independent integrals

4\. **Thermodynamics of continua (and links with statistical physics)**

- Definitions
- The first law of thermodynamics
- Classical irreversible thermodynamics – the local equilibrium hypothesis
- Why do we need the second law? Entropy. Caratheodory thorem
- The second law of thermodynamics
- Criteria for equilibrium
- Legendre transformation. The Helmholtz and Gibbs free energies
- The Claussius-Duhem inequality
- The Onsager-Casimir relations. Linear phenomenological laws.
- Steady-state non-equilibrium. Minimum entropy production.
- Limitations of classical irreversible thermodynamics.

In the limit of time, we will also discuss:  
5\. **Constitutive equations**

- Limitations on the choice of constitutive relations imposed by thermodynamics
- Simple forms: linear and non-linear elasticity (Hookean, Neo-Hookean, Mooney-Rivlin, hyperelastic solids).
- Dissipation potentials. Link with plasticity and visco-elasticity.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will learn the generalized theory of deformation from which all formulations commonly used in engineering are derived, as well as the basics of thermodynamics of continua, including the restrictions imposed on the functional forms of constitutive equations

Homework: approximately 6 HW sets; one Midterm exam; Final exam.
