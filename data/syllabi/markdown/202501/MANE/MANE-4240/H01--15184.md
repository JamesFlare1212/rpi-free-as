In a Project Format course, the achievement of learning objectives is demonstrated by successful completion of a semester-long, individual term project. Although there are live interactive sessions, there are no homework assignments or exams and learning is demonstrated through completion of three project reports (Milestones) that successfully combine, blend, and integrate the key course topics with the selected project topic. The course also involves active participation in live interactive sessions where students hold discussions about course topics and demonstrate their grasp of them by showcasing their progress with their projects. Students receive full credit only when the successful seamless integration of course topics and the project topic is clearly demonstrated. ***Close continuous interaction between the instructor and individual students in the form of one-on-one mentoring meetings is essential for successful achievement of the learning objectives of this course.***

RPI has long been recognized world-wide as a technological institution pioneering innovative teaching and learning approaches. The Project Format courses represent a current trend in learning experimentation in that tradition.

## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

### Course Description

This is a course about the fundamentals of the finite element method (FEM) and its application in computational science and engineering applications. The course focuses on the implementation of the Galerkin formulation of the finite element method for the determination of approximate solutions of partial differential equations encountered in scientific and engineering applications. The course covers the foundations of the method and evolves into its applications to real engineering problems. It starts with a presentation of differential and variational formulations of commonly used governing equations. It continues with a description and discussion of finite element basis functions and their incorporation into the original variational formulation to produce the finite element equation.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The following key topics will be covered, each constituting a major learning milestone:

1. Differential and Variational Formulation of Engineering Problems and Analytical and Numerical Methods of Solution.
2. Finite Element Basis Functions and Galerkin Finite Element Method for One-Dimensional Boundary Value Problems.
3. Galerkin Finite Element Method for Two- and Three-Dimensional Boundary Value Problems and More Complex Problems.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Describe, discuss and explain the fundamental theoretical basis of the finite element method (variational formulation, finite element basis functions, solutions of systems of algebraic equations).
- Demonstrate skill in the application of the method together with existing finite element software for the determination of approximate solutions to meaningful, realistic engineering problems
- Critically examine the results of finite element computations and provide sound, authoritative judgement regarding their validity and reliability.
