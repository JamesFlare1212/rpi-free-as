## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Aerodynamics and dynamics of lifting rotors in Vertical Take-Off and Landing (VTOL) applications. Design concepts by which rotor weight and stress are minimized and vehicle control is provided. Weight and engine power trends for configuration definition. Center of gravity and aerodynamic lift and moment for equilibrium and desired aircraft attitude. Methods for determining size, weight, and cost for a given payload, useful volume, and specified performance. This is a communication-intensive course.  
This course is cross-listed as MANE 6210

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Interpret customer needs and relate them to helicopter-specific performance metrics
- Understand and articulate relative advantages and disadvantages of different types of vertical takeoff and landing (VTOL) aircraft
- Use low-order models to perform “back-of-the-envelope” calculations to design and estimate performance of VTOL aircraft
- Write technical reports and deliver presentations about their aircraft design at different stages of the design process.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students (working on a team of up to 10 students) are assessed via presentations and technical reports throughout the semester.  
There are four presentations.  
1\. Benchmarking -  
Identify the requirements and constraints on a complex engineering problem -- VTOL aircraft designed to a mission defined by the Vertical Flight Society (VFS) Student Design Competition (SDC).  
Analyze existing vehicles that perform similar roles, identify what vehicle aspects result in higher performance in particular areas (i.e. power consumption in hover/cruise, or payload capacity)  
2\. Preliminary Design  
Basic sizing for new vehicle designed to the mission defined by the VFS SDC  
Alternative design concepts  
Subsystem identification and preliminary design considerations  
3\. Detail Design  
More detailed design and performance of new vehicle  
Consideration of various factors that influence the design details (e.g. noise, safety, economic factors)  
4\. Final Design  
Details about the completed vehicle design(s) and performance  
Mission analysis on selected configuration  
In addition to the presentations, there are two reports. The first covers the same material as the first two presentations, while the latter covers material from all four presentations (i.e. the entire engineering design process). This report is typically submitted for the SDC.
