## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The objective of this course is to introduce the concepts involving nonlinearities in the analysis of solids and structures using the finite element method in a practice-oriented platform. In addition to covering the theory behind the nonlinear behavior of engineering structures in response to loads and constraints, the course will dwell in simulating such behavior in various materials and applications. Students will be exposed to utilizing commercial finite elements software for a well-rounded experience.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

# Topics:

- Review of linear finite element concepts
- Nonlinear continuum mechanics of solids
  - o finite strain and stress measures,
  - o total and updated Lagrangian approaches,
  - o variational methods – virtual work principle.
- Non-linear finite element procedures
  - o incremental solution of nonlinear equations,
  - o Newton-Raphson method.
- Finite elements for elasto-plasticity
- Finite elements for hyper-elasticity
- Contact of solids using finite elements

### Other Course Policies:

# In-class exercises

In-class exercises/quizzes (ICE/ICQ) will be assigned during (or at the end) of each lecture to reinforce the understanding of the covered topic. Students can interact with the instructional team to complete the ICEs during the class duration while for ICQs, no discussion will be entertained. Both will be open-book format and will carry equal weightage. The choice of ICE/ICQ will be announced before the start. The lowest **three** of in-class exercise/quiz scores will be dropped from the calculation of final grades. The dropped scores cover all absences including excused absence, illness, and sports related absence.

# HOMEWORK

Homework shall be assigned differently for the 4000 level and 6000 level students and may include application problems using FE software. Please make a conscious effort to write clearly starting from writing appropriate expressions first before starting your calculations. Only neatly written assignments will be graded. In this course, collaboration in the form of discussion of approach to the problems is permitted to a degree. It is expected that each student’s work will be his or her own, in both form and content. This means that even if students collaborated in working on problems in the homework assignment, the final work that is handed in must not be a clone of another student’s work. It is good practice to list the people that you collaborated with. The lowest of the homework scores will be dropped from the calculation of final grades. The dropped scores cover all absences including excused absence, illness, and sports related absence.

# Tests

Two equally weighted tests will be administered during this course. Problems in tests may be assigned differently for the 4000 level and 6000 level students and shall include application problems using FE software. Scientific calculator and a single A4/letter sized **self-written crib sheet (both sides)** will be permitted. The crib sheet needs to be attached to the submitted exam. The crib sheet **must contain only equations with small subtitles and must not have any figures, explanations, or worked examples**. Dates for the tests shall be found in the course schedule (at the end of this document). There is no final examination.

Grading challenges for tests will be entertained within a week after they are returned. A missed test will result in a grade of zero unless permission is granted beforehand. Permission will not be granted except for serious illness or a family emergency. Sport related events do not qualify for missing a test in general – if a major event is planned during the test date, please get in touch with the instructor during the beginning of the semester. Such situations must be documented through the Student Experience Office (Academy Hall, x 8022, se@rpi.edu) and approved by the instructor. In such cases, the test will be rescheduled for an alternative date and/or time. All requests with pre-approved exceptions for scheduling conflicts, additional exam time and/or minimum distraction environment must be submitted to the instructor at least 3 weeks before the test, preferably sooner for organized arrangements.

# Collaboration and academic integrity:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that the students turn in are their own. Acts which violate this trust, undermine the educational process. Students are expected to always conduct themselves in a professional manner.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In addition, attempts to commit dishonesty, or to assist in the commission or attempt of such act, are also violations of the academic dishonesty policy. If found in violation of the academic dishonesty policy, students may be subject to two types of penalties. The instructor administers an academic (grade) penalty, and students may also enter the institute judicial process and be subject to such additional sanctions as: warning, probation, suspension, expulsion, and alternative actions as defined in the current *Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities.* The following specific collaboration rules apply to this class:

1. Students are encouraged to collaborate in the solution of homework and practicum problems but **submit independent solutions that are NOT copies of each other**. If a student is found in violation of this policy, **a grade of zero will be awarded for the first violation and a grade of ‘F’ in the course for the second violation**. The same policy applies if any unfair means, including receiving help from online sources or solutions from prior years, have been resorted to.
2. **Collaboration during tests or receiving help from external or online sources is strictly prohibited**. **Any violation will result in a grade of ‘F’ in the course.**
