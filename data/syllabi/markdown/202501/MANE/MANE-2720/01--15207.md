## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The objective of this course is to introduce the concepts in fluid mechanics topics viz. Fundamental concepts on fluids, Fluid Properties, Fluid Statics, Kinematics of fluids, Fluid flow and classification, Control volume, Conservation equations, Differential treatment of fluid flow, Dimensional analysis, Inviscid and viscous flow, Flow inside pipes, Boundary layer flow, and Fundamentals of compressible flow.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The following topics will be covered in this course.

- Fundamental concepts of Fluid Mechanics
  - o Viscosity, properties of fluids, classification of flows
- Fluid statics
  - o Pressure, buoyancy
  - o Hydrostatic forces on surfaces
- Basic equations in integral form
  - o Control volume
  - o Conservation laws
  - o Reynolds transport theorem
  - o Bernoulli’s equation
- Basic equations in differential form
  - o Momentum, energy, mass equations
  - o Navier Stokes equation
- Incompressible inviscid flow
- Internal viscous flow
- Dimension analysis
- Inviscid and viscous flow
- Flow inside pipes
- Boundary layer flow

# In-class exercises

In-class exercises (ICEs) will be assigned at the end of each lecture to reinforce the understanding of the covered topics. Students can interact with the instructional team to complete the ICEs within the class duration. The lowest **three** of in-class exercise scores will be dropped from the calculation of final grades. The dropped scores cover all absences including excused absence, illness, family-related absence, sports related absence etc. Any communication regarding missed ICEs will not be entertained.

# HOMEWORK assignments

Homework submission needs to be clear and legible. Please write clearly starting from your assumptions, appropriate expressions before starting your calculations. In this course, collaboration in the form of discussion of approach to the problems is permitted to a degree. It is expected that each student’s work will be their own, in both form and content. This means that even if students collaborated in working on problems in the homework assignment, the final work that is handed in must not be a clone of another student’s work. It is good practice to list the people that you collaborated with. Please refer to the schedule (at the end of this document) for the due dates of the homework assignments. The **lowest** of the homework scores will be dropped from the calculation of final grades. The dropped score(s) cover all absences including excused absence, illness, family-related absence, sports related absence etc.

# Tests

Three closed-book tests will be administered in the class and during (or inside) the class timings for a duration of about 60 minutes. The lectures applicable for each test are mentioned in the course schedule (see below). Scientific calculator and a single A4/letter sized **self-written crib sheet (both sides)** will be permitted. The crib sheet needs to be attached to the submitted exam. The crib sheet **must contain only equations with small subtitles and must not have any figures, explanations, or worked examples**. Dates for the tests shall be found in the course schedule (at the end of this document). The **lowest** of the test scores will be dropped from the calculation of final grades. The dropped score(s) cover all absences including excused absence, illness, family-related absence, sports related absence etc. **There will not be any makeup tests**. Grading challenges for tests will be addressed within two weeks after they are returned. Students with accommodation(s) shall contact the instructor as soon as possible at the start of the course.

# SUBMISSION INSTRUCTIONS:

All submissions on assignments, including ICEs and homework assignments, must be made in Gradescope. Gradescope deadlines supersede all other deadlines. Please make it easy for the graders by neatly presenting your work. Units are important. Each submission should be made as a **single pdf** file. Note that only PDF files will be accepted. Other file formats need to be converted to pdf prior to submission. Submission in the correct file format is solely the responsibility of the student. No points will be given for files in other formats nor that are emailed to the TAs or the instructor.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

# Collaboration and academic integrity:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that the students turn in are their own. Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational process. Students are expected to always conduct themselves in a professional manner.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In addition, attempts to commit dishonesty, or to assist in the commission or attempt of such act, are also violations of the academic dishonesty policy. If found in violation of the academic dishonesty policy, students may be subject to two types of penalties. The instructor administers an academic (grade) penalty, and students may also enter the institute judicial process and be subject to such additional sanctions as: warning, probation, suspension, expulsion, and alternative actions as defined in the current *Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities.* The following specific collaboration rules apply to this class:

1. Students are encouraged to collaborate in the solution of homework and practicum problems but **submit independent solutions that are NOT copies of each other**. If a student is found in violation of this policy, **a grade of zero will be awarded for the first violation and a grade of ‘F’ in the course for the second violation**. The same policy applies if any unfair means, including receiving help from online sources or solutions from prior years, have been resorted to.
2. **Collaboration during tests or receiving help from external or online sources is strictly prohibited**. **Any violation will result in a grade of ‘F’ in the course.**

Mobile Devices Policy for ***Resident*** Lecture: All mobile devices (cell/smart phones, tabs, etc.) must be stored securely away during lecture and are not to be used unless specifically directed otherwise by the instructor. Use of (or **ANY** interaction with) a mobile device during an exam without explicit permission of the instructor will be interpreted as the illicit transfer of exam data, will be considered an act of cheating and will be treated as such.

If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
