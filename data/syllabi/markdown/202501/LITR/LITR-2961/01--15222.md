## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Think of a comic book: immature, vulgar, pre-literate, commoditized, pornographic, lowest-common-denominator entertainment. Or maybe, like famed novelist and essayist Zadie Smith, you’re imagining “the best book—in any medium—about our contemporary moment.” Comics in the United States have a long and fraught history. They have long been a staple of US culture—from newspaper strips of the nineteenth century, serialized superhero comics in the New Deal era, underground comix of the 1960s and 70s—but perhaps only with the rise of the so-called “graphic novel” have they begun to gain a mainstream reputability.

So, how did comics’ fortunes rise so dramatically and become the fastest-growing medium in the publishing industry? This course will venture some answers to this question through a handful of comics from the past 50 years, including underground comix, superhero and horror comics, graphic memoirs, and graphic journalism. These texts will help you build a vocabulary for understanding and interpreting comics while introducing you to the history and formal properties of the comics medium. Most of our readings will be “graphic novels” in the sense that they are comics for which readers will need a bookmark (to use one of Art Spiegelman’s definitions).

You can expect to use this knowledge to write some of your assignments in comics form. The course also acts as a general introduction to the language of comics and will be of interest to anyone wanting to learn about visual culture and how to be a competent reader of contemporary art and literature. This course does not require any previous knowledge of comics—just a willingness to think and experiment with this exciting medium.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Reading and Interpretation: Read and interpret literature, culture, and media from a range of periods that represent diverse perspectives; build core skills in reading comprehension, interpretation, synthesis, and argumentation
- Writing and Communication: Articulate thoughts and ideas clearly and effectively in a wide range of modalities, genres, and forms of writing; articulate clear arguments of scholarly value; identify areas for improvement and implement changes to your writing style; develop a basic grasp of audiences and rhetorical situations; communicate effectively in one-on-one, group, and public settings.
- Visual Literacy and Design: Gain a proficient understanding of visual storytelling and the comics medium; read visual media critically, situating it in its historical and cultural contexts; gain new competencies in writing in the comics medium.
- Critical Thinking and Problem Solving: Exercise sound reasoning to analyze texts and issues, make decisions, and overcome problems; obtain, interpret, and use quantitative and qualitative knowledge correctly; research and fact check; demonstrate originality and inventiveness in addressing complex concepts and problems; identify strengths and weaknesses of solutions, conclusions, or approaches to problems and determine best possible resolutions.

Students will receive detailed written feedback in response to each of their submitted assignments.  
Participation and preparation for the course will be graded during an end of semester consultation meeting with the professor. During this meeting, students will reflect on their progress throughout the semester and how they have met the student learning outcomes. Grades for preparation and participation will be determined collaboratively by professor and student. The object of this consultation (and it's accompanying written self-evaluation) is to allow students to articulate their own competencies, identify strengths and areas for improvement, and to evaluate themselves in a way that is more reflective of real-world scenarios (e.g., jobs after graduation).  
The professor reserves his right to adjust this preparation/participation grade as he deems appropriate, e.g., if a student tries to take undue advantage of the consultative grading agreement.

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the work students turn in is their own work. Acts that violate this trust undermine the intellectual rigor and integrity of this course and of the educational process in general. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of academic dishonesty and you must familiarize yourself with these.

I take plagiarism and other forms of academic dishonesty seriously, including generating assignments (in part or whole) with AI. Anyone engaging in academic dishonesty will be referred to the Dean of Students, who will determine appropriate disciplinary measures. Students will also earn 0% on an assignment with plagiarized or AI-generated content. You can visit RPI’s Student Rights, Responsibilities, and Conduct page for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy.
