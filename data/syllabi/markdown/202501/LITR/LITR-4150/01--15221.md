Essays (4-5 pages each)

You are required to write a **total of three papers**. You will have **7 opportunities** to answer a paper prompt and may choose which papers to write. You may opt to write **one** of these papers in the form of a **creative work** which imitates the style of a text under discussion, with **footnotes** indicating the **literary techniques** that are being imitated (focus on imitating style and form, not theme and content). This will be due on the same day as the analytic essay for that text.

At least **one paper must be submitted by Jan 31 and two by Mar 21.** If you miss these deadlines, your final grade will **drop 5%** for each missed deadline.

Please submit your papers via email as a **Word document** attachment (or a **Google docs** link) to deeryj@rpi.edu. Keep a copy of everything you submit for the duration of the course. If you are sending a link to Google docs, remember to grant me access.

**Resubmission:** unless you score **95% or above on your first paper** (*Machine Stops* or *Brave New World*), you will be asked to **resubmit** it **within two weeks** of my returning it to you.

When resubmitting, make changes to the **same draft I returned to you** and keep my comments visible. **Track your revisions**: i.e., make sure your changes are marked and visible to me. Name your file “Resub First and Last name.”

Guideline for Assessing Essays

In general, you will be evaluated according to how well you engage with class material and how well you articulate your ideas in well-written papers. Such papers will demonstrate three key elements—**relevance, coherence,** and **writing proficiency**.

Papers should have:

- a clear thesis
- coherent organization and development
- focused paragraphs
- sentence-level competency (punctuation, grammar)

We will devote some time in class sessions to writing expectations and guidelines.

**Class Presentation**

Each person will be asked to give one report in the form of a **PowerPoint** presentation or equivalent (**5-10 minutes**). The topic will be assigned at the end of the previous class. **Format:** include a topic or question for the class to discuss further. **No more than 3 sentences per slide**. If there is a particular time you would like to make this report, let me know in advance and I will try to accommodate you. Send slides **before class starts** to deeryj@rpi.edu

*Tip: A good presentation will avoid mere plot summary and will focus on the assigned topic. It will demonstrate that you are familiar with the text, that you have given the topic some thought, and that you can articulate your ideas clearly.*

**Class Participation**

The participation grade will be based on your general contribution to **class discussion**. To do well, make sure you have completed the assigned reading and have thoughts or questions ready to share. Periodically, there will be class debates that will require no prep outside class. To give you some sense of how you are doing, you will receive an estimate of your participation grade halfway through the course.

You are being assessed both for the quantity and quality of your contributions: **quantity** referring to the frequency and **quality** to the degree of insight.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

*The* *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* and *The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement* define various forms of academic dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent your own work or designated group’s work. **If you plagiarize once,** you will receive an **F on the assignment.** If you plagiarize again, you will **fail the entire course** and may be reported to the *Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education* as appropriate. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask me for clarification. The basics: when using information from another source **(including anonymous online sources**) always cite the original text or URL and distinguish it from your own commentary. If you directly cut and paste another person’s language, you need to use it as a **quotation**.
