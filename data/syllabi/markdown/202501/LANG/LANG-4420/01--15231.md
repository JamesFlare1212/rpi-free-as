## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students who successfully complete this course will acquire the following competencies and be able to perform communicative tasks pertinent to Novice High level of language proficiency described by the American Council on the Teaching of Foreign Languages: 1. Interpersonal Communication: Students will be able to communicate and exchange information about familiar topics (e.g., clothing, sports, leisure activities, sickness) using phrases and simple sentences, sometimes supported by the memorized language. Sometimes can handle short social interactions in everyday situations by asking and answering simple questions. 2. Presentational Communication: Students will be able to (1) present information on familiar topics (e.g., clothing, sports, leisure activities, and illness), (2) write short messages and notes on familiar topics related to everyday life, or ask for information in writing. 3. Interpretive Communication: Students will be able to (1) understand words, phrases, and simple sentences related to everyday life, (2) recognize pieces of information and sometimes understand the main topic of what is being said or being read to. 4. Cultural Competence: Students will be to (1) investigate, explain, and reflect on the relationship between the practices and perspectives of cultural topics (e.g., clothing, dating, and entertaining) and (2) exhibit emerging ability to participate in some social media activities and respond appropriately to the most commonly used cultural cues.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Language class is **different** from other classes. In other classes, you can catch up by reading centralized materials and doing some intensive work for 1-2 hours. However, language learning is a skill, just like swimming or playing a musical instrument. **D****aily practice is essential for success**! You can find more details in the “Course Documents” Section on the LMS.

***Attendance & Performance***: Language learning requires utilizing the vocabulary and grammar you learn each week to engage with others where the target language is spoken. Active participation is essential. Some assignments may involve tasks such as recording your voice or participating in role-plays. If you are feeling unwell without a doctor’s note, you must email the instructor before the class. Excused absences will not impact your grade, and you can request to make-up any missed class work. However, if you were granted an absence but failed to complete the make-up work, you will earn attendance points but not class performance points. Other the other hand, ***u******nexcused absences do not permit make-up work***.

***Routine Tasks***: Daily practice is crucial for successful Chinese learning. *To enhance proficiency-based language learning**, assignments for this course are distributed throughout the week,*** necessitating 20-30 minutes of practice every day. Be sure to review the tasks and due dates for each new week on LMS. There are ***THREE (3) deadlines*** *for the weekly routine tasks*: (1) Before Tuesday and Friday classes, and before Sunday at midnight.

Students are required to thoroughly review the assignments and their respective due dates for the week, which are posted LMS in advance. Any submissions received after the expected time will incur a deduction of 10% of the total possible points for every 24-hour period that elapses. *If you do submit your **homework late**, please notify the instructor accordingly.*

***Projects***: Throughout the semester, this course will include multiple projects that necessitate *self-regulation for effective planning and monitoring of completion*. For additional information, please refer to the "Project Instruction and Rubric" folder located in the LMS Course Documents.

***Quizzes & Exams***: This course consists of two exams and multiple unit review tests. Each exam comprises of a written and oral part. The unit tests serve as comprehensive practice for the course material. To prepare for the exams, refer to the exam study guides before the exams. **\*\*\* Note**: If you need **extra time on exams** or **any other accommodations**, please provide me with **a memo issued by Disability Services for Students (DSS) at the start of the semester**.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**P****olicy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism in this course**

It is VERY EASY to spot cheating in a foreign language course. Students are not permitted to turn in work done by someone else. Getting assistance from a native speaker on assignments is OK, but your final product must be your own work as it is easy to identify the excessive structures beyond your level of proficiency. Besides, the use of a WEB TRANSLATE (e.g., Google Translate) for translation other than vocabulary or phrase is not permitted.

The first time I suspect cheating I warn the student and tell why I suspect (e.g. use of non-course grammar or vocabulary) and I take off 50% points of the grade. If it's very obvious I do not give a warning. I will just give a zero. If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of F for the course or be reported to the Student's Office.
