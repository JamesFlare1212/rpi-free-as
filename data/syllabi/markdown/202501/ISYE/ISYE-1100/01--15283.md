## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

An introduction to industrial and systems engineering (ISE). Major elements of the ISE disciplines are overviewed in the context of operations engineering problems. Topics include deterministic and stochastic applications of operations research methods, soft computing, applications of probability and statistics, engineering economics, discrete event simulation, and decision analysis.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Regular class periods are devoted primarily to guest lectures introducing the power of industrial and systems engineering in a wide range of topics and industries. Some class periods will be devoted to lectures, discussion, and in-class exercises for solving operations engineering and ISE design problems. No textbook is required.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course is expected to contribute to the following learning outcome: (3.1) an ability to identify, formulate, and solve complex engineering problems by applying principles of engineering, science, and mathematics.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

This class is graded on a Pass/Fail basis. To pass the class, students must earn 11 attendance points by either attending the lectures in person and responding to the corresponding reflection quizzes on LMS or by submitting a 300-word reflection response after watching the lecture recording. Students can attend at least 9 lectures and at least 2 outside lectures pre-approved by the instructor to earn attendance points.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

This class is graded on a Satisfactory/Unsatisfactory (S/U), or Pass/Fail, basis.

1. Attending at least 11 live class sessions and responding to the corresponding reflection quizzes on LMS by the specified deadlines.
   - This is the preferred way of earning attendance points.
   - If you are unable to attend class and inform the professor of your absence, you may receive attendance points by watching the recorded lecture videos and submitting via LMS a 300-word reflection response on the presentation. The response should describe what the presentation was about, at least one piece of information that you found useful or new, and at least one question you would have liked to ask the speaker that was not asked.
2. Attending at least 9 live class sessions (and responding to the corresponding reflection quizzes on LMS by the specified deadlines) and attending at least 2 outside lectures (and submitting a reflection response on each outside lecture by the specified deadlines).
   - The outside lectures need to be pre-approved by the instructor and require students to provide to the instructor (1) a piece of evidence of attendance (e.g., a selfie at the event or sign-up information) and (2) a 300-word response on the topic of the seminar, and how Industrial and Systems Engineering could impact or inform the seminar topic.

Students can make inquiries directly to the course instructor at any point during the semester to determine if they are in danger of failing.
