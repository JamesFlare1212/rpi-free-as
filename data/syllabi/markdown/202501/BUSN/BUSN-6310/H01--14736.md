## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

In this course, students use financial analysis to evaluate organizational performance. Students calculate and evaluate financial reports and ratios, drawing recommendations for improving performance from their intemporal and cross-competition analysis. Students use optimization and simulation techniques to model the impact of changes on the organization’s financial performance. Students develop budgets for new products and ventures using sound principles and techniques, evaluating capital sourcing options and costs.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Influence and lead individuals and teams to achieve organizational objectives.
- Use data to evaluate and respond, in context, to a problem, decision, or research question.
- Diagnose, prescribe, plan and execute change (product and process) to ensure the organization achieves its objectives.
- Design a strategic planning process, formulate a strategy, and implement a strategy to achieve sustainable competitive advantage for a firm.
- Analyze and use accounting, financial, and operational information to measure and influence organizational performance.
- Deploy continuous improvement and quality management on an organization’s production and distribution systems and/or processes.
- Identify and evaluate markets for the organization's goods and services, develop strategies for achieving organizational growth rates, and implement successful campaigns to specific markets.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

### Evaluation Criteria

This course uses a series of projects that serve as the context in which students learn and then demonstrate that learning. Specifically, this course has five major projects:

1. **Project 1 –** In this project, you are going to get a quick primer into how the financial aspects and offices of your organization work, what legal/ethical responsibilities you have as an Executive of the organization, how capital markets work, and how your organization accesses them, and even a look at what the Board of your organization does…as an Executive you will be presenting to them often, so it is important to be ready. After this, you will be ready to launch into evaluating strategy.
2. **Project 2 –** As the Chief Strategy Officer, one of the ways you will come up with specific strategies is by deeply evaluating how the organization is currently functioning, given the underlying mission/vision. In this project, you will use a series of financial ratios and metrics to formulate potential areas for change or investment--in other words, for strategic focuses. Once you’ve evaluated your organization, you’ll then use the same methods to evaluate your closest competitor as a way to determine how your ratios/metrics look relative to others in the market. You will use root-cause analysis to pinpoint why we may be under or out-performing our competitors…and from these results, you will identify 2-3 very specific strategies that can be undertaken to improve the organization.
3. **Project 3 –** In this project, you will evaluate two of the recommended initiatives you outlined in project 2 and you will build a “pro forma” financial model for each to determine whether or not making that change would have positive financial results. You’ll develop a financial analysis that shows both the estimated expenses and revenues, making assumptions to build your model. Finally, you will calculate ROI/NPV to bring the results into “today’s dollars” and then make a recommendation about undertaking the initiative.
4. **Project 4 –** In the previous project, you made several assumptions as we always do in developing financials. However, we inherently build risk into our financial models every time we bring an assumption into the model (which we always must do since we are trying to model something that will happen in the future). You will learn to use simulation as a way to evaluate the risk around your assumptions - - and you will determine a “more likely” outcome given the various risks around the assumptions. Then, you will isolate one of your variables in the model and use past data to forecast forward as to how that variable may change in the future.
5. **Project 5 –** In your final project, you will choose a different path from that you recommended in projects 3 and 4. Let’s let this be a surprise for now, but suffice it to say that we are going to shift things in a different direction!
