## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Discussion of current issues relevant to the origins of life in astrophysics, biology, chemistry, and earth sciences.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

**Required Course Text:** *Astrobiology: A Very Short Introduction,* 1st Edition (2014) by David Catling (required). This book is a very short, simple read to get everyone caught up on the basics of astrobiology. It is currently available online for <$15. Everyone in the class is ***required*** to have read this book by the beginning of the second class meeting and be prepared to discuss.

**Other suggested reading material (on reserve in the library or on the LMS site)**

- *Origins of Life A Cosmic Perspective* (2017) by Douglas Whittet
- *The Astrobiology Primer v2.0.* Domagal-Goldman, SD and KE Wright, eds. 2016. Astrobiology 16(8) 561-653.
- *2015 Astrobiology Strategy.* Lindsay Hays, ed. Published by NASA. Download here

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Course Goals / Objectives** This is an ***interactive*** readings and seminar course in which we will discuss current topics in astrobiology and the origins of life through the view of earth sciences, chemistry, biology, and astrophysics. Each week will consist either of a guest speaker or discussion of papers related to a previous speaker. Students are required to come prepared to seminar having read assigned papers, and be ready to fully participate in Q&A and discussion. The goal of the course is to provide broad exposure to current topics in Astrobiology, and hone reading, discussion, and synthesis skills.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Course Logistics**

*Course Structure:* Each course meeting will consist either of a guest speaker presentation followed by Q&A, or by a general class discussion that focuses on a particular topic and several associated peer-reviewed journal articles.

*Course Meetings:* Class meetings will be held in JRSC 1W18 (RARE suite). A**ttendance and participation are mandatory** and will be included in assessment measures.

*Learning Management System/Blackboard Learn and WebEx:* This course is primarily conducted in person during class meeting times. The course schedule, reading and handout materials, and any assignments or reading material will be distributed primarily in class, and secondarily via LMS and WebEx. We will have several guest speakers this semester who will be joining us remotely. They will present their talks and stay for Q&A via WebEx. Students on the RPI campus are expected to join the class in person

*In-class discussion*

Proper and effective scientific discourse requires clear communication of ideas followed by open discussion amongst many scientists. This is especially true for multidisciplinary areas of science, like astrobiology, where people have different specializations and perspectives. It is important to acknowledge the broad perspectives and expertise of those involved. Progress in multidisciplinary sciences is made by sharing knowledge and experience through asking and answering questions in diverse groups (like this class). Students should feel free to ask questions throughout in-class discussions even though the answer might not be known. In class discussions should also focus on exploring new research questions and approaches.

Everyone in the class is expected to read any articles provided and to participate in the discussions. Student’s involvement will be evaluated by the instructors each week. Note that discussion participation must be meaningful to count toward your grade, and that vague or uninformed comments (*e.g. “I didn’t understand the paper”*) will not suffice.

***NOTE: If it becomes apparent that students are not preparing for class (e.g. not reading the literature), graded written assignments will be added to the course requirements.***

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Synthesize and assess oral presentations and participate in oral discourse
- 2\. Critically read and evaluate scientific literature from multiple disciplines
- 3\. Effectively communicate and discuss scientific experiments and outcomes
- 4\. Recognize gaps in our current understanding of Astrobiology and the origins of life

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

This course is designed to be a readings, seminar, and discussion course in which students develop the skills to read, understand, and discuss the scientific literature as well as emerging scientific results presented orally. Students enrolled in ISCI 4510 will be responsible for (1) regular and meaningful participation in each class period (2) responding briefly in LMS to assess seminar presentations. As such, student participation is a critical part of achieving these outcomes. Students are expected to read articles ***prior*** to each class meeting, and come prepared to discuss the material. Therefore, active participation and attendance comprise more than half of the student’s final grade in the course. Instructors reserve the right to introduce graded assignments if students do not engage in discussion or come to class unprepared.

- In-class discussion/involvement (60%); Attendance (40%)
