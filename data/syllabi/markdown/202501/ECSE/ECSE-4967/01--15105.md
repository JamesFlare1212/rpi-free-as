## Course Goals

### Goals:

As technologies based on quantum phenomena continue to proliferate, an understanding of the underlying physics of how these systems operate is critical for work in those fields. This course will begin by providing a foundation in the basic theory of quantum mechanics, such as the interpretation of wave functions, solutions to the Schrodinger equation for simple potentials, and formalism (i.e. Hilbert spaces, Hermitian operators and observables). The course will then focus on applying those fundamentals to develop tools for finding solutions to realistic quantum systems and to understand the physical models behind modern nanoscale technologies such as quantum tunneling-based devices, quantum dots and nanowires, superconductors, and quantum computing hardware (qubits, gates, circuits).

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Explain the physical consequences of the probabilistic nature of quantum physics and how that differs from the deterministic nature of our everyday experiences with classical physics
- Solve the Schrodinger equation for simple potentials and interpret the solutions both in terms of the formalism of quantum mechanics and semi-classical arguments
- Apply basic quantum mechanical theory and approximation methods to model and solve realistic quantum electronic systems
- Describe ways in which qubits, quantum gates, and measurements on them can be physically implemented and discuss their advantages and disadvantages

*10 Homework Assignments* – submission through Gradescope

Quantum mechanics is best learned through practice and homework assignments are correspondingly a central part of the learning process for this course. Homework problems will emphasize developing a deeper conceptual understanding of quantum mechanics, solving problems closely related to the lecture material, and applying tools from the lecture material to model and solve realistic quantum systems. Homework will generally be assigned and due weekly, except during exam weeks. Homework assignments that are submitted late are subject to a penalty of 10% of the total possible points for the assignment per day late. The final grade for homework assignments will consist of the average of the highest eight homework scores.

*25 Post-Lecture Exercises*

After each lecture, students will be assigned a few short questions or exercises to complete based on that lecture’s material. These exercises will help solidify students’ understanding of the new concepts by having them apply the fundamentals of the lecture material to basic problems. Each set of exercises is due by the beginning of the following lecture.

*2 Midterm Exams*

Two midterm exams will assess students’ learning of the material in the first two units of the course. Exams will evaluate both conceptual understanding and the ability to mathematically solve problems from the course material. The exams are independent, closed-book assessments held during the usual lecture time and space. During the exam, students will be allowed a crib sheet that is provided by the instructor. A student may leave the exam session to use the restroom, but must leave any communicating devices behind. Breaching the course’s exam policy will be treated as a violation of the academic integrity code.

Students are required to communicate with the instructor prior to missing a scheduled assignment or exam. Official requests for alternate exam times and/or additional time to complete exams will be accommodated if received two weeks before the scheduled exam time. In cases in which arrangements for a makeup exam cannot be made within a reasonable timeframe, the instructor and student will discuss alternatives.

*Research Paper*

As a capstone assignment for the course, students will write a research paper about a chosen qubit technology that is outside the scope of the lecture material. In the research paper, the students will give a high-level overview of the qubit technology; explain how qubits of this type are physically realized; mathematically analyze the physical model of the qubit, gate operations, and measurement processes; simulate a simple quantum circuit using the physical model of the qubit; and discuss the outlook for the qubit technology in the near future.

*Class Participation & Attendance*

Ten percent of the students’ grade will be based on participation and attendance. This will include attendance and active participation during class. This assessment will be based on the observations of the course staff and completing in-class questions and assignments.

### Other Course Policies:

**Policy on Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) Tools**

Artificial intelligence (AI) tools can be useful in helping you gain different perspectives on the course’s content by providing information that supplements what is already provided as part of the course materials. The general policy for the use of AI tools in this course is that AI tools *may* be used for gathering information and locating new sources of information, but *may not* be used to generate any content that is submitted by a student as part of an assignment. Every element of all assignments you submit for this course must be fully prepared by you, and consequently, the use of generative AI tools for any part of a work that you submit will be treated as plagiarism. If you have questions, please contact me.

For use of AI tools in gathering information (for example, in the final research report), the following policies apply:

- When using AI tools to source information for an assignment, students must transparently state which tools they have used and which information has been obtained from these tools.
- All information originally sourced by AI tools must be fact-checked and accurate. In each case, you will need to provide at least one independent source for the material you have obtained from an AI tool.

**Academic Integrity**

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

Intellectual integrity and credibility are the foundation of all academic work. A violation of Academic Integrity policy is, by definition, considered a flagrant offense to the educational process. It is taken seriously by students, faculty, and Rensselaer and will be addressed in an effective manner. If found responsible for committing academic dishonesty, a student may be subject to one or both types of penalties: an academic (grade) penalty administered by the professor and/or disciplinary action through the Rensselaer judicial process described in this handbook. Three relevant academic integrity violations to emphasize include:

Collaboration: Collaboration is defined as deliberately facilitating an act of academic dishonesty in any way or form; for example, allowing another student to observe an exam paper or allowing another student to “recycle” one’s old term paper or using one another’s work in a paper or lab report without citing it as another’s work.

Copying: Copying is defined as obtaining information pertaining to a graded exercise by deliberately observing the paper of another student; for example, noting which alternative a neighboring student has circled on a multiple-choice exam.

Plagiarism: Plagiarism is defined as representing the work or words of another as one’s own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference. Examples include using sentences verbatim from a published source in a term paper without appropriate referencing, or presenting as one’s own the detailed argument of a published source, or presenting as one’s own electronically or digitally enhanced graphic representations from any form of media.
