## Course Goals

### Goals:

The nature of optics, physics, and operation principles of optoelectronic devices and systems for communication, display, lighting, and computing. Topics include optics fundamentals, optical cavity, waveguides and fibers, light emitting diodes, laser diodes, photodetectors, solar cells, liquid crystal, birefringent materials, properties and devices, modulators and optical display. Several complex optoelectronic systems will be introduced such as light detection and ranging (LiDAR) systems, photonics based neuromorphic computing and quantum computing.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

No Course Learning Outcomes are available for this course.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1. Explain the fundamentals of wave confinement and propagation in passive photonic devices such as waveguides and optical fibers, and determine propagation constant, optical cavity Q-factor, free-spectrum range and dispersion, etc. (Homework assignments 1-4 and quiz 1-4)
2. Analyze and evaluate the performance of these devices based on the working principles of light emitting diodes, laser diodes, photodetectors and optical modulators. (Homework assignments 5-8 and quiz 5-8)
3. Apply the physics of optical polarization, ordinary and extraordinary waves in birefringent materials to determine the polarization of optical systems and calculate phase retardation plate thickness. (homework, lecture and quizzes)
4. Analyze the semiconductor material properties for optoelectronic devices such as energy band diagram of type I and II heterogeneous junctions, quantitatively determining the energy levels in a quantum well, noise analysis for photodetectors. (homework and quizzes)
5. Calculate optical power transmission in birefringent materials, and analyze amplitude and phase modulators (homework and quizzes)
6. Demonstrate both written and oral communication skills and in-depth knowledge in one particular technical area (term paper and presentation)

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Homework:** There will be 6-8 homework problems assigned in accordance with the quizzes. These problems can be done individually or in a group, but each individual must submit their own homework. Please note that homework grading is based on effort only. In general, **no** late assignments will be accepted. However, extensions may be requested **in writing** **prior to the assignment due date** and evaluated on a case-by-case basis.

**Term paper:** Course term paper will be performed individually and involve choosing a research topic to investigate in depth. Students may choose from a list of recommended topics or propose own topics, subject to instructor approval. Term paper should involve a detailed analysis of the chosen topic, computation validation, survey of relevant technology and state-of-the-art report. Term paper is not required for ECSE 6962 students while it serves for bonus points for ECSE 4962 students.

**In-class quiz:** There will be 8 in-class quizzes with each of 20-25 minutes in length. The quiz is **open book and open lecture notes**. No computer, cell phone, internet access, smart watches are allowed in these quizzes. Students must work independently on these quizzes. The lowest grade quiz will be dropped in final grade calculation.

**Term-paper presentation:** A full-length term paper is not required; however, a one-page summary must be submitted for the presentation. The presentation will be 20 minutes in total, consisting of 15 minutes for the presentation itself and 5 minutes for Q&A.
