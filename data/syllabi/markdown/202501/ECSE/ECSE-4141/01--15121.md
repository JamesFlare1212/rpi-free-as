## Course Goals

### Goals:

A cross-listed senior/graduate course that introduces students to electric power generation from renewable sources and its integration into the power grid. Renewable energy is essential for a decarbonized power sector. With the commitment by many governments to achieving carbon-neutrality by 2050, renewables will replace most thermal power plants in the next 20 years. Developing such a new power system is a grand challenge for electrical engineers. This course teaches the fundamentals that students need in order to tackle this challenge,

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Fundamentals of photovoltaic and wind energy; principles and control of solar and wind power plants; as well as operation and control of power systems with renewables.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students successfully completed the course will be able to: 1) explain the principles of power generation from PV and wind turbines, 2) design main control functions and characterize the performance of PV inverters and PV power plants, 3) design main control functions and charac-terize the performance of different types of wind turbines, and 4) quantify the effects of renew-able generation on power system operation and control

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students successfully completed ECSE 4141 will be able to: 1) explain the principles of power generation from PV and wind turbines, 2) analyze the principle operation of PV inverters and PV power plants, 3) analyze the principle operation of different types of wind turbines, and 4) classify the effects of renewable generation on power system operation and control. Students successfully completed ECSE 6141 will be able to: 1) explain the principles of power generation from PV and wind turbines, 2) design main control functions and characterize the performance of PV inverters and PV power plants, 3) design main control functions and characterize the performance of different types of wind turbines, and 4) quantify the effects of renewable generation on power system operation and control.
