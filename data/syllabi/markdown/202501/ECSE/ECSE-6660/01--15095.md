## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course provides an in-depth study of the technologies and protocols involved in building the Internet-of-Things (IoT), with specific focus on networking at the edge of the Internet. This includes understanding of wireless communication and link layer technologies, multi-access and scheduling mechanisms, mobility models, routing in disconnected networks, energy-efficient edge networking, loss tolerant transport protocols, and their applications to emerging areas such as vehicular networks, RFID systems and smart buildings. The course also discussed IoT Security and data/content distribution, aggregation, and compression. This course has a strong emphasis on **hands-on experience** utilizing Raspberry Pi’s, Arduino’s, and NI software radio boards, and a significant part of the course assessment will be based on a **final project** focused on building a wireless based application such as indoor localization for IoT devices. Students are encouraged to suggest their own individual projects.

Topics to be covered include

1. Review of networking architectures and standards
2. Review of wireless PHY layer technologies
3. Wireless access technologies for IoT: WiFi, LTE, ZigBee, Z-Wave and BLE
4. Routing and network layer protocols for IoT: RPL and 6LoWPAN
5. Low-overhead IoT transport: CoAP and MQTT
6. Network management for IoT: LWM2M and NETCONF
7. IoT mobility models and mobility handling protocols
8. IoT Security
9. Energy efficient IoT networking
10. Sensor data aggregation and analytics
11. Applications to vehicular networking, environmental monitoring, healthcare, smart buildings etc.

### Other Course Policies:

**Final Project:**

The project provides an opportunity for the student to immerse themselves in the design and application of IoT in their favorite area of interest. To favor creativity, critical thinking, brainstorming, and team organization, each project will typically involve ***up to 2 students*** *(doing the project individually is fine, but if you want to do the project in a group of 3 or more students, you need to provide a strong justification why so many members are needed)*. The final project report will be submitted at the end of the semester, together with a presentation, and a recorded demo. Any software code will need to be uploaded with appropriate documentation in order for the instructor to check by re-generating the results. More details about the project will be shared throughout the class.

**Important:** Students cannot use a previously developed product, whether by them in another class, or through a third party, towards getting credit for the final project. Any open-source or third-party software needs to be explicitly acknowledged and will not count towards credit. Failing to acknowledge such open/third-party software will be considered plagiarism and will be handled according to our academic dishonesty policy (see below).

**Quiz/Homework/Lab Assignments:**

- Per-class Quizzes will be assigned on LMS before every lecture (Lecture 2 onwards) and will be constitute of simple questions that will require you to pay attention to the material discussed in class. They will be due the end of day (11:59pm on the same day).
- Homework/Lab assignments will be on Piazza. Students need to submit their solutions on Gradescope by the deadline. These assignments will be posted roughly on a weekly basis, with homeworks and lab assignments due in alternate weeks.
- Late assignment solutions will not be accepted. In case of satisfactory excuse, grade will be assigned to average of other homework/lab assignments, respectively.
- All assignments are to be completed on your own. You are allowed to consult with other students in the current class or the instructor (e.g. through Piazza) regarding the conceptualization of the problem and possible methods of solution, but you may not share details or your final answer with other students. You may not copy solutions from anyone or any source. Failure to follow the rules will be considered violation of academic integrity.

You may not knowingly provide your work to be copied. Doing so will also be considered violation of academic integrity as well
