## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Jon Kabat-Zinn said that “Mindfulness means paying attention in a particular way: on purpose, in the present moment, and nonjudgmentally.” Throughout the day we tend to spend much of our time thinking about the past and the future. Mindfulness involves giving particular attention to our moment-to-moment experiences in a way that emphasizes observing, rather than judging or evaluating. Everyday activities such as driving a car or eating a meal provide us the opportunity to practice this focused, moment-to-moment attention. In addition, we can practice mindfulness through formal meditation. There is empirical evidence of the many health benefits of mindfulness meditation, including the potential to produce fundamental changes in brain structure and epigenetic change. In this class, we will discuss the historical and psychological foundations of mindfulness and empirical findings supporting its positive effects. Students will participate in structured exercises. This course is a very hands-on approach to mindfulness…you will be required to practice mindfulness and meditation and develop your mindful skills. It is also more of a discussion course.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Students will learn what mindfulness is and will practice mindfulness daily.
- Students will apply mindfulness to activities such as eating, exercising, driving a car, and interacting with others.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1. **Maya Angelou’s *Letter to My Daughter* (10 pts) March 11th**

- Paper due prior to class.
- Prepare an (informal) oral overview of 5 different essays from the book (probably the same as what you wrote about in your paper). You will present on one of them in class. Class discussion.
- Write a 3 page paper highlighting how her book affected you and how it changed (or reinforced, or didn’t change) how you think about life.

2. **Review of Holly Roger’s *The Mindful Twenty Something* book (20 pts)**

Write 4 papers that are 1-2 page double-spaced self-reflection and participate in class discussion. Highlight in your essay how her book affected you and how it changed (or reinforced, or didn’t change) how you think about life. ***Reflection is due before class begins***. Clarification: You will have a total of 4 papers, each worth 5 points. Submit on LMS. Note: this is **NOT** a summary of the readings, but a self-reflection.

3. **Koru App Daily Log (Mindfulness Institute for Emerging Adults) 20pts**

There are daily log opportunities in the app. Make sure you complete 20 days of reflections for 4 weeks during the time we will be discussing this book. Complete 2-3 things you are grateful for each day, and then reflect on the meditation you did that day. Questions you could answer: Which meditation did you do? How did you feel before and after the meditation? How did the meditation impact you?

This assignment is completed in the Koru app.

4. **Big Panda and Tiny Dragon, 10 points (written paper and present in class) Paper due the day of your sign up presentation**

*The Journey: Big Panda and Tiny Dragon.*

Write a 2-page double-spaced paper. Choose a few passages that resonate with you in some way. Explain how the passages impacted you and how they made you think about your own life journey.

We will have signups for class presentations (about a minute).

For example: on p. 62 “*I’m worried,”* said Tiny Dragon. “*I don’t know what to do next.” “For just a moment*,” said Big Panda, “*stop, breathe, and listen to the wind in the bamboo.”* This is such an inspiring way to remind me to be in the moment, especially when things are so uncertain. Yesterday I was asked a challenging question and I wasn’t really sure how to answer at first, so I stopped and took a breath to collect my thoughts, and then when I had my answer, I spoke. Listening to the bamboo is a way to clear our minds of the thing we are worried about so we can think of a way to move forward.

5. **Thich Nhat Hanh Presentation, (10 points) Jan 28th**

Class time: research quotes/meditation poems by Thich Nhat Hanh (or someone similar) and choose 2 quotes that resonate with you. Present a short 1-2 minute presentation of what those quotes mean to you, or why they resonate with you. Create a PowerPoint slide with Introduction (your name), and a slide for each quote. You can be creative and add graphics, pictures, and aesthetic creativity to your slides. We will present the same day.

6. **Mindfulness Applications in Real World, 10 pts (Present Tues Feb 21st)**

Choose a partner to work with. Class time will be spent researching an example of a mindfulness study implemented in either an academic, business, military, or medical area. You will prepare a 5min presentation of a summary of what was implemented and why, and the outcomes of the research (i.e., the mindfulness intervention impacted…)

You and your partner must both speak in the presentation.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.
