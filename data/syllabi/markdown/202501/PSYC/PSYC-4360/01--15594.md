## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students who successfully complete this course will be able to identify and evaluate influential experiments, experimental design, and methods used in the Behavioral Neuroscience field.
- Students who successfully complete this course will be able to describe the basic functions of nerve cells, that neurons communicate by electrical and chemical means, and general psychopharmacology principles.
- Students who successfully complete this course will be able to identify brain targets and mechanisms for sensation/perception, movement, and other complex behaviors (ingestion, reproduction, memory, emotion, language, attention, consciousness).
- Students who successfully complete this course will be able to explain how the mammalian nervous system develops and changes throughout the lifespan.
- Students who successfully complete this course will be able to describe the current understanding of the mechanisms of brain health and disorders and their treatment.
- Students who successfully complete this course will be able to apply their understanding of Behavioral Neuroscience to analyze and interpret data collected in class and described in readings and communicate their perspectives.

***Brief descriptions of assessment measures (details on LMS, will be discussed in class)***

**Attendance and participation:** Attendance and active engagement in class is expected and students can earn points for both participation (points vary per class) and attendance (4 points per class) in each class. Participation includes working in groups or alone on activities during class time, written reflections, and contributing to class discussions.

**Methods assignment:** This is a required assignment that will be completed outside of class and consists of steps completed on LMS/google.

**Group project:** Students will work in groups to analyze data and present their findings at the end of the semester. For this presentation, student groups will be given data on emotional or cognitive measures of human subjects to analyze and interpret and present to class. The presentation and reflection on the process are required and due on our last day of class. Project plans, two progress reports, and presentation drafts are optional and have specific due dates throughout the semester; these must be turned in on time to earn points.

**Journal Article Readings and Critiques:** We will read and discuss 3 journal articles throughout the semester. Students are expected to read the papers before class, submit responses on LMS before class, and contribute to in-class discussion for participation points. Students can write a critique of the journal articles. These should be brief (1 page, single-spaced) and include a summary of the paper and student’s thoughts about the paper.

**Papers:** Students can complete short papers on specific topics (one per module).

**Short answer questions:** Students can answer short-answer questions based upon lectures and textbook readings (one per modules 1 and 2, two for module 3).

**Quizzes (1-15):** Each chapter will have a quiz available to take. Quizzes will be timed and consist of multiple-choice and true/false questions.

**Exams 1, 2 and 3:** There will be three exams available during the semester consisting of multiple-choice, short answer, and true or false questions on the material for each module. Questions will be related to materials (e.g. lectures, readings, exercises). Exams will be timed and on LMS.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grading is not competitive and students are encouraged to study and discuss materials together. However, unless explicitly stated otherwise (e.g. group project), any work turned in must be the student’s own original work completed for this class. Students’ final course grades will be based on their performance on the assessments in this course (described below). Grades will be posted in the Blackboard LMS gradebook regularly and students will be able to assess their standing in class throughout the semester by checking this gradebook. If you ever have any questions regarding your grades, please ask me. Please note that grades on assessments and final course grades are non-negotiable and will be based upon the same metrics described in the syllabus for each and every student. Any appeals about final grades (e.g. due to mistake in input/calculations, deviation from syllabus) must be brought to my attention through email before the last day of finals.

This course uses an additive grading system where there are several opportunities throughout the semester for you to earn points. The points earned translate to your final course grade. This grading system is an example of what is called a gameful approach in teaching. Like a game, there are goals and different challenges that you can complete to reach those goals. One goal you may have is to earn a certain letter grade in the course. Goals for all of us are the learning outcomes described above. You will demonstrate that you have achieved these learning outcome goals by completing assessments associated with them. Your performance on these assessments, as well as points earned for attending and participating in class, will translate to points earned on your journey towards these goals.

Everyone begins with 0 points. There will be 1435 total points that can be earned this semester. As you accomplish assessments in the class, you build up points towards your final grade. There are two required tasks* and readings you are expected to do without directly earning points (e.g. textbook readings, journal article readings), but the majority of the assignments in the class are on a self-selected basis and can take many forms. Some are collaborative, some are individual, some are completed in class, and some are completed outside of class. It is up to you to figure out which of the available assessments you will take on in order to build up enough points to get the grade you want by the end of the semester.

You can earn attendance and participation points by being here and engaged for the entire class and completing participation challenges during class time. If you are not in class or do not complete the participation challenges, then you cannot earn these points.

Our course is organized into three modules. There are two required assessments*, but all of the other points from assessments that you can earn are your choice. In this way, you self-select what challenges you will take on towards your goals in this course. For some assessments, including the required ones, there are specific due dates. Most other assessments have due dates at the end of Modules 1, 2, and 3, which serve as check points in our course this semester. Assessments must be turned in on time and no extensions will be given, but you will be able to choose the pace at which you complete many of these (see table below).

**Why are we doing it this way?** I have studied the art and science of teaching for many years, including several trainings in the last two years on gameful learning approaches. I want to foster engagement in our class for individuals working on their own and in groups to support students’ learning of the material and reaching their goals. I want you to take an active part in this. I know it can be stressful to juggle many classes and other activities so I think this approach will allow needed flexibility, agency, and choice while maintaining high standards as well as cultivate intrinsic motivation, which has stronger and more long term effects for learning than relying solely on extrinsic motivation.

****There are only two required assignments in this course****: The Methods Assignment in the beginning of the semester and the Group Project Presentation at the end of the semester. Although these are separate assignments, they are related in that what you complete in the Methods Assignment on your own leads to the Group Project Presentation that is completed working with your classmates in your group. You will need to do more than just these required assignments to earn a passing grade in the course. These assignments will not be accepted late. **If required assignments are not turned in on time, you will not be able to earn the points on these assignments and your final course grade will be lowered by a letter grade for each one missed.** For example, if you earned enough points for an A, but then do not turn in the Methods assignment, you would receive a B. If you do not do both of these assignments and have earned enough points for an A, then you would receive a C.*

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism** All exams, quizzes, and assignments (except the group project) that are turned in for a grade must be the student’s own original work for this class only. In cases where help was received (does not apply to quizzes and exams), a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration (e.g. if someone else helped with editing or provided feedback). Work composed in whole or in part by artificial intelligence (e.g. ChatGPT) will be considered cheating. Plagiarizing, as defined by Webster’s, is “to steal and pass off (the ideas or words of another) as one’s own” or use of another’s material without crediting the source. Submission of any assignment, quiz, or exam that is in violation of this policy will result in penalties as described above. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
