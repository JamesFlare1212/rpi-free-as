## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is an upper-level studio-seminar on games and care: examining how care is represented in games, making games that incorporate care, making games with care, playing games with care, and thinking about games in relation to our individual and social structures of care. Weekly readings, screening, and example games explore philosophical, ethical, political, artistic and playful aspects of care. Students create individual and group game projects. Graduate students write short papers in conjunction with the projects.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The goal of this course is to gain understanding and insight into concepts of care, study games in this context, and apply concepts of care to design and creation of original games.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- \- Describe, discuss, and compare theories of care - Analyze, reflect on, and critique representations and mechanics of care in games - Make games that incorporate concepts of care
- Graduate students registered for the 6000 level section of this course will be able to: - Synthesize understanding of theories of care in relation to games through written assignments

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

This is a studio-seminar: a reading, discussion, and making class. There are 4 projects, which make up the majority of the overall grade. Presenting and participating in critiques is a required part of each project. Students are also expected to come to class prepared for discussion and for studio work, meaning you have done the reading ahead of time and are prepared to talk about it, and have brought your laptop and/or other needed tools and materials for working on your project during studio time.

Graduate students will have 4 short papers assigned, related to each of the projects and to the readings.
