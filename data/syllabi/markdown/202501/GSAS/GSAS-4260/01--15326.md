## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Utilize key terms in game narrative design
- Reverse engineer ludo-narrative systems
- Design ludo-narrative systems
- Develop creative practice in community

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

## Weekly Log

Each week you will earn points by logging ongoing tasks using a form in the LMS.

*Play**ing*

- You will earn points for solo and group play time outside of class
- Solo play time will range from 1-2 hours per week
- Groups will meet for 1-2 sessions, totaling approximately 2-3 hours

*Supporting*

- You will earn points for giving and receiving feedback, camaraderie, and insight

- You will choose from a provided list of ways to earn points

*Attending*

- You will earn full points for attending at least 22 of the 27 class sessions
- You are expected not to attend if you have symptoms of illness

*Submitting*

- You will earn full points for submitting at least 90% of assignments on time
- Most weeks this will include completing a reading in advance of class

## Designer’s Notebook

- You will keep a composition notebook that compiles your notes, ideas, and artistic process
- Entries will include reflections on readings and games, design ideas, and in-class exercises
- A random sample of notebooks will be collected for feedback most weeks

## Prologue Exam

- You will take a test (Prologue Exam) in class at the end of the second week (1/16)
- The test will cover key terms and concepts for narrative design
- The test will include multiple choice, fill-in-the-blank, and short answer questions

## Reverse Engineering Project (REP)

- You will analyze a chosen game’s narrative design

## Story Game Project (SGP)

- You will develop a short-form analog narrative game

## Outside Community Project (OCP)

- You will complete a short practical project that contributes to a community outside our class
- You will choose the community and contribution with guidance from the class
- You can collaborate with others in the class for this contribution

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
