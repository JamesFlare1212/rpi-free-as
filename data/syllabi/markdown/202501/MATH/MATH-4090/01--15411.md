## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course provides an opportunity for the development of theorem-proving skills in the field of mathematical analysis. Expansion of a knowledge base comes as a by-product of energy expended in theorem proving and subsequent exposition. Analysis topics included are: sets, functions, the real numbers, cardinality, induction, decimal representations of real numbers, Euclidean spaces, abstract vector spaces, and metric spaces. This is a communication-intensive course.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

**Textbook:** Analysis With and Introduction to Proof, Sixth ed., by Steven R. Lay, Pearson, 2023. Access to the textbook — physical or electronic — is **required.**

## Course Content

### Content Details:

We will study sets, functions, cardinality, properties of real numbers, convergence of sequences, limits of functions and metric spaces.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to read mathematical proofs and verbally communicate how each sentence contributes to deducing the assertion.

- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to write high quality mathematical proofs.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is **recommended** for all meetings of the course. Attendance is not taken in lecture but students are responsible for all content covered during lecture and quizzes are given most weeks. Attendance is taken Worksheet days as part of the worksheet participation grade for Worksheets which are uploaded to Gradescope. Worksheet points are based on a combination of attendance/participation and attempted completion points and are a part of the overall course grade.

### Other Course Policies:

**Exams:** The material covered on Exams will be announced on LMS when the date of the exam draws near. Exams will be given during lecture, barring Institute restrictions. Exams will include some combination of definitions, true/false, multiple select, multiple choice, short answer problems and proofs. Exams will be closed book and closed notes and will be timed.

Make-up exams without penalty will only be given to students with an [excused absence documented by the Student Success office](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/student-success/excused-absence) or students who make arrangements with the instructor at least 24 hours prior to the start of the exam. **Missing an exam should not be taken lightly.** Thus, students missing an exam due to an excused absence should notify the instructor to make arrangements for a make-up exam ***before the exam begins***. If a student with an excused absence fails to inform the instructor within 24 hours of the exam, it is at the discretion of the instructor to allow the student to take a make-up exam with possibility of a penalty applied. If a student misses an exam due to an unexcused absence (e.g., did not know there was an exam, feeling ill the day of the exam, overslept, etc.), then the student must inform the instructor within 24 hours of the exam. The student may elect to take a make-up exam at a time arranged by the instructor and a 30% penalty will be applied to the score.

**Quizzes:** Quizzes will be 20 minutes in length and will be given on Fridays during lecture. Quizzes will be over material covered the prior week in lecture and are closely tied to the worksheet of the week. They will typically consist of 3-4 questions including definitions, multiple choice/multiple select, short answer and one proof. No make-up quizzes will be given for any reason. Students who miss a quiz due to an prescheduled absence and at least 24 hours prior to the quiz provide the instructor with documentation as listed by the [Student Success office](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/student-success/excused-absence) will be excused from a quiz. If a student misses a quiz for an unexcused absence or neglects to inform the instructor of a prescheduled absence with sufficient notice, the student will receive a 0 on the quiz. To compensate for this, 8 of the 10 quiz scores (up to two (2) quiz scores will be dropped) will be used when calculating the overall quiz grade at the end of the semester. If a student misses 3 or more quizzes - excused or unexcused - they must speak with Dr. Kiehl in-person to determine if any adjustments will be made to the calculation of their quiz average at the end of the semester.

**Worksheets:** Worksheets will be distributed during the last half of lectures roughly once a week with an online version available on Gradescope. These worksheets consist of definitions, short problems and proofs, which will be discussed and filled out during lecture. A student’s credit for lecture worksheet discussions will be a combination of participation/attendance in lecture and uploads of (partially)completed worksheets to Gradescope no later than 11:59 PM the day assigned. No late worksheets will be accepted and no make-up worksheets will be allowed. 8 of the 10 worksheet scores (up to two (2) worksheet scores will be dropped) will be used to calculate this portion of a student’s grade. Students who are unable to attend lecture the day of a worksheet, for any reason, are encouraged to upload their worksheet to Gradescope by 11:59 PM that day to receive up to half credit.

**Presentations:** There will be three video presentation assignments made during the semester. Presentations will be assigned in coordination with in class exams during the semester. Slides for presentations will be created using Beamer and recordings of presentations will be done via WebEx. Details regarding problem presentation assignments will be posted on LMS when the presentation dates draw nearer.

**Homework:** There will be 10 homework assignments due throughout the semester. Assignments are due no later than 11:59 PM on the due date. Each homework consist of 2-4 problems that must be done in LaTeX (see below). Homework assignments will be submitted on Gradescope as a compiled pdf file and for some submissions the LaTeX code will be submitted on LMS.

Homework is due on the days and times posted on LMS. Homework assignments are between 35 - 50 points. Late homework will receive a 2 point deduction if submitted less than 24 hours late, a 6 point deduction if submitted 24-48 hours late and a 15 point deduction if submitted 48-72 hours late. Homework will not be accepted after 3 days late (72 hours). An excused absence, will not automatically result in an adjustment to the late penalty as these are out of class assignments with 1 week allowed for completion. Late penalties on homework will only be reduced or waived *at the discretion of the instructor*. This is generally reserved for extended illness and when notification of the absence/illness was done in a timely manner.

At the end of the semester, a student’s homework grade will be calculated out a smaller number than the total number of homework points available. For example, if there are 350 homework points at the end of the semester, the homework grade might be calculated out of 325 points with a cap of 325 so that the max homework grade is 100%. You can assume there will roughly 25 extra homework points available to you throughout the semester.

The rules for collaborating on homework problems with others are as follows:

Before working with anyone, each student must read the **entire** homework problem set and (minimally) do the following

- Look and memorize all relevant definitions and Theorems.
- Make an outline of what needs to be shown.
- Make and attempt at writing the proof.

Once students have satisfied the above criteria, students may seek help and collaborate with others (inside and outside of office hours) on figuring out how to write, refine and improve proofs using the following guidelines:

- Students may get help on problems from an instructor, TA, ALAC provided tutor or another student currently taking the course.
- Students may not get help on problems from anyone outside of the class without explicit permission from the instructor.
- Students are responsible to independently write their submission from their own notes taken during any above interactions.
- Students must include a list of anyone with whom they collaborated (outside of office hours) at the end of each homework assignment. If you have a homework study group, all individuals participating in your study group must list names of participants in the group on every homework.
- LaTeX code and LaTeX files **may not** be copied or shared with other students.
- Submissions with glaring similarities will violate academic integrity policies. The penalties for violating academic integrity policies are shown in a subsequent section on this syllabus.
- Students are responsible for protecting their work and preventing it from being copied by others. In the event of an academic integrity policy violation, all students involved will be held accountable and penalized.
- In order to receive full credit on homework assignments, proofs must be constructed based on the definitions, properties and theorems presented in the course (book and lecture).

**LaTeX:** Learning LaTeX is part of the course. Students must use LaTeX in writing homework. Students will be required to submit the original LaTeX code on LMS for some homework sets. This is to help provide feedback to students about the appropriate ways of writing LaTeX to make it a useful system. In addition, there may be questions about LaTeX syntax on exams. Students should review log files from the LaTeX compiler to make certain that the LaTeX syntax is correct. (LaTeX compilers sometimes overlook incorrect syntax which may produce correct looking output but may eventually lead to intractable difficulties as the document grows.) Sample LaTeX files for homework will be provided for download and usage.

### Additional Course Information:

**LaTeX:** Learning LaTeX is part of the course. Students must use LaTeX in writing homework. Students will be required to submit the original LaTeX code on LMS for some homework sets. This is to help provide feedback to students about the appropriate ways of writing LaTeX to make it a useful system. In addition, there may be questions about LaTeX syntax on exams. Students should review log files from the LaTeX compiler to make certain that the LaTeX syntax is correct. (LaTeX compilers sometimes overlook incorrect syntax which may produce correct looking output but may eventually lead to intractable difficulties as the document grows.) Sample LaTeX files for homework will be provided for download and usage.

LaTeX is free to download. While the download and installation is time consuming, the advantage of downloading LaTeX is that you can edit files locally on your own machine without connection to the Internet and you can easily control your own source files. Most downloads come with a LaTeX editor, but LaTeX may be edited with any text editor. There is a choice of downloads with two major ones being TexLive and MikTex. Either will suffice for this class.

Alternatively, students may use an online LaTeX editor such as “Overleaf”. While this makes it a little easier to get started, it requires Internet connectivity and it controls your files. Note that the homework rules above explicitly forbid sharing LaTeX files in any fashion and thus prohibit using any of the sharing facilities of online programs such as “Overleaf”.
