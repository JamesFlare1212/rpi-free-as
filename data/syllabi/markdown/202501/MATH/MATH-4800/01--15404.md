## Course Goals

### Goals:

- To understand how computers represent numbers and carry out computation, as well as the impact on numerical computing;
- To understand the basic concepts in numerical computing, such as conditioning, stability and convergence;
- To learn and mathematically understand commonly used numerical algorithms in scientific computing and to carry out mathematical derivation and numerical analysis for these algorithms;
- To implement and to evaluate standard algorithms, and more importantly, to be able to interpret/analyze numerical results and observations, and to extract useful information and draw meaningful conclusions;
- To be able to choose or to design numerical methods to solve a given problem.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Contents: Topics from Chapters 0-6, including foating point arithmetic, solving linear and nonlinear  
(scalar or system of) equations, interpolation and data fitting, numerical integration, numerical methods  
for differential equations; If time permits, selected topics from other chapters may also be discussed.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

On Attendance: Be advised that the textbook does not include all the materials that will be covered in class.  
For this reason, attendance is strongly recommended. Those who have to miss a class due to unforeseen  
reasons are responsible for finding out what is discussed or announced in class. Be on time for lectures. If  
you have to leave a lecture early, do so during the class break if possible.

### Other Course Policies:

**Policy on late submission:** Each homework is due by a specified time & date. Homework reports must  
be submitted as single documents via LMS/gradescope. Late submission is strongly discouraged, and it will  
result in reduction in grade of  
5%: on the same day of deadline or up to 1/3 of a day (i.e. 8 hours);  
10%: up to one day; 20%: up to two days  
No late homework is accepted after two days, or after reference solutions are posted on LMS, whichever  
comes rst. Any request for extension without penalty needs to be made at least one day (24 hours)  
before the deadline.  
**Policy on collaboration:** Though you can discuss homework problems with other students or work in groups,  
the work you turn in must be written by yourself and represent your own independent understanding on  
the subjects. If your work involves significant help from other people, online sources, reference books or  
papers, or the codes from the textbook or shared by the instructor (yes you can modify the codes and use  
them in your homework), it is required that you explicitly state this in your report to avoid penalty in  
grade.  
It is strictly forbidden to share or exchange computer files, or to copy any part of the homework  
solutions from others. Such behavior will be considered the highest breach of academic integrity. Any  
violation will result in a grade of zero for the entire homework set (of all involved individuals if there  
is any), and can land you in the Dean of Students Office.
