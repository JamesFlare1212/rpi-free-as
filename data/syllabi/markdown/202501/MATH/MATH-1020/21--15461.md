## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Description:** Techniques and applications of integration, polar coordinates, parametric equations, infinite sequences and series, vector functions and curves in space, functions of several variables, and partial derivatives. Prerequisite: MATH 1010 (from the [Rensselaer Catalog](http://catalog.rpi.edu/))

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Basic symbol manipulation skills pertaining to integration.
- Ability to state and explain basic Calculus definitions and theorems and their applications.
- Ability to describe curves parametrically and apply Calculus concepts and techniques to curves described in this way.
- Ability to express curves via polar equations and to solve Calculus problems in the polar coordinate system.
- Ability to apply Calculus to selected problems in Science, Engineering and Mathematics.
- Ability to use convergence tests to analyze the behavior of infinite series.
- Ability to represent functions using power series and solve problems using these representations.
- Ability to describe lines and planes in space in equation form and solve problems using these representations.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance

Students are responsible for all material covered in class so attendance of every lecture is strongly recommended and encouraged. If a student is absent, they should get the class notes posted each week on LMS and keep up with the homework assignments as assigned on Achieve and LMS as well as make sure to submit their Gradescope problem(s). The best way to stay on top of the course is to come to class and recitation, think about the material as it is presented and remember to submit your Gradescope Problems.

### Other Course Policies:

Exams: Exam problems will consist of problems for which the students must show all their work, as well as multiple-choice, multiple-select, true/false, and fill-in-the-blank questions. Exam problems for which the students must show all their work are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples. Answers to odd-numbered textbook problems can be found on Achieve at the end of the E-book. Missed exams may only be made up if a student has notified the instructor prior to the exam (in the rare case of an emergency, students may have until midnight of the exam day to notify the instructor). The exam may only be made up during the Wednesday test block that follows that exam or receive a grade of 0. The 4th and last exam will be given on the last day of class, therefore its makeup exam will be given during the scheduled final exam slot. There is no final exam for this course. Exams will be uploaded to Gradescope by the instructor, where students will be able to check their grades and feedback. It is the responsibility of the students to review their exams for any discrepancies between their work and points awarded as described by the rubric.

Gradescope Problems: Each class, I will assign some problem(s) to check understanding and get extra practice. Students can work on these in groups or individually, then submit them using Gradescope (take a picture and upload your submission). Make sure your submission goes through, many students like to download the Gradescope App which notifies you when your submission goes through successfully. However, you can use any device by going to www.Gradescope.com. Students can submit their work from the time we start working on them in class until midnight two days later. So they will be due on Wednesday and Saturday nights, unless announced otherwise. After that, submissions will not be accepted. Submissions will be graded for correctness as they would be on exams.

Homework: Achieve homework assignments will be due twice weekly on Mondays and Thursdays at 11:59 PM and will be graded automatically by the system. Students may submit these assignments late up to two days after the original deadline, but a penalty will be applied to the score. A 10% penalty will apply to assignments submitted the day after the deadline and a 20% penalty will apply to assignments submitted two days after the deadline. No other due date extensions will be granted for any reason.

Additional problems will be assigned from the textbook and will be posted on LMS under each week. They will not be collected nor graded, however, success in the course depends on successful completion of all homework, both Achieve homework and textbook homework. As stated under Exams, exam problems are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples. This means that you must be able to correctly solve and show all your work, as well as fully understand your answer to each homework problem and lecture example to consider yourself well-prepared.

Change to the grading scheme may be made at the discretion of the instructor.

Getting help: don’t hesitate to ask and get help. You can ask math questions during class, office hours of the instructor, and office hours of the Teaching Assistants. You can also use email. When asking questions, it helps to be as precise as possible and to have a picture of the work you have tried. For example, if you ask a homework question by email, please attach a picture of your work so that I may check it to provide better and faster assistance. There is also tutoring and support offered by ALAC and our undergraduate TA.

Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

Calculator Policy

Calculators will not be allowed on exams. Problems will be designed so that this does not create an issue. Occasionally, a calculator may be required to complete a homework problem, but students should not rely on calculators for simple arithmetic or trigonometric calculations.

Classroom Decorum

Phones may not be used during class and should be silent. Computers and tablets may not be used for anything other than taking notes. Students who are unable to courteously comply with these policies must note that they are probably missing most of the lecture, including practice questions, common errors or hints that may be given about graded assignments, quizzes, or exams.

Health and Safety Protocols

Students are required to follow the health and safety protocols of RPI, which are subject to change throughout the semester. Please be courteous to others when feeling sick. Consider wearing a mask, covering your face when sneezing and/or coughing, and washing your hands more frequently.

Visitors

Visitors are prohibited during all class meetings. Only students who are registered for the class should be present in the classroom. Students may only attend class meetings at the time and place listed on their schedule on SIS unless cleared by the instructor.

THERE ARE NO EXTRA CREDIT ASSIGNMENTS GIVEN AT THE END OF THE SEMESTER. All course credit comes from the assignments, quizzes, Gradescope Problems, and exams assigned and administered regularly throughout the semester. Successful completion of the class indicates an in-depth understanding of the content AND the ability to correctly and precisely communicate this understanding by solving relevant problems using symbolic representation. If you cannot do the actual math, you cannot pass the course.
