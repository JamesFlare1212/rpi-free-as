## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Basic symbol manipulation skills pertaining to integration.
- Ability to state and explain basic Calculus definitions and theorems and their applications.
- Ability to describe curves parametrically and apply Calculus concepts and techniques to curves described in this way.
- Ability to express curves via polar equations and to solve Calculus problems in the polar coordinate system.
- Ability to apply Calculus to selected problems in Science, Engineering and Mathematics.
- Ability to use convergence tests to analyze the behavior of infinite series.
- Ability to represent functions using power series and solve problems using these representations.
- Ability to describe lines and planes in space in equation form and solve problems using these representations.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Exams:** Exams will be given during lecture with the exception of DSS students and excused absences as outline below. Attendance will be taken at all exams using sign in sheets. Details about each exam, including the material to be covered on the exam and time limit, will be announced on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/) when the date of the exam is near. The dates for Exams 1–3 are listed above in the section **Grading.** Details on exams for DSS students are given below in the **DSS** section.

A conflict exam will be given only to students who have made prior arrangements with the instructor. Students eligible to take a conflict exam are DSS students (prior arrangements made by providing DSS letter) and students with a pre-scheduled absence (e.g. official excused absence documented by Student Success to attend a conference, athletic competition) on the day of the exam who have notified the instructor of said absence as soon as they learn of it and no later than one class period prior to the regularly scheduled exam. Documentation excusing the student from class at the time of the regularly scheduled exam ([**excused absence documented by the Student Success office**](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/current-students/academic-and-personal-support/requesting-excused-absence) **or documentation from a coach with game departure time noted)** must be received by the instructor before the day of the regularly scheduled exam. Any student who “shows up” without making prior arrangements with the instructor, will not be allowed to take the conflict exam. If you know ahead of time that you are going to miss an exam, for any reason, you must discuss this with the instructor no later than one class period prior to the exam.

**In-semester** **make-up exams will not be given.** **The final exam is the make-up exam.** Thus, if a student misses an exam for any other reason (because they overslept, was ill on the day of the exam, didn’t know where the exam was, or didn’t know there was an exam, to give a few examples), the student will receive a score of 0 on the exam. Likewise, if a student misses a conflict exam, the student will receive a 0 on the exam. This score will be dropped as the lowest score at the end of the semester and be replaced by their score on the final exam. Thus the remaining 2 exam scores plus the final exam score times two (added in as two identical exam scores) will count toward the student’s course grade.

If a student has an unforeseen absence, it is best practice to **email** **the instructor** within 24 hours of missing the exam to inform the instructor of the nature of their absence. This particularly important because **only one exam can be replaced by the final exam score, thus if** a student misses more than one exam during the semester, they **must email** **the instructor** within 24 hours of missing the additional exam(s) to make arrangements to discuss options, ***if any***. A student who has not been in contact with the instructor and has missed two exams is most likely to have a 0 averaged into to their final grade.

Note that the policy outlined here is for in-semester exams only. The policy for the final exam is outlined below.

**Final Exam:** The Final Exam will be cumulative and will be administered during the finals period April 28 - May 2. **All students must take the final exam at the time scheduled by the Office of the Registrar. Exceptions will only be made if there is a scheduling conflict as defined** [**here**](https://info.rpi.edu/registrar/grades/#FinalExamScheduleandConflicts)**, or if a student is excused by the Student Success office.** Please note that the Student Success office will only issue an excuse for final exams for extreme circumstances, such as hospitalization, and not for travel. (Read more here.) All students **must** take their exam at the assigned time for MATH 1020 Sections 1 - 8. You may NOT take a MATH 1020 final exam given by a different instructor. ***Do not make arrangements for travel at the end of the semester until the Final Exam Schedule from the Registrar’s Office is published or schedule your travel after the last day of final exams - May 2.***

**Homework:** Achieve homework assignments will be due once or twice weekly on Tuesdays and Fridays at 11:59 PM and will be graded automatically by the system. Students may submit these assignments late up to two days after the original deadline, but a penalty will be applied to the score. A 10% penalty will apply to questions completed the day after the deadline and a 20% penalty will apply to questions completed two days after the deadline. Any questions completed before the deadline receive full credit. No other due date extensions will be granted for any reason. To further compensate for this, several homework assignment scores will be dropped at the end of the semester.

Additional problems will be assigned from the textbook and will be posted on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/) in the **Homework: Achieve & Textbook** content area found in the left toolbar. Though these problems will not be collected nor graded, they should not be viewed as optional. Success in the course depends on successful completion of all homework, both Achieve homework and textbook homework. Exam problems are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples. Full solutions to odd-numbered textbook problems can be found on Achieve. When logged in to our Achieve course, view content by assignments and scroll to the bottom of the page. The student solutions manual for chapters 5-7 and 10-14 may be found below No Due Dates.

**Quizzes:** Quizzes will be given in-person during recitation most weeks of the semester. The quiz schedule and material covered will be made available on LMS in the **Recitation Quizzes** content area. Students must take quizzes at the recitation time that is listed on their schedule on SIS. No make-up quizzes will be given for any reason. To compensate for this, two (2) in-person quiz scores will be dropped at the end of the semester. Only students who miss a quiz due to pre-scheduled absence (e.g. official excused absence documented by Student Success to attend a conference, athletic competition) may be excused from a quiz. A student with two(2) or more excused absences must discuss with the instructor how their quiz average will be calculated for the course.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance:** Students are responsible for all material covered in lectures whether they are present or not. Attendance is expected in lecture, but will not be checked.  
Students are required to attend lectures when exams are given and recitations when recitation quizzes are given unless alternative arrangements have been made with the instructor before the time of the assessment.

Students are encouraged to attend **TA office hours** if they have questions, but attendance is not required.

### Other Course Policies:

**Disability Services for Students (DSS):** Students with a memorandum from the [Disability Services for Students (DSS)](http://studenthealth.rpi.edu/disabilityservices) office identifying accommodations for exams must present the memorandum to the instructor in order to receive their accommodations. Providing the accommodations requires advance planning. Students will not be granted accommodations on an exam unless a memorandum from DSS has been submitted to the instructor by 2 PM two business days before the exam. For example, to receive accommodations on Exam 1, a DSS memorandum must be submitted no later than 2 PM on Friday, January 31. DSS memorandum may be submitted via email or in print to the instructor. Students wishing to use their DSS accommodations on Recitation Quizzes must provide their DSS memorandum at least one lecture prior to their scheduled quiz. Thus for Quiz 1 on Monday the memo must be received by 2 PM on Friday, January 10 and for Quiz 1 on Thursday the memo must be received by 2 PM on Tuesday, January 14.

Quizzes and Exams for DSS students will be arranged by the instructor and will be given during the Wednesday block on the dates listed on the Quiz schedule and exam schedule respectively. Information regarding the location and duration of the Conflict/DSS exam will be sent via email, thus DSS students are advised to monitor their emails during the week prior to the exam for specific information about how their accommodations will be met. Any DSS student who takes a quiz or exam in the regularly scheduled recitation or lecture block will forfeit their DSS accommodation and will only be allowed regular time on the assessment. DSS accommodations can only be provided to students who attend the quiz/exam during the Wednesday time block.

**Grade Appeals:** If ***upon reviewing the answer key and solutions for an exam*** a student feels that the work on their exam was graded incorrectly, then the student may submit a grading appeal to the instructor on Gradescope. Regrade requests will only be accepted during the grade appeal window specified on LMS once the grades are released. Regrade requests made outside of Gradescope will be ignored.

**Calculator Policy:** Calculators will **not** be allowed on exams. Problems will be designed so that this does not create an issue. Due to the online nature of Achieve homework, a calculator may be required to complete a homework problem, but students should not rely on calculators for simple arithmetic or trigonometric calculations.

**Health and Safety:** Students are required to follow the health and safety protocols of RPI, which are subject to change throughout the semester.

**Visitors:** Visitors are prohibited during all class meetings. Only students who are registered for the class should be present in the classroom. Students may only attend class meetings at the time and place listed on their schedule on SIS.

**Classroom Decorum:** Students must be on time for class and should refrain from leaving and reentering the classroom during class. If a student has a legitimate reason for arriving late to class or being excused early from class, then they should discuss this with the instructor before class. Phones may not be used during class and should be silent. Computers and tablets may not be used for anything other than taking notes. Students who are unable to courteously comply with these policies may be asked to leave the room.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:** Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://rpi.app.box.com/s/d4ndpn3f7qdwxh9jaz5n1hkkr0mnd25e) (pp. 16–18). A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
