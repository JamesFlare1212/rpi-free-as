## Course Content

### Content Details:

The theory underlying vector spaces, algebra of subspaces, bases; linear transformations, dual spaces; eigenvectors, eigenvalues, minimal polynomials; inner products, adjoints, orthogonal projections, and complements. Prerequisite: MATH 2010 (from the Rensselaer Catalog 2022-2023). Other topics: Determinants, complex vectors and matrices, singular value decomposition.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will learn to work with Vector Spaces: Understand the concept of Fields, vector spaces, subspaces, linear dependence and independence, basis, and dimension.
- Students will learn to apply Linear Transformations: Define and analyze linear transformations, compute their matrix representations, and determine kernel and image spaces.
- Students will learn to use Determinants and Eigenvalues: Compute determinants, eigenvalues, and eigenvectors, and apply them to diagonalization and applications in differential equations and dynamical systems.
- Students will learn to write clear and accurate basic proofs using the concepts of this course
- Students will learn to apply Theoretical and Computational Techniques: Develop problem-solving skills to apply linear algebra concepts to real-world applications in computer science (Cholesky decomposition, Jordan decomposition,...), and other fields.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to

- Demonstrate knowledge and understanding of the basic ideas of Linear Algebra, including concepts of linear systems, independence, theory of matrices, linear transformations, determinants, vector spaces and subspaces, bases and dimension, eigenvalues, eigenvectors and diagonalization, inner products, etc.

- write clear and accurate proofs using the concepts of this course,
- apply Linear Algebra concepts of this course to solve problems.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams and quizzes must be the student’s own. A student may not change an answer on an exam or quiz before submitting it for an appeal. In addition, no calculators, computers, tablets, open books, open notes, phones, headphones, or smartwatches may be used during exams or quizzes. Talking or communicating with other students in any way during exams or quizzes is also prohibited. Students may discuss homework with other students, the TA, and the instructor, but the work submitted must be the student’s own. Submissions with glaring similarities will violate academic integrity polices.

The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.

Electronic devices (including computers and calculators) and formula sheets will not be permitted during exams.
