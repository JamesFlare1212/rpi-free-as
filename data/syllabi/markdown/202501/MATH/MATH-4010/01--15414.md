## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course description:** Groups, rings, polynomial rings, fields, integral domains, with emphasis on group theory; homomorphisms and isomorphisms; normal subgroups, cosets, ideals, modules; quotient groups and quotient rings; other topics chosen from number theory, polynomials, and Galois Theory. (from the [Rensselaer Catalog](http://catalog.rpi.edu/))

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate knowledge of standard theorems and definitions in abstract algebra.
- Demonstrate the ability to apply definitions and theorems to solve problems.
- Demonstrate the ability to construct sound mathematical arguments.
- Demonstrate the ability to express these arguments clearly in writing, i.e., to write mathematical proofs.

**Exams:** The material covered on each exam will be announced on [LMS](https://lms.rpi.edu/) when the date of the exam is near. Exams will be given in person during lecture in LOW 3112. **Students who receive testing accommodations from the** [**Disability Student Services (DSS)**](https://studenthealth.rpi.edu/list-services/disability-student-services) **office may need to take their exams at a different time due to student, proctor, and room availability.**

If a student has a pre-scheduled absence (e.g. an athletic competition, official excused absence to attend a conference) on the day of an exam, then they must notify the instructor via email as soon as they learn of the absence and **no later than one week before the regularly scheduled exam**. Documentation excusing the student from class at the time of the regularly scheduled exam must be received by the instructor before the day of the exam. This documentation must be either an excused absence documented by the Student Success office or, if the absence is due to an athletic competition, documentation from a coach. Provided the above conditions are met, the student will then take a conflict exam at a different time.

If a student misses an exam due to an unforeseen absence (e.g. illness on the day of the exam), then they may take a make-up exam provided they notify the instructor via email **before the exam begins**. The make-up exam may differ in format from the regularly scheduled exam (e.g. oral exam) and must be taken within one week of the regularly scheduled exam. It is the sole responsibility of the student to contact the instructor to make arrangements for a make-up exam when an exam is missed.

If a student misses an exam and fails to notify the instructor via email before the exam begins, then the student may not take the make-up exam and receives a score of 0 on the exam.

**Final exam:** There is no final exam in the course.

**Homework and quizzes:** Homework and quizzes will be given regularly. Homework assignments will not be collected or graded, but solutions will be discussed in class. Quiz questions may include statements of definitions or theorems and problems similar to those done in class or in the homework.

No make-up quizzes will be given for any reason. To compensate for this, the two lowest quiz scores for each student will be dropped at the end of the semester.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:** Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://rpi.app.box.com/s/p1b39vybm914b5cao4csrgl84bfrrh2l) (pp. 17–19). All work submitted on exams and quizzes must be the student’s own. In addition, no calculators, computers, tablets, open books, open notes, formula sheets, phones, headphones, or smartwatches may be worn or used during exams or quizzes. Talking or communicating with other students in any way during exams or quizzes is also prohibited.

The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. This zero will not be able to be dropped at the end of the semester. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action. **Disability Student Services (DSS):** Students with a memorandum from the [DSS](http://studenthealth.rpi.edu/disabilityservices) office identifying accommodations for exams must email the memorandum to the instructor **by Friday, January 10** since providing the accommodations requires advance planning. In your email, you must state the following:

- full name,
- course (e.g. MATH 4010 or Abstract Algebra),
- which accommodations on your DSS memo you are requesting to use in the course, and
- which assignments (exams and/or quizzes) you are requesting to use your accommodations on.

Students will not be granted accommodations unless a memorandum from DSS has been received.
