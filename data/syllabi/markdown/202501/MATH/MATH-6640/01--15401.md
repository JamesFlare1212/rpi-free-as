## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

There is no required textbook, however, the following texts are recommended for supplemental study: Complex Analysis by Stein & Shakarchi (good high-level theoretical development), Complex Variables: Introduction and Applications (2nd Edition) by Ablowitz and Fokas (focused on applications, especially asymptotics), and Applied Complex Variables by Dettman (detailed rigorous treatment of theory and applications). I also highly recommend Elias Wegart's phase plotting software and book as tools for visualizing functions of complex variables.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Mastery of basic theory of complex-valued functions, including differentiability, characterization of singularities, power series representations, contour integral representations, analytic continuation, and conformal mapping.
- Mastery of basic theory and practice of Laplace and Fourier Integral Transforms, particularly (a) in computing transforms and inverse transforms and (b) in understanding the interplay of smoothness and decay of functions and their transforms.
- Ability to apply techniques from complex analysis and integral transforms to solve equilibrium and time dependent partial differential equations, to derive asymptotic behavior of solutions, and to select applications in signal processing and function approximation.

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
