## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course explores the philosophical and ethical implications of technological developments that promise to shape -- and perhaps to jeopardize -- human life and society in the 21st century and beyond. Artificial Intelligence. Genetic modifications and technological implants for enhanced abilities. Students will improve their insight as well as critical reasoning skills as we examine, analyze, and evaluate such controversial topics through the lens of philosophical reasoning and questioning. Throughout the course we will ask what sort of beings do we want to be and what sort of society do we want to have.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Students will be able to explain and communicate the essential characteristics of Philosophy and its methods.
- 2\. Students will be able to explain the concept of total existential risk, and will be able to identify several proposals for mitigating significant portions of the total risk to humanity.
- 3\. Students will gain ability to explain different kinds and degrees of human enhancement, to identify relevant ethical and social issues, and to discuss pros and cons of different types of enhancement.
- 4\. Students will be able to identify philosophical issues, themes, and ethical concerns related to the field of artificial intelligence.
- 5\. Students will be able to improve their skills at philosophical discussion.
- 6\. Students will improve their philosophical writing ability.

## Course Calendar

### Calendar Details:

A day-by-day calendar of topics and readings was provided to you in a separate document that can be found on LMS under "Course Syllabus," but the following is a condensed version of the course calendar:  
Week of 1/6: Overview of course; nature of Philosophy and its subfields; beginnings of Philosophy; basics of arguments and how they are used in daily life and in philosophy.  
Week of 1/13: What is Ethics?; intrinsic vs. instrumental value; Principle of Impartiality; Utilitarianism vs. Duty-based ethics (deontology); critical viewing of documentaries Video: "Blind Spot: Peak Oil and the Coming Global Crisis").  
Week of 1/20: Evaluating documentaries, continued (Video: "The Breakthrough in Renewable Energy").  
Week of 1/27: Start book *The Precipice* by Toby Ord. Humanity's future potential if we survive. Concept of "Existential Risk." Natural existential risks, such as asteroids and super-volcanoes; lack of appropriate attention to existential risks.  
Week 2/3: Anthropogenic existential risks, such as nuclear weapons, climate change, and severe environmental damage. Future risks, such as human-engineered pandemics and A.I. with goals not aligned with ours. Dystopian scenarios.  
Week of 2/10: Fear of technology creating monster scenarios; combining and comparing risks; international coordination to minimize risks. **PAPER 1 DUE**.  
Week of 2/17: Research on existential risk; our long-term potential as a species; REVIEW FOR EXAM 1.  
Week of 2/24: **EXAM 1**. Start Unit 3 on Biomedical enhancement; enhancement of humans through implants, prosthetics, etc. (Video: "Fixed: The Science/Fiction of Human Enhancement").  
Week of 3/3: NO CLASSES (Spring Break).  
Week of 3/10: Start book *Better than Human* by Allen Buchanan. Adamant views for and against human enhancement. Criticism of enhancement as "playing God" or interfering with the wisdom of "Mother Nature. "Why unguided evolution is not the embodiment of wisdom. Are human nature and the human good the same? Enhancement may in fact be necessary for our survival.  
Week of 3/17: To what extent would genetic changes be irreversible? Risk can never be completely eliminated. Concerns the rich will get biologically richer. Focusing on unjust inequalities. Why governments will care about enhancements. Would enhancements corrupt our characters? Should we seek moral enhancements? Front door vs. back door enhancements.  
Week of 3/24: Video on gene editing and "designer babies;" Start book *Life 3.0* by Max Tegmark; digital utopians and techno-skeptics; beneficial A.I. movement; misconceptions and myths about A.I. **PAPER 2 DUE**.  
Week of 3/31: What is intelligence? What is learning? Creativity and strategy; Deep reinforcement learning agents; Natural language and A.I. A.I. use in various sectors of society; A.I. and autonomous weapons systems. A.I. and the future of employment.  
Week of 4/7: Video: "A.I. vs. Human Brain;" Achieving human-level A.I. How an A.I. could take over the world. Cyborgs and uploads; What is most likely to happen?; What the next 10,000 years may look like; Prospects for egalitarian utopia. A.I. as benevolent dictator?; A.I. as a protector god?  
Week of 4/14: Video: "2077 -- 10 Seconds to the Future;" REVIEW FOR EXAM 2.  
Week of 4/21: **EXAM 2**.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
