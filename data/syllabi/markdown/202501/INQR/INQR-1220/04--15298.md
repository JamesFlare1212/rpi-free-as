## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course investigates the relationship between Information Technology (IT) and the individuals and groups who use it -- which is to say, virtually everyone. The course has two main goals. First, it seeks to define and explain core concepts in the field of IT in a fashion that is not overly technical. Second, it examines the historical, social, cultural, political and economic factors that have helped to shape, and have been shaped by, the dimensions of IT that we will study.

Reading presentations

Over the course of the semester, each student will play the role of discussion leader in section for one day of section. On the assigned day, the discussion leader will begin discussion of the assigned reading by providing a five-minute summary of the main arguments presented in the reading, then posing at least three questions for discussion. Discussion questions should be open-ended “how/why/what” questions, not be “yes/no” questions.

We will coordinate schedules for presentations when sections first meet. It's OK (and, depending on the size of sections, potentially unavoidable) if multiple students present on the same readings and the same day. In that case students may choose to collaborate on the presentations if they wish (i.e., by working together on a single presentation), although they are also free to present independently.

Research paper: Evaluating an information system

Length: Approximately 1500 words

In this paper, you will explore the ways in which information flows throughout technological systems. Students will choose an information system to research. You may choose any information system, and creativity in your selection will be rewarded; however, here are some examples of information systems that students have fruitfully studied in the past:

Credit reporting systems (Equifax, etc.)

RPI SIS

Facebook

GitHub

Slack

Google Ads

Internal Revenue Service

Windows

Linux

Human language

Programming languages

Printing press

Telegraph

Reddit

Your paper should:

1: Briefly describe the information system, including its history and its current state.

2: Describe how information moves between the system and a human user of the system.

3: Describe how the information system interacts with other information systems.

4: Describe how human users interact with one another through the information system.

5: Most importantly, explain how cultural, social, political and/or economic factors influence the way the information system works.

To the extent possible, the paper should draw on scholarly literature to help drive its analysis. A good place to find free scholarly articles is jstor.org (you may need to be on campus, or be logged into the RPI VPN, to access most of the JSTOR content freely). Your paper should cite all sources according to the conventions of the Chicago Manual of Style (see above).

The final paper should be submitted on LMS by the due date identified in the course meeting schedule below.

Quizzes

On four occasions during the semester, students will complete quizzes designed to test their knowledge of lecture and reading material. Quizzes will take place in lecture. For each quiz, you will be presented with a list of six terms, and you must choose four to identify by writing one or two sentences that explain what the term means in the context of the course. You will have twenty minutes to complete each quiz, or longer if you are entitled to accommodations.

There is a strict no-makeup policy for quizzes; however, your lowest quiz score will be disregarded when calculating final grades.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
