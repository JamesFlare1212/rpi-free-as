## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides an introduction to the principles and practices of Artificial Intelligence (AI). Students will learn foundational AI techniques and concepts, including search algorithms, knowledge representation, machine learning, reasoning, and applications in various fields.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach, 4th Edition by Stuart Russell and Peter Norvig

## Course Goals

### Goals:

In this course, we aim to:

1. Inspire curiosity and enthusiasm for the rapidly evolving field of AI.
2. Build knowledge and techniques in major topics of AI
3. Enhance Programming Skills for Implementing AI Solutions
4. Discuss the Ethical and Societal Implications of AI
5. Prepare for Future Study and Application of AI

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand and implement basic AI techniques.
- Apply AI algorithms to a variety of problems.
- Analyze the performance of different AI methods.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1. Understanding Core AI Concepts and Techniques
   1. Learning Outcome: Students will be able to explain and apply fundamental concepts and techniques across core AI topics.
   2. Assessment Measures
      1. Exams (70% Total): Exams 1, 2, and 3 will test students’ knowledge and understanding of AI principles and methodologies
      2. Written Homework (15%): Regular homework assignments will reinforce students’ grasp of theoretical concepts and problem-solving techniques.
2. Developing Programming Skills for Implementing AI Solutions
   1. Learning Outcome: Students will gain proficiency in programming AI algorithms, particularly in Python
   2. Assessment Measures
      1. Projects (15%): Projects will involve designing, coding, and evaluating AI models and applications
3. Demonstrating Analytical and Problem-Solving Skills
   1. Learning Outcome: Students will be able to critically evaluate different AI methods, analyze their performance, and choose appropriate techniques for various applications.
   2. Assessment Measures:
      1. Exams (70%): Each exam will include analytical questions that require students to assess the strengths and limitations of various AI approaches
      2. Projects (15%): Projects will involve designing, coding, and evaluating AI models and applications
      3. Written Homework (15%): Regular homework assignments will reinforce students’ grasp of theoretical concepts and problem-solving techniques.

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

**Website and Announcements:** You are responsible for checking the course website and submitty regularly for announcements and course materials, as well as your e-mail for communications related to the class.

**Lectures:** Students are highly encouraged to attend all classes. You are responsible for all material covered and announcements made in the lectures.

**Laptops and Electronic Devices:** No laptops or other electronic devices are allowed in lectures. Even if you diligently take notes on your laptop, the bright screen and the activity are extremely distracting to the people behind you. Because of this, "movie theater" rules apply: no laptops, phones, or other devices with a screen should be out during lectures unless authorized by the instructor. Students who continually disrupt the class will be asked to leave.

**Exams:** No external aids are permitted during exams. This includes books, notes, papers, computers, calculators, or other devices. If you miss an exam due to a valid reason (e.g., illness), submit the necessary documentation to the class dean (do NOT send it to the course staff), request the class dean to provide a letter verifying your absence, and then coordinate with us to schedule a make-up exam. *Important: Scheduling a make-up exam is your responsibility.*

**Getting Help:** If you need help on projects, please ask your questions on Piazza or contact TAs and mentors during office hours. Please note that the instructor does not help with debugging projects.

**Acknowledging Help:** If you benefit from a discussion with your friend or another work you found on the Web, you must acknowledge the author of the original work.

**Regrading:** Problems regarding grading must be reported within one week after the grades are made available. This applies to all homework, projects, and exams (except for the last exam, for which we will handle requests within 2 days to meet RPI's final grade submission deadline). After that, all grades are finalized and will not change unless the student can present an official excuse letter to explain why the request was not submitted in time.

**Late Submission Policy:** Everyone has two days max for any single written homework or project, and in total, no more than 6 late days for all homework and projects (except in an established illness or emergency---in that case, please send me an official approval).

**Use of AI tools in assignments:** Students shall **NOT** use AI tools during in-class exams. For homework and projects, it is a violation of course policy to misrepresent work that you submit or exchange with your instructor by characterizing it as your own, such as submitting responses to assignments that do not *acknowledge* the use of generative AI tools.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and the Graduate Student Supplement (For 4000 level and above courses) define various forms of Academic Dishonesty, and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

Students should fairly represent their own work, and misrepresenting others’ work as your own could be a violation of academic integrity.

Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in (1) an academic (grade) penalty and (2) reporting to the Associate Dean of Academic Affairs and either the Dean of Students (for Undergraduates) or the Dean of Graduate Education (for Graduate students).

**In this course, the academic penalty for a first offense is zero grade for the relevant portion of the grade. A second offense will result in failure of the course.**

If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
