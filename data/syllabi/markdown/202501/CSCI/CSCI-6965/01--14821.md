## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

This course delves into advanced topics in the design of quantum computer systems. We will begin by developing the mathematical foundations of quantum computation and information, followed by an in-depth exploration of powerful techniques in quantum application and architecture design. Students will gain hands-on experience using IBM's Qiskit software tools to write quantum programs and execute them on IBM Quantum System One on campus. Key topics covered include quantum programming, quantum algorithms, quantum compilation, memory management, quantum error mitigation and correction. Students will also engage with research papers, participate in discussions, and undertake a semester-long group research project. Prior knowledge of quantum computing is helpful but not mandatory.  
Throughout the course, students will have the opportunity to program and run their algorithms on RPI's state-of-the-art quantum computer by requesting access via link1, enabling them to directly engage with cutting-edge technology. By the end of the course, groups of students will expect to present a team project related to the course topic.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Class Participation (10%) Active engagement in class discussions and sharing insights from literature are crucial for a collaborative learning environment.  
Quiz (25%) To reinforce comprehension of core principles and advanced techniques, quizzes will assess students' grasp of mathematical foundations in quantum computation, quantum programming, and other key course topics such as algorithms, compilation, and error mitigation. Quizzes are designed to ensure students are prepared for hands-on work with Qiskit and IBM Quantum System One.  
Coding Practices (25%) This component evaluates students’ ability to apply quantum programming skills through hands-on exercises using IBM’s Qiskit. Assignments will involve developing and testing quantum algorithms, practicing compilation techniques, and exploring memory management, error mitigation, and correction. Students are encouraged to write clean, efficient code that demonstrates an understanding of the underlying quantum principles, preparing them for advanced work on RPI's quantum computer and the semester-long team project.  
Project Proposal (15%)  
•  
Initial Presentation (5%): Present your project idea to the class, outlining the problem, proposed solution, and expected outcomes.  
•  
Proposal Writing (10%): Submit a detailed written proposal that includes motivation, related work, methodology, and project plan.  
Group Project (25%)  
•  
Final Presentation (10%): Present your final project to the class, demonstrating your findings and the implications of your work.  
•  
Final Project Report (15%): Submit a comprehensive report detailing the entire project process, results, and conclusions.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
