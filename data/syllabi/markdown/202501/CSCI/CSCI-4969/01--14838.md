## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course introduces methods that leverage the basic analysis techniques learned in Robotics I to develop numerical and algorithmic techniques needed to endow robots with the "intelligence'' to devise strategies to solve problems they will encounter. Once these abilities are sufficiently well developed, robots will become safe and autonomous, thus paving the way for pervasive personal robots. Topics include: configuration space representation, cell decomposition, roadmap methods, rapidly-exploring random trees, simultaneous localization and mapping, contact modeling, grasping, and dexterous manipulation.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Students who complete this course satisfactorily will be able to understand and apply the following  
major topics:  
• Localization  
• Kalman Filter  
• Mapping  
• Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM)  
• Motion Planning: Bug Algorithm, Potential Field, Sampling Approach  
• Distance Calculation, Moving Obstacles  
• Multi-robot planning  
• Grasping, Force Control  
• Point Contact with Friction, Friction cone

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- use sensors and landmarks for localization and mapping
- find a feasible path and trajectory for a mobile robot
- design coordination for multi-robot systems
- characterize contact models and design force control for grasping

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Even though attendance sheets will not be formally collected, students should try to attend the lectures unless they have a good reason not to.

### Other Course Policies:

Lab Etiquette: Lab access is necessary for the satisfactory completion of the projects, which accounts for a good percentage of your overall grade. Managing shared workspaces and resources is an important skill for all professionals. Students will be held to high standards with regard to cleanliness, professionalism, and consideration. Lab rules will be communicated to the students and they need to be followed at all times.  
Late Submissions: Because there might be unexpected events (e.g., work or family commitments) there is 0% penalty for 48 hours (no questions asked) for late submissions of the following types of assignments: homework, lab assignment reports, and final project report. After this period students would get 0 credits. You can not get bonus points if you submit late. Quizzes and final presentations should be submitted on the posted due date, after which students would get 0 credits. If you are unable to complete the assignment on time due to sickness, communicate this to the instructor before the posted due date (not the late submission date) so the necessary extensions can be granted.  
Grade Appeals: Requests for regrade must be made (in writing) within 72 hours of the grade being communicated to the student. Any concerns regarding overall course performance must be communicated to the instructor as soon as possible. Final grades will not be modified once published in SIS unless there is a demonstrable error made by the instructor (i.e., a calculation error).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Specifically, in this course, quiz and homework submissions must be performed individually and must represent the student’s own work. Lab assignments and projects will be performed in groups. In cases where external help was received or given in these assignments, a note should be included to indicate the collaboration. Usage of materials (books, internet sites, etc.) outside the listed resources should be cited. Evidence of academic dishonesty will result in a zero for the associate assignment and a report to the Dean of Students for possible additional actions.  
**Note on Generative AI:** Students may use generative AI tools (such as ChatGPT) as part of their research and preparation for assignments, or as a text editor, but the text that is submitted must be written by the student. For example, students may use AI to generate ideas, and questions that they then revise, expand, or cite properly. Students are only allowed to use these tools when collaboration is allowed. Hence, students are not allowed to use such tools for quizzes or homework assignments. Students should also be aware that the outputs of these tools are not always reliable or accurate. Any use of such tools and the extent of the use should be acknowledged  
and cited properly. Violations of this policy will be treated as academic misconduct. If you have any questions about this policy or if you are unsure whether a particular use of AI is acceptable, please do not hesitate to ask for clarification.
