## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The application of the fundamentals of chemistry, thermodynamics, mathematics, and transport phenomena to the design and evaluation of stage-wise and continuous contacting apparatus and systems for separating and purifying chemical materials. Steady-state and transient processes are studied. This is an undergraduate level course.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Sons, Inc. 2015.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The objective of this upper level undergraduate course is to familiarize chemical engineering students with the fundamental principles of separation processes. We will examine both equilibrium controlled separation processes as well as separation processes that involve both mass transport and equilibrium considerations. In order to probe the key concepts in depth, the course will focus primarily on distillation, absorption and membranes. However, throughout the course, a wide variety of separation processes will be brought to the student’s attention to broaden the discussion. In addition to teaching the fundamental principles involved in these unit operations, the course will also introduce the students to specific subtleties associated with a wide variety of separation processes both old and new. In class problems will be used throughout the course to deepen the students’ understanding of the material. Computer instruction will also be employed to illustrate important characteristics of these separation systems. In addition, Aspen will be used to solve more complex problems.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Separations Overview and Single Stage Separations

- Classes of Separation Processes (Chapter 1)
- Gibbs’ Phase Rule (4.1), Binary Vapor–Liquid (4.2)
- Equilibrium Two-Phase Flash Calculations (4.3)
- Ternary Liquid–Liquid Equilibrium Systems (4.4)
- Liquid-Solid, Gas-Liquid and Gas-Solid Systems (4.6, 4.7, 4.8)
- Cascades: Single Stage, Multiple-Contact Stages (Co-current, counter current, crosscurrent, two section distillation, membranes, hybrid systems) (5.1, 5.2, 5.3, 5.4, 5.5)

Distillation

- Distillation Systems and Equipment (6.1)
- Binary Distillation, Equipment and Design Considerations (7.1), McCabe Thiele Technique (7.2)
- Operating lines for enriching and stripping sections; q lines for the feed, counting off stages, optimum feed location, limiting cases of total reflux and minimum reflux

Exam 1

Distillation (cont’d)

- Binary distillation continued (7.3, 7.4.1, 7.4.2) operating pressure, heat duties and correlations of tray efficiency and multiple feed/sidestreams.

Absorption in Packed Towers

- General Design considerations (6.2)
- Graphical Equilibrium Stage Method for Trayed Towers (6.3)
- Kremser Group Method for Determining the Number of Stages (6.4)
- Stage Efficiency and Column Height (6.5)
- Selections from Flooding, Column Diameter and Tray Layout (6.6)
- Rate Based Method for Packed Columns (6.7)
- Packed-Column Pressure Drop and Mass Transfer Efficiency (6.8)

Exam 2

Membrane Separation Processes

- Membrane materials and modules (Section 14.1, 14.2)
- Transport in Membranes (Section 14.3)
- Dialysis (Section 14.4)
- Reverse osmosis (14.6)

Overview of Bioseparations

Final Exam

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Explain and apply the fundamentals of material balances, thermodynamics, and transport phenomena to the types of separations problems commonly encountered by practicing chemical engineers.
- 2\. Design basic separations equipment including distillation columns, adsorption towers and membrane devices to meet product stream specifications and productivity requirements.
- 3\. Be knowledgeable and conversant with the field of chemical process separations.
- 4\. Advance with a solid foundation to the Chemical and Biological Engineering laboratory courses and to the Chemical Process Design course.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Assessments will include team-based work (problem sets, in-class problems, team materials) and individual work (in-class quizzes and surveys, two midterms, final exam).

Problem Sets (teamwork) There will be about ten or eleven problem sets which you will complete in self-selected groups of, in most cases, three. Each problem set will consist of several graded problems. For many problems involving material balances, we’ll use a version of the “Felder and Rousseau” problem solving and presentation format: 1. sketch and label a system diagram, 2. state basis of calculations, 3. state assumptions, 4. list available equations and data, 5. perform degree of freedom analysis, 6. outline method of solution, 7. solve problem, 8. comment on solution relative to expectations. The assignments will be available for viewing and printing at the Problem Sets site and will be due on-line as listed on the Syllabus and in the Problem Sets themselves. Each group will turn in one copy of their completed problem set; I suggest that each student in each group keep a copy of each completed problem set for their own records as well. Solutions will be posted on Blackboard. Problem sets must be turned in by the due date and time. Late problem sets will be corrected but will receive a grade of zero. More information on the structure of the team materials and format for submitted team materials is given in the Teamwork Guidelines section.

In-class Problems (teamwork) There will be occasional problems that will be completed and turned in by groups of, in most cases three, in class. These will be roughly equivalent to a single problem set problem.

Teamwork Materials (teamwork)

Team organization, interim team evaluations and final team evaluations along with problem set covering memos will comprise the grade teamwork materials.

Quizzes and Surveys (individual work) There will be occasional in-class assessments and surveys of mastery of material. These may or may not be announced in advance. Grading will be simplified for these: 2 pts correct or substantially correct/thoughtful response, 1 pt reasonable effort, 0 pts minimal/no effort.

Exams (individual work) There will be two in-class midterm exams and a comprehensive final exam during finals week. Make no travel plans until after the published final exam date and time for the class. There will be no early or make-up final exam given.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

If you have any questions at all concerning issues of academic integrity for any aspect of this course including quizzes, surveys, exams, in-class problems, teamwork assessments or problem sets, please ask for clarification!

Problem Sets and In-Class Problems Each group member is expected to contribute their fair share to the groups' efforts. This means neither racing far ahead of the rest of the group so as not to give others an opportunity to contribute, nor coasting so that you contribute little or nothing to the group's progress. The covering memo for each problem set and the interim team member evaluations for each block of problem sets are opportunities to provide feedback on your performance and that of your teammates relative to the expectations you have set for each other. The final team evaluation is expected to be consistent with the trends expressed in the memos and interim evaluation: if you can't tell someone to their face during the completion of assignments that their performance is not meeting expectations, you should not hide behind an anonymous evaluation form to give them a poor final evaluation. In situations where participation is lopsided, individual grades for group work may differ.

You may talk about concepts and approaches to problems with anyone inside or outside of class. However, the work submitted by each team for each in-class problem or homework set must be that of you and your team members exclusively. Students found to have copied any part of their homework from another group, a solution manual or from an online database will be considered in breach of academic honesty and will result in a zero grade for the assignment for parties involved. A repeated breach of these requirements will result in an F in the course for all parties involved and notification of the Dean of Students.

Quizzes, Surveys & Exams Collaboration (exchanging verbal or written information or copying) during individual work including a quiz, survey or exam is, of course, prohibited. In addition, the use of email or the internet during quizzes or exams for any purpose is strictly prohibited. All mobile devices (cell/smart phones, computers, pagers, etc.) must be stored securely away during these activities and are not to be used unless specifically directed otherwise by the instructor. Use of (or ANY interaction with) a mobile device during an exam without explicit permission of the instructor will be interpreted as the illicit transfer of exam data, will be considered an act of cheating and will be treated as such. All parties involved will receive an F in the course and the Dean of Students will be notified.

Other Outrageous offenses such as arranging for a substitute to take an examination, attempting to obtain examination questions or solutions in advance, or attempting to change a grade record will result in a failing grade for the course and a referral to the Dean of Students for Institute sanction.
