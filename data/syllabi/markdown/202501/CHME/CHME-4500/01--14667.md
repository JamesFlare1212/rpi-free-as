## Course Goals

### Goals:

The purpose of this course is to introduce undergraduate students to the theory and practice of chemical reactor analysis and design. The MATLAB package will be used, and many of the homework assignments will require computer-based simulations. The course is often taught in a studio format, combining lectures, discussions and computer-based simulations in a single laptop-based classroom. Thus, students should plan on bringing their laptop computers to each class.  
An agreement for a new student version of MATLAB should be completed and a new license installed:

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate an understanding of the modeling, design and operation of batch stirred tank reactors, continuous stirred tank reactors (CSTR), and plug-fow reactors (PFR), and compare the performance characteristics of the different reactor types.
- Demonstrate an understanding of multiple steady-states, reactor stability, and runaways.
- Demonstrate an understanding of the safety implications of reactor design.
- Use computer packages (such as MATLAB) to solve reactor models.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Homework and exam will be used for the course learning outcome assessment.  
TENTATIVE EXAM SCHEDULE  
Mid-term exams will be held during regular class hours. Closed book and notes. There will be no make-up quizzes without a written excuse from the health center or the dean of students.  
Mid-Term Exam #1: 10 AM EST, February 10, 2025, Monday  
Mid-Term Exam #2: 10 AM EST, March 20, 2025, Thursday.  
Final Exam: Date/time will be available or discussed during the semester.  
Graded homework and exams will be returned in class or by TA.  
HOMEWORK ASSIGNMENTS AND PROJECTS  
Homework will be uploaded on LMS. The majority of the homework assignments and projects will be performed in groups of TWO students; each student is responsible for understanding the entire assignment. Homework is due at the beginning of class. Late homework, including homework turned in during or after class will be accepted, with a penalty of 20% for each day late. Although an assignment may have several problems, often a few questions will be selected at random for grading.  
Groups may discuss the problems with other groups, but are not allowed to share solutions (MATLAB m-files, etc.). Violations will be handled in accordance with the student handbook.  
Note about Showing Work: In this class, the process of getting to an answer is as important (if not more) than the actual answer. Therefore, you must show and justify all of your work to receive credit. If you do not show your work, you will receive zero credit (even if the answer is correct).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Grade Appeal  
A student may appeal a grade by attaching a memo to teaching assistants to the specific exams or homework assignment that is being appealed. This memo must state the specific reason(s) for the regrade. A verbal appeal is not accepted. The student has one week after the grades have been assigned to contest the grade. The regrading process will be based on proper physical and chemical implication(s) of the answer to the given question. Be professional in the appeal process.  
Use of Electronic Devices  
All mobile devices (cell/smartphones, computers, pagers, etc.) must be stored securely away during lecture hours and are not be used unless the use of a device is specifically directed by the instructor. Use of (or ANY interaction with) a mobile device during an exam without an explicit permission of the instructor will be interpreted as the illicit transfer of exam data and will be  
considered an act of cheating and will be treated as such.
