## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course is a continuation of CHME 4010 (Transport Phenomena I), but in many ways will be different from CHME 4010. The focus of this course is on multidimensional problems, coupled heat, mass, and momentum transport, and boundary layer analysis.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

**Required text:** “Transport Phenomena Fundamentals” by Joel Plawsky, CRC Press, 2014 (fourth edition)

**Optional texts:** “Transport Phenomena” by R. B. Bird, W. E. Stewart, E. N. Lightfoot, Wiley, (second edition)

“Fundamentals of Momentum, Heat, and Mass Transfer” by J. R. Welty, C. E. Wicks, R. E. Wilson, Wiley, (fourth edition)

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Simplify models based on geometry and values of dimensionless groups
- • Construct and solve microscopic balance equations
- • Construct and solve macroscopic balance equations
- • Solve coupled momentum/energy/species transfer problems

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

**Note about Showing Work:** In this class, the process of getting to an answer is as important (if not more) than the actual answer, and you need to be able to clearly communicate that process. Therefore, you *must show, explain, and justify* all of your work to receive credit. If you do not show your work, you will receive zero credit (even if the answer is correct). For example, a series of equations without explanation will not receive credit even if all equations are correct. If you are algebraically manipulating equations to simplify, you should state that. Unless specifically requested in the problem statement, it is not acceptable to use packages such as MATLAB, Maple, Mathematica, etc. to perform manipulations, integrals, etc.  
**Regrading Policy:** If you feel your work was not accurately graded, you must submit a regrade request *in writing within one week* of receiving the grade. You must cite the posted solutions in your written argument why you believe it was not accurately graded.  
**Late Quizzes and Homework:** Quizzes will not be accepted past the due date/time. The quiz that is dropped is done to account for times where circumstances arise. In general, homeworks will not be accepted past the due date/time. But each person can one time submit up to 24 hours after the due date/time without penalty. If it is submitted more than 24 hours late or if you have already used the one late homework, it will not be accepted.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
