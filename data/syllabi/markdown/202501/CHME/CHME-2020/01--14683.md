## Course Goals

### Goals:

Thermodynamics is one of the threads that tie together the chemical-engineering sciences. This course (CHME 2020), in conjunction with CHME 2010, provides students with thermodynamic expertise for analyzing chemical-engineering processes. Topics include process flowsheeting, solution thermodynamics, phase equilibria, chemical-reaction equilibria, and applications of thermodynamics to problems in chemical-process design. In particular, upon successful completion of this course, the student should be conversant with methods for representing properties of real fluids and their mixtures and with techniques for formulating and solving problems in multiphase and multi-reaction equilibria.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**MAJOR TECHNICAL TOPICS COVERED**  
Heat effects: sensible, latent, chemical, and physical  
The PVTx equation of state  
Corresponding-states concepts  
Energies and entropies of real fluids  
Solution thermodynamics  
Fugacity concepts  
Phase equilibria, especially VLE  
Chemical-reaction equilibria  
Applications of the above concepts

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

COURSE GRADING  
Design Projects = 24% (three, 8% each)  
Homework = 6% (approximately 8 to 12)  
Exam #1 = 20%  
Exam #2 = 20%  
Final Exam = 30%

• Design Projects  
Three (3) design projects will be assigned, collected, and graded. An adequate solution of these exercises requires a group effort; for this course, the nominal group size will be four (4) students. Design reports will be submitted on or before the due dates listed on the Schedule of Events. Failure to submit on time will lead to grade penalties – 5% points are deducted for every hour of delay after the final submission time.  
Form groups! Decision due by Friday (1/17/2025), by email to Dr. Hedden  
• Foreman Reports  
Each design project report will be accompanied by a Foreman Report detailing the individual contributions of each group member. The Foreman Report is a signed statement attesting to their specific, of equal proportion, and independent contribution to the Design Project.  
Each student will submit their own Foreman Report confidentially through LMS after the design report is turned in. A template will be provided through which you can rate your teammates based on effort, quality of work, and attendance of meetings. If there is a consensus that a given team member did not contribute sufficiently, the instructor(s) reserve the right to lower that student’s grade and/or assign a grade of zero.  
• Homework  
Homework will be assigned on a regular basis (generally due on Fridays). Outlines of solutions will be provided after the submission deadlines. Each problem is graded on the basis of effort/completeness/correctness as OK (1) or Not OK (0). Solutions with minor errors may receive a grade of OK (1) at the TA’s discretion, but incomplete solutions or those with serious errors will receive a grade of Not OK (0) on the problem in question.  
• Mid-Semester Exams  
Two mid-semester exams will be held. All students will take both exams. The acceptable excuses for absence from an examination are: 1) a documented medical excuse from the Infirmary due to an emergency, 2) an appropriate excuse statement from the Dean of Students, and 3) an official excuse on RPI letterhead for required athletic competition on the scheduled exam date. Athletic excuses must be presented to the instructors well in advance of the examination (please allow several days minimum), or we may choose not to accommodate them.  
Mid-semester exams typically will consist of three (3) to five (5) problems. Since you may bring your text and a crib sheet to the exams, you should not expect examination problems to be replicas of examples or problems done in handouts, homeworks, lectures, or the text. We are looking for understanding of the material; a good measure of understanding is the ability to tackle new problems with acquired skills  
• Final Exam  
There will be a scheduled final examination, which all students will take. The final-examination period is April 28th – May 2nd. Make no travel plans until your complete final-exam schedule has been established. NO EXCEPTIONS.  
All students must take all exams (including the final). If you must miss an exam for extenuating circumstances (see "Mid-Semester Exams", above), it is your responsibility to personally contact us in advance. Special examination arrangements must be made face-to-face (in person).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

We live in an age of ethical ambiguity; expectations are often wildly discordant with effort, and the entrepreneurial imperative appears to some to offer a generalized shelter for the morally impaired. This situation should concern us all. We therefore append the following statements regarding academic integrity.  
1\. Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments which students turn in are their own. Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Students should familiarize themselves with this portion of the Rensselaer Handbook and should note that the penalties for plagiarism and other forms of cheating can be quite harsh. Fair warning!  
2\. A local definition of "cheating" seems necessary. For this course, let us use the following:  
a. Collaborative work of any kind on an examination, or excessive inter-group cooperation on the design projects, constitutes cheating. "Collaboration" and "cooperation" include sharing your work with others, as well as receiving and using the work of others.  
b. Consulting “back work” not provided by the instructors, meaning graded or ungraded design projects or homework assignments from past years, is considered cheating and is strictly forbidden. Design reports showing evidence of copying, paraphrasing, or adapting writing or calculations from previous years’ students, no matter how much or little work was borrowed, will be grounds for a grade of zero on the report in question for the entire group.  
c. Using AI-generated text (ChatGPT, Google Gemini, etc.) to solve homework exercises or write part or all of your design reports is prohibited and may result in a grade reduction or a grade of zero.
