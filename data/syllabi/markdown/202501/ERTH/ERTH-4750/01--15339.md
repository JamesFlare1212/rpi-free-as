## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- demonstrate proficiency and independence in a suite of GIS skills and use those skills for analysis and problem-solving in a variety of fields.
- download, prepare, and trouble-shoot data for analysis using a wide variety of data sources, not just work with prepared data that are given to them.
- develop accurate workflow to plan GIS analyses and apply what they have learned to different but related problems.
- develop a personal GIS portfolio and resource collection that will be useful to them in the future as you encounter problems that can be tackled using GIS either in future courses or in a job.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Weekly Maps:** Each week you will be making and turning in a map from the Layout View. All maps need to be PDFs and will be graded on the analysis done in addition to the aesthetics and design of the map (see rubric on LMS). All maps must include metadata (title, legend, N arrow, scale, basemap and text box with name, date and data source). *Note that for full points on map analysis, you must go beyond the requirements for the map*. Be sure that for your finished map, if someone picks it up off the floor, it can be understood without you having to explain it. Maps will be due on LMS at 11:59 pm on Monday night each week.

After maps are graded, you will have the opportunity to revise and resubmit your map *within 1 week* to earn back 50% of points you may have lost.

**GIS Portfolio:** This is not just a collection of your maps! Portfolios must include a spreadsheet of data sources and information about downloading and prepping data from each site, an annotated Word doc of useful websites, a collection of projects and data from the course, work flow charts, and an organized folder that contains these items. You will also need to include revised maps if you were missing elements on the first submission. This is more than a collection of your GIS stuff, this should be useful for the future! More information can be found on LMS about components to include. There will be a resource portfolio check half-way though the semester.

**Final project and poster session**: You will choose a question to answer that mapping and spatial analysis can be used to answer. Your project must include an analysis to answer a question, simply making a map or set of maps for this project is not acceptable. Additionally, you cannot simply repeat one of the class exercises with a new area or set of data. You will need to find and manipulate your own spatial data, and prepare a poster that includes not only a high quality map but also text boxes and additional elements that explain the problem, the analysis and the results. Your project can be on any topic you find interesting, but needs to include new, meaningful map that you are producing.

*You are required to attend the final poster session to pass the class.*

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

This course relies heavily upon in-class mapping and problem solving, where you will work independently with guidance from an instructor and/or the TA. At the beginning of each class, the instructor will give an introduction and overview of the assignment, get the students started on their work, cite potential pitfalls and challenges, etc. Therefore, it is essential to arrive ON TIME to each class and be present for the introductory component - Students that arrive more than 15 minutes late will be asked to leave and will complete the assignment on their own.

### Other Course Policies:

**Late Assignment policy:** Assignments will be accepted up to 1 week past the due date. Scores on late assignments will be penalized 10% each day they are late. So, if an assignment is due on Sunday at 11:59 pm, if you turn it in on Monday you will lose 10%. *After 1 week, the grade becomes a zero.*
