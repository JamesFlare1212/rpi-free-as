## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Ocean basins and margins; origin, distribution, chemistry, and history of sediments; physical and chemical properties of seawater; global atmospheric and oceanic circulations and climatic interactions.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Explain the major concepts of oceanography
- Analyze: Distinguish between the different types of plate boundary and their impacts on ocean basin formation.
- Understand: Explain the processes that drive ocean circulation and their influence on global climate systems.
- Apply: Illustrate the relationships between marine ecosystems and physical oceanographic features using real-world examples.
- Evaluate: Assess the effectiveness of various human interventions in mitigating ocean pollution and its ecological consequences.
- Remember: Define key terms related to oceanography, such as ocean currents, salinity, and plate tectonics.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- **Define key terms related to oceanography**  
  **Assessment Measure**: Administer a multiple-choice or short-answer quiz where students identify and define key terms such as ocean currents, salinity, and plate tectonics.
- **Explain the processes that drive ocean circulation**  
  **Assessment Measure**: Assign a written essay or presentation where students describe the mechanisms of thermohaline circulation and its impact on global climate patterns, supported by diagrams or models.
- **Illustrate the relationships between marine ecosystems and physical oceanographic features**  
  **Assessment Measure**: Conduct a project where students create concept maps or visual models linking marine ecosystems (e.g., coral reefs) to features such as upwelling zones or continental shelves.
- **Distinguish between the types of seafloor spreading**  
  **Assessment Measure**: Include a case study analysis where students compare and contrast data (e.g., seismic activity, magnetic striping) to determine seafloor spreading patterns in different regions.
- **Assess the effectiveness of human interventions in mitigating ocean pollution**  
  **Assessment Measure**: Facilitate a group debate or written critical analysis where students evaluate real-world policies or technologies aimed at reducing ocean pollution, supported by scientific evidence.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
