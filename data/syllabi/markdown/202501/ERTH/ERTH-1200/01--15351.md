## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will focus on learning about geological environment of humankind: the atmosphere, oceans, groundwater, rivers, glaciers, deserts, and coasts. We will explore the processes by which these and other features develop and change, the opportunities or hazards they present, and the ways in which humans can modify their development.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand how wind, water, ice and biology can change the surface of the Earth
- Understand Earth’s climate system
- Apply principles of logic and reasoning to analyze geologic problems
- Understand how geology relates to your everyday life, and how you interact with the Earth
- Travel to new and exciting places and explain to friends and family how those landscapes formed and may change in the future

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Tasks**: Throughout the semester there will be 12 short mini-assignments (i.e. Tasks) where you will have the opportunity to apply what we are learning to a problem or to dig into a topic in more depth. These will be posted on LMS at least a week in advance.

**In-class engagement:** Research shows that regular engagement with material through the semester increases retention, and also leads to better grades. In class, we will be utilizing a variety of techniques in the classroom to improve your learning and retention. Chiefly, this will include working independently and in small groups on activities, taking surveys to gage overall classroom understanding, and generating discussion. For some of these activities, points will be awarded for participation/completion and for some, points will be awarded for accuracy. Generally, there will be ~5 points/week you can earn for engagement. There is no make-up for missed engagement.

**Quizzes:** There will be 8 quizzes throughout the semester to check for content understanding and to ensure you are engaging in distributive studying. Each quiz will be administered at the start of class on Tuesdays. Late arrivals will not be given additional time. One make-up will be allowed for quizzes but must be made-up within 48 hours and arranged prior to the quiz. The dates of the quizzes are on the schedule and will not change.

**Journal Entries:** The geology journal is designed to help you be reflective about what you are learning in class and connect course material to your everyday life. Note-these are not a summary of class topics! You can complete up to 5 journal entries throughout the semester.

**Exams:** There will be 3 exams throughout the semester. Each exam will cover the topics preceding that exam (**not cumulative**-however, keep in mind that we will be building upon prior knowledge). There will be **no** make-up of exams without a legitimate excuse discussed **prior** to the exam (I reserve the right to determine what is “legitimate”).

## How to Be Successful in this Course

Once upon a time, I was also a student in an Introductory Physical Geology course because it was the only science course that fit into my schedule. It was a hard course, and I had to work my butt off in that class! There was lots of new terminology and quantitative reasoning, and I often felt lost and overwhelmed. In fact, the grade I received in the course was the lowest I received in college! However, it ended up being my favorite class and resulted in me switching my major to Geology (originally, I was a Pre-Med student)! I am not smarter than you, I’ve just been thinking about geology longer than you. You may not think you are a “science person”, but you have all the skills to be successful in this class. Plus, you have me here to help you!

Based on experience, here are some suggestions for success:

1. Read before and after class. Even if you don’t buy the text, if we are talking about igneous rocks, google igneous rocks and see what you find out. By introducing yourself to key terms, it will make following class easier.
2. If you have a question at any point, ask it! This could be in class, during office hours, an email, slipping a note at the end of class.
3. Focus on concepts. How does information build on each other? How are concepts related to each other?
4. Make sketches as notes!
5. All PowerPoint notes will be posted after class. Instead of focusing on getting down all the words on the slide, focus on key phases, questions, and concepts. Your notes should be in our own words, not mine!
6. Don’t forget to take notes on class discussions, activities and videos

Allow yourself to have fun
