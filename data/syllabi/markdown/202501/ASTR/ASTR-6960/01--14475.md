## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is an upper-level undergraduate course with an extra component for graduate students. This course covers a wide range of topics related to the interstellar medium (ISM) of galaxies, especially that of our own Milky Way galaxy. We will learn various physical processes associated with different phases of the ISM, ranging from cold neutral HI gas clouds to ionized HII regions and hot ISM gas. We will learn different observational techniques that are actively used in cutting-edge research to examine the nature of the ISM, and focus on current outstanding problems in the field. Basic knowledge of atomic physics and radiative transfer will be introduced at the beginning of the semester.  
Over the 16-week course, we will spend time on both lectures and student-led discussions. Whether you are an undergraduate or graduate student, I expect all of you to participate actively in the course. Tuesdays will be primarily lecture days, with me introducing a key ISM topic. Then Fridays will be student-led discussions of relevant papers.  
\_Student-led discussion format\_: For each discussion, we will read and discuss two papers together, with one classical paper (loosely defined as papers published before 2000) and one more recent paper. For each paper, each student will sign up as either a discussion leader or reader. Discussion leaders will prepare a short overview of the paper to present in class, and will lead the discussion; readers will read the paper, write a paragraph to summarize the paper, and submit a few questions for class discussion in writing, due ahead of time.  
\- Each undergraduate student is expected to serve as the discussion leader at least once  
\- Each graduate student is expected to serve as the discussion leader at least two times  
\_My expectation\_: This course is not only about learning an exciting Astronomy subject, it's also about acquiring necessary research skills such as reading literature, asking questions, and engaging in respectful, professional, and collegial discussion with your classmates (and your future colleagues). I expect everyone to contributes to a supportive, intellectually curious learning atmosphere.

### Goals:

We will explore a wide range of physics and interstellar phenomena together in this course. ## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- At the end of the course, the students should be able to: - describe the phases of the ISM and what observational techniques let us probe each phase - sketch a thermal phase diagram and explain the concept of stable and unstable equilibria - describe how dust affects ISM physics, including heating and chemistry, and its effect on light at different wavelengths - sketch the spectral energy distribution of the interstellar radiation field near the Sun and explain the emission mechanisms that dominate at different wavelengths - describe our current understanding of star formation and what open questions remain
- The students will also practice a number of key skills. These are skills that aren’t perfected in one semester; we will all continue to improve these skills over the course of our careers. By the end of the semester, you will have practiced: - Reading papers and recognizing how they fit into the landscape of the field - Asking questions and discussing new material with colleagues

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students' performance will be measured based on four categories: discussion leadership, weekly reading summary and questions, two midterm exams, and final project presentation. Details on how each category is evaluated can be found in Grading Criteria.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

\*\*Discussion leadership: 20%\*\*  
\- Undergraduate students: 20% for leading one discussion  
\- Graduate students: 20% for leading two discussions (10% each)  
\*\*Weekly Friday reading summary and questions: 20%\*\*  
\- There will be 10 Fridays that we have paper discussions (2% for each paper discussion task). Discussion leader automatically get the 2% and will be graded based on their discussion leadership. Students who aren't leading the discussion are expected to: (1) read the paper (2) write a summary of the paper (3) prepare a few questions and (4) submit the summary and the questions in PDF on LMS ahead of the discussion. \_Note\_: students will only receive 50% of the corresponding grade if they submit the reading assignment to LMS but do not attend the discussion.  
\*\*Two Midterm Exams: 40%\*\*  
\- There will be two midterm exam (20% each), and no final exam.  
\*\*Final project and presentation: 20%\*\*  
\- This course will culminate in individual final projects: your chance to “go deep” on a topic of your choosing. Final projects will consist of a written document or website and a 15+5 minute presentation to your classmates (15 minute talk followed by 5 minutes of questions). I expect everyone to be an active and encouraging “conference attendee” during these presentations.  
\*\*Total: 20% + 20% + 40% + 20% = 100%\*\*

\*\*Policy on the use of artificial intelligence (AI)\*\*  
You are allowed to use AI in this course to help you understand the lectures, reading assignments, and projects.  
\- \_Disclosure Requirements\_: whenever you use AI in this course, you are required to disclose it by: (1) taking screenshots of the prompts you enter in AI chat bots and its corresponding answers and submitting the screenshots together with your homework assignments/projects, \_and\_ (2) you \_must\_ include statements in your write-ups such as "I have used XX AI (specify which one) to help me understand this reading/project assignment and identify potential solutions. My final answer to the question is based on my own understanding and critical thinking of the matter with AI assistance."  
\- \*Consequences for non-disclosure\*: failure to disclose the use of AI in your work related to this course will immediately result in a grade of zero in that corresponding homework assignment or project.
