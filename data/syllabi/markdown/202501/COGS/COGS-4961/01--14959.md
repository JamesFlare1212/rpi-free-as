## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

In this course students will:

1. advance their knowledge of a foreign language of their choice through independent study
2. learn strategies for lifelong language learning informed by linguistics, psychology, and the offerings of modern technology
3. gain experience in independently setting goals and planning a strategy to achieve them
4. learn about scientific topics related to foreign languages and language learning, such as language typology, age effects on language learning, cognitive consequences of multilingualism, generative grammar, construction grammar, cultural pragmatics, neo-Whorfianism, literary translation, machine translation, and endangered languages
5. improve their communication skills (the course is CI).

Students will be expected to spend a minimum of 5 hours a week on this course, divided between class time and independent study.

- Class meetings:
  - One full-period meeting every two weeks that will include lecture, discussion, and writing exercises.
  - One small-group session every two weeks (30 min.) to report progress.
- Independent study:
  - The minimal time requirement is 5 hrs. minus the time spent that week in class:
    - on lecture weeks, 3 hrs. 10 min. of independent study
    - on small-group weeks, 4 hrs. 30 min. of independent study
  - The minimum study time must be spread almost evenly across the week; otherwise, you won’t make progress and you won’t get credit for the time spent.

This course is Communication Intensive (CI).

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Scientific topics that will be treated outside of language learning include:

- language typology and universals
- generative grammar
- construction grammar
- neo-Whorfianism
- human and machine translation
- cognitive effects of multilingualism

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will make progress in learning the language of their choice.
- Students will gain practice in self-guided study, including selecting appropriate materials, creating weekly objectives, fulfilling those objectives, and reporting progress.
- Students will learn about, and do CI-oriented exercises involving, scientific topics related to linguistics and foreign language learning.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The course grade will be computed as follows:

- 40%: time spent on the course, as described above. Time spent must be supported by appropriate progress as judged by me at scheduled class meetings, otherwise it doesn’t count. For most students, progress reports are likely to include lists of vocabulary that the student will be tested on.
- 5%: successful selection of material and organization of the work plan; good judgment about replanning if there is a need for that.
- 5%: consistency of work throughout the semester: no excuses or procrastination.
- 25%: attendance at lectures and performance on class assignments related to the scientific literature portion of the course.
- 25%: all work in small groups: participation, presentations, discussion.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
