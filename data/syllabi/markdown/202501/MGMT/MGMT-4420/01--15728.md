## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The Student Managed Investment Fund course will actively invest hypothetical capital using financial market principles. This semester the experience in this course will be done as a practicum style format, not investing actual RPI capital funds. While the use of hypothetical capital is obviously not the same, the goal of the fund is still to produce excess returns consistent with the performance of equity/fixed income portfolio through the use of fundamental, technical, and quantitative strategies driven by economic fundamentals. In light of the fact that real capital is not at stake, the account will be given a slightly different degree of latitude to operate than it otherwise would have (in moderation). For example, in addition to a traditional long-term value approach, the account may selectively use leverage, employ basic options strategies, actively trade, or include futures, commodities or international investments subject to predetermined risk parameters. With that being said, the portfolio will employ primarily exchange traded funds (ETFs) as the relevant investment vehicles of choice. The goal of the course is to directly apply, in a real-world environment, the analytical financial skills developed within other Lally courses and to provide students with both instructor and peer real-time feedback for their work.

Students will be actively engaged in discussion of economic events, market developments, investment strategies, and portfolio risk in every class session. The course will begin with collaborative development and/or revision of an investment policy statement (IPS). Students will subsequently analyze out-of-sample effectiveness of established investment strategies using financial market data. Following fund startup, each week students will fulfill specific fund roles and prepare a slide deck for presentation to the fund team.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

*The Student Managed Investment Fund* course aims to provide students with practical, albeit hypothetical, experience as part of an investment management operation. The goal of the fund is to produce superior risk adjusted returns to its benchmark, specified in its Investment Policy Statement (IPS). The main objectives of the Student Managed Investment Fund course are:

- 1\) Produce, Review, and Approve an Investment Policy Statement (IPS)
  - Students will produce an IPS that describes allowable holdings, asset allocation, risk/return expectations, and other constraints of the fund. The IPS must be approved by the instructor. Preparation of an IPS provides an essential framework for investment activity through the course.
- 2\) Discuss Portfolio Implications of Ongoing Developments in Capital Markets
  - Every week, student(s) will present a summary of recent financial market events with a presentation slide deck. Each student will add his or her own analysis and items of interested related to economics, markets, and quantitative methods. The Market Developments role will also summarize the week’s episode of Bloomberg ETF IQ to incorporate additional current trends in ETF markets. These discussions allow students to share ideas, learn more about the relationship between economics and security prices, and enhance communication skills. Communications will occur in-between class meetings via the WebEx teams.
- 3\) Develop Investment Strategies / Produce Idea Generation
  - All students will initially analyze out-of-sample performance of investment strategies (e.g., asset allocation, individual securities, momentum, post earnings announcement drift, etc.) and present to peers for potential portfolio inclusion within one month of the start of the course. All investment pitches will provide recommendations for timely, relevant ideas considering rationale, pros and cons, position size, risk controls, and overall portfolio appropriateness.
- 4\) Portfolio Decisions
  - All pitches will be voted on by the class for portfolio inclusion. Pros and cons of each position will be discussed in class before anonymous voting occurs. Voting students will have to provide a brief rationale for their decisions. Performance of all investment ideas (both invested and uninvested to examine opportunity costs) will be tracked. Investment strategy proposals must be scheduled for the class date prior to the strategy presentation.
- 5\) Manage Portfolio Composition and Risk
  - In every class meeting, members will monitor portfolio composition and market activity throughout the course and prepare specific metrics (e.g., attribution analysis, historical and estimated volatility, net systematic risk, etc.) for weekly discussion. Risk managers will also establish risk thresholds for which, when triggered, positions are automatically subject to mandatory reevaluation by the students. This provides students an opportunity to apply risk management principles to hypothetical capital portfolios.
- 6\) Communicate Investment Strategies / Portfolio Decisions with Advisory Board
  - All members of the class will contribute to Investor Relations and will prepare periodic (monthly) electronic communications for consideration by the fund’s advisory board. These reports will include information about portfolio holdings, risk, proposed strategies, performance analysis, and future outlook for markets. Depending on advisory board availability, the IR team will give a brief presentation to the board describing operations of the Student Managed Investment Fund. The advisory board provides both capital and guidance for the fund: students will function as fiduciaries to the board and incorporate their feedback within subsequent portfolio decisions.
- 7\) Prepare Portfolio for Inactive Period
  - Before the course ends, students will vote on strategies to prepare the fund’s holdings for a period of passive management (i.e., without active student involvement). Students will prepare all developed strategy information, risk models, and other documentation for transition to the next student management team.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- The goal of the course is to give students practical investment management experience with hypothetical capital and allow students to use skills developed in other courses in a practical setting. Undergraduate students will develop applied skills in security analysis, behavioral bias management, portfolio management, business knowledge, data analytics, problem solving, critical thinking, leadership, teamwork, diversity and effective business communication. Roles will rotate weekly (among market developments, strategy generation, and portfolio risk management in addition to investor relations responsibilities) and will also be allocated on both a volunteer basis and the needs of the fund for personnel in each role. Graduate students will expand on the above learning outcomes using more advanced quantitative methods. Rather than using simulation and historical data alone, students will further develop and refine critical skills for financial market analysis in real-time including quantitative financial modeling, dynamic asset/sector allocation strategies, and time series analysis. Students may also employ programming to assist them in their research process and overall analysis. Graduate students will primarily manage the quantitative team and portfolio analytics.
