## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The purpose of this course it to give students an in depth, conceptual understanding of entrepreneurship and the entrepreneurial process. Entrepreneurship fundamentally differs from traditional strategic planning processes employed in large established corporations. This course gives you a flavor of what it is like to start your own business and the multitude of challenges you will face, especially at early stages, as you nurture and scale it into a full-fledged company.

The course’s objective is to equip you with tools and frameworks that will enable you to make better decisions as an entrepreneur. We will be dealing with aspects such as:

- What is entrepreneurship?
- The entrepreneurial mindset and way of thinking
- Understanding what makes for a good and viable opportunity
- The role of business plans
- The Lean Startup approach
- The importance of business models
- Issues involved in raising capital
- Marketing challenges in entrepreneurial ventures
- Challenges in scaling up a venture
- Harvesting the venture, and exit

Entrepreneurship is inherently a multidisciplinary endeavor. Hence we will be drawing on theories and perspectives from a variety of disciplines, including strategy, leadership, innovation, marketing and finance.

In today’s world all managers are required to think entrepreneurially. Hence this course will be relevant not just for students who are considering becoming entrepreneurs themselves, but also for those of you who intend to work for large organizations, consulting firms, venture capital/private equity, or any other entity dealing with new businesses.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Learning Objectives for MGMT 4520:**

On completion of this class, 4520 students should be able to:

1. Define entrepreneurship and differentiate it from traditional strategic planning.
2. Apply conceptual frameworks to identify and evaluate entrepreneurial ventures.
3. Develop business plans and utilize the Lean Startup approach to develop entrepreneurial strategies.
4. Analyze the challenges involved in raising capital for entrepreneurial ventures, and identify appropriate sources of capital.
5. Evaluate marketing challenges specific to early-stage ventures and propose solutions.
6. Present and discuss entrepreneurial concepts with clarity and persuasiveness.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Main Case- Report and Presentation**

Students will be divided into groups of 3 to 5 members. Each team will be assigned a case to analyze. For this assignment the group will be responsible for preparing a written report and making a formal presentation of their analysis and recommendations in class. Broadly speaking, your presentation will cover the background of the case, the issues identified (see the discussion questions for each case outlined below in the syllabus), recommendations for the future, and any other points that you think are relevant.

Each team will prepare a report that is no longer than *12 pages* (please see the guidelines for written reports below). The in class presentation will be for a maximum of *20-25 minutes* (guidelines are also provided below for presentations). The written report is due the day the case is scheduled to be presented. Late submissions will not be accepted.

Note- The cases discussed in class are designed to illustrate specific concepts and decision scenarios. Please therefore do not look up any online material or ‘solutions’ for the case, including ChatGPT or other LLMs. Any attempts to do so will be treated as a serious ethical violation, and in the minimum equivalent to a first offense (see below, and also see LLM policy).

**Brief Case Report**

In addition to the main case, each group will be assigned a second case to analyze. In this report (maximum of 3 pages, excluding the cover page), you are required to identify the central issues in the case, develop a brief analysis and justification, and provide your main recommendations for the future. ***The brief report is due the day the case is scheduled to be presented, and should be submitted to me ahead of class***. Late submissions will not be accepted, and failure to turn in a report on the due date will lead to a lower grade.

*For 6620 students the expectation is that the case analysis report and presentation and the brief report will be more nuanced, and will synthesize the case data and information to be more analytically rigorous.*

**Venture Concept Project**

The Venture Concept Project (VCP) is a semester long project involving your group and you taking an opportunity and developing it into a full fledged company. The project requires you to think through the various challenges involved as you develop your venture up to a point where it is viable and self-sustaining.

As a first step in the project, your team and you will pitch the ideas you have in mind to the class and obtain initial feedback. These pitches will take place on January 24th and January 28th. Given the short time frame, it is critical that you start thinking about what you want to work on as a group as soon as possible.

Once having identified an attractive idea, over the rest of the semester you will build an organizational, financial, marketing, and operating plan around it. The VCP will provide an opportunity to apply various frameworks and concepts we cover in class in a systematic manner. It will thus touch upon all the aspects that are core to the class, including those identified on page 1 and beyond.

In addition to the initial pitch, the VCP has three main deliverables: (1) A set of brief presentations outlining your key hypotheses and your Minimum Viable Product on February 11th (2) An interim report and presentation due on 25th Feb/ 28th Feb (2) A final presentation due on April 18th / April 22nd , and a final report due during the final exam period. Each report will be a maximum of *15 pages* (this report will be single spaced) excluding appendices. Each presentation will be for a maximum of *15 minutes* (see guidelines for reports and presentations below).

The interim report and presentation for the VCP will develop the idea and opportunity in greater detail following the initial pitch and MVP presentations. You will address issues such as the nature of the opportunity, the value proposition and customer need being satisfied, the product concept, the strategy of your company (i.e. how you intend to differentiate yourself and the capabilities you will develop), the state of competition, the strengths of the management team, and your business model.

The final report and presentation will build on these aspects and cover marketing, finance, operating and organizational issues. Here you will identify critical success factors, your approximate cash needs, your key operating milestones and timelines, your internal organizational structure etc.

As part of the VCP, you will be *required* to talk to 3-*6 people* outside class (no family members!) and obtain feedback on the viability of the business concept. Ideally you should talk to potential customers, but you should not be limited to them. You should try and meet with industry experts and organizations, competitors, distributors, investors, product and service designers, scientists, suppliers, etc. *Please prepare a summary of the interviews you conduct and what you have learnt and include it in the appendix as part of your first or second deliverable***.**

Note: To develop a strong foundation for the assignment, please start going through the following:

- Note in the HBS course pack titled “Writing a Business Plan: The Basics”
- “How to Write a Great Business Plan” by W.A. Sahlman (on LMS)
- “Updating a Classic: Writing a Great Business Plan” https://hbswk.hbs.edu/item/updating-a-classic-writing-a-great-business-plan
- Watch the video: https://www.ycombinator.com/library/8g-how-to-get-startup-ideas

The VCP requires consistent work throughout the semester with substantial input from all team members. The sooner you start working on the project, the better.

*For 6620 students the expectation is that all aspects of the project will be developed more in depth and rigorously, reflecting the student’s ability to take on leadership and mentorship roles in an entrepreneurial venture.*

**Retrospective Analysis Report**

This report requires you to identify *three failed entrepreneurial ventures* and analyze them in detail *(for 6620 students **four failures and one success**).* The entrepreneurial landscape is littered with failures. Yet, much of the focus of the popular press, academics, and students of entrepreneurship tends to be on success stories. This paints a vitiated picture of the entrepreneurship process and prevents us from fully appreciating its complexities and challenges.

While the ventures should preferably be startups, I am willing to consider entrepreneurial failures/examples by large, established corporations. The project requires you to identify the root causes of failure (or success) and analyze them in depth, while relating them to the ideas covered in class.

The Retrospective Analysis report will be due in class on Tuesday, 8th April, 12:00 PM. The report will be a maximum of 10 pages (12 pages for 6620 students; see guidelines for reports below).

*For 6620 students the expectation is that the retrospective analysis report will link each failure and success clearly and in depth to class concepts to identify the underlying causes.*

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and the Graduate Student Supplement (For 6000 level and above courses) define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.

In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In instances where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in (1) an academic (grade) penalty and (2) reporting to Lally’s Associate Dean of Academic Affairs and either the Dean of Students (for Undergraduates) or the Dean of Graduate Education (for Graduate students).

**In this course, the academic penalty for a first offense as assessed by the instructor is 30% of the overall course grade*.* A second offense will result in failure of the course as noted in Lally’s Three Strikes Policy.** If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

Note also that during exams etc., no unauthorized of resources is permitted that can aid in your taking the exam (e.g. using LMS to determine correct answers). Doing so will result in a failing grade on the exam, and you may be awarded zero points. If you have any doubt about this policy, please check, as the final decision on whether you have used appropriate resources rests with me.

**LLM Policy**

You are encouraged to use AI tools such as LLMs to supplement your learning in class. LLMs can be a powerful tool for researching entrepreneurial opportunities, industries, and technologies. However they can also be wrong, and in many instances they can be superficial and not in depth enough. Hence you should critically examine and question whatever information they produce.

In this class, you may use LLMs to conduct independent research as a first step. Beyond that, all submitted assignment material, such as class slides and written reports, should be your own intellectual contribution, and should be developed by you. If you use LLMs for this purpose (e.g. cutting and pasting LLM outputs etc. in a presentation or report without substantial input and modification as determined by me), it will constitute a violation of academic integrity policies, and will count in the minimum as one strike .

Note also that you are *explicitly prohibited* from using Chat GPT or any other LLM to solve case questions, or obtain information pertaining to the cases assigned in class. Doing so, again, constitutes a violation of academy integrity policies, and would count in the minimum as a strike.
