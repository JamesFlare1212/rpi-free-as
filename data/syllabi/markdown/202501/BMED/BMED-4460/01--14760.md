## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Identify key problems in biological image analysis (e.g., noise reduction, segmentation, feature extraction) and formulate algorithmic solutions by applying principles from mathematics, science, and engineering.
- Develop and implement image analysis pipelines that meet specified needs in biomedical applications, integrating considerations such as scalability, reproducibility, and computational efficiency.
- Select and use appropriate software libraries (e.g., NumPy, OpenCV, scikit-image, PyTorch) and AI/ML methods (e.g., convolutional neural networks, transformers) to solve complex imaging problems, demonstrating competency in programming and algorithmic thinking.
- Conduct quantitative analysis of biological images, interpret the results (e.g., assessing segmentation accuracy or classification metrics), and justify conclusions based on statistical and visual evidence.
- Present findings and methodologies clearly, both orally and in writing, to diverse audiences (scientific or lay), using proper visual aids and scientific documentation practices.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Grading – 85% (for both 4000 and 6000 levels):**

The final grade in this course will be based on the student’s total score on all components of the course. The total score is broken down into the following components:

1. Homework assignments (35% of grade): 7 homework assignments (due as assigned) that will include coding activities in Python. Each homework submission needs to be submitted via LMS on or before the due date (which is typically one week after the homework assignment was made available). ***Late submissions will lead to point reduction of 1 point/day after the due date*** unless a valid and verified excuse can be produced.
2. Test I (15% of grade): The test is scheduled for ***Friday,* *February 21, 2025, during class time for a duration of 80 minutes.***Without a valid excuse, the missed test will be graded as zero.
3. Test II (15% of grade): This test is scheduled for ***Friday, April 18, 2025, during class time for a duration of 80 minutes.***Without a valid excuse, the missed test will be graded as zero.
4. Group project (20% of grade): focuses on the application of image analysis methods to a real-world challenge problem. Students will be working in small groups, and the project will be organized into various phases. The grade will be based on the progress and results in a series of presentations and the final report.

**Grading – 15% (6000 level):**

To attain the remaining 15% score, 6000 level students will prepare and deliver an in-depth technical presentation on selected research topics (30 minutes presentation with 10 minutes Q&A) and collaborate with 4000 level students to present a poster. Dr. Yan will help guide the development of these presentations. A list of topics will be provided to the students. The presentation will be graded by the TA and Dr. Yan.

**Grading – 15% (4000 level):**

To attain the remaining 15% score, 4000 level students will make poster presentation of a paper covered by the corresponding 6000 level presentation and answer questions from the audience. The poster presentation will be graded by the TA and Dr. Yan.
