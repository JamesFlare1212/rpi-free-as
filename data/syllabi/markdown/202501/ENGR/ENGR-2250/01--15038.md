## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Application of control volume balances of mass, momentum, energy, and entropy in systems of practical importance to all engineers. Identification of control volumes, properties of pure materials, mass and energy conservation for closed and open systems, second law of thermodynamics, Bernoulli equation, fluid statics, forces and heat transfer in external and internal flows, conduction, and radiative heat transfer.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

D.A. Kaminski, and M.K. Jensen, Introduction to Thermal and Fluids Engineering, Wiley (2005/2011)

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1 - Explain and apply the fundamentals of thermodynamics, heat transfer, and fluid mechanics (e.g. conservation of mass, momentum, and energy, etc.) to types of problems commonly encountered by a practicing engineer.
- 2 - Advance from a solid foundation to more in-depth courses in this field (e.g. Aeronautical, Environmental, Materials, Mechanical, and Nuclear Engineering).
- 3 - Be knowledgeable and conversant with the field of thermal fluids (e.g. Biomedical, Civil, Computer and Systems, Electric Power, Electrical, and Industrial and Management Engineering).
- 4 - Acquire skills in analysing and solving engineering problems.

### Other Course Policies:

**Learning Management System (LMS)**

An LMS page has been created for the course. If you are registered for the course, you should have been added automatically. Students should verify that they have access to the page. **Announcements, quizzes, homework, and lectures will be posted to the LMS page.**

**Homework**

Homework problem sets will be assigned on the course LMS site. The problem sets are to be done individually. Each worked problem should contain a brief problem statement and the corresponding solution. Solutions should be annotated with diagrams and easy to follow. Units should always be included. Mathematic equations without any explanation will receive limited credit. Illegible and late homework will not be accepted. Problems should be typed, or neatly written and scanned, and submitted on the course LMS as a .pdf file. The document should be named using the following convention:

*HW#_rcsid.pdf*

For example, homework eleven by John Smith: HW11_smithj123.pdf

**Quizzes**

Multiple-choice quizzes will be given to review concepts introduced in lecture and reflect the most recent homework assignments. Quizzes are ungraded and are used to gauge class comprehension only. Quizzes are administered though Webex.

**Term Assessments**

Three equally weighted at-home assessments will be offered throughout the semester. The final exam will be a student-created problem set due at the close of the semester. Details on the assessments are provided on the course LMS.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

This statement is intended to cover most, but not all, forms of academic dishonesty. (You should refer to *The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* for detailed information on Institute policy in this regard.)

Homework:

Collaboration among the students in the class is permitted and, in fact, encouraged. Seeking help from other sources is also encouraged. However, the work you submit should be your own. **Students found to have copied any part of their homework from a solution manual or from an online database will be considered in breach of academic honesty and will result in a zero grade for homework assignment**.

Exams:

Use of Chegg.com or similar online services to obtain solutions to exam problems is explicitly prohibited. The instructor and TA will monitor these services during the exams; evidence of use of one of these products will **be considered in breach of academic honesty** and will result in **a failing grade for the course** and a recommendation to the Dean of Students that **the student(s) in question be suspended**. Furthermore, collaboration during an exam (exchanging verbal or written information or copying via use of email or chat) is prohibited.

Other:

Outrageous offenses such as arranging for a substitute to take an examination, attempting to obtain examination questions or solutions in advance, or attempting to change a grade record will result in a failing grade for the course and a recommendation to the Dean of Students that the student(s) in question be suspended. If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
