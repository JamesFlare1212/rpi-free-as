## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will demonstrate knowledge of creative nonfiction writing in general and the subgenres in particular by utilizing appropriate terminology to analyze and discuss published essays.
- Students will establish a consistent creative practice by regularly producing short creative writings.
- Students will demonstrate engagement in the writing process through observing, drafting, peer critique, and revision.
- Students will actively engage in a creative community of peers through participation in workshop and discussion.
- Students will synthesize learning outcomes by constructing a learning portfolio that showcases their skills and development in the field of creative nonfiction writing over the course of the semester.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

This is a small seminar class, the success of which is entirely dependent on active student participation. Therefore, I expect all students to participate to the extent of their abilities. Everyone has two “freebie” absences to use for sleep, self-care, or other obligations without point penalty, and for this reason, I don’t need official excusals. Otherwise, I expect that you will come to class every class (school athletes see me at the beginning of the term), barring extended illness. Each subsequent absence beyond the first one will drop your grade by ten points (1%). If you miss more than five classes total, you will receive no credit for the course even if you are otherwise passing. Students who need medical accommodations to allow for a flexible attendance policy may obtain this from the Office of Disability Services.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

All assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work completed for this class in particular. If you have a compelling reason for wanting to use previous work of yours for the class, it must be substantially and fundamentally revised, and you must get approval from the instructor first.

Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty of a zero for the assignment and the potential to fail the course. Second offenses will result in automatic failure of the course and the potential for disciplinary action by the institution.

If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
