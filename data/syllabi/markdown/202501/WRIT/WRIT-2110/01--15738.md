## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

*“The function of writing is more than to tell it like it is—it is to imagine what is possible.”*

*-bell hooks*

This course helps students learn to write effectively, convincingly, and appropriately for a variety of situations, tasks, and goals. Each week you will be presented with a different rhetorical strategy to master. We will engage each strategy by examining works crafted by esteemed writers who utilize specific modes of writing. You will practice and hone these strategies through assignments to be completed both in and outside of class. Collaborative discussion and small group work will be essential, assuring that you receive adequate feedback and that you implement your strategies in ways that convince and/or communicate meaning to an audience or team. In completing oral and written assignments, you will demonstrate competence in engaging one or more strategies, assuring you are prepared to write effectively and carefully throughout your college career and beyond.

### Goals:

**Course Goals/Objectives:** This course will teach students to compose written texts using a variety of different rhetorical strategies appropriate for both professional and academic contexts. **Note: This course is designated as Communication Intensive.**

## Course Content

### Content Details:

the cause and effect essay, argumentation, compare & contrast method, defining, describing, using dialogue, narrating, explaining processes, drafting & revision, presentation and convincing an in-person audience

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Comprehend and Inquire: Show an understanding and recognition of a plethora of effective means of strategic modes and be able to respond through meaningful critical inquiry, formulating both questions and answers.
- Assess different writing tasks and plan your approach: Craft written texts for a variety of different situations, with attention to the preparation process which may include free-writing, brainstorming, assessing sources, outlining, drafting, and revising according to peer and instructor feedback. Learn to take criticism and revise for a variety of concerns, not limited to sophistication, clarity, and style.
- Engage diverse writing modes: Learn to identify and implement specific writing strategies to effectively communicate your ideas and/or findings to an audience.
- Organize your written and oral work: Present and compose texts that effectively argue, compare, define, describe or analyze in a thorough, convincing manner, and in a way that considers tone, voice, genre, and style. Overall, learn to communicate effectively and appropriately, in a way that is appropriate for the situation and audience.
- Create your own texts/arguments and hone your scholarly voice: Through practice, class work, assignments, and reflection, you will hone your written and oral performance, understanding how to approach assignments, gather and analyze research materials and/or evidence, synthesize materials, and finally composing your written texts using one or more rhetorical strategies with confidence.
- Explain your strategies: Reflect on your own writing in a way that shows an understanding of your topic, anticipating questions, concerns, or issues that may arise from an audience, group, or team.
- Engage diverse writing modes: Learn to identify and implement specific writing strategies to effectively communicate your ideas and/or findings to an audience.

**Participation:** There is a participation grade for this course and to receive full credit for each class meeting, you must be prepared for class. Specifically, to get full participation points each class you will need to:

-have your course materials ready: Pen and paper are required, as well as a copy of that week’s assigned readings, whether in digital or hard copy.

-be prepared for class having read all assigned readings.

-share verbally if I ask for comments on in-class work from everyone.

-turn in any in-class written assignments that I ask to collect at the end of class.

-not spend class time chatting, working on anything not related to this class, or using technology such as laptop or phone for personal or recreational reasons.

**Essay in Miniature:** This will be a short critical writing assignment (3-5 pages) that explores one’s experience of a problem, idea, concept, question, phenomenon, or issue. Major assignments (such as the essay in miniature, the course research paper, or the oral presentation) will receive a **grade deduction of 10% per day when turned in after the due date.**

**Course Research Paper:** The capstone writing project for the course will be a research paper where you analyze an issue, perform research and synthesize your findings, form an opinion, and argue a specific point (8-10 pages). You will write to participate in a conversation regarding a relevant question or issue. Major assignments (such as the essay in miniature, the course research paper, or the oral presentation) will receive a **grade deduction of 10% per day when turned in after a due date.**

**Weekly Strategy Practice: due most Mondays:** Every week I will present a task in class. These must be completed by the following Monday before the beginning of class, typed, and turned in on LMS. ***Late entries will receive up to 50% credit but will not be accepted after a week beyond the due date.***

**Preparation Assignments: due most Thursdays:** A research paper cannot be completed well in a day or two. Thus, the preparation assignments will assure you are well-prepared and pacing yourself, progressing on the steps needed to build throughout the semester to the finished product. Preparation assignments should be typed and turned in on LMS. ***Late entries will receive up to 50% credit but will not be accepted after a week beyond the due date.***

**Oral Presentation:** The presentation will be based on your final paper research. You will present your findings to your peers, persuading them of your argument. (Students must be prepared to present on the first day of presentations although not all will be able to present on the first day.) **Not being ready to present will be counted as lateness and will receive a 10% grade deduction for class days attended unprepared.**

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance Policy: Attendance is mandatory** as well as an important factor for your success in this course. All students are granted **four absences** without penalty, no questions asked. This does not mean that you should plan on missing four classes; Missing classes means that you do not benefit from valuable class discussions and lectures, placing your learning experience at a disadvantage. Rather, the absence allowance offers students leniency in case of illness or personal matters that arise unexpectedly. Fifth and sixth absences will result in a deduction of five points from your final grade each time. **Missing more than six classes will result in failure of the course.** If you miss more than six classes, you should drop or withdraw from the course to avoid a failing grade on your transcript. If you withdraw from the course, I suggest meeting with your academic advisor to discuss your next steps. If you add the class after the semester begins, your missed classes will not count as absences, but you will need to contact me to agree on due dates for missed work, and failure to meet these due dates will result in a lateness deduction as documented on the syllabus.

Some things to keep in mind:

\- While I ask that you communicate with me and let me know if you must miss class, you are not required to bring me medical documentation, photos, etc. pertaining to absences.

-Even excused absences count toward the four allowed.

-Missing a significant amount of a class meeting will also result in an absence. (This includes arriving late, leaving early, skipping group work, not completing an in-class assignment, etc.)

-It is your responsibility to ensure that you have been noted as present on my roster each day. If you come in after I have taken attendance or passed around an attendance sheet, you need to let me know at the end of class that you were present for the day.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:** If you are not yet aware at this point in your college career, academic dishonesty, including plagiarism, intentional and unintentional, is a serious offense at the college level and can result in your dismissal from the university. **All submitted work must be your own.** Words or ideas from any source (including but not limited to lecture, print, or digital mediums) must be properly cited. **Written work generated by AI and turned in as your own also counts as plagiarism.** This includes Chat GTP. DO not use ChatGTP or any other AI program to generate ideas, formulate or generate text, or reword your own work. **You are not permitted to use ChatGTP for this course in any capacity.** Changing phrases from a source and putting them in your own words also needs to be cited. No one may complete in part or in entirety any of your class assignments besides you. Plagiarism will be reported to the Office of Judicial Affairs and will lead to a failing grade on the assignment in question or the course.
