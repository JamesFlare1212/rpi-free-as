## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Description:**

*“*Our language can be seen as an ancient city: a maze of little streets and squares, of old and new houses, and of houses with additions from previous periods; and this surrounded by a multitude of new boroughs with straight regular streets and uniform houses.”

— *Ludwig Wittgenstein*

Learning to use language as a social and academic tool—in both its written and spoken forms—is the chief concern of this class. Through reading, drafting, collaborating, revising, asking questions, and of course practicing, this class is designed to help you hone your writing skills for a variety of different contexts, genres, and audiences.

The course centers on the theme of animal studies and will include readings about animals from a diverse array of disciplines and genres. Animals’ roles in our lives are widespread. Animals serve as pets, livestock, test subjects, food, and companions, but they have also served philosophical roles as cultural symbols and literary characters. Thus, this topic allows for many different avenues of thought and inquiry. After exploring how different disciplines and thinkers have approached and written about animals, you will be ready to compose your own written texts on the subject and enter the scholarly conversation yourself.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Analyze and inquire: Show an understanding and recognition of various writing forms used in different contexts, and practice responding through meaningful critical inquiry, formulating both questions and answers.
- Engage diverse writing modes: Learn to identify and craft an appropriate response corresponding with specific tasks.
- Navigate between different genres and purposes for your writing, and in ways that are appropriate for a variety of situations and audiences.
- Learn to identify and implement specific writing and rhetorical strategies to effectively communicate your ideas and/or findings to an audience.
- Organize your written and oral work: Present and compose texts that effectively consider grammar, tone, voice, genre, and style.
- Create your own texts/arguments and hone your scholarly voice: Through practice, class work, assignments, and reflection, you will hone your written and oral performance, understanding how to approach writing assignments, analyze texts through writing, gather and analyze research materials and/or evidence, synthesize materials, and finally compose written work within a specific context.
- Explain your writing context: Reflect on your own writing in a way that shows an understanding of your writing process.
- Assess various rhetorical situations, predict counterarguments, and compose discipline-based writing tasks.

**Analytical Essay:** This Analytical Essay will be an opportunity for you to close read, form your opinions on a course text, and then show how those points work (5-6 pages). Students will need to validate their reading of the text in the broader context of human-animal studies. The Analytical Essay will receive a **grade deduction of 10% per day when turned in after the due date.**

**Research Essay:** The second major writing project for the course will be a research paper where you perform research and synthesize your findings, form an opinion, and argue a specific point regarding your analysis of a course text (5-6 pages). You will write to participate in a scholarly conversation surrounding the topic of human-animal studies, in the context of your text. The Research Essay will receive a **grade deduction of 10% per day when turned in after the due date.**

**Oral Presentation:** The presentation will be based on your research essay. You will present your findings to your peers, persuading them of your argument. (Students must be prepared to present on the first day of presentations although not all will be able to present on the first day.) **Not being ready to present will be counted as lateness and will receive a 10% grade deduction for class days attended unprepared.**

**Weekly Tasks:** Every week I will present a task in class that will usually be due before the following Monday class. These must be typed and turned in on LMS. ***Late entries will receive up to 50% credit but will not be accepted after a week beyond the due date. The rubric for weekly tasks is copied below:***

4 points: The task demonstrates exceptional written work that is clear, concise, and shows critical thinking, with minimal to no grammar mistakes or typos, and is completely finished.

3 points: The task is completely finished and demonstrates adequate written work that is fairly concise and shows adequate critical thinking that is good but could be developed or polished further.

2 points: The task is only partially complete, and/or needs more work to reach adequate or excellent style, level of critical thinking, and full development. This score also applies to work that is well-developed but is difficult to understand due to typos, grammar issues, and poor diction.

1 point: The task is lacking in style, development, grammar, and sophistication. This score may also apply to a task that does not follow directions properly or completely.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance Policy: Attendance is mandatory** as well as an important factor for your success in this course. All students are granted **four absences** (excused or unexcused) without penalty, no questions asked. This does not mean that you should plan on missing four classes; Missing classes means that you do not benefit from valuable class discussions and lectures, placing your learning experience at a disadvantage. Rather, the absence allowance offers students leniency in case of illness or personal matters that arise unexpectedly. Fifth and sixth absences will result in a deduction of five points from your final grade each time. **Missing more than six classes will result in failure of the course.** If you miss more than six classes, you should drop or withdraw from the course to avoid a failing grade on your transcript. If you withdraw from the course, I suggest meeting with your academic advisor to discuss your next steps. If you add the class after the semester begins, your missed classes will not count as absences, but you will need to contact me to agree on due dates for missed work, and failure to meet these due dates will result in a lateness deduction as documented on the syllabus.

Some things to keep in mind:

\- While I ask that you communicate with me and let me know if you must miss class, you are not required to bring me medical documentation, photos, etc. pertaining to absences.

-Missing a significant amount of a class meeting will also result in an absence. (This includes arriving late, leaving early, skipping group work, not completing an in-class assignment, etc.)

-It is your responsibility to ensure that you have been noted as present on my roster each day. If you come in after I have taken attendance or passed around an attendance sheet, you need to let me know at the end of class that you were present for the day.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:** If you are not yet aware at this point in your college career, academic dishonesty, including plagiarism, intentional and unintentional, is a serious offense at the college level and can result in your dismissal from the university. **All submitted work must be your own.** Words or ideas from any source (including but not limited to lecture, print, or digital mediums) must be properly cited. **Written work generated by AI and turned in as your own also counts as plagiarism.** This includes Chat GTP. Do not use ChatGTP or any other AI program to generate ideas, formulate or generate text, or reword your own work. **You are not permitted to use ChatGTP for this course in any capacity.** Changing phrases from a source and putting them in your own words also needs to be cited. No one may complete in part or in entirety any of your class assignments besides you. Plagiarism will be reported to the Office of Judicial Affairs and will lead to a failing grade on the assignment in question or the course.
