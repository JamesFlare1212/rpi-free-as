## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Learning Outcome #1: Specialized/Industry Knowledge: students will be able to demonstrate the ability to utilize the knowledge of specialization of their major field of study: architecture.
- Learning Outcome #2: Broad and Integrative Knowledge: students will be able to demonstrate the ability to bring together learning from industry knowledge, experience, and/or different fields of study to discover and explore the implications of concepts and questions that bridge essential areas of learning/practice as well as integrate their knowledge to advance solutions in support of a humane, just, and democratic society.
- Learning Outcome #3: Civic/Democratic and Global Learning: students will be able to demonstrate the ability to recognize higher education’s responsibilities both to democracy and the global community. Students engage in integration of their knowledge and skills by addressing and responding to civic, social, environmental, economic, equity, inclusion, and social justice challenges at local, national, and global levels.
- Learning Outcome #4: Intellectual Skills: students will be able to demonstrate the ability to use information resources, engage diverse perspectives, reasoning, and communicative fluently.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Assessment Measure Descriptions:**

1. ***Floor Plans and Sections = 32 points***
2. ***Exploded Axonometric Drawings = 20 points***
3. ***Tectonic Models = 40 points***
4. ***Project Manifesto: 300 – 500 words = 8 points***

***Assessment Measure #1: Descriptive Drawing: Plans and Sections***

While it is assumed that three-dimensional computer modeling will occur through all phases of project development, two-dimensional orthographically projected floor plans, and sections will also be used instrumentally in design. These drawings MUST:

1. be built up from scratch – a three-dimensional model may be used as a reference underlay to help produce the drawing; however, it must not be included in the drawing.
2. explore and evaluate design decisions.
3. describe accurate floor, wall, and roof thicknesses.
4. describe primary horizontal and vertical spatial relationships
5. describe entry, circulation, rest, departure.

****(****Each of the 5 components equal 6 points, totaling 30 Points)*

***Assessment Measure #2: Exploded Axonometric Drawings***

Exploded axonometric drawings will be used to creatively analyze and study three-dimensional part to whole relationships in the projects.

***Assessment Measure #3: Physical Models***

Physical models must capture the primary three-dimensional spatial qualities of the project. Part to whole relationships must be evident in the constructive logic of the models.

***Assessment #4: Project Manifesto: 300 – 500 words***

The project text should serve to succinctly capture the overarching ideas of your design proposals.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Grading Criteria:**

1. You will receive one total grade at the end of the semester. You will receive a mid-semester evaluation from your professor, detailing your level of performance. Your final grade may be higher or lower than mid-semester projections, pending your subsequent level of performance. This Final grade will reflect your overall performance and progression over the span of the entire semester.

2. Weekly critical feedback and direction will be provided to the student through individual desk critiques as well as periodic group critiques and discussions. This weekly feedback is meant to keep each student aware of their overall level of development and achievement in the course. Students are encouraged to

request additional clarification of feedback if necessary.

3. The distribution, due dates, and deliverables of all projects and assignments may be

adjusted by your professor to reflect the overall or individual progress of work in the studio.

4. All deliverables represent the final basic minimum requirements for each project. Additional preliminary and / or subsequent drawings, models, photographs, and writings may be assigned by your professor.

5. At the end of the course, students will be asked to complete a self-assessment survey in which they will evaluate their own performance with respect to each assessment measure. Completing the Student Assessment at the end of the course is required, as it is essential for the school’s accreditation. Failing to complete this assessment will result in a 5-point deduction from the overall grade.

6. At the end of the course, select students will be required to submit all final digital and physical works to Publications. Publications will assign a liaison to the first-year studio; all selected students will be responsible for submitting work to the liaison. Publications will archive the digital and physical works and return physical works to the students. Failing to submit all work to Publications will result in a 5-point deduction from the overall grade. Additional submission requirements may be established by each instructor.

Grades will be established according to the following policy:

\- A (EXCELLENT: 100-94 points = A; 93-90 points = A-) Exceptional performance strongly exceeding the requirements of the course, showing strong academic initiative and independent resourcefulness.

\- B (GOOD: 89-87 points = B+; 86-84 points = B; 83-80 points = B-) Performance above the norm; accurate and complete; beyond the minimum requirements of the course; work demonstrates marked progress and initiative.

\- C (AVERAGE: 79-77 points = B+; 76-74 points = B; 73-70 points = B-) Satisfactory work that adequately meets minimum requirements and demonstrates satisfactory comprehension, communication skills, and effort; demonstrates little initiative to investigate the problem without substantial input from the instructor; work shows little improvement.

\- D (INFERIOR: 69-67 points = B+; 66-64 points = B; 63-60 points = B-) Unsatisfactorily meets minimum requirements; demonstrates minimum comprehension, communication skills, and effort at an inferior level; initiative lacking; improvement not noticeable. (D grades not available to graduate students).

\- F (FAIL: 59 points and below) Does not meet minimum requirements; fails to adequately demonstrate comprehension, communication skills, and effort.

Section critics will provide students with a mid-semester evaluation. Assessment measures will be evaluated at the midterm and/or final reviews.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own performance. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

**Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism**

Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of this course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student. Any miss-representation of work shall be evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student will result in a failing grade for the course.
