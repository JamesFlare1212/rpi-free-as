## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**CATALOG COURSE DESCRIPTION**

This studio is a technology-based design studio emphasizing the materialization and making of architectural design projects. The integration of building code requirements for fire protection, life safety, accessibility, building environmental systems, structure, construction, and materiality is central to the effective achievement of design intent. Students become aware of how these issues affect and inform design decisions. They learn to integrate technology, systems, and materials in the comprehensive resolution of building design and gain exposure to construction documents and design documentation. Construction and site visits are an integral part of the studio as is an integrated electronic media seminar on CAD applications. Students must co-register for ARCH 4540 Professional Practice 1, a concurrent 2-credit course that introduces codes, the regulatory process, agreements, contract documents, building design cost control, and administration.* This course is required of all architecture undergraduates in the B.Arch program.  
**PP1 replaced with Building Integration 2 in spring 2025, the BI2 course will cover regulatory, life safety issues and in addition, environmental, MEP, structures and construction systems, facade technologies in direct relation to the CDS2 project development.*

**COMPREHENSIVE DESIGN STUDIO SEQUENCE OVERVIEW**

Comprehensive Design Studio 1 & 2 (CDS1 & CDS2) form a two part sequence for the production of a comprehensive architectural design in the core curriculum of the professional degree program. These studios recognize the dilemma of achieving design synthesis and building integration, including technological, material, regulatory, environmental, all with a quality of resolution within the limits of any given academic semester’s time frame. Thus, with these studios organized as a two part sequence, students work through specific stages of development in the architectural design process from schematic and design development through building systems integration, construction systems development, environmental response and performance, with resolved regulatory requirements that approach the level of detailed development and integrated thinking demanded for construction and documentation. To ensure that each student achieve competency with respect to identified learning areas in Student Criteria 6* (see following descriptions) each student will develop and deliver a single project in which they are the primary author. Collaboration plays a critical role in those projects which are connected to the Bedford Chair and engineering students and as well through a series of engagements with consultants and design professionals offering feedback and critique in the form of weekly workshops, reference addendums, with presentations and reviews integrating both internal and external critics representing a broad array of perspectives in professional practice (design and engineering) as well as stakeholder communities.

The process of developing a design for construction—one in which enclosure, environmental systems, structure, and building codes are well considered in a design scheme, does not inevitably ensure the resultant building scheme will reach a level of synthesis that could be considered a work of architecture. Conversely, the development of an elegant, provocative architectural design scheme does not represent the end of the architects responsibility to development and ultimately authorship of that responsibly and effectively incorporates all of these systems to achieve the status of a synthetic work of architecture. It is arguably a tall order for any design studio or sequence within a professional degree program and to this end it is both the project and the process that seeks to provide an arena for experience and evidence for the student’s ability to develop a synthetic work of architecture.

In summary, architecture is a technical art, where both art informs the technical and the technical informs the art. The Comprehensive Design Studio 2 seeks to resolve this reality in the context of a project with the aim of producing knowledge towards a synthetic work of architecture.

In order to pursue these larger goals, the studio presents the wider array of issues that need to be addressed in the design at all stages of design development. Rather than thinking of design, then program, then environment, then site, then structure, etcetera, as distinctly sequential and compartmentalized in terms of the issues they represent, this studio sequence leads towards a holistic approach where students confront a wider range of design issues at the beginning, throughout the process and with iterative layers of development. Fundamentally, this iterative process of visiting and re-visiting critical issues and drivers in the architectural design, provide the opportunity for students to develop specific skills through scale, layers of critical thought, and through narrative, drawings, diagrams and models to capture not only the process of integrative design, but also to attain a project approaching a holistic architectural response.  
***NAAB Student Criteria 6 (SC.6) Building Integration - How the program ensures that students develop the ability to make design decisions within architectural projects while demonstrating integration of building envelope systems and assemblies, structural systems, environmental control systems, life safety systems, and the measurable outcomes of building performance.**

**BUILDING PERFORMANCE IN THE CONTEXT OF COMPREHENSIVE DESIGN STUDIO 2**

Buildings are increasingly complex assemblies that are being called upon to perform many specific technical, aesthetic and social tasks. The design development phase requires an architect to integrate those aspects of the building enterprise that lead to material, physical construction according to codes and other performance requirements. Responses range from passive strategies that mitigate the requirement for resource consumptive systems, to highly complex intelligent systems that make the building, spaces, surfaces and systems responsive; to integrated systems approaches that fold the two together by employing forms, materials, and systems to serve multiple integrated functions. Though the studio is focused on the integration of building systems including codes, means of egress, structure, envelope, storm water drainage, flood control, HVAC (heating, ventilation, air conditioning), lighting and site development, the properties and aesthetics of materials and construction, are equally important considerations, particularly with regard to how they inform and/or influence design decisions. We will focus on materials, composition, and ‘detail’ as they relate to and support design intentions/ambitions. Texture, color, pattern, finish, and affect have a significant place and will be specifically addressed in a selected and strategic focus area of development.

The studio’s emphasis is on the refinement and making of a building using processes through which designs continue to gain conceptual strength and clarity over time. Technical resolution and an understanding of the interrelationship between systems, components, performance, and design are central. During the design development phase, it is expected that the systems integration and detailed development of your project support and enlarge ideas and intentions. Design is always an act of opening up a line of questioning; even when the goal is to converge and specify in order to actualize.

**SITE & PROGRAM**

Given the relationship between CDS1 and CDS2, questions of program and site are initially established in the CDS1 portion of the studio sequence. It is expected that a project advancing from CDS1 into CDS2 to have established a mature understanding of the site conditions and programmatic requirements. It is additionally expected that the project will have achieved an overall comprehensive design strategy along with a clear set of concepts and ideas for the design of a public multi-story building of approximately 70,000 square feet of programed space and located in and serving an urban context. Although many of the deliverables of CD2 will require further development of the site and program as part of the design process, under no circumstances will alternate sites or complete reassignment of program be permitted.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- BUILDING ENVELOPE SYSTEMS AND ASSEMBLIES: Demonstration of integrated building envelope systems in a comprehensive architectural project.. Design solutions must exhibit a resolution of building systems based on sound building science approaches, contemporary manufacturing methods, and conceptually clear approaches to construction and assembly.
- STRUCTURAL SYSTEMS: Demonstration of the primary structural system in a comprehensive architectural project in relation to material and construction systems (for example: steel, wood, masonry, concrete), construction sequencing, and resolvable approaches to the major structural load paths in a architectural design.
- ENVIRONMENTAL CONTROL SYSTEMS: Demonstration of integrated passive and active environmental control systems. Integrated Active systems include distribution of mechanical conditioning and dedicated outdoor air systems addressing indoor air quality. Integrated passive systems include mixed mode conditioning, incorporation of thermal mass performance (as appropriate to the design) and daylighting control.
- LIFE SAFETY SYSTEMS: Demonstration of sufficient means of egress and life safety provisions as based on first principles, universal standards, provisions for universal access (accessibility) and governing national and local codes as appropriate.
- MEASURABLE BUILDING PERFORMANCE: Demonstration of measurable building performance with respect to daylight as a driver of design in an integrated architectural project.
- DETAIL DESIGN AND INTEGRATION – Demonstrate the ability to develop integrated architectural project that is linked to the organization of the buildings program, site, building, environmental and constructions systems. Representations include diagrams, models, interior and exterior renderings, plans, sections and architectural details.

### Other Course Policies:

**STUDIO ENVIRONMENT**

The studio spaces where CDS2 classes meet is considered centrally relevant to your learning experiences beyond the scheduled meeting times of the course. Students will have a fixed (electively selected) desk where the primary production of design and development documentation can occur. The studio will be organized in a manner that is conducive to peer-to-peer mentoring as well as individual learning across digital and physical tool-sets. Space for model making, printed set drawing review and a computer workstation will be provided. It is advised that all personal computer and peripheral devises be secured to the studio desk in your area when you are not present in studio.

The studio should become (both for the studio participants and those visiting the studio) a theater of work in progress. Original models, site photos and drawings (images of the published work you are developing) should be posted for continual reference. Digital images of your models, diagrams, your research projects, new drawings and prototype models should also be posted, as should precedents, projects, articles and images that underpin your research during the semester. However, it is important to understand that the studio process is equally about curating those object, articles and references such that the studio as a whole remains a creative working and professional social environment.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

*“The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process. Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust will merit a failing grade in this course and the violator(s) will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.”*

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts, which violate this trust, undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and the Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with them.

In this studio much of the work is done in teams. In cases where teamwork is required, partners should be prepared to indicate the role of each collaborator. The work of others (outside the partnership) in a project, report or precedent study, etc., should always be cited. All individual assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a grade of zero. Second offenses may result in failure of the course and/or further action as defined by the handbook. If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
