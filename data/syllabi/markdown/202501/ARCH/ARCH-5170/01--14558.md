## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Today’s technology has reached a level, where it inconceivably evolves and orbits around our daily lives. It allows us to unfold a plethora of information, while itself constantly and quietly mutates into what we have yet to see. Vital to our society, digital technology holds great possibilities to help us prosper. Yet its rapid growth also speaks of the need to maintain a good grasp of the changes.

Our role here is not to cease its growth; rather, it is to understand its essence and to equip ourselves with such “tools” in pursuit of advancements. Creative fields are at the epicenter of this kind – we find new tools and harness their movements in our design as extensions of natural human capacity. Thus, the technosphere, with its digital tools and intelligence, demands acquisition of the proficiency required for its full deployment.

With choreographed series of challenges, the four Digital Constructs prepare students for full control of digital tools and establish design authorship. Digital Constructs IV culminates this successive dialogue, with more advanced knowledge, skills, and techniques of digital tools and representations in a threefold continuum:

1. Algorithmic Design Students will start learning the basics of algorithmic designs. This stage introduces a logical sequencing of design executions through the understanding of data structures. Students will be asked to generate a series of design iterations as a spur to the following design development. They will further develop design details on architectural envelopes to increase design complexity while carefully organizing its tectonics and assembly.
2. Design Communication This stage is to introduce the fundamentals of scene setting. Students will gain an ability to identify the views that convey the design intentions, and to complement them with entourage and details that are specifically necessary to the perspectives. They will also develop custom textures and materials as well as post-processing techniques including AI tools to enhance their design communication.
3. Introduction to Building Information Modeling (BIM) This phase will introduce the use of multiple interfaces, including Revit. Students will gain an ability to identify each software’s advantages and to organize design projects throughout different applications.

Thus, practiced techniques will not only lead you to success in finding architectural formulae within the coursework, but also be of help in developing your work in other courses, specifically AD4, CD1, and CD2. Students are strongly encouraged to actively think and explore opportunities beyond this course frame, in order for the digital skills acquired to match your personal interests and become part of your digital proficiency.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- ALGORITHMIC DESIGN: Students will have the ability to formulate basic design workflows in grasshopper to generate parametric linkages between architectural components, massings, structures, and the articulations of the architectural envelopes. Students in graduate school will have the additional ability to extend the design depth using scripting languages including Python.
- DESIGN COMMUNICATION: Students will have the ability to compose a 3d scene in V-Ray, using camera setups, various types of lights, scene shaders, environment maps, custom textures, materials, entourage, and backgrounds, and the ability to augment the rendering process using post-processing techniques, and AI tools, in accordance with their design intentions. Students in graduate school will have the additional ability to increase the representational breadth through a series of mapping techniques.
- INTRODUCTION TO BUILDING INFORMATION MODLEING (BIM): Students will have the ability to import 3d models and produce scaled architectural drawings using Rhino and Revit. Students in graduate school will have the additional ability to independently produce the drawing set with a customized title block.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is required at all class meetings during the semester. **Two (2) unexcused absences from class will result in the lowering of the grade by one (1) letter. Four (4) unexcused absences from class during the term result in an automatic F.** Whether an absence (health, immediate family death, officially approved field / sport trip, etc.) is excused or unexcused is determined by the Students Experience Office (4th Floor Academy Hall, x8022, se@rpi.edu), upon presentation of required documentation, according to established policies and procedures (see http:// http://se.rpi.edu/policies/ea/).

### Other Course Policies:

Late submission Late submissions will be subject to a grading penalty and may not be accepted without above-mentioned legitimate excuses. For students with an ADHD diagnosis, extra time applies only to exams, and quizzes completed in class. It does not apply to projects or labs where the students are able to plan more time outside of class to complete assignments. Note that a design-based class such as Digital Constructs consists solely of project-based assignments. Students are encouraged to attend faculty office hours and tutoring sessions to help plan the completion of studio, lab, or project-based assignments.

Class participation Students are expected to participate actively in all class discussions, productions, and project reviews. They are expected to come to class with all required assignments completed. On-time attendance at all scheduled meetings is mandatory besides the final presentation and other reviews.

Students are required to work in class during class hours and have the required materials and basic tools to develop the assignments, including a working computer running the required software. Failure to work in class with undivided attention and disruptive behavior (tardiness, leaving early, lack of participation, general socializing, etc.), as well working on assignments from other classes during class, will count as absences.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
