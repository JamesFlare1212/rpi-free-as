## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Learning Outcome #1: Students will be able to analyze the structure and language of film.
- Learning Outcome #2: Students will be able to communicate film ideas verbally and visually through architectural mediums.
- Learning Outcome #3: Students will be able to translate ideas of film into architectural concepts.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

*Assessment Measures:*

1. *Creation of an Architectural Film Story Board*
2. *Design of a Space to Screen Films*
3. *Creation of a Short Film*

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

*Midterm Project Requirements: 30% of Grade*

*Due Date February 27th* *- Uploaded to the Google Doc Drive.*

1. *A written concept (100 - 300 words).*
2. *An architectural film story board for the concept of a 3-minute short film to be screened in a space for 100 viewers. This must include 100 – 300 words of a concept description.*

**We will propose a challenge to the Neutral Black Box Syndrome that defines the typical film experience.*

*Typically, the Work of Art (Film in this case) is more important than the Architecture (Screening Space),*

*the project should challenge this assumption by giving equal importance to the film and architecture. The*

*architecture of the space becomes a part of the experience with the film.*

*Final Project Requirements: 60% of Grade*

*Due* *Date - April 17th* *- Uploaded to Google Doc Drive*

1. *3 Minutes Short Film (File type that can be attached to an email)*
2. *Plan, Section, Elevation and one 3D view of the 100-person film space. (A single layout 4:3 ratio)*

*Assessment of Student Performance and Development*

*You will receive one total grade at the end of the semester. You will receive feedback from your professor*

*on your midterm project proposal. Students are encouraged to request additional clarification of*

*feedback if necessary. The midterm project proposal will be developed and submitted as the Final Project at the end of the semester.*

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

*Academic Integrity:*

*Intellectual integrity and credibility are the foundation of all academic work. A violation of Academic*

*Integrity policy is, by definition, considered a flagrant offense to the educational process. It is taken*

*seriously by students, faculty, and Rensselaer and will be addressed in an effective manner.*

*If found responsible for committing academic dishonesty, a student may be subject to one or both types*

*of penalties: an academic (grade) penalty administered by the professor and/or disciplinary action*

*through the Rensselaer judicial process described in this handbook.*

*Academic dishonesty is a violation of the Grounds for Disciplinary Action as described in this handbook.*

*A student may be subject to any of the following types of disciplinary action should disciplinary action be*

*pursued by the instructor: disciplinary warning; disciplinary probation; disciplinary suspension, expulsion*

*and/or alternative actions as agreed on by the student and hearing officer. It should be noted that no*

*student who allegedly commits academic dishonesty will be able to drop or change the grade option for*

*the course in question and is not eligible to request an F examination for the course.*
