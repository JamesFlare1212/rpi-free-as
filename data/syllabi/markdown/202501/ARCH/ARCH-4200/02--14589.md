## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

A graphic portfolio of architectural design work is a living document representing the diversity, competence, and growth of abilities throughout the career in architecture. It is an essential and required document for securing employment and advancement in any architectural field from professional practice to academics. This course introduces students into the design and development of their portfolios that include work from every level of their education and professional experience. Professional development in this course entails three items: portfolio, CV, and NCARB mentoring for a path toward licensure. The course is designed for students who are preparing for their ARCH AWAY experience to be able to demonstrate to potential employers the full spectrum of their architectural design capabilities with graphic proficiency, and to be eligible to gain AXP credits during the internship period.

This is a one credit in-person course, covering professional development topics to assist students in preparing and developing the AWAY plan and job search strategy. This course instructs students how to acquire AXP credits through traditional and non-traditional experience settings. For example, students can earn (prior to graduation) a significant amount of AXP credits, some of which can be through internships and others which can come in the form of community services, design build experiences, general construction experiences, project management, and independent student competitions.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- OUTCOME 1: GRAPHIC COMMUNICATION: Ability to construct image flow to support both graphic and verbal narratives of their design projects, at a level of high competence with various graphic design software such as InDesign.
- OUTCOME 2: DESIGN BREADTH: Ability to integrate work examples exhibiting a diverse range of skills including, design studio, history theory, technical and environmental courses.
- OUTCOME 3: PROFESSIONAL SKILLS: Ability to conceive, design, and construct a portfolio, a curriculum vitae, that is tailored to architecture, and the allied design fields.
- OUTCOME 4: CAREER PATH: Ability to understand how to establish an NCARB record and log AXP (experience credits) through mentorship. Formulate a path to licensure including the AXP and the ARE (Architecture Registration Exam) by participating in career development events, including Blast-off and career fairs, and meeting with their NCARB mentor.

Attendance: weekly desk critiques 5 points

Digital final submission: a package including a portfolio, a CV, and a letter of interest 10 points

AEFIS student assessment (required)* 5 points

*Incomplete assessments will result in no grade for the course.

Total 100 points

Section critics will evaluate each assessment measure individually. For example, an ‘excellent’ one in Assessment Measure 3: Integration of a variety of projects, may receive 9.5 points, a ‘very good’ one 8.5 points, and an ‘average’ one 7.5 points. See grading rubric below for specific descriptions of excellent, very good, average, etc. Total points available for the semester is 100. If proof of NCARB registration is presented to NCARB mentors, students will receive an additional 5 points. At the end of the course, students will be asked to complete a self-assessment survey in which they will evaluate their own performance with respect to each assessment measure. Completing the Student Assessment at the end of the course is required, as it is essential for the school’s accreditation.

Grades will be established according to the following policy:

\- A (EXCELLENT, 100-90 points) Exceptional performance strongly exceeding the requirements of the course, showing strong academic initiative and independent resourcefulness.

\- B (GOOD, 90-80 points) Performance above the norm; accurate and complete; beyond the minimum requirements of the course; work demonstrates marked progress and initiative.

\- C (AVERAGE, 80-70 points) Satisfactory work that adequately meets minimum requirements and demonstrates satisfactory comprehension, communication skills, and effort; demonstrates little initiative to investigate the problem without substantial input from the instructor; work shows little improvement.

\- D (INFERIOR, 70-60 points) Unsatisfactorily meets minimum requirements; demonstrates minimum comprehension, communication skills, and effort at an inferior level; initiative lacking; improvement not noticeable. (D grades not available to graduate students).

\- F (FAIL, below 60 points) Does not meet minimum requirements; fails to adequately demonstrate comprehension, communication skills, and effort.

Students will receive one formal grade at the end of the semester. In addition to the final grade, students will receive verbal assessments and advice through weekly presentations. Notify your instructor in advance if you know that you cannot attend class for any reason.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance

Attendance is required at all class meetings throughout the semester. Two (2) absences from class will result in the lowering of the grade by one (1) letter. Four (4) absences from class during the term result in an automatic F. For medical absences of up to a week, formal excuses are not required, and students must work directly with faculty to arrange missed work. If illness causes more than a week of absences, students must provide a doctor’s note or documentation to the Student Success Office, and the Class Dean will inform faculty. COVID-19 and flu cases follow this same process. Students are responsible for completing any work that they might miss due to illness, quarantine/isolation, or other circumstances. The instructor will make every effort to work with and provide reasonable modifications for students who cannot attend class due to illness to complete assignments and exams. In some cases, virtual learning may not be possible. Ultimately, it is the responsibility of the student(s) who miss class to contact the instructor so they may agree upon a plan of action for making up missed assignments.

Failure to fully engage in class or display disruptive behavior (such as tardiness, leaving early, lack of participation, or socializing) will be considered absences, as will working on assignments from other classes during class time.

Late assignments

Late assignments will be subject to a grading penalty and may not be accepted without above-mentioned legitimate excuses.

Class participation

Students are expected to participate actively in all class discussions, productions, and project reviews. They are expected to come to class with all required assignments completed. On-time attendance at all scheduled meetings is mandatory besides the final presentation and other reviews. Students are required to work in class during class hours and have the required materials and basic tools to develop the assignments, including a working computer running the required software, such as InDesign. Failure to work in class with undivided attention and disruptive behavior (tardiness, leaving early, lack of participation, general socializing, etc.), as well working on assignments from other classes during class, will count as absences.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**POLICY ON COLLABORATION AND PLAGIARISM**

Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of this course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work which was not solely completed by the student. Any misrepresentation of work shall be evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student will result in a failing grade for the course.

**THE RPI STUDENT SUPPLEMENT OF RIGHTS & RESPONSIBILITIES**

Intellectual integrity and credibility are the foundation of all academic work. Misconduct in Scholarly Activities is, by definition, considered a flagrant offense to the educational process. It is taken seriously by students, faculty, and Rensselaer and will be addressed in an effective manner.

Since Misconduct in Scholarly Activities is a violation of Professional Ethics guidelines, the student found in violation of said guidelines may be subject to any of the following sanctions: disciplinary warning; disciplinary probation; disciplinary suspension, expulsion and/or alternative actions as defined by the Vice Provost and Dean of Graduate Education. It should be noted that no student who allegedly commits Misconduct in Scholarly Activities will be able to drop the course in question.

Scholarly Misconduct does not include honest error or honest differences in interpretations or judgments of scholarly matters. If the alleged misconduct is not substantiated, the Institute will undertake diligent efforts to restore the reputation of those under investigation. Should either the inquiry or the investigation shows that the allegations of misconduct were not made in good faith, those making the allegations will themselves be subject to disciplinary action.
