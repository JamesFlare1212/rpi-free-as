### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

An exploration of the fundamental principles of human physiology, thermal and luminous comfort, and indoor quality. Emphasis is on bioclimatic and psychrometric climate analysis and its relationship to architectural design, understanding the energy exchange between body in space, the natural meaning of enclosures, and nonstructural materials and systems. The focus is on passive heating, cooling, and daylighting systems and their design. Exercises include vital sign analysis of existing spaces (thermal, air, luminous), forming hypotheses of building performance, using scientific instrumentation, tenant survey techniques, and physical modeling and simulation techniques related to daylighting and shading techniques.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Outcome 1 - Simulating Environmental Principles in Architecture: Students will demonstrate broad knowledge of the principles, methodologies, and terminology of ecology and sustainability as they apply to architectural design.
- Outcome 2 - Design Application and Representation: Students will be able to integrate environmental principles and passive systems introduced in course lecture materials and assignments into their studio design work through text, schematic drawings/diagrams, and other media.
- Outcome 3 - Thermodynamics in Buildings: Students will be able to identify and apply fundamental principles of thermodynamics and and how they affect architectural design and building performance including passive conservation, thermal mass, controlling solar heat gain and passive cooling/ventilation
- Outcome 4 - Daylighting and Shading Principles: Students will be able to identify and apply fundamental principles of daylighting and architectural shading and and how they affect architectural design and building performance.
- Outcome 5 - Building Health: Students will be able to identify and apply fundamental principles of material and indoor air hygeine as they relate to occupants and greater environmental impacts.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Assignments, Quizzes and formal exams will be issued as per the course schedule and count towards the final class grade as per the course learning outcome matrix. Below is a summary of the intent of each assessment measure:

Assignment 1: Thermal simulation

Using THERM software, students will simulate a building detail and quantify the psi value of the thermal bridge.

Assignment 2: Daylighting simulation

Using Climate Studio, students will perform a daylighting analysis of a ‘shoebox’ model, export information and interpret the results.

Assignment 3: Design synthesis

Using either Climate Studio or THERM, students will perform an analysis on an existing condition in the design of a case study building. Based on the results of the analysis, students will formulate a hypothesis and test the hypothesis with the goal of improving occupant comfort or building performance.

Quizzes: Consist of 5 multiple-choice, true/false or multiple answer questions based on lecture content outlined in the schedule. All quizzes will be taken and graded through LMS.

Exams: Midterm and final exams will be issued consisting of 25 multiple-choice, true/false or multiple answer questions. The exams are based on cumulative semester content.

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance will be taken during each class and counts towards your final class grade. Please see the Assessment Measures section for assessment breakdown. More than three unexcused absences will result in a failing grade for the course.

To qualify as an excused absence, the student should contact their section instructor prior to the missed class or shortly after with the reason for their absence. It is up to the discretion of the instructor to accept or deny the excuse. Notes from the Student Health center or doctor are not required.

All lecture slides and recordings will be posted to the LMS and it is the student’s responsibility to review all content in preparation for course assessments.

During all virtual classes, attendance will be taken between 8a and 8:15a ET and again at the end of class. If the student is not logged in within the first time period *and* full attendance is not verified at the end of class, they will not receive attendance credit for that day.

Attendance grades will be issued based on the following metrics:
