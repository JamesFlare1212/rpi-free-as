## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Description:**

This two-credit course explores the key professional activities that are critical to correlating the art and science of architecture with

the business, ethics and practice of architecture. The course relies on the history of architecture, the current legal constructs, business experience, ethical actions, and the guidance and standards provided by the AIA and by licensing entities. The experience of the co-instructore will provide the context and storytelling to ground the material and enliven the delivery.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Course Objectives:**

1\. Provide an overview of the depth of Professional Practice

2\. Introduce students to Project Management

3\. Provide a summary of architecture firm organization and management

4\. Introduce students to options in delivery methods, the Architect’s contractual obligations and

the role of architects during construction

5\. Expose Students to legal issues of the profession

6\. Expose students to the methods used for determining costs associated with projects and the

delivery of architectural services

7\. Familiarize students with ethical obligations and conduct requirements

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Topics to be addressed in detail are:**

1\. Ethics

2\. Architecture as a profession and the requirements for practicing

3\. Business models of A/E firms

4\. Project Management and coordination with consultants

5\. Financial management of an architecture firm

6\. Project costs and operational costs.

7\. Understanding value

8\. The contractual aspects of architecture

9\. Delivery methods for contracting and constructing projects

10\. Marketing and business development.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Student will be able to demonstrate through in-class discussions, assignments and testing the ability to properly identify the relevant ethics, regulatory requirements, and fundamental business practices of architecture. a. Professional Ethics and Conduct – Student will have the ability to identify the ethical issues involved in the exercise of professional judgment in architectural design and practice and to locate where to find the ethical requirements of the profession. b. Regulations – Student will have the ability to analyze and identify the legal roles and responsibilities of the architect as determined by regulations including legal practice structures in New York State. Student will be able to identify the legal considerations in the practice of architecture and professional service contracts. c. Business Practices - Student will have the ability to identify the proper basic principles of a firm’s business practices, including finances, project management, risk, business planning, marketing, business development, organization, and project delivery.
- Student will be able to demonstrate through assignments, in class discussions and testing the ability to identify licensure paths and career opportunities. a. Student will be able to identify the requirements of the AXP and ARE. b. Student will be able to organize the typical career path chronologically. c. Students will be able to discuss the different types of career paths.
- Student will be able to demonstrate through a group project, meetings with professor, in-class discussions, assignments and testing the ability to identify, organize and discuss leadership and collaboration in diverse multidisciplinary teams and how to solve complex problems in dynamic contexts. a. Student will demonstrate comprehension by meeting with the professor and submitting a response to a request for proposal utilizing Project Management skills in a group to collaborate to choose a proper legal theoretical architecture firm, choose real world consultants, prepare an organization chart, describe a viable project delivery method calculate a basic service fee, distribute the fee by phase both internal and external, calculate the architectural staff hours that can be assigned to the typical basic services phases and prepare a milestone and bar chart schedule based on all project parameters. b. Student will have the ability to identify the relationships and responsibilities among key stakeholders in the design process.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students will illustrate competency though homework assignments, in-class quizzes, two tests during the course period (no "final" exam), and one collaborative Project Management project. QnA periods during class and attention during class is also a factor.

- Homeworks - confirm the reading of assigned materials through evidence of navigation of several resources in order to respond.
- Quizzes - short, IRT, and designed to cross-check validity of homework scoring by confirming retention of key material.
- Tests - longer in-class exams that test retention as well as ability to think through situations in ethics, leadership and other course material.
- Project - a collaborative piece of work to create a firm structure, defining the legal needs, type of firm, staffing plans, utilization and billing rates, and marketing aspects. Part of this project is also a well-written response to a request for proposal for a project.
