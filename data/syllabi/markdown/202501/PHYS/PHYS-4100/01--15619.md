## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Quantum mechanics beyond Schrödinger wave mechanics. The postulates of quantum mechanics. Second quantization, Dirac notation, Hilbert spaces, perturbation theory, and applications to simple systems.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

John Townsend, “Modern Approach to Quantum Mechanics”, 2nd edition ISBN: 9781891389788

## Course Goals

### Goals:

By the end of the course, and upon satisfactory completion of all HW assign- ments, class activities and exams, the students will have an elementary understanding of Quantum Mechanics and, more importantly, its interpretation.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Calculate probability and probability amplitudes
- Understand meaning of Pauli matrices, and being able to calculate matrix representation of spin 1 systems
- Establish Hamiltonian as the generator of time evolution operator
- To be able to combine two spin-1/2 particles
- To be able to calculate the probability density in simple 1D wells
- Understand the impact of commutation relationships on the solution of the bound states of a spherically symmetric system
- To understand the solutions of the 1D Harmonic Oscillator in quantum mechanics
- To understand time-independent perturbation theory up to second order for degenerate and non-degenerate cases
- Understand the implication of indistinguishably on the possible symmetry of wave-function under the effect of the exchange operator
- To be able to solve the problem of the hydrogen atom

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Learning will be assessed using a combination of quizzes, homework assignments, reading assignments, and exams, as described elsewhere in this syllabus.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Reading Assignments:

There will be a reading assignment in advance of every class period. In order to receive credit

for the reading assignment, the student has to submit a question on the topic to the instructor

through LMS no later than 6:00 pm the day before the scheduled class time. The question has to

be substantial, e.g. ”Can you explain equation XYZ?” will not receive credit. The lowest reading

assignment score will be dropped to compute your final grade in the course. Therefore, if you can

miss one reading assignment without a penalty. The reading assignment will account for 10% of

the final grade.

Homework:

The homework assignments and respective due dates will be posted on the class calendar. The

homework assignment will be due at the beginning of class meeting on the due date. In general,

late homework will not be accepted. The homework will account for 15% of the final grade.

Quizzes:

There will be quizzes given randomly throughout the semester at the beginning of the class meeting.

The lowest two quiz scores will be dropped in calculating the average score. The quiz average will

account for 15% of the final grade. No makeup quizzes will be given.

Exams:

There will be three exams during the semester to test your progress and mastering of the material.

The exams will be given during class time. Please see the class calendar for dates. There is no

final exam. In calculating the exam average the two exams with the highest scores will each count

as two exams, while the exam with the lowest score will count as a single exam. So that the exam

average will be calculated from a total of 5 scores. The exam average accounts for 60% of your

final grade.

If a student misses an exam due to extenuating circumstances, a make up oral exam will be given.
