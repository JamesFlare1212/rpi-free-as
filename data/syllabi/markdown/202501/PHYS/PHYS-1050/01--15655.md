## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a one semester overview of physics fundamentals with an empha- sis on concepts and applications. Topics will include mechanics, including equilibrium and statics, fluids, oscillations, and waves; basics of thermodynamics and heat flow; electricity and magnetism; electromagnetic radiation and optics; and elements of atomic and nuclear physics.

The class style will be a combination of lecture and interactive learning through in-class discussions.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

“Physics and Technology for Future Presidents”, R. Muller, ISBN: 9780691135045

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Upon successful completion of the course, the students will have gained an insight into the fundamental concepts of physics that govern our every day lives. The students will be able to apply this insight to better understand every day phenomena in their personal and professional life.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Establish a notion of energy and power as defined in physics.
- Understand how atoms and molecules are the building blocks of matter.
- To be able to articulate the difference between heat and temperature.
- Understand what a force is and how Newton’s Laws govern motion.
- Understand the structure of the atom and how nuclear reactions result in energy output.
- Understand how nuclear reactors work.
- Understand electricity and magnetism and how they govern electric motors and generators.
- Establish a notion of what a wave is, in particular electromagnetic and sound waves.
- Gain an overview of the entire spectrum of electromagnetic waves including light.
- Understand climate change and global warming.
- Understand how quantum physics relates to everyday experiences and tools.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Reading Assignments:

There will be a reading assignment in advance of every class period. (Please see the class calendar.)

In class I will be covering only the highlights of each chapter, assuming that you have already read

the chapter and are familiar with the material. This is a lot of material, so don’t get behind! Get

in a routine of reading the before the lecture. That way you will have a basic understanding of the

concepts and good questions to ask. Reading the book is the most important part of this

course! When you are done with the reading assignment, try to answer the review questions at

the end of each chapter. You do not have to hand in any solutions, but this is a good check for

yourself if you comprehend the material.

One learns physics by repetition. Read quickly, but multiple times. With each time, it makes more

sense. Don’t worry if you don’t understand things right away – just keep on reading.

Attendance

Attendance of the class meeting is required. If you have to miss a class meeting, send an email with

your reasoning to the instructor before class starts. Notifications received after class has started

will in general not be honoured.

Quizzes

In order to give you credit for doing the reading and attending, there will be quizzes given randomly

throughout the semester during the class meeting. The quizzes will be in form of multiple choice

questions (see end of chapters) at the beginning and/or the end of the lectures. The quiz average

will account for 20% of the final grade. No makeup quizzes will be given.

• Discover

• New Scientist

• Economist

Your homework grade will be 0/1 - if you didn’t hand it in, 1/1 - if you handed it in on time and

The homework will be worth 10% of your final grade.

Exams:

There will be two exams during the semester to test your progress and mastering of the material.

Half of the exam will be multiple choice questions and the other half will be essay style. The exams

will be given during class time on the following dates: Feb 29 and April 22. There will be no final

exam. In calculating the exam average, the exam with the highest scores will count as two exams,

while the exam with the lowest score will count as a single exam. So that the exam average will be

calculated from a total of 3 scores. The exams account for 50% of your final grade.

Mentoring Sections:

Mentoring basically means recitation. During that time you will practice the course material by

discussing essay type questions on that weeks material (see end of chapter essay topics).

Mentoring will count for 20% of your final grade in PHYS 1050. Mentoring is a mandatory part

of this course and it is not possible to get a 100% in the class without adequate eﬀort, attendance,

and participation in the sessions. In the event of an absence, email your mentor and cc Prof. Georg

ahead of time and we will work on a make-up. Grading per session is done on a trinary scale and

is awarded as follows:

• 0 – The student is not present at the session (unexcused absence)

• 1 – The student is present but does not actively participate (e.g. the student is preoccupied

with electronic devices, does not collaborate with other students during group activities.)

• 2 – The student is present and actively participates during the session (e.g. - The student is

not distracted by electronics, collaborates with other students during group activities, answers

questions or presents group work when asked).

Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.
