## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Field theory of electricity and magnetism with emphasis on solving boundary value problems. Dielectric and magnetic materials. Maxwell’s equations and wave propagation with applications to optics. Relativistic electrodynamics.  
PHYS 4210 Electromagnetic theory is an intermediate level course for Physics majors that emphasizes the fundamental relationship between electric and magnetic fields. Students taking this course should have a good working knowledge of differential equations, vector calculus, and linear algebra.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Topics will include:

Solution of boundary value problems in electrostatics and magnetostatics;

the application of vector calculus and tensors to find electric and magnetic fields and potentials;

the relationship between electric and magnetic fields in special relativity;

electrodynamics including accelerating charges;

electromagnetic waves in free space and in materials;

introduction to waveguides and antennas.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- The successful student will be able to solve electrostatic charge distribution and boundary value problems using vector calculus tools including the divergence theorem; line, surface and volume integrals; and Laplace and Poisson equations; and multipole expansions.
- The successful student will be able to solve magnetostatic current distribution and boundary value problems using vector calculus tools including Green’s and Stoke’s theorems; line, surface and volume integrals; and Laplace equation; and multipole expansions.
- The successful student will be able to solve boundary value and wave propagation problems including waveguides, dispersion in materials, and reflection at interfaces.
