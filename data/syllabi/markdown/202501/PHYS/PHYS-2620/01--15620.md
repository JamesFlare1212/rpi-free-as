## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Optics impacts every aspect of our daily life, ranging from i-phone camera, TV, telescope, fiber optics, to today’s 3D display & photonic-chips.

In this course, students will learn Fundamental Principle of Optics that

includes ray optics, EM optics, wave-optics and diffractive optics. The

topics are of fundamental importance and will pay the way to a better understanding of advanced topics such as Lasers, Photonics, and Quantum Optics.

**Prerequisites or Other Requirements**: PHYS 1100 and PHYS 1200 or equivalent or permission of instructor.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

At the end of this course, a student should understand basic concepts of geometrical optics, matrix method for ray tracing, wave optics, the concept of interference and coherence of light, superposition of light, interferometry, scalar theory of diffraction of light, Fraunhaufer diffraction and polarizations. Students should be able to set up and solve problems in relationship to the topics mentioned above.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- The successful student will: a. Demonstrate understanding of key concepts applying to geometric optics, electromagnetic EM optics, wave optics and diffractive optics. b. Demonstrate knowledge of the basic physical concepts of ray optics, image formation of thin lens, thick lens and their combination. c. Demonstrate knowledge of the matrix optics and its application to ray tracing technique, deriving the principle points and focal length of a thick lens combination. d. Demonstrate knowledge of the principle of wave optics, its nature arising from the notion of wavelength and its dispersive relationship with the frequency of light. e. Demonstrate knowledge of coherence of light, interference of light wave and Michelson interferometer as a method to measure the wavelength of light and speed-of-light. f. Demonstrate knowledge of scalar diffraction theory and its application to far-field optics and setup foundations for Fourier optics. g. Demonstrate knowledge of polarization of light, transverse nature of electromagnetic field and its consequence on light transmission, reflection and refraction.
