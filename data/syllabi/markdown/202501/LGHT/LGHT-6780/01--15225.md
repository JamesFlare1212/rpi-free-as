## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The graduate students at the LRC receive the best technical foundation for pursuing a career in lighting. In addition to technical skills professional success depends upon an appreciation of organizations within which a lighting career is pursued and how change in lighting technologies, practice, and processes can be driven. Therefore, it is the collective view of faculty that our students should receive some formal assistance in making the transition from the LRC to their chosen profession.

To this end, students will get an introduction to business concepts and gain hands-on experience working individually and in teams.

Students learn how to provide technical support for a new business ventures. This section will cover methodology, tools, and communication needed to develop a business plan. The course educates the students about entrepreneurship and the entrepreneurial process. In doing so they learn about the process involved in developing a business plan. Students, working as a team, will conduct a case study to practice concepts such as alignment, performance & goal setting and measurement.  
To gain hands-on experience the students work on developing a new product using their strong knowledge in lighting technologies and applications. They will conduct literature survey and listening to guest lectures from industry experts who have experience starting businesses and helped industry champion the lighting products. They learn to write clear summaries of literature survey findings and lessons learned from guest lectures. In addition students learn to work individually and in groups and share responsibilities. They also learn about protecting intellectual property and the cost of doing business. Finally, they make clear presentations to communicate the value proposition to the internal teams and external customers, similar to what whey are expected to do after graduation in their future careers.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The course objective is to provide students with some formal assistance in making the transition from an educational environment to a for-profit organization where they will begin their working career. To this end, students receive an introduction to business concepts and interact with LRC and RPI faculty and business leaders. Students also work in teams to develop case studies and business plans related to lighting products, services, or organizations to apply the concepts of the course and develop in-depth, practical analyses. At the completion of this course, it is expected that students will be able to:  
-Explain what is entrepreneurship and the entrepreneurial process  
-Explain the process involved in developing a business plan, detailing the technical and financial aspects of launching a new product.  
-Synthesize and communicate effectively, the technical and business aspects of an enterprise.  
-Work individually and in groups and share responsibilities

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

If there is reason to believe a student in this course may have been involved in academic dishonesty, the instructor will contact the student(s) and schedule a meeting to discuss the allegations. When meeting with the student, the instructor will review the circumstances and evidence related to the suspicion of academic dishonesty and allow him or her the opportunity to provide his or her perspective on the situation. If it is concluded that the student has violated the Institute academic dishonesty policy, the instructor will determine the appropriate academic (grade) penalty and will communicate this decision to the student in writing. In this course, penalties for academic dishonesty are as follows:

- For a first, minor violation – The student will be given a warning and reminded of the types of activities that are considered a violation of academic integrity.
- For a second offence – The student will be given a grade of “F” or zero on the assignment in question.
- For a third offence – The student will be given a failing grade for the course.

In cases where students are found to be in violation of the academic integrity policy, the written decision of the instructor will be copied to the Senior Judicial Administrator in the Dean of Students Office, along with a brief summary of the case facts and a copy of any supporting documentation (i.e., exams/assignments involving cheating, crib sheets, witness statements, etc.). A copy of the incident of academic dishonesty will be kept on file in the Dean of Students Office.
