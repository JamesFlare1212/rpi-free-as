## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Lighting Technologies & Applications is a 4-credit course intended to provide students with an in-depth understanding of the components of lighting systems and enable them to critically explore their use. Through lectures, lab sessions, readings, research assignments, and application projects, students will acquire principles and working knowledge of the relevant equipment and techniques for developing lighting solutions.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

In this course, students will:

- Learn to assess the characteristics of light sources, luminaires, and lighting controls as they apply to their integration into lighting systems for general illumination. Some of the characteristics covered in class include, among others, photometric, electrical, thermal and mechanical properties, useful lifetime, installation and maintenance, and cost.
- Understand the operation and key performance characteristics of lighting systems under ideal and realistic conditions.
- Explore various applications of lighting systems and their components, and identify what makes them effective.
- Learn to evaluate and compare alternate lighting systems based on multiple criteria, such as photometric performance, lighting economics, and energy efficiency.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

- Lighting terminology and introduction to photometry units
- Fundamentals of photometry
- Overview of legacy light sources
- Introduction to solid-state lighting and basics of LED technology
- Electrical, thermal and optical properties of LEDs
- LED system integration issues
- Application issues with LEDs
- Light distribution, basics of optics and photometric reports
- Principles of lighting calculations, lumen method
- Lighting controls
- Lighting economics
- Overview of lighting standards
- Advanced lighting topics

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are expected to attend all classes, however, there is no need for a medical excuse to be provided for absence of up to a week (five days). Students missing more than a week of classes due to illness will be required to provide a doctor’s note or other supporting documentation to the Student Success Office. The Class Dean will communicate the receipt of the note (with expected end date) to the relevant faculty. Students are responsible for completing any work that they might miss – including assignments, quizzes, labs, exams, etc. – due to illness, quarantine/isolation, or other circumstances. When possible, students should notify the instructor in advance of an absence. The instructor will make every effort to work with and provide reasonable modifications for students who cannot attend class due to illness to complete assignments and exams. Students should keep in mind that virtual learning or class recording may not be possible. Students who adhere to these processes will not be penalized per the attendance policy (class participation) for the course. Students are expected to maintain punctuality and to participate actively in all class discussions, studio and laboratory sessions, and project reviews. They are expected to come to class with all required reading and assignments completed. On time attendance at all scheduled meetings is mandatory. Late assignments will only be accepted for valid reasons as described above. An unsatisfactory record of attendance may result in a failing grade, and/or dismissal from the program. Students are expected to maintain punctuality, and late arrivals may be recorded as absences.
