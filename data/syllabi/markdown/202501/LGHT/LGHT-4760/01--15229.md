## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The *Lighting Workshop* is an upper-level undergraduate and graduate research and design studio course, integrating scholarship, design, entrepreneurship, and communication in a project-specific context. Illumination is one of the most critical aspects of any built environment that enables the aesthetics of the space to be appreciated while serving the visual and physiological needs of the occupants. The perception, formation, and representation of architectural and urban spaces is facilitated through layers of light and darkness. Using the shades of light and darkness, lighting designers shape the perception of objects and space, and influence activities within them. In this course, students will explore the opportunities to create architectural experiences and spatial perceptions by working with light and dark in various project contexts and their frameworks. This includes showcasing objects, supporting specific activities, and shaping the experience of a space, but also negotiating differing occupants' needs. Students will gain skills, in-depth knowledge, and hands-on experience while working on group and individual projects. The aim of this course is to prepare students to take on leadership roles and face real-world challenges, especially related to illumination in the built environment.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

At the conclusion of the course, the students will be able to:

1. Develop creative solutions to lighting-related problems by understanding relevant technologies, societal, cultural, environmental, and economic issues.
2. Evaluate a given lighting application and its integration of research and design.
3. Demonstrate effective oral, written, and graphic communication skills through in-person presentations, computer visualizations, and other types of presentations.
4. Demonstrate effective project management skills, including task and milestone planning, and fulfilling deliverables.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

- Introduction and overview of lighting design industry and related professions and practices
- Fundamentals of lighting design approaches and tools
- Individual and group work in and out of class on group project (lighting design of a local park and building facade)
- Individual work in and out of class on individual project (design competition)
- Weekly project reviews, presentations, pin-ups, and/or desk critiques

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Student assessment will be based on a review of their performance on individual and group work, presentations, and other class assignments. Preparation, quality of work, and student participation in class discussions are considered for the final grade. For the 4000-level students, the final grade breakdown includes individual assignments (class project 2) (30 points), group project part 1 (class project 2, concept design) and part 2 (class project 2, detail design) (65 points), and class participation (5 points).

- For class project 1 (Design Competition), students will be assessed based on their interim in-class presentations and their final submittal package to a design competition.
- For class project 2 (Lighting Design Group project), students will be assessed on the quality of the information collected during the site visit and in background research prior to in-class discussion; the improvement in their design process; in-class progress presentations; the quality of the materials produced for a final presentation with the customer (drawings, renderings, schedules, budgets, details), and their participation in a final presentation to the customer.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are expected to attend all classes, however, there is no need for a medical excuse to be provided for absence of up to a week (five days). Students missing more than a week of classes due to illness will be required to provide a doctor's note or other supporting documentation to the Student Success Office. The Class Dean will communicate the receipt of the note (with expected end date) to the relevant faculty. Students are responsible for completing any work that they might miss - including assignments, quizzes, labs, exams, etc. - due to illness, quarantine/isolation, or other circumstances. When possible, students should notify the instructor in advance of an absence. The instructor will make every effort to work with and provide reasonable modifications for students who cannot attend class due to illness to complete assignments and exams. Students should keep in mind that virtual learning or class recording may not be possible. Students who adhere to these processes will not be penalized per the attendance policy (class participation) for the course. Students are expected to maintain punctuality and to participate actively in all class discussions, studio and laboratory sessions, and project reviews. They are expected to come to class with all required reading and assignments completed. On time attendance at all scheduled meetings is mandatory. Late assignments will only be accepted for valid reasons as described above. An unsatisfactory record of attendance may result in a failing grade, and/or dismissal from the program. Students are expected to maintain punctuality, and late arrivals may be recorded as absences.
