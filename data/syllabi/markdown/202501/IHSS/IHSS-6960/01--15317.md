## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

- With the seemingly sudden advent of ChatGPT, Image Generation, Face recognition, and many other so-called Artifically Intelligent systems arising in the past few years, many people have come to believe that AI is a relatively new area. In this course, we will learn about the history of AI from someone who lived it – using a draft of a book that was written by Eugene Charniak, an early AI researcher who passed away suddenly this past year (*AI & I: An Intellectual History of Artificial Intelligence).* Of particular interest, we will explore how the past two decades have seen a split in the approaches to AI, one based on computing power and data, the other on a combination of human cognition and symbolic reasoning systems. Students will learn about the strengths and limitations of modern AI systems, at the same time coming to understand the roots of the field.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to explain some of the key events and researchers involved in the field (evaluated through submitted short essays)
- o Our text will be presenting a specific view of the history, from the author’s own experiences. The Professor and guest lecturers will be presenting from their own histories/interests. Students will need to understand how to explore the differing opinions. (evaluated through a final presentation)
- What fun would it be to take a course about AI that didn’t include using some of the up-to-date tools such as ChatGPT, DALL-E and other such tools -- students will learn to evaluate the capabilities and deficiencies of these systems, set in a historical context (those who don't learn from the past are doomed to repeat it)
- It is impossible to understand the potential impacts of regulation with respect to AI regulation and legislation without understanding what has and has not worked in the past.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- This class will involve writing a few of short (about 500-1000 word) pieces, some with and some without the help of AI assistants (ChatGPT primarily).
- Class discussion will be a crucial aspect of the learning, so participation will be important.
- A project with 2-3 person groups writing an essay and presenting on a topic in the history of AI will be the final project and will likely have several parts. The written submission of this project (including slides and text) will be required, but no final exam will be held.
- UNUSUAL to RPI, the assumption coming in is that you will earn an A in the course. You can lose that A by not handing in assignments, missing a large number of classes, and/or not doing the project. The course is not expected to be a heavy work load in terms of what you produce, but will attempt to push you to think very differently about AI by the end of the term – i.e. the emphasis is on thinking and analyzing
