### Prerequisites or Other Requirements:

This course assumes a solid foundation in econometric methods, specifically those covered in Econometrics 1, which is a required prerequisite. Students are expected to have proficiency in mathematical proof writing, elementary algebra, and multivariate calculus, including partial differentiation, integration, and optimization. A working knowledge of probability theory, including the Law of Large Numbers (LLN) and the Central Limit Theorem (CLT), is also essential. Familiarity with the concepts and methods from an undergraduate course in statistics or econometrics is assumed.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

References

• Econometrics (BH), by Bruce Hansen, 2021.

• Econometric Analysis of Cross Section and Panel Data (JW_PD), by Jeffrey M.Wooldridge, 2010.

• Introductory Econometrics (JW_ECM), by Jeffrey M.Wooldridge, 2016.

Supplementary Reading Materials

In addition to the primary textbook(s), the following supplementary materials are recommended

to deepen your understanding of the topics covered in this course:

• Econometric Analysis, by William H. Greene

• Econometrics, by Fumio Hayashi

• Mostly Harmless Econometrics, by Joshua D. Angrist and Jörn-Steffen Pischke

• Introduction to Econometrics, by James H. Stock and Mark W. Watson

• Microeconometrics: Methods and Applications, by A. Colin Cameron and Pravin K. Trivedi

As the course progresses, additional materials, including academic articles, lecture notes, and

problem sets, will be distributed in class to complement the readings and provide context for

the material. These resources are intended to enhance your grasp of advanced econometric

techniques and their applications.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Problem Sets. Problem sets will be assigned approximately once per week, with one week

to complete each set. They are generally evaluated for completion, though specific parts may

be graded in detail if deemed beneficial for learning. Please review your answers against the

provided solutions and reach out for help if needed.

Each problem set will include both written exercises and computer-based exercises. For the

computer exercises, you must submit your responses (a PDF file) along with the corresponding

code (an R Markdown file) via the LMS by 8 PM EDT on the due date. Late submissions will not

be accepted.

Collaboration is encouraged, and you may work with peers to solve problems. However, it

is essential that you also practice independently to develop the skills necessary for exams and

future work. Each student must write and submit their own answers for every assignment.

Copying answers from online sources or other students is strictly prohibited and will be handled

in accordance with the Academic Integrity Policies outlined below.

Remember, problem sets are primarily a learning tool designed to reinforce concepts and

prepare you for exams. They are not graded harshly, so focus on making a genuine effort to

understand and solve the problems.

C. Exams. There will be two exams, one midterm and one final exam. All exams will be held

in class. They will be closed book, closed notes exams, however students are permitted and

encouraged to bring a 8.5" °ø 11" sheet (one-sided) of notes. Make-ups for exams will not be given

except under extraordinary circumstances.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
