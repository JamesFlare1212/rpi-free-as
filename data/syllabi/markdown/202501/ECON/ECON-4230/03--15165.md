### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

This is a course on environmental economics. By the end of the course, you should have a clear understanding of market-based tools used in environmental policy-making. This will help you analyze policies targeting environmental issues such as natural resource extraction, energy consumption, pollution, and climate change.  
Topics include (but are not limited to): non-market valuation, taxes/ subsidies, cap-and-trade, pollution constraints, discounting, managing renewable and non-renewable resources, environmental justice, and climate impacts. We will read news articles and papers relevant to the course materials. Documentary screenings on relevant topics are to be expected, followed by in-class discussions led by the instructor.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

### **Grade Component Descriptions**

#### **Student Presentations (15%)**

- **Presentation (10%)**  
  There will be **10 student presentations** throughout the semester, beginning **January 17** and occurring most Fridays. The readings and presentation schedule are posted on the LMS website.

  Students will work in **pairs** and deliver a **20-minute summary** of the assigned reading. Presentations must include **supplemental graphs and images** relevant to the topic.

  Each presentation must conclude with **five open-ended discussion questions** addressing broader implications, potential criticisms, and relevance to **current environmental policies**. **Slides must be submitted on LMS by 2:00 PM on the presentation day.**

- **Discussion Facilitation (5%)**  
  Following the presentation, the group will lead a **class discussion**, actively engaging with the reading by posing questions and providing commentary.

#### **Participation (15%)**

Participation is an important component of the final grade. Students must attend class regularly and earn participation points by:

1. Actively engaging in reading discussions
2. Completing **weekly in-class reading quizzes**
3. Working on **in-class exercises**

Students unable to attend class should notify the instructor in advance via email.

#### **Homework Assignments (15%)**

There will be **four** homework assignments throughout the course, each worth **3.75%** of the total grade.

#### **Open-Notes Exams (30%)**

The course includes **two non-cumulative exams** consisting of short and long response questions. Students may bring their own **class notes**, but **cannot** reference lecture slides, textbooks, assigned readings, or use the Internet. **Taking clear, organized notes in class will be beneficial for the exams.**

The class immediately before each exam will be reserved for review. **There will be no make-up exams**—missing an exam results in a grade of zero.

#### **Policy Paper (25%)**

Each student will write a **1500-word policy paper** examining an **environmental policy issue** of their choice. The paper should present a clear analysis and evaluation of the policy. The following deadlines apply:

- **March 14, 2024 (12:00 PM):** Submit a **one-paragraph summary** of the chosen topic
- **March 28, 2024 (12:00 PM):** Submit a **first draft or detailed outline**
- **April 15, 2024 (12:00 PM):** Submit the **final draft**

#### **Late Assignment Policy**

Late homework submissions will be penalized according to the following formula:

**Grade = max{0, (Assignment Grade) × (1 - 0.2 × Days Late)}**

This means that **each day an assignment is late, 20% of the grade is deducted.** If an assignment is late by **five or more days**, the grade will be **zero**.

For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own.
