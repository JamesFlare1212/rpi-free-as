### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

Students will collectively work on developing a site to be determined. There will be about 5 “building teams” of 6 members each (4 structural, 2 geotechnical students) to work on a specific building.

There will be 3 “non-building teams” that will assist and coordinate with the building teams. The transportation team will work on site design, circulation, parking, trip generation and traffic flow. There will be two transportation sub-teams – one for physical design and the other for systems design such as traffic engineering, highway capacity, etc. The water resources team will work on site drainage and water supply. The sustainability team will work on LEED and Envision certifications.

Teams will be assigned by the instructors on the basis of skill distribution.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The primary motivation of this course is for students to learn about the processes of civil engineering design through working on a practical design problem. The course is intended to integrate skills learned over the entire undergraduate curriculum and not necessarily to learn new engineering techniques. The course is intended to improve students’ skills in project planning, teamwork, problem solving, communication, and presentation. This course is not only required as part of the civil engineering curriculum, but it is also the only course in the civil engineering curriculum which fulfills the School of Engineering communications intensive course requirement.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The projects for this semester will focus on the development of a parcel in the Town of Bethlehem. Existing topographic maps are in LMS as are traffic and soil boring data.  
Each building team will search for a building to design. The teams will seek an architectural rendering, a typical floor plan, and an elevation. These designs might include a commercial building, hotel, commercial office, or a medium density residential building. Students who, through internships or personal relationships, have access to architectural renderings, floor plans and elevation drawings may, upon authorization of the instructors, use these plans for their project. Each team will develop a plan for the building project, structural and geotechnical design for one of the facilities, develop a construction plan, and prepare a construction cost estimate.  
The task of the transportation team is to perform a site design including parking, pedestrian and vehicle circulation and to develop a traffic study for the site, including appropriate traffic control devices. Modern site design concepts such as “complete” streets should be included in the design.  
The water resources team is responsible for site drainage and water supply requirements.

The **sustainability team** is responsible for the incorporation of sustainability features (e.g. LEED and/or Envision certification) into the building and transportation teams.

**Team organization:**

Each team will have a team leader whose primary responsibility is to coordinate team activities and act as the liaison between the team members and the instructional staff. The instructor may assign a team leader.

Each team should maintain a diary and notebook which will contain your work for the semester. It will be part of the basis of your grade. This also makes a good portfolio for future job interviews.

Team members should make use of a communications tool such as Google groups to assure good communications related to your project.

In addition to the team leader, each team member will have some collateral responsibility for the project. Suggested responsibilities include (1) responsibility for project deliverables (2) responsibility for liaison with course instructional staff (3) responsibility for liaison with mentor staff (4) responsibility for quality assurance (5) coordination with transportation, water, and sustainability teams. These collateral responsibilities are to **ensure that the activity is done – not to actually do the work.**

**Professional advisors:**

Each team will be assigned one or more volunteer professional advisors (mentors) who will assist and critique your work. Building teams will have an individual structural advisor and a geotechnical advisor. Their role is to be “coaches” on professional practice as well as subject matter experts. Several of them are RPI alumni who have successfully completed this course. Your team will meet with advisors at least three times throughout the semester.

Your first meeting with the **external advisor** must be completed by the end of the second week of the semester. Your advisor should be consulted on major design decisions and apprised of your progress through meeting minutes and progress reports.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

You are expected to attend class **promptly** and regularly. In addition, your team may require additional meetings to accomplish the work. We suggest **at least two** weekly meetings outside of class time be used for project work. Teamwork is part of your professional practice assessment.

### Other Course Policies:

The use of cellphones in class is ***strictly prohibited***.

Each design project will include some interesting design feature such as a cantilever or covered entrance which will require some structural analysis. A strictly “big box” design will not be sufficient.

Each team will be required to develop and maintain a project schedule. The baseline schedule in assignment #3 should be used throughout the semester as a guide for progressing the project. It is recommended that each team have a copy of some scheduling software such as Microsoft Project. A freeware project management software system is available at www.jxproject.com.

Technical  
• Calculations should be clear and be logically derived, citing design criteria when applicable. The calculations should be able to be followed by others and yourself years down the road.  
• Engineering is about considering alternatives. When you design, consider multiple alternatives seriously. You should also do several iterations of your calculations.
