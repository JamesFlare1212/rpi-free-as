## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Water Resources Engineering provides a foundational treatment of the design of systems to control the quantity, timing, and distribution of water to meet the needs of human habitation and the environment within the Civil and Environmental Engineering curriculum at Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute. Topics covered include the hydrological cycle, design criteria for water distribution systems and water storage reservoirs, pump performance, applied pipe flow analysis, and pipe networks. Open channel hydraulics is also strongly featured, including the performance of hydraulic control structures such as weirs, bumps, and contractions. Theory is strongly complemented with experimental laboratory sessions. Contemporary and social and environmental issues are introduced at various points throughout the term including equity issues related to infrastructure, green infrastructure, and climate change.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand the major components and performance criteria of water distribution systems
- Appreciate non-technical perspectives on water and water use (social, economic, environmental, etc.)
- Model physical systems (pipes, pumps, pipe networks, channels, streams, hydraulic structures) by employing principles of conservation of mass, energy, and momentum to develop equations describing their behavior.
- An ability to identify, formulate, and solve complex engineering problems by applying principles of engineering, science, and mathematics.
- Solve conservation equations via concepts of specific energy to determine characteristics (flow, runoff) and compute a variety of flow and water surface elevation scenarios and observe these in laboratory experiments.
- Develop skills in computer modelling of water distribution systems and open channel flow systems using software
- An ability to recognize ethical and professional responsibilities in engineering situations and make informed judgments, which must consider the impact of engineering solutions in global, economic, environmental, and societal contexts.
- An ability to communicate effectively with a range of audiences
- An ability to function effectively on a team whose members together provide leadership, create a collaborative and inclusive environment, establish goals, plan tasks, and meet objectives.

## Course Calendar

### Calendar Details:

Organization:

The course is structured into:

- **Lecture**: two-110-minute lectures per week. Each class will be divided into lecture sessions and in-class participation sessions by the students. Lectures notes will be posted on the Blackboard LMS.
- **Labs**: three laboratory experiments and a software “lab” held on Wednesday by sections. Typical labs run for 1 hour and are conducted with small teams of 4-6 classmates.
- **Seminar**: four guest seminar speakers discussing various aspects of lead service line issues in Flint MI and around the USA are scheduled throughout February and March, as outlined below. Students must read “The Poisoned City - Flint's Water and the American Urban Tragedy, by Anna Clark”. Seminar is from 1 – 2pm in the CBIS Auditorium according to the schedule below.
- **Recitation/office hours**: the instructor and TAs host weekly recitations or office hours as listed above.

Labs:

There are 3 laboratory experiments and an EPANET software “lab”. Attendance at all labs is required. Students will engage with the experimental method by observing, modelling, predicting, and evaluating fluid behavior. Typically, a theoretical model will predict results, and experimental observations will be used to evaluate the limits of the theory. Students will write a succinct report on the experiment and discuss the experimental observations in the context of theory. The labs for S25 are:

- EPANET (flow modelling software. Note: does not include a lab report and is not included in lab grade)
- Pumps
- Contraction
- Spillway & hydraulic jump

There will be 10 homework assignments, issued according to the schedule below. Homework assignments will be submitted digitally on LMS as a PDF. Students should scan their assignments and ensure good scan quality and legibility. The lowest homework grade will be dropped (HW 10 excluded).

*Late HW submission polic*y:

- 1-point deduction - submitted within 1 hour of deadline;
- 4-point deduction - submitted between 1 and 24 hours of deadline;

Some extended answer problems will require the use of software. Students are encouraged to become familiar with MATLAB, however, most packages will suffice (i.e., MS Excel, Python, etc.). Homework assignments will be graded as follows:

- One question (selected by instructor or TA) graded for correctness (5/10 points);
- The remaining homework questions are complete (5/10 points).

You are encouraged to collaborate with other students on homework assignments, however, each student is expected to submit their own work. **Students found to have copied any part of their submission from a solution manual or from an online database will be considered in breach of academic honesty. Appropriate recourse will be** **sort based on the circumstances.**

*Homework presentation:*

**1)** It is encouraged that students use “ruled” paper (physical or digital). Each problem should begin at the top of a new page with the problem # and text problem # shown.

**2)** The upper margin of the first page of your homework should show your name, the homework assignment number, the date the homework is turned in, and the page number. The upper margin of the first page of each problem should show the problem number and page number. Other pages should include the page number. The page number should be given in the upper right corner in the format A/B where A is the current page number and B is the total number of pages in your homework.

**3)** Computations should be arranged in logical order and each step recorded in full (other engineers should be able to easily follow your work). The order of presentation of work should be as shown below. Any work that does not substantially follow this order of presentation will receive no credit.

\- List given data and diagrams

\- List required results

------------------------------------- (use a line to separate information given to you from your work)

\- Calculations and diagrams

\- Final answer (boxed in)

**4)** Do not make short-cuts. Perform one operation at a time and make a complete record of it. Only experienced engineers can safely take short-cuts. An exception may be made for repetitive or tabular computations, in which case it is enough to provide a complete record of a sample calculation.

**5)** Final results should be clearly indicated by *boxing* them in.

**6)** The correct units should be provided for intermediate results and final answers. A result is of no value if units are not provided. Note that careful consideration of units will often prevent mistakes.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

This statement is intended to cover most, but not all, forms of academic dishonesty. (You should refer to The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities for detailed information on Institute policy in this regard.)

Generative AI and AI-assisted technologies:

Regarding the use of Generative AI and AI-assisted technologies by students, the course policy is as follows:

- Only use these technologies to improve readability and language, not to replace key student learning tasks such as interpreting data or drawing analysis conclusions. Apply the technology with human oversight and control, and carefully review and edit the result, as Al can generate authoritative-sounding output that can be incorrect, incomplete, or biased.
- Disclose in their submissions the use of Al and Al-assisted technologies in the working process. Students are ultimately responsible and accountable for the contents of their submissions and must therefore be able to demonstrate that the material generated has been cross-checked and verified from a different source/reference.

Furthermore, in general, students using Generative AI and AI-assisted technologies as a type of brainstorming tool should:

- Indicate that you used AI as a supplemental content generator.
- Specify the AI system you used and the date of the last update.
- Check the accuracy of all data, information, and knowledge you receive.
- Be vigilant to avoid committing plagiarism.
- Recognize that the system has no conscience, morality, or ethics (and maybe lacks the ability to be creative/innovative)

***CEE Statement on Department Culture***

The wide variety of human attributes, both intrinsic and extrinsic, whether self-identified or perceived result in a collective mixture of similarities and differences, including in the way we learn. When leveraged, diversity leads to improved creativity, innovation, and stronger outcomes for all. We recognize that a variety of students come with a variety of needs. We therefore strive to adjust resources and support accordingly to address specific situations and learning styles, with the aim of fostering an environment in which *all* students can access high-quality educational opportunities. For details on the Institute's diversity, equity, and inclusion policies, see https://info.rpi.edu/diversity.

Maintaining a productive and inclusive educational environment is a shared responsibility. All members of the classroom community (students, instructors, TAs, guests) are expected to act in accordance with the values outlined above. Any conduct that impedes the ability of any classroom member to participate fully in or benefit from the desired educational experience will be addressed and may be subject to disciplinary action at the discretion of the instructor, department head, or member of the administration as deemed appropriate. Misconduct such as hostile behavior, derogatory language, harassment of any form, or sex-based discrimination as defined by RPI Title IX and Sexual Misconduct Policy or included in the Student Handbook, will not be tolerated.
