## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Goal:** The goal of the course is to provide a “very rigorous” introductory level training with math deriviation in quantum mechanics and its materials/devices applications, for the audience of engineering students. The materials and topic are carefully chosen by Prof. Bao to cover most of the quantum topics that engineering students would potentially encounter in the research.

**Learning Outcomes:** 1) Students will be able to understand the concept of basic quantum mechanics, 2) able to do some very basic calculations 3) Students will be able to communicate with researchers and understand the current focus of quantum materials research and applications.

**Text:** Lecture notes will be the main resource for the course, additional reading lists will also be provided during each class

**Academic Integrity:** The relationship between students and faculty is based upon trust, and the continued maintenance of this trust is necessary for education to be successful. Students need to trust faculty to make appropriate judgments as to the structure and content of the course. Faculty members need to trust that the work turned in by students represents their own effort. Violation of this trust undermines the educational process. In addition, as engineers, you may be designing products (e.g., aircraft, the computers that control them, or the runways they land on) that lives depend on. As a result, there is no tolerance for breach of academic integrity such as cheating, plagiarizing, or inappropriate sharing of exams.

**Anyone caught cheating or plagiarizing will be subject to disciplinary actions, such as a failing grade for the course and referral to the Dean of Students.**

Cheating includes sharing answers, stealing answers, and taking take-home exams together (in person or remotely). Plagiarism means copying words from someone else's work without citation, even if you "change the sentence a bit." If you “only” share your exams to someone else, you are as guilty as the person copying it. If you do use material from an appropriate source (e.g., the Internet or a journal paper), make sure you refer to it properly in your presentation. The complete Academic Integrity guidelines are available in the “Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights & Responsibilities” found on The Dean of Students website (https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/05/29/2020/notice-student-rights-and-responsibilities).

If a student has to miss an exam for an unavoidable and compelling reason, the student may be excused from the exam at the instructor’s discretion, provided that the student presents a suitable written excuse. A written excuse is required from the Dean, a doctor, or a coach. ***The percent credit of the midterm exam will be allocated to the final presentation (The final presentation is now worth 100% instead of 50%).***

**Mon/Thu Topics (subject to** **minor** **change****s****)**

**Weekly topics:**

#01 Introduction of course and instructor. Review of classical physics. The birth of quantum mechanics.

#02 Plank model and Bohr model.

#03 Schrödinger’s Equation

#04 Schrödinger’s Equation (Harmonic oscillator and Hydrogen model)

#05 Dirac Notation, Uncertainty principle, more examples, summary of the concept of quantum mechanics

#06 Crystal structure and symmetry, periodic lattice, reciprocal space, symmetry, and defects

#07 Band theory of solids, Bloch wave, band structure, density of states, and quasiparticles

#08 Quantum confinement, Van der Waals materials, and their optoelectronic properties

Take-home midterm

#09 Van der Waals materials and their optoelectronic properties. Color centers in solids, Quantum dots, and single-photon sources

#10 Superconductivity

#11 Quantum Bit and Single-Qubit Operation

#12 Entanglement and Multi-Qubit Operation

#13 Quantum circuits and Quantum Algorithm

#14 Physical Implementation of Quantum Computer

#15 Final proposal presentation 20 min each person 5 min Q&A

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
