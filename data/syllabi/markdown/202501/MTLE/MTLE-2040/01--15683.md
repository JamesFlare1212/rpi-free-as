## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Elementary programming concepts, basic data analysis and visualization using the Python programming language for students with little or no prior programming experience.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Lecture notes will be the primary reference for this course. Supplementary reading will be recommended from the following free online resources:

- *Think Python* by Allen B. Downey. Available online freely at https://greenteapress.com/wp/think-python-2e
- Documentation and tutorials of the Python packages we will use:

- ◦ NumPy: https://numpy.org/doc/stable/user/absolute_beginners.html
- ◦ MatPlotLib: https://matplotlib.org/tutorials
- ◦ SciPy: https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/tutorial

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- write computer programs for basic calculations and simulations
- visualize and analyze engineering data using computer programs

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

We will use three assignments to assess students learning outcomes.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Homework assignments every two weeks with short programming exercises. Attend all lectures and complete all assignments to pass the course.

Note: this course is graded satisfactory/unsatisfactory.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).
