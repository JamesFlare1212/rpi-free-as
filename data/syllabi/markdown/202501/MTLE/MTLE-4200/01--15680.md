## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

The class lectures will closely follow and complement the book

*Electronic Properties of Engineering Materials* by James Livingston (Publisher: Wiley).  
You should come prepared for each class session by completing your reading assignments to facilitate the assimilation and understanding of the material presented in class. Subsequent multiple readings during homework problem solving, preparation for the weekly quizzes, final exam will ensure an efficient and fruitful learning experience.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course is aimed at developing an understanding of the electronic structure of materials and their relationships with observable electronic and optical properties described by simple classical and quantum models.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Tentative schedule of topics and reading assignments:** Sessions 1-10 will describe electrons using a classical approach. Sessions 11-13 will provide an introduction to quantum mechanics. Sessions 14-28 will describe electronic properties using a quantum mechanical approach.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- a basic understanding of how solids interact with electromagnetic fields and radiation;
- a knowledge to relate and explain observable electronic and optical properties of different materials classes (e.g., metals, semiconductors, insulators, polymers) in terms of electronic structure;
- a familiarity with fundamental concepts of quantum mechanics as relevant to materials science and engineering (e.g., electron/X-ray wavelengths for spectroscopy and microscopy); and
- an acquaintance and facility with terminology used to describe the electronic structure across multiple disciplines (e.g., physics, chemistry, electrical engineering), e.g., bandgap, effective mass, HOMO-LUMO separation, and delocalized states, to name a few.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

The relationship between students and faculty is based upon trust, and the continued maintenance of this trust is necessary for education to be successful. Students need to trust faculty to make appropriate judgments as to the content and structure of the course. Faculty members need to trust that the work turned in by students represents their own effort. Violation of this trust undermines the educational process. In addition, as engineers, you may be designing products (aircraft, the computers that run them, or the runways they land on) that lives depend on. Hence, there is no tolerance for breach of academic integrity such as cheating, plagiarizing, or inappropriate sharing of laboratories or quizzes. *Anyone caught cheating or plagiarizing for the first time will receive zero points in the assignment or test. A second offence will result in an F grade for the course. The Dean of Students office may also decide on further measures.*

Cheating includes sharing answers, stealing answers, and placing text strings or equations on your calculator or phone. Any interaction with a mobile device during an exam without explicit permission of the instructor will be considered to be an act of cheating and will be treated as such. Plagiarism means copying words from someone’s work, even if you “change the sentence a bit”. If you share your homework, you are as guilty as the person copying it. If you do use any source other than the textbook or your notes from class, you need to ALWAYS reference it.
