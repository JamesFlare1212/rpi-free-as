## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Rigorous development of classical thermodynamics as applied to prediction of materials properties. Nonideal gases, solutions, phase equilibria, chemical equilibria, defects.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

David R. Gaskell and David E. Laughlin “Introduction to the Thermodynamics of Materials”; Taylor and Francis, 6th Edition, 2018.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The two main objectives of the course are to:  
\- provide a thorough knowledge of fundamentals of classical thermodynamics, and  
\- explore some selected applications of thermodynamics in materials science.  
To achieve these objectives, we will resort to lecture style instructions comprising of the introduction of new concepts, derivation of formulas, and solutions of sample problems. Lectures will, for the most part, follow the order of the text, although not always on a page-by-page basis.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Upon successful completion of the course the students will acquire knowledge of fundamental classical thermodynamics and will be able to (1) derive thermodynamics relationships using partial differential derivatives and calculus, (2) relate principles of thermodynamics to the phase behavior of materials, (3) assess the response of the materials to thermodynamic conditions such as pressure, temperature, etc., and (4) relate basic thermodynamic properties of materials to the behavior of atoms and molecules, i.e., to make a connection between microscopic and macroscopic world.
