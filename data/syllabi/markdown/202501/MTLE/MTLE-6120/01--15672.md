## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The goal of the course is twofold: to present modern concepts of the electronic properties of the materials, and to develop the ability to read (and understand) scientific papers. The first goal will be achieved through a series of lectures and sets of graded quizzes, midterm exams and presentation. A considerable attention is devoted to insulating materials, not only because they are a part of my research interest and numerous groups within the department, but mostly because they illustrate a number of important concepts in the solid state physics. For the second goal, we will organize students’ presentations of selected scientific papers or area of active research.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

1. **Theoretical Background:**

- **Maxwell’s Equations in Materials:** Understanding the foundational principles governing electromagnetic fields in different materials.
- **Classical Drude Theory of Conduction:** Exploration of electrical conductivity in metals.
- **Basic Quantum Mechanics:** Introduction to the fundamental concepts of quantum mechanics.
- **Atoms, Many-Electron Theories, and the Periodic Table:** Examining the structure of atoms and their organization in the periodic table.
- **Quantum Kinetics and Fermi’s Golden Rule:** Delving into the kinetics of particles at the quantum level.
- **Band Theory of Solids:** Understanding how electrons are arranged in solid materials.

2. **Material Properties:**

- **Fermi Theory of Metals:** Insights into the electronic properties of metals.
- **Electron Transport:** Studying phonons and electron-phonon scattering.
- **Intrinsic and Extrinsic Semiconductors:** Examination of semiconductor properties and their classification.
- **Insulating Materials:** Understanding different types such as dielectrics, ferroelectrics, and piezoelectrics.
- **Magnetism:** Exploring various forms including diamagnetism, paramagnetism, and ferromagnetism, as well as hysteresis.
- **Superconductivity:** Studying the phenomenon of zero electrical resistance.
- **Optical Properties:** Investigating absorption, emission, luminescence, fluorescence, and lasing.
- **Low-dimensional Materials:** A look into the properties of materials with reduced dimensions.

3. **Interface Properties:**

- **Metal-Vacuum Interfaces:** Exploring thermionic and field emission.
- **Metal-Metal Junctions:** Understanding the Seebeck effect, thermocouples, and the Peltier effect.
- **Metal-Semiconductor Schottky Junctions:** Examining their characteristics and applications.
- **Semiconductor p-n Junction Diodes:** Discussing LEDs, lasers, and photovoltaic technologies.
- **Semiconductor Transistors:** Covering their roles in logic and memory applications.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- o Understanding the Physics of Electrons in Materials: Students will gain a comprehensive understanding of how electrons' behavior in various materials gives rise to a diverse range of electronic, magnetic, and optical properties. o Application of Material Properties: Students will learn how these properties can be harnessed and optimized for use in technological applications. This includes an exploration of practical implementations in various fields. o Navigating Research Literature: Students will develop the skill to effectively navigate and critically analyze current literature in the active research areas of electronic, magnetic, or optical materials. This includes understanding the latest advancements and methodologies in these domains.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- Regular quizzes based on material from two previous classes and most recent homework assignment; one lowest quiz score will not be counted towards the grade. (Homework assignments should be completed and self-graded with posted solutions in preparation for the quiz.)
- An in-class midterm examination in March, which along with previous quizzes will be used to provide you with performance feedback.
- An in-class final examination in the month of April .
- A short oral presentation on an area of active research in insulating, electronic, optical or magnetic materials in the final class (~Month of April).
- Participation in class by asking questions and contributing to discussions

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

**Academic Integrity:** Trust is the cornerstone of the student-teacher relationship. This trust is twofold: students must trust that teachers design courses thoughtfully, with well-considered structure and content. Conversely, teachers must trust that the work students submit is authentically their own. Breaches of this trust erode the integrity of the educational process. As part of upholding this trust, students are encouraged to familiarize themselves with the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement. These documents outline various forms of academic dishonesty and are essential to understanding the standards expected in our academic community. In this course, collaboration is actively encouraged for self-graded homework assignments, which do not count towards the final course grade. However, it is important to note that during quizzes and examinations, while books and printed notes are permitted, collaboration is strictly prohibited. Any violation of this policy will have serious consequences. A first offense will result in a zero score for the affected assignment or examination. A second offense will lead to an F grade for the entire course
