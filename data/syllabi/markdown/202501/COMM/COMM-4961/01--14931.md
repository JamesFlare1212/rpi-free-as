## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Give students an understanding of how public policy and legal regimes influence free speech practices in the United States.
- Provide students with an understanding of specific legal norms and tendencies on specific free speech issues including copyright and intellectual property, government censorship, and obscenity.
- Give students an understanding of the historic forces which have shaped freedom of speech.
- Provide students with training in researching and composing evidence based arguments on free speech issues.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Unit Exams: Each of the four units in the course will be assessed using an exam which will be completed in class. Each exam will include short answer and multiple choice questions. Each exam will be accompanied by a study guide provided by the instructor.

Read and Response Questions: Over the course of the semester students will respond to four readings selected by the instructor. The response will take the form of a short essay response to a prompt provided by the instructor. These responses may require a very small amount of additional research on the part of the student. Instructor will discuss the prompt in class prior to the due date.

Research Paper: Over the course of the semester students will complete an extended research project on a free speech controversy of their choice. These papers can either take the form of an analytical research project or a policy proposal. These research papers are broken up into discrete stages over the course of the semester to facilitate instructor feedback. Grades for each stage are independent and will reflect specific expectations for the assignment discussed at greater length in the assignment sheets on the LMS. The final draft of these papers will be no less than 3000 words in length and cite no less than 10 sources of academically rigorous quality.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**AI Policy:**

Recent advances in artificial intelligence have created a number of tools which could theoretically be used to replace student generated work. The aim of the course is to teach the skills which this use of artificial intelligence circumvents. For the purpose of this course the use of artificial intelligence except in specific circumstances discussed and approved by the instructor ahead of time will result in a zero being awarded for the assignment.

**Academic Misconduct:**

Academic misconduct undermines the integrity of RPI, the course, and the person who commits misconduct. The instructor takes such concerns extremely seriously and any violations will be handled according to the process outlined in the RPI student handbook, including an initial assignment of a zero for work which violates the policy.
