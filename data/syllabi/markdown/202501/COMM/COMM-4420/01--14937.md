## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Analyze technological artifacts. Identify specific ways technological mediation impacts knowledge, needs, emotions, values, and experiences that are both individual and social-cultural in origin. • Engage clients. Learn to more deeply see and listen to your future client’s needs more deeply while guiding them to more successful outcomes. • Explain your ideas in ways that reflect a thorough understanding of the subject and your audience’s needs, knowledge, values, etc. by anticipating and answering the audience’s likely questions and elaborating appropriately (i.e. to consider things your audience takes as “givens”). • Explain appropriate technology, not only in your design processes but in the kinds of questions you answer or avoid, and the kinds of information you choose to include and exclude. • Practice and hone your speaking and listening communication skills in both one-on-one, small group, and large group settings. • Reflect on how you want technology to function. • Begin to establish a toolkit of “practices” that you can take with you for designing your own content and analyzing the work of others.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1. **Undergraduate Reading Journal Reflections (30%).** For your main after-class work you will be reading and reflecting on the readings and constructing reading notes. These “notes” your notes **10 notes will be turned in for credit**. The hope is that these notes will serve as part of your portfolio, reference for class discussion, and archive for future work. Your notes should be about two paragraphs long addressing the main point of the reading, applications for your work, and how the reading informs/can be understood through the lens of the pervious lectures or readings. I am looking for evidence that you read the chapters closely and understood the content. Hand these in as you do them in hard copy.

2. **HCI based sustainability project 30%.** In lieu of taking a midterm, you will work on pitching a creative HCI project that focuses on sustainability as part of your portfolio. To make this work you need to talk with me about the project and come up with a plan. Both the points and deadline for completion will be the same as a midterm. Projects require professor approval by the end of week 4. Tests are fine but community engagement is even better. As RPI builds its commitment to sustainability let’s do our part and use our time together in a positive way. These projects can look at webpages UX, be original object design focusing on computational technology (robotics, software, hardware), or even building your own webpage. In addition, I will accept projects that audit the energy use of technologies- particularly in the context of our own community (thinking quantum, servers, labs etc.).

3. **Final Project: User Experience Study (35%).**
   1. In small groups or as individuals you will pick a case study to run a complete UX study using one of the many methods learned in class.
   2. The goal of this project is to overview issues and opportunities that can enhance the success of these clients’ projects.
   3. This project will use a common format.
   4. Group based and thus will be 15-20 pages (total for the group) in length (the 15-20 does not include your reference page and cover page/letter). Individual papers should be 10 pages.
   5. The aim of the project is to help these clients and support their mission through UX.
   6. You will, as part of the overall grade, quickly present this work to the class.

**Participation (5% of final grade)**

Showing up to most things in life is the most meaningful; it’s even more meaningful when you are fully attentive, fully engaged, fully present. We are all human, and we all have an infinite number of other things we can (or could) attend to at any given moment. My invitation for the semester is to **be fully present and attentive** in class. *This means silencing your cell phones and keeping your computer away unless it is required for class that day* (which I will communicate to you during the class meeting prior).

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

*The* *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* and *The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement* define various forms of academic dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade *must represent your own work*. **If you plagiarize once,** you will receive an **F on the assignment.** If you plagiarize again, you will **fail the entire course** and may be reported to the *Dean of Students* as appropriate. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask me for clarification. The basics: when using information from another source **(including anonymous online sources**) always cite the original text or URL and distinguish it from your own commentary. If you directly cut and paste another person’s language, you need to use it as a **quotation**. *We will talk specifically about citation styles and how to make sure that you are giving “credit” when it is required of you in spirit of scholarly collaboration.* In regard to using AI (e.g. ChatGPT or other Open AI resources): While it may be tempting to use this resource, please note that there is no “check” to ensure the information that you are receiving is correct. Researchers are finding that ChatGPT can provide misinformation putting your grade at risk. Using this resource is NOT considered *your own work* and **will** be deemed as an instance of academic dishonesty if discovered. Easy ways we can tell you used AI writing programs: 1. You are unable to explain the concepts in your own words 2. You are using vocabulary that is uncommon 3. There is a dramatic difference between your in-class writing skills and your assignments. Rather than try to game the system why not take this as an opportunity to grow as a writer.
