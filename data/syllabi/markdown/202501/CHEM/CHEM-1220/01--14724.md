## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate a comprehensive understanding of the core areas of chemistry (analytical, inorganic, organic, and physical) to lay a strong foundation for advanced studies in science and engineering disciplines.
- Apply quantitative problem-solving skills to chemistry and chemical systems, integrating fundamental concepts from chemistry and mathematics to analyze and solve complex problems
- Identify and describe key mechanisms of organic reactions, including substitution (SN1, SN2) and elimination (E1, E2) reactions of alkyl halides, demonstrating an understanding of reaction conditions, intermediates, and transition states.
- Adhere to best practices in laboratory safety and technique, including accurate mass and volume measurements, solution preparation, dilution, titration, elementary separation, and synthesis methods, while maintaining a clean and safe working environment.
- Collect, analyze, and interpret experimental data using scientific methods, accurately identifying sources of experimental error and applying appropriate statistical and analytical techniques to draw valid conclusions.
- Utilize scientific literature and reputable sources to inform experimental design, interpret results, and engage in independent research, demonstrating proficiency in referencing and synthesizing information from peer-reviewed articles and textbooks.
- Communicate scientific concepts clearly and effectively both in written and oral formats, conveying chemistry-related ideas, inquiries, and results to professionals in academia, industry, and research.
- Develop a curiosity for chemistry that fosters continued learning and exploration beyond the course and into life.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

## Academic Integrity:

The foundation of the relationship between students and faculty is built on mutual trust. For education to be effective, this trust must be maintained. As students, you are expected to trust faculty to design and structure the course appropriately, while faculty must trust that the work you submit represents your own effort and abilities. Any violation of this trust undermines the educational process and the integrity of the academic community.

In this course, maintaining academic integrity is of the utmost importance. As future professionals, many of you will be responsible for designing critical systems—such as aircraft, medical devices, and infrastructure — where the consequences of failure can be severe. In the same way, academic dishonesty in this course can have significant consequences. Therefore, **any form of cheating, plagiarism, or inappropriate sharing of work will not be tolerated.**

Examples of academic dishonesty include, but are not limited to:

- **Cheating**: This includes sharing answers during exams or assignments, using unauthorized materials (such as searching for answers online), or stealing answers from others.
- **Plagiarism**: This is the act of copying someone else’s work, even if you modify the wording slightly. If you use someone else’s ideas or results, you must properly cite the source.
- **Inappropriate sharing of work**: If you share your laboratory reports, assignments, or answers with others, you are just as guilty as the person who copies your work. This includes submitting work from previous years or copying results from past lab reports.

If you use external sources for your work, be sure to cite them appropriately. Failure to do so is considered plagiarism, even if the source is widely available or not specifically mentioned in the assignment instructions.

The **Rensselaer Student Handbook** and the **Dean of Students Office Webpage** (link) provide detailed descriptions of what constitutes academic dishonesty. Please take the time to familiarize yourself with these guidelines, and do not hesitate to ask if you are unsure about what constitutes a violation.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

## **Top Reasons Students Struggle or Fail in General Chemistry**

Chemistry can feel like an intimidating subject for many students, even those with a genuine interest in science, due to its reputation for challenging grades. However, chemistry doesn’t have to be the obstacle it’s often made out to be. By avoiding these common mistakes, you can improve your chances of success.

1. **Psyching Yourself Out**

You’ve heard it before: *"I think I can, I think I can..."* A positive mindset is crucial for success in chemistry. If you convince yourself from the start that you will not do well or fail, you're setting yourself up for a self-fulfilling prophecy. Preparation is key—if you've done the work, you have every reason to believe you can succeed. Chemistry doesn’t have to be something you dread. Develop a positive relationship with it and focus on mastering the material.

2. **Procrastinating**

“Never do today what you can put off until tomorrow”—this mindset can be disastrous in chemistry. Early in the course, things might seem easy, but don’t let that lull you into a false sense of security. Chemistry builds on itself, and missing even small concepts early on can quickly snowball. Pace yourself and commit to studying regularly. Set aside time each day to work on chemistry and avoid cramming before exams. Consistency is key to mastering the material.

3. **Insufficient Math Preparation**

Chemistry requires a solid foundation in math. Make sure you understand basic algebra before diving into the subject, especially for unit conversions. Chemistry involves problem-solving on a daily basis, and math is an essential tool for this. While calculators can be helpful, don’t rely on them too much—your ability to work through problems by hand is what truly counts. To aid in this, keeping your units for every number and use of “the Factor-Label Method” or “Dimensional Analysis” is critical! It really helps!

4. **Neglecting the Textbook**

In many courses, textbooks are optional or secondary, but not in chemistry—especially here at RPI. The textbook is not only required, but also essential. Even if lectures are engaging, you’ll need the textbook for homework assignments and to reinforce your understanding. Don't skip the reading, and make sure to keep up with any required lab write-ups. A study guide and other online resources can be helpful, but the textbook is indispensable for your success.

5. **Not Reading the Textbook**

Reading is the foundation of learning chemistry. This includes the textbook, lecture slides, handouts, and lab write-ups. If you skip the reading, you’re missing out on crucial concepts. Ideally, you should read the textbook before class to prepare for the lecture—this allows you to understand the material better and ask meaningful questions. If the lecture doesn’t make sense, it means you need more preparation ahead of time. I’ll often assess your preparation in class through individual questions, so being ready is essential.

6. **Not Doing Your Own Work**

It can be tempting to rely on AI tools, study guides, or the answers at the back of the textbook to complete assignments quickly, but don’t fall into this trap. Using these resources for understanding is fine, but *doing your own work* is crucial. You won’t have these crutches during exams, and that's where your grade will really count. Focus on the process of learning and mastering the material, not just finishing the assignment.

7. **Not Practicing Enough**

Some chemistry concepts require understanding, while others require memorization. But most of it is about practice—working through problems until they become second nature. Just like a professional athlete practices their skills endlessly, you’ll need to solve many problems to build proficiency. Don’t just copy answers from a classmate or the back of the book—make sure to do the work yourself and *show your work* when solving problems. Try practicing problems beyond the assigned ones to expand your understanding.

8. **Skipping Class**

One of the easiest ways to set yourself up for failure is to miss class regularly. While it’s possible to teach yourself some chemistry on your own, learning the material isn’t the same as passing the course. Missing class means missing important lectures, lab activities, and discussions that will help you prepare for exams. Even if there’s no formal attendance policy, attending class is crucial for success. Be present, stay engaged, and take full advantage of the opportunity to learn.

Success in chemistry is achievable with the right mindset and approach. Avoid these pitfalls, stay consistent, and you’ll be well on your way to mastering the subject.
