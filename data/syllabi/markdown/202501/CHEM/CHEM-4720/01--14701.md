## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

*Proteomics and Structural Bioinformatics* is a survey of modern techniques of protein biochemistry, bioinformatics, and proteomics, and their applications in the broad field of protein chemistry and functional genomics. It is targeted to upper-level undergraduate and graduate students in chemistry and/or biology (co-listed). It will include a discussion of basic concepts of protein structure and function, protein characterization and purification, enzyme kinetics, NMR, cryoEM, X-ray crystallography, mass spectrometry, molecular modeling, bioinformatics, and various techniques of functional and structural genomics, together with readings from current literature. Special emphasis will be placed this year on protein structure prediction with new AI-based methods, including *AlphaFold* and *RosettaFold*.

Students will participate in reading and presenting papers from the recent scientific literature in the areas of structural virology (including structural biology of corona viruses), cancer biology, de novo protein design, structural bioinformatics, protein dynamics, ordered water, membrane proteins, protein interaction networks, drug design, structure prediction, deep learning, protein folding, and protein evolution. Students will develop skills in bioinformatic database searching, molecular modeling with PyMol, and presentation of scientific data and results. Students in CHEM 6720 will also complete exercises in writing a scientific paper or preparing a short research grant proposal.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

- (1) Learn basic concepts by which protein sequence encodes protein structure and function.
- (2) Learn fundamental biophysical principles defining protein’s conformation and its conformational dynamics.
- (3) Learn to use the basic tools of sequence-based bioinformatics, including motifs, BLAST, PSI-BLAST, Hidden-Markov models (HMMs), and multiple sequence alignments (MSAs).
- (4) Learn general methods for analytical biochemistry, protein purification and crystallization.
- (5) Learn underlying principles of cryo-electron microscopy (cryoEM), X-ray crystallography, and nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR), and to appreciate their strengths and weaknesses in experimental protein structure determination.
- (6) Understand and use evolving methods of contact prediction from evolutionary sequence co-variance data.
- (7) Be aware recent advances in machine learning that can be applied to structural biology problems.
- (8) Appreciate strengths and weaknesses of recent advances in protein structure prediction, and to carry out structure predictions using assigned protein sequences.
- (9) Learn how to use kinetic data to evaluate models of enzyme mechanisms.
- (10)Appreciate the roles of protein dynamics in determining protein function

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to describe the structures and physical organic chemistry of amino acids and polypeptides.
- Students will be able to explain how protein sequence defines protein structure, and how to design sequences to adopt particular structures.
- Students will demonstrate skills for evaluating protein sequence and structure similarity, and for using database searching tools to identify homologous sequences, building multiple sequence alignments, and creating phylogenetic trees based on protein sequence and structure.
- Students will carry out exercises combining evolutionary information and protein structure to identify functional properties of proteins, and to predict inter-residue contacts within proteins that have been conserved by evolution.
- Students will be able to use state-of-the-art methods for protein structure and protein disorder prediction.
- Students will be able to describe the principles of experimental protein structure determination, including protein purification, crystallization, cryoEM, X-ray crystallography, NMR, and amide hydrogen/deuterium exchange measurements.
- Students will demonstrate their ability to use structural and functional database searches to describe protein structure and function of protein unknowns.
- Students will have a theoretical framework sufficient to design and analyze simple enzyme kinetic studies.
- Students will be introduced to the roles of molecular dynamics in enzyme function, allostery, and molecular recognition processes
- Students will gain experience in presenting a scientific lecture.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Both 4000 and 6000 level students are expected to submit take-home exam essays prepared in a high-quality report format and ppt presentations in clear and high-quality formats. Responses for take home-exams will include text, figures (cut and paste from literature or hand drawn), and equations where appropriate. **Aesthetics of the written presentation also count**.

**4000 level Students:** Expected to show basic understanding of principles or processes outlined in class in these assessments. Each of the following will contribute 20% to the grade: (i) Take-home Exam I; (ii) Take-home Exam II; (iii) Take-home Exam III; (iv) In class quizzes and exercises; (v) Student Presentation.

**6000 level Students:** Expected to provide high-quality visually-interesting graphical presentations in ppt slides, and examples of how principles or processes learned in class can be applied in their own research and/or broadly in scientific research. Each of the following will contribute 15% to the grade: (i) Take-home Exam I; (ii) Take-home Exam II; (iii) Take-home Exam III; (iv) In class quizzes and exercises; (v) Student Presentation. The Research Paper / Proposal will count 25%.

In presenting a scientific lecture (Student Presentation) to the class, 6000 level students will be expected to integrate results from a specific paper with the state-of-the-art knowledge across the field of structural biology, and will be required to explain the impact of the subject work for society in language suitable for a lay audience.

Final grades will also take into account class participation, especially in the Student Presentations. Class participation will assessed by noting the number and insightfulness of questions raised during lectures by the course instructor, visiting lecturers, and student presenters.

Grades will be curved relative to performance of the previous 5 years of this course.

Students registered at the 6000 level cannot receive “D/D+” grades.

Student’s may appeal grades by presenting their concerns to the course instructor.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:
