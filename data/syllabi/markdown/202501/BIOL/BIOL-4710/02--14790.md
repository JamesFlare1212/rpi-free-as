## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students will work in groups of 2-3 sharing duties and responsibilities of the laboratory work **equally**. The purpose of this course is twofold: the first is to learn general protein handling procedures such as Bradford assays, protein precipitation techniques, differential centrifugation, and column chromatography. The second is to learn how to organize your data, label figures, interpret results and write succinctly about the results of your experiment.

Although different proteins will be purified using different methods there is some commonality between the procedures that I want you to recognize. Therefore, I expect all students to plan and prepare for laboratory procedures ahead of time especially in the second half of the course. This means reading the text book and assigned papers to help you write a purification protocol which will be evaluated by me and your TA at the end of the course.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

- ü **Tests** **25 %** (10% Test 1; 15% Test 2, cumulative)
- ü **Notebook** **25 %** (10% first evaluation and 15% final evaluation)

Must include detailed description of protocols and all results including ***conclusions*** from each experiment, e.g. Bradford assays with standard curves and a chart of each protein sample to be tested in SDS-PAGE, mass of the protein(s) to be loaded and the volume needed based on the concentration of the sample. Please see Notebook section below regarding the grading rubric for notebooks. Gel pictures needs to be dated, labeled and secured in your notebook, results described and conclusions written.

- ü **Lab Assessment** **10 %** (e.g. apply acquired skills and knowledge to new experiments, follows protocols and ***demonstrates patience and professional behavior***, generate reasonable SDS-PAGE results from the middle to last series of experiments, *cleans up work area and puts things away (clean balances)*, actively participates in planning and performing tasks, **and asks good relevant questions or to confirm a procedure if you are unsure**. Planning is a key part of any experiment.
- ü **Four lab reports – 40 %** papers (all papers must be submitted to LMS as a PDF file):
  - 7.5 % Bradford and SDS-PAGE gel summarized for labs 1 and 2 (~ 4 pages)
  - 10 % Proteomics summary paper Western blot (~ 6 pages)
  - 12.5 % Alkaline phosphatase purification chart and summary of results (~6 pages) Size exclusion column (SEC) for Alkaline phosphatase
  - 10 % Beta galactosidase paper ion exchange vs. affinity (~ 8 pages)

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

- This course requires that students work together, in groups of two or three. Lab work and data analysis must, therefore, be shared within each group. Consultation between groups on lab procedures and data handling is fine; **however, each student is required to keep an independent and detailed notebook**. Copying of data from one notebook to another is only permitted during lab time unless explicit permission to do otherwise is obtained. The notebooks are considered the property of the course until the semester is over.
- Unless otherwise indicated, you should cooperate with one another in and outside of class on the solution of problems. You may collaborate with your partner on reports for the Methods and Results sections only. All research reports required for the course must be **independently** written. You may not collaborate on examinations or misrepresent another person's work as your own on examinations or papers. Plagiarism from online sources, fellow students, textbooks will result in a zero for the assignment. Persistent cheating will result in an F for the course.
- You may not bring crib sheets to examinations, and you may not write on or alter examination materials that you submit for re-grading. Doing so will result in a zero (0) for the exams. The minimum penalty for cheating is an F on the assignment in question; persistent cheating will result in a grade of F for the course.
