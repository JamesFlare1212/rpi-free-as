## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The Molecular Biology I course will focus on the organization of prokaryotic and eukaryotic genomes and the molecular processes for storing, maintaining, and expressing genomic information in cells. The emphasis of this course will be on the regulation and gene expression from the transcription of chromosomal DNA through the processing and translation of the gene transcript. The molecular tools and techniques for studying genomes and the applications of genome sequences to basic scientific research, medical and forensic studies will be discussed. This is a taught at the undergraduate level but could serve as a graduate level course.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

**Molecular Biology: Principles of Genome Function** Craig et al. 3rd ed. Oxford University Press 2021.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Learn about the molecule interactions that take place inside eukaryotic and prokaryotic cells that maintain DNA stability and how gene expression is controlled.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

- Use proper scientific vocabulary to communicate information regarding molecular biology of a cell
- Demonstrate knowledge of structures, properties, and biological interactions between the major molecules in cells.
- Demonstrate a thorough understanding of Structure, organization, and packaging of typical eukaryotic and prokaryotic genomes
  1. Events involved in initiation, elongation, termination and processing of RNA and protein and the regulation of these events.
  2. Functional RNA and its role in regulation of genome activity
  3. Events involved in genome maintenance
  4. Role of mutation, recombination, and mobile genetic elements in generation of genome diversity and evolution
  5. Compare and contrast major features of transcription, translation and replication in prokaryotic and eukaryotic organisms.
