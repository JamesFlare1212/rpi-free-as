## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The goal of this course is to provide both biology majors and non-majors with a solid foundation in the physical and biological aspects of the marine environment. With this foundation, we will delve into a discussion-based investigation in current topics in marine biology. This course is offered at the undergraduate and graduate levels

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Undergraduate students define and explain the foundational knowledge and core concepts of marine biology and physical oceanography using specialized terminology Graduate students articulate mastery of the foundational knowledge and core concepts of marine biology and physical oceanography.
- Undergraduate students describe and evaluate foundational knowledge and core concepts in these areas to solve novel problems, analyze realistic scenarios, and make predictions from in integrative view of these fields Graduate students articulate the development of foundational knowledge and core concepts in these areas to solve novel problems, analyze realistic scenarios, make predictions, and designs and executes a literature review.
- Undergraduate students differentiate and evaluate theories and approaches to the study of the marine environment Graduate student dissects and reformulates biological questions in the marine environment
- Undergraduate students prepare and present information and data from the primary literature to construct knowledge and communicate that knowledge effectively Graduate students create a projected using information and data from the primary literature to construct knowledge, communicate that knowledge effectively, and articulates a proposed areas of interest for future research.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
