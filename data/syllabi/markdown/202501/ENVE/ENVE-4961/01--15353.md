## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Controlled Environment Agriculture (CEA) is an innovative approach for growing crops that optimizes environmental parameters (light, CO2, minerals, etc.) in the shoot and root system. Students will learn about different CEA systems such as vertical farms and hydroponic systems. Furthermore, the science behind light absorption, photosynthesis, and the production of beneficial secondary metabolites such as carotenoids and phenolics will be discussed. The course will provide practical training in crop production and management with hands-on lab experiments with commercial hydroponic systems.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The objective of this course is to introduce students to the field of hydroponic vegetable production and basic phytochemistry. Various aspects of crop growth and development will be explored including nutrient demand, light utilization/reactions, and production of primary and secondary metabolites. In the lab, students will learn how to operate a hydroponic unit in an experimental lab and will grow different leafy greens. Students will also perform light characterization and plant biochemistry experiments using state-of-the-art instruments and lab protocols. Finally, students will get an opportunity to visit a local vertical farming facility and see firsthand the operation of a CEA system.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\) Explain how hydroponics can contribute to sustainable and local food production.
- 2\) Describe the ideal growth environment for different plants in terms of nutrient availability, light quality and quantity, and temperature.
- 3\) Operate different analytical instruments which are routinely used in plant production and food quality labs, including quantum sensors, infrared gas exchange systems, and spectrophotometers.
- 4\) Learn how to grow healthy and nutritious plants and diagnose different disorders from nutrient deficiencies.

## Exam Policy

Exams will be closed book, and reference tables and equations will be provided. There will be multiple format questions (multiple choice, short answers, and calculations). Calculators use is allowed during exams. The use of mobile devices (cell phones, computers, etc.) is prohibited.

A missed exam will result in a grade of zero unless permission is granted before the scheduled exam time and is documented through the Student Experience Office (Academy Hall, x8022, se@rpi.edu). In such cases, the weight of other exams will be equally increased to account for the missing grade.

The midterm assessment for this course will be based on homework submissions until the assessment date. Students who have concerns about their midterm performance should meet with the instructor to discuss approaches to improving their performance in future work or the possibility of dropping the course before the drop deadline. Details of the course design project (presentation and report) will be made available on LMS.

## Homework Policy

Students should submit clear and legible **printed copies of their homework at the beginning of the class period**. Students are encouraged to work in groups, but each student is expected to submit their own work. However, students should not consult online resources that contain solution manuals as it constitutes a violation of the Academic Integrity policies. Reading of course textbook is highly recommended to supplement topics covered in class and for additional practice exercises.

Homework should be submitted by the due dates specified on the Course Calendar (one week after assignment). Three points out of 10 (or 30% of total points) will be deducted for late homework submitted within the first 48 hours after the due date. Homework submitted after 48 hours will receive a zero grade.
