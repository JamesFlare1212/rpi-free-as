## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Welcome! This course explores ethical issues posed by the proliferation of artificial intelligence technologies. In order to do so, we will first discuss the philosophical issues with ethical theories, and how we can be justified in making ethical judgments in particular cases. We will then survey a number of issues, some of which are as follows. How should we approach thinking about ethical obligation generally? How should we think about designing an ethical algorithm? As technology like autonomous cars are integrated into our lives at scale, what challenges do they pose for human-robot interaction design, psychology, law, and socio-political systems? Which ethical approach or theory should an automated process be programmed to follow when there’s no legal guidance? How ought we make sense of obligation and responsibility in the context of the decisions of an autonomous AI (designer, user, AI itself)? Trust is an essential component of social human life. But how will integrating AI into our lives require us to understand the novel philosophical and legal issues related to interpersonal trust between people and AI? How can we know if AI has a moral status, and deserves the respect a person deserves? Is it permissible for companies to train their AI products using copyrighted material? Or through interactions with the general public? If artificial super intelligence is achieved, how likely is it that an artificial agent’s interests will align with ours? What should we make of the concerns about job displacement due to AI technologies? How worried should we be about AI? Are we too worried? Not worried enough? What early indicators should influence our attitude about how to answer that question?

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. Students should develop an explicit rather than tacit understanding of the ethical challenges posed by AI.
2. Students should be able to develop some views about the ethical issues of AI. While some of these problems are very abstract, others present (almost daily) dilemmas and so students ought to leave the course better prepared to think effectively about these problems.
3. Develop the ability to communicate arguments and explanations clearly and effectively, in both oral and written form.
4. Distinguish and evaluate different philosophical arguments.
5. Be able to collaborate, communicate clearly, plan and work with other people on abstract and complex group projects.

There are two tracks, or pathways, through which you can earn a grade in this course.

The first track involves completing two written take-home exams instead of writing essays. Each take home exam will be worth 20% of your final grade. This track is designed for students who want to achieve a basic mastery in the subject matter of the course without devoting significant time to developing original thought regarding debates in the ethics of AI. This may be desirable for students who are struggling mentally in these trying times with issues like stress, anxiety, loss of loved ones, or a low sense of wellbeing or motivation. The idea is that for students in this kind of position, more numerous but smaller and structured assignments will help them to be successful. I wouldn’t say this track is “less work” or even easier per se, but I do see it as psychologically more manageable.

The second track involves writing an essay instead of completing any of the take-home exams. This essay will count as 40% of your final grade. The paper is 6-10 pages in length. I will provide suggested topics, but you will be free to write on a topic of your choice so long as it is obviously related to our course content. It is your responsibility to propose a topic and meet with me at least twice: once to approve the topic and general structure of the paper, and another time before your completed draft is submitted. You will then receive comments for editing a final submission.

Optionally, you are welcome to complete both take-home exams, and the paper (and I’ll grade you based on which track would have returned the highest grade), but I would strongly advise against this. You’ll be overworked, and my intuition tells me that this would be unlikely to significantly improve your grade (instead, just devote more effort and attention to a smaller number of assignments, right?).

All students will be asked to submit a small number (think between eight and twelve) short quizzes on the assigned reading and lecture content. These quizzes in total will make up 20% of your final grade. I will drop your two lowest quiz grades.

Lastly, each student will participate in a final group presentation project worth 40%, which involves groups of 2-3 students working on a 25-30 minute presentation. I will be setting aside multiple class periods specifically to work on this project with your group members, although you will need to meet with group members and work on the project outside of class time as well.

Lastly, exceptional participation with lecture content will count as a bonus, up to 1/3 of a letter grade at the instructor's discretion (for example, a maximum bonus could raise a B+ to an A-).

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
