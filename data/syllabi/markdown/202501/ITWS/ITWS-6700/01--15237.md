## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course teaches students about the roles and infrastructure of IT departments in modern organizations, IT software engineering technologies and methodologies for software development life cycle through hands-on experience. The course is for students with software development backgrounds to enhance their knowledge of software development and management, the software development industry, open source community, etc.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

To prepare students for the quickly changing landscape of software development from an industry and community perspective. Elucidation of the current state of the art in software development and directions. Specification, design and implementation of a ¾ semester long SD project (individual or team). Advancement in at least two areas in the Software Development Life Cycle. Improve software development project planning, execution and presentation skills.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

1. Plan software development management processes
2. Apply software development management methodologies
3. Become familiar with the software development industry, and use open source to improve working efficiency
4. Develop documents for software development management, such as requirements, project plans, etc.
5. Develop documents for software development, such as function specification, design, and testing
6. Apply advanced programming technologies, such as design patterns, frameworks, IDEs, and build automation
7. Develop a 3-tiered architecture web application and apply demonstrable software engineering management (6000-level)

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

This course is designed to be a topic and project course in which students develop the skills to research topical areas of software development, assess the current state and directions, and present to the class. As such, student engagement and participation is a critical part of achieving these outcomes.

- 4000-level assessment – see learning outcomes
- 6000-level assessment – note learning outcomes and a higher level of demonstration
- Topical lecture (35%)
- Quizzes (N ~ 20, at 1% each, i.e. \~20%)
- Software Development Projects (35%)
- In-class discussion/involvement and attendance (10%)

*Presentations*

Each student will be required to give at least two presentations during the semester – \~1 topical lecture (\~30 mins) and \~1 software development project presentations (duration determined based on individual versus team composition). Students sign up for topical lecture presentation dates and will be able to (mostly) choose between topics. Details on presentations will be given in the week 1 and week 2 classes.

*In-class discussion*

Everyone in the class is expected to attend lectures and presentations and to participate in the discussions – n person or or message in Discord using discussions. At present there is no plan to use LMS discussion boards but if the discussions become complex that avenue may be required. Note that discussion participation must be meaningful to count toward your grade, and not vague or uninformed (*e.g. “I didn’t understand” or a thousand thumbs-up – they are welcome but will not count*).

*Post-topic lecture quizzes*

Following topical presentations, every student except the presenter is required to answer a short quiz of ~ 3 questions based on the topical presentations in each class – note this means up to FOUR quizzes per week for 5-6 weeks. Each quiz will be developed by the student presenting the lecture and approved by the Instructor. The student developer will not complete the quiz but will receive full grade for the quiz they construct. Quizzes will be submitted through LMS and will be due by 1159 pm on the Friday after the presentation. Word format is the only format that will be accepted – any other format will receive no credit.

Note 6000-level students will not be assigned C- or D grades, i.e. C- or less is an F.
