## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course introduces students to the field of information technology and web science, the types of problems encountered in the field, and the approaches used to solve them. Through a series of activities and projects, students are introduced to topics such as web systems design, emerging web standards, database systems, security, and computer networking. Guest speakers highlight information technology practices in industry. Small groups of students work on a project during the semester and present their results at the end of the course.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1. Weekly detailed assignments are generally posted on the course LMS website one week prior to their due date.
2. In class “labs” are assigned for many of the technology classes. These detailed “lab assignments” are posted on the course LMS website prior to their due date. These “labs” can generally be completed during the class session. If not completed during the specific class session, they must be submitted prior to the next class meeting.
3. Business Case discussions and write-ups are specified in the Course Calendar, will be explained in class and will be available on the LMS. Case submissions are due prior to day’s classes.
4. Late submissions will be accepted for 1 week following due date for a 10% grade reduction for lateness. Submissions after that will receive a 30% reduction. Case written submissions – generally due before class the day of the case discussion - will not be accepted after the due date/time and will receive zero credit. No submissions will be accepted after the last day of class *preceding* presentation days.
5. Quizzes/Exams will be given during the course as specified in the Course Calendar and in class.
6. The Term Project provides an opportunity for student groups to design and mock-up a web application. It is an opportunity to demonstrate their competency with concepts covered in class. The application can serve any purpose, real or imagined, in the present or the future. Teams of two to four students (normally 3) will be randomly assigned to work together on the Project. The Teams will submit a Project Final Report, project code, and will present their Projects to the class. Note: Only technology and techniques covered in class are valid, no exceptions without prior approval. This is a group project and will receive a group grade. However, failure to present with your group will result in an individual failure for the Project. Individual grades may also be adjusted by +/- 10% based on peer review.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Assignments from the “labs”, project, and business cases are outlined in the Course Calendar. Specific detailed assignments will be posted on LMS. Each student must post their answers to the written assignments in the online discussion groups and written responses prior to the assigned class. Each assignment will be given a grade. The grades for homework, projects, business cases, labs, quizzes, term project, participation and final exam will be posted on LMS.

The quizzes will cover material from the readings, lectures, class discussions, labs, cases and projects. There will be quizzes during the semester as shown in the Course Calendar. All quizzes will comprehensively cover the entire course. Each quiz is worth a minimum of 10%. ## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

A portion of your grade will be received through classroom participation, online discussion boards, labs, and the term project. Therefore, it is expected that you attend every class and participate in team meetings associated with the term project. A participation grade will be given for each individual, for each quarter, of the course.

A student may request an excused absence for health or career (job interviews) reasons by emailing the instructor requesting the absence, and with reasonable notice, prior to the beginning of that particular class.

Participation Grading on a 5 Point Scale: Quality is more important than quantity

0 – absence without an excuse

1 – 2 in attendance, but not paying attention, e.g. sleeping, or doing email or Internet unrelated to class, and not apparently having done pre-class reading.

3 – in attendance, paying attention, appear to have done the pre-class reading, but not otherwise contributing to the class, little or no contributions to online discussion boards

4 – 5 in attendance, paying attention, having done the pre-class reading, and contributing to the class by answering questions, making thoughtful comments, engaging in the class discussion and significant contributions to online discussion boards and term project.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic dishonesty

Integrity is an extremely important part of any person’s character and behavior. This course expects the highest level of personal and academic integrity. Students may discuss labs and business case assignments with other students in the class, but each individual must research, write and submit their case assignments individually. Term Project assignments are done collectively by the Project Team and one submittal is made for the entire Team. In all assignments, citations must be done for all references including websites. Any breach of the academic integrity code listed in the Rensselaer Handbook will be considered grounds for failure in the course.

*Academic Integrity*

This course will utilize a *zero-tolerance policy* for any form of cheating or other academic misconduct. All submitted materials will be subjected to copying and plagiarism checkers. University policy deals with the problem of academic dishonesty, cheating, and plagiarism. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. No violation will be tolerated in this class. The sanctions provided in this policy will be used to deal with any violations.

Academic dishonesty includes, but is not limited to, copying material from another student or use of written notes during an exam, representing others’ work as your own, changing answers on an exam paper after the paper has been graded, direct duplication of materials found in any online form, without suitable attribution/ license acknowledgement, and specifically for any software; scripts, packages, distributions, etc. Any student caught cheating will be assigned an F for the course. Furthermore, violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education for undergraduate and graduate students, respectively).

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. In this class, all materials that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s or group’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting, please *ask* for clarification before submitting.

*A special note on plagiarism.*

Please see the definition of plagiarism given below, as outlined in The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights. To be clear, any time material that is not the students’ own is used, it should be in quotes and contain a citation. If a student paraphrases work from another, it also requires a citation. Furthermore, even when citations are used, others’ work cannot be used as a student response on an assignment. In the case of software we will address the topic of “copying” which is considered a common practice in software development in relation to ethics and conduct of software development.

*“Plagiarism Representing the work or words of another as one’s own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference. For example, using sentences verbatim from a published source in a term paper without appropriate referencing, or presenting as one’s own the detailed argument of a published source, or presenting as one’s own electronically or digitally enhanced graphic representations from any form of media.”* - ACADEMIC INTEGRITY (2014-2016 Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights & Responsibilities, August 2014)
