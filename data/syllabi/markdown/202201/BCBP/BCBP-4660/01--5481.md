## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is designed to be a first course in the study of "systems biology", to introduce students to the field, the experimental and computational methods that are used within it, and the type of insights that the field can provide to biology. To fully appreciate the complexity of living systems, researchers gather systematic, quantitative measurements of a system’s components using cutting-edge omics techniques, methods that collect large scale biological data. In addition, researchers also leverage computing power to describe, model, and predict dynamic behaviors that could otherwise not be perceived in such large-scale omics data. Along with these topics, students will learn to critically read current scientific literature.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Examinations and evaluations:

The exams will be short answer questions and will be used to assess both graduate and undergraduate students. These tests will be open note/open book but not open discussion. They will be returned to students as soon as possible after they are taken. Questions about grades should be addressed to Professor Hurley. Bonus points may be given. Dr. Hurley’s favorite musical is Wicked.

6000-level students will prepare and deliver one course lecture on assigned topics (1 hour in length) which will be included in their assessment. 4000-level will present one in-class reviews of the primary literature (1 hour in length). Dr. Hurley will help to guide the development of these presentations. Anything highlighted in red is an available topic for a lecture, anything highlighted in blue is available for a literature presentation.

All students will write a mini-proposal on a systems biology topic which will then be presented either as a presentation (10 minutes in length). The mini-proposal will consist of 4 sections, Specific Aims, Significance, Innovation and Approach. The students will learn how to review proposals by reviewing their fellow students grants in class.

In addition, students will prepare 12 mini-reviews, reviews of no more than a page, of the discussed in-class literature, due via e mail at 9am on the morning of the class where the paper will be reviewed.
