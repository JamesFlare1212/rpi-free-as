## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a continuation of Chinese IV, a course for the standard modern Chinese language (Mandarin). Students learn more Chinese characters and words, reach a total of about 950 characters, 1700 words, and use more complicated grammatical structures, e.g., idiomatic expressions, expressions of comparison, concession, and subjection, etc. In sum, students learn more in all four aspects – listening, speaking, reading, and writing – presented in Chinese IV.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

In the course of the semester, students are engaged in communicative tasks inside and outside of the classroom. We will learn how to describe weather, travel, and climate, academics (study abroad, clubs, hobbies, etc.), discuss on- and off-campus housing, online shopping, etc. More emphasis will be given to writing, along with reading, speaking, and listening. Students will be exposed to innovative learning environments based on new technologies and language learning theories. A broad spectrum of Chinese culture will also be brought to students throughout the semester.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students who successfully complete this course will acquire the following competencies and be able to perform communicative tasks pertinent to Intermediate Mid-level of language proficiency: 1. Interpersonal Communication: Students will be able to (1) carry out and continue conversations involving personal views on familiar topics with many details and in uncomplicated settings (e.g., academic applications, clubs), (2) use conversation skills to join and participate in a spontaneous discussion on a variety of familiar topics (e.g., housing, online purchase and return), and (3) use the language to exchange information with people from the target culture about familiar topics and personal opinions in uncomplicated situations (e.g., describing weather and travel). 2. Presentational Communication: Students will be able to (1) Use a series of connected sentences in presentations to describe experiences, events, and opinions, (2) Use readily available technology tools and digital literacy skills to present academic information in the target language, and (3) use series-connected sentences to describe arts, sports, games, and media from the Chinese culture. 3. Interpretive Communication: Students will be able to (1) understand the main idea and many details of familiar topics in a series of connected sentences, conversations, presentations, and messages, and (2) Understand the meaning of longer messages on familiar and unfamiliar topics displayed in the community created by communities of learners of the same target language. 4. Cultural Competence: Students will be to (1) deconstruct written and spoken texts for cultural attitudes, viewpoints, and values, and (2) Evaluate the traditions of Chinese culture and American culture (e.g., college admissions and store return policy).

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

*Participation & Performance*: Language learning involves using the vocabulary and grammar you learned to interact with others for all purposes of life in a target language society. You are required to participate and perform different language learning tasks/activities in class. You receive full credit of participation for excused class absence and can schedule a make-up work for the absence. No make-ups for any unexcused absences and lateness.

*Homework*: Daily practice and homework are important integrals for successful Chinese learning. **For the best effect of proficiency-based language learning, assignments of this course are spread out over the week** so that you practice 20-30 minutes daily. Each new week’s homework is posted on the Monday of the previous week on LMS and there are *THREE (3) due times for the routine weekly homework*: (1) Tuesday before class; (2) Friday before class, and (3) Sunday Midnight.

*Unit Dictation*: Dictation is a form of testing the listening and writing of vocabulary and sentences. You write down what you hear. ***Unit Dictations usually will be taken place after we finish each Unit (two chapters)***. You are not allowed to make up dictations for unexcused tardiness and/or absence.

*Quizlet*: Quizlet is an online flashcards website allowing you to study each chapter’s vocabulary with different modes of study. Much of the vocabulary learning and assessment will be through this Web 2.0 platform. An invite to join will be sent to you in week one.

*Project*: There will be several projects in this course that require self-regulation to plan and monitor the completion throughout the semester. Details on due dates, directions, and assessments will be explained in class. You can also find them under “***Course Documents***” on LMS Blackboard.

*Exam*: There will be two exams in this course. Each exam consists of two parts: written and oral. The two exams test your knowledge of vocabulary, grammar, cultural knowledge as well as your competence in listening, speaking, reading, and writing of the Chinese language designated by the course level. Each exam covers four chapters of the textbook.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

It is VERY EASY to spot cheating in a foreign language course. Students are not permitted to turn in work done by someone else. Getting assistance from a native speaker on assignments is OK, but your final product must be your own work as it is easy to identify the excessive structures beyond your level of proficiency. Besides, the use of a WEB TRANSLATE (e.g., Google Translate) for translation other than vocabulary or phrase is not permitted.

The first time I suspect cheating I warn the student and tell why I suspect (e.g. use of non-course grammar or vocabulary) and I take off 50% points of the grade. If it's very obvious I do not give a warning. I will just give a zero. If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of F for the course or be reported to the Student's Office.
