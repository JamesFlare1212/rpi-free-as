## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

LANG 1410 is the first half of a year-long beginning level course in Modern Mandarin Chinese. This course assumes no previous knowledge of the subject. The course is designed to provide students with fundamental skills in listening, speaking, reading, and writing Mandarin Chinese. Oral and aural skills will be emphasized. Background on Chinese culture will be introduced as an element of the course. For entry-level, non-native speakers only.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students who complete this course will acquire the following competencies and be able to perform tasks pertinent to Novice Low level of proficiency described by the American Council on the Teaching of Foreign Languages: 1. Interpersonal Communication: Students will be able to ask highly predictable and formulaic questions and respond to such questions by listing, naming, and identifying. Example topics are: introducing people/self, making an appointment, inquiring about occupation, checking schedules, or expressing preferences for food and drink. 2. Interpretive Communication: Students will demonstrate knowledge of words, phrases, and formulaic language that has been practiced and memorized to get meaning of the main idea from simple, highly-predictable oral or written texts (e.g., those related to personal background, prior knowledge, or experiences), with strong visual support. 3. Presentational Communication: Students will be able to (1) present information on very familiar topics using a variety of words, phrases, and sentences that have been practiced and memorized; (2) produce several high-frequency words and formulaic expressions in Pinyin and Chinese characters. 4. Cultural Awareness: Students will be able to (1) use culturally appropriate gestures and formulaic expressions in highly practiced applications; (2) Derive meaning from texts that are heard, read, or viewed; (3) use some memorized culturally appropriate gestures, formulaic expressions, and basic writing conventions (e.g., Pinyin spelling rules and character structures).

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

*Participation & Performance*: Language learning involves using the vocabulary and grammar you learned to interact with others for all purposes of life in a target language society. You are required to participate in various language learning tasks/activities in and outside classrooms. Some will require recording your voice and role-plays. You receive full credit of participation for excused class absence and can schedule a make-up work for the absence. No make-ups for any unexcused absences and lateness.

*Unit Dictation*: Dictation is a form to test your listening and handwriting for the unit vocabulary and sentences. During the dictation, you write down what you hear. Unit Dictations usually take place after we finish each Unit (two chapters). You are not allowed to make up dictations for unexcused tardiness and/or absence.

*Quizlet*: Quizlet is an online flashcards website allowing you to study each chapter’s vocabulary with different modes of study. Much of the vocabulary learning and assessment will be through this Web 2.0 platform. An invite to join will be sent to you via RPI emails.

*Project*: There will be several projects in this course that require self-regulation to plan and monitor the completion throughout the semester. Details on due dates, directions, and assessments will be explained in class. You can also find them under “***Course Documents***” on LMS Blackboard.

*Exam*: There will be two exams in this course. Each exam consists of two parts: written and oral. The two exams test your knowledge of vocabulary, grammar, cultural knowledge as well as your competence in listening, speaking, reading, and writing of the Chinese language designated by the course level. Each exam covers four chapters of the textbook.  
*Homework*: Daily practice and homework are important integrals for successful Chinese learning. **For the best effect of proficiency-based language learning, assignments of this course are spread out over the week** so that you practice 20-30 minutes daily. Each new week’s homework is posted on the Monday of the previous week on LMS and there are *THREE (3) due times for the routine weekly homework*: (1) Tuesday before class; (2) Friday before class, and (3) Sunday Midnight.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

It is VERY EASY to spot cheating in a foreign language course. Students are not permitted to turn in work done by someone else. Getting assistance from a native speaker on assignments is OK, but your final product must be your own work as it is easy to identify the excessive structures beyond your level of proficiency. Besides, the use of a WEB TRANSLATE (e.g., Google Translate) for translation other than vocabulary or phrase is not permitted.

The first time I suspect cheating I warn the student and tell why I suspect (e.g. use of non-course grammar or vocabulary) and I take off 50% points of the grade. If it's very obvious I do not give a warning. I will just give a zero. If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of F for the course or be reported to the Student's Office.
