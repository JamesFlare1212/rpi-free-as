## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Description:** Techniques and applications of integration, polar coordinates, parametric equations, infinite sequences and series, vector functions and curves in space, functions of several variables, and partial derivatives. Prerequisite: MATH 1010 or equivalent. (from the [Rensselaer Catalog](http://catalog.rpi.edu/))

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Basic symbol manipulation skills pertaining to integration.
- Ability to state and explain basic Calculus definitions and theorems and their applications.
- Ability to describe curves parametrically and apply Calculus concepts and techniques to curves described in this way.
- Ability to express curves via polar equations and to solve Calculus problems in the polar coordinate system.
- Ability to apply Calculus to selected problems in Science, Engineering and Mathematics.
- Ability to use convergence tests to analyze the behavior of infinite series.
- Ability to represent functions using power series and solve problems using these representations.
- Ability to describe lines and planes in space in equation form and solve problems using these representations.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance:** Students are responsible for all material covered in lectures and recitations whether they are present or not. Attendance is expected, but will not be checked.

### Other Course Policies:

**Exams:** The material covered on each exam will be announced on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/) when the date of the exam is near. Exams will be given in person during lecture in EATON 214.

Students who miss an exam, whether due to an excused or unexcused absence, will receive a zero on the exam. These students will have the opportunity to take a make-up exam at the date and time scheduled by the [Office of the Registrar](https://info.rpi.edu/registrar/grades/#FinalExamScheduleandConflicts) for the final exam during finals week, Monday, May 2–Friday, May 6. The score of the make-up exam will replace the zero score. Important notes:

- Attendance is taken at exams. Students who are present for the regularly scheduled exams will have their scores on those exams counted toward their course grade. That is, a student may not show up for an exam and then decide to take a make-up exam.
- If a student misses more than one exam during the semester, then the student may take more than one make-up exam during the final exam period.
- No make-up exam will be given for the make-up exam during the final exam period for any reason (e.g. travel, illness, exam conflicts). If a student misses an exam during the semester and then misses the make-up exam during finals week, the student receives a zero on the exam.

**Final Exam:** There is no final exam in the course. Make-up exams for exams 1–3 will be administered during the final exam period scheduled by the [Office of the Registrar](https://info.rpi.edu/registrar/grades/#FinalExamScheduleandConflicts) as explained above under **Exams**. Only students who missed an exam during the semester may take a make-up exam during finals week.

**Recitation Quizzes:** Quizzes will be given most weeks during in-person recitations. Material to be covered on each quiz will be posted on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/). Students must take quizzes in their assigned sections in order to receive credit for the quiz.

During online recitations, practice quizzes may be given. These will not be collected nor graded. The teaching assistants will go over the solutions after the practice quiz to demonstrate what would be required for full credit.

No make-up quizzes will be given for any reason. To compensate for this, the lowest quiz score for each student will be dropped at the end of the semester. Only students who miss a quiz due to an [excused absence documented by the Student Success office](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/current-students/academic-and-personal-goals/requesting-excused-absence) may be excused from a quiz.

**Homework:** Achieve homework assignments will be due once or twice weekly on Mondays and Thursdays at 11:59 PM and will be graded automatically by the system. Students may submit these assignments late up to two days after the original deadline, but a penalty will be applied to the score. A 10% penalty will apply to assignments submitted the day after the deadline and a 20% penalty will apply to assignments submitted two days after the deadline. No other due date extensions will be granted for any reason. To further compensate for this, several homework assignment scores will be dropped at the end of the semester.

Additional problems will be assigned from the textbook and will be posted on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/). Though these problems will not be collected nor graded, they should not be viewed as optional. Success in the course depends on successful completion of all homework, both Achieve homework and textbook homework. Exam and quiz problems are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples. Full solutions to odd-numbered textbook problems can be found on Achieve. When logged in to our Achieve course, view content by assignments and scroll to the bottom of the page. The student solutions manual for our textbook may be found below No Due Dates. Tip: download and save these files for use in future courses!

**Extra Credit:** No extra credit opportunities will be available during the course, so be sure to give your best effort on exams, quizzes, and homework assignments throughout the semester.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:** Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://rpi.app.box.com/s/d4ndpn3f7qdwxh9jaz5n1hkkr0mnd25e) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams and quizzes must be the student’s own. In addition, no calculators, computers, tablets, open books, open notes, phones, headphones, or smartwatches may be used during exams. Talking or communicating with other students in any way during exams is also prohibited. Students may discuss homework with other students, tutors, the TAs, or instructor, but the work submitted must be the student’s own.

The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
