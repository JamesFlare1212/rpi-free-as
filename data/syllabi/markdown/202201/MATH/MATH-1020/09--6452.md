## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Description:** Techniques and applications of integration, polar coordinates, parametric equations, infinite sequences and series, vector functions and curves in space, functions of several variables, and partial derivatives. Prerequisite: MATH 1010 (from the [Rensselaer Catalog](http://catalog.rpi.edu/))

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Basic symbol manipulation skills pertaining to integration.
- Ability to state and explain basic Calculus definitions and theorems and their applications.
- Ability to describe curves parametrically and apply Calculus concepts and techniques to curves described in this way.
- Ability to express curves via polar equations and to solve Calculus problems in the polar coordinate system.
- Ability to apply Calculus to selected problems in Science, Engineering and Mathematics.
- Ability to use convergence tests to analyze the behavior of infinite series.
- Ability to represent functions using power series and solve problems using these representations.
- Ability to describe lines and planes in space in equation form and solve problems using these representations.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Exams:** Exams will be given in person when the Institute allows it. Exams will be given during lecture. Attendance will be taken at all exams using sign in sheets. Details about each exam, including the material to be covered on the exam and time limit, will be announced on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/) when the date of the exam is near. The dates tentative for Exams 1–3 are listed above in the section **Grading.** Details on exams for DSS students are given below in the **DSS** section.

If a student misses an exam for any reason, the student will receive a score of 0 for the exam. The student will have an opportunity to replace this score by taking a make-up exam that will be *given during finals week*. The date of this make-up exam is set by the Registrar’s office and all students must take it at the scheduled time. If a student has an excused absence AND communicates with the instructor ***prior to the scheduled exam***, an alternate date may be offered at the instructor’s discretion.

If a student hands in an exam or signs into an exam but does not hand one in, they can not replace their exam score using the make-up exam even if their score is a 0. No student will be allowed two chances to take an exam. This means that you may not take the make-up exam to improve your grade on an exam that you sat for during the semester.

**Homework:** Achieve homework assignments will be due once or twice weekly on Tuesdays and Fridays at 11:59 PM and will be graded automatically by the system. Students may submit these assignments late up to two days after the original deadline, but a penalty will be applied to the score. A 10% penalty will apply to assignments submitted the day after the deadline and a 20% penalty will apply to assignments submitted two days after the deadline. No other due date extensions will be granted for any reason. To further compensate for this, several homework assignment scores will be dropped at the end of the semester.

Additional problems will be assigned from the textbook and will be posted on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/) in the **Achieve & Homework** content area found in the left toolbar. Though these problems will not be collected nor graded, they should not be viewed as optional. Success in the course depends on successful completion of all homework, both Achieve homework and textbook homework. Exam problems are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples. Full solutions to odd-numbered textbook problems can be found on Achieve. When logged in to our Achieve course, view content by assignments and scroll to the bottom of the page. The student solutions manual for chapters 5-7 and 10-14 may be found below No Due Dates.

### Other Course Policies:

**Disability Services for Students (DSS):** Students with a memorandum from the [Disability Services for Students (DSS)](http://studenthealth.rpi.edu/disabilityservices) office identifying accommodations for exams must present the memorandum to the instructor by Friday, February 4. Providing the accommodations requires advance planning. Students will not be granted accommodations unless a memorandum from DSS has been submitted to the instructor by noon on the Thursday before the exam. For example, to receive accommodations on Exam 1, a DSS memorandum must be submitted no later than noon on Monday, February 14.

Exams for DSS students will be given during the Wednesday block on the class schedule on the Wednesday immediately following the date of the exam. (For exam 1 this would mean that DSS students take the exam on Wednesday, February 16 starting at noon.) DSS students should NOT come to lecture on the date of the exam, they should only attend their schedule DSS exam time.

**Grade Appeals:** If upon reviewing the answer key and solutions for an exam a student feels that his or her work was graded incorrectly, then the student may submit a grading appeal to the instructors on Gradescope. Regrade requests will only be accepted during the grade appeal window specified on LMS once the grades are released. Regrade requests made outside of Gradescope will be ignored.

**Health and Safety:** Students are required to follow the health and safety protocols of RPI, which are subject to change throughout the semester. Please note that instructor/TA will practice social distancing whenever possible and thus will not be available to speak with students in the classroom at the beginning or end of class.

**Visitors:** Visitors are prohibited during all class meetings. Only students who are registered for the class should be present in the classroom. Students may only attend class meetings at the time and place listed on their schedule on SIS.

**Classroom Decorum:** Students must be on time for class and should refrain from leaving and reentering the classroom during class. If a student has a legitimate reason for arriving late to class or being excused early from class, then they should discuss this with the instructor before class. Phones may not be used during class and should be silent. Computers and tablets may not be used for anything other than taking notes. Students who are unable to courteously comply with these policies may be asked to leave the room.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:** Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://rpi.app.box.com/s/d4ndpn3f7qdwxh9jaz5n1hkkr0mnd25e) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams must be the student’s own. Students may use their textbook and notes during exams, but the use of online resources outside of LMS is prohibited. Communicating with other people in any way during exams about the content of the exam is prohibited. Students may discuss homework with other students, TAs, and instructors, but the work submitted must be the student’s own.

The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
