## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Functions, limits, continuity, derivatives, implicit differentiation, related rates, maxima and minima, elementary transcendental functions, introduction to definite integral with applications to area and volumes of revolution. (from the [Rensselaer Catalog](http://catalog.rpi.edu/))

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate basic and advanced symbol manipulation skills.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to convert between calculus concepts and their graphical, numerical, and symbolic representations.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to apply calculus concepts to selected problems in science and engineering.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to apply certain fundamental theorems, basic concepts, and rules from calculus to solve symbolic and graphical problems.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to present problems related to the theory of calculus and its applications to science and engineering disciplines.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance:** Students are responsible for all material covered in lectures and recitations whether they are present or not. Attendance is expected, but will not be checked.

### Other Course Policies:

**Exams:** The material covered on each exam will be announced on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/) when the date of the exam is near. Exams will be given in person during lecture; room TBA.

Students who miss an exam, whether due to an excused or unexcused absence, will receive a zero on the exam. These students will have the opportunity to take a make-up exam at the date and time scheduled by the [Office of the Registrar](https://info.rpi.edu/registrar/grades/#FinalExamScheduleandConflicts) for the final exam during finals week, Monday, May 2–Friday, May 6. The score of the make-up exam will replace the zero score. Important notes:

- Attendance is taken at exams. Students who are present for the regularly scheduled exams will have their scores on those exams counted toward their course grade. That is, a student may not show up for an exam and then decide to take a make-up exam.
- If a student misses more than one exam during the semester, then the student may take more than one make-up exam during the final exam period.
- No make-up exam will be given for the make-up exam during the final exam period for any reason (e.g. travel, illness, exam conflicts). If a student misses an exam during the semester and then misses the make-up exam during finals week, the student receives a zero on the exam.

**Final Exam:** There is no final exam in the course. Make-up exams for exams 1–4 will be administered during the final exam period scheduled by the [Office of the Registrar](https://info.rpi.edu/registrar/grades/#FinalExamScheduleandConflicts) as explained above under **Exams**. Only students who missed an exam during the semester may take a make-up exam during finals week.

**Recitation Quizzes:** Quizzes will be given most weeks during in-person recitations. Material to be covered on each quiz will be posted on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/). Students must take quizzes in their assigned sections in order to receive credit for the quiz.

No make-up quizzes will be given for any reason. To compensate for this, the lowest quiz score for each student will be dropped at the end of the semester. Only students who miss a quiz due to an [excused absence documented by the Student Success office](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/current-students/academic-and-personal-goals/requesting-excused-absence) may be excused from a quiz.

**Homework:** Achieve homework assignments will be due once or twice weekly on Tuesdays and Fridays at 11:59 PM and will be graded automatically by the system. Students may submit these assignments late up to two days after the original deadline, but a penalty will be applied to the score. A 10% penalty will apply to assignments submitted the day after the deadline and a 20% penalty will apply to assignments submitted two days after the deadline. No other due date extensions will be granted for any reason. To further compensate for this, several homework assignment scores will be dropped at the end of the semester.

Additional problems will be assigned from the textbook and will be posted on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/). Though these problems will not be collected nor graded, they should not be viewed as optional. Success in the course depends on successful completion of all homework, both Achieve homework and textbook homework. Exam and quiz problems are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples. Full solutions to odd-numbered textbook problems can be found on Achieve. When logged in to our Achieve course, view content by assignments and scroll to the bottom of the page. The student solutions manual for our textbook may be found below No Due Dates. Tip: download and save these files for use in future courses!

**Disability Services for Students (DSS):** Students with a memorandum from the [Disability Services for Students (DSS)](http://studenthealth.rpi.edu/disabilityservices) office identifying accommodations for exams must present the memorandum to the instructor by Friday, January 21. Providing the accommodations requires advance planning. Students will not be granted accommodations unless a memorandum from DSS has been received.

**Calculator Policy:** Calculators will not be allowed on exams or quizzes. Problems will be designed so that this does not create an issue. Occasionally, a calculator may be required to complete homework problems, but students should not rely on calculators for simple arithmetic or trigonometric calculations.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:** Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://rpi.app.box.com/s/d4ndpn3f7qdwxh9jaz5n1hkkr0mnd25e) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams and quizzes must be the student’s own. In addition, no calculators, computers, tablets, open books, open notes, phones, headphones, or smartwatches may be used during exams. Talking or communicating with other students in any way during exams is also prohibited. Students may discuss homework with other students, tutors, the TA, or instructor, but the work submitted must be the student’s own.

The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
