## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

First-order differential equations, second-order linear equations, eigenvalues and eigenvectors of matrices, systems of first-order equations, stability and qualitative properties of nonlinear autonomous systems in the plane, Fourier series, separation of variables for partial differential equations. Prerequisite: MATH 1020 and some knowledge of matrices (from the [Rensselaer Catalog](http://catalog.rpi.edu/))

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Mark H. Holmes, Introduction to Differential equations, 2ed, XanEdu Publishers.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Mathematical models of practical problems in science and engineering often involve differential equations. The equations may be written as a single equation of a certain order or may be written as a system of first-order equations, and they may be linear or nonlinear. The goal of this course is to teach analytical techniques for the solution of elementary differential equations that arise often in areas of application.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Successful completion of this course should enable you to demonstrate an understanding of fundamental techniques for the solution of first-order and second-order ordinary differential equations.
- Successful completion of this course should enable you to demonstrate an understanding of Fourier series and separation of variables for certain partial differential equations.
- Successful completion of this course should enable you to demonstrate an understanding of solution techniques and the behavior of solutions of systems of first-order differential equations.
- Successful completion of this course should enable you to present written solutions to problems in a clear, concise and coherent fashion.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are responsible for all material covered in lectures and recitations whether they are present or not. Quizzes will take place in recitations.

### Other Course Policies:

**Homework:** Homework will be assigned weekly. Late submissions will not be accepted. The lowest homework assignment score will be dropped at the end of the semester.

Success in the course depends on successful completion of all homework. Quiz and Exam problems are frequently similar to homework assignments and lecture examples.

**Quizzes:** A short quiz will be given most weeks during recitations. Dates and topics covered on each quiz will be announced in advance.

**Lectures:** Students should preview the textbook sections to be covered in class **before the class**. It is not necessary to have a thorough understanding of the material after a first reading, but knowing the basic facts and content should help in following the class discussion. This way we can spend more time on examples.

Lectures will be held online during the first two weeks of classes. In-person instruction in the room listed on the schedule on SIS will resume when the Institute allows it.

**Classroom Decorum:** Students must be on time for class and should refrain from leaving and reentering the classroom during class. If a student has a legitimate reason for arriving late to class or being excused early from class, then they should discuss this with the instructor before class. Phones may not be used during class and should be silent. Computers and tablets may not be used for anything other than taking notes. Students who are unable to courteously comply with these policies may be asked to leave the room.

**Recitations:** A Teaching Assistant (TA) will conduct weekly recitations. During these 50-minute sessions, the TA will discuss problems and answer questions about the material, including homework problems. A short quiz will be given most weeks during recitations. Dates and topics covered on each quiz will be announced in advance.

Recitations will be held online during the first two weeks of classes. In-person instruction in the room listed on the schedule on SIS will resume when the Institute allows it.

**Remote Students:** Students who have been approved by RPI to attend classes remotely must notify the instructors as soon as possible so alternate arrangements can be made.

**Calculator Policy:** Calculators will not be allowed on exams. Problems will be designed so that this does not create an issue. Occasionally, a calculator may be required to complete a homework problem, but students should not rely on calculators for simple arithmetic or trigonometric calculations.

**Health and Safety Protocols:** Students are required to follow the health and safety protocols of RPI, which are subject to change throughout the semester. Students who are not cleared for campus access are prohibited from attending in-person class. While masks are required in classrooms, eating and drinking in class is prohibited. Failure to comply with health and safety protocols will be met with a request to comply, a request to leave the class, or a dismissal of the entire class and referral of the non-compliant student to the Dean of Students.

**Visitors:** Visitors are prohibited during all class meetings. Only students who are registered for the class and cleared to be on campus should be present in the classroom. Students may only attend class meetings at the time and place listed on their schedule on SIS.

**Grade Appeals:** If upon reviewing the answer key and solutions for an exam or quiz a student feels that their work was graded incorrectly, then the student may submit a grading appeal to the instructor. Students have three days after the scores of exams or quizzes are released to submit an appeal. You may only appeal scores based on factual criteria (a correct answer was marked wrong, or scores were added incorrectly). Grade appeals should not be used to request more partial credit

*The above is subject to change.*

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

In the case of homework assignments, collaboration between students is encouraged. However, each student must write his/her own solutions and submit his/her own problem sets. Copying someone else's solution or using a problem-solving site (such as Chegg) is not allowed.

In the case of exams, there is no collaboration of any kind permitted, and each student will be required to write out and sign an Honor Pledge on each exam confirming this. If cheating is suspected, then an explanation will be requested. If the explanation is not satisfactory, then an exam grade of zero will be given and a report will be made to the Dean of Students.  
If assignments and exams need to be handled remotely, we are expecting a high level of maturity, responsibility and integrity from students. We are comfortable with this as these qualities will be expected throughout your careers, and in life generally.
