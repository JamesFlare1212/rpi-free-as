## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is devoted to advanced methods rather than theory. Content includes such topics as matched asymptotic expansions, multiple scales, WKB, and homogenization. Applications are made to ODEs, PDEs, difference equations, and integral equations. The methods are illustrated using currently interesting scientific and engineering problems that involve such phenomena as boundary or shock layers, nonlinear wave propagation, bifurcation and stability, and resonance.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Successful completion of the course should enable you to demonstrate an understanding of asymptotic approximations.
- Successful completion of the course should enable you to demonstrate an understanding of the difference between regular and singular perturbation problems.
- Successful completion of the course should enable you to demonstrate the ability to employ the basic techniques of perturbation theory.
- Successful completion of the course should enable you to demonstrate an appreciation of the utility and limitations of perturbation expansions.
- Successful completion of the course should enable you present written solutions to mathematical problems in a clear, concise and coherent fashion.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Homeworks assigned every other week, two in-class examinations, final examination.

## Grading Criteria

The course grade will be based on a weighted average of performance in the homework (45%), the two tests (15% each), and a comprehensive final examination (25%).

The instructor reserves the right to move a borderline grade up solely at his discretion, should the record demonstrate a consistent upward trend or a consistent performance at a certain grade level with the exception of a rare mishap. The course grade will be determined entirely by the numerical score as computed above. No mechanism such as additional work or a project will be made available to improve the score.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Working together on homework assignments in small groups is encouraged, and you should feel free to discuss among the group how to approach a problem or how to carry out certain calculations. However, detailed sharing of solutions is prohibited, and work submitted for grading is expected to be your own rather than a copy of some one else's work.

Finding a definition on line is fine, but handing in the entire solution to a problem found on line is plagiarism unless the source is properly cited. The HW is intended to help you learn the subject, and is not a test of your web-surfing ability.

Collaborating on examinations is not permitted either. If cheating is suspected, an explanation will be sought. An unsatisfactory explanation will result in a grade of zero and a report to the Dean of students.
