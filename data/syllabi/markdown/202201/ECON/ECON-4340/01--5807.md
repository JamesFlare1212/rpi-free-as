## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course describes how individuals make financial decisions, and how those decisions might deviate from those predicted by traditional financial or economic theory. Throughout the semester we will examine how the insights of behavioral economics complement the traditional finance paradigm by introducing some of the main psychological biases in financial decision-making and examining the impacts of these biases in financial and other economic decisions. This course will also introduce students to behavioral and experimental methodologies used in finance, economics and other disciplines.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- By the end of this course, students, in their written and verbal assessments, will demonstrate proficiency in identifying some of the main behavioral financial economic concepts (i.e. psychological biases) in economic decision-making.
- By the end of this course, students, in their written and verbal assessments, will demonstrate proficiency in assessing data and methods, and current research on how these biases impact financial and economic decisions.
- By the end of this course, students, in their written and verbal assessments, will demonstrate proficiency in knowledge of how individuals make financial and economic decisions and how these differ from rational behavior.
- By the end of this course, students, in their written and verbal assessments, will demonstrate proficiency in policies and other ways to improve individuals’ financial decision-making.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**General Participation:**

- In general, you are expected to join all lectures and be an active participant. If you cannot join lectures for any reason, you are expected to communicate this to me as soon as possible. In addition to attending class, I expect you to participate in in-class activities such as MobLab, participate in and contribute to in-class discussions (by asking questions, providing answers or comments etc.), and to have read the materials that will be assigned.
- I will keep track of each student’s active engagement weekly. I will determine your final grade based on the quality of in-class participation and general classroom citizenship.
- Please bring your laptops. However, you are expected to have all your electronics put away until they are needed for class. If you need your electronic device to take notes etc., you must obtain my prior approval.

**Paper Discussions**

- During each paper discussion day, we will discuss two papers in class. These will be either published or unpublished papers (chosen and assigned by me). Half of the class will be assigned one of the papers and the other half will be assigned the other paper.
- For each of these classes, you will be responsible for reading your assigned paper very carefully. Additionally, you are also expected to read the abstract and the introduction section of the other paper.
- Each of these papers will be led by 2-3 students, depending on the class size. Leaders are responsible for providing a quick summary of the paper at the beginning of class and engaging with other students by answering their questions as well as asking thoughtful questions.
- Whether it is your assigned paper or not, all students are expected to participate in the discussion of both papers.
- The papers will be assigned and posted about a week in advance. You should start reading the paper as soon as you can.

***Grades:*** Your grade will be determined based on i) how well you led the discussion, and ii) the quality of your participation during other paper discussions.

**Quizzes:**

- At the beginning of each paper discussion day, students will take a 15-20 min long mini quiz on their assigned paper. These quizzes will also have questions related to the topics discussed in class and other readings assigned recently. Make sure to attend all lectures and stay up to date with assigned readings to perform well in these quizzes.

**Homework Assignments:**

***Write-Up Submissions***

- Each student will produce a short write-up (around 300 words). This write-up must involve

1. i) a short description of a financial situation and
2. ii) a discussion of how one or more of the topics we discussed in class impacted that financial situation.

- The financial situation must be a real-life example, and the situation can be personal, professional, or something from current events.
- You are expected to use a professional language. Make sure to proofread your write-up for grammatical errors and typos before submitting.
- You are required to upload these write-ups to LMS by their indicated deadlines (listed on course calendar).
- Because of how the peer evaluation system in LMS works, late work cannot be accepted. **No exceptions**. Since the homework assignment is announced well in advance, it is your responsibility to start working on it as soon as you can.

***Peer-Evaluations***

- For each homework, you will read and evaluate two write-ups uploaded by other students. These peer-evaluation deadlines are also listed on the course calendar.
- You are expected to evaluate the write-ups based on the following criteria:
  - o Overall Quality
  - o Quality of the financial situation
  - o Appropriateness of the topic for the chosen financial situation
- Make sure to create a subsection in your peer-evaluation for each of the above criteria and carefully comment on them.
- Important: Your evaluation helps me determine how well you understood the concepts discussed in class.

***Homework Grade***

In determining your grade, I will consider how closely you followed the instructions and how well you understood the concepts discussed in class. The write-up portion will constitute 60% and each evaluation will constitute 20% of your homework grade.
