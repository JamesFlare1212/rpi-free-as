## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is designed to help students build a solid ground in data analytics and policy evaluation. Particular attention is given to the analysis of ‘panel’ or ‘longitudinal’ data sets. Topics to be studied include fixed effects and random effects models and their estimation and inference in the linear panel data framework. With a range of applications from the literature, we will also discuss some recently developed econometric techniques for policy evaluation. In particular, quantile regression will be introduced to investigate the heterogeneous treatment effects. Some other recent advances in data analytics including LASSO and adaptive LASSO will be briefly introduced.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Identify and summarize the important features of panel data models;
- Apply modern econometric techniques to analyze panel data and conduct policy evaluation;
- Demonstrate knowledge about the significance of current developments in panel data analysis by writing an applied research paper;
- Gain competence in the statistical software R.

## Course Calendar

### Calendar Details:

- **Attendance**: I will randomly check attendance for 5 times. First absence will be waived. After that, any absence will lose 20% of the credits for this category. If you have a legitimate reason to miss the class, please let me know **in advance**.
- **Quizzes:** There will be 4 quizzes in total. All quizzes are closed-book and -notes. I will announce quizzes in advance. Quiz will be graded on a 0-100 scale.
- **Assignments:** Unless otherwise noted, one assignment will be given at the end of each two or three lectures. You can work in groups, but each student must turn in his or her own version of the assignment. All assignments will be graded on a 0-100 scale (plus bonus points if applicable).
- A **midterm exam** will take place on ***February 17*** during class. Exam questions must be answered by yourself. This exam is open-book and -notes.
- **More about grades:** Problem sets may simply be checked for the percentage completed, or may be graded based on content. Late assignments will have their grades multiplied by 0.8 if handed in no more than one week late, or by 0.5 if handed in up to two weeks late, but cannot be credited if more than two weeks late or if after 4:00 p.m. on the last day of classes in the term. If answers to assignments are provided, please check them to identify any problems you are having and learn how to deal with these problems.
- **Final Project:** Each student will work on a project that involves using econometric methods to solve a real-world problem in a specific setting. The topic of this final project is flexible. You may select a topic that interests you based on the availability of data. Your final project report will be more like a mini-research paper. You need to collect the economic data related to your topic, apply the appropriate econometric methods, articulate your results and construct tables and figures. A complete project must consist of (1) introduction, including literature review, (2) econometric models, (3) data analysis, (4) conclusion, and (5) reference. Your project should be no more than 15 pages (excluding reference, tables and figures). The final project should be submitted before ***5:00 p.m., April 27***. You are encouraged to discuss your project with me. No late projects will be accepted.

This course will also focus on the importance of computational methods and *reproducibility* in econometric research. Thus, **you should always submit your electronic code files along with the summary of results and conclusions if computational analysis is required.** If you have any questions about your submission, feel free to contact me. The recommended software for the computational aspects of the course is R. R is an open source project. It is available without charge from http://cran.r-project.org/.
