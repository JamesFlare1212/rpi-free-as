## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

## This course provides an introduction to experimental methods in economics. It teaches you how to test economic theories, as well as introducing you to the experimental methods that are used to investigate questions that may not be possible to study using existing data sets. Students will be introduced to state-of-the-art research methodology in experimental economics.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Identify and understand the elements of experimental design
- Analyze and critique an experimental design
- Analyze human motivation and behavior across different economic paradigms
- Evaluate existing work on selected topics in behavioral and experimental economics
- Design an experiment, run the experiment, analyze data, present findings, and produce a final project report (ECON 6360 Only)

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

***General Participation:***

- I will determine your grade based on your participation and your general classroom citizenship. I will take your special circumstances into consideration. If your circumstances change, make sure to communicate this to me as soon as you can. I will keep track of your active engagement weekly.
- I expect you to attend class, participate in in-class activities such as MobLab, participate in and contribute to in-class discussions (by asking questions, providing answers or comments etc), and read the materials that will be assigned.
- In general, you are expected to join all lectures and be an **active** participant. If you cannot join lectures for any reason, you are expected to communicate this to me as soon as possible.

***Paper Discussions***

- During each paper discussion day, we will discuss two papers in class. These will be either published or unpublished papers (chosen and assigned by me). Half of the class will be assigned one of the papers and the other half will be assigned the other paper.
- For each of these classes, you will be responsible for reading your assigned paper very carefully. Additionally, you are also expected to read the abstract and the introduction section of the other paper.
- Each of these papers will be led by a few students, depending on the class size. Leaders are responsible for providing a quick summary of the paper at the beginning of class and engaging with other students by answering their questions as well as asking thoughtful questions.
- Whether it is your assigned paper or not, all students are expected to participate in the discussion of both papers.
- The papers will be assigned and posted about a week in advance. You should start reading the paper as soon as you can.

***Grades:*** Your grade will be determined based on i) how well you led the discussion and ii) the quality of your participation during other paper discussions.

***Quizzes:***

- At the beginning of each paper discussion day, students will take a 15-20 min long mini quiz on their assigned paper. These quizzes will also have questions related to the topics discussed in class and other readings assigned recently. Make sure to attend all lectures and stay up to date with lecture notes and assigned readings to perform well in these quizzes.

***Homework:***

- Guidelines will be shared with you in class and on LMS. This will be a simple assignment to get you comfortable with using excel for simple statistical analysis.

***Referee Report:***

- Guidelines will be shared with you in class and on LMS. Referee report deadline is indicated on the course calendar. Please notice that it is due by **1:30 pm** via email.

***Research Project (For ECON 6360 Students):***

You will be put in a group, and each group will be responsible for conducting one experiment. More detailed instructions to come, but here are the main parts.

1. *Find two or three research question(s) that interest you and provide a draft of your experimental design for each question* (**due March 3rd**). First, you will prepare a one-page proposal (you must use the template that I will provide) and receive feedback. At the end of this process, you will decide which research question to pursue.
2. *Submit your research proposal* (**due March 20th**). Once you decided which research question to study, you will motivate that question, review the literature, and provide a more detailed experimental design. In light of the feedback you will receive from me, you will move to the next step.
3. *Prepare Experimental Materials* (**due March 31st**). This means that you will have to prepare all the materials that you will use to run your experiment. This includes instructions, decision sheets, and any other materials that you may need for your experiment. You need to turn in all of your materials to me for my approval. You will revise your materials if needed. All experiments will take place using google forms.
4. *Conduct the experiment in class or online*. Your group will conduct the entire experiment. You will explain the instructions to the class, run the experiment, keep records, collect the data etc.
5. *Present the experiment in class*. This includes presenting your motivation and research question, summary tables and graphs, calculating useful statistics, performing statistical tests, explaining any relevant theory, comparing the experiments to others we have done in class and discussing the conclusions you draw from the experiment. I will provide a template for this presentation.

*Evaluation*. At the end of your presentation, your classmates will evaluate your research. We will also have an in-group evaluation. This means that each group member will evaluate one another. I will incorporate these evaluations to determine your grade from the group project.

6. *Final Project Write up*. (**due May 4th**) You will update and extend your research proposal, incorporate your findings. This is the final product of your hard work!

**Research Project Grades (ECON 6360):**

Students will earn numerical grades using the following weighting:

*Meeting deadlines (10%)*

You are expected to meet all deadlines. You will be penalized for each deadline you miss.

*Quality of Submitted Proposals (15%)*

The quality of your idea pitch and research proposals, how closely you follow instructions, as well as how well you revise your proposal based on the feedback you receive will help me determine your grade.

*Presentation (30%)*

The quality of your presentation and how closely you follow my instructions will help me determine your grade.

*Final Project Write up (45%)*

The overall quality of your research project write-up, data analysis and presentation, literature review, how well you follow my instructions etc. will help me determine your grade.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy may result in a grade of zero. If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of F for the course.
