## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will focus on learning about geological environment of humankind: the atmosphere, oceans, groundwater, rivers, glaciers, deserts, and coasts. We will explore the processes by which these and other features develop and change, the opportunities or hazards they present, and the ways in which humans can modify their development.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand how wind, water, ice and biology can change the surface of the Earth
- Understand Earth’s climate system
- Apply principles of logic and reasoning to analyze geologic problems
- Understand how geology relates to your everyday life, and how you interact with the Earth
- Travel to new and exciting places and explain to friends and family how those landscapes formed and may change in the future

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Post Class Assessments:** After each class, you will either be given a short quiz on BlackBoard OR be given a writing prompt to respond to on BlackBoard in the form of a minute paper in order to gage comprehension.

- Quizzes will mostly be multiple-choice questions. You may submit as many attempts as you would like, and your grade on each assignment will be the average of all attempts.
- A minute paper is a short (1/2 page) writing assignment designed to take no more than 5 minutes in order to determine the extent to which you comprehend important concepts from class. These are used to discover common “sticking” points and assess information that was unintentionally vague or difficult to understand—this is a low-stakes way to allow misconceptions to be addressed before you get to a high-stakes exam question. It is also an opportunity for you to evaluate what you recall and allow you to self-assess (for this reason, you should not answer these with your notes-use just what you retained and can recall). Minute papers will be graded based on effort and completion, as opposed to accuracy as follows:
- 0 points-not submitted
- 1-2 point-Incomplete response (not addressing prompt)
- 3-4 points-Partial response (addresses prompt but is unclear)
- 5 points-Full response (fully addresses prompt, and is clear)

You will be given the last 15 minutes of class to complete post class assignments but can choose to take them after class. *All post-class assignments need to be completed within 24 hours.* Two Post Class Assessments will be dropped at the end of the semester.

**Real World Geology:** Throughout the semester, you will write **two** discussion board posts about geology in the world. This could be describing a TV show or Podcast where relevant topics were discussed, updating on media stories involving relevant topics, etc. Your post should directly relate to topics we have discussed in class, but there may be other interesting parts you would like to also include. Your post must include:

1. A brief overview/summary written in your own words
2. Any insights you have into the topic based on what you learned in class
3. Link to the original source

The first post is be due by Friday, February 18th and the second is due by Friday April 1st.

**Exams:** There will be 3 exams throughout the semester. Each exam will cover the topics preceding that exam (**not cumulative**-however, keep in mind that we will be building upon prior knowledge). There will be **no** make-up of exams without a legitimate excuse discussed **prior** to the exam (I reserve the right to determine what is “legitimate”). Exams will be taken online via LMS*.* They will open at 8:00 AM the day of the exam and you will have 24 hours to complete the exam. You can take the exam at anytime that works for you within the timeframe. However, the exam will be timed-once you start the exam you will only have 60 minutes to complete (unless you have an exam accommodation). Exams must be taken independently without the use of notes, textbook or the internet. Sharing exam questions with students who have not taken the exam yet is not allowed. If this is found to happen, both students will receive a zero.

**Optional Final Exam**: During finals week, there will be an optional cumulative final exam. If you are happy with all of your exam scores, you do not need to take this. However, if you are unsatisfied with one of your exam scores, taking the final will replace the exam grade for your lowest exam. If you score lower on the Final than on all of your exam scores, the Final Exam will not be used in your final grade calculation. This is your opportunity to go back, revisit material and try again!

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance:** I assuming you are coming to learn. Attendance will not be taken, as I also assume you are responsible for your own education and are paying for this course. Nevertheless, you should know that poor attendance at lectures is generally associated with a poor final grade. The lectures will contain material not covered in readings. Class notes will be posted on LMS. However, do not assume that looking at the lectures without coming to class will afford you the knowledge you need to pass the class.

### Other Course Policies:

**Late Policy:** If an assignment is due in class, it will be due at the start of class. If it is turned in at the end of class, it is considered late. Assignments will be accepted up to 1 week past the due date. Scores on late assignments will be penalized 10% each day they are late. So, if an assignment is due in class on a Tuesday at 2:00 pm and you turn it in between Tuesday at 2:01 pm and Wednesday at 2:00 pm, you will lose 10%. *After 1 week, the grade becomes a zero.*

**Accommodations:** If you need any special accommodations (learning challenges, religious holiday conflicts, planned classes you will be missing), please set up a meeting with Dr. Cadieux during the first 2 weeks of classes.
