## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Each lab is worth 20 points. Labs will be completed and turned in and graded on completion, effort and correctness. Each lab will be due at the start of the next lab period.

**Lab Grading Rubric**

***Participation, completion and data collection: These criteria examine whether the student is actively involved in the required lab activities and gathered all the required data***

| **Points** | **Criteria** |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Student makes little to no effort to fully complete lab. Data collection blank or missing most of the critical information; if drawings are needed, they are missing |
| 3-1 | Student shows moderate effort to organize lab information; some missing and incomplete answers |
| 6-4 | Some data present, but many of the critical values are missing or clearly incorrect; some drawings are missing or incomprehensible |
| 9-7 | All data present and generally correct, but poorly organized or unclear; drawings are messy and/or poorly labeled |
| 10 | All data present and correct; clearly organized; drawings are neat and well labeled. Answers are complete |

***Content: These criteria focus on analyses based on the data collected and questions asked related to the results.***

| **Points** | **Criteria** |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Missing or highly inaccurate interpretations of the data; answers to all or most questions missing; misunderstandings of terms and concepts |
| 3-1 | Poor job discussing the results; major flaws in reasoning or explanations; not all questions answered |
| 6-4 | Ok job discussing the results; flaws in reasoning or explanations; answers not fully developed |
| 9-7 | Good job answering questions and interpreting the data, but too general, cursory, or incomplete **or**with some inaccuracies or flaws in reasoning |
| 10 | Excellent answering questions and interpreting the data; questions answered fully with no inaccuracies |

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Missed labs:** There are **NO** excused absences for lab. The lowest lab grade will be dropped, in order to allow every student the opportunity to miss one lab due to illness, a schedule conflict, etc., without penalty. (Exceptions will be made for COVID-related absences and missed labs).

### Other Course Policies:

**Missed labs:** There are **NO** excused absences for lab. The lowest lab grade will be dropped, in order to allow every student the opportunity to miss one lab due to illness, a schedule conflict, etc., without penalty. (Exceptions will be made for COVID-related absences and missed labs)

**Late Assignments:** I understand that we are currently living in an uncertain time and there may be circumstances that come up and do not allow you to complete assignments on time. If you are having health or technology issues, please communicate with Dr. Cadieux as soon as you can (i.e. **BEFORE it is due**!) so that flexibility can be arranged for a lab due date. If you do not communicate with me prior to the due date, late assignments will lose 2 points each calendar day.

**Re-do Assignments:** Because a lot of lab work will be done independently, there is the possibility that you may work through a lab assignment and misinterpret the questions, or struggle to understand a concept (if this happens, make sure you reach out to TAs or Dr. Cadieux for help!). To build in some flexibility in case this happens, once you receive a grade and feedback on your lab assignment you have the opportunity to re-do the assignment and submit it for a re-grade. ***Each student can re-do up to two labs!*** Re-do assignments must be turned in ***within a*** week of receiving feedback (i.e. You turn in Lab 1 on Wednesday, September 9th and receive feedback on Monday September 15th—if you choose to re-do the assignment, the revised assignment is due on Monday September 22nd). *You cannot choose to re-do all lab assignments in the last week to boost your grade!* **If you miss a lab/don’t turn in a lab, you cannot re-do it!**

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Science is a collaborative subject! You are strongly encouraged to discuss course material with your classmates. Working together allows students to ask each other questions and brainstorm ideas together, but the resulting work should reflect each student’s own understanding of the topic. On labs where you are allowed to share results with your peers, specific instructions will be provided. However, unless specifically told otherwise, you should not be sharing your written or drawn work for submission with classmates.

***Submission of any work that is not your own will result in a failing grade for the course.***
