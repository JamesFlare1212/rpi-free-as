### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**1. Analytical Skills** – Students will demonstrate ability to read an existing building and distill an architectural idea from a case study project. Students will demonstrate ability to communicate reading of an existing building in a series of clear graphically annotated diagrams. Students will demonstrate ability to analyze and diagram an urban condition.  
**2. Context** – Students will demonstrate ability to understand context in the form of existing buildings, site, and solar orientation as well as demonstrate ability to design in response to context.  
**3. Generative Drawing** – Students will demonstrate ability to think abstractly and imaginatively in two and three dimensions and will demonstrate how abstraction informs an architectural proposition.  
**4. Programming and Massing** – Students will demonstrate ability to understand and organize a simple architectural program and show how it informs building massing.  
**5. Structure** – Students will demonstrate ability to understand and design realistic structural thicknesses of wall, floor, and roof assemblies.  
**6. Two-dimensional Orthographic Drawing** – Through iteratively developing a series of floor plan, section, and elevation, students will demonstrate ability clearly represent architecture and accurately design a building at a range of scales from site to preliminary floor and wall assemblies.  
**7. Large-scale Physical Modeling** – Students will demonstrate ability to construct a large-scale physical model that represents a portion of a building in detail. Students must demonstrate ability to bring together multiple materials and ability to skillfully craft a physical model.  
**8. Three-dimensional Imagery** – Students must demonstrate ability to compellingly communicate conceptual and aesthetic aspects of an architectural project.  
**9. Writing** – Students must demonstrate ability to clearly and concisely communicate an architectural agenda through using writing as a complement to graphic and physical production.  
**Additional GAD2 Learning Outcomes**  
The Masters of Architecture program delivers a NAAB accredited degree and is also geared toward instructing students who may subsequently pursue PhD research, other post-professional study, and/or a career path in architectural education. As such, the graduate experience in this course is distinct from the undergraduate experience.  
In addition to those noted above for AD3, GAD1 results in the following learning outcomes:  
1\. Students will demonstrate development of a distinct set of research skills that allow critical inquiry into socio-cultural and disciplinary aspects of multi-unit mixed use housing design.  
2\. Students will demonstrate socio-cultural and disciplinary knowledge in written and graphic form and demonstrate how this knowledge critically informs the design of museums.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Assessment**  
All deliverables described above must be submitted for the final review. Course grades will be determined as follows:  
\- 10% – precedent documentation and analysis  
\- 10% – context and site analysis  
\- 10% – generative drawings  
\- 10% – programming and massing  
\- 20% – demolition plan, floor plans, sections, and elevations  
\- 15% – unit design and detail chunk model  
\- 10% – three-dimensional imagery  
\- 05% – project manifesto  
\- 10% – peer grading (peer grades will be submitted to section leaders weekly)  
Grades will be established according to the following policy:  
\- A (EXCELLENT) Exceptional performance strongly exceeding the requirements of the course, showing strong academic initiative and independent resourcefulness.  
\- B (GOOD) Performance above the norm; accurate and complete; beyond the minimum requirements of the course; work demonstrates marked progress and initiative.  
\- C (AVERAGE) Satisfactory work that adequately meets minimum requirements and demonstrates satisfactory comprehension, communication skills, and effort; demonstrates little initiative to investigate the problem without substantial input from the instructor; work shows little improvement.  
\- D (INFERIOR) Unsatisfactorily meets minimum requirements; demonstrates minimum comprehension, communication skills, and effort at an inferior level; initiative lacking; improvement not noticeable. (D grades not available to graduate students).  
\- F (FAIL) Does not meet minimum requirements; fails to adequately demonstrate comprehension, communication skills, and effort.

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is required at all class meetings during the semester. Two (2) unexcused absences from class will result in the lowering of the grade by one (1) letter. Four (4) unexcused absences from class during the term result in an automatic F. Whether an absence (health, immediate family death, officially approved field / sport trip, etc.) is excused or unexcused is determined by the Students Experience Office (4th Floor Academy Hall, x8022, se@rpi.edu), upon presentation of required documentation, according to established policies and procedures (see http:// http://se.rpi.edu/policies/ea/).  
Students are required to work in studio during studio hours, and have the required materials and basic tools to develop the assignments. Work includes participation in site visits, design and development, pinups, presentations, discussions and field trips. Failure to work in class with undivided attention and disruptive behavior (tardiness, leaving early, lack of participation, general socializing, etc.), as well working on assignments from other classes during class, will count as absences.
