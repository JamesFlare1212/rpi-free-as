## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**COURSE DESCRIPTION**

The Integrated Design Schematic Studio (IDS) is the first part of a two-semester comprehensive design studio which aims to prepare students for the complexities of architecture. For this semester students will focus on the broad strokes of making a fully functional building which means that students will not only focus on the design, but also determine appropriate structural and mechanical systems, as well as lay out the spaces in accordance to local building and zoning codes including accessibility and egress requirements. Schematic design, as described by The Architect’s Handbook of Professional Practice, establishes the scope and conceptual design of a project, and the scale and relationships among the proposed building components. The primary objective is to arrive at a well-defined, feasible concept and to present it in a clearly understandable form.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Learning Outcomes Health, Safety, and Welfare in the Built Environment [NAAB SC.1] — students will understand the impact of the built environment on human health, safety, and welfare at multiple scales, from buildings to cities. Professional Practice [NAAB SC.2] — students will understand professional ethics, the regulatory requirements, the fundamental business processes relevant to architecture practice in the United States, and the forces influencing change in these subjects. [partial fulfillment] Regulatory Context [NAAB SC.3] — students will understand the fundamental principles of life safety, land use, and current laws and regulations that apply to buildings and sites in the United States, and the evaluative process architects use to comply with those laws and regulations as part of a project. [partial fulfillment] Technical Knowledge [NAAB SC.4] — students will understand the established and emerging systems, technologies, and assemblies of building construction, and the methods and criteria architects use to assess those technologies against the design, economics, and performance objectives of projects. [partial fulfillment] Design Synthesis [NAAB SC.5] — students will develop the ability to make design decisions within architectural projects while demonstrating synthesis of user requirements, regulatory requirements, site conditions, and accessible design, and consideration of the measurable environmental impacts of their design decisions. Building Integration [NAAB SC.6] —students will develop the ability to make design decisions within architectural projects while demonstrating integration of building envelope systems and assemblies, structural systems, environmental control systems, life safety systems, and the measurable outcomes of building performance. ### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**PHASE I - SITE ANALYSIS + PROGRAM** For this first phase, students are to create a thorough yet expeditious analysis of existing conditions and context as well as program requirements. Context can be physical as well as social, economic, and political. Analytical drawings and diagrams shall be produced to describe the site and its relationship to multiple contexts.

**Deliverables for Program**:

- Adjacency Diagrams - create 2d diagrams that establish relationships and heirarchies between the program elements
- 2D+3D Diagrams - identify relationships and scales of all program elements in three dimensions.
- TEXT setting forth not only your understanding of the facility and its proposed program elements, but describing the LOGIC OF THE SPATIAL RELATIONSHIPS among these.
- Day in the life Narratives - identify three potential users of different background and imagine how they will use the facilities for certains period of tim.e

**Deliverables for Site Analysis and context:**

- Site Plan
- Site Collage - illustrate historic, social, political forces at play with respect to this site and neighborhood
- Zoning Axonometric Diagrams
- Climate/Environmental Diagram - illustrate how orientation and exposure will become meaninful variables for this site intervention.
- Mobility Diagram - how does one arrive to this site by foot, bike, bus, subway, automobile
- Land-Use Diagram - identify the land uses for buildings in the area around the site
- 3d Model - A 3d Rhino model from the city’s website is provided with building massing of the neighborhood. Missing items or those requiring further definition for the site and immediate bounding conditions need to be incorporated.
- Site photography may be used in select cases to punctuate points or yield graphic effect but will not replace the analytical drawings and models in the list above

**PHASE II - CONCEPT DESIGN** In this phase, students will utilize their readings of the site and understanding and ideas of program distribution to yield conceptual solutions that yield 3-dimensional form and space and make meaningful connections to the existing context. This will be an iterative process with two milestone pinups: one in which two schemes will be put forward for presentation and evaluation followed by the midterm presentation in which one of the two previous schemes, or a synthesis of the two will be developed. Initial ideas of structure, circulation, envelope, and environmental approach will be part of this phase.  
**Deliverables:**

- Concept/Parti Diagrams
- Program Diagrams
- Site Plan - 1/32”=1’-0”
- Building Plans - 1/8”=1’-0”
- Sections 1/8”=1’-0” (Minimum 2)
- Process Study Models
- MIDTERM Physical Model - 1/16”=1’-0” . This should be formatted to fit into the class’ 1/16”=1’-0” site model.
- DRAFT Technical Drawing Set

**PHASE III - SCHEMATIC DESIGN** Following the midterm review, projects will go through iterative development to advance and coordinate the projects into integrated proposals. Students should aim to refine programmatic relationships, clarify circulations, develop the structural system, articulate a detailed envelope proposal, and integrate environmental approaches. A clear proposal that addresses these issues will be needed for a smooth transition into the IDD Studio.

**Deliverables:**

- Site Plan - 1/32”=1’-0”
- Building Plans - ⅛”=1’-0”
- Perspectival Building Sections (line drawing with perspective behind) - ⅛”=1’-0”
- Building Elevations - ⅛”=1’-0
- Exploded Axonometric/Isometric Diagram - NTS
- Structural Diagram - NTS
- Program Diagram - NTS
- Environmental Diagram - NTS
- Study physical model with immediate site context at ⅛”=1’-0”

**PHASE IV - PRESENTATION PREPARATION** Following the penultimate review, students should concentrate on developing the project through drawing and finalizing the Technical Drawing Set. In this phase students will build the final physical model with immediate site context at ⅛”=1’-0”  
**Deliverables:**  
1\. Building Plans (all)- ⅛”=1’-0”

2\. Building Sections (2) - ⅛”=1’-0”

3\. Building Section (1) -1/4”=1’-0”

4\. Perspectival Wall Section (line drawing with perspective behind) - ½”=1’-0”

5\. Renderings - Minimum of two exterior and two interior views

6\. FINAL Technical Drawing Set

Optional

7\. Detail @ typical Envelope/slab intersection - 3”=1’-0” (plan and section)

8\. Detail @ High Line/building entrance -3”=1’-0” (plan and section)

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**GRADING CRITERIA**

Course grades will be determined as follows:  
15% – Phase 1: Deliverables, Research, Site & Program  
15% – Phase 2: Deliverables, Conceptual Design And Building Organization  
50% – Phase 3: Deliverables, Schematic Design Final Presentation + all Physical Models  
15% – Phase 4: Deliverables, Schematic Design Tech-Set  
5% – Attendance and Engagement with The Studio Desk Crits, Pin-Ups, And Reviews

Grades will be established according to the following policy:

- A (EXCELLENT) The design work is exceptional in every way. It has been undertaken with uncommon insight and energy and the ideas have been pursued with criticality and creativity. The project shown a strong ability to integrate systems and exceed the functional requirements. The results demonstrate the ability to innovate.
- B (GOOD) Project is a creative and energetic response to the program, the work is timely, complete and well presented. Performance above the norm; accurate and complete; beyond the minimum requirements of the course; work demonstrates marked progress and initiative.
- C (AVERAGE) Satisfactory work that meets minimum requirements and demonstrates satisfactory comprehension, communication skills, and effort; demonstrates little initiative to investigate the problem without substantial input from the instructor; work shows little improvement. Building systems are treated in a default manner and are not significantly transformed with respect to other systems.
- D (INFERIOR) Unsatisfactorily meets minimum requirements; demonstrates minimum comprehension, communication skills, and effort at an inferior level; initiative lacking; improvement not noticeable. The work is not produced and submitted on time, or is incomplete. The various building systems show minimal integration.
- F (FAIL) Does not meet minimum requirements; fails to adequately demonstrate comprehension, communication skills, and effort. major project presentations may be missed or have inadequate or missing components or the work
- does not demonstrate the care and thoroughness befitting a professional education. Systems show no integration.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**  
The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic  
Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and  
teachers. Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers  
have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers  
must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own performance. Acts that violate this trust  
undermine the educational process.  
**Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism**  
Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of this course. All assignments that are turned in for a  
grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not  
solely completed by the student. Any mis-representation of work shall be evaluated and will result in a failing  
grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student will result in a failing grade for the course.  
All infractions will be reported to the Dean of Students and become part of the students record.  
**Academic Integrity Statement**  
(2014-2016 Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities, August 2014)  
Intellectual integrity and credibility are the foundation of all academic work. A violation of Academic Integrity  
policy is, by definition, considered a flagrant offense to the educational process. It is taken seriously by  
students, faculty, and Rensselaer and will be addressed in an effective manner.  
If found responsible for committing academic dishonesty, a student may be subject to one or both types of  
penalties: an academic (grade) penalty administered by the professor and/or disciplinary action through the  
Rensselaer judicial process described in the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities.  
Academic dishonesty is a violation of the Grounds for Disciplinary Action. A student may be subject to any of  
the following types of disciplinary action should disciplinary action be pursued by the professor: disciplinary  
warning, disciplinary probation, disciplinary suspension, expulsion and/or alternative actions as agreed on by  
the student and hearing officer. It should be noted that no student who allegedly commits academic  
dishonesty will be able to drop or change the grade option for the course in question. A record of disciplinary  
action is permanently maintained by the Institute as noted below:  
RECORD OF DISCIPLINARY ACTION (2014-2016 Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights &  
Responsibilities, August 2014, p.15)  
Any disciplinary action can be disclosed to federal, state or local government entity, law enforcement,  
licensing or certification board, or corporate entity upon request of said agency if and only if: (a) by subpoena  
or (b) a student signs a confidentiality waiver for said agency or government entity.
