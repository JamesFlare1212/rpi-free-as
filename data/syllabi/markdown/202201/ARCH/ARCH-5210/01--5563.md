## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Architectural Design 4 tackles the problem of designing housing in downtown Troy, or more specifically *multi-unit collective* housing, in which some aspect of the domestic realm is shared among multiple domestic spheres. While many aspects of our personal and professional lives are increasingly communicated into collective spheres, a majority of our domestic spaces are structured by the prevailing American ethic of individual property, individual building. This ethic has manifested itself predominately in suburbs developed since World War II, and has now become such a fixture of a particular type of consumer-driven lifestyle that it is being exported to other countries, other cultures.

Clearly this is not a sustainable future as the world becomes increasingly urbanized and more extreme pressures are placed on the Earth’s resources. So as a typology, multi-unit collective housing must be understood as a global cultural necessity due its relative efficiency in terms of quantity of land and building materials required to construct each housing unit. Additionally, this semester will explore adaptive reuse of the former St. Mary’s Church in downtown Troy, both pragmatically as an energy saving strategy for building, and critically as a contextual exercise. The semester-long project will be to design a mixed-use, multi-unit housing project entirely within the existing perimeter of the St. Mary’s Church building.

Pragmatically speaking, a successful adaptive reuse project repurposes an existing structure in a new way. It takes advantage of embodied energy in the existing structure and reduces the amount of active construction energy required to complete a project while considering issues of constructability between new and existing building systems. Critically speaking, a successful adaptive reuse project investigates relationships between existing and new architectural elements from contextual and aesthetic standpoints.

The studio will address the design of housing at range of scales from the small, intimate scale of quotidian ritual to the scale of the building, its various components, and its site. There will be structured moments in the semester when work will jump from one scale to another to effectively develop the project at the scale of the entire building and its site, and at the scale of details within an individual housing unit. Working in teams of two will ensure that projects are thoroughly developed at a range of scales.

Designing housing is an opportunity to materialize ethics of exchange and communication. It is an opportunity to mold and shape the form and materiality of the city into experiences of daily life. Ultimately, an architect’s attitude toward designing housing is a socio-political act. Projects designed in AD4/GAD2 must successfully navigate the pragmatics of generating and organizing a complex architectural program into a coherent building that sensitively responds to and shapes its user’s needs, while also maintaining high levels of formal, material, and aesthetic experimentation that push the discipline forward.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**COURSE COMPONENTS, SEQUENCE AND DELIVERABLES**

1. **Precedent Documentation and Analysis**

Each two-person team will be assigned one housing precedent and one adaptive reuse precedent to document and analyze. Each team will acquire a working knowledge of the complexities of housing and adaptive reuse within these two specific projects and make a presentation to the entire studio about them, so that every student is exposed to a range of design strategies for housing and adaptive reuse.

Student teams will produce a series of drawings that comprehensively document one housing and one adaptive reuse precedent including plans, sections, elevations, and other drawings required to fully document the project. These drawings must be constructed from scratch in two dimensions. Images of drawings scanned from books and collected online may be used as underlays for these drawings, but they must not be traced. Rationalized dimensions, organizational principles, and architectural alignments must be used in a consistent and precise manner to CONSTRUCT the drawings rather than TRACE them. Develop clear, consistent, legible graphics for all drawings. Scale and format of drawings to be determined with section critic’s guidance. Include north arrow and graphic scale.

From these documentary drawings students will develop a series of analytical diagrams that describe the functioning, organization, and relation of the project to its surrounding site. Analytical diagrams must address the following:

- \- architectural parti
- \- overall organizational strategies in plan and section
- \- orientation to north and solar exposure
- \- distribution of program in plan and section
- \- horizontal and vertical circulation
- \- structure
- \- site circulation and relations
- \- repetition and variation in unit types (housing project)
- \- relationship between new and existing (reuse project)
- \- overall building dimensions (in feet and inches)
- \- unit, corridor, and stair dimensions (in feet and inches)
- \- gross square footage
- \- net square footage

Scale and format of diagrams to be determined with section critic’s guidance.

*AD4 & GAD2 Deliverables - Precedent Documentation:*

A set of constructed two-dimensional drawings comprehensively documenting one housing project and one adaptive reuse project. **Output drawings directly from Rhino rather than using Illustrator to assign graphics.**

*AD4 & GAD2 Deliverables - Precedent Analysis:*

A series of notated analytical diagrams addressing issues above. Scale and format of diagrams to be determined with section critic’s guidance.

2. **Context and Site Analysis**

Before beginning design of the building, students will make an analysis of *context* and *site.*

Context for the project is the St. Mary’s Church building at the corner of Washington St. and Third St. in downtown Troy, NY. The existing building fills its lot, and the new intervention must remain within this perimeter. Students will be provided with a ‘digital twin’, a highly detailed Rhino representation of the existing building. From this model and observations made on site* students must develop a series of diagrams analyzing the existing building. Consider the following in developing contextual diagrams:

- \- horizontal and vertical datums and grids organizing the building in plan and section
- \- spatial hierarchies
- \- access points
- \- significant architectural features (i.e. stained glass windows, ornamental features, etc.)
- \- structural system of the existing building
- \- horizontal and vertical circulation in the existing building
- \- solar exposure and daylighting of existing building

Site for the project is downtown Troy. Like many small cities in the United States, Troy has undergone several transformations in its history from colonial beginnings, to rapid industrial development spurred by rail and water transportation infrastructure, to a loss of manufacturing and disinvestment, to a current period of some redevelopment. Architecture, how it is conceived and used is of course a part of the changing nature of the city. Buildings in Troy originally conceived for manufacturing and industry are being converted to residential and commercial uses. Brownstone buildings originally conceived as single-family houses, then converted into multi-unit apartment buildings are now being converted back to single-family houses. Church buildings are another part of this evolution of the city. As the city’s population de-secularizes and churches are decommissioned, there is an opportunity to repurpose disused church buildings.

Students must develop a city or neighborhood-scaled urban analysis focusing on an aspect of how use of the city has evolved over time. It may focus on one of more of the following: land use, building typology, income, cultural institutions, transportation, etc. This urban analysis will be used to inform programming of the project.

*AD4 & GAD2 Deliverables – Context and Site Analysis:*

A series of notated analytical context and site diagrams addressing issues above. Scale and format of diagrams to be determined with section critic’s guidance.

**If possible, under applicable institute COVID regulations an in-person site visit will be scheduled to tour the interior of the building.*

3. **Generative Drawings**

Context and site analysis will lead into a generative exercise that will become the basis of a formal and conceptual language for the project. Specifics of this generative exercise will be determined by each section critic individually.

*AD3 & GAD1 Deliverables – Generative Drawings:*

A set of speculative drawings/models based on the precedent analysis that form the basis of a formal/conceptual design language for the design project. Scale and format of drawings to be determined with section critic’s guidance.

4. **Programming and Massing**

The primary goal of this semester is to design a project that reuses the St. Mary’s Church building for housing and a public program to be determined individually by each student team. The project must be highly considered and resolved in terms of reuse strategy, planning, egress, structure, and envelope design. Begin the design process by exploring program and massing.

The project must include 30 units of housing. Students must determine type and distribution of units (ie. studio, one-bedroom, two-bedroom, etc.). The project must include one public program. Students must determine the type, size, and location of this program. Use context and site analyses in developing program. Consider how access and egress is organized and controlled for residents and public.

Students are required to develop a programming document that determines:

- \- number, type, size, and distribution of housing units
- \- any ancillary spaces deemed necessary and/or desirable for the project (lobby, meeting rooms, gym, etc.) and their sizes
- \- type, configuration, distribution, and size of public program
- \- overall project square footage
- \- ratio of built area to open space

Develop a physical massing model at 1/16” scale that explores and resolves distribution and organization of program in relation to the existing building structure and envelope. This massing model must clearly denote existing structure and existing building envelope in relationship to new housing and public programs. **Massing must not exceed perimeter of existing building, or 120’ above the sidewalk.**

*AD4 Deliverables – Programming and Massing:*

A written document specifying all aspects of program described above. Physical massing model at 1/16” scale.

*GAD2 Deliverables – Programming and Massing:*

A written document specifying all aspects of program described above. Physical massing model at 1/16” scale. 300-word text describing how context and site analysis informs approach to housing and public programming. This text must function as a thesis for how the project will operate critically as both an adaptive reuse and urban intervention.

5. **Demolition Plan, Floor Plans, Sections, and Elevations**

Building on skills developed last semester in AD3/GAD1 two-dimensional orthographically projected plans, sections, and elevations will continue to be used instrumentally in design. Like last semester, these drawings MUST:

- \- be built up from scratch – a three-dimensional model may be used as a reference underlay to help produce the drawing, however it must not be included in the drawing
- \- be used to explore and evaluate design decisions
- \- bring together aspects of the precedent analysis and generative drawings with design decisions made through explorations of massing, programming, and context
- \- describe all components of architecture at accurate scale
- \- be used to describe accurate floor, wall, and roof thicknesses
- \- be used to describe simple floor, wall, and roof assemblies
- \- be graphically clear through effective use of line weight and line type

Refer to Architectural Graphic Standards as well as the reference file provided to the studio for information regarding architectural drawing standards.

*AD3 & GAD1 Deliverables – Building Floorplans, Sections, and Elevations:*

One demolition plan minimum, three floor plans minimum (ground level, upper level), two sections minimum (longitudinal and cross), two elevations at 1/4” scale. **Output drawings directly from Rhino rather than using Illustrator to assign graphics.**

6. **Unit Design and Detail Chunk Model**

Architecture always operates across a wide range of scales, from the global scale of the project and its site to the local scale of how individual parts of a building are put together, and everything in between. In this phase of the project, shift design efforts from large-scale organization and figuration of the building to a smaller-scale design of individual housing units. Consider the following in designing housing units:

- \- entry sequence
- \- apertures in exterior walls/roof/floors of the building
- \- apertures in interior walls/roof/floors of the building
- \- relationship of private and public spaces within the unit
- \- sectional qualities
- \- aggregation of units
- \- window access for bedrooms according to fire code

Building on skills developed in building the tectonic detail last semester in AD3/GAD1, explore a portion of the building in a large-scale detail chunk model. This model must represent both new and existing architectural components. It must explore and show the following:

- \- detailed design of residential and public interior space in relation to exterior of building
- \- detailed exterior envelope development of the new addition
- \- relationship of new and existing architectural elements
- \- relationship of structure (new and existing) to envelope (new and existing)
- \- accurate floor, wall, and roof thicknesses
- \- simple floor, wall, and roof assemblies

This model does not need to show the full height of the building, it may be a chunk extracted from 2-3 floors of the building at any selected height.

*AD3 & GAD1 Deliverables – Unit Design and Tectonic:*

Plans and sections of 2-3 adjacent units at 1/2” scale.

Physical model extracting a detail through new addition, existing building, and site. 1/2” scale. Model materials to be determined with section leader’s guidance.

7. **Three-Dimensional Imagery**

Three rendered views of the project are required: one aerial view, one eye level view of building exterior, and interior view within the new building. Test and save multiple camera options for each view. Once views have been selected, add detail to the three-dimensional model in areas visible in the views. Research rendered images and find a reference example(s) that references an appropriate aesthetic for the project. Develop a material and texture palette, a technique for handling context, and an overall aesthetic that complements and reinforces conceptual ideas of the project. Consider how an image conveys a vision of realism rather than being realistic, and how it is hyperreal rather than photoreal.

Images must be rendered in Rhino VRay and post-processed in Photoshop rather than output in highly deterministic visualization software such as Lumion, Sketchup, etc. Consider what aspects of image creation will be handled in Rhino vs. Photoshop. Refer to lecture given on digital imagery in AD3/GAD1, refer to image making techniques and process introduced in Digital Constructs 3 and 4.

*AD3 & GAD1 Deliverables – Three-Dimensional Imagery:*

Three rendered views of the new addition in relation to existing building and site. Size and format of images to be determined with section leader’s guidance

8. **Project Manifesto**

A manifesto is a concise, incisive declaration of intent; it is an argument for a position taken. Write a manifesto for the project describing the following:

- \- how the project addresses lessons learned in precedent analysis
- \- how housing and public programs respond to context and site analysis
- \- the strategy for preserving and/or replacing elements of the existing building
- \- an approach to the aesthetic relationship between existing and new elements of architecture
- \- any other aspects of the project deemed most important

*AD3 Deliverables – Project manifesto:*

300-word text graphically incorporated into final presentation boards.

*GAD1 Deliverables – Project manifesto:*

1000-word text, supported by bibliographic citations, that argues for the project from socio-political and disciplinary perspectives. The text must address specifics of the design approach and how they position the final work in relation to historical and/or contemporary museum and/or gallery projects.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Analytical Skills – Students will demonstrate ability to read an existing building and distill an architectural idea from a case study project. Students will demonstrate ability to communicate reading of an existing building in a series of clear graphically annotated diagrams. Students will demonstrate ability to analyze and diagram an urban condition.
- 2\. Context – Students will demonstrate ability to understand context in the form of existing buildings, site, and solar orientation as well as demonstrate ability to design in response to context.
- 3\. Generative Drawing – Students will demonstrate ability to think abstractly and imaginatively in two and three dimensions and will demonstrate how abstraction informs an architectural proposition.
- 4\. Programming and Massing – Students will demonstrate ability to understand and organize a simple architectural program and show how it informs building massing.
- 5\. Structure – Students will demonstrate ability to understand and design realistic structural thicknesses of wall, floor, and roof assemblies.
- 6\. Two-dimensional Orthographic Drawing – Through iteratively developing a series of floor plan, section, and elevation, students will demonstrate ability clearly represent architecture and accurately design a building at a range of scales from site to preliminary floor and wall assemblies.
- 7\. Large-scale Physical Modeling – Students will demonstrate ability to construct a large-scale physical model that represents a portion of a building in detail. Students must demonstrate ability to bring together multiple materials and ability to skillfully craft a physical model.
- 8\. Three-dimensional Imagery – Students must demonstrate ability to compellingly communicate conceptual and aesthetic aspects of an architectural project.
- 9\. Writing – Students must demonstrate ability to clearly and concisely communicate an architectural agenda through using writing as a complement to graphic and physical production.
- 1\. Students will demonstrate development of a distinct set of research skills that allow critical inquiry into socio-cultural and disciplinary aspects of multi-unit mixed use housing design.
- 2\. Students will demonstrate socio-cultural and disciplinary knowledge in written and graphic form and demonstrate how this knowledge critically informs the design of museums.
