## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course engages students in a critical examination of television and its historical and present status as a dominant popular culture medium. Television in this context is broadly understood as an evolving medium for motion pictures in the age of technological convergence. Using a variety of analytical and theoretical approaches, we’ll consider the significant influence television has on individual, social, and cultural experiences—addressing questions of representation, identity, economics, and ideology. The class uses a collaborative learning process, allowing students to influence the content and emphasis of the class and reflecting the democratic nature of popular culture media.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1\) You will develop a faculty for critical media engagement with television, learning how to apply aesthetic, historical, and critical frameworks as tools towards deepening your understanding of the medium. Watching numerous genres and modes of television, you’ll apply those critical lenses to their close study. Of particular importance is learning how to make connections between conceptual issues and ‘television’ as a cultural phenomenon and practice (of viewing, critiquing, sharing, and creating). Although this class focuses on television, it’s lessons can be applied to and contextualized in the larger mediascape; you should gain a deeper understanding of how media shapes us, as individuals, as groups and communities, and as cultures.

2\) As a complement to these critical tools, you will enhance your reflexive media awareness—your processual and dialogic understanding of your relationship to television—so that you can more fully articulate your experience, contextualize it, and empathize with others’ perspectives. You will develop your communicative/generative skills by articulating and refining your thoughts in oral, written, and presentational forms, and (if you are so inclined) by creating media works in various formats as a way of engaging with concepts we encounter in this class.

3\) Finally, this class uses a collaborative learning process; the course will be shaped by your interests and prior knowledge as a community, as well as by my disciplinary, personal and pedagogical expertise. Through the use of non-traditional educational strategies, you’ll engage in meta-examinations of the learning process, considering how the course functions as a ‘medium’ for learning.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- CLO 1 Identify, explain, and evaluate conceptual and theoretical strategies involved in media studies as relates to television.
- CLO 2 Apply critical lenses to media texts, and debate the value and function of those critical strategies.
- CLO 3 Summarize and discuss the historical and socioeconomic origins of television as an evolving popular culture phenomenon.
- CLO 4 Work collaboratively with a group of peers to compose a multi-faceted critique of a media text.

Assignments / Assessment Brief Descriptions

What follows are brief descriptions of the various activities and assessments that can gain you points in the class. More detailed guidelines are available on the LMS.

PARTICIPATION:

Self-Assessments of Participation (Preparation + Presence & Professionalism)

[3 X 30,000, including the final self-assessment and course reflection] (*expected)

This class is based in discussion and community; your active participation is essential.

Three times over the semester, you will write brief self-assessments based on our criteria:

1\) Preparation for Class: This is a measure of how thoroughly you’ve prepared for the classes, including completing mini-assignments, demonstrating clear understandings of readings, engaging in extra research, and participating in online discussion boards and forums. For the most part, any item that appears on the semester schedule in all caps as something to ‘READ’ or ‘PREPARE’ or ‘’WATCH’ denotes something that connects to this category.

2\) Presence & Professionalism: This includes your full attendance to class, mentally as well as physically, your conscientiousness in engaging with your classmates, and your courtesy professionalism in corresponding with the professor about potential absences or concerns. This also includes ‘contributions’ to the discussion / dialogue / community of the classroom. ‘Contributions’ is left open-ended, because some will often offer comments in full-class discussion; some will be more active in small break-out groups; some will make comments on online forums, or answer mini-assignments in great detail (this is where ‘Preparation’ spills over into ‘Presence’); some of you will find other ways to contribute to our community’s examinations of TV.

Keep in mind, you’ll be self-assessing and negotiating ‘Participation’; using my notes, I will be keeping you honest in that self-assessment. This is also a chance for you to reflect on the class, and give me direct feedback on how the class is going. Your comments will shape the class.

\* Note: The final self-assessment will include a reflection on the final project process and the course itself as a whole—a chance to provide me feedback that will help me to shape the class in the future.

‘You’re the Critics’ Days – Group Class Leading [40,000]

During weeks 5-8, in small groups of 2-4 students, you will prepare and present a chapter from the textbook How to Watch Television for the rest of the class. In each case, you will be expected to: A) Choose an episode or clip(s) for us to watch the day before your ‘Critics Day’; B) Very briefly recap the main points of that chapter; C) Apply that chapter’s idea or method to a TV show or shows of your own choosing; D) Involve the rest of the class in a discussion or activity related to its concepts.

MEDIA AWARENESS AND WELLNESS ACTIVITIES

A TV Experience Short Essay [20,000] (*expected)

At the beginning of the semester, you will write about a TV experience that was very important to you. You will be given guidelines on things to think about. These activities will help us introduce ourselves, and will be used as a focus for us to think through our relationship to TV.

TV Journal and Reflection [30,000]

You’ll create a journal of your television viewing over an extended period (recorded in whatever format you choose, as long as you can share it), and then write a reflection on that journal.

TV That Brings You Joy / Catharsis / Connection [10,000, repeatable 2X max]

For this simple exercise, you’ll watch TV that gives you a sense of joy, or catharsis, or connection, and you’ll write about why it does so, connecting it with what we’ve talked about in class. It’s that simple.

‘Revisiting your TV Experience’ Short Essay [20,000]

By the end of the semester you may revisit the essay that you wrote about in the first weeks, and reconsider that essay/media/phenomenon in light of what we did in class. In this case, you’d be expected to apply one of our critical lenses, or do research inspired by them, so that you ‘re-situate’ your understanding of that TV show (and/or a similar one that the original show makes you think of…).

EXPLORATIONS

‘One More Critical Lens’ Pecha Kucha Presentations [30,000 each, 2X max]

You can individually explore one more of the textbook chapters and present that to the class as one more ‘critical lens.’ In this case you will A) Recap main points and examples and B) Apply the lens to a show of your own choosing. You will use a modified ‘pecha kucha’ presentation format. ‘What’s ‘pecha kucha’?’ you ask!? I’ll show examples, but basically you have a short time and a set number of slides to convey a point. In this case, we’ll do 7 minutes, using 7 slides; you may use a script.

Explorations, Spin-offs and Rabbit Holes [Variable – Roughly 10,000 ~ 30,000 points possible per entry, depending on the scope of the projects, with a 60,000 point maximum across the semester]

With presentations occurring several times in the class (see the calendar), you will have the option of doing ‘explorations’ in TV studies. I will provide ‘exploration’ and ‘rabbit hole’ examples in class, and give guided ‘spin-off’ possibilities through extra reading materials and suggestions in a folder on the LMS and on the Discord. The idea behind these is to give you a chance to explore some aspect of TV that you are interested in, have you share it with the group, and give you ‘credit’ for it as a part of your learning in the class. You will provide some kind of a report—either a written essay, a slides document, or a ‘product’ in another medium of your choosing—and you will debrief the rest of the class during our presentation periods.

Examples might include: Doing a creative project that explores a chapter’s concepts more experientially (like an editing project or mash up inspired by a chapter, or a photography project inspired by one of the shows or readings we do); reading a substantial critical, historical, theoretical, or economically-based article from TV or media studies and sharing its main points and significance with the rest of the class; watching a TV-related documentary series and reviewing it for the class. If you come up with something completely different, suggest it to me and I’m sure we can work something out; if you have an idea for a topic but aren’t sure where to look, talk with me and I can help you to find something.

NOTE 1: Bonus for early submissions of ‘media awareness,’ ‘pecha kucha,’ and ‘exploration’ activities. There are 3 target dates for these types of activities. If you submit on the 1st target date, you will receive a 20% bonus on the points that you receive for that submission. If you submit on the 2nd target date, you will receive a 10% bonus for that submission.

NOTE 2: On connecting ‘media awareness’ activities, ‘pecha kuchas’, and ‘explorations’ to the critical binge-watching/final project...often, I’ve found that students will do one of these ‘explorations’ and then find that they are inspired to expand the explorations and/or include them in aspects of their final projects; this is highly encouraged.

‘CRITICAL BINGE-WATCHING’ / FINAL PROJECTS

For this assignment, and as a kind of hybrid final project for the class, in 5 groups of 2-5 students each (groupings determined by genre interests), you will decide on a show that you will examine in depth. You will all watch a predetermined number of episodes (i.e., binge-watch it), and find insightful ways to apply critical lenses to them; each member of the group will take a different lens/approach to the show, usually corresponding to a critical lens from one of the textbook chapters we’ve looked at (though your ‘lens’ could be something slightly different than the ones we’ve covered, or combine them).

Each of the 5 groups will have one entire class period to share your findings. You will: A) As a group, take 7-10 minutes to introduce the show for those who haven’t seen it; B) Use ~20 minutes to show us some clips or a short episode that gives us a sense of the show; C) Individually, each take approximately 7-10 minutes to show how a different critical lens sheds light on that show (ask yourself, ‘How does this lens change how I see the show?’)--the form that your critical project takes can be of several formats [see below]; D) compare and contrast your perspectives and/or open this up to the rest of the class for discussion.

For additional points, you may also prepare an individual creative project or video essay that can accompany the presentation, or an individual critical paper that can be turned in at the end of the semester, summarizing in writing the findings from the binge-watching activity.

Points distribution for the critical binge-watching activity:

Critical Binge-Watching Presentations [50,000 total]

Each of you may receive up to 50,000 points for the presentation work, although this is not a group-graded project. Your work on the collective aspects, and your contributions to the individual aspects, will each be evaluated separately.

Critical Binge-Watching Individual Project Submissions [variable, 10,000 - 50,000 points]

Each of you may also submit, either on the day of the presentation or at the end of the semester, an individual summation of your work for the binge-watching assignment. In the case of a video essay or a creative project, on the day of your presentation you should at least submit a work-in-progress of your individual project. In many cases, this can form the bulk of your individual ‘lens’ on the show that your group binge-watched.

Critical Binge-Watching Reflection Paper 30,000

OR short 4-7 Minute Video Essay 30,000 - 60,000

OR Creative Project 10,000 - 50,000

Paper: You will write a finely-honed Research Paper or Critical/Rhetorical Essay of approximately 1500-2500 words (7-10 pages).

Video Essay: You will create a brief video essay that illustrates a critical stance on, or an in-depth personal exploration of, some aspect of the show. I will show previous students’ examples.

Creative Project: You will engage in a Creative Project, instead of a final paper, working in whatever medium you’d like. This should somehow engage with concepts from the class.

For all final project options, you must include research sources that you will cite in a proper MLA formatted ‘works cited’ page.
