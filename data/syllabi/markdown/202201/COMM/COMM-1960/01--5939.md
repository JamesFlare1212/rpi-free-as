## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is designed to provide international graduate TAs with communication training so that they are able to present academic information clearly, interact effectively with students, practice classroom management skills, accommodate different learning styles, and understand cross-cultural aspects of teaching and learning.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Smith, J.A., Meyers, C.M., & Burkhalter, A.J. (2007). Communicate: Strategies for international teaching assistants. Waveland Press.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

By the end of the course, students will:

- demonstrate knowledge and competency in pronunciation, spoken grammar, fluency, and listening skills in English
- develop presentation styles that meet the needs of different types of learners
- gauge undergraduate students’ (audience) comprehension and respond to questions effectively
- understand cultural differences and how they impact teaching and learning
- detect fossilized errors in their own speech patterns and set goals for on-going improvement

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Teaching styles and intercultural communication skills  
Introducing yourself and a syllabus/class  
Pronunciation compensation strategies  
Describing visual aids  
Process analysis  
Checking for understanding and responding to student questions  
Presentation skills  
Eliciting discussion and leading group work  
Addressing and preventing common classroom issues

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1. Professionalism and Participation – 10%

Teaching is a profession with high expectations for modeling, cooperative learning, and establishing presence in the classroom. This is especially the case in virtual discussions in which statements are more easily misconstrued or misinterpreted. This course is designed to be interactive and target communication skills; therefore, your active and respectful participation is critical. Participation will consist primarily of in-class discussion on Webex, contributions to assignments on Blackboard, and completion of weekly assigned homework. It will be necessary to be familiar with the assigned materials in order to attain full credit. One or more non-submitted and/or late assignments will result in missed points that may affect your final grade.

2. Written journals – 20%

You will demonstrate your understanding of assigned readings, and contribute to class discussions, by writing several journal entries in response to prompts based on the reading. Journal entries should relate directly to the content in the assigned reading, and can incorporate your own ideas, reactions, and experiences related to teaching and learning.

3. Teaching Demos – 30%

You will have two teaching demonstrations throughout the semester. These teaching demonstrations will be short mini-presentations to the class, in the style of a lecture you would deliver as a course instructor. The purpose of these teaching demos is to practice your public speaking, apply the skills we are working on in the course, and build confidence and expertise discussing the content of your subject matter knowledge.

4. NativeAccent – 40%

Each TA’s speaking skills will be different. NativeAccent is a program designed to target students’ individual needs in the areas of English pronunciation, grammar, word stress, and fluency. Your grade in this area will be based on 1) your engagement (how often, and how much time, you use NativeAccent) and 2) your improvement throughout the semester.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
