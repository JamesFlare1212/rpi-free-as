## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course covers theory, research, and practice in interactive, multimedia interface design. Topics include visual perception, information architecture, rapid prototyping, usability testing, cross-cultural design, interactive data visualization, augmented reality, and intellectual property.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

The textbook for this course is *Designing Interfaces: Patterns for Effective Interaction Design* (3rd edition) by Jenifer Tidwell, Charles Brewer, and Aynne Valencia. There is also a chapter from the 2nd edition of *Designing Interfaces* (Jenifer Tidwell) that you must read for a required class exercise. Reading assignments from these books and additional reading assignments and videos to view are listed in the attached class schedule.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course will focus on the design of user interfaces for interactive, multimedia computer programs. The class will present theoretical and technical issues in user interface design.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Lectures and discussions will include topics such as:

- data visualization
- information architecture for interactive computing
- effective screen design for user interfaces
- cultural dimensions of interface design
- usability testing
- intellectual property

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to critically analyze different types of data visualizations and interfaces designs.
- Students will be able to define the audience and the objectives for an interactive program and apply visual design, data visualization, and HCI research to meet those objectives. Using this information, they will be able to develop a design document, wireframes, and a storyboard.
- Students will be able to develop an interactive prototype.
- Students will be able to test the prototype, evaluate the results, and propose revisions for the interface design.
- For the data visualization project, students will be able to: o effectively communicate about problems/issues in this field in which data is a relevant tool, including writing about, presenting on, and visualizing data; o critically evaluate the pros/cons of different types of data visualizations to determine the most effective ways to visualize specific data relationships; and o create effective data visualizations by identifying the audience and objectives, deciding what data relationships meet the objectives, and determining the most effective interactive data visualizations to show specific data relationships.
- Students will be able to analyze peer projects and provide constructive feedback on the projects during class discussions.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Class Attendance 5****% of Final grade**

Class attendance is required and will comprise 5% of your final grade. I will take attendance during the first 10 minutes of class. You may miss up to four classes without penalty and receive an A for class attendance. However, if you miss more than four classes, you will receive an F for that part of your final grade. Provide a **formal excuse** if you do not want an absence to count as one of your four allowed absences.

**Projects**

Assignments and required class workshops/exercises are described below.

Details for each assignment will be covered in class lectures and handouts that will be distributed and posted on LMS. The Analysis of Interactive Programs assignment is an individual project. All other projects are team projects. **Due dates are listed in the class schedule.**

**NOTE: If we have in-person classes when assignments are due, you must print the assignments in color and submit them as hardcopy at the start of class on the due date.**

**If class is meeting online when an assignment is due, you may submit the assignment (in color) via email at the start of class.**

**Analysis of Interactive Programs** **(Individual Project) 10% of final grade**

You will analyze the strengths and weaknesses of two websites and/or interactive programs, **one of which should be an interactive data visualization**. You should read the article “The Impact of Aesthetics on Attitudes towards Websites” (https://www.usability.gov/get-involved/blog/2009/07/aesthetics-and-attitude.html) and address the points outlined in the class handout. You should include screens from each website/program in the paper (500-750 words **total** for the analysis of **both** programs). See the assignment handout for additional requirements.

**Class presentations are required. If you fail to do a presentation, your grade for this project will be dropped one letter grade.**

**Data Visualization Project (Team Project) 20 % of final grade**

You will use interface design principles and data visualization research to create an interactive design to visualize data relationships and analyze feedback on the design. You will select the data to analyze, the audience and objectives for data visualization, and use data design research and theory to determine the best way to visualize the data using specific charts, data filters, and interaction to enable the user to compare different relationships. This project will involve a class presentation and a written paper. A handout with more details will be distributed later in the semester.

**Class presentations are required. If you fail to do a presentation, your grade for this project will be dropped one letter grade.**

**Design and Development of Interactive Program (Team Project)**

**There are three stages; each stage is 20% of final grade.**

You will have an opportunity to develop an interactive, multimedia program on a topic of your choice. The project that will be divided into three stages:

1\) Design Document, 2) Prototype, and 3) Usability Testing.

Each stage of the project will be graded separately, and the grades will be weighted as follows in your final grade:

**Design Document 20% of final grade**

**Prototype 20% of final grade**

**Usability Testing 20% of final grade**

**There will be class presentations for each of these three stages in the project.**

**Class presentations are required. If you fail to do a presentation, your grade for this project will be dropped one letter grade.**

**Progress Reviews 5% of final grade**

The progress reviews are additional class presentations that show your progress with the development of the prototype and the materials for the usability testing (for the interactive multimedia website/program on a topic of your choice). Your grade for the progress reviews will be determined as follows:

You complete four presentations: A

You complete three presentations: B

You complete two presentations: C

You complete one presentation: D

You do not complete any presentations: F

**Class Workshops and Exercise**

There will be a Social Media Workshop and an Augmented Reality Workshop in class. Each workshop will include a team exercise on social media and augmented reality interface design.

There will also be a required class exercise near the end of the semester. For that exercise, each of you will try all of the prototype programs (except the one you created) and provide written comments for the teams to use, so they can make final revisions before testing begins.

If you do not participate in these workshops and the class exercise, you must complete the work on your own and present the work in class. **Failure to complete either of the workshops or the class exercise will result in an Incomplete for the course.**

**Final Grade**

To summarize, you final grade will be determined as follows:

Class Attendance 5 %

Analysis of Interactive Programs 10%

Data Visualization Project 20%

Design Document 20%

Prototype 20%

Usability Testing 20%

Progress Reviews 5%

In this class, I will assign letter grades for each stage of the interactive project and the progress reviews. Your final grade will be determined by translating each letter grade into a numerical equivalent based on the following scale: A=4.0, A-=3.67, B+=3.3, B=3.0, B-=2.67, C+=2.3, C=2.0, etc. Your average for the course must be a 4.0 to receive an A. Your average must be at least 3.67 for an A-, at least 2.67 for a B-, etc. Also note the following:

**When class presentations are required for a project, you must do a** **presentation or your grade for that project will be dropped one letter grade.**

**All projects must be completed in order to receive a passing grade.**

**As previously noted, failure to complete the two workshops and the class exercise (see section titled “Class Workshops and Exercise”) will result in an Incomplete for the course.**

If you have any questions about your grade at any time during this course, you should see me as soon as possible.

**Late Projects**

All projects are due on the designated due date. Late projects will be lowered one letter grade for each day they are late. Saturday, Sunday, and holidays count as late days.

If you feel that a personal or academic problem is going to prevent you from meeting a project deadline, you should speak to me at least two days before the due date (not the day the project is due) to see if an extension is possible. At that time,

if I grant an extension, I will also set another due date. That deadline will be firm. **Projects that are handed in after the extended deadline will be lowered one letter grade for each day they are late.**
