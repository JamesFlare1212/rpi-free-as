## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a cross-listed course mainly focusing on Integrated marketing communication (IMC). IMC is a cross-functional process for creating profitable relationships with customers and publics by strategically controlling all messages sent to groups in a consistent, distinctive, and memorable way. Students learn to integrate marketing communication tools (e.g., advertising, public relations, sales promotion, event marketing, direct and digital marketing, and personal selling) to advance an organization’s success and brand equity. This is an interactive and hands-on course where students will develop skills in creating, planning, implementing, and evaluating client-oriented projects by developing an IMC campaign. Students are responsible for applying the content in Marketing Principles (MGMT 2430) to the project in this course. Students need to be especially familiar with the material pertaining to promotion, consumer decision making, segmenting, targeting, differentiation, and positioning.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course is interactive and hands-on by design. There will be varied learning opportunities in formats such as traditional lecture, in-class experiential exercises, assignments, and an experiential group project. The goal of the course is to help you understand how to develop an innovative and effective integrated marketing communications plan with potential to make a positive impact for an organization.

The objectives of this course are:

1. To help students gain an understanding of target customer needs and how IMC is being used by marketers to develop brand identities that leverage those needs
2. To help students identify and explain the major components of the IMC process
3. To acquaint students with analyzing IMC situations and problems
4. To provide students with the opportunity to develop skills in creating, implementing and evaluating IMC plan
5. To challenge students to consider ethics, diversity and global issues relevant to marketing communication

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Demonstrate conceptual and procedural knowledge of integrated marketing communications, including the concepts, components and process of IMC
- 2\. Articulate significant challenges involved in a particular IMC situation and implement IMC strategies to solve IMC problems
- 3\. Develop practical skills in creating, planning, executing, and evaluating client-oriented IMC projects and communicating IMC plans in both oral and written work
- 4\. Deliver high quality oral presentations, using both facts and data
- 5\. Integrate the IMC terminologies and theories to analyze, judge, and evaluate advertising commercials
- 6\. Demonstrate understanding and the ability to recognize the ethical issues in marketing communications
- 7\. Monitor and audit the activity of a social media channel and summarize the finding following specific IMC requirements

**GROUP PROJECT**

The primary objective of this team-based project is to acquaint students with the complexities facing companies as they communicate product offerings to the marketplace. The Integrated Marketing Communications (IMC) Plan Project involves student groups developing, organizing, and managing student-run IMC agencies. Each group needs to adopt a formal agency name (e.g., Brink Media) and develop a detailed IMC plan and prepares the deliverables for a client.

**Deliverables:** The IMC Plan Guideline will be posted on LMS and updated regularly. Project deliverables include two status reports, one final report, one final presentation, and original files for project implementations. Each group will give a 25-minute group presentation during the last week of class. The entire class is expected to participate and vote for the best presentation and presenter.

**Expectation:** You are expected to present your work in a manner that is consistent with a business professional. Work should be well-organized and flow in a manner that allows the reader to understand the purpose without having to try to figure out what you are saying. Write the paper from the third person point of view (grammar). Make sure that you are diplomatic to the client and that you are recommending these actions. Proofread for spelling and grammatical errors. Use headings, subheadings, and bullets as needed. This is expected for all homework assignments and this project. If a section does not make sense, multiple points may be deducted. These points will be taken off the final grade.

**Groups:** You will be allowed to form your own groups, but I reserve the right to place latecomers into appropriate homes and assign students using different learning mode (synchronous vs. asynchronous) to different groups. Groups should have about 4 members and strive for heterogeneity in composition. Please submit your group decision on LMS before the due date listed on Course Calendar. **Critical Note**: Your project grades rely on your group’s performance and your individual performance. At the end of the semester, group members will rate one another in terms of their relative contribution in group work. As such, shirking group responsibilities is by far the surest route to oblivion.
