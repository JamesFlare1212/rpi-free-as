## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The objective of this course is to provide and introduction to a range of ideas in both corporate and investment finance, and to provide a solid foundation in the principles and practice of financial decision making. Relatively little is assumed in the form of prerequisites: basic statistics, algebra, basic accounting, and a general interest in business. The course is delivered as a first course in finance with a focus on corporate finance. We will cover topics including personal finance, capital budgeting, debts, equities, capital structure, and financial decision making in firms. We will also introduce the relation between risk and return and how these concepts can be applied to the fair valuation of financial instruments.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course seeks to provide you with the tools and concepts that every general manager, entrepreneur, or manager in other functional fields should know. As such, the course is presented from a corporate finance perspective and should not be seen as an investments, banking, or personal finance course. However, essential, and important aspects of each of these topics will be covered as these are all important from the manager’s perspective.

1\. Students should develop an understanding of basic financial topics such as risk versus return, the time value of money, the valuation and pricing of financial assets, and the benefits of diversification.  
2\. Ability to apply the above techniques to carry out capital budgeting tasks.  
3\. Understanding of basic financial securities including equities, debt instruments, and derivatives.  
4\. Ability to value securities using basic concepts such as risk versus return, diversification, and market efficiency, and the time value of money.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The grade for the course will be based on performance in five activities: a mid-term exam, a final exam, problem sets, quizzes, and class participation.

**Problem Sets:**  
Problem sets should be done in groups. As such, students should form groups of no more than 4 students. Those students that are not in a group, will be randomly assigned to form groups. Each group will submit one copy of the completed problem set, which is due at the start of class on the day it is due. Each group member needs to individually solve the problems to check the reliability of the answers to the problems within the assignment. Just as important, it also enhances their learning, keeping in mind that problem sets are an important learning device. If you free-ride and rely on the work of the other group members, you will not learn the material and your performance on the exams will suffer. **No late homework will be accepted.**  
**Exams:** *There are two exams for the course, a midterm and a final. The midterm will be administered about halfway through the semester and the final will be administered during the Exam Week* **Grading:** Cases are graded at the group level.
