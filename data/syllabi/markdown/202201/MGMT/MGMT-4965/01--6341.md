## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The objective of this course is to introduce a systematic and analytical approach in marketing decision-making and to build skills and confidence in undertaking such analyses on your own. We will study tools for generating marketing insights from empirical data in areas such as segmentation, targeting, positioning, customer choice, new product design, and forecasting. This will be a hands-on course in which you apply the tools studied to actual business cases. We will cover various analytical techniques typically bundled under “marketing analytics” such as regression, cluster analysis, discriminant analysis, logit model, conjoint analysis, bass model, etc. We will NOT be approaching these topics theoretically, that is, via equations or proofs. Instead, we’ll learn how and when to apply each model and what they tell marketers. Students from MGMT 6965 will also complete a group project in which you collect and analyze data or introduce your fellow students to a marketing analytics technique not studied in class.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Unlike conventional integrative courses that focus on conceptual material, this course will attempt to provide skills to translate conceptual understanding into specific operational plans – a skill in increasing demand in organizations today.

Specifically, the course objectives are to:

1. Explain the role that analytical techniques and computer models can play in enhancing marketing decision making in modern enterprises.
2. Introduce a systematic and analytical lens to business processes and relationships.
3. Expose students to business examples demonstrating the value of the analytic approach to marketing decision making.
4. Introduce appropriate software tools to successfully apply the models and methods taught in the course to real problems.
5. Guide students in interpreting and utilizing the results of statistical modeling to enhance decision making in real-world marketing practice.
6. Provide an opportunity to conduct a marketing analytics final project from inception to completion (For MGMT 6965 Only).

## COURSE STRUCTURE

The basic pedagogical approach is to employ a mix of learning methods consistent with the relatively compressed ‘module’ format of this course. There will be lectures, discussions, video presentations, software tutorials, case analyses, presentations, and an exam. Class sessions will be devoted to probing, extending and applying the material in the readings and the cases. One could call this “**Tell-Show-Do**”, a sequence providing hands-on experience in using the course materials for making marketing decisions. Lectures will cover the concepts and models you need in order to understand – and to apply – a scientific approach to marketing. Applications are illustrated in the cases, readings, and the examples; the software tools allow for hands-on opportunities to apply the concepts and models to resolve real-life marketing problems.

During a case session, one or two group will present the case. The instructor together with the presentation groups will lead you through a discussion of the case. The instructor will act as a facilitator, asking questions, driving conversation, and keeping track of ideas as they emerge. You will learn more if you come to class prepared to share your ideas and your experiences, and open to listening to your fellow participants as they share theirs.

**Groups:** All assignments in this class, including case write-ups, case presentations and final project presentations, involve group work. I will assign you to a group of about 4~5 members, preferably with diverse background and registered at the same level (4965 vs 6965), but I will also consider your preference. Please find your friends and let me know your preference in the first two weeks. **Critical Note**: Your case and project grades rely on your group’s performance and your individual performance. Group members will regularly rate one another in terms of their relative contribution in group work. As such, shirking group responsibilities is by far the surest route to oblivion.

**EXAM**

There is an open-book final exam, so having a copy of the text could be of benefit.

Only if there is emergency, severe medical problem, or officially sanctioned Institute activities can you schedule a makeup exam. To do so, you need to get pre-approval from me and submit the formal Excused Absence Request through your class dean.

Final exam consists of questions that evaluate students’ ability of applying appropriate model on different contexts. Questions in the final exam might be based on the case assignments, readings, in-class exercises, or mini homework.

All case assignments in this class involve group work. If you put in effort in the group work and understand the readings, the exam should be relatively straightforward. It will be very helpful for you on the exam if you will have worked all the assignments, including those which you are not presenting. The exam might involve multiple choice, data analysis, short answer and short essay questions designed to permit you to display the breadth and depth of your understanding of course concepts and materials.

**CASE ANALYSIS (GROUP)**

This course focuses on “learning by doing.” In that spirit, teams will empirically analyze five assigned cases and develop their recommendations.

**Case Summaries 4000 level**: For each case that we will discuss, your team will submit an absolutely-no-more-than-two-page executive summary that contains your recommendations and rationale for them. Use the students’ case discussion questions as a guide in developing your recommendations.

**Case Summaries 6000 level**: For each case that we will discuss, your team will submit an absolutely-no-more-than-two-page summary that contains your recommendations and rationale for them. **Your recommendations must be based on strategic logic within the context of the case**. Use the students’ case discussion questions as a guide in developing your recommendations.

**Case Presentation**: Each team will also do at least one in-class **case presentation** (20 min) and **facilitate the discussion** in the case session (the number will depend on the number of teams in the course; we will have at least one team, but preferably two, presenting and leading discussion on each case). This is not shorthand for “blow off the cases you are not presenting”. Presentations will be arranged well in advance for your team to prepare appropriately. These will be assigned randomly, once groups stabilize. You **need not** to submit an executive summary for the case you present. Instead, send me your slides and analyses. **Anal-Retentive Note**: Please send summaries in PDF; for presentations, I’ll need both your PPT and “native” format (XLS, DOC, etc.). Include your group number (I’ll send those out) in all filenames and on all documents. These case write-ups are due 2 hours before class: 10AM on the day of case sessions.

**Expectation:** You are expected to present your work in a manner that is consistent with a business professional. Work should be well-organized and flow in a manner that allows the reader to understand the purpose without having to try to figure out what you are saying. Write the paper from the third person point of view (grammar). Make sure that you are diplomatic to the client and that you are recommending these actions. Proofread for spelling and grammatical errors. Use headings, subheadings, and bullets as needed. If a section does not make sense, multiple points may be deducted. These points will be taken off the final grade.

**FINAL PROJECT (GROUP)**

Each group from MGMT 6965 will complete a final group project. Two types of topics are appropriate for final project. First, you can apply one of the tools studied in class to a particular business problem, preferably a real problem facing a specific company. This will most likely involve the collection of primary data and an in-depth analysis of the data using the Marketing Engineering software. Second, you can choose a marketing analytics technique that we didn’t cover in class and introduce your fellow students to this technique. You can use our textbook, other textbooks, as well as cases on Enginius to prepare for your “teaching”. The presentations are at the end of the semester, but you should start thinking about possible topics for your project early in the semester and talk to me about ideas suitable for the project.

To make sure that you are on track, I would like each group to submit a **midterm** **proposal PPT** outlining the problem that you propose to study before 10AM on the day of presentation (see Calendar). This day, I would like each group to present a 5-10 minute proposal outlining the objectives of the project, your general approach to the problem, data to be employed, how it is to be analyzed, how this might solve the problem you are analyzing, and any problems you are encountering in the project. It is expected that this will be rough. The objectives of this exercise are to make sure that each project is feasible, for me to provide direction, and for the class to get a feel for the range of projects being proposed.

During the final presentation, each group should prepare a 25-minute presentation to the entire class. The presentation should include: 1) executive summary, 2) research objectives; 3) a description of the methodology and the analysis; 4) analysis of data findings; 5) a summary of the results; and 6) recommendations, 7) appendix and reference. The **final presentation PPT** is due at 10AM on the day of presentations (see Calendar). The project will be graded based on the quality of the oral presentation and the written materials as well as a peer evaluation of project group members.
