### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

# Regular Chapter Quizzes (15%)

Quizzes will be required for most of the 14 chapters. Each quiz will be equally weighted. Chapter 1 will be for practice and will not count in grading. The lowest score will be eliminated from the calculations.

# Individual “On Location: Video Case Analysis” Write-Ups (15%)

You will submit six write-ups. They are for the chapters being covered on 1/28 (Ch 10: 2), 2/11 (Starbucks), 2/25 (Ch 9: 4), 3/18 (questions following Silicon Valley’s…), 4/8 (5: 3) and 4/19 (Ch 14: 4). The first, third, fifth, and sixth are in the “On Location: Video Case Analysis” sections, which are the last ones in each chapter. Text follows each video that describes an extension of the chapter material to a practical aspect of work. The text is followed by four “Critical Thinking” questions. The numbers following the colon for the chapter assignments above indicate the specific “Critical Thinking” question to address for each write-up. For the second (Starbucks, due 2/11) and fourth (questions following Silicon Valley’s…, due 3/18), respond to the guideline questions that accompany each on the syllabus. You are asked to write 250-350 word responses to the questions and submit them in the relevant Assignment place in Blackboard.

The write-ups should be analytical and reflective rather than simply descriptive. Description tells what happened. Analysis assesses how and why it happened. Any description should serve the analysis. That is, it should consider the whys? and hows? rather than simply reporting the whats? Carefully think through the issues and present a logically reasoned argument to support your point of view. Try to incorporate material from relevant classwork, chapters, readings, and/or lectures. There is a sheet on the Assignments page with a set of rubrics that we will use to evaluate the write-ups.

All write-ups should be clearly written, well-organized, and submitted by the dates they are due. Late submissions will be penalized by 10 points per session.

**Individual Paper (15%)**

Each student will be responsible for writing a paper. Information on the nature of the paper will be provided early in the semester.

**Group Topic Paper and Presentation (12.5% + 12.5% = 25%)**

Each group will be responsible for writing a paper and making a class presentation. Information on both will be provided early in the semester.

I will judge the quality of the group presentations and the class will provide input. Relevant criteria for judging the presentations will include:

- (1) Scope - did the presentation cover the relevant material in sufficient breadth and depth?
- (2) Clarity - was the explanation of ideas and concepts clear?
- (3) Quality of analysis - how well did the group analyze the main issues?
- (4) Quality of application – how well did the group connect conceptual material to the topic of the presentation?
- (5) Contribution – to what extent did the group increase our understanding of the topic? and
- (6) Interest – how well did the group hold our interest?

**Final Exam (15%)**

We will discuss the nature of this component in detail toward the end of the semester.

# Contributions to the Class (15%)

Class participation is an integral aspect of this course. Please read and be prepared to discuss the assigned readings and cases listed on the syllabus for each session. Participation includes active contributions to your group, both during class activities and for the group paper and presentation. Quantity and quality will be considered in evaluating participation. If you are interested in participating but have difficulty initiating it, please let me know so that we can try to ease the process.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute is committed to the principles of intellectual honesty and integrity. All members of the RPI community are expected to maintain complete honesty in all academic work, presenting only that which is their own work in tests and assignments.

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and the Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a grade of zero on the first assignment where a violation is detected, and the infraction will be reported to the Associate Dean for Academic Affairs. The Lally School has a Three Strikes Policy. A second instance of cheating by the same student in any course will result in the student receiving an F for that course. A third instance of cheating will result in expulsion from the program.

Plagiarism is always a violation. For any assignment, I will tell you in advance what the expectations are for individual versus group work.

If you have any questions regarding what is acceptable related to any aspect of course work, please contact me before the situation becomes problematic.
