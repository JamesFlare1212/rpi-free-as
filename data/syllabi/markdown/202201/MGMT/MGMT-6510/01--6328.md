## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The primary objective of this course is to provide the first or second semester financial engineering student with basic quantitative and analytical tools necessary for sound financial decision making, particularly in quantitative finance and risk analytics contexts. The course will emphasize financial computation and simulation techniques, whereby students learn standard methodologies to obtain numerical solutions to financial problems. Students completing this course will be expected to have sufficient knowledge in fundamental computational areas such as calculus, differential equations, and stochastic processes to pursue more advanced graduate level work in financial engineering and to work with very basic financial modeling problems in the profession. This course will also introduce the basics of simulation including random variable generation, statistical analysis of simulation output, and variance reduction methods. Applications of simulation covered include the valuation of securities, estimation of the “Greeks,” assessment of risk management strategies, and using simulation to make investment decisions and measure investment performance. R will be the primary computing language used in this course.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

## COURSE GOALS/OBJECTIVES

1. Develop quantitative and computational skills applicable to financial engineering and risk analytics.
2. Able to successfully apply computational tools such as numerical analysis, Monte Carlo simulation, finite difference methods, lattices, linear, and optimization to the analysis of financial instruments and markets.
3. Able to apply basic computational skills to successfully value and replicate a variety of types of financial instruments under varying market conditions and settings for hedging and regulatory purposes.
4. Able to apply standard hedging and relative valuation and hedging techniques for portfolio analysis.
5. Apply basics of simulation – random variable generation, statistical analysis of simulation output, and variance reduction methods.
6. Ability to simulate to value securities, estimate the Greeks, and assess risk management strategies.
7. Ability to use simulation to make investment decisions and measure investment performance.
