## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The Student Managed Investment Fund course will actively invest real capital using financial market principles. The goal of the fund is to produce excess returns consistent with the performance of equity/fixed income portfolio through the use of fundamental, technical, and quantitative strategies driven by economic fundamentals. The goal of the course is to directly apply, in a real world environment, the analytical financial skills developed within other Lally courses and to provide students with both instructor and peer real-time feedback for their work.  
Students will be actively engaged in discussion of economic events, market developments, investment strategies, and portfolio risk in every class session. The course will begin with collaborative development and/or revision of an investment policy statement (IPS). Students will subsequently analyze out-of-sample effectiveness of established investment strategies using financial market data. Following fund startup, each week students will fulfill one of three specific fund roles and prepare a slide deck for presentation to the fund team: strategy generation, portfolio/risk management, and market developments. Strategy generation will develop new investment ideas and present them to the class for voting on portfolio inclusion. Risk management will provide updates on portfolio risk and monitor individual strategy performance. Market developments will provide a snapshot of relevant

news and trends in the marketplace. Fund managers will prepare investor relations communications (e.g. a one-page summary, semester report, and/or a presentation) to fund alumni.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The Student Managed Investment Fund course aims to provide students with practical, real money experience as part of an investment management operation. The goal of the fund is to produce superior risk adjusted returns to its benchmark, specified in its  
Syllabus for MGMT 4420/6240-01: Student Managed Investment Fund 3 of 12 Spring 2021  
Investment Policy Statement (IPS). The main objectives of the Student Managed Investment Fund course are:  
1\) Produce, Review, and Approve an Investment Policy Statement (IPS)  
o Students will produce an IPS that describes allowable holdings, asset allocation, risk/return expectations, and other constraints of the fund. The IPS must be approved by faculty before any modifications can be made to the existing portfolio. Preparation of an IPS provides an essential framework for investment activity through the course. A team of two or three students will lead the class in preparing this IPS.  
2\) Discuss Portfolio Implications of Ongoing Developments in Capital Markets  
o Every week (see the schedule below), one student will present a summary of recent financial market events with a presentation slide deck. Each student will add his or her own analysis and items of interested related to economics, markets, and quantitative methods. The Market Developments role will also summarize the week’s episode of Bloomberg ETF IQ to incorporate additional current trends in ETF markets. These discussions allow students to share ideas, learn more about the relationship between economics and security prices, and enhance communication skills. Communications will occur in-between class meetings via the WebEx teams.  
3\) Develop Investment Strategies  
o All students will initially analyze out-of-sample performance of investment strategies (e.g. asset allocation, individual securities, momentum, post earnings announcement drift, etc.) and present to peers for potential portfolio inclusion within one month of the start of the course. All investment pitches will provide recommendations for position size, risk controls, and portfolio appropriateness.  
4\) Portfolio Decisions  
o All pitches will be voted on by the class for portfolio inclusion. Pros and cons of each position will be discussed in class before anonymous online voting occurs. Voting students will have to provide a brief rationale for their decisions. Performance of all investment ideas (both invested and uninvested to examine opportunity costs) will be tracked by the Director of Research role. Investment strategy proposals must be scheduled on the class date prior to strategy presentation.  
5\) Manage Portfolio Composition and Risk  
o In every class meeting, members of the Director of Research role will monitor portfolio composition and market activity throughout the course and prepare specific metrics (e.g. attribution analysis, historical and estimated volatility, net systematic risk, etc.) for weekly discussion. Risk managers will also establish risk thresholds for which, when triggered, positions are automatically subject to mandatory reevaluation by the students. This provides students an opportunity to apply risk management principles on real capital portfolios.  
6\) Communicate Investment Strategies and Portfolio Decisions with Advisory Board  
o All members of the class will contribute to Investor Relations and will prepare periodic (approximately monthly) electronic communications for consideration by the fund’s advisory board. These reports will include information about portfolio holdings, risk, proposed strategies, performance analysis, and future outlook for markets. Depending on advisory board availability, the IR team will give a brief presentation to the board describing operations of the Student Managed Investment Fund. The advisory board provides both capital and guidance for the fund: students will act as  
Syllabus for MGMT 4420/6240-01: Student Managed Investment Fund 4 of 12 Spring 2021  
fiduciaries to the board and incorporate their feedback within subsequent portfolio decisions.  
7\) Prepare Portfolio for Inactive Period  
o Before the course ends, students will vote on strategies to prepare the fund’s holdings for a period of passive management (i.e. without active student involvement). Students will prepare all developed strategy information, risk models, and other documentation for transition to the next student management team. A team of two or three students will lead the class in non-active portfolio preparation.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

STUDENT LEARNING OUTCOMES  
The goal of the course is to give students practical investment management experience with actual capital and allow students to use skills developed in other courses in a practical setting. Undergraduate students will develop applied skills in security analysis, behavioral bias management, and effective business communication. Roles will rotate weekly (among market developments, strategy generation, and portfolio risk management in addition to investor relations responsibilities) and will also be allocated on both a volunteer basis and the needs of the fund for personnel in each role.  
Graduate students will expand on the above learning outcomes using more advanced quantitative methods. Rather than using simulation and historical data alone, students will further develop and refine critical skills for financial market analysis in real-time including quantitative financial modeling, dynamic asset/sector allocation strategies, and time series analysis.  
COURSE ASSESSMENT MEASURES  
Attendance, Voting, and Class Participation (30% of Course Grade)  
Attendance is mandatory. Students are expected to attend all classes and are required to notify the instructor should any absence occur. Students are expected to inform themselves about current events in and participate in class discussions about economics, financial markets, security selection, and portfolio composition. The course will make extensive use of WebEx Teams for management of the portfolio outside of class hours.  
Students will also vote on security selection and portfolio allocations periodically. Discussion before voting will occur in class, but all voting will be done electronically. Students will have an opportunity to provide anonymous feedback/rationale in electronic polling for each position vote. Voting and feedback will contribute to this class participation grade.  
Fund manager individual trade decisions and rationale will be recorded in a Trade Diary document in a Word/Excel document or on Google Drive before any vote can be recorded. Each student will provide a short justification (two or three sentences including any relevant data analysis) for every trade vote. Students will update this trade diary

before any vote and will receive feedback on the diary and course performance twice during the semester (see the course calendar for more information).  
Role Deliverables (45% total)  
Deliverables for students will vary based on weekly role selection (Strategy Generation, Portfolio Risk Management, and Market Developments as well as Investor Relations). An electronic signup system (Google Docs spreadsheet) will be available so that students can reserve a fixed number of open slots for roles based on the course calendar. Failure to prepare a scheduled role deliverable or find a substitute for said scheduled deliverable will result in a 10% deduction from the student’s course grade. Any questions about these deliverables should be directed to the instructor. Each of the deliverables is as follows:  
Strategy Generation (Bi-weekly)  
Without idea generation, the fund cannot invest capital. Students will present investment ideas of their own choosing (subject to the restrictions of the investment policy statement) for inclusion in the portfolio following a vote. It should also include any and all relevant quantitative models, liquidity/fee considerations, comparable analysis, and valuations. The purpose of the pitch is to convince students that the position is worthwhile for inclusion in the portfolio. The student will also deliver a strategy assessment to summarize performance of ideas generated by students during the class. For example, a strategy assessment may choose to highlight previous ideas that had not been invested but are performing well (or poorly) and list the best and worst performing ideas of the prior week. Strategy assessments are designed to provide a unique student perspective on previously generated research. There is no limit to the number of strategies that a student may present, but a student must present at least 2 strategies over the course of the semester.  
To build an initial list of investable strategies, each student will be required to deliver a 15 minute (maximum) presentation to the class by the end of February. Any inherited ETFs from prior management must be presented immediately. These first and future presentations will describe an investing strategy involving one on multiple ETFs (which could include asset allocation models, quantitative factors, diversified ETFs, etc.) that is subject to instructor approval before presentation. The strategy review should include all relevant information but must include the following:  
1\) Description of the ETF(s), Strategy, and Underlying Securities (Holdings, Fees, Liquidity, etc.)  
2\) Analysis of Long Term Strategy Performance (>1 year) including Asset Allocation, Returns, Volatility, Correlation among Asset Classes, etc.  
3\) Analysis of Short Term Strategy Performance (< 4 months)  
4\) Recommendation for Portfolio Inclusion including Order Type, Size, and Price  
5\) Risk Assessment Recommendation Including Stop-Loss or Position Alert based on Price Movement

Advisory Board Presentation (Once or Twice During Semester, Date/Time TBD)  
During the middle of the course (subject to change), students from the Investor Relations team will present information regarding the fund’s holdings and activities to an external advisory board. The IR team will also answer questions from the advisory board and record any comments for future fund consideration. Students will prepare at least one, and preferably two, separate PDF communications (a one-pager and a second, more comprehensive document – see End of Semester Portfolio Preparation below) for the advisory board describing the fund’s activity, holdings, risk, investment policy, and strategy outlook. These documents will be e-mailed to the advisory board for feedback.  
IPS Development (At Course Beginning)  
At the beginning of the course, student will develop and/or revise an Investment Policy Statement describing the target asset allocations, allowable holdings, description of the voting system for both buys and sells, and other general restrictions (e.g. a position cannot be added to the portfolio unless it has been pitched/researched by a student). The fund composition may not change until the IPS has been finalized. While all students in the class will contribute to discussion regarding the components of the IPS, students who assume the IPS Development Lead deliverable will be responsible for building, refining, and delivering the final IPS document for approval.  
Market Developments (Weekly)  
Students will prepare presentation slides and lead class discussion regarding global market events, economics, and any news that the presenting student believes is relevant to the fund and markets. This role will also summarize this week’s episode of Bloomberg ETF IQ. The discussion will last approximately 30 minutes. This gives students a chance to share their perspective on financial markets, learn from peers and instructors, and improve communication skills. Each student will perform this role at least once over the course of the semester.  
Director of Research: Portfolio Risk Assessment (Bi-weekly)  
Weekly risk assessment will include quantitative analysis of the portfolio holdings (and strategies pitched but not included in the portfolio) and include any applicable risk and performance metrics. If the portfolio does not include any holdings or contains a simple allocation (e.g. a single, diversified ETF), the risk assessment must include additional detailed analyses of the current state of market wide risk. Each student will perform this role at least once over the course of the semester. The Director of Research is also

responsible for aggregating student trade proposal votes and submitting trade instructions (for successful votes) to the faculty advisor.  
End of Semester Portfolio Preparation and Exit Report (At Course End)  
Since the portfolio will not be actively managed when the class ends, the holdings and risk composition of the fund may need to be adjusted for a dormant period until the incoming class assumes management. All students will contribute to End of Semester Portfolio Exit Report and the Director of Research will take a leadership role in adjusting the portfolio accordingly AND preparing any and all documentation, models, and other relevant materials for the incoming class. The fund is intended to exist in perpetuity and improving the transition to new management is essential for future success. The fund managers will prepare a one page tearsheet and extended exit report detailing the views, methodologies, performance, and operations of the fund.  
Peer Evaluations (15% total)  
At the middle and end of the semester, each student fund manager will rank his/her colleagues based on their performance and contribution to the fund and submit their rankings to the class instructor for compilation. The purpose of these evaluations is to measure accountability to fellow student fund managers and to discourage free-riding.  
Fund Performance (10% total)  
Overall fund performance relative to the benchmark will contribute one-tenth to each student’s grade. Any performance higher than or similar to the benchmark will receive full credit.

### Attendance Policy:

Class attendance is mandatory and will be recorded electronically.
