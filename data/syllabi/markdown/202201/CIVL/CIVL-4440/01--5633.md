## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Principles of displacement-based structural analysis; development of element and structure stiffness matrices; direct stiffness method for matrix structural analysis of trusses, beams, and frames; computer analysis of structures; introduction to the finite element method of analysis.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Matrix Structural Analysis by W. McGuire, R.H. Gallagher, and R.D. Ziemian. Second Edition, 2000.  
ISBN: 978-1-507-58513-9

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The objective of this course is to develop a working knowledge of matrix methods of structural analysis as applied to both determinate and indeterminate structures that exhibit linear behavior. Students will do this by building on the knowledge gained through statics, mechanics of materials and introductory structural engineering.  
Upon successful completion of the course, students will have an understanding of how the three fundamental principles of structural mechanics (equilibrium, compatibility, and constitutive relations) are utilized for solving structural analysis problems via matrix methods. They will also be able to use structural analysis software to perform such analyses with an understanding of how the software performs the analysis.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to use matrix methods to analyze statically determinate and indeterminate linear elastic trusses, beams, and frames to determine support forces, internal forces, and deformations.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to use computer software, with knowledge of its underlying matrix-based computations, to perform structural analysis.

Achievement of outcomes will be assessed based on performance on individual questions within each exam.

**Class Participation** **Policy:** Participation in class is likely to be strongly correlated with course grades. Class participation includes: Attending class, answering questions when asked, presenting homework solutions when asked and engaging in associated discussions about the homework.  
**Exam Policy:** Details on how exams will be administered will be provided before each exam. Permission will not be granted except for serious illness, a family emergency or an RPI-approved extracurricular activity. Such situations must be documented through the RPI Office for Student Success (4600 Academy Hall, success@rpi.edu).

**Textbook Reading** **Policy:** Reading the textbook is an important component of student learning in this course. Thus, reading assignments will be posted on the course website. The reading assignments may include material that is applicable to multiple classes (e.g., an entire chapter may be assigned but then be covered over multiple classes). Students are expected to pace themselves accordingly. Students are strongly encouraged to complete the reading assignments since doing so will likely correlate well with establishing a good understanding of the course material and thus performing well on the homework and exams. Furthermore, exam questions may be based on material from the textbook.

**Course Materials Policy:** Unless otherwise noted, all course materials provided to students in this course have been prepared by the instructor and are intended for use in this course only. These materials are NOT to be distributed in any manner to any other persons who are not enrolled in this course (e.g., do not upload to any websites). Violation of this policy may in some cases constitute a breach of academic integrity as defined by Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute.  
**Syllabus Revisions:** The instructor reserves the right to revise the syllabus during the semester if deemed necessary to best meet the needs of the students in the course.

**Homework Policy:** 1) Homework assignments will be posted on the course website. The primary purpose of the homework is to help students learn the course material and thus to be well prepared to participate in class, to present and discuss homework and to take exams.  
2\) To aid in the learning process, students should initially work on the homework problems on their own. Having done that, students should discuss their work with other classmates to help each other arrive at the correct solution or to check solutions to verify that the correct result was obtained. However, this must be done without becoming overly reliant on other students. In addition, students should consult with the instructor if they have questions about the homework.  
3\) Homework will not be collected and will not be graded. Each student has the responsibility to create a complete homework solution and to check their solution when the homework is discussed in class.  
4\) On or after the homework due date, one or more students will be selected to present their solutions to the class. All students should come to each class well prepared to present their solution (it is not required that a student's solution be completely correct but it should be well thought-out so that the student can clearly explain what they did). Presentation of a homework solution counts toward the class participation portion of the course grade.  
5\) It is strongly recommended that each student maintain an organized set of completed homework problems since they will be useful for understanding the material in the course and for preparing for and taking exams.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

In this class, **all homework solutions that are developed by a student must represent the student’s own work**, although, after attempting to solve problems on their own, students are encouraged to discuss their work with other classmates to help each other arrive at the correct solution or to check their solution. Development of a solution that is in violation of this policy (e.g., one student copying work done by another student or a student copying from a previously developed solution including a solution from a previous semester or a solution that is contained within a solution manual) will result in a course grade reduction by up to one full letter grade (e.g., from B to C). In addition, subsequent work done by the student will be scrutinized to evaluate potential cheating. Finally, the Dean of Students, who maintains records of academic dishonesty, will be notified. Additional disciplinary actions may be taken by the Dean of Students.

In this class, **all exams must represent the student’s own work**. If cheating occurs during an exam, the student will receive a grade of zero for that exam. In addition, and in accordance with the *Handbook*, the Dean of Students Office will be notified of the incident of academic dishonesty. Additional disciplinary actions may be taken by the Dean of Students. In addition, all **mobile/electronic devices (cell/smart phones, computers, etc.) must be stored securely away during an exam** and are not to be used unless specifically directed otherwise by the instructor. Any use of, or interaction with, a mobile/electronic device during an exam without explicit permission of the instructor will be interpreted as the illicit transfer of exam data and thus will be considered an act of cheating.
