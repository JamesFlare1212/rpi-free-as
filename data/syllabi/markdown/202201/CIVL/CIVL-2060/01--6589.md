## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Incorporating theory, analysis, design and experimentation, this course provides a foundational treatment of water resources engineering within the Civil and Environmental Engineering curriculum at Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute. Topics covered include water resources sustainability, water distribution systems and the hydrological cycle. It has a strong emphasis on hydraulic processes, building on concepts taught in Fluid Mechanics, including pipe flow, pipe networks, and open-channel flow. Hydraulic control structures such as weirs, bumps, contractions and spillway are considered in depth with experimental laboratory sessions. Unsteady hydrological routing problems are also introduced. Contemporary and social issues are introduced at various points throughout the term: equity issues related to infrastructure, green infrastructure, and climate change.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand the hydrological cycle and compute water budgets
- Develop an appreciation for water sustainability and impacts of climate change on future water resources
- Understand the major components and performance criteria of water distribution systems
- Appreciate non-engineering perspectives on water and water use (social, economic)
- Model physical systems (pipes, pipe networks, channels, streams, hydraulic structures) by employing principles of conservation of mass, energy, and momentum to develop equations describing their behavior.
- Solve these equations via concepts of specific energy to determine characteristics (flow, runoff) and compute a variety of flow and water surface elevation scenarios and observe these in laboratory experiments
- Develop skills in computer modelling of these systems using commercial software (Pipe-Flo).

### Other Course Policies:

***Other Course Policies***

Organization:

The course is structured into two-110-minute lectures per week. Each class will be divided into lecture sessions and in-class participation sessions by the students. Lectures will be conducted on an iPad and lecture notes will be posted on the LMS.

Labs:

There are 4 laboratory experiments. The experimental method is described as observing, modelling, predicting, and evaluating fluid behavior. Typically, theoretical model will predict results for the experiment. Theory and measured results can then be compared and evaluated. Students will write a succinct report on the experiment and discuss the experimental observations in the context of theory. The labs for S22 are:

1. Pumps
2. Sluice gate
3. Broad crested weir
4. Hydraulic jump

*Homework presentation*

**1)** It is encouraged that students use engineering “ruled” paper (8 ½ * 11 in.) with a clean left edge (i.e., no perforations). This paper is available at the bookstore. Each problem should begin at the top of a new page with the problem # and text problem # shown. Digital tablets using ruled template paper are also acceptable.

**2)** The upper margin of the first page of your homework should show your name, the homework assignment number, the date the homework is turned in, and the page number. The upper margin of the first page of each problem should show the problem number and page number. Other pages should include the page number. The page number should be given in the upper right corner in the format A/B where A is the current page number and B is the total number of pages in your homework.

**3)** Computations should be arranged in logical order and each step recorded in full (other engineers should be able to easily follow your work). The order of presentation of work should be as shown below. Any work that does not substantially follow this order of presentation will receive no credit.

\- List given data and diagrams

\- List required results

-------------------------------------------- (use a line to separate information given to you from your work)

\- Calculations and diagrams

\- Final answer (boxed in)

**4)** Do not make short-cuts. Perform one operation at a time and make a complete record of it. Only experienced engineers can safely take short-cuts. An exception may be made for repetitive or tabular computations, in which case it is enough to provide a complete record of a sample calculation.

**5)** Final results should be clearly indicated by *boxing* them in.

**6)** The correct units should be provided for intermediate results and final answers. A result is of no value if units are not provided. Note that careful consideration of units will often prevent mistakes.

*Clearness and neatness*

**1)** Figures and letters must be neat and large enough to be easily read.

**3)** Erasing must be clean and thorough. Smudgy, dirty work is not acceptable.
