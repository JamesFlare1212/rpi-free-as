## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course covers the basic methods of planning, organizing, and controlling the production of goods and services. The student will be exposed to the mathematical and graphical models which have been developed and applied to operations. The student will apply these models to problem situations supported by PC-based software and interpret the results in terms of actions or policies as they relate to the operations problem presented.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students will be able to:

- Define the key principles of planning, organizing, and controlling the production of goods and services
- Apply and interpret the mathematical models of operations management
- Design a production system or process to meet desired economic and volume goals
- Identify, formulate and solve engineering problems using tools of operations management
- Use techniques, skills and modern tools in designing, organizing, and managing operations

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Overview of methods used in the design and operation of production and service systems. Topics include forecasting, capacity planning, line balancing, production scheduling, staff scheduling, inventory control, just in time, time study, and project planning. Goal of course is to educate students in basic operations management principles and models.

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

**Missing Tests or Assignments:** Missing tests are only allowed if there is an emergency verified by the Dean of Student’s office. There are *no* makeups for tests. Missing assessments without official excuses will receive a zero.

**Excusal from Course Assignments and Examinations:** If you know ahead of time that you will miss an exam/assignment deadline, then you must make arrangements before the assessment is due. Failure to do so will result in 25% off for each 24-hour period the assessment is late. More information in the syllabus.

**Exam Protocol:**

All exams are open book and notes, and use of calculator is allowed. All answers must be recorded on the exam answer sheet and full credit is awarded for correct numerical answers as well as direct answer to the problem presented in the question. Little to no credit is awarded for effort that yields incorrect numerical values. The professional world measures performance on correct results not effort working on the problem or data.

**Exam Regrading Request:**

Exams submitted for regrading must not be altered from the original work submitted for grading. Altering work for regrading is academic dishonesty and will be treated as such. All requests must be made in writing by attaching a well phrased note to the original work explaining in full the reason for the request and the points you think you deserve for your error. Requests for a regrade must be submitted no later than next class meeting after the material is handed back to the class for the request to be considered.

**Special Testing Accommodations:**

All students with testing accommodations will be by the instructor and teaching assistant for all tests. Make sure to email your accommodation letter in the beginning of the semester in order to be added to the list.

**End-of-semester grades are not given**. Instead, end-of-semester grades are calculated according to a student’s performance on the individual assessment requirements and the weights in this syllabus. **Final grades are, final.** Therefore, final grades will not be changed unless a numerical error is made in computing your final grade. After final grades have been calculated, I will not respond to emails received requesting a grade change unless it is in response to a numerical calculation error. Please do not ask for extra credit or extra work after final exams have been administered.
