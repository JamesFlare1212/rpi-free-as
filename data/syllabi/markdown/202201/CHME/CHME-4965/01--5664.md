## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course covers structure, processing, and property relationships for industrially important plastic materials. Fundamentals of fluid flow, rheology, heat transfer, and transport phenomena are applied to analyze polymer processing operations including extrusion, multi-phase mixing, calendering, blow molding, thermoforming, fiber spinning, compression molding, injection molding, and recycling.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1\) Students can apply mathematical methods to solve problems related to the theory of polymer processing operations.

2\) Given polymer products like pipes, bottles, automobile dashboards, etc., students will be able to identify techniques, skills, and modern engineering tools necessary to manufacture them.

3\) Students will demonstrate a rational approach to solving engineering problems related to polymer processing operations.

4\) Students will be able to understand contemporary technical, economic, social, and political aspects of the global plastics recycling industry.

5\) Students will appreciate the dynamic nature of the plastics industry by understanding emerging trends in materials (e.g. bioplastics) and methods (e.g. 3D printing), and thereby recognize the need for life-long learning.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Introduction to polymers & processing, glassy and semicrystalline polymers; single-screw extrusion, twin screw extrusion, thermoforming, polymer melt rheology, mixing operations, molding processes, secondary shaping processes, plastics recycling. If time permits, special topics such as polymers in microelectronics applications may be covered.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Quizzes: 25 % Group project: 25 % Exams: two, 25 % each

Approximately 7 to 10 homework problem sets will be assigned during the semester. Homeworks should be finished on the assigned date, but will not be collected or graded. Quizzes of up to 20 minutes duration will follow selected homeworks, after we have reviewed the solutions in class. Exact repeats of homework exercises may appear on quizzes. No make-up quizzes will be given.

One midterm exam will be given in class time, and a final exam will be scheduled at the end of the semester. It is the student's responsibility to attend or arrange for a make-up with the instructor prior to the exam date in case of illness or emergency. Exams typically contain a mixture of essays, multiple choice questions, short answer questions, and numerical problems.  
The team-based project will requires design of a plastic injection molding process using specialized finite element simulation software. Students will use Autodesk Moldflow Plastics Insight to simulate a plastics injection molding process and optimize processing conditions for a given set of material properties. Teams of three students will submit their final projects and a brief term paper describing their findings.

- Students scoring below 50% on both exams will receive a course grade of D or F, regardless of course average. Otherwise, the following grade scale will be used based on course average:
