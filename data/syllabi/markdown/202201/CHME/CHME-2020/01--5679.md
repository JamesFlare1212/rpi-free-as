## Course Goals

### Goals:

Thermodynamics is one of the threads that tie together the chemical-engineering sciences. This course (CHME-2020), in conjunction with CHME-2010, provides students with thermodynamic expertise for analyzing chemical-engineering processes. In particular, on successful completion of this course the student should be conversant with methods for representing properties of real fluids and their mixtures, and with techniques for formulating and solving problems in multiphase and multi-reaction equilibria. A summary of the major topics is provided below.

Heat effects: sensible, latent, chemical, and "physical"

The PVTx equation of state

Corresponding-states concepts

Energies and entropies of real fluids

Solution thermodynamics

Fugacity concepts

Phase equilibria, especially VLE

Chemical-reaction equilibria

Applications of the above concepts

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

We live in an age of ethical ambiguity; expectations are often wildly discordant with effort, and the entrepreneurial imperative appears to some to offer a generalized shelter for the morally impaired. This should concern us all. We therefore append the following statements regarding academic integrity:

1. Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments which students turn in are their own. Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The **Rensselaer Handbook** defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Students should familiarize themselves with this portion of the Rensselaer Handbook and should note that the penalties for plagiarism and other forms of cheating can be quite harsh.

2. A local definition of "cheating" seems necessary. For this course, let us use the following: collaborative work of any kind on an examination, or excessive inter-group cooperation on the homeworks, constitutes cheating. "Collaboration" and "cooperation" include sharing your work with others, as well as receiving and using the work of others.

3. No communication between students or use of written, typed and/or electronic information in any form is allowed during the exams unless by explicit written consent from the instructor. Sending messages/emails/texts/etc. via mobile devices as well as accessing information online or stored on any electronic device (other than information explicitly allowed by the instructor) is not allowed during exams, and will result in a grade of zero on the exam for the first offense and failure of the class on the second offense. The only allowed use of mobile/cell/smart phones is to receive incoming text messages for purposes of campus safety. Finally, please ask the instructor for clarification if you have any questions concerning this policy before taking an exam.
