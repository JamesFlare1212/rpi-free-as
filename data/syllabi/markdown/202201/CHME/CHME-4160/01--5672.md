## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The purpose of this course is to provide senior chemical engineering students with hands on experience operating and analyzing common chemical engineering unit operations. The unit operations covered in this laboratory course build on the theoretical material covered in previous and concurrent chemical engineering courses. Emphasis is placed on planning and executing the experiments, analyzing data and dissemination of results. Courses at RPI are generally expected to require 2-3 hours of outside work for each credit hour. You should expect, therefore, to commit at least 6 hours/week outside the classroom for this course.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Each of the four experiments will contribute equally to the final grade, with each experiment being graded according to the following policy.

Final Reports (4): 100 % of course grade

Foreman Reports (4): individual effort adjustment (if needed) on each report grade

If students contribute equally to a given laboratory report, the Foreman report will not affect the grade on that report. If contributions were unequal, the instructor reserves the right to assign higher or lower letter grades to individual team members based on their contributions.

Laboratory reports are assigned letter grades (A, A-, B+, etc.) by each instructor based on technical correctness, quality of the report preparation, and effort level. Course grades are determined from the average grade among the four reports using a numerical weighting scheme.

## Course Calendar

### Calendar Details:

There will be at least six experiments available during the course of the semester. Each group will conduct four of the available experiments. Each experiment will take place in a three (3) week “cycle”. Experiment schedules will be posted on LMS.

Week of Experiment: Preliminary discussion with TA and Experiment

Following week: Interim Discussion (and second attempt of experiment if needed) Third week: Final report due by 5 PM on your laboratory day

Each of the experiments has a supervising instructor responsible for the grading of the final report.

Following the experiment, an interim discussion with the supervisory instructor or the TA will take place during the second week. It is the responsibility of each group to schedule this meeting with the appropriate instructor or the TA. For the interim discussion, the group is expected to have organized all of the collected data and started their analysis. Each group member is to be familiar with the data and analysis. Interim discussions are the time to ask any questions about the data or analysis and the instructor’s expectations for the final report.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance**  
The regular lab hours are listed as above, but some experiments may run over, depending on the students’ level of preparation and any problems encountered with the procedure. All students within a group are required to stay in the lab until the experiment and cleanup are completed. Students who need to leave early should obtain prior written approval from the instructor, or a grade deduction may result.  
**Laboratory Teams**

The unit operations laboratory experiments in this course require teamwork for successful completion. Laboratory teams will consist of four people. Students are free to choose their own groups during the first week of class. Students who are unable to form a group will be assigned to a group by the instructor.

### Other Course Policies:

Laboratory manuals (electronically posted on LMS) and basic personal protective equipment (safety gear) are required for this course. It is important that you bring your safety gear to each lab session. You are not allowed in the lab without safety glasses and a lab coat. In addition, we are required to wear nitrile gloves when working with chemicals.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Post-Experimental Procedures**

Running a laboratory safely and efficiently requires organization and adherence to safety guidelines. The TAs will make sure that you have all material, supplies and equipment ready and available to you before you begin the experiments. Your job will be to ensure that the materials, supplies and equipment are returned back to the location in which you found them before beginning your experiment. All chemicals, waste materials, and used PPE (e.g., gloves) must be discarded in the appropriate waste containers. Neglect and/or lack of attention to post-experimental cleanup and reorganization may result in a reduction to your course grade.

**Academic Integrity: Laboratory Reports**  
Evidence of collaboration, plagiarism, or falsification of laboratory data will be dealt with according to the Student Handbook.
