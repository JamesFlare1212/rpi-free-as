## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course aims to teach students how to utilize rheological characterizations to probe the state of materials for product design and functions. Emphasis is placed on the concept and characterization of viscoelasticity and how to connect the measured viscoelasticity to product properties. Courses at RPI are generally expected to require 2-3 hours of outside work for each credit hour. *You should expect, therefore, to commit at least 2 - 3 hours/week outside the classroom for this course.*

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand basic concepts of viscoelastic properties.
- Understand working principles of rheometer.
- Conduct hands-on measurements of viscoelastic properties of selected materials using Anton Paar MCR 302 rheometer.
- Present compelling and convincing documentation.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

**MATERIAL SELECTION / DISCUSSION / REPORT**

In this laboratory course, three materials selected will be investigated, and each material will be reviewed, characterized, and documented in four weeks through a highly interactive process. Students are required to actively participate in the discussion.

A lab group consists of three key roles: leader, experimenter(s), and plot designer. i) Leader will investigate background information, lead discussions, oversee overall hands-on characterization, and write the final report. ii) Experimenter(s) will conduct hands-on measurements and actively communicate with other members for any adjustments in measurements, iii) Plot designer will prepare compelling and visually attractive plots for the report. Plot designer actively communicates with other members for data collection and experiment plan adjustments. Plot designer is also responsible for the figure draft meeting. These roles rotate, and students will receive grades based on their roles for each laboratory session.

- Week 1: Lead experimenter investigates relevant information on the material of interest and provides a brief presentation. The information includes but, not limited to chemical composition, microscopic and macroscopic structures, application conditions (air, aqueous, temperature, etc.), key viscoelastic properties for functions of interest, and characterization strategy. Hypothesize and predict structure-property relationships. The instructor will ask probing questions to stimulate the discussion. After the pre-lab discussion, experimenter(s) begin hands-on measurements with other members.
- Week 2: Plot designer prepares figure drafts, present, and leads the discussion. Discuss the current data and suggest any further measurements. Experimenters continue hands-on measurements based on the discussion.
- Week 3: Experimenters continue hands-on measurements if necessary. Lead experimenter writes the report.
- Week 4: Report due and material selection for the next session.

Each rheology laboratory session will be concluded with a final lab report. A lab report should contain the following sections

1. **Title Page** – Title, date, student names, and roles. Include abstract in the title page (less than a half-page).
2. **Introduction** – Discuss the purpose of measurements and explain why conducted measurements were chosen. Some considerations for the introduction: What are the key viscoelastic properties of your material for target functions? To interrogate the key viscoelastic properties, what measurements were conducted? What characterization results do you expect?
3. **Result and Discussion** – Provide a concise and technical narrative with well-presented plots summarizing the measured viscoelastic properties. Note the key characteristics of the measurements and explain the results.
4. **Conclusion** – Summarize the key finding and future developments (less than a half-page).

Use the Times New Roman font, 12 points and single line space. Page margin is 1 inch on each side.
