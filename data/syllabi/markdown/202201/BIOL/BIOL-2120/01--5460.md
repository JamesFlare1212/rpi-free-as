## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is an introductory course in cell structure and function. It is intended for Biology and Biomedical Engineering majors, and covers such topics as chemical composition of cells, molecular structure function of organelles, membrane transport, and the basic biochemistry underlying cellular behavior including cancer cells.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand and properly use scientific vocabulary common for cell and molecular biology.
- Demonstrate understanding of prokaryotic and eukaryotic cell structure and function.
- Describe the structure and function of the biomolecules found in all living organisms.
- Describe the synthesis of DNA, RNA, and proteins.
- Explain the processes involved with gene expression.
- Explain the processes by which animals acquire nutrients and eliminate wastes.
- Explain the structure and function of membranes and intracellular compartments.
- Link modern advances in cell and molecular biology to a better understanding of diseases.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

4 tests, 1 cumulative final exam, and homework are used to assess students. Reading the textbook, coming to lecture and asking questions, and doing the homework gives students the best learning experience.

**DESCRIPTION OF ASSESSMENTS**

Cheating or academic dishonesty of any kind will result in 0 points awarded for your overall lab grade.

- **Exams**
  - ­ There will be five exams: four **Unit Exams** and a comprehensive **Final Exam**. All exams are equally weighted.
  - ­ The exam with the **lowest score will be dropped** at the end of the semester. Thus, the Final Exam can be treated as optional – if you are satisfied with your course grade and choose to skip the Final Exam, a score of 0 will appear for this exam on LMS, but it will not affect your course grade since it will be dropped as your lowest exam grade.
  - ­ Exams will consist of a combination of multiple choice, short-answer, and long-answer written questions. Exams will be held on LMS and will be accessible on the dates listed on the *Lecture Schedule*.
  - ­ Exams are timed; unless stated otherwise, you will have **70 mins for Unit Exams** and **100 mins for the Final Exam**. You must have a stable internet connection to receive your full time. If you have accommodations for extra testing time, you must inform Dr. Klein if you intend to use this extra time at least **two weeks** **before** the exam is held. You must have The Office of Disability Services for Students (DSS) email Dr. Klein to establish your accommodations in this class (see *Accommodations for Students with Disabilities*).
  - ­ A make-up exam may be available if documentation for your absence on the exam date is submitted and the make-up is approved by Dr. Klein. The format of a make-up exam **may differ significantly** from the standard exams. No makeup exam can be given for the Final Exam.

- **Homework Assignments**
  - ­ Pre-class and Post-class homework assignments for each lecture will be administered via **SmartWork**. A link to these assignments can be found on LMS.
  - ­ Pre-class assignments should be completed before the corresponding lecture, but there is no penalty if these assignments are completed after the lecture so long as they are submitted before the due date. You are responsible for submitting assignments in a timely manner; **late assignments will NOT be accepted**. Due dates are shown on the Smartwork website.
  - ­ Assignments are weighted by the number of points available in each assignment; some assignments will have more questions or multi-part questions that are worth more points. E.g., an assignment worth 16 points is weighted exactly double an assignment worth 8 points. All assignments will be averaged together based on this weighting and reported to LMS as a rolling average.

- **Extra Credit**
  - ­ Post-class discussions will be held on LMS under the heading "Extra Credit Discussions." Meaningful contribution to **ALL** of these discussions will be rewarded with extra credit at the end the semester. These discussions are unrelated to this class's discussion board for course content.
  - ­ Additional class-wide extra credit opportunities may be available throughout the semester; these will be announced through LMS announcements. Extra credit opportunities will **NOT** be given to students on a case-by-case manner.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attending class and review sessions is highly recommended; while failure to attend class will not result in any direct penalties, having direct interactions with Dr. Klein and your TA's will certainly enhance your grasp of the subjects covered in class. If a class cannot be held in-person (e.g., due to snow storms, COVID-19 quarantine), classes will be held on WebEx and recordings of each WebEx class session will be made available on LMS as soon as possible. To reiterate, you should come to class, it will benefit you in the long run, but you are not required to do so and will not be penalized for absences.

### Other Course Policies:

**Grading**: Written questions must be intelligible and answered in English. No points will be awarded for answers that fail to adhere to this rule. If you feel that the grade you received on an assessment is not justified, contact your TA for clarification on your grade. If the issue is not resolved by your conversation with the TA, you can appeal your grade to Dr. Klein, but you must **submit justification** via email (with your TA CC'ed) explaining the reason(s) why you believe your grade should be changed.  
**Online Assessments**: Exams will be held on LMS and homework (i.e., pre-class and post lecture assignments) will be administered through W. W. Norton's Smartwork website. Cheating is considered to include but is not limited to **collaborating with other students on exam questions, passing information about exam questions or exam answers to or from other students, making transcripts or copies of exams, submitting answers to free-response questions in words other than your own (i.e., plagiarism), defeating timed exam lockouts on LMS to gain extra time, and copying homework assignment answers from other students**. If a student is found to be cheating, Dr. Klein reserves the right to issue a score of 0 for the assessment and an **F for your course grade**. These policies also apply to students who assist others in cheating. Exams held online are considered “open-book” assessments, but this does not mean you can copy answers from your textbook; all answers must be **in your own words**.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work.
