### Teaching Assistant Information:

This is an introductory course in biological systems focusing on major topics in evolution, human genetics & medicine, and ecology. It is an undergraduate level course. It is intended for non-majors and majors and covers a variety of topics that impact our daily lives and the grand challenges of this decade and your generation. Activities, such as student presentations and written responses to in lab questions enable students to interact, and discuss these topics. The goal is to provide an understanding of the scientific method, and it’s fundamental principles, to develop analytical thinking skills in the context of modern biology, and to develop data dexterity for understanding and solving grand challenges in the life sciences. ## Course Goals

### Goals:

## Course Goals / Objectives

1. Principle Concepts: Students will be able to grasp and master introductory biological principles underscoring the Scientific Method. Students at a technological university will gain basic biological literacy in laboratory techniques.
2. Predict and Propose: Students will be able to connect and link core biological concepts, and apply them to the bases of protocols used in the laboratory course. Students will gain experience in formulating and testing hypotheses, using appropriate *in silico* and computational materials and methods, and analyzing data sets quantitatively and/or qualitatively to arrive at conclusions, and predict and propose possible solutions.
3. Pre-requisites: Students will be able to satisfy prerequisites necessary in order to take advanced courses in the biological sciences. Students will be prepared to take the second introductory biology course, BIOL 2120 and Biostatistics, BIOL 4200. BIOL 1010 serves as the first course for life science majors and for students interested in the health professions. We have separate sections for majors and non-majors in this introductory BIOL 1015 laboratory course.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The laboratory course content is divided into five modules:

1. The Scientific Method - Testing Hypotheses, and Data Analysis.
2. Cell Biology - Mitosis and the Cell Cycle.
3. Molecular Biology - DNA Replication, and Protein Synthesis.
4. Genetics - Mendelian and Hardy Weinberg Models.
5. Ecology and Evolution - Ecosystems, Biodiversity, Conservation, Cladistics, and Phylogeny using Computational/Bioinformatics Data.
