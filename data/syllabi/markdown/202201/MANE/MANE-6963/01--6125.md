### Prerequisites or Other Requirements:

There are no specific prerequisites but you are expected to be familiar with and able to independently refresh your memory about the following topics: nuclear fission and neutron multiplication, the life cycle model for neutron chain reactions (i.e., the six factor formula), basic diffusion theory, and introductory neutron transport theory. For students who were undergraduates at Rensselaer this is satisfied by having taken Physics of Nuclear Reactors, though Fundamentals of Nuclear Engineering may be sufficient.

### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

Course Summary  
This senior level undergraduate and introductory level graduate nuclear engineering course presents the theory and practice of nuclear criticality safety in three parts:  
I. The science and art of criticality safety.  
II. The culture and practice of criticality safety.  
III. Performing criticality safety evaluations.  
Part I will use a traditional lecture and studio practice approach. Part II will use a roundtable discussion and case study approach. Part III will combine studio and field work.  
Because of the pandemic protocols – including having the first two classes online – these parts will be somewhat intermixed. It is also highly unlikely that we will get permission to visit a fissionable material site (the Rensselaer Critical Facility), so Part III will be from simulations and remote observations instead of field work.  
Course Objectives  
The overall objectives of this course are to prepare you to:  
∙ evaluate and report on the criticality safety of nuclear systems with fissionable materials;  
∙ work in the culture and regulatory environment of criticality safety; and  
∙ interact with criticality safety specialists.  
In particular, this course should prepare you to meeth the following topical objectives.  
Knowledge and comprehension objectives:  
∙ define criticality safety;  
∙ identify and interpret criticality safety information, standards, and regulations;  
∙ depict the nuclear fuel cycle and describe its primary operations;  
∙ depict, compare, and contrast real and hypothetical criticality accident scenarios;  
∙ identify and compare appropriate subcritical limits for a given system;  
∙ depict and describe the process of analog Monte Carlo neutron transport.  
Application and analysis objectives:  
∙ apply hand calculation methods to analyze neutron multiplication in fissionable systems and estimate margin of criticality safety;  
∙ apply inverse multiplication analysis to plan an approach to critical experiment;  
∙ apply Monte Carlo computer calculations to analyze neutron multiplication in fissionable systems and estimate margin of criticality safety.  
Synthesis and evaluation objectives:  
∙ perform (simulated) safe approach to critical experiments;  
∙ perform a criticality safety inspection;  
∙ prepare and present a formal criticality safety inspection report;  
∙ perform a criticality safety evaluation;  
∙ prepare and present a formal criticality safety evaluation report.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Course Policy on Academic Integrity  
Rensselaer’s policy on academic dishonesty is explained in The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities. You are expected to review and understand that information. In addition, the following policies apply specifically to this course.  
∙ Committing, or attempting to commit an act of academic dishonesty, or assisting in the commission or attempt of such an act, is a flagrant offense to the process and culture of education; expect my reaction to it to be harsh.  
∙ You are expected to refrain from any academically dishonest act, and to be vigilant in protecting your work from others who might attempt to commit an act of academic dishonesty.  
In this course, academic dishonesty can take the following forms.  
∙ Taking a quiz or test with any assistance from another person, regardless of who they are, unless the instructor specifically states that it is a group activity (e.g., a group oral exam).  
∙ Using any materials (such as a crib sheet or a calculator) that you did not bring for you and you alone to use on a quiz or exam, unless given explicit permission by the exam proctor (e.g., typically for extraordinary circumstances such as the failure of your calculator during a test, etc.).  
∙ Using any materials (such as a text, extra crib sheets, a laptop, a phone, etc.) that are not explicitly permitted during an exam.  
∙ Submission of a discussion forum post that, except for short phrases or properly referenced material (e.g., quotations, figures, etc.), consists of any content not created by and original to you.  
∙ Submission of a report that, except for short phrases or properly referenced material, consists of any content not created by and original to you.  
∙ Indicating someone as “present” (e.g., on an attendance sheet) who is not present in class at the time the indication is made.  
The penalty for violating these policies is at the discretion of any or all members of the instructional team. In general, the penalty for any violation is  
∙ failure of the course and notification to various Department Heads and/or Deans.
