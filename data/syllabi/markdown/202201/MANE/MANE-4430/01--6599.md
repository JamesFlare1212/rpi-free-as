## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course will provide and introduction to measurements of experimental systems utilizing neutron sources. It will focus on measurements relating to neutron interactions with matter including the detection of neutrons through interactions in materials, neutron reaction crosssections, neutron diffusion and slowing down, Doppler broadening of cross-sections and how this applies to nuclear reactors, both neutron and photon activation analysis and its applications, and the fission process and measurement of delayed neutrons.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Provided lab manuals

## Course Goals

### Goals:

To introduce the students to experimental measurements with neutrons and apply topics learned in previous classes to practical measurements

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The course will focus on experimental measurements utilizing various neutron sources. The topics will cover neutron interactions with matter and various methods of neutron detection, neutrons slowing down and neutron diffusion, measurement of neutron cross-sections as well as the temperature dependence on cross-section and Doppler broadening, neutron and photon activation analysis and it’s applications, and the fission process and the measurement of delayed neutrons.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Be able to calculate uncertainty for experimental measurements
- Be able to perform experiments with a wide variety of detection systems
- Demonstrate understanding of how neutron detectors operate
- Demonstrate understanding of neutrons slowing down in media and diffusion theory
- Demonstrate understanding of how cross-sections are calculated and measured
- Be able to calculate Doppler broadening of a cross-section based on temperature
- Demonstrate understanding of neutron and photon activation and it’s applications
- Demonstrate understanding of the fission process
- Be able to calculate intensity of fission products through measurement of delayed gammas
