## Course Goals

### Goals:

#### Course objective:

The objective of this course is to introduce the concepts involving nonlinearities in the analysis of solids and structures using the finite element method in a practice-oriented platform. In addition to covering the theory behind the sources of nonlinearities in response to loads and constraints in engineering structures, the course will dwell in simulating such behavior in various materials and applications. Students will be exposed to utilizing commercial finite elements software for a well-rounded experience.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

- Review of linear finite element concepts
- Nonlinear continuum mechanics of solids
  - o finite strain and stress measures,
  - o total and updated Lagrangian approaches,
  - o variational methods – virtual work principle.
- Non-linear finite element procedures
  - o incremental solution of nonlinear equations,
  - o Newton-Raphson method.
- Finite elements for elasto-plasticity
- Finite elements for hyper-elasticity
- Contact of solids using finite elements
- Finite elements in dynamics (time permitting)

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

#### In-class exercises

In-class exercises (ICE) will be assigned during (or at the end) of each lecture to reienforce the understanding of the covered topic. Students can interact with the instructional team to complete the ICEs during the class duration. Remote students (in case) are expected to attend live webex sessions and are expected to submit the in-class exercises before the due time. Three lowest scores of ICEs will be dropped.

#### HOMEWORK

Homework may be assigned differently for the 4000 level and 6000 level students and may include application problems using FE software. Please make an effort to write clearly without omitting steps in calculations. The lowest score of one homework will be dropped. Only neatly written assignments will be graded. In this course, collaboration is permitted and encouraged on the weekly assigned problems to a degree. It is expected that each student’s work will be his or her own, in both form and content. This means that even if students collaborated in working on problems in the homework assignment, the final work that is handed in must not be a clone of another student’s work. It is good practice to list the people that you collaborated with. Late submissions will not be entertained without valid excuse.

#### Tests

Two equally weighted tests will be administered during this course. Dates for the tests shall be found in the course schedule (at the end of this document).

Grading challenges for tests will be entertained within a week after they are returned. A missed test will result in a grade of zero unless permission is granted beforehand. Permission will not be granted except for serious illness or a family emergency. Such situations must be documented through the Student Experience Office (Academy Hall, x 8022, se@rpi.edu) and approved by the instructor. In such cases, the test will be rescheduled for an alternative date and time. All requests with pre-approved exceptions for scheduling conflicts, additional exam time and/or minimum distraction environment must be submitted to the instructor at least 1 week before the first test, preferably sooner for organized arrangements.
