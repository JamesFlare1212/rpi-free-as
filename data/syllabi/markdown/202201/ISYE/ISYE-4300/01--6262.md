## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Students will become more familiar with and better at developing and implementing ISE tools, particularly optimization, simulation, data-driven, and computational tools.
- • Students will be able to identify which modeling tools are appropriate for which problems.
- • Students will be able to identify what the limitations are and what potential unintended consequences exist in using a given ISE modeling tool in a given problem context.
- • Students will be able to model complex sociotechnical systems, including the interactions and trade-offs associated with different stakeholders, objectives, and constraints.
- • Students will gain new understanding of the complexities of critical sociotechnical challenges and how ISE tools can be developed and used to guide better understanding, better communication, and better design of sociotechnical systems and processes.

**Class Participation and Engagement:** The scholarship on teaching and learning shows that class participation increases student learning. Therefore, class attendance is required. In-class participation and engagement will be assessed as part of your final course grade. Your grade for this assessment will be determined via a guided self-assessment (that means by you) to be completed throughout the semester. Three separate formal assignments are required, one at the beginning of the semester will include a self-assessment and you setting personal goals. Then you will reflect on and assess these via a midterm reflection. At the end of the semester, you’ll make a final assessment and recommended grade. This information will be collected and reflected on by the instructor and used to determine your Class Participation and Engagement grade.

**Activity-Based Learning Opportunities:** These assessment opportunities are designed for learning and exploring new ideas and skills (which often comes with first failing and then through feedback finally grasping the new knowledge or skill). Therefore, these assessments will be graded on a binary basis: full credit will be given if you completed the assignment and turned it in on time. No credit, you did not do it. Most of these will be in-class activities or guided discussions, where students must turn in worksheets or other guided activity documents. A few of these opportunities will require out-of-class inquiry work. The assignment weights will vary depending on the effort.

**Knowledge and Skill Assessments.** These are opportunities for students to demonstrate their mastery of new knowledge and skills explored throughout the course. In contrast to the activity-based learning opportunities, these will be graded individual assessments based on mastery. There will be multiple such assessments throughout the course, and assignment weights will vary depending on the effort required of the students. They will also take multiple forms, including (but not limited to) quizzes based on reading assignments, case studies, in-class and online targeted quizzes geared to a specific concept, software implementations, and homework sets that reinforce or expand on material covered in the lecture. Students must illustrate their own understanding of the material; copying solutions from other students, other sources, previous semesters, or book solutions verbatim is not allowed and is considered a form of academic dishonesty.

**Course Project.** The course project will be team-based with multiple deliverables. Self-selected teams of two to four students will complete the course project. The course project requires teams to identify an important sociotechnical system problem, and then apply the modeling process to achieve either better understanding or a better design to a sociotechnical problem of their selecting. This project has multiple deliverables, which build upon each other and provide an opportunity for feedback, reflection, and improvement. Further information, grading rubrics, and guidance will be provided during the semester and via LMS.

**Systematic Course Quiz.** There will be one systematic course topic quiz that is an individual effort. The purpose is for students to demonstrate their mastery of the course’s main takeaways. More information about the format will be discussed in class.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
