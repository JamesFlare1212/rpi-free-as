## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course covers foundational models for information security, policies, authentication, access control, database security, assurance, auditing, and intrusion detection. It also devotes substantial time to secure coding practices. Students will develop a solid foundation upon which to build an information security career in the subfield of their choosing. Cryptography is not covered since it is a focus of other courses.Prerequisites: CSCI 2500 or ECSE 2660, and CSCI 1200.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will demonstrate knowledge of the theoretical foundations of information security.
- Students will demonstrate knowledge of a broad range of issues that must be addressed to design and build secure systems.
- Students will demonstrate knowledge of common programming problems that are often exploited by viruses, worms, and other malware.
- Students will be able to investigate and present topics in information system security at a level so that other students obtain an understanding of the key issues.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Studio Exercises (weekly homework assignments) -- there will be up to 10, for a total of 15%

Coding Project – individual project in which students will work to implement a trusting trust attack (aka the Ken Thompson Hack) (due end of term) (25%)

Presentation -- there will be a presentation in which you (individually or in a small group of no more than 3 people) explore a security topic not covered in class (20%)

Security/Privacy Assessments -- One written assessment of one new technology and its security and privacy considerations (25%)

Attendance/Participation -- Each class begins with an approximately 20 minute discussion of the day’s security news. Students are expected to come to class having read some interesting, timely security news, prepared to discuss it and put it into broader conversation with other students’ news items and news items previously discussed (15%)

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Cheating and plagiarism guidelines:  
The studio exercises must be completed by each student independently unless otherwise specified. You may discuss your approach to deriving the answers with your fellow classmates, but you must write and defend your own answers in your own words. Copied answers will be grounds for disciplinary actions. See the academic integrity statement in the course syllabus.  
The coding project must be completed by each student independently. Students may not peruse GitHub or other code hosting sites for solutions to the coding project.  
Students must write their assessment papers individually and in their own words. Plagiarism occurs when students fail to properly cite their research sources in the paper.  
Students must produce their presentations solely in their groups without consulting other students. Plagiarism occurs when a presentation group fails to properly cite their research sources in the presentation.  
Cheating and plagiarism policy:  
First offense: Failure for the course

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

Some of the techniques learned in this course can be used for malicious purposes. These techniques are taught because they are necessary for a complete understanding of information security. Using such techniques for malicious purposes may be a crime, depending on context. Using such techniques against any RPI machines, including but not limited to other students’ machines, the shared course server, or any RPI computers, are grounds for immediate failure of the course.
