## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will introduce students to techniques used in modern observational astronomy research. We will practice these techniques on existing datatsets, and obtain our own data with the Hirsch Observatory 16” telescope. Students will carry out their own small research project and produce a report in an astronomy journal format. After taking this class, students will be prepared to carry out undergraduate or graduate research projects in observational astronomy. In particular, they will be able to reduce simple optical CCD images and spectra, and extract data from common on-line databases. They will have a working knowledge of LaTex, AstroPy and Stellarium. This class is appropriate for graduate students or upper level undergraduates.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Astronomical coordinate systems, Optical telescopes and detectors, Data reduction techniques, Photometric calibration of images, Wavelength calibration of spectra, Astronomical observations in other regions of the spectrum

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to compose a descriptive astronomy-style research report in LaTex, with graphs created using Python.
- Students will be able to describe optical telescopes and detectors using common vocabulary.
- Students will be able to interpret the reliability of data using statistical techniques.
- Students will understand coordinate systems and observational constraints, allowing them to plan astronomical observations.
- Students will be able to generate imaging data using a telescope and CCD camera.
- They will be able to correct raw astronomical data (images and spectroscopy) using standard analysis techniques in AstroPy.
- Students will be able to calibrate photometric and spectroscopic data from optical telescopes.
- Students will be able to query on-line astronomical databases using SQL.

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

Homework, projects, and final projects will be due according the timeline below. Students are encouraged to discuss homework assignments with each other, but the work that is turned in must be the student’s original work. ***Submitted homework must clearly show the steps leading up to each answer. Solutions that can only be understood by looking at the methods on another student’s paper will be evaluated for academic dishonesty.***

Data-taking for observational projects should be done with a partner. However, each person should analyze the data separately and write up his/her own research report. Students can compare results, but the submitted project should be the result of the submitting student’s own analysis and writing.

Normally, the Tuesday class will typically be in a lecture format. The Friday class is typically an activity format, and **laptops are required**. Because the beginning of the semester is online, the schedule has been shuffled so that the book learning is earlier in the semester and the activities are later. We will adjust the schedule and goals of the course if we must be online for a significant portion of the semester.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students who cheat will receive a failing grade for the course. Cheating includes: copying, or allowing another student to copy, a test or assignment; altering a graded test or assignment before requesting regrading; copying submitted solutions from published solution manuals or previous years; using materials other than those that are allowed on an exam; misrepresenting the work of another group or individual as your own. It is considered cheating to help another student cheat. ***It is imperative in your written projects that you do not copy text or images from the web or other sources without attribution, and that you do not misrepresent your collected data.***
