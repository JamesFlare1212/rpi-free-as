## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

The course will loosely follow *Conquering the Physics GRE, Third Edition by Yoni Kahn and Adam Anderson. Readings and some suggested problems will be assigned from the text.*

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Upon completion of the course and all recommended and optional coursework, students should understand their opportunities for graduate education in physics, the graduate application process, and will be prepared to take the GRE subject test in physics.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The course will extend over 14 weeks (see tentative schedule). Much of the content of the course is optional/recommended. Because much of the course focuses on preparing for the physics GRE, and because of the importance of this exam for students intending to earn advanced degrees in physics, it is recommended that students use every resource the course makes available in preparation for the exam. However, it is understood that this is a two-credit course and that recitations are not listed on sis, making conflicts possible, so students will not need to provide any excuse or explanation if unable to attend recitations or unable to complete the **optional** assignments. Readings from the text are required and expected by the corresponding lecture, but no regular grade will be taken regarding them. It is suggested that students work the multiple choice problems in the book as they go through it, as these are one of the best resources they have. Course topics are as follows:

1. Graduate School and the Application Process
2. Overview of the GRE Subject Test in Physics
3. Classical Mechanics on the PGRE
4. Electrodynamics on the PGRE
5. Quantum Mechanics on the PGRE
6. Atomic (and Molecular) Physics on the PGRE
7. Thermodynamics and Statistical Mechanics on the PGRE
8. Optics and Wave Phenomena on the PGRE
9. Special Relativity and Lab Methods on the PGRE
10. Special Topics on the PGRE
11. Test-taking and preparation strategies for the PGRE

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Understand opportunities for graduate education in physics and adjacent fields
- 2\. Understand the graduate application process for physics and adjacent fields, including standardized testing
- 3\. Understand core topics in undergraduate physics which are expected of incoming graduate students
- 4\. Be able to solve physics problems in the topics covered at the level which may appear on the Physics GRE Subject Test

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Learning will be assessed formally using assignments throughout the semester as described elsewhere in this syllabus, and informally by the student through recommended homework and practice tests. Students are expected to assess whether they have achieved the learning outcomes for each topic as the course progresses, with assignments primarily serving to prove that students are taking the course seriously enough to justify earning credit.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

The course is designated as Satisfactory/Unsatisfactory. A Satisfactory grade will be obtained by students who complete and submit the required assignments. Recommended assignments and readings do not need to be turned in.
