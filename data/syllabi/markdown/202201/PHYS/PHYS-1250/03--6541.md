## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a 4-credit physics course intended for self-selected students who have completed the equivalent of Physics I and Calculus I. The emphasis of this course will be electromagnetic theory including circuits, and optics.

Lectures will be pre-recorded and should be viewed asynchronously. Synchronous lecture class hours will be focused on problem solving activities and Q&A and will be recorded and made available. Labs are held separately and will involve longer formal write-ups and data analysis than PHYS-1200. A second difference between this class and the regular sections of PHYS-1200 will be a greater emphasis on concepts, vector calculus, and scientific computing. This class will cover slightly less Modern Physics than PHYS 1200 in order to leave more time for in-depth understanding.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Course topics:

- 1\) Electrostatics (Charge and Coulomb's Law, Electric Field and Gauss' Law, Potential, Gradient and Divergence, Capacitors and dielectrics)
- 2\) Magnetic fields (definition, force on moving charges and currents, Biot-Savart Law, Faraday's Law, Ampere's Law, Lenz's Law, magnetic properties of matter, line integrals)
- 3\) ac and dc Circuits (current, current density, resistance, capacitance, inductance, junction rules, loop rules)
- 4\) Electromagnetic waves (Maxwell's equations, energy transport, nature of light)
- 5\) Optics (properties, reflection and refraction, geometric optics, interference and diffraction, polarized light, photons)
- 6\) An introduction to matter waves.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate key factual knowledge of electromagnetism and circuits, waves and vibrations, and basic quantum physics. Examples of such knowledge include the order of magnitude of the wavelength, speed, and frequency of sound, light, and radio waves; and typical power consumption in common electrical devices.
- Demonstrate understanding of key concepts applying to electromagnetic and circuits, waves, optics, and basic quantum physics.
- Be able to follow written and oral instructions to complete physical measurements of electric, magnetic, and optical quantities.
- Translate a word, diagrammatic, or graphical description of a physical situation into a solvable mathematical description.
- Solve straightforward quantitative physical problems that involve one or two physical concepts in this course.
- Recognize when sufficient information is given to allow the student to solve for required quantities.
- Solve unfamiliar problems and assess unfamiliar physical situations based on the physical concepts of this course.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Online Homework: We will use MasteringPhysics for all online homework. Each set will include problems for which credit is earned and practice problems for no credit. Due dates and times are specified for each homework set. Online homework is due at the time and day specified for each set. Late homework will be accepted but the maximum credit that can be earned will drop with time after the due date.

Written Homework: Written homework will usually involve one problem for which you will be expected to clearly write out your solution. Your solution should include a statement of what you are seeking, assumptions, logical approach, relevant relations. Clarity of explanation will count for up to 20% of the grade.

Labs: There will be a two-hour lab session once a week for each lab section. Active participation as judged by your TA’s and me counts for 50% of your lab grade. (This will be included in your grade on your written labs, so that any lab handed in, in which you actively participated, will have a minimum grade of 50%.) All data you use should be acquired through active participation if possible. (You ***must*** cite the source if you do not use your own data! Failure to cite the source for data can result in a ***zero*** grade for the lab.) You will complete a write-up based on worksheets and/or Jupyter Notebooks. You are expected to keep a real-time notebook of your work – we will check for its presence and use regularly. The notebook itself will count as a full lab grade.

Quizzes: Weekly quizzes will be focused on the topics for homework and lab due in the two classes and lab previous to the quiz. Quizzes will be during lecture class hours unless pre-arranged otherwise. You will be permitted one letter-size page (one side) of notes for each quiz.

Lecture Class participation: Lecture attendance and participation is not required, but is strongly recommended. You must complete and submit class activities by the due date posted for each activity (usually 24 hours after the relevant class) for full credit.

Exams: Midterm (2-hour) and Final (3-hour) Exams will be cumulative (multi-topic). You will be permitted one side of one letter size sheet of paper for the midterm and two sides for the final. You may not consult or collaborate in any way with people other than the proctors during the exam.
