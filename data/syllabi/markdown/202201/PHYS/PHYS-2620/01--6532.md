### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

**Course Format:** The course consists of two classes per week that will be taught in lecture/discussion format, and six labs over the course of the semester. The lecture/discussion will cover topics listed in the course description, reading assignments, and from my own notes. The labs will allow students to experience optical phenomena as they gain theoretical understanding from the homework and classes.

**Homework:** Written homework is due weekly as posted on LMS. Your homework should be submitted through Gradescope by the posted due date. Late homework will not be accepted and a grade of zero will be given for that assignment. Your homework will be graded on your solutions, which must be explained clearly and logically.

**Laboratory:** The purpose of the lab experiments is to allow to become more familiar with optics phenomena as you gain theoretical understanding from the homework and lectures. You are expected to participate by touching, moving, measuring, discussing and understanding everything in the lab. Do not hesitate to ask any question related to the physics or technology. You are expected to keep a notebook with descriptions of your set-up, experimental procedure, and observations. The purpose of the notebook is for you or another student to remind yourself of what you did in the laboratory in sufficient detail so that I can reproduce your work or decide whether you measured the right thing a month after the fact. I will occasionally ask to see your notebook. The purpose of the laboratory write-up is to get you to think in certain ways about what you have seen or measured as such they are not expected to be extensive. You need only hand in a neat version of what we ask for in the laboratory write-up.

**Laboratory Participation and Attendance:** Your laboratory grade will consist of two parts. 50% of the Lab grade will come from attendance and participation in the laboratory. Participation means that you come to laboratory prepared, that you take an active role in carrying out and discussing the experiment in class, and that you keep a satisfactory notebook. There will be no make-up labs other than during the week of the lab unless you have a medical or administrative excuse. 50% of the grade will come from evaluation of your write up.

**Optics Final Project:** Students will choose a topic in modern optics to work on as their final project. The project will consist in writing a short (~1 page) summary of the chosen topic, followed by an oral in-class presentation to be given during the last 2 weeks of classes. The projects will be graded on a per-effort basis, i.e. based on how much you have learned, how far and deep you have gone beyond the textbook, etc.

The presentation should consist of an introduction which should include a motivation for the research being done on the topic and a brief highlight of the important concepts. The bulk of the presentation should describe the methodology and important results. You should end with a conclusion describing where you think the field is going. This should of course be based on current literature so make sure you cite your references.

Below is a short list of possible topics. You can pick any sub-topic from each general topic and be as specific as you want, or you can pick a topic covered at any point in the semester and examine it at an advanced level. You are also welcome to pick a topic that is not on this list. In any case, please run your choice by me by April 1st.

Topics: lasers, holography, nonlinear optics, ultrafast optics, photonic crystals, metamaterials, plasmonics, optical tweezers, optics for medical imaging.

## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Description:** This course is an introduction to the properties of light and its interactions with matter. Topics include wave, particle, and ray treatments of light; geometrical optics and imaging systems; interference and diffraction; polarization; Fourier methods for imaging; and coherence. We will also discuss some topics in modern optics such as lasers, nonlinear optics, and nano-optics.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to analyze fundamental optics problems.
- Students will be able to understand the basic concepts of physical and geometrical optics.
- Students will be able to design fundamental optics experiments.
- Students will be able to take data from an optics experimental setup.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts, which violate this trust, undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. Students are encouraged to discuss solutions to homework problems with one another before the class due date, but all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. Do not copy someone else’s homework and do not cheat on exams. If submission of any assignment is in violation of this policy, then you will be charged with academic dishonesty, and reported to the Dean of Students. You will receive a non-droppable zero for that work. If submission of any exam is in violation of this policy, then you will receive an F for the course and you will receive a written warning that will become part of your permanent record.
