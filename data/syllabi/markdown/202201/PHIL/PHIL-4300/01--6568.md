## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Concerns about the place of humans in relation to the natural world and the impact of our activities on the sustainability of the environment have come to the forefront in the last half century. These concerns have now taken on a high degree of urgency. Environmental Philosophy applies the insights and methods of Philosophy to a broad set of questions and concerns regarding the environment and our place in it.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Students will develop the skill of carrying on meaningful and productive philosophical discussion.
- 2\. Students will develop and utilize the skill of writing in a philosophical manner, employing analysis and argumentation to support a thesis.
- 3\. Students will be able to identify ways in which the global biosphere as well as local ecosystems are under threat from human practices.
- 4\. Students will be able to explain major distinctions within environmental philosophy, such as the distinctions between intrinsic value and instrumental value, and between anthropocentric and non-anthropocentric approaches.
- 5\. Students will be able to define the positions known as Deep Ecology and The Land Ethic by way of articulating the main points of each of these movements, as well as being able to identify criticisms of these positions.
- 6\. Students will develop skills of working in a group to research, prepare, and give a group presentation. Each student will gain practice in oral communication by delivering his or her portion of the presentation.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

In this class, all exam answers and discussion papers that are turned in for a grade must represent the student's own work.  
Submission of any exam or assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty. If found to be in violation of the Academic Integrity Policy, students may be subject to two types of penalty: The Instructor may administer a grade penalty and the student may be reported to the Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education, as appropriate.
