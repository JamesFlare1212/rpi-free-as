## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

1. **Course Outline**

Structures of metals, ceramics, and polymers and methods of structural determination will be discussed. The structure includes crystal structure, structural changes (transformation), defects, and microstructure. The crystal structural determination x-ray diffraction will be covered. Local structure of non-crystalline materials, such as glasses and polymer by FT-IR spectroscopy will also be covered.

**Chapter I Basic Crystallography with Notation and Crystal Structures of various materials**

- (A) Crystal Structure

Metals- f.c.c, b.c.c, h.c.p. tetragonal

Ceramics including Semiconducting Materials - Rock Salt, Corundum, Perovskite, Zinc Blend, Fluorite, Anti-fluorite, Spinel, Diamond, and Silicates and Silicas.

Polymers- orthorhombic

(B) Non-Crystalline Structure

Radial distribution of liquids and glasses

(C) Changes in Structure

Phase transformations (Crystal transformation, Order and Disorder, Phase separation and Crystallization), Crystallinity, Glass transition

**Chapter II** **X-Ray Diffraction and Its Applications to Structural Determination of Materials**

- (A) Generation and Properties of X-ray
- (B) Absorption of X-ray
- (C) Diffraction of X-ray
- (D) Line Broadening
- (E) Structure Factors

**Chapter III**. Defects and their influence on selected properties

- (A) Point defects- Vacancy and Interstitial, Schottky and Frenkel defects and Diffusion
- (B) Non-stoichiometry - Transition metal oxides, Impurity with different valence.
- (C) Dislocations - edge and screw, mixed dislocations. Plastic deformation.
- (D) Surfaces and Interfaces, Properties of nano materials.

**Chapter IV**. Local structure determination of non-crystalline materials: IR Spectroscopy

**Chapter V.** Microstructure—Ternary Phase Diagram

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Data acquisitions and analyses of two experiments are demonstrated. Students will learn how the data are acquired and then receive sets of the obtained data. Each student is required to analyze the data and submit a report on each topic. The report should follow the format of standard technical literature, having such sections as Introduction, Experimental Procedure, Results, Discussion and Conclusion. Referred literature should be cited at the end.

- (I) X-ray diffraction of crystalline materials.

Student will be shown the XRD equipment , Student will learn how the X-ray diffraction data are obtained. Student will receive x-ray diffraction data of an unknown samples. Students are asked to identify the crystalline material from various candidate materials.
