## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Electronic Properties of Engineering Materials, by J. Livingston (Wiley press). The book is required since the class will closely follow the textbook, use the same terminology, and take the homework from the text. Any version of the textbook is fine for this class.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The goal of this course is to develop an understanding of the electronic structure of materials (metals, semiconductors, insulators, polymers) and how the electronic structure determines observable und useful physical properties like electronic and optical properties. The course includes an introduction to quantum mechanics.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The following is an outline of the covered topics, it can be divided into the first part of the class which describes electrons using a classical approach (sessions 1-10), followed by the introduction of quantum mechanics (sessions 11-13) and the second part of the class which describes electronic properties using a quantum mechanical approach (14-28). The second part is both conceptually and mathematically more challenging.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students should have a basic understanding of the interaction of solids with electric and magnetic fields, as well as electro-magnetic radiation.
- Students should be able to explain observable physical properties, in particular electronic and optical properties, of different materials classes (metals, semiconductors, insulators, polymers) by using basic arguments involving the corresponding electronic structure.
- Students should be familiar with quantum mechanical concepts, as relevant to materials science and engineering, e.g. the wavelength of electrons in electron microscopy.
- Students should be comfortable with terminology used to describe the electronic structure in various disciplines including physics, chemistry, and electrical engineering, for example band gap, effective mass, HOMO-LUMO separation, delocalized states, etc.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Class Participation:** Class participation is important. It is worth 5% of the total grade and is based on attendance and class involvement (asking questions, answering questions). The instructor should be informed by email of any absence from class prior (if possible) to the absence. Attendance is recommended. However, if a student believes that she/he studies better from only reading the book, the student is not required to attend class. In that case, the instructor should be informed of such a decision so that a method for grading the “class participation” can be negotiated. RPI’s excuse policy is at: http://studentlife.rpi.edu/student-success/excused-absence

**Homework:** Homework assignments will be announced in class and/or by email and need to be submitted through LMS. Teamwork is encouraged, but simple copying is not acceptable. If you have been working “closely together” with (an)other student(s) on the homework, please indicate their names on the homework. Otherwise, the grader will assume that you were copying illegally, which is considered “cheating” (see below).

**Laboratories:** Approximately three laboratory assignments will be done in teams of ~4 students. The teams are formed by random assignment or sign-up. Each team submits one laboratory report (per assignment) by LMS by the announced due date. The reports will be graded and each member of a team receives the same grade. Laboratories will be held in room MRC 234. Each team will be scheduled to perform their experiments at a different time/day. Communication between team members is the responsibility of the team members. This applies to making sure that all members will be available and present during the scheduled time in the lab, and also for coordination, writing, and submission of the laboratory report.  
**Exams/(Quizzes):** The tests in this course are typically “open book”, that is, students can use the textbook during the tests. If your version of the textbook does not include a list of Physical Constants and a Periodic Table of the Elements, this should be added to the textbook, a copy can be downloaded from LMS or will be handed out in class. It is not permitted to bring own notes to the exams/quizzes (with the exception of some highlighting and notes within the textbook). Also, bring a calculator to all exams/quizzes.

(C)

The big quizzes will each be ~1 hour long.

(D)

The grade (A, A-, B+, B, B-, C+, C, C-, D+, D, F) will be determined after the final exam. No quiz result will be cancelled, but the grades are calculated using a curve. Students will be informed of their standing, after about one third of the semester and again near the end of the semester. Appeals and questions regarding grades and grading errors should be directly made to the grading TA or instructor. A written appeal of the decision by the instructor can be submitted within 5 Institute business days to the Head of the Materials Department.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Anyone caught cheating or plagiarizing for the first time will receive zero points in the assignment or test.**

**Anyone caught cheating or plagiarizing for the second time will receive an F in the course.**

**In addition, cases of academic dishonesty are also reported to the Office of the Dean of Students**, which keeps a report of the incident on file, and may also decide of further corrective measures.

Cheating includes sharing answers, stealing answers, and placing text strings or equations on your calculator or phone. Any interaction with a mobile device during an exam without explicit permission of the instructor will be interpreted as the illicit transfer of exam data, will be considered an act of cheating and will be treated as such. Plagiarism means copying words from someone’s work, even if you “change the sentence a bit.” If you share your homework, you are as guilty as the person copying it. **If you do use any source other than the textbook or your notes from class, you need to ALWAYS reference it, this includes Web-pages.**
