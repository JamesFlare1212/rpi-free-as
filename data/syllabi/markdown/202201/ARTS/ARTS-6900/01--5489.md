### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The Arts Graduate Colloquium is a forum where artists, researchers, theoreticians, historians, architects, curators, and Rensselaer faculty and graduate students will be invited to present their creative work and research.

Students enrolled in the colloquium will select the invited participants and contribute to the organization of these events. They will also suggest topics, questions, and readings for class discussion. The colloquium is intended to be a catalyst for an arts, humanities, and social science discourse that enhances the artistic and intellectual community at Rensselaer. The semester will end with conference-style presentations from members of the colloquium and faculty and graduate students in the department will be invited to the event.

Graduate students will attain knowledge of diverse approaches to creative work and research: by engaging in class discussions; by doing research on presenters work and topics addressed in the course; and through research assignments and class presentations of different formats.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. ## **Academic Integrity**

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty. In the case of major acts of plagiarism (e.g., text copied verbatim from an unidentified source) the student will receive a grade of F for the course. In the case of minor acts of plagiarism (e.g., referencing the findings of others without appropriate citations) the student will fail the assignment and receive a reduction of final course grade by one letter grade. If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
