## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The *Chemistry of the Environment* course was originally founded by several distinguished professors of Chemistry here at Rensselaer. In many ways, it was an early & prescient exemplar of confidently 'multidisciplinarizing' outwards from Chemistry's firmly-rooted purview. It's presently (Spring 2022) being both reprised and revamped, with a deliberate view now towards fostering the student's capacity to effectively utilize chemical theory and descriptive chemical knowledge in the problem-solving service of forming rigorously-defensible chemical arguments to address enviro-chemical challenges in industrial, regulatory, & legal contexts. The emphasis is on developing the student's 'chemical intuition,' such that they'll feel a growing confidence to be able to usefully & proactively engage with their professional colleagues at work, with analysts at commercial analytical laboratories, with governmental regulators, and with legal houses (particularly in the area of Expert Witness work).

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This Course over-archingly intends to impart to the student an awareness of what it's actually like in practice to work as a scientist/engineer in a professional capacity out in the environmental sector, where Chemistry-informed appreciations & explanations are frequently crucial from the perspectives of private industry (esp. oil-&-gas, mining, chemical manufacturers, etc.), of regulatory agencies (both federal & state authorities), and of legal entities (e.g., liability apportionment, 'brownfields' realty).

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will learn the crucial importance of carefully crafting rigorously-defensible Chemistry-informed arguments in the problem-solving service of addressing diverse enviro-chemical challenges from the perspectives of Industry, of Regulators, and of Legal houses.
- Students will learn to appreciate the career-importance of deliberately cultivating (while they're here at RPI) acquaintanceships with fellow students across various departmental & formal school demarcational boundaries, whose congenial approachability in the years to come (particularly when one's 'on-the-fly' and urgently in need of collegial advice at short-notice) can make a tremendous difference, especially as regards enviro-chemical situations.
- Students will learn to appreciate the importance of being defensibly thorough in making their chemical arguments/explanations, while also aiming for conciseness & succinctness in their submissions/presentations.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work.
