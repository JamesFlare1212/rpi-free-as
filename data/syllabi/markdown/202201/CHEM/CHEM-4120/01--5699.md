## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- The expected outcomes of this course are: Students will be able to use a variety of instruments that are common in modern chemistry laboratories. Students will be able to interpret and explain the significance of instrument outputs. Students will be able to manipulate experimentally derived data to determine a desired quantity. Students will be able to write technical communications for both professional and lay audiences. Students will be able to present an effective oral presentation on a technical subject.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

## Summary reports are required for all experiments. Outlines are given so that they can be of uniform format to aid in consistent grading, and to make clear what should be included. No introduction or discussion beyond what is specifically asked for on the outline is required. Each student should prepare an independent summary unless specifically instructed otherwise, and submit it electronically on Blackboard supplemented by paper copies of material that cannot be easily converted to electronic form. A formal report, modeled on a paper in a professional journal, is required for the Acetal experiment and for part of the Cyclic Voltammetry experiment, and another in the style of a popular science article for Dipole. These reports are graded for their quality as communications and not for the quality of the results in them, which are graded separately. All reports are expected to be done independently.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

## Academic Integrity

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

Any work presented in reports that does not appear in the note book is defined as "dry-labed"; in other words, faked or copied. Any report determined to contain dry-labed, faked or copied material will receive as grade the negative of its normal value. (NOTE – in some cases, a student must use other people’s data to complete a report because his/her data are not useable and the experiment cannot be repeated. In this case, a clear acknowledgement of the source of the data must be given, and there will be no penalty for its use.) Plagiarism will result in a grade of “F” for the course. Plagiarism consists of copying other people’s writings. It is generally permissible to quote, with proper attribution, brief excerpts of perhaps a sentence or less, when there is reason to use the writer’s original words, but reproduction of longer passages, even when properly referenced, is not acceptable. Simply changing a few words is not adequate either,

Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
