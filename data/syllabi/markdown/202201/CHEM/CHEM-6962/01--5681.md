## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

*Proteomics and Structural Bioinformatics* is a survey of modern techniques of protein biochemistry, bioinformatics, and proteomics, and their applications in the broad field of protein chemistry and functional genomics. It is targeted to upper-level undergraduate and graduate students in chemistry and/or biology (co-listed). It will include a discussion of basic concepts of protein structure and function, protein characterization and purification, enzyme kinetics, NMR, cryoEM, X-ray crystallography, mass spectrometry, molecular modeling, bioinformatics, and various techniques of functional and structural genomics, together with readings from current literature. Special emphasis will be placed this year on protein structure prediction with new AI-based methods, including *AlphaFold* and *RosettaFold*.

Students will participate in reading and presenting papers from the recent scientific literature in the areas of structural virology (including structural biology of corona viruses), cancer biology, de novo protein design, structural bioinformatics, protein dynamics, ordered water, membrane proteins, protein interaction networks, drug design, structure prediction, deep learning, protein folding, and protein evolution. Students will develop skills in bioinformatic database searching, molecular modeling with PyMol, and presentation of scientific data and results. Graduate students will also complete exercises in writing a scientific paper or preparing a short research grant proposal.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

(1) To understand the basic concepts by which protein sequence encodes protein structure and function.  
(2) To understand fundamental biophysical principles defining protein’s conformation and its conformational dynamics.  
(3) To understand and use the basic tools of sequence-based bioinformatics, including motifs, BLAST, PSI-BLAST, Hidden-Markov models (HMMs), and multiple sequence alignments (MSAs).  
(4) To be aware of general methods for analytical biochemistry, protein purification and crystallization.  
(5) To understand the underlying principles of cryo-electron microscopy (crypEM), X-ray crystallography, and nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR), and to appreciate their strengths and weaknesses in experimental protein structure determination.  
(6) To understand and use evolving methods of contact prediction from evolutionary sequence co-variance data.  
(7) To be aware recent advances in machine learning that can be applied to structural biology problems.  
(8) To appreciate strengths and weaknesses of recent advances in protein structure prediction, and to carry out structure predictions using assigned protein sequences.  
(9) To understand how to use kinetic data to evaluate models of enzyme mechanisms.  
(10) To appreciate the roles of protein dynamics in determining protein function.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Over the course of study, all students will acquire the following proficiencies: (i) Description of the structures and physical organic chemistry of amino acids and polypeptides.
- (ii) Understanding and intuition about how protein sequence defines protein structure, and how to design sequences to adopt particular structures.
- (iii) Skills for evaluating protein sequence and structure similarity, use database searching tools to identify homologous sequences, building multiple sequence alignments, and creating phylogenetic trees based on protein sequence and structure.
- (iv) Expertise in combining evolutionary information and protein structure to identify functional properties of proteins, and to predict inter-residue contacts within proteins that have been conserved by evolution.
- (v) Using state-of-the-art methods for protein structure and protein disorder prediction.
- (vi) Practical aspects of experimental protein structure determination, including protein purification, crystallization, cryoEM, X-ray crystallography, NMR, and amide hydrogen/deuterium exchange measurements. (vii) Experience in structural and functional database searches, including protein structure prediction.
- (vii) Experience in structural and functional database searches, including protein structure prediction.
- (viii) A moderately advanced theoretical framework sufficient to design and analyze simple enzyme kinetic studies.
- (ix) Appreciate the roles of molecular dynamics in enzyme function, allostery, and molecular recognition processes
- (x) Basic skills needed for writing successful grant proposals and scientific papers.
- All students will also gain experience in presenting a scientific lecture.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Graduate (6000 level) Students:** Expected to provide high-quality visually-interesting graphical presentations in ppt slides, and examples of how principles or processes learned in class can be applied in their own research and/or broadly in scientific research. Each of the following will contribute 18% to the grade: (i) Take-home Exam I; (ii) Take-home Exam II; (iii) Take-home Exam III; (iv) In class quizzes and exercises; (v) Student Presentation. The Research Paper / Proposal will count 10%.

In presenting a scientific lecture (Student Presentation) to the class, 6000 level students will be expected to integrate results from a specific paper with the state-of-the-art knowledge across the field of structural biology, and will be required to explain the impact of the subject work for society in language suitable for a lay audience.

Final grades will also take into account class participation, especially in the Student Presentations. Class participation will assessed by noting the number and insightfulness of questions raised during lectures by the course instructor, visiting lecturers, and student presenters.

Grades will be curved relative to performance of the previous 5 years of this course.

Students registered at the 6000 level cannot receive “D/D+/D-” grades.

Student’s may appeal grades by presenting their concerns to the course instructor.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

## **The course consists largely of take home exams. Students are required to sign a statement for each take-home exam certifying that they have not discussed or communicated in any way with any current or past class member of the class regarding the exam. Students discovered to have compromised this academic integrity will be assigned a failing grade for the exam**.

For certain exercises, collaborative or group work will be encouraged where appropriate. In these cases, your collaborators must be identified in the submitted assignment. The instructor will specify when collaboration will be explicitly forbidden (e.g., individual take home exams). If you are uncertain, please contact the instructor by email.

Plagiarism involves copying text from a source without proper credit. Plagiarism is a form of Academic Dishonesty. A grade of zero will be given on the first assignment where a violation is detected. If there is a subsequent infraction, the student will receive a grade of F for the course.

Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
