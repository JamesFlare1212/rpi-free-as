## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Strategic writing means writing for a purpose, with a specific audience in mind, and being aware how you as a writer make certain rhetorical choices while writing in order to communicate more effectively with a particular audience, within the context of a specific rhetorical situation. Additionally, this class serves as an introduction to writing and researching at the college level, and will teach students how to locate and assess peer-reviewed scholarly sources through the library and how to incorporate the ideas of others into one’s own writing and how to cite sources in a way that makes it clear which ideas are the student’s own, and which are coming from outside sources. This is a writing-intensive, process-driven class, so students will submit papers in multiple drafts and receive comments from the instructor and from their peers at multiple stages throughout the process.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Learn to write focused, well-organized texts for a variety of academic audiences and rhetorical situations.
- Learn to view writing as a process, to receive constructive feedback from both instructors and peers, and to revise writing through multiple drafts.
- Learn to use the library databases to locate reputable, scholarly sources and to incorporate ideas from these sources into one’s own writing while providing appropriate documentation in the correct citation format.
- Learn to view writing within the context of an ongoing conversation, and learn to make worthwhile original contributions to the types of conversations that typically take place in the academy.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

All work submitted for this class should be the student’s original work, and should have been written for this class. When students include the ideas of others in their writing, they should make it clear in the text and through in-text citations which ideas and quotes are from outside sources. In cases where group work is assigned, the names of all members in the group must be listed, and the submission must contain only work by those students. Submission of any assignment in violation of this policy will result in a zero on the assignment. A second violation will result in automatic failure of the course and potential disciplinary action by the institution.
