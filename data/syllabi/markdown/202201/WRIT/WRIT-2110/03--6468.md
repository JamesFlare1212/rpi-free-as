## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is designed to assist students in becoming more self-possessed and versatile writers. The course offers instruction in linguistic, rhetorical, and sociocultural concepts through its overarching focus on language: how it works, where it falls short, and how to harness it for specific purposes. Critical thinking and composing are conceptualized as processes, which includes the creation, development, organization, and revision of ideas and arguments. Students will complete a series of writing assignments in order to participate in contemporary debates occurring in both public and academic contexts related to their professional fields of interest. This course will give students foundational knowledge, skills, and materials to develop greater proficiency in their chosen fields and to generate powerful professional profiles based in the principles of strategic writing.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Write focused, audience appropriate texts for a variety of strategic purposes.
- Revise and improve both the content and form of their own writing.
- Write in response to a variety of rhetorical contexts using diverse rhetorical strategies
- Build arguments based on observation, research, and analysis that bridge public, professional, and academic contexts.
- Cite sources according to academic conventions (MLA style), which includes knowing when and how to integrate summary, paraphrase, and direct quotation.
- Apply the principles of rhetoric and strategic writing toward building professional knowledge and producing texts relevant to professional growth.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**The Social-Impact Narrative.** How can we deploy narrative rhetoric to enliven writing about challenges in our chosen professional fields? In this essay for a hybrid public/academic audience, you will use narrative rhetoric (tell a persuasive story) to illuminate an issue of concern in your field of study and to position yourself as an aspiring change-maker in your chosen profession.  
**The Cultural Analysis.** In this essay for a public audience, you will consider an issue of concern related to your field of study and discuss *how* the issue affects and/or is affected by popular culture and *why* the issue should be considered beyond your field of study. Rather than making an argument, you will strategically use **analysis** to prompt your audience to rethink their values surrounding the issue.  
**The Sociopolitical Argument.** How can we combine analysis with research to take a solid position on a contemporary issue relevant to our chosen professional fields? This assignment asks you to build on the linguistic and rhetorical skills, as well as cultural awareness, you practiced while completing previous assignments. In this essay for an academic audience, you will need to identify and research an ongoing sociopolitical conversation relevant to your chosen profession. This will involve both journalistic and academic research. You will then construct a rhetorically sound argument in order to add your own perspective to a pre-existing sociopolitical conversation.  
**The Professional Profile** (culminating presentation). How can we deploy our newly acquired rhetorical and strategic communication skills to establish a cohesive and engaging professional profile? In this culminating assignment for a professional audience, we will role-play a reality show (think Shark Tank) in which teams of students will “compete” to be chosen as Communication Ambassadors for the university. Communication Ambassadors are teams of students who showcase to current and prospective students how RPI undergraduates wield excellent strategic communication skills that serve them in their professional fields. Students will work individually on a professional profile (website, digital page, or slideshow), which will include text and visuals to pitch an individual’s professional interests, expertise, and aspirations using materials developed over the semester. Teams of students will then weave their individual profiles together to pitch themselves as a collaboration of change-makers worthy of professional attention and investment.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

All assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work completed for this class in particular. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. If you have a compelling reason for wanting to use previous work of yours for the class, it must be substantially and fundamentally revised, and you must get approval from the instructor first.

Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty of a zero for the assignment and the potential to fail the course. Second offenses will result in automatic failure of the course and the potential for disciplinary action by the institution.

If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
