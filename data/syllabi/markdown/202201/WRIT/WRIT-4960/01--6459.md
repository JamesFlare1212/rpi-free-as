## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will use the ‘visual essay’ as a focus of study, leading to a deeper understanding of visual rhetoric from both practical and academic perspectives. The course will explore the parameters of the 'essay' as a format/genre, examining iterations from literary essays to contemporary internet 'video essays;' we'll look at all kinds of examples, including ‘film essays,' TV shows in essayistic style, graphic and infographic essays, as well as examples of ‘photo essays,’ ‘comics essays,’ and instances of essays that exist in music video, video game, and other formats.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- CLO 1 Summarize and discuss the historical and cultural foundations of the 'essay' derived from literary traditions
- CLO 2 Identify, explain, and evaluate conceptual frameworks related to the personal essay across media formats, with a particular emphasis on understanding how those essay frameworks translate from logocentric into visual media
- CLO 3 Apply critical lenses to visual essay texts, and debate the value and functions of those critical strategies in evaluating them
- CLO 4 Work collaboratively with groups of peers to compose multi-faceted critiques of visual essays in various formats, and present those critiques to the larger class
- CLO 5 Produce a creative visual essay inspired by carefully examined visual essay texts, or a produce a critical text on a visual essay in response to model essay texts

The class proceeds on a 100-point scale, and there are a potential 110 points to accumulate if you should choose; you might be able to do that through an expansion of certain assignments (to receive platinum or diamond medals), or a revision of assignments, or through an ‘essayistic exploration’ bonus activity.

‘Medal’ Based Scoring

Assignments will be assessed using a ‘medal’ system [a form of 'specifications grading']. For certain larger projects, we may establish a more detailed rubric for grading if you so desire, or reach an understanding through discussion. In most cases, though, if you do the work that guidelines ask for.

Medal Points % Description

Diamond* 120% A sparkling exemplar for the class, full of dazzling insight

Platinum* 110% An exceptionally perceptive or clever exploration of assignment

Gold 100% Weighty, polished, addressing all required aspects of assignment

Silver 90% A response with some clarity, but maybe lacking on some aspect

Bronze 80% Some clarity, missing clear elements from guidelines

Copper 70% Missing elements, and bends heavily under the stress of examination

Nickel 60% Partial or relatively flimsy assignment

Tin\*\* 50% Dull or lifeless response, crumples in the breeziest of discussions

\*To receive a ‘Platinum’ medal requires that you go above and beyond the requirements of the assignment, and the ‘Diamond’ requires even more in-depth work of great quality. \*\*Below ‘Tin’ means you simply failed to turn in the assignment, or it’s too incomplete to grade

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
