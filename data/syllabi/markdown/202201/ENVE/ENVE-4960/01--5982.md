## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand the basis for state and federal regulations associated to environmental impact assessments, site assessments and environmental remediation.
- Compare different advanced methods for assessing soil and groundwater contamination and choose the most adequate ones based on geological conditions
- Distinguish the level of detailing required for a Phase I and for a Phase II assessment and be able to elaborate a technical report for a contaminated site
- Recognize the possible opportunities for Civil and Environmental Engineers in the consulting field
- Develop critical thinking and analytical abilities to evaluate case studies and present results of your considerations to an audience

### Other Course Policies:

Homework Policy  
Late homework deliveries will not be accepted. Students who have a valid reason for not submitting the homework on time should contact the instructor.  
The homework will be collected and graded. Some problems may only be checked for completeness while other problems may be graded in more detail. Further, the weighting assigned to each homework problem will vary depending upon the complexity of the problems.  
Homework Problems should be written in paper, scanned and uploaded to the system. I know it seems an unreasonable request, but writing down (instead of typing, copying, pasting, editing multiple times) is a great way to fix what you learned.  
For scanning, I recommend using Adobe Scan (available for Android and IoS). It is a free, reliable app (no adwares), that allows to take multiple pictures and convert in a single PDF document. The document can be e-mailed or saved in a cloud service (Google Drive).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic Integrity  
Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own.  
Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities (www.rpi.edu/dept/doso/handbook.html) defines various forms of academic dishonesty and procedures for responding to them.  
Students should familiarize themselves with this portion of the Handbook and should note that the penalties for academic dishonesty can be quite severe. This now also applies to students who use platforms such as Chegg.com to hire someone to do their homework/quizzes.  
In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work, although students are encouraged to work with each other by collaborating on assignments (see item 4 under Homework Policy above).  
Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy (e.g., one student copying work done by another student or a student copying from a previously developed solution will result in a grade of zero for that assignment. The first student who sends me a print of this paragraph will get five marks of extra credit on any of their exams. Also, cheating on an exam, which includes using services such as Chegg.com, will result in a grade of zero for that exam and, in accordance with the Handbook, notification to the Dean of Students Office of the incident of academic dishonesty.
