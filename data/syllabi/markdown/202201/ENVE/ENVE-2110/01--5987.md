## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

A first, “survey” course in environmental engineering for the undergraduate major and for the civil engineer (or any engineer) interested in the impacts of civilization on the natural environment and public health. This class will present an overview of the field (what do Environmental Engineers do?), seek to point out the physical, chemical and biological principles that govern the interaction of anthropogenic activity (what people do) and the environment, and show how these principles are used to predict, prevent and restore the deleterious effects of pollution

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1\) Learn the language of environmental engineering.  
2\) Understand and synthesize the whole-system approach to problems in air, water and on, in or underground.  
3\) Learn what Environmental Engineering unit operation and processes can do and how to synthesize that information to rationally apply many operations, and processes in series and parallel to accomplish overall goals.

### Other Course Policies:

Textbook Reading Policy  
Reading the textbook is considered by the instructor to be a significant and important component of the course. Thus, reading assignments will be posted on the course website. Students are strongly encouraged to complete the reading assignments since doing so will likely correlate strongly with establishing a good understanding of the course material and thus will likely lead to good performance in the course. Furthermore, exam questions may be taken from the textbook.  
Homework Grading  
Homework will be graded on the following classifications:  
• Complete: Everything is ok (formulas, concepts, calculations). No further recommendations.  
• Complete*: Complete with minor errors (formulas, signals, calculations). Recommended to check the solutions.  
• Partial: Problems were delivered, more than 50% of the problems are correct, but had major conceptual issues. Strongly recommend to re-do your work from scratch, compare to the solutions posted and talk to professor or T.A. for clarification.  
• Incomplete: Didn’t complete the task (missing problems, homework not delivered), violated academic integrity standards, more than half of the problems incorrect.  
Complete and Complete* get full credit for the task (grade = 100), Partial gets a deduction of 15% (grade = 85) and Incomplete gets no credit for the task (grade = 0)  
Homework Policy  
1\) Submitted homework should be presented in a professional format (i.e., legible work that is presented in a logical order such that it can be readily followed). See detailed "Homework Requirements" below.  
If a problem is illegible or is not presented in a logical order that can be readily followed or does not substantially follow the guidelines in the "Homework Requirements", the problem will be regarded as not completed and will be assigned a grade of zero.  
2\) Late homework will generally not be accepted. Students who have a valid reason for not submitting the homework on time should contact the instructor.  
3\) You are encouraged to solve the homework problems through collaboration with your classmates. However, it is also suggested that each student attempt the homework problems on their own prior to any collaborations so as to understand their personal deficiencies. In this way, collaborative work will be most effective for each student.  
4\) Although students are encouraged to collaborate (learn from each other), each student must turn in their own work (copying among students is not acceptable). If copying occurs, all students involved will be assigned a grade of zero for the assignment. In group assignments all contributors must be acknowledged.  
5\) The homework will be collected and either all or a portion of the problems will be graded. Some problems may only be checked for completeness while other problems may be graded in detail. Further, the weighting assigned to each homework may vary depending upon the complexity of the problems.  
6\) Homework assignments and solutions will be posted on the course website.  
Calculator Recommen-dation  
We encourage students to learn how to use calculators allowed by the NCEES (National Council of Examiners for Engineering and Surveying) for the Fundamentals of Engineering (FE) exam. The list of accepted models is available at https://ncees.org/exams/calculator/  
Final Exam Substitutive Exam  
For this course, the ”Final” Exam, still to be schedule by Registrar, will be offered to substitute the lowest grade. The lowest grade will be replaced even if the Final grade is lower than the current lowest grade.  
Missing an Exam  
If you are not present for a midterm exam, a make-up test will not be given, but you are allowed to take the Final exam to replace the missed exam.  
In case a student has three exams or missed one or more exams without justifiable excuse and cannot show up to the Final Exam, their grade will be calculated considering all previous exams.  
In case a student misses two or more exams during the semester, with justifiable excuse, the Final Exam will replace all exams.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic Integrity  
Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own.  
Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities (www.rpi.edu/dept/doso/handbook.html) defines various forms of academic dishonesty and procedures for responding to them.  
Students should familiarize themselves with this portion of the Handbook and should note that the penalties for academic dishonesty can be quite severe. This now also applies to students who use platforms such as Chegg.com to hire someone to do their homework/quizzes.  
In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work, although students are encouraged to work with each other by collaborating on assignments (see item 4 under Homework Policy above).  
Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy (e.g., one student copying work done by another student or a student copying from a previously developed solution will result in a grade of zero for that particular assignment. Also, cheating on an exam, which includes using services such as Chegg.com, will result in a grade of zero for that exam and, in accordance with the Handbook, notification to the Dean of Students Office of the incident of academic dishonesty.
