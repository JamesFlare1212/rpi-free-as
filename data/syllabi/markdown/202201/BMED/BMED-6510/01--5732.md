## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**This is a hybrid course where students enrolled in BMED 4510 (undergraduate) and BMED 6510 (graduate) meet simultaneously with different level of difficulty at undergraduate & graduate levels for assignments, quiz and examination**

**Course description:**

Mechanical regulation of biological systems will be discussed. Topics include Principles and concepts of mechanobiology; Embryogenesis and histogenesis of tissues with particular references to skeletal system; Physical forces at cellular, tissue and organ level; Mechanical regulation of cellular behavior, tissue growth and organ development; Limits of mechanical regulation: Biochemical influences; Application of mechanobiology to tissue regeneration.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Objective:** To introduce the principles and concepts of mechanical regulation of biological systems applied to tissue regeneration with special emphasis on orthopaedics.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Student Learning Outcomes: At the end of the course a student will be able to evaluate, analyze and apply the principles and concepts of mechanical regulation of biological systems from cellular to organ levels to a variety of situations with particular references to regeneration of skeletal tissues.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**1. Homework**: Instructor will assign weekly homework based on the material covered in the class and in the form of reading assignments that will be announced during the semester. Depending on the class size, students will be assigned specific reading material for a short presentation which will be followed by a class discussion on the assigned material.

**2. Quizzes (2)**: Tuesday Feb. 4th; Friday March 18th  
**3. In-class examinations (3) :** Friday February 18th; Tuesday March 29th; Tuesday April 26th  
**4. Term-Project:** Students will choose a topic of their interest from the list provided by the instructor to conduct independent library research, participate in group brainstorming sessions and write an independent research grant on the selected topic. Submission of the research grant will be followed by a class presentation and discussion session. Grading of the term project will be based on a written report (50%), class presentation (25%) and participation in group and in-class discussion (25%). The research project should be submitted on or before April 1st. Presentations are scheduled for: April 8, April 12, April 15 and April 19

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

In this class, all assignments that are turned in or a grade must represent the student's own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a zero score on that assignment in the first instance. A repeated violation (i.e. more than once) of this policy will result in “F” grade on the course. If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. Being found guilty of the following forms of academic dishonesty described on in the Rensselaer Handbook will result in an "F" grade in the course along with the other disciplinary actions listed in the Handbook and found to be appropriate: academic fraud, collaboration during an exam, copying during an exam, cribbing during an exam, fabrication, plagiarism, sabotage, substitution.
