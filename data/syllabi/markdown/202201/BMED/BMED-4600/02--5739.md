## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is a guided approach to development of design skills for Biomedical Engineering seniors. It gives the student practical experience with a wide variety of different aspects of design and product development. Students work individually and in teams to address biomedical design problems using methods drawn as necessary from engineering and from the physical and mathematical sciences. Discussion sessions involve students and include presentations of their work. This is a communication intensive course. Prerequisite: Senior standing. Spring term annually. 3 credit hours

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This is the capstone design course and communications intensive course for biomedical engineering students. The course requires that students engage in teamwork to define needs and design devices or systems. Oral and written communication skills are emphasized in various reports and presentations throughout the semester. Students will gain experience in engineering design of biomedical products, processes, and systems. Students will also develop strong written and oral presentation skills, as well as the ability to lead and contribute to multidisciplinary teams in industrial, academic, and clinical environments.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Notes on objectives**:

The term *engineering* *design* refers to open-ended, creative activity in engineering to meet stated needs while also satisfying real-world constraints such as cost and safety. *Open-ended* means that the need(s) can be met in different ways by more than one engineering approach or solution. Students should arrive at a best engineering solution, and then describe and demonstrate the feasibility of the chosen solution.

BME Design is also known as a *capstone* *design* experience. The term *capstone* means that the course builds upon prior coursework and knowledge. New subject matter is covered in the course when needed, but the student should bring together prior knowledge and experience to create successful design(s).

There is a distinction between developing a proof-of-concept prototype and designing a product for manufacturing. Design for manufacturing (DFM) is a critical skill for engineers who will do product development, research to support product development, manufacturing of products, or quality. Because DFM is such a critical skill, the emphasis of this course is on DFM and not merely prototype development.

**Course Structure**:

Teams will be formed of +/-5 students per project. Each team will be advised by a faculty member and an outside “expert”. These teams may eventually subdivide into smaller teams. Student teams will develop ideas into functional prototypes over the course of the semester. Each team will prepare progress reports and presentations during the course of the semester to keep faculty and other student teams apprised of their progress. Emphasis will be placed on the successful design for manufacturing and development of a prototype by semester end. Final designs will be presented by the students to the general public at the Design Showcase. Awards including “Best Design” will be given at the conclusion of the Design Showcase.
