## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The principles and physical applications of thermodynamics will be developed. Basic concepts in chemical and quantum statistical mechanics will be introduced and their relationships to thermodynamics will be discussed.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The intent of this course is to introduce the principles and physical applications of classical thermodynamics and statistical mechanics so that students can develop an intuitive understanding of how macroscopic observations can be explained from microscopic effects. In addition, basic concepts in classical and quantum statistical mechanics will be introduced and their relations to thermodynamics will be developed. By the end of the course, students should get a new appreciation of thermodynamics concepts and the power of statistical mechanics in explaining those concepts.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Topics include:**  
Heat, Probability, Temperature, Boltzmann factor, Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution/Pressure/Collisions, First Law, Heat engines, Entropy, Thermodynamics potentials, third law, Equipartition theorem, Partition function, Ideal gas, Chemical Potential, Photons, Phonons, Relativistic gas, Real gases, Cooling real gases, Phase Transition, Bose-Einstein + Fermi-Dirac, Ising model; Quantum gas, Thermodynamics of Earth.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to provide definition and explanation of concepts such as: temperature, entropy, phase transitions, partition function, Gibbs energy, Helmholtz function.
- Students will learn all four laws of thermodynamics and explain their origin and applications
- Students will be able to calculate the partition functions of simple quantum mechanical systems (e.g., harmonic oscillator, hydrogen atom) and obtain thermodynamic potentials from them.
- Students will be able to explain basic statistical mechanics of quantum gas. Those concepts include Fermi-Dirac and Bose-Einstein distributions.
- Students will be able to apply their knowledge of thermodynamics to complex systems such as the Earth atmosphere and provide a critical description of the effect of global temperature increase.
- Students will demonstrate the elementary use of Monte Carlo computational techniques to study phase transitions or similar problem
