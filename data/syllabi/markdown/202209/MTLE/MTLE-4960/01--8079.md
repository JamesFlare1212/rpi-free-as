## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Introduction to data science and machine learning, with case studies in discovery of structure-property relationships and new materials from experimental and computational data. Brief review of required background in linear algebra and statistics with hands-on exercises in Python. Data science topics: model fitting, clustering, dimensionality reduction, ontologies, Bayesian inference and design of experiments.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Outline of topics that will be covered in this course:

- Introduction to material informatics
- Exploratory data analysis: a demo
- Python and NumPy boot camp
- Statistics of single variables
- Relationships between variables
- Basic linear regression
- Bayesian statistics
- Design of experiments
- Databases
- Model fitting, prediction and cross-validation
- Classification
- Dimensionality reduction
- Clustering and density estimation
- Image analysis
- Neural networks
- Genetic Programming
- Case studies in material informatics (student presentations)

The first half of the course will establish familiarity with necessary statistical and computational techniques using relatively simple data sets and techniques, while the second half will survey advanced techniques with more complex data sets.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- select appropriate data-science methods leveraging statistical and mathematical foundations
- apply data-science techniques to research and engineering design problems in materials science
- assess the uncertainty of conclusions derived from data-driven methods
- apply cutting-edge data-science methods from recent literature to their research projects
- compare new data-driven approaches for materials science and identify their efficacy and limitations
- communicate research findings to a professional audience

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- Weekly homework with theoretical and computational exercises.
- Short research project with literature survey and application of a data science method, with a written report and oral presentations
- Participation in class by asking questions and contributing to discussions

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

In this class, working together and discussing solution strategies is strongly encouraged for all assignments, but final submitted work should be written individually. All discussions and references consulted should be properly acknowledged on each assignment.

A grade of zero will be given on the first assignment where a violation is detected, and a referral to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduates, Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students). If there is a subsequent infraction, the student will receive a grade of “F” for the course and will be referred to the appropriate Dean.
