## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will provide the background and a set of examples of how computational methods can be applied to the design of materials with desired structures and properties. The methods will span multi-length and time scales, including approaches at continuum, atomistic and electronic scales. Lectures will be complemented by computer labs with hands-on exercises using publicly available or commercial software packages.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

No required textbook, all class materials will be in the form of handouts. Reading materials and lecture notes will be uploaded to: https://lms.rpi.edu. Several books are reserved at the library as additional references:

1. A.R. Leach, "Molecular Modeling: Principles and Applications ", 2nd Ed., Prentice Hall, 2001.
2. Frenkel and Smit, "Understanding Molecular Simulation: From Algorithms to Applications", 2nd Ed., Academic Press, 2002.

3. J. M. Haile, "Molecular Dynamics Simulation", 1992, Wiley. A textbook on elementary molecular dynamics simulation methods.

4. Allen and Tildesley, "Computer Simulation of Liquids", Clarendon Press, Oxford, 1987. A general introduction to computer simulation.

5. Richard M. Martin, "Electronic Structure: Basic Theory and Practical Methods", Cambridge University Press, 2004.

6. Landau and Binder, "A Guide to Monte Carlo Simulations in Statistical Physics", Cambridge University Press, 2005.

7. Richard LeSar, “Introduction to Computational Materials Science”, Cambridge University Press, 2013.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\) Demonstrate a solid understanding of the theory behind computational methods.
- 2\) Pose well-developed research questions in materials science and engineering that can be addressed through computational methods.
- 3\) Use software packages to effectively design materials with desired structures and properties.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

## Homework assignments (outcome 1-3):

Homework assignments focus on simulation work and data analysis. Each problem set is designed to build upon the material covered in the preceding lectures, and to use software packages that illustrate different theoretical and simulation approaches.

## Lab reports (outcome 3):

Short lab “reports” are due at the end of each lab. Labs are designed to teach students how to use software packages. Students need to run simulations to provide solutions to problems in the reports.

## Exams (outcome 1-2):

There will be a mid-term exam (75 mins) and a final exam (120 mins).

## Term project (outcome 2-3):

Students will be asked to complete a team term project on the computational design of materials with desired structure and properties.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students may discuss homework; however, the actual assignment submitted must represent an independent effort on your part.

Anyone caught cheating or plagiarizing for the first time will receive an F in the assignment or exam.

Anyone caught cheating or plagiarizing for a second time will receive an F in the course, and a report will be made to the Dean of Students.

Cheating includes sharing answers, stealing answers, and placing text strings or equations on your calculator, cell phone, or other electronic devices. Plagiarism means copying words from someone else's work, even if you "change the sentence a bit".
