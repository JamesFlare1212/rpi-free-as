## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to demonstrate proficiency working with concepts related to: demand-supply;
- Students will be able to demonstrate proficiency working with concepts related to: consumer preferences
- Students will be able to demonstrate proficiency working with concepts related to: profit maximization
- Students will be able to demonstrate proficiency working with concepts related to: market structure related issues
- Students will be able to demonstrate proficiency working with concepts related to: information economics
- Students will be able to demonstrate proficiency working with concepts related to: uncertainty, risk, and decision making

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Three exams (25% each) and iClicker scores (25%). If iClicker scores are lower than the exam average, then final grade is based solely on each exam counting 33.3%.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Three exams (25% each) and iClicker scores (25%). If iClicker scores are lower than the exam average, then final grade is based solely on each exam counting 33.3%. Letter grades are based on standard grading curve. Grades may be curved upward at discretion of instructor.  
No scores are dropped. All exam and iClicker grades may be appealed with the instructor.  
All grades are available on students' iClicker app and on Blackboard.  
iClicker scores will be based on 90% of total points. So, you can miss up to 10% of iClicker points for any reason. However, there are no opportunities to make up missed iClicker points, regardless of the reason the points were missed.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work.
