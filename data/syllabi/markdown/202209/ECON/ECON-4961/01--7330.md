## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The goal of this course is to understand the various ways in which international trade affects our natural environment. We will study the role of trade in contributing to air pollution, shifting dirtier industries to countries with lax environmental regulations, creating a cleaner environment via efficient production, natural resource depletion, and how trade and environmental policies interact. We will first understand the causes and consequences of international trade by studying classical and newer trade theories with related empirics. Then we will apply these tools to study the consequences of international trade in the presence of market failure, i.e., environmental externalities. Finally, we will study how trade and environmental policy can help correct these market failures and the role of international environmental agreements.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Proficiency in core theories and concepts in international trade and environmental economics.
- An ability to analyze and interpret empirical findings in international trade.
- An ability to apply theories and empirical evidence to break down the impact of international trade and trade policy on welfare and environment.
- Develop assessments on issues at the intersection of trade and environment.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Problem Sets (20% of grade) –** I will assign two problem sets over the course of this semester. The problem sets will comprise questions on deriving theoretical results, interpreting empirical

findings, and breaking down an argument based on the concepts covered in lectures. I recommend working in a group to discuss assignments, but the solutions must be written and submitted individually. Please make sure written assignments are legible. I will deduct 5% of the assignment grade for untidy work.

**Exams (50% of grade) –** We will have two midterm exams. The midterm exams will comprise questions, both conceptually and in difficulty, similar to assignments. The exams will be closed book.

**Project (30% of grade) –** Students will write an essay on a topic of their choice at the intersection of international trade and the environment. I will provide you with detailed guidelines on the project about one-third of the way through the semester. The project would comprise three main components—a description of the issue and why it is interesting, existing literature and other articles analyzing the issue, your own assessment of the issue based on economic concepts and topics covered in class. Students can work in groups of up to 3.
