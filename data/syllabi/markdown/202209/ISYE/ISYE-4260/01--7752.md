## Course Goals

### Goals:

The goal of the course is to enable students to use various cognition models (e.g., information processing, situation awareness, ...) to support and model human performance in complex tasks using cutting-edge technologies such as wearable sensory devices.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

- Human sensory, motor, and decision systems are discussed, as well as principles for designing visual and auditory displays
- Students will learn models of information processing and situation awareness to measure performance, mental workload in the complex working environment
- Students will learn to use cognitive task performance to model user behavior in specific applications using task analysis and cognitive performance modeling methods (e.g., GOMS, KLM, NGOMSL).
- Students will develop computational cognitive models of human performance using different software tools (e.g., CogTool, Cogulator).
- Experimental designs and statistical models for behavior assessment will be reviewed.
- The student will employ wristband technology (Empatica), an eye tracking device, Virtual Reality, and a flight simulator to evaluate and model human performance.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to Identify the key principles of information processing theory and human performance in complex tasks;
- Measure, compare, interpret and evaluate human cognitive states, including situation awareness and mental workload;
- Learn and conduct (cognitive) task analyses using verbal protocol;
- Develop and model human performance using techniques including KLM, and NGOMSL.
- Weight various experimental designs, statistical analysis for human performance modeling
- Employ wearable technologies and immersive devices to model operator's performance assessments.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Student performance will be evaluated based on the in-class activities, exams, homework, lab reports, articles, projects, and individual and group activities. These assessments will be used to provide feedback on students' performance and to improve them.

## **Assignments**

**Tests:**

- o The students will have **two exams** throughout the semester.
- o The second exam covers all concepts throughout the semester.

**Homework Assignments:**

- o Throughout the semester exploratory assessment activities will be assigned to reinforce or expand on material that is covered in the lecture.
- o Ten (tentative) homework sets will be assigned, and these are to be worked on outside of class.
- o Students may collaborate on the assignments, meaning students can discuss the assignments, but every student must complete and turn in his/her written solutions in his/her own words (unless otherwise stated in the assignment instructions).
- o Students must illustrate their understanding of the material, which means that copying solutions from previous semesters or book solutions verbatim is not allowed and is considered a form of academic dishonesty.
- o ***Homework is meant to be a learning tool. If you are having difficulty, find help right away – don’t wait until you fall even further behind!***

**Participation points:**

- o Participation will be accumulated throughout the lectures through slido and other in-class activities.
- o Videos, podcasts, and lecture recordings may be available prior to class when there is an in-class activity.
- o In-class assignments and slido participation weights will vary depending on the effort required of students.

**Final Project:** To be developed towards the end of the course, including 15-20 minutes project presentations during the last week of classes.

- o Students produce a 3-5 page final report.
- o The topic should be approved by the instructor and presented in 5-7 minutes in the class.
