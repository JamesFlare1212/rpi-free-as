## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

An overview of the principles involved in the design and operation of supply chains with applications to manufacturing and service industries.

This course explores the design and operation of supply chain operations. We will address questions of network design, demand forecasting, aggregate planning, coordination, inventory management, routing, and scheduling in transportation. The lectures will tend to emphasize the theoretical aspects of these topics, with the development of quantitative approaches to aid in decision-making and managerial insights. Readings from the text will be supplemented through guest lectures, journal articles, and extra handouts.

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

**Assignments**

**Tests:** The students will have three Mini-exams, two exams throughout the semester and third exam will be on the last day of the class. The final exam covers all concepts throughout the semester.

**Homework Assignments:**

- o Throughout the semester exploratory assessment activities will be assigned to reinforce or expand on material that is covered in the lecture.
- o Four homework sets will be assigned.
- o Students may collaborate on the assignments, meaning students can discuss the assignments, but every student must complete and turn in his/her own written solutions in his/her own words (unless otherwise stated in the assignment instructions).
- o Students must illustrate their understanding of the material, which means that copying solutions from previous semesters or book solutions verbatim is not allowed and is considered a form of academic dishonesty. Homework is meant to be a learning tool.

- o *If you are having difficulty, find help right away – don’t wait until you fall even further behind!*

**Participation points:**

- o Participation will be accumulated throughout the lectures through slido (or other application) and other in-class activities.
- o Videos and lecture recordings may be available prior to class when there is an in-class activity.
- o In-class assignments and slido participation weights will vary depending on the effort required of students.

**Final Project:** To be developed towards the end of the course, including project presentations the last day of class.

- o ISYE 4210 students produce a 3-5 page final report.
- o ISYE 6610 students produce a short paper of the final project of 5-8 pages, including a literature review. For the literature review, students must read a minimum of 5 peer-reviewed papers that need to be submitted in the middle of the semester.
- o Please check the course syllabus for the due dates.

**Course Material on LMS**

**Homework Assignments:** Homework instructions will be progressively made available.

**Past tests and Homework:** Past tests and homework only offer *some* additional questions to practice. They should never be used as a guide on how to prepare for your tests.

**Missing Tests or Assignments:** Missing tests are only allowed if there is an emergency verified by the Dean of Student’s office. There are *no* makeups for tests. Missing assessments without official excuses will receive a zero.

**Excusal from Course Assignments and Examinations:** If you know ahead of time that you will miss an exam/assignment deadline, then you must make arrangements before the assessment is due.

*Each student has the opportunity to move the weight of a homework assignment to the weight of its associated exam. For example, if a student is not able to submit the homework assignment on time, then they can elect to have the homework weight transferred to the exam that the homework material is on. This option can be used multiple times throughout the semester AS LONG as the student documents in writing (via an email to the instructor) their intention to transfer homework weight to exam weight BEFORE the homework assignment is due.*

- o Late assignments (without arrangements before the assessment is due) will receive a zero unless an emergency arises that is approved by the Student Experience Office. Failure to submit will result in 25% off for each 24-hour period the assessment is late.

If an emergency arises and a student cannot submit assigned homework on or before the scheduled due date or cannot take an exam on the scheduled date, the student (or the Student Experience Office) MUST give notification to the instructor NO MORE THAN 48 HOURS AFTER the scheduled date. If you have an emergency, the campus is here to help, please coordinate with the Student Experience Office – 4th floor of Academy Hall, x8022, se@rpi.edu

**Exam Protocol:**

- o All exams are open book and notes, the use of a calculator is allowed and computers may be allowed.
- o All answers must be recorded on the exam answer sheet and full credit is awarded for correct numerical answers as well as a direct answer to the problem presented in the question.
- o Little to no credit is awarded for an effort that yields incorrect numerical values. The professional world measures performance on correct results, not effort working on the problem or data.

**Exam Regrading Request:**

- o Exams submitted for regrading must not be altered from the original work submitted for grading. Altering work for regrading is academic dishonesty and will be treated as such.
- o All requests must be made in writing by attaching a well-phrased note to the original work explaining in full the reason for the request and the points you think you deserve for your error. Requests for a regrade must be submitted no later than the next class meeting after the material is handed back to the class for the request to be considered.

**Special Testing Accommodations:**

All students with testing accommodations will be by the instructor and teaching assistant for all tests. Make sure to email your accommodation letter in the beginning of the semester in order to be added to the list.

**End-of-semester grades are not given**.

- o Instead, end-of-semester grades are calculated according to a student’s performance on the individual assessment requirements and the weights in this syllabus.
- o **Final grades are, final.** Therefore, final grades will not be changed unless a numerical error is made in computing your final grade. After final grades have been calculated, I will not respond to emails received requesting a grade change unless it is in response to a numerical calculation error. Please do not ask for extra credit or extra work after final exams have been administered.
