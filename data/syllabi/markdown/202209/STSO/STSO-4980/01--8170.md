### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

**What will we cover in this class? : Course Description**

This course will support the development of your senior thesis, the culminating project for the major in Science, Technology, and Society, and the major in Sustainability Studies. This course will be the first of two semesters proposing, conducting and presenting senior thesis research.

**How will we do this?**

We will form a supportive learning community as we engage in this research and writing process. Each of us will conduct empirical research that contributes to research in the field of Science and Technology Studies and Sustainability Studies. We will engage with the concepts and methods used in these interdisciplinary fields. Our class meetings will be formatted as a workshop. We will discuss our shared readings about the research and writing process, practice regular research writing exercises, conduct peer review, and share our research and writing approaches, challenges and, strategies. We will learn about the history of extractive research practices, alternative approaches to social science research, and research ethics. This class will prepare you to hit the ground running next semester. You will end this semester with a Research Proposal informed by research you will begin conducting this semester. Your proposal will be evaluated and we will outline a research plan before the end of the semester.

Our main focus this semester (Fall): 1) Defining research questions, 2) Conducting a review of existing literature, 3) Learning about, selecting, and practicing research methods, 4) Writing a research proposal, 5) Data collection

Next semester (Spring): 1) Data collection, 2) Analysis, 3) Reading and revisiting literature, and 4) Writing- new sections of the thesis and revising existing work as you collect new evidence, reflect on debates in the field, confront critiques of your work, and deepen your analysis.

**What will I learn in this class? : Learning Outcomes**

After the successful completion of this course you will have the tools to:

\- Delineate a research question

\- Construct a literature review that contextualizes and connects research questions and design

\- Design an ethical small-scale empirical research project using methods such as ethnographic participant observation, interviewing, archival research, and discourse analysis

\- Understand the relationship between data analysis and theory

\- Collaborate in thoughtful and constructive peer review and discussion

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
