## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This seminar examines the history of mental health in Western cultures from a sociological perspective. Numerous subjects pertaining to mental health/illness will be studied in their social, cultural, political, and philosophical contexts. Some topics include diagnosis, symptomatology, and therapeutics; asylums, confinement, and medicine as social control; psychiatry (and anti-psychiatry), the biomedical model, and psychopharmaceuticals; psychology and psychotherapy; radical, countercultural, and critical theories of mental health; race, ethnicity, feminism, and capitalism; the American prison system; social media, depression, and anxiety; mindfulness and meditation; and psychedelic-assisted therapy. Students will be encouraged to think critically and engage in dialogue with each other over the assigned texts. Course evaluation is based on careful readings of scholarly works, participation and attendance in lecture, the leading of class discussion, and two papers.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Learn the history of mental health/illness, including its various disciplines, practices, diagnostic and therapeutic philosophies, and its social contexts and impacts
- Gain familiarity with leading historical debates in mental health/illness and therapeutics
- Develop the tools to read closely and think critically and historically about the nexus of medicine, mental health, society, and the individual
- Hone thinking, writing, and orating skills
- Take a position on and compose written analysis of contemporary topics in mental health and wellbeing and connect them to their historical roots
