### Prerequisites or Other Requirements:

Facility with elementary linear algebra, with vector calculus at the level of MATH-4600, and with differential equations at the level of MATH-2400, is required. In particular, it is assumed that you are familiar with elementary aspects of PDEs such as separation of variables and Fourier series. Ability to use Matlab and a computer algebra system such as Maple will be helpful.

## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Topics to be covered

- First-order scalar PDEs: linear and nonlinear
- Second-order hyperbolic equations (wave equation and generalizations)
- Second-order elliptic equations (Laplace's equation and generalizations)
- Second-order parabolic equations (Diffusion equation and generalizations)
- Second-order PDEs: classification
- Systems of first-order PDEs
- Miscellaneous topics

## Course Text(s)

No specific text is required. A selected list of reference texts appears below.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Successful completion of the course should enable you to analyze the formation and solution of representative problems in the physical sciences that are modeled by PDEs.
- Successful completion of the course should enable you to analyze whether a PDE problem is well-posed.
- Successful completion of the course should enable you to achieve an understanding and appreciation of the qualitative properties of solutions of hyperbolic, parabolic and elliptic PDE models.
- Successful completion of the course should enable you to present written solutions to mathematical problems in a clear, concise and coherent fashion.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Homework assignments, two mid-term tests, optional final examination.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

There will be two mid-term take-home tests and an optional final examination. Tests are tentatively scheduled for the weeks of October 10 and December 5.  
The course grade will be based on a weighted average of performance in the homework (60%) and the two tests (40%). You will receive a provisional score out of a 100 based on the above formula at the end of the term. This will be your course score if you choose not to take the final examination. If you do opt for the (comprehensive) final examination, then the course score will be composed of the provisional score (75%) and the score in the final examination (25%). Numerical scores will be changed into letter grades at the end of the term according to the following scheme:  
A (93-100), A- (89-92), B^ (85-88), B (81-84), B- (77-80), C+ (72-76), C (68-71), C- (64-67), D (50-63), F (0-49).  
Your course grade will be determined entirely by your performance. Your need to get a good grade (to stay in school or keep your financial aid, for example) does not play a role. I reserve the right to move a borderline grade up at my discretion, should the record demonstrate a consistent upward trend or a consistent performance at a certain grade level with the exception of a rare mishap. The grading scheme is quite independent of the class average. Thus, you are not competing against your fellow students.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

### Other Course Policies:

Homework will be assigned approximately every other week, collected, marked and returned. Each assignment will be scored out of 100 points, unless otherwise indicated.  
Please write your solutions clearly and coherently, with the work displayed in a sequential manner and sufficient explanation provided so that your strategy and approach are transparent to the reader. It is enough to merely summarize routine manipulations involving algebra or calculus, but concepts and techniques covered in this course must be explained in detail. Any numerical computations involving MAPLE or MATLAB must be accompanied by a copy of your code or worksheet, as appropriate. Your score in the homework will depend on both mathematical correctness and clarity of presentation.  
Legible, handwritten solutions will be acceptable, but the use of a typesetting system such as LaTeX is strongly recommended. {Do not turn in your rough attempt at solving a problem; once you have worked out the solution, copy it neatly or typeset it before submission. Solutions that are illegible or lack sufficient explanation may be returned without grading.  
Posted solutions will be a good template for writing your own solutions.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Working together on homework assignments in small groups is encouraged, and you should feel free to discuss among the group how to approach a problem or how to carry out certain calculations. However, detailed sharing of solutions is prohibited, and work submitted for grading is expected to be your own rather than a copy of some one else's work. Finding a definition on line is fine, but handing in the entire solution to a problem found on line is plagiarism. The HW is intended to help you learn the subject, and is not a test of your web-surfing ability.
