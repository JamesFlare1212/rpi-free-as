## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

We will cover techniques and applications of integration, parametric equations and polar coordinates, infinite sequences and series, vectors and vector calculus, functions of several variables, and partial derivatives. This corresponds to material from chapters 7, 8, and 10 – 14.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Basic symbol manipulation skills pertaining to integration.
- Ability to state and explain basic Calculus definitions and theorems and their applications.
- Ability to describe curves parametrically and apply Calculus concepts and techniques to curves described in this way.
- Ability to express curves via polar equations and to solve Calculus problems in the polar coordinate system.
- Ability to apply Calculus to selected problems in Science, Engineering and Mathematics.
- Ability to use convergence tests to analyze the behavior of infinite series.
- Ability to represent functions using power series and solve problems using these representations.
- Ability to describe lines and planes in space in equation form and solve problems using these representations.

**Attendance**

Students are responsible for all material covered in class so attendance of every lecture is strongly recommended and encouraged. If a student is absent, they should check LMS regularly AND talk to a fellow classmate about the class notes, events, and announcements or stop by office hours. Sometimes details can elude LMS and a fellow classmate can do a better job of alerting you of class information.

**Exams:** Exam problems are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples. Full solutions to odd-numbered textbook problems can be found on Achieve at the end of the E-book.

Missed exams may only be made up if a student has an excused absence from the Student Success office AND has notified the instructor prior to the exam start time OR has made prior arrangements with the instructor. If the student has an excused absence from the Student Success office and notifies the instructor within 24 hours of the exam start time, the student will be allowed to make up the exam with a 20% grade penalty. Otherwise, students who miss an exam will receive a grade of 0 for that exam.

If arrangements have been made for a make-up exam, the student must make it up during that time or receive a grade of 0.

**Quizzes:** Quizzes will be administered during recitation every week (either Tuesday or Friday depending on which section you are in). The first week, the quiz will be a practice quiz. There are no makeup quizzes, but a lowest score or more may be dropped.

**Homework**: Achieve homework assignments will be due twice weekly on Mondays and Thursdays at 11:59 PM and will be graded automatically by the system. Students may submit these assignments late up to two days after the original deadline, but a penalty will be applied to the score. A 10% penalty will apply to assignments submitted the day after the deadline and a 20% penalty will apply to assignments submitted two days after the deadline. No other due date extensions will be granted for any reason. To further compensate for this, several homework assignment scores will be dropped at the end of the semester.

Additional problems will be assigned from the textbook and will be posted on LMS. These are mandatory, but they will not be collected nor graded. Success in the course depends on successful completion of all homework, both Achieve homework and textbook homework. As stated under **Exams**, exam problems are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples.

**Grade Appeals**

If upon reviewing an exam a student feels that his or her work was graded incorrectly, then the student may submit a grading appeal to the instructor. The student should submit, in writing, an explanation of the grading issue along with the original exam. The student should not make any marks on the original exam. Students have one week after the exam is returned to submit an appeal.

Change to the grading scheme may be made at the discretion of the instructor.

**Calculator Policy**:

Calculators will not be allowed on exams. Problems will be designed so that this does not create an issue. Occasionally, a calculator may be required to complete a homework problem, but students should not rely on calculators for simple arithmetic or trigonometric calculations.

**Classroom Decorum**

Phones may not be used during class and should be silent. Computers and tablets may not be used for anything other than taking notes. Students who are unable to courteously comply with these policies may be asked to leave the room.

**Health and Safety Protocols**

Students are required to follow the health and safety protocols of RPI, which are subject to change throughout the semester. Students who are not cleared for campus access are prohibited from attending class. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:** Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://rpi.app.box.com/s/d4ndpn3f7qdwxh9jaz5n1hkkr0mnd25e) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams must be the student’s own. Students may use their textbook and notes during exams, but the use of online resources outside of LMS is prohibited. Communicating with other people in any way during exams about the content of the exam is prohibited. Students may discuss homework with other students, TAs, and instructors, but the work submitted must be the student’s own.

The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
