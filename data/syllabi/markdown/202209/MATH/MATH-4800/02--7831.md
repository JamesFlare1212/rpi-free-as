## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Numerical Analysis by Timothy Sauer (3rd edition; 2nd edition is also fine)

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Topics from Chapters 0-6, including floating point arithmetic, solving linear and nonlinear (scalar or system of) equations, interpolation and data fitting, numerical integration, numerical methods for differential equations; possibly also the topics on eigenvalue problem and/or unconstrained optimization in Chapters 12-13.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- To understand how computers represent numbers, the computer arithmetic, their impact and significance to numerical computing
- To understand the basic concepts in numerical computing, such as conditioning, stability and convergence
- To learn and mathematically understand commonly used numerical algorithms in scientific computing, and to carry out mathematical derivation and numerical analysis for these algorithms
- To implement and to evaluate standard algorithms, and more importantly, to be able to interpret/analyze numerical results and observations, and to extract useful information and draw meaningful conclusions;
- To be able to choose, or even to design (if the need arises), numerical methods to solve a given problem

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Two midterm exams, one final exam; Homework assignments.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Collaborations:  
For homework assignments, you are encouraged to discuss with other students. However, the work you  
turn in must be written by yourself and represent your own understanding on the subjects. If your  
work involves significant help from other people, online sources, reference books or papers, or the codes  
from the textbook or shared by the instructor (yes you can modify the codes and use them in your  
homework), it is required that you explicitly state this in your report to avoid penalty in grade.  
It is strictly forbidden to share or exchange computer les, or to copy any part of the  
homework solutions from someone else. Such behavior will be considered the highest breach of academic  
integrity. Any violation will result in a grade of zero for the homework assignment (of all involved  
individuals if there is any), and can land you in the Dean of Students Office.  
For exams, there is no collaboration of any kind permitted. Any violation will result in a grade of  
zero for the exam, and a report will also be made to the Dean of Students Office.
