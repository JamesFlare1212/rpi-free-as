## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Functions, limits, continuity, derivatives, implicit differentiation, related rates, maxima and minima, elementary transcendental functions, introduction to definite integral with applications to area and volumes of revolution. (from the [Rensselaer Catalog](http://catalog.rpi.edu/))

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate basic and advanced symbol manipulation skills.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to convert between calculus concepts and their graphical, numerical, and symbolic representations.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to apply calculus concepts to selected problems in science and engineering.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to apply certain fundamental theorems, basic concepts, and rules from calculus to solve symbolic and graphical problems.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to present problems related to the theory of calculus and its applications to science and engineering disciplines.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are responsible for all material covered in lectures and recitations whether they are present or not. Attendance is expected, but will not be checked. Attendance is required for mentoring sessions and is part of the mentoring grade.

### Other Course Policies:

**Exams:** Exams will be given during lecture with the exception of DSS students. Attendance will be taken at all exams using sign in sheets. Details about each exam, including the material to be covered on the exam and time limit, will be announced on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/) when the date of the exam is near. The dates for Exams 1–4 are listed above in the section **Grading.** Details on exams for DSS students are given below in the **DSS** section.

**Make-up exams will only be given to students with an** [**excused absence documented by the Student Success office**](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/current-students/academic-and-personal-support/requesting-excused-absence) **or, if the absence is due to an athletic competition, documentation from a coach must be provided.** Thus, if a student misses an exam because they overslept, didn’t know where the exam was, or didn’t know there was an exam, to give a few examples, the student may not take the make-up exam and will receive a score of 0 on the exam.

**Missing an exam should not be taken lightly.** Thus, students who miss an exam due to an excused absence must make every effort to notify the instructor ***before the exam begins*** to avoid grade penalties.

- If a student misses an exam due to an excused absence and notifies the instructor before the exam begins, then the student may take a make-up exam without penalty.
- If a student misses an exam due to an excused absence and notifies the instructor after the exam begins but before 11:59 PM on the Saturday after the exam, then the student may take a make-up exam and a 30% penalty will be applied to the make-up exam score.
- If a student misses an exam due to an excused absence and fails to notify the instructor before 11:59 PM on the Saturday after the exam, then the student may not take the make-up exam and receives a score of 0 on the exam.
- If a student misses an exam and does not have an excused absence, then the student may not take the make-up exam and receives a score of 0 on the exam.

It is the sole responsibility of the student to notify the instructor to make arrangements for a make-up exam when an exam is missed due to an excused absence.

**Homework:** Achieve homework assignments will be due once or twice weekly on Tuesdays and Fridays at 11:59 PM and will be graded automatically by the system. Students may submit these assignments late up to two days after the original deadline, but a penalty will be applied to the score. A 10% penalty will apply to assignments submitted the day after the deadline and a 20% penalty will apply to assignments submitted two days after the deadline. No other due date extensions will be granted for any reason. To further compensate for this, several homework assignment scores will be dropped at the end of the semester.

Additional problems will be assigned from the textbook and will be posted on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/). Though these problems will not be collected nor graded, they should not be viewed as optional. Success in the course depends on successful completion of all homework, both Achieve homework and textbook homework. Exam problems are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples. Full solutions to odd-numbered textbook problems can be found on Achieve. When logged in to our Achieve course, view content by assignments and scroll to the bottom of the page. The student solutions manual for chapters 1–6 may be found below No Due Dates.

**Quizzes:** On most weeks, there will be a quiz on Wednesdays. The quiz will consist of several of the simpler problems from previously completed homeworks (Achieve and Textbook). Some of these problems may be likely exam questions, but to get full credit on these problems on the exam they must be completed without errors. This an opportunity for students to test their own ability to do these problems without making mistakes. The way students complete this quiz will vary depending on whether they have been assigned to mentoring or not. Review the information in the **Quizzes** content area on LMS to determine whether or not you were assigned a mentor and for additional details on how to receive full credit for this portion of the course.

- **Students with Mentors** will take the quiz in their mentoring sessions and will be shown how to upload their quizzes to Gradescope. Mentors will provide written feedback on the completed quizzes in Gradescope.
- **Students without Mentors** will take the quiz on Achieve between 8AM and 6PM on Wednesday. On the quiz, all the questions must be completed before submitting the quiz and seeing whether the answers are correct. Questions are graded on a no partial credit basis and students will have three attempts to get a perfect score.

**Disability Services for Students (DSS):** Students with a memorandum from the [Disability Services for Students (DSS)](http://studenthealth.rpi.edu/disabilityservices) office identifying accommodations for exams must present the memorandum to the instructors by Friday, September 10. Providing the accommodations requires advance planning. Students will not be granted accommodations unless a memorandum from DSS has been received.

**Calculator Policy:** Calculators will not be allowed on exams. Problems will be designed so that this does not create an issue. Occasionally, a calculator may be required to complete a homework problem, but students should not rely on calculators for simple arithmetic or trigonometric calculations.

**Health and Safety Protocols:** Students are required to follow the health and safety protocols of RPI, which are subject to change throughout the semester.

**Visitors:** Visitors are prohibited during all class meetings. Only students who are registered for the class should be present in the classroom. Students may only attend class meetings at the time and place listed on their schedule on SIS.

**Classroom Decorum:** Students must be on time for class and should refrain from leaving and reentering the classroom during class. If a student has a legitimate reason for arriving late to class or being excused early from class, then they should discuss this with the instructor before class. Phones may not be used during class and should be silent. Computers and tablets may not be used for anything other than taking notes. Students who are unable to courteously comply with these policies may be asked to leave the room.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://rpi.app.box.com/s/d4ndpn3f7qdwxh9jaz5n1hkkr0mnd25e) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams must be the student’s own. A student may not change an answer on an exam before submitting it for an appeal. In addition, no calculators, computers, tablets, open books, open notes, phones, headphones, or smartwatches may be used during exams. Talking or communicating with other students in any way during exams is also prohibited. Students may discuss homework with other students, mentors, TAs, and instructors, but the work submitted must be the student’s own.

The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
