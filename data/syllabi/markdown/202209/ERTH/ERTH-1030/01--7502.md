## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is intended to provide students who are not majoring in a science or engineering field an introduction to the natural sciences. For our purposes, the term

*"natural sciences"*

is taken to mean the sciences of the natural world, as distinct from the man-made (or artificial) world, although it will be clear the two are related. This type of course is

*comprehensive*

in that it covers the basics needed to understand many of the natural sciences --biology, chemistry, geology, and physics --at scales ranging from that of the atom to that of the universe. Of course, the coverage of each discipline cannot, of course, be comprehensive. There is just too much ground to cover. In this course we will focus on the most fundamental of sciences --Physics, Chemistry, Astronomy and Planetary Science -- in which very simple systems may be discussed. With this background, we can later touch on those sciences that draw upon these basics to examine progressively more complex systems

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

## Course Content

### Content Details:

- Science Basics: The Scientific Method, measurements, data, correlations, proportionality
- Motion, forces, acceleration
- Laws of motion; momentum; gravitation
- Work, energy and power
- Heat and Thermodynamics
- Electromagnetism
- Waves and Vibrations
- Structure of the Atom
- Atoms in combination: chemical bonds
- The Nucleus
- Stars and Cosmology
- Relativity
- Quantum Mechanics and Elementary Particles
- (if time allows) Plate Tectonics

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand how the Scientific Method works
- Understand how scientists use units, correlations, and proportionality
- Understand and apply basic principles of classical physics such as the laws of motion and gravitation, work, energy, power, thermodynamics, and electromagnetism
- Understand and apply basic principles of modern physics such as quantum mechanics and relativity
- Understand the fundamentals of wave propagation
- Understand the basic structure and properties of the atom
- Understand basics of chemical bonds
- Understand basic cosmology
- Understand the fundamentals of Plate Tectonics

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Homework:** There will be about 7 homework assignments during the term (roughly one every two weeks). Questions and problems will be taken primarily from the lecture notes; their purpose will be to get you to think about material presented in lecture and give you practice in problem-solving.

**Exams:** There will be 3 exams (two during the term and a final on the last regular day of class; there is no “final” exam). Exams are cumulative and can cover all previous material from lecture and readings, although most of the questions will be on material covered since the previous exam. Expect about 10% of the point value on the second exam to come from material covered on the first exam, and 20% of the point value on the third exam to come from material covered on the preceding two exams. The exams will include short-answer (possibly involving simple calculations), multiple-choice and true/false questions.

**Participation:** Attendance in class is not required - and will not be monitored on a day-to-day basis - but this class really works a lot better if everyone actively participates. Also, if you participate in class, you will find the exams and homework much easier, and it will always work to your advantage at the end of the semester if I have some reason to conclude that you have been taking the class seriously. Consider the "participation" percentage as a potential penalty if it appears you made little to no effort to communicate.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

Based on experience, here are some suggestions for success:

1. Read about topics in the text before they are covered in lecture. This should help you spend more time listening and less time taking notes, so you may get more out of the lectures.
2. Ask questions when you don't understand. The class will be small and every attempt will be made to create a friendly atmosphere, so please don't hesitate to speak up. Don't hesitate to contact the instructor by phone or e-mail.
3. Review graded exams and homework as they become available.
4. Pay attention to the material in the lectures (in class / on-line). Don't just go by the book.
5. Excuses always sound better if I hear them BEFORE something is due.
6. You are responsible for knowing what goes on in class. If you miss some part of class, you need to find out what happened. Talk to a friend, talk to me, check the web site.
7. Attend the lectures (and bring your brain as often as possible!). The lectures will follow the text fairly closely in terms of the material covered, but in many cases we will use alternative approaches designed to strengthen your grasp of difficult concepts.
