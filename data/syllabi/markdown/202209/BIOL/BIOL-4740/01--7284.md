## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The first part of the semester will be spent learning about mammalian cell biology and associated cell culture techniques. You will use techniques that include PCR, immunocytochemistry, and Western blotting to obtain information about gene expression in cells. You will develop the experimental design for an **independent project** during the first part of the semester and submit a formal proposal for your project along with appropriate protocols. In the second part of the semester, you will carry out the research you proposed. At the end of the semester, you will present your findings in a formal presentation to the class and write a publication-quality manuscript. You will also be expected to keep accurate notes of your methods, results, and conclusions. Recitation time will be used for extended lab experiments. Time will be taken during some lab periods to introduce or discuss material as a class. **This is a communication intensive course.**

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Upon successfully completing this course, students will demonstrate the following:

- Proficiency in content knowledge concerning various cell biology research techniques
- Ability to design experiments and develop laboratory protocols necessary to complete these experiments
- Ability to collect, organize, and analyze data through the maintenance of a laboratory notebook that clearly documents laboratory procedures and presents results using well-labeled figures
- Ability to communicate experimental findings through a presentation and a publication-quality manuscript and class presentation
- Ability to critically analyze scientific literature
- Professional behavior and independence in a laboratory environment

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Lab Notebooks**

- ­ Your electronic lab notebook will be provided to you as a Google Docs file that you can edit; physical notebooks will not be used. Therefore, you **MUST bring your laptop to all in-person labs** in order to record that day's work.
- ­ Good record keeping skills are an important part of your professional development. You must keep complete notes during every lab, as most of the work we do builds on previous work throughout the semester. Further information can be found under the *Lab Notebook* section on LMS.
- ­ Klein will check lab notebooks twice this semester. These dates will **not** be announced in advance, so **your lab notebook should be ready at all times.** When your notebook is checked, you will be asked to export your Google Docs notebook as a PDF file and submit it to LMS. More detailed instructions are given on LMS.

**Presentations**

- ­ Students will be expected to a give **journal club** style presentation of a peer reviewed scientific research articles on the date marked on the *Lab Schedule*. This type of presentation is largely informal but should include a reasonable background section, an analysis of the article's findings, and critiques of the article. This presentation is meant to be given by each student individually; Dr. Klein will announce any changes to the policy.
- ­ Each group of students will present their **independent project** work at the end of the semester as part of their Project Presentation.
- ­ All presentations are due at the **start of lab** on the days specified in the *Lab Schedule*.

**Written Assessments**

- ­ Written assessments, including quizzes, are due on LMS on the dates/times specified in the *Lab Schedule*. Due date times can be checked on LMS.
- ­ Collaborating with students on non-group activities like quizzes is **NOT** See *Grading Policies & Academic Dishonesty* for more details.

**Lab Citizenship**

- ­ A lab requires its members to show respect to other lab members, their experiments, and their shared equipment. Each student is required to be a participating lab citizen and demonstrate their participation by (1) completing their part of shared tasks appropriately, (2) keeping all supplies and experiments in their appropriate locations, (3) discarding all waste appropriately, especially biohazards, (4) maintaining a clean and safe work space, and (5) maintaining the lab for our use and the use of other courses in the same room.
- ­ Every student is entitled to full participation in class without interruption. Therefore, all students are expected to be in lab and **prepared to begin on time**. Disruption of class, whether by latecomers, disruptive devices, or inconsiderate behavior, is not acceptable.
- ­ Each infraction of lab policy, including the rules listed under the *COVID-19 Precautions & Restrictions* and *Lab Safety* sections, will result in the **deduction of 1% from your final grade**. You can earn multiple deductions in a single day if your behavior is poor enough.

- You are expected to attend all lab periods and recitation. If a lab is held remotely, you will be expected to attend lab via WebEx during the normal lab and recitation meeting times.

If you know of an upcoming absence, please contact Dr. Klein and your lab partner as soon as possible to discuss options.

**COVID-19 PRECAUTIONS & RESTRICTIONS**

**Masks**

- ­ **N95** **or KN95 respirators (masks) are to be worn by all students and faculty in BIOL-4740 lab spaces**. Masks must be worn properly, covering both the mouth and nose.
- ­ Students who violate this policy by refusing to wear an N95 or KN95 mask or refusing to wear one correctly will not be allowed to participate in lab and will be recorded as absent. As a reminder, absences will result in a **deduction of 5%** from your lab grade per absence.

**Cleaning of Spaces**

- ­ Students are required to disinfect their work surfaces in the lab before they begin work and after they have finished for the day. 70% ethanol will be provided for disinfection. Disinfecting lab chairs and stools is also encouraged. This is a standard lab policy in tissue culture labs irrespective of COVID-19 precautions.

**Student Health**

- ­ Students who test positive for COVID-19, under quarantine, or suspect they have contracted COVID-19 must report this to Student Life. Once faculty notification is made, Dr. Klein will make every reasonable effort to accommodate the student’s absence and will communicate that accommodation directly to the student.
- ­ To maintain accurate attendance tracking, **students must submit** **documentation for an excused absence to LMS** (see *Attendance Policies*).

Refusal to comply with any appropriate request to limit the spread of COVID-19 will be treated as a classroom disruption and disciplinary actions and sanctions will be taken through the judicial process in the Student Handbook.

**LAB SAFETY**

- Follow all rules that Dr. Klein issues in writing or verbally.
- Do not engage in any activities that will endanger yourself or those around you.
- **Wear gloves** when interacting with any laboratory equipment and **change gloves often**. You will be provided gloves.
- **You must wear safety glasses or goggles** at all times during lab except when using microscopes - disinfect eye pieces of microscopes with ethanol before and after use.
- **Lab coats** are required; you must purchase your own lab coat. Wear clothing that entirely covers the legs and torso.
- Always **wear you N95 or KN95 mask correctly** while in lab spaces.
- You must wear **shoes that enclose** **the entire foot**; footwear like sandals and crocs are not allowed.
- Report any spills or accidents to Dr. Klein immediately.
- When finished, you must clean your work area and return it to the condition you found it in; **disinfect your work areas with 70% ethanol**.
- Follow traffic flow guides in lab; do not crowd other students while they are working at a shared location or on shared equipment.
- Use of **cell phones** is not allowed in lab except when indicated by Dr. Klein.
- Do not adjust valves or any components connected to **pressurized vessels** like liquid nitrogen storage tanks or CO2 tanks.
- Dispose of **biohazardous material**, **chemical hazards**, **glass**, and **metal sharps** in the designated locations.
- Book bags, backpacks, and other personal items must be stored **under your lab bench**, away from other student's belongings. Be sure to avoid making your personal belongings a tripping hazard.
- Violation of any rules stated here, in your lab manual, or verbally stated by Dr. Klein will count as deductions for poor lab citizenship, and major violations can have you removed from the lab.
