### Prerequisites or Other Requirements:

Prerequisites or Other Requirements

Corequisite: Students in ARCH 4830 Comprehensive Design Studio 2 are required to co-register for this class, ARCH 4540. In Spring semesters, this will include the graduate level course co-requisite, ARCH 6620 paired with ARCH 5380.

PP1 is a co-requisite for students taking ARCH-4830 Comprehensive Design Studio 2 (CDS2). The CDS2 Studio attempts to realize the design process through the design development phase and into construction documents, integrating all aspects that lead to actual construction. Students must be concerned with building systems including structures, material assemblies and services, and how these systems inform design decisions. As additional design disciplines, students design a code-compliant building and develop construction documents for all or major portions of the work.

Because students are expected to take the material from the PP1 course and use it to inform studio project development, ARCH-4830 and ARCH-4540 are conceived as complementary to each other. Students who are not Architecture majors may take PP1 without also taking the IDD Studio. While they may not benefit from immediate application of the course material in a design setting, they will acquire a general and logical understanding of how codes, sustainability, costs and conduct inform the design process.

## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

An introduction to architectural practice as related to accomplishing design projects. An overview of professional obligations, registration and conduct, architects’ roles in project delivery, and office organization and management for delivering professional services. In-depth examination of architects’ responsibilities for health, safety, and welfare in design; building code requirements for fire protection, life safety, and accessibility; economics of building systems and assemblies; design and construction contracts; and design documentation.

Students must obtain a copy of the following texts, both should be available at the RPI Union Bookstore:

- Building Codes Illustrated, Sixth Edition, A Guide to Understanding the International Building Code, by Ching and Winkel;

Students will need to work with an on-line reader version of the 2018 International Building Code (3rd printing as adopted by New York State), a.k.a. the 2020 “Uniform Code” of NYS. Please note that NYS has Uniform Code Supplements that change a few sections, but none should affect our project developments. Please note the on-line version of the code through the State of New York is not perfect, but it should be suitable for our reference needs in Professional Practice I this semester. There are two ways to view the applicable code: https://up.codes/viewer/new_york/ibc-2018 or https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/NYSBC2020P1

As it pertains to estimating, we will make use of construction cost data from recent publications to quantify material and labor costs associated with a fictional building project as well as the projects of the students in IDD.

Not required, but “Recommended-to-Own” texts are:

- Professional Practice: A Guide to Turning Designs into Buildings, by Paul Segal
- Architectural Building Codes, by James G. Scott

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Regulatory Context (NAAB SC.3): students will understand the fundamental principles of life safety, land use, and current laws and regulations that apply to buildings and sites in the United States, and the evaluative process architects use to comply with those laws and regulations as part of a project.
- 2\. Technical Knowledge (NAAB SC.4): students will understand the established and emerging systems, technologies, and assemblies of building construction, and the methods and criteria architects use to assess those technologies against the design, economics, and performance objectives of projects.
- 3\. Design Synthesis (NAAB SC.5): students will develop the ability to make design decisions within architectural projects while demonstrating synthesis of user requirements, regulatory requirements, site conditions, and accessible design, and consideration of the measurable environmental impacts of their design decisions.
- 4\. Building Integration (NAAB SC.6): students will develop the ability to make design decisions within architectural projects while demonstrating integration of building envelope systems and assemblies, structural systems, environmental control systems, life safety systems, and the measurable outcomes of building performance.
- 5\. Health, Safety and Welfare in the Built Environment (NAAB SC.1): students will understand the impact of the built environment on human health, safety, and welfare at multiple scales, from buildings to cities.
- 6\. Professional Practice (NAAB SC.2): students will understand professional ethics, the regulatory requirements, the fundamental business processes relevant to architecture practice in the United States, and the forces influencing change in these subjects.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance in person is required unless otherwise arranged ahead of class. This is an in-person course; any students unable to attend in person must submit lecture notes based on posted slideshows and apply lessons to studio project for credit. LMS will also be used to gauge access time by those students not attending class. As mentioned in both the syllabus and the first lecture of Introduction, this counts for 20% of your grade. This translates to roughly 1 point for each lecture, field trip (even though they are most likely virtual this semester) or desk crit session.

Since topics presented in this class require extensive explanation and discussion of the subtleties of Professional Practice I, **attendance is mandatory**. Unexcused absences from class WILL affect your grade negatively. Students are responsible for preparing themselves with the necessary texts and handouts for each class. If any school-wide lectures fall on the day of a class, class attendance is optional if attending the school-wide lecture, but students will still be responsible for obtaining the lecture information from the professor or another student. **The class may be required to attend the school-wide lecture in lieu of having class lecture; if so, attendance will be counted via lecture attendance recorded by the school.**

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own.
