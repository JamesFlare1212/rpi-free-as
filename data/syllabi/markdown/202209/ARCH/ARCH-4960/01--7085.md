## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This seminar will focus mainly in Argentina, Chile, and Brazil, and aims to identify and critically think about some current issues from a multidisciplinary approach. We will use an array of materials including works of literature, films, music, and essays paying particular attention to the enduring legacies and challenges of some specific historical issues through topics such as mestizaje, hybridity, informality, gender, changing roles of women, and expressions of popular culture. The materials used in this course are meant to serve as triggers for class discussion. As any attempt to affix the constant shifting field of what we call Latin America would be partial. Our task will not be to find the “right” answer or a “definition” but to let the works speak to us in unexpected ways, question ourselves, and help us to revise preconceptions we might have in order to get a better understanding of Latin America in the contemporary global discourses.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\) The student will have an introductory knowledge of cultural and theoretical issues that are currently shaping the intellectual discussion in Latin America.
- 2\) The student will have an introductory knowledge of topics, issues in discussion, and recent developments in contemporary Latin America.
- 3\) The student will be familiarized with works of Latin American canonical culture and popular cultures.
- 4\) The student will demonstrate the ability to critically think, analyze, and contextualize disparate sources of information from different fields concerning Latin America.
- 5\) The student will demonstrate the ability to gather, asses, and comparatively evaluate relevant information in order to support conclusions related to specific assignments and projects.
- 6\) The student will apply effective oral, written, and visual communication skills to present a coherent and sustained argument in specific topics.
- 7\) The student will be able to articulate her/his/their insights about the readings/viewings.
- 8\) The student will respectfully receive and respond to other’s comments.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Course Requirements:** One weekly discussion seminar where students discuss issues emerging in presentations, readings and viewings, and present updates of their work. Students are required to complete the assigned readings/viewings in advance and come to class prepared to participate actively. Students must provide weekly brief reports/questions of the assigned material and may be asked to lead class discussions at least once per semester. As core assignment students will work in the development of a final essay of 1200-1600 words or a photographic essay on a topic of their choice (upon faculty approval).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:** Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Violations this trust undermine the educational process. Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. The breach of this trust will merit a penalty of “No Credits” in the first occurrence and a failing grade in this course if the issue repeats a second time. The violator(s) will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.

In this class, all presentations and submissions represent the student’s work.

Proper credit for work, text or images not your own is mandatory.

SUBMISSION OF ANY ASSIGNMENT THAT IS IN VIOLATION OF THIS POLICY WILL RESULT IN A PENALTY OF “NO CREDITS” IN THE FIRST CASE (AND THE ASSIGNMENT, PROJECT, OR EXAM CONCERNED) AND AN AUTOMATIC “F” (FAIL) FOR THE ENTIRE COURSE IF THE ISSUE REPEATS A SECOND TIME.

If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
