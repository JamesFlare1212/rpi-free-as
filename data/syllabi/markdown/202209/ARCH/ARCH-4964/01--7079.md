## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This series of digital workshops is meant to be an introduction to Building Information Modeling, primarily using Revit 2022, but also introducing Vectorworks and ARCHICAD. We will approach design using a continuous workflow process to model and produce working design development / construction document drawings. We will explore how to use the software most effectively without limiting creativity.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Understand the relevance and benefits of Building Information Modeling as a methodology and continuous workflow used in building design and construction.
- 2\. Utilize BIM software as a continuous workflow, with a shared Central Model in which multiple team members can model/draw simultaneously.
- 3\. Be able to assemble a Design Development level Construction Document drawing set using proper graphic techniques, title blocks, schedules, and annotations.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1\. Understand the relevance and benefits of Building Information Modeling as a methodology used in building design and construction. - Weekly Assignments; Midterm and Final Tech Set Submissions  
2\. Utilize BIM software as a continuous workflow, with a shared Central Model in which multiple team members can model/draw simultaneously. - Regular BIM Model Uploads/Checks; Final Model Submission  
3\. Be able to assemble a Design Development level Construction Document drawing set using proper graphic techniques, title blocks, schedules, and annotations. - Weekly Assignments; Midterm and Final Tech Set Submissions

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process. Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust will merit a failing grade in this course and the violator(s) will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.
