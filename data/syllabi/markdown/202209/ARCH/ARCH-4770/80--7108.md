## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The studio will investigate where the private and public limits lay within a built environment. We will question traditional mechanisms of social organization by proposing alternative systems of ownership that embrace the new realities of demographics, financial constraints, and shared economies. The studio will encourage a rigorous questioning of past and present norms and seek innovative answers to how to reconcile shared living with basic private needs.

Design exercises will explore how physical and non-physical thresholds can modulate innovative transitions between private and public. The studio will look to reinterpret the relationship between public and private in the context of a compressed single (1 Body ‘minimum basic dwelling’ (project1), and the opportunities that could emerge from the compression for new types of social/communal/ public interiors (project2) within a shared site in the urban landscape of Troy.

Overlap between the private single body dwellings will be encouraged whether they take the form of a shared semi-private interiors or shared semi-private exteriors, thus transforming the private single body dwelling into an assembly that straddles between the private and the public.

Looking into PUBLIC INTERIORS We will concentrate on the social Interaction (2+ Bodies up to unlimited density) and design for a public interiority that starts with domestic shared spaces (kitchen, laundry) but goes beyond amenities to create new possibilities for living by defining flexible spaces that have agency in the city and the community. What is the scale of this space and what are the spatial characteristics that define it as space of gathering are questions we’ll focus on as we collectively bring together an assembly of individual dwellings searching for a whole where its totality is greater than it’s individual parts.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Project-Based Assessment: will use deliverables such as printed research books, project books, presentation  
drawings, and physical models and will be based on completeness and creative effort.

Process-Based Assessment: will use interim reviews of 2d and 3d deliverables and be based on the success in developing concepts and the completeness of quantifiable creative effort.

Work in Studio¬-Charrette: the majority of the work should be generated in studio. The time of the class will be spent on a charrette mode workshop

Desk Critics: timed individual conversations

Students will be expected to actively contribute their verbal and/or written feedback on a weekly basis at desk crits and internal class pin ups

Pin-Up Presentations: Pin ups are all studio conversations. Design discussions are open. Free speech, friendly relations, and intense exchange of information and opinion should characterize these pin ups conversations. All students are required to present and participate. Notes on the feedback given is due the following class.

Quality of time spent on the studio work is more important than quantity, and students should manage their time wisely in order to effectively complete all of their work. Students are expected to fully participate in class discussions. This necessarily requires that each student complete all required research, readings and assignments on time.

Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach.
