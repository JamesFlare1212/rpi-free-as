## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Overview

The project of the INCEPTION Studio is to fully design - and play - an immersive spatial experience.

The studio will explore the potential of designing, creating, and translating the paradoxical and impactful aspects of virtual experience we are accustomed to within physical reality.

The range of spaces studied/tested will encompass architectural memories from walking tours or through projection from and into architectural drawings, as well as personal reconstructions of past memories and dreams - including near-implausible or ‘suspension of disbelief’ experiences drawn from scientific theories or science fiction.

The studio will look into the potential of virtual architecture platforms and immersive conditions to allow inhabitation of spaces that are not easily rendered through 3D space and matter, but which have a clear space in the imagination, including: spatial paradoxes, 3 1/2D & 4D space, non-spatial dimensions, perspectives, projections, parallax, infinite spaces, juxtapositions, simultaneities, and network structures.

There is an opportunity in this studio to engage topics or structures related with the current events fostered by the pandemic, which have substantially changed the nature and frequency of use of physical spaces and increased the use of virtual spaces and protocols (gestures, rules).

We will focus on understanding and developing structural and ‘material’ tectonic potentials to experience and realize these qualities, while inquiring upon their influence on the body and behavior, and creating critical assessments of the standalone quality of the experiences, as architectural bodies themselves.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Course Goals/Objectives

Introduction to concepts, technologies and materials involved in the production of virtual architecture experiences. Introduction and discussion of concepts that relate architectural and cultural experience with interactive, immersive design.

Develop critical understanding of the role of virtual and immersive constructs and techniques to inquire and develop ways in which architectural experience can be produced and complex concepts of site be formulated.

Learn formats that integrate audiovisual and interactive components in an immersive environment, and test their impact on spatial experience.

Explore constructions that integrate spatial and time-based relationship to produce particular outcomes of experience (and/or) representation.

Develop, complete, test and evaluate a final design which is an interactive environment that embodies a thesis for the relationship between a concept goal and a final experienced outcome.

Develop potential insights and analysis from this inquiry and production to architectural design culture at large.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

STUDIO THEMES

Architectures of: Paradox, Non-Sense, Palimpsest

In “The Architecture of Deconstruction: Derrida’s Haunt”, Mark Wigley declares Architecture to be the discipline where the dream of Deconstructive Discourse falls apart. His argument being that the intrinsic vocabulary of architecture - that of constructibility - is the basis upon which analytical thought in philosophy and cultural disciplines is founded upon. And the phrase I just used, supposedly demonstrates it.

There has been nevertheless a continuous legacy of interest by architects in using architectural representation to inquire on how to bring forth aspects of characters which are present in a situation and that are important to dimensions of ‘constructibility’, but which cannot be rendered in the traditional dimensions and techniques of tridimensional space.

Inevitably, concepts of analysis and narrative collude here, as translation devices put themselves between reality and its representation.

Several Architecture examples which have been called Deconstructionist - for the reasons above mentioned - have difficulty occupying the same conceptual room, while being nevertheless a possible section of this interest.

Libeskind’s Micromegas research drawings are impossible perspectives, because the line has too much memory of moments past of its own perception: too busy making itself belong to more than one moment in space.

This same refusal to accept all spatial and other variables such as gravity, time dimension or distance, appears in Eisenman’s Palimpsests and diagram research and in the Endless Cycles imprinted in Kiesler’s houses.

One could also see this in Carlo Scarpa’s detail obsession, an attempt to zoom in to the microscopical dimension of the infinite contained in the turning of a corner, or in the connection between two material forces subjected to geometry. Or still in Gaudi’s relentless extension of tension forces in and out of the symbolic.

All these examples, stage Architecture and constructibility as something that also belongs in/to the mind and restlessly gnaw at what are its perceived limitations when matter stands in the way of other expressible dimensions.

Incommensurable Realities

There is, contemporarily, a great demand for representing apparent contradictions of real life.

As we have evolved ever better models of reality, we find them increasingly doubled or paired - models of complexity, matter, weather or the universe continue to challenge comprehension and are often dual: needing either two or more non-integrated theories to explain their behaviors or only working within a particular framework of measuring that evolves a complete new code, logic and measuring instrumentation. Hofstadter’s model of the strange loop for consciousness is an example of these state of affairs.

As known entities grow increasingly smaller and larger - nanoscale and hyper objects - the tendency might continue to be that of accepting, temporarily, assemblies of explanations for divergences of behavior in what should be a coherent whole. The 90’s and early 2000’s digital architecture renaissance in architecture supposedly engaged this problem through evolving alternative geometries and architectures sourced from the same original models which were trying to solve these ambiguities. This period was specially influenced by the connection with new media theory. Brian Massumi, a new media theorist, coming from a cultural studies perspective - which influenced architecture theory through translations of Deleuze and Guattari’s work - talks about a “duplicity of form”: the double way in which agents receive the form or image of something. This happens in “spontaneously and simultaneously in two orders of reality, one local and learned or intentional, the other nonlocal and self-organizing”. Meaning, the aprehension of the world is made through two simultaneous, non-integrated, separate ways: intention and affect, meaning and sense, perception and experience.

Alex Galloway says that the interface of the 90’s, perhaps never left. In The Interface Effect (2012), he argues that a condition of ambiguity is important also to the figure of the interface, for it to continue to mediate representation in itself as well as represent the ‘window’ (threshold).

The navigation of the incommensurable is also an important character now as Virtual Reality flees escapism - by reattaching itself through Mixed Reality Reality to unpacking dimensions of the real -; and news media focuses on entertainment - preying on boredom.

Both models - modeling and mining - rely on the existence of a plastic game set with easily definable avatars and clear cut operations. Within this context, there is immense opportunity in rethinking and playing out the fuel and machinery that augments, distorts - is in charge - of perception and comprehension.

Gameplay and Virtual Space Characters

The objective of designing ‘gameplay’ is to create an a priori attention to how questions about sequence and narrative are intrinsically tied to scale and contiguity.

All impossible architectures above mentioned have a common resistance to thought, that results from the attempt of building them as one entity. But as we consider them more as models that live in another kind of ‘materiality’ we do not quite understand yet, the more their reality becomes not only possible: but compelling and inspiring.

Who knows what kinds of other mind spaces and brain architectures may we be creating as we emphasize other navigations for our senses to cope with?

The objective is to re-install a new sense of spatial volumetric and of time, a new spatial being, a specter, a new presence that can resume from the distance of its surface, attracting, repelling, spinning, plugging within minds and bodies in new architectural ways.

For this reason, we will be working less with external conditions of reward and/or traditional gameplay and instead attempt to focus on the intrinsic conditions of space play that can evolve other models for ‘level’ - including emotion, affect and drive - and in this way, generate representational dilemmas and constructs.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Learning Outcomes

Students will be able to critically analyze and compare different subjects related with the studio thematic and create cross-relations between architecture theory, precedents and their underlying structures and concepts.

Students will be able to distinguish, compare and evaluate potentials of virtual constructs as part of architectural design typologies.

Students will be able to critically address issues of scalability and connectivity between components of spatial experience, in a multi-dimensional way, and to actively use them to produce new structures of narrative, function and aesthetic experience in architecture culture.

Students will have the opportunity to engage the political, social, cultural and ethical problematics deriving from particular and general aspects of increasing information extraction and communication intensive societal and spatial fabrics through digital materials.

Students will be able to demonstrate the ability to independently propose, refine and develop a project that explores a concept goal and can be tested for its experiential impact.

Students will be able to demonstrate creative ability in the design and development of the project as well as a capacity to document and present their work in a clear and effective way: students will use particular software (game design engine) that connects 3D modeling with interactive navigation of a digital virtual environment and integration of visual and acoustic environments.

Outline of Course Work

Classes will engage lecture, seminar discussion, and design studio formats.

Students are required to attend, participate and contribute to all classes - all assignments are mandatory.

Students are required to maintain a consistent and ongoing work pace by which weekly tests of their concepts and strategies are realized, evaluated and calibrated.

Students will produce a final team (2-3 people) project and design a presentation that matches and upholds the expectations of testing for the proposal.

Phase 1: Precedent Research and Collective Discussion

Phase 2: Tutorials on Format

Phase 3: 3D Modeling and Time Sequence of Concept Design

Phase 4: Convert 3D model to Game Design Engine Software or other platform

Phase 5: Calibrate Narrative/Sequence to Output Format

Phase 6: Project Presentation in Final Review

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Statement of Academic Integrity

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process. Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust will merit a failing grade in this course and the violator(s) will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.  
Students engaging in plagiarism will be asked to redo their projects and failing to do so, will automatically be a F in the course.
