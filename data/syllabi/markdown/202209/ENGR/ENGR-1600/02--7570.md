## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to identify the major properties of the different classes of materials (metals, ceramics, glasses, polymers, and electronic materials).

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Homework:** Homework will be administered and graded online through LMS. Students must complete the assigned homework by the due date (Sunday’s) to receive credit. **There are no individual extensions.** You will need to correctly complete **90%** of the assignments to receive full credit for homework. Many exam questions will be based on the homework questions.

**Laboratory/Activity Sessions:** The course includes several labs/activities which can be done in person and, in the case classes become remote or a student is quarantined, there are videos. The lab/activity manual is available electronically via Blackboard (LMS). Late lab/activity reports will receive 50% off the grade and will receive no credit if submitted more than 7 days after the deadline. If a student needs an extension for an unavoidable and compelling reason, the student may be given an extension at the instructor’s discretion provided that the student presents a suitable written excuse. The written excuse is required from the Dean, a doctor, or a coach.

**Lab/activity reports, including data analysis and questions, are due by midnight on Fridaysapproximately one week after the lab/activity is completed. It must be submitted on LMS as a PDF file only. Other formats may not be graded and may not receive credit.**

**Exams:** There will be three 50-minute cumulative Exams (see schedule below) administered in person during the Wednesday recitation period (1:00 - 1:50 pm). Exams may include material covered in lecture, team problems, reading, homework, laboratories and activities. No makeup exams will be given. If a student misses an exam for an unavoidable and compelling reason, the student may be excused from the exam at the instructor’s discretion provided that the student presents a suitable written excuse. The written excuse is required from the Dean, a doctor, or a coach. ***The percent credit of the excused exam will be allocated to the final exam (e.g., if you get excused from an exam, the final examination is now worth 40% instead of 30%).***

**Final Examination: There will be a mandatory comprehensive final examination given during exam week. The examination can only be given during that time unless you have three exams that day.**

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:** The relationship between students and faculty is based upon trust, and the continued maintenance of this trust is necessary for education to be successful. Students need to trust faculty to make appropriate judgments as to the structure and content of the course. Faculty members need to trust that the work turned in by students represents their own effort. Violation of this trust undermines the educational process. In addition, as engineers, you may be designing products (e.g., aircraft, the computers that run them, or the runways they land on) that lives depend on. As a result, there is no tolerance for breach of academic integrity such as cheating, plagiarizing, or inappropriate sharing of laboratory reports, quizzes, or homework.

**Anyone caught cheating or plagiarizing will be subject to disciplinary actions, such as a failing grade for the course and referral to the Dean of Students.**

Cheating includes sharing answers, stealing answers, and taking on line exams together (in person or remotely). Plagiarism means copying words from someone else's work, even if you "change the sentence a bit". If you share your laboratory report, you are as guilty as the person copying it. If you do use material from an appropriate source (e.g., the Internet), make sure you reference it properly in your report. More specific guidelines for writing a lab report are given in the Laboratory Manual (LMS website).
