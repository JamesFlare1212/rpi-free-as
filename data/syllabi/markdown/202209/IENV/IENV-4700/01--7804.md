## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

A course that focuses on the Hudson River Basin as an environmental microcosm and a vehicle through which to illustrate the natural science of river systems with particular attention to human influences. This interdisciplinary environmental science course is for environmentally oriented junior, senior, and graduate students

## Course Goals

### Goals:

At the completion of this course, you will be able to:

1. Understand basic watershed scale biogeochemistry through detailed study of the Hudson.
2. Synthesize the knowledge you’ve gained in your many courses and use that knowledge to address real world environmental and natural problems
3. Perform independent research on a topic in a well-organized, well-documented manner using scientific literature and popular media
4. Use evidence based arguments to support your conclusions
5. Further develop your ability for oral and written persuasive argument

Deepen your understanding about challenging environmental science issues

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Your progress will be evaluated through participation, reading responses, homework assignments, written reflections and projects. No exams will be given.

**Engagement and participation:** Active participation in class is an essential part of the learning process. You are expected to participate in class discussions and hands-on learning activities, as well as peer instruction and evaluation. You are encouraged to share your experience, perspective, questions, and analysis of text, reading materials and assignments. Attendance will not be taken. However, if you do miss class, you are responsible for obtaining any material, announcements and assignments covered in class. Please don’t email me with the following question: “Did I miss anything important in class today?”-My answer will always be “YES!”

**Homework and Reflections:** There will be a number of out-of-class assignments to complete throughout the semester. These are designed to check for preparation and understanding, generate discussion, encourage participation, and deepen comprehension of course material. When we go on a trip or have a guest speaker, these may be reflections. Depending on the assignment, these will either be due at the beginning of the next class session, or one week from assigned.

**Paper summaries:** On many Fridays, we will discuss scientific papers that relate to topics introduced on Tuesday. Your summary needs to include key points from the paper, including describing important figures. This will prepare you for Friday discussion.

**Discussion summaries:** After each discussion, you will write a summary that incorporates the papers and synthesizes the discussion. The summary should include information about the issue, history and implications.

**Projects:** There will be two large projects, which will both allow you to perform independent research on the Hudson River. In the first, you will ask a question using an established data set, and in the second, you will ask a question that is of interest to you and decide what data set would be needed to answer the question.

*Data Analysis Project:* The Hudson River Environmental Conditions Observing System (HRECOS) is collecting high frequency water quality and meteorological data from electronic sensors located in and along the Hudson River and connecting water bodies. This data is freely available at: http://www.hrecos.org. Using this data, you will: propose a question of interest to you based on water quality of the Hudson River, and answer the question using data available to you. Additionally, you can collect ancillary data to add to the HRECOS data set if you would like to-but this is not required. You will present your results as a GSA style presentation (10-15 minute presentation).

*Tributary Watershed Plan:* A watershed plan compiles information for a particular watershed, defines targets, and sets priorities to achieve specific goals. The following local tributaries do not have a watershed plan: Poestenkill, Wyantskill, Mill Creek. For this project, in groups you will map the watershed, assess current quality, compile existing data, identify goals and values, identify stakeholders, prioritize issues and begin to establish a plan.
