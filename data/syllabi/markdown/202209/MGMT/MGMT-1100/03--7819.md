## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a required first course for management majors and minors. It emphasizes broad, basic principles of managerial functions and processes using an interdisciplinary approach to goal-oriented situations of private and public organizations.

The course is designed to introduce students to the key managerial functions and processes used in businesses. We will cover finance, accounting, marketing, operations, entrepreneurship, business analytics, and other general management concepts. For those of you that are business students, the class will serve as an introduction to these topics, such that you can be familiar with them as you work through your curriculum at Lally. For those of you that are not business students, the course will give you familiarity with these concepts so that you can use them, or at least have a basic understanding of them, as you go to work in organizations as engineers, scientists, architects, etc.

We will use readings and lectures to provide content, but then will use cases and exercises extensively in order to help you apply the ideas. In some deliverables you’ll apply concepts to specific questions but in others you’ll be tasked with making decisions and recommendations that would impact an entire business. That is one of the main objectives of this class: to get you experience in the complex, multi-faceted decision making that is done by organizational leaders. This culminates in your final project, wherein you’ll analyze a business and present your findings and recommendations either in a paper or in professional presentation format.

You will also build team, interpersonal, and communication skills.

Problem/Solution (P.S.) Challenge (20 percent):

For an exercise this semester in MGMT1100 (Management in the Digital Age), we will be examining whether digital communication in a business sense has an impact on success. Through an exercise called the P.S. Challenge (problem solving), interdisciplinary student teams will be tasked with solving a business problem each week for 10 weeks, aligning with the weekly topic and learning outcomes of the course. (This challenge aligns with each of the course’s five learning outcomes.) There will be a point system and you will be competing for a portion of your grade. For those of you that are extremely grade-conscious, do not worry. You can still be very successful in this course if your team does not perform well. (Please see “Class Participation/Attendance” section above.)

Student teams will communicate exclusively through Slack, which, according to its value proposition, “is a messaging app for business that connects people to the information they need. By bringing people together to work as one unified team, Slack transforms the way organizations communicate.” Fortune 500 companies use Slack and given the last two years, the nature of work and business has changed because of the pandemic, meaning dispersed or remote teams rely on communication via digital means more than ever. In Week Two, you will read or listen to an October 2021 story in The Atlantic that highlights how Slack has changed the workplace dynamic in terms of transparency, efficiency, and overall productivity. (The podcast is fantastic.)

Slack also captures all communication — in its chat functionality. Its interface provides a look similar to a social media platform but emphasizes communication via the written word, links, PDFs, images, and other means of transmission. This is also active and experiential engagement, which, in a classroom setting is defined in different ways. Given the pandemic and the possibility of remote learning at any time in the Fall semester, this still allows students to communicate via technology to complete the exercise and fulfill the course objective.

We will then download all the messages sent over the course of the semester. Over 10 weeks, this will produce tens of thousands of messages or communications. In turn, we plan to use the machine learning technique, Natural Language Processing (NLP), to analyze sentiment analysis, tone, and other collaborative communication constructs within a business sense. Ultimately, perhaps civil, casual, and less rigid communication within a team dynamic leads to more successful endeavors especially among interdisciplinary teams. You have grown up communicating more via digital means than in an interpersonal capacity, so this may be more important than ever as previous generations leading legacy organizations and hiring this new generation, may be able to adopt different communication standards to arrive as the same place — productive and profitable. How do you “win”? How does this work? Creativity is rewarded. Innovation is rewarded. In theory, the “best” business ideas should rise to the top. We will discuss more in Week One with full details provided on the LMS and then close the course with a review of the findings on the last day. Fun? Guaranteed. Team construction will also be discussed as a class as creative control of this exercise is as much yours as it is mine.

Quizzes (20 percent):

There will be three quizzes throughout the semester, and will primarily cover the topical content, i.e., finance, accounting, marketing, operations, entrepreneurship, etc. We’ll talk about this more in class ~1 week before each quiz. Each may consist of multiple-choice questions, fill in the blanks, and some calculations.

Consulting Grand Final Project (40 percent):

Your “final” for this class is a team assignment and includes a presentation (synchronous or asynchronous — TBD) of the company you have researched over the course of the semester. The presentation includes supporting documentation which illustrates the research, planning and preparation done by your team. The overall purpose of the assignment is for you to identify challenges the company is facing, come up with recommendations to improve the business, and provide your best assessment of where this company will be in five years. Your recommendations should be achievable, well thought out, and capable of having a substantial impact on the company.

The project should build off the entire course. Think of it in four phases: (1) assess the situation/ market/company (using tools from readings/lectures/experiences); (2) generate ideas for recommendations; (3) select recommendations and present them; and (4) present your assessment of their five-year outlook. We will discuss your ideas in individual team meetings the last week prior to the presentations. (Please see calendar below.)

Your team will be graded on the quality of your analysis, your recommendations, the quality and professionalism of your presentation, your use of concepts from the class, and your ability to provide a recommendation(s) with an accompanying detailed implementation plan and respond to challenges during a question-and-answer session.

This is a research project in which you and your team will analyze one company (from an approved list) in depth. There will be one company chosen for this early in the term and all teams will analyze separate companies. The reason for the early introduction is so you can track performance early and often to really immerse yourself in the company’s activity over time instead of a rushed two weeks. This allows for deeper thought and analysis. Detailed instructions and evaluation criteria will be posted on LMS and discussed in time for you to do the work. Each of the five criteria will be assessed on a 1-4 scale and the rubric will be reviewed again during our individual meeting.

In addition, the team will submit their presentation as a deliverable. A final written paper is not needed, so be sure the presentation is clear and well written. Any additional research, pro-forma financials, data gathered, supporting materials, etc. can be submitted to the LMS drop box as separate appendices.

Although this is a team project, the instructor reserves the right to allocate grades individually, based on each individual member’s contributions to the project including the presentation and to the question-and-answer session.

Again, note that I will ask you to complete confidential peer reviews of your team members at the end of the term. Your grade on this group assignments may be impacted by your teammate’s evaluation of your contributions.
