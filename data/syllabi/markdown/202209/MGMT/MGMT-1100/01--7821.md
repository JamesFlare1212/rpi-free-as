## Course Goals

### Goals:

This is a required first course for management majors and minors. It emphasizes broad, basic principles of managerial functions and processes using an interdisciplinary approach to goal-oriented situations of private and public organizations.

The course is designed to introduce students to the key managerial functions and processes used in businesses. We will cover finance, accounting, marketing, operations, entrepreneurship, business analytics, and other general management concepts. For those of you that are business majors, the class will serve as an introduction to these topics, such that you can be familiar with them as you work through your curriculum at Lally. For those of you that are not business majors, the course will give you familiarity with these concepts so that you can use them, or at least have a basic understanding of them, as you go to work in organizations as engineers, scientists, architects, etc.

We will use readings and lectures to provide content, but then will use cases and/or exercises extensively in order to help you *apply* the ideas. In some deliverables you’ll apply concepts to specific questions but in others you’ll be tasked with making decisions and recommendations that would impact an entire business. That is one of the main objectives of this class: to get you experience in the complex, multi-faceted decision making that is done by organizational leaders. This culminates in your final project, wherein you’ll analyze a business and present your findings and recommendations in a professional presentation format.

You will also build team, interpersonal, and communication skills.

## COURSE ASSESSMENT MEASURES/GRADING CRITERIA

***Class Participation: 10%***

You’ll find that complex business decisions often don’t have a ‘correct’ or ‘best’ answer. In some areas, such as accounting, there is a correct way to do things, of course. However, much of what we discuss will require informed judgment, points of view, and differing opinions. This makes participation very important – the class is not just about me telling you what to think. Therefore, your contribution to discussions is necessary. Your participation grade is based on your “presence,” as opposed to mere attendance, and your contribution to discussions, both in the larger class and in your groups.

Note that I will ask you to complete confidential peer reviews of your team members at the end of term. Thus, **your team members** will be assessing **you** as well. **Your grade for participation and on your group assignments *may* be impacted by your teammate’s evaluation of your group participation and contributions to group deliverables.**

If you are going to miss class, please notify me in advance by email. An excused absence does not exempt you from the assignment due for that day. If you are sick and need to take the class asynchronously, you must notify me via email and let me know your situation. **Unexcused absences and excessive excused absences will reduce your class participation grade.** Three unexcused absences will reduce your class grade one-half a full letter per each absence after (ex. ***Homework: (26%)***

The assignment will be given orally in the class prior to the due date and are also posted on the syllabus and LMS. Homework comprehension questions are posted in the Homework tab of LMS, or in the Pearson MML.

There are Mini simulations on Pearson’s MyManagementLab that are also part of your homework. Decisions made within the simulation earn students’ points. The number of points possible depends on which path the student chooses throughout the simulation. The percentage score they earn from the simulation equals the percentage of assignment points that will be recorded in the grade book.

- Within the simulation, each decision has a point value of 5 points (best choice), 3 points (an ok choice), 1 point (not the best choice) or 0 points (incorrect--usually for quantitative or ethically based questions), based on the level of correctness of each decision made by the student.
- Each decision point contributes to the percentage that has been designated by you as the assignment’s worth. The number of points the student receives for each decision (0 to 5 points), is reflected in the final results page.
- Keep in mind that the maximum number of points for each decision do not equal 100 points, but instead, is only reflective of the level of correctness for each decision. NOTE: the maximum number of ‘in-simulation” points (5 points for each decision) will not add up to 100 points total. For example, if a student selects all of the best choices for each decision, he or she will receive all possible points for that simulation, resulting in the student achieving 100% of the assignment points to be recorded in the grade book.
- These percentages are then applied to the assigned points for that simulation, which is listed in the detailed evaluation chart below.

***Quizzes: (32%)***

There will be three quizzes throughout the semester, and will primarily cover the topical content, i.e., strategy, finance, accounting, marketing, operations, etc. It may reference the cases, but I’m not testing you on detailed case knowledge. We’ll talk about this more in class ~1 week before each quiz. Each may consist of multiple-choice questions, True/False, fill in the blanks, and some calculations.

***Final Consulting Grand Challenge: (32%)***

The Consulting Challenge is a research project in which you and your team will analyze one company (from an approved list) in depth over the course of the semester. There will be one company chosen for this early in the term and all teams will analyze separate companies. The final presentation includes supporting documentation which illustrates the research, planning and preparation done by your team. The overall purpose of the assignment is for you to identify challenges the company is facing, come up with recommendations to improve the business, and provide your best assessment of where this company will be in five years. Your recommendations should be achievable, well thought out, and capable of having a substantial impact on the company.

The project should build off the entire course. Think of it in 4 phases: (1) assess the situation / market / company (using tools from readings/lectures/milestones #1) and identify the biggest issues your firm is facing. From your analysis, convince me that you know what is going on! (2) generate ideas for recommendations; (3) select recommendations and present them, and (4) present your assessment of their 5 year outlook. We can discuss your ideas in individual team meetings the last week prior to the presentations.

Your team will be graded on the quality of your analysis, your recommendations, the quality and professionalism of your presentation, your use of concepts from the class, and your ability to provide a recommendation(s) with an accompanying detailed implementation plan and respond to challenges during a question and answer session.

Each team should include references/citations (MLA format), and a spreadsheet that includes pro-forma financials that incorporate investment needed and illustrate the growth projections, such that they cover a number of years.

In addition, the team will submit their presentation as a deliverable. A final written paper is not needed, so be sure the presentation is clear and well written. Any additional research, pro-forma financials, data gathered, supporting materials, etc can be submitted to the LMS drop box as separate appendices.

**Although this is a team project, the instructor reserves the right to allocate grades individually, based on each individual member’s contributions to the presentation and to the question and answer session.**

Again, note that I will ask you to complete confidential peer reviews of your team members at the end of the term. Your grade on this group assignments ***may*** be impacted by your teammate’s evaluation of your contributions.

**Missed Exams, Quizzes, and Assignments** *Management is about deadlines and details.* Any student work turned in after the assigned due date will be accepted only if (a) arrangements are made with the instructor prior to the due date; or (b) a valid and documented emergency prevents submission of the assignment by the assigned due date. Only an official excuse from your class dean will be accepted.

**NO LATE SUBMISSIONS OF ASSIGNMENTS WILL BE ACCEPTED.** Understandably, there are circumstances (e.g., job interviews, illness, family matters, etc.) that may cause you to miss class.
