## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course gives students an in depth understanding of the strategic issues involved in managing technology and innovation within firms. Additionally this course also gives students exposure to the implications of a select set of emerging technologies that are tectonically impacting the business landscape. Through this course, students will:

1. Develop an understanding of how technologies and industries evolve over time, and the organizational, sociological and economic foundations that underpin technological change.
2. Be exposed to the constraints that arise in mature firms in adapting and reacting to disruptive technologies.
3. Gain an appreciation of how various elements such as markets, customer characteristics, and value creation/value capture interact to influence firm behavior in technologically intensive environments.
4. Understand the role of eco systems and platforms
5. Develop insight into the organizational and economic effects of emerging technologies impacting the business landscape, including Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, and other developments such as Blockchain.

The course is relevant for students interested in managing organizational change and technology strategy in large firms. Additionally, it is relevant for entrepreneurs and managers interested in starting a new venture in a technologically driven industry. The course would also benefit those that are interested in pursuing careers in venture capital, or management consulting.

The course will utilize a combination of articles, cases, team projects, workshops, and presentations. A few of the articles assigned are academic in tenor, and the objective is to expose students to the basic concepts and thinking in the field of technology management. Students are nevertheless expected to understand the main principles of these articles, and how they build on the topics covered in class. Theoretically the course will draw on insights from several disciplines, including industrial organization, strategy, finance, entrepreneurship and organizational behavior.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Describe how technologies evolve over time, and the organizational, sociological and economic factors that affect evolution.
- Identify the constraints that arise in mature firms in adapting and reacting to disruptive technologies, and some potential ways to overcome them
- Explain various types of innovation and how they impact firms and strategy
- Describe the role of the eco system in technological evolution and commercialization, and the impact of various players such as customers, suppliers, complementors
- Frame how emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence and machine learning are reshaping the business world in practical and strategic terms

**Class Participation**

Class contribution is an essential and significant component of evaluation. In order to facilitate a constructive discussion, please go through all the readings and cases assigned for a particular session (see the class schedule below). You should be able to offer insights into the central themes being discussed in the session, and outline the main ideas/concepts in each reading and case. Depending upon how the class progresses, I may also assign additional articles from time to time. You are expected to go through those additional readings as well, and come prepared accordingly.

Strong class participation is grounded in the ideas and concepts we cover. General participation that is disconnected from the readings and class discussions will not be rewarded.

In terms of attendance, you are required to attend all classes throughout the semester. Absences will result in a lower participation grade.

**Case Analysis- Report and Presentation (Group)**

The case analysis report and presentation accounts for 15% of the final grade. You will be responsible for preparing a written report and making a formal presentation of your analysis and recommendations in class. The enclosed schedule indicates the date each case is to be presented (The Kodak case is a general discussion case for the whole class). Broadly speaking, your presentation will cover the background of the case, the issues identified (see the assignment questions for the cases below), recommendations for the future, and any other points that you think are relevant.

For this assignment you will be required to prepare a report that is no longer than 12 pages, excluding the cover page (please see the guidelines for written reports below). In addition, you will make a presentation in class for a maximum of 20 minutes (guidelines are also provided below for presentations). Note that the report is due the day the case is scheduled to be presented. Late submissions will not be accepted.

The cases discussed in class are designed to illustrate specific concepts and decision scenarios. Please therefore do not look up any online material or ‘solutions’ for the case. Any attempts to do so will be treated as a serious ethical violation, and in the minimum equivalent to a first offense (see below).

**Brief Report (Group)**

In this report (maximum of 3 pages, excluding the cover page), you are required to identify the central issues in the case, develop a brief analysis, and provide your main recommendations. The brief report is due the day the case is scheduled to be presented, and should be submitted to me ahead of class. Late submissions will not be accepted, and failure to turn in a report on the due date will lead to a lower grade.

**Workshop Assignment (Group)**

The workshop assignment requires you to develop an insightful presentation on an assigned topic (e.g. tracing a technology and its evolution, an example of a patent war etc.). You will be given brief guidelines ahead of the assignment, and your task is to develop a presentation of about 20 mins as a group. The purpose is to not only get you thinking about the topic, but also to inform the rest of the class and enhance their understanding of the subject. It is essential that you develop an in depth analysis and come well prepared to present on the day that the workshop assignment is scheduled, since you will be playing the lead role in these discussions.

**Innovation Project**

The purpose of this project, broadly speaking, is threefold. To understand (1) how an emerging technology impacting today’s business landscape is taking shape, (2) what strategies firms in this space are adopting to compete and gain market share, and (3) how incumbent firms and technologies are responding to the threat and fighting back.

Further details on the assignment are provided below. This project has the following deliverables: (1) a choice of technology to be made latest by September 20th , (2) an interim presentation covering Phase I (October 18th/October 21st ), (3) an interim report due on October 21st covering Phase I, (4) a final presentation covering Phase I feedback and Phase II deliverables (December 2nd/ December 6th), (5) a final report covering Phase I feedback and Phase II, due during the final exam period (December 15th ).

Note: All work related to the Innovation Project is expected to be original work undertaken in this class. Repurposing projects or material from another class constitutes a serious ethical violation.

**Exam (Individual)**

An exam worth 20% will be held on December 9th. This exam will cover all the material discussed, including the cases. The emphasis will be on testing your understanding of the material, rather than on how well you remember various concepts.
