### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Participation in Case Discussions:

This is a case-based course. Students should read the assigned materials, analyze the cases and come prepared to discuss their findings in the class. It is not sufficient to make simple comments in the class. Students are expected to take ownership of class discussions and help the class to develop a shared understanding of issues related to a topic or a case. Please come prepared to express your views, defend your ideas, raise questions, probe other students’ viewpoints, and engage in rigorous discussions.

Leading Case Discussions **(for MGMT 6080 only)**:

Students will be asked to form groups to lead case discussions. Each group will be assigned to one case throughout the semester. Every group will make a presentation pertaining to their case in the beginning of the first class where the case is scheduled for discussion. Furthermore, the group is expected to take the lead of the class discussion following their presentation.

Written Case Analysis:

Each student is expected to prepare a written brief for all cases except for the case for which they are assigned as a discussion leader. For each case, a two-page (Times New Roman, 12pt font size, single-spaced) brief should be submitted in pdf format using the designated LMS assignment tab. The deadline is the beginning of the first class time in which the case is scheduled for discussion – late submissions cannot be graded. This is an individual task and students are expected to submit their original analysis of each case.

Case briefs will be graded for both format and content. Errors in spelling, grammar, or punctuation can detract from or distort the message you are trying to communicate. As a result, such errors will lower your grade. The grading of the briefs will be based upon the quality of your analysis. Merely restating case facts will not help your grade and, in fact, will use up valuable space in your brief. In writing your briefs, assume that you are a consultant to the company who is being paid to analyze the company's situation and make a set of recommendations.

Simulation Games:

Simulation games will be used to provide students an opportunity to understand the complexities in managing supply chains and making decisions. Three simulation games will be played in the class.

*Beer Distribution Game* simulates a multi-tier supply chain where communication across tiers is not allowed. Students play this game as a team with the goal of minimizing their team’s backorder and inventory holding costs. Each student will be responsible for ordering and shipment decisions for their individual role in the team.

*Harvard’s Global SCM*, which simulates a global cell phone manufacturing firm, would require students to engage in forecasting, demand planning, production planning and contracting. Students play this game in several rounds and are evaluated on their supply chain performance. Students play individually in this game.

*The Fresh Connection*, which is focused on a consumer products firm, provides students the opportunity to experience the tradeoffs and complexities in managing supply chains to achieve strategic goals. Students play this game as a team and will have to work together to achieve effective outcomes. Student teams are evaluated based on the year end performance of their firm. The game is played in multiple rounds with performance evaluated in each round. Each team is also required to submit a report through LMS summarizing their experience, strategies, outcomes, and key learnings. Check out the course calendar on the last page for the report submission deadline. Details on the report format will be explained in class.

The last two games require subscription. Details on how to subscribe for the games will be provided in class.

Exam:

There will be one exam. Check out the course calendar on the last page for the exam date. Exam requires individual work; students cannot collaborate in any way. Copying or communicating during the exam is cheating.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
