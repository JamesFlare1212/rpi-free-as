## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides an introduction to the subject of vibrations. Topics include: free and forced linear vibrations of damped and undamped mechanical and electrical systems of n-degree of freedom; continuous system vibration; manual and computer methods of finding natural frequencies; self- and nonself-adjoint problems; eigenfunction expansion; integral transforms perturbation techniques; stability criteria. Vector algebra and calculus are used throughout in developing the theory and in problem solving. Required use will be made of *MATLAB/ Python.*

## Learning Outcomes

Students are expected to be able to:

- Formulate complex vibration situations,
- To linearize occasional nonlinear equations about a point of equilibrium,
- To test the system stability,
- To determine resonant frequencies and damping characteristics,
- To set up and to compute the overall short time and long-time solution,
- To determine approximations for those resonant frequencies.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The conservative and nonconservative, homogeneous single degree of freedom oscillator with examples in mechanical and electrical systems (2 weeks).

\- The nonhomogeneous single degree of freedom oscillator solved by: (a) Method of

undetermined coefficients, (b) Convolution (1 week).

\- The multi-degree of freedom Lagrange's equations of motion with damping, constraints, and generalized forces with selected examples (1 week).

\- Inertia and stiffness matrices via both the direct and analytic methods (1.5 weeks)

\- Free vibration of n degree of freedom conservative systems (eigenvalues, eigenvectors

principal coordinates, normalized principal coordinates, orthogonality, computer solutions,

repeated eigenvalues) (1 week).

\- Forced vibration of n degrees of freedom conservative systems (with the use of principal coordinates to obtain the net solution) (1 week).

\- Vibration with damping (use of Rayleigh dissipation function and Hamilton's canonical form) (1 week).

\- Proportional damping with n degrees of freedom (1 week).

\- Vibrations as with damping (use of Laplace transform technique) (1 week).

\- Homogeneous and non-homogeneous nonself adjoint problems (1 week).

\- Approximate and numerical techniques (2 weeks).

\- Continuous System Vibration

\- Finite elements

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The Rensselaer Handbook defines various forms of academic dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Students should familiarize themselves with this portion of the Rensselaer Handbook and should note that the penalties for plagiarism and other forms of cheating can be quite harsh.

In this course collaborative work ***is*** permitted and encouraged on the homework problems. Collaboration is ***not*** permitted in the examinations, quizzes, and the final project.
