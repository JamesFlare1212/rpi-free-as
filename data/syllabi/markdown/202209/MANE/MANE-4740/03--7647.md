## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

1. **Course Description**

This laboratory course offers a culminating experimental experience in the field of thermal and fluids engineering for MANE students. The theoretical background gained in previous courses is now consolidated and expanded by hands-on experiments and analysis of thermal and fluids systems. This lab course is intended to familiarize students with a variety of thermodynamic, heat transfer, and fluid flow phenomena in the context of the operation of major pieces of equipment. Both fundamental concepts as well as practical/applied ideas will be explored.

You will work together in teams, and there are a total of four laboratory experiences. Three weeks will be spent on each laboratory experience. You start preparations a week ahead of a scheduled experiment by reviewing the theoretical background and studying the experimental setup and procedures. You will be given a prelab assignment to complete, which will be due the day you perform the experiment. Each laboratory experience consists of two experimental investigations. You will be given a homework assignment of analyzing the data from your experiments and preparing your results. You may collaborate with your group members on this, but you will be asked to submit your own work individually. One week after you conducted the experiment, you and your group will meet in a studio-style discussion with your section’s instructor to review your results and talk about details of the lab. Your “Results Homework” is due at the start of this discussion session and you should come prepared to (individually) explain how you obtained the values you submit. The rest of the discussion session time is available to you as a group to begin writing an extensive laboratory report which will be due after another week. The laboratory reports will be similar to technical reports produced by practicing engineers. It is recommend that you take advantage of the time during the discussion session to organize who will work on which parts of the report and to ask your instructor questions about the lab. Your reports will be graded as a group. Your prelabs, homeworks, and reports will be evaluated by your lab section instructor.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

1. **Textbooks**

There are no required texts for this class.

- *Introduction to Thermal and Fluids Engineering by Kaminski and Jensen* is the most recommended reference.
- The Library also contains the following books which may be used to reinforce fundamentals of heat transfer, thermodynamics and fluid mechanics: *Fundamentals of Thermal-Fluid Sciences* by Çengel and Turner, *Brief Introduction to Fluid Mechanics* by Young, Munson and Okiishi, *Fundamentals of Engineering Thermodynamics* by Moran and Shapiro, *Fundamentals of Heat and Mass Transfer* by Incropera and DeWitt, and *Fluid Mechanics* by White. In addition, you may find the book by Figliola & Beasley (*Theory and Design for Mechanical Measurements* 2nd Edition, 1995, Wiley) to be a useful reference.
- Materials specific to the four experiments performed in this class will be provided through handouts and lecture notes posted on LMS. Extensive handouts have been prepared on the first three experiments. These handouts include an introduction to the project, theory, specific objectives, equipment description and operating instructions, and lab report requirements. Another handout has been prepared to provide information on uncertainties in experimental data. It should be studied as carefully as the ones on the individual experiments.

**Lab Material Reading**

Much more can be gained from an experiment if before attempting to perform the experiment the student has the background knowledge about the objectives and concepts (theory) governing the processes to be studied. It is also critical to understand the objectives and the plan of work for each lab before coming to the lab. This information is provided in the extended handouts for each experiment. **Please read carefully all of the handouts.** Some supplemental materials may also be provided on LMS for those wishing a deeper understanding of the theory, instrumentation, and/or standards related to the experiments.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. **Course Objectives**

The topics to be covered will be a combination of new material specific to this lab course and material covered in previous courses.

The specific objectives of the course are:

1. To demonstrate thermodynamic, heat transfer, and fluid flow principles;
2. To integrate theory and practice through the operation of experiments and through the acquisition and analysis of experimental data;
3. To familiarize students with complex, realistic systems and the interactions among thermodynamics, heat transfer, and fluid flow;
4. To introduce some new concepts and techniques in the three topic areas;
5. To develop abilities to understand, experiment with, and analyze thermal and fluids systems, including analysis and decision making in the face of experimental uncertainty;
6. To exercise technical communication skills in a variety of modes.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

1. **Laboratory experiments**

The experiments (in the order in which they will be performed) and the general concepts associated with each are listed below:

1. Hydraulic flow loop -- instrument calibration, friction factors (CII 5118)
2. Wind tunnel -- field concepts including velocity field, hydraulic boundary layers, transition to turbulence, heat transfer coefficients, multimode heat transfer (CII 5222)
3. Air conditioning system -- refrigeration cycles, psychrometrics, heat transfer, energy balances, refrigerant properties (CII 5118)
4. Student Designed -- The fourth lab is designed by the groups. You will submit your plan in place of a prelab and will present your findings in place of a report. (Your presentation should cover all the same categories of content that would have been in a report.) There is no homework for Lab 4. You will have the option of using any of the lab setups from the first three labs or other equipment may be available (ask instructors).

**Notes on Data Intensive Tasks:**

For all laboratory experiments: After collecting the data during the laboratory session, students are expected to explain their methods for collecting the data, how the data was reduced, and what the results were. They are expected to provide an assessment of experimental uncertainty and a discussion of the validity of their results. Finally, the students write a formal engineers report in which they present all of the findings discussed above. The grades given on the prelab, homework, and report are aggregated to give an overall grade for a given experiment.

***Lab reporting details**

The written lab report is typically due two weeks after performing the experiment (see course schedule). The report will be graded principally on its technical content, but also on the quality of the writing, clarity, appearance, and format. Please submit it as a PDF. You will be provided with a grading rubric for the report and more report writing instruction in lecture.

**\*\*Peer assessment, and deficient team participation penalty**

You and the other members of your team are expected to participate fully in each laboratory experience and in the preparation of all team work. You will be given the opportunity to evaluate yourself and your peers. Trends in less than full participation will be reflected in deductions from an individual’s peer assessment score.

**\*\*\*Online Quizzes**

Throughout the semester (see course schedule), there will be a series of quizzes administered via LMS on topics covered during lectures and in the laboratory. The primary intent of the quiz is to draw attention to details in both the lab manual and the laboratory procedures manual that may be important during the lab, and to be sure students understood the underlying physical principles of the experiments. You may use any materials you want during the quiz, but you must take it alone, without any assistance from anyone else. The quizzes will be timed and multiple-choice.

**Final Exam:** There is no final exam. (A timeslot for this course might appear on the finals schedule, but that is just a placeholder.)

**Grade Curving:** The difficulty of the material may require curving the class grade, but this is seldom needed given that students have a high degree of control of their level of performance on assignments. Equalization of the sections will be performed to account for differences in report grading between instructors.

**Grade modifiers:** Grade modifiers (+, −) will be used in assigning the grade in this class.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

1. **Academic Integrity and Rules of Behavior**

Rensselaer’s policy on academic dishonesty is explained in the *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities*. You are expected to review and understand that information. In addition, the following policies apply specifically to this course.

- Committing, or attempting to commit an act of academic dishonesty, or assisting in the commission or attempt of such an act, is a flagrant offense to the process and culture of education; expect our reaction to it to be harsh.
- You are expected to refrain from any academically dishonest act, and to be vigilant in protecting your work from others who might attempt to commit an act of academic dishonesty.

In this course, academic dishonesty – *cheating* -- can take the following forms.

- Submission of a lab report that, except for short phrases or properly referenced material (e.g., quotes and figures from the lab manual, etc.), consists of any content not created by and original to the report authors listed. Plagiarism may be penalized according to the severity and deliberateness of the act, from reduction of report grade to failure of the course and referral to the Dean of Students for dismissal from Rensselaer.
- Taking a quiz with any assistance from another person, regardless of who they are, or the medium of communication for assistance (talk, phone, texting, etc.).
- Using any materials (such as a cell phone, camera) that are not explicitly permitted during a quiz. (Quizzes are open book, open notes, open lab reports, open laptop, open internet searches, but closed communication.)

Mobile Devices: During a quiz all mobile devices that are not your laptop (e.g., cell/smart phones, pagers, cameras, etc.) must be stored securely away and are not to be used unless specifically directed otherwise by the instructor. The use of, or any interaction with, a mobile device during a quiz without explicit permission of the instructor will be interpreted as the illicit transfer of quiz data, which will be considered an act of cheating and treated as such.

The penalty for violating these policies is at the discretion of any or all members of the instructional team. In general, the penalty for any violation is failure of the course and referral to the Dean of Students

Collaboration

You are expected to collaborate with the members of your laboratory group with respect to the conduct of your experiments and the preparation of your reports. However, as stated above, your report, including the experimental data and observations described therein, must be those of your own group. In rare cases (e.g., lab equipment failure) your instructor may provide you with experimental data to use as if you had obtained it yourselves; this will be considered “your data”, regardless of the actual source.
