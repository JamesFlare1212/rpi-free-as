## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Stress, strain, balance equations, elementary constitutive models. Elements of plasticity. Effect of temperature and deformation rate on mechanical behavior of engineering materials. Theory of dislocations. Deformation of metals and strengthening mechanisms. Creep behavior and viscoelastic models of material behavior.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Mechanical Behavior of Materials, T.H. Courtney, McGraw Hill, 2000

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Course learning objectives**:

- \- formulation of solid mechanics – small deformations, elementary constitutive laws for engineering materials
- \- nano and micro-scale physics of elastic and plastic deformation
- \- macroscopic mechanical behavior of metallic materials and polymers

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Overview of constitutive laws for engineering materials**

Review of notions of stress, strain, strain energy,

Governing equations of solid mechanics

Elastic constitutive laws,

Elements of plasticity and rate independent constitutive laws,

Rate dependent material behavior: phenomenology and constitutive laws.

2. **Introduction to the Theory of dislocations**

Elements of crystallography and point defects in crystals,

Classification and observation of dislocations,

Movement of dislocations (glide, climb, cross-slip),

Origin and multiplication of dislocations,

Dislocation interactions and their role in plastic deformation,

Dislocations in particular crystal structures.

3. **Twinning**
4. **Deformation of crystalline engineering materials and strengthening mechanisms**

Work hardening,

Strengthening due to grain size variation*,*

Solid solution hardening*,*

Precipitation hardening*,*

Heat treatments for hardening control,

Application: hardening of steels*.*

5. **Creep**

Macroscopic and microscopic phenomenology of creep deformation*,*

Mechanisms of deformation and rate equations*,*

Elements of design with creep*,*

Applications: Ni-alloys*.*

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will learn the formulation of solid mechanics for small deformations, and elementary constitutive laws for engineering materials
- Students will develop an understanding of the generic features of the mechanical behavior of a broad class of engineering materials and of the relationship between the macroscopic behavior and the microstructure.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Assessment of CLO will be performed via HW assignments and exams (one midterm and a final exam).
