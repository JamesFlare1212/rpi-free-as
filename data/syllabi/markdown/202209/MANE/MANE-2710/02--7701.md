## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Course Description/Contents:  
• Introduction to thermodynamic systems and interactions for undergraduate students, including heat and work  
• Thermodynamic properties relevant to energy systems  
• Conservation of energy and the “First Law of Thermodynamics” for closed and open systems  
• The “Second Law of Thermodynamics” and Entropy with application to common work-using and work-producing systems, including elementary power and refrigeration cycles

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Text is available electronically, and the **electronic version is necessary (Wiley+ Platform)**.  
Authors: Michael J. Moran, Howard N. Shapiro, Daisie D. Boettner, and Margaret B. Bailey.  
Textbook Title: Fundamentals of Engineering Thermodynamics.  
Textbook Edition: 9th Ed., Wiley.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Course Goals:  
• It provides the foundation for the thermal-fluids systems part of the MECL program and is essential preparation for AERO and NUCL students as they progress into their own required course sequences.  
• It introduces the laws of thermodynamics and control-volume balances of mass, energy, and entropy in the manner commonly encountered by practicing engineers.  
• It will foster an understanding of the performance of work-producing and work-using systems that are common in mechanical engineering and also a part of aeronautical and nuclear engineering.  
• It introduces the thermodynamic properties of gases, liquids, and solids, as well as two-phase mixtures, which are essential in energy systems.  
• It develops a more sophisticated problem-solving ability related to complex systems, commensurate with the expectations of engineering graduates.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- demonstrate an understanding of the fundamentals of mass, energy and entropy balances on control volumes
- evaluate thermodynamic properties of pure substances in gaseous, liquid and solid phases and in two-phase mixtures
- demonstrate an understanding of the Second Law of Thermodynamics as it applies to energy systems in engineering
- evaluate entropy changes of pure substances in gaseous, liquid and solid phases and in two-phase mixtures
- analyze the performance of work-using and work-producing systems involving complex processes and interacting components

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Homework:  
Homework typically consists of four problems. The solutions must be uploaded on LMS/Gradescope/Wiley+ platforms on Friday (due before class). Homework submitted late will receive reduced credit ONLY if the solutions have not been posted yet. Homework due dates are posted on the course syllabus. Homework assignments will be posted approximately one week before the due date. You must submit clear, readable, complete documents, which the Teaching Assistants can read. Your documents should be prepared as archival information that you can rely on for studying or for future use.  
In-class Assignments/Quizzes  
These activities include quizzes (typically at home) and in-class assignments related to the lecture materials. The Wiley+ Platform is typically used to deliver the quizzes. In-class assignments may use Gradescope and/or Wiley +.  
Mid-Semester Exams:  
We will have three mid-semester exams, 1 hr 50 min each (if starting at 8:00 AM), taken during the lecture time. Exams will be CLOSED-textbook and CLOSED notes, but a CRIB SHEET with easily accessible information may be used.  
Final Exam:  
There will be a three-hour final exam. The Final Exam Period is MAY 2 – 6, 2022. You should not make plans for a vacation or being away until your final exam schedule is established. We have no control over the scheduling of final exams.

Academic Integrity and Rules of Behavior  
The Provost has asked each instructor to delineate carefully a policy concerning academic honesty for his or her course. These statements are intended to cover most, but not all, forms of academic dishonesty. (You should refer to The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities for detailed information on Institute policy in this regard.)  
Homework: Collaboration among the students in the class is permitted and, in fact, encouraged. Seeking help from other sources is also encouraged. However, the work you submit should be your own. Evidence of your merely transcribing another person’s homework or from some other source will result in a zero grade for the problem, for all parties involved. A second offense will result in a zero grade for the entire homework assignment in question, for all parties involved. A third offense will result in a zero grade for homework for the entire semester for all parties involved, with a report made to the Dean of Students Office.  
Exams: Collaboration during an exam (exchanging verbal or written information or copying) is, of course, prohibited. Also prohibited is the use of unauthorized materials during an examination. All parties involved will receive an academic penalty, which will range from a numerical grade of zero for the exam to a failing grade in the course, as deemed by the instructor to be appropriate to the infraction. Submission of an exam with a request for re-grading after having changed an answer will result in a similar academic penalty. All incidents will be reported to the Dean of Students Office.  
Other: Outrageous offenses such as arranging for a substitute to take an examination, attempting to obtain examination questions or solutions in advance, or attempting to change a grade record will result in a failing grade for the course and a recommendation to the Dean of Students that the student(s) in question be suspended.
