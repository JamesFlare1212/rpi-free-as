## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

*Modern Techniques in Experimental Chemistry* is an Industry-accented lecture & lab course in Chemistry, covering selected topics in the following areas: Organic Chem., Inorganic Chem., Physical Chem., & Analytical Chem. Its main purpose is to introduce upper-level Chemical Engineering students to core chemical considerations (‘*what/how a chemist is thinking*’) during some representative lab exercises & in associated lecture material. The contents of the lectures & lab activities reflect typical chemical contexts & applications seen in industrial settings (*e.g.,* synthesis, analysis, materials characterization, process control, chemical troubleshooting, safety, competently supervising interns, submitting/discussing samples & specific analytical requirements with career chemists at commercial analytical labs).

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. Students will learn to appreciate the importance of emphasizing careful experimental set-up, and of recognizing & minimizing potential sources of experimental error (incl. contaminants, interferences, side-products, surface effects, aerial oxidation, photochemical degradation, etc.). Pre-lab activities, note-taking skills, methods of data analysis, & the practice of writing-up lab reports will all be valuable exercises as regards the student’s future interactions with (& ‘*being on the same page as*’) career chemists at commercial labs, as well as when presenting results at project management meetings, making/supporting patent applications, providing expert-witness testimony, & publishing professional scientific/technical papers & articles.

2. Students will learn the essential integration between lab skills/techniques, making careful observations/measurements, instrumentational capabilities/limitations, & the meaningful/defensible interpretation of chemical results. The aim being to present such types of diverse chemical problem-solving examples through experimentation & analysis as can be met in Industry, leading to an enhanced flexibility & growing self-confidence for the CE student in both wielding chemical knowledge & being able to rationally question others’ explanations. Error measurements, responsible experimental error estimations by the individual experimenter, & discussions of methods of error analysis are emphasized. The CE student will learn useful & practical aspects about how a career chemist typically approaches problem-solving in any given situation.

3. Students will learn the proper use of basic chemical lab apparatus/glassware & of more advanced equipment (incl. distillation columns, viscometers, & calorimeters). Three distinct types of titration methodology will be introduced: Acid/Base; Complexometric; & Electrochemical. Separative methods (incl. distillation & gas chromatography) are also included. Spectroscopic methods will be used throughout the course, including Atomic Absorption Spectrophotometry, Ultraviolet/Visible Spectroscopy, Infrared Spectroscopy, & Nuclear Magnetic Resonance Spectroscopy. A solution calorimeter will be used for studying aspects of the thermodynamics of solutions. Students will learn competence with advanced chemical laboratory techniques including mass measurements, volumetric measurements, preparing solutions, making dilutions, performing titrations, interpreting data generated by advanced instrumentation, phase-separations, & methods of synthesis. The CE student will get to experience ‘*hands-on*’ some of the everyday lab realities & necessary considerations for obtaining useful & defensible analytical results/data, having paid due regard both to lab safety and to each piece of apparatus’s/instrumentation’s inherent limitations & detection-limits. The CE student will experience interpreting actual analytical data which they’ve obtained on their own samples, while considering corollary factors such as possible interferences, contaminants, side-products, degradations, etc.

4. Students will learn to be able to communicate their project-specific requirements, ideas, & questions/concerns about chemical matters pertaining to their work/interests in an effective & accurate manner during discussions with professional chemists who they’ll meet in Industry (esp. at commercial analytical labs). The ability to confidently interact with career chemists & to wield chemical arguments defensibly will stand the student in good stead with their supervisors & managers, particularly as regards problem-solving on-the-jobsite, ensuring safety (*e.g.,* HAZWOPER), & when interacting with regulatory bodies. The CE student will grow in their overall awareness & competence to be able to discuss a project’s chemical analytical requirements (incl. defensible data integrity respecting conclusions being drawn); seeking to boost each student’s core chemical confidence to ‘*speak up*’ with suggestions or concerns during project-management meetings.

5. Students will develop competencies & confidence in the handling & processing of laboratory experimental data, as well as in being able to search for chemical information about specific chemical systems/reactions/interferences/etc. in the chemical literature.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Lectures & Recitation:** Tuesday & Friday will be 50-minute lectures; recitation will be on Wednesday. The material presented in the lecture will cover & expand on the material in the required weekly reading & related experiments. Excused-absences from lecture should be cleared with the course Instructor. Each student is permitted two (2) unexcused lecture absences; more than two unexcused lecture absences will result in deductions of points from your participation grade at the Instructor’s discretion.

**Exams:** There will be three (3) exams, which will (like the ‘*real world*’ of problem-solving that awaits you out in Industry) freely draw upon any of the material covered in lecture, in the assigned reading, met in assigned/suggested problems, or anything covered/discussed in lab. If a student misses an exam for an unavoidable & compelling reason, then that student’s exam may be re-scheduled at the Instructor’s discretion, provided that the student presents a suitably-authenticated written excuse from the Student Experience Office, a physician (doctor/dentist/specialist), or an official athletic/sport coach of the Institute.

**Reading Assignments and Review Problems:** It’s important for you to have read the relevant chapters in the text before coming to class. Chapter problems will be assigned for associated material & to reinforce lecture content. The assigned problems will not be graded, instead the problems should be used as study-aids for exams.

**Attendance in Lab is Mandatory.** Chemistry is fundamentally an experimental science which incorporates specialized apparatus (esp. fragile glassware), nuanced sample-handling protocols, as well as both observational & measurement-making skills. Two lab periods per week (M/R for Secn_2; T/F for Secn_1) are for the gaining of practical lab experience & instruction from accomplished practitioners. Experimentally-recorded data is not to be unduly shared (esp. copied); the data that you record from your own experimental set-up is the unique set of data that you’ll then work with in order to generate your final lab report, along with all of your unique observations & the chemical ‘history’ of your experiment (*e.g.,* excursions of temperature, *p*H, aerial oxidative ‘aging,’ drying-out, illumination, etc.). A laboratory notebook must be kept (pen/ink is permissible, but graylead pencil is typically used in-the-field), pre-labs are due on the day of your lab session (*i.e.,* prior to commencing lab-work), and the summarizing final lab report is due **one week after** the experimental work’s been completed (unless advised otherwise by the Instructor/TA). Your physical attendance in lab is mandatory in order for you to be able to pass this course; any excused-absences from lab must be documented (*e.g.,* a doctor’s note, a sport-coach’s note, etc.) & cleared through the course Instructor in a timely manner.

**Late Reports.** Lab reports are due **one week following** the completion of the experimental work. If a report is turned in late, one (1) point will be deducted for each day that the report is late. After one week’s tardiness, the report will then not be accepted and a zero score will result for that work.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

# Academic Integrity:

The instructional relationship between students & faculty/TAs is based on mutually-respectful trust, upon which genuine education is dependent to be successful. Students trust faculty to make appropriate judgments about the content & structure of the course; Faculty & TAs trust that the work turned-in by each student represents that person’s own honest effort. A violation of this essential trust undermines the educational process.

Breaches of academic integrity include cheating, plagiarism, or the inappropriate sharing of lab write-ups, data, or related materials, either from past cohorts or from current students.

**The penalties of a breach in academic integrity for this class are the following:** 1st-offense: a zero for the work in question, (*i.e.,* report, exam, etc.). 2nd-offense: a zero for the work & a drop in the course letter grade by one whole level (*e.g.,* an A would thuswise become a B). 3rd-offense: a failing grade for the class, without any opportunity to drop the course (thereby being unable to avoid the fail from appearing on the official academic transcript).

The Rensselaer Student Handbook & the Dean of Students Office webpage (http://doso.rpi.edu/update.do?artcenterkey=676) defines various forms of academic dishonesty. Please familiarize yourself with these serious matters, and ask for clarification if you have any questions/concerns about what constitutes academic dishonesty.
