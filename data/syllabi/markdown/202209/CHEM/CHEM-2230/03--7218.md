### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

Students enrolled in this course are required to be concurrently enrolled in the Organic Chemistry I lecture course, or to have successfully completed it. In Organic Chemistry Laboratory I, students will learn practical ways to put their theoretical chemical knowledge to work, formulate and test hypotheses, attain knowledge of and develop skills in fundamental laboratory techniques applicable to the study, separation, purification, synthesis and identification of organic compounds, and practice scientific communication through proper notebook keeping and writing laboratory reports.

Due to the current circumstances where we are not able to meet in person, all labs and recitation will be conducted online. Some of the labs will use a simulated platform through Labster and the Royal Society of Chemistry, utilizing the latest HTML5 technology. These lab simulations will focus primarily on applying your lecture gained knowledge to a hands-on but virtual laboratory experience. The remaining experiments will be conducted based on detailed step-by-step pictures of the experiment, technique focused videos, simulations and videos of similar experiments as well as experimental data and results. Each lab will be performed during the laboratory sessions to ensure that students get sufficient help and guidance from the instructors and teaching assistants.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Student Learning Outcomes:** (1) Students will learn the importance of proper experiment set-up, including pre-laboratory activities, note taking, data analysis and reporting of results.  
(2) Students will show competence with chemical laboratory techniques including mass measurements, volume measurements, solution preparation, dilution, titration, instrumental methods, and synthetic techniques. Spectroscopic methods will be used; these include Infrared and Nuclear Magnetic Resonance spectroscopies. Separation methods of thin layer/column chromatography and gas chromatography will be used.  
(3) Students will show competence in handling and processing laboratory (experimental) data.  
(4) Students will be able to search for relevant information in the chemical literature.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

Each submitted report must be your own work and not copied from, or shared with, other students except when specifically authorized by your instructor.

In this course, academic dishonesty includes “fudging” laboratory data such as weights, melting points, etc. The fabrication of scientific data and information is an egregious violation of the underpinnings of scientific inquiry and the scientific method. Students are also expected to carry out all experimentation and data analysis independently, i.e., without referencing information collected by or conclusions deduced by others.

A violation of this policy will be treated as outlined below.  
**Penalties for Academic Integrity Violations**

A first instance of academic dishonesty will result in a grade of 0% on the experiment during which the dishonesty took place and the incident will be reported to the Dean of Students Office. A second instance of academic dishonesty will result in automatic failure of the course without option to withdraw, and again the incident will be reported to the Dean of Students Office. Procedures for appealing an academic integrity decision can be found at http://doso.rpi.edu/update.do? artcenterkey=676.
