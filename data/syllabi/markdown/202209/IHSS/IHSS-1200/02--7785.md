## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Principles of Economics is a one semester survey course in micro- and macroeconomics which has two goals: (1) to introduce a wide variety of concepts and applications in the broad subject of economics, and (2) to function in its special Rensselaer designation as a HASS Inquiry and Data Intensive Course. Economics is the study of our choices. Traditionally, these choices have been framed as how best to employ scarce resources to produce goods and services and distribute them for consumption. To describe these choices we will introduce the concepts of opportunity cost, demand and supply theory, and market structures and consider the role of government in resource allocation choices. Also presented are macroeconomic topics such as Gross Domestic Product, economic growth, inflation, unemployment, long-run trends and short-run fluctuations, money, and banking. As a HASS Inquiry course, a foremost objective will be to evaluate multiple diverse perspectives on contemporary and complex global issues and address their implications to social equity. Furthermore, the course is designated as a Data Intensive course and provides opportunity to practice analysis of economic data.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

MyLab Assignments - 45%, (B) Average of four exam scores OR score on final exam - 45%, (C) HASS Inquiry Score – 10%

Final Grades: The letter grade breakdown is as follows, rounded: A 94 to 100; A- 90 to 93; B+ 87 to 89; B 84 to 86; B- 80 to 83; C+ 77 to 79; C 74 to 76; C- 70 to 73; D+ 67 to 69; D 60 to 66; F 59 and below. Grades are rounded to the nearest whole number.

MyLab Assignments: Assignments using the Pearson MyLab online system will be due quarterly. For the first three sets of assignments, there is a 7-day period of time following the due date during which assignments may be completed late for a 10% penalty on all questions unanswered by the due date. After the 7-day late period, you must contact the TA with an explanation of extenuating circumstances. The fourth and last set of assignments allows a shortened late completion period of only 4 days. All Reading Quizzes and Homeworks are available from the start of the semester through their due date/time. Due dates are cut-off dates for the submission of work; due dates are not meant to be dates for you to begin or even finish work. It is expected that all students will complete work in a time efficient manner according to Course Calendar on the last page of this syllabus. This means that work should be completed on a weekly basis and in advance of the due date. Any student who saves work to complete on the due date must expect that technical difficulties will arise and prevent the finishing of said work by the cut-off time and credit will be lost.

Tests: There will be four tests administered online via MyLab. The basis for tests will be all assigned textbook readings, all lecture material, and all MyLab assignments. Each test will be made available for a period of seven days; refer to MyLab for specific availability dates. There is no late window for tests. Test format is open book/note, multiple choice, and numerical answer. Each student will receive a unique test. Tests must be completed in one session and will be timed at 110 minutes.

**MAKEUP TEST POLICY:** *Makeup tests will only be allowed for absences having an official Rensselaer excuse issued by the Health Center or the Dean of Students, and submitted to the professor by two days after the test. The makeup test is scheduled during the week after the test. If you miss a test and do not have an official Rensselaer excuse, you must take the optional final exam to improve your test average.*

Optional Final Exam: An optional final exam will be offered as a way for students to attempt to improve their grade. The final exam will cover all course material. It will not simply be a repeat of questions from the mid-semester tests. If you take the final exam, the score you obtain on the final exam will be compared with the average of your mid-semester tests. You will be awarded the higher of these scores as your grade for the test portion of the course. Therefore, it cannot hurt you to take the optional final exam. *If you miss any of the four mid-semester* *test(s) and do not have a proper excuse (see policy on makeup tests above), then plan on taking the final exam to improve your grade.*

HASS Inquiry: This course is identified as a HASS (Humanities, Arts, and Social Sciences) Inquiry course. The purpose of a HAAS Inquiry course is to provide opportunity for the discourse of diverse and divergent ways of thinking about complex global issues. In particular, we strive to be thorough in understanding many perspectives with regards to important issues and the outcomes they create for society, culture, the environment, the economy, and many other realms. Additionally, we must evaluate the ethical responsibilities of protecting the public’s health, safety, and welfare. These are the specific objectives charged to a HASS Inquiry course. Being that this HASS Inquiry course is also an economics course, we will gain additional benefit from learning the ways in which economics helps us to understand and evaluate events, phenomena, and outcomes. Instructions for the HASS Inquiry assignments are available on LMS. **There is no makeup assignment for HASS Inquiry; if you miss these assignments, then you will lose credit for this part of the course and it will be impossible to get a final course grade of ‘A’**. The purpose of the HASS Inquiry assignment is to collectively share thoughts and ideas. For this reason, work submitted late, no matter how well-written, is not useful and will not earn credit for HASS Inquiry. Written work is strictly monitored among current and former students; any student who copies work from another student is violating the academic integrity policy of this course and will be penalized accordingly – the see the Policy on Academic Integrity, below.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

By enrolling in this course, all students are committed to absolute agreement to and understanding of the course policy on academic integrity.

- \- With regards to MyLab assignments, teamwork is allowed. However, students are expected to put forth their own effort and not simply take answers from other students. By doing one’s own work on assignments, one gains deeper understanding and is better prepared to do well on tests.
- \- It is expected that students will do their own work to complete tests. Tests are open book/open note. Teamwork on tests is counterproductive because each student receives a unique and timed test.
- \- Plagiarism involves the intentional copy of sentences or paragraphs from published or online sources without citation or from work completed by a current or former student. Written work submitted will be monitored for plagiarism from any source, including present and former students. Plagiarism, either in entirety or in part, is a violation of the policy on academic integrity.

In general and to a great extent, there is a degree of “you get out of it what you put into it.” Faculty cannot possibly monitor all dishonest activity, nor can they force students to learn. It is your responsibility to get your money’s value out of this and any college class. **Any student found to engage in dishonest activity in any aspect of this course will be penalized with failure of the course.**
