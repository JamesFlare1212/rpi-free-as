## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Welcome to *Well-being: Cultivating Curiosity!*

From the course catalog: How do we maintain a sense of well-being in our lives? Each person’s path to well-being in body and mind is unique—arising from an awareness of our needs, goals and what we find fulfilling. We will use the theme of curiosity to explore what makes us tick, what makes us feel balanced, stressed, or calm. This interdisciplinary course uses practice-based learning, in-class writing, lectures, creative play, and reading. The class is Communication Intensive.

Additional info: *Attendance and curious participation* in class is required. *Come!* Your attendance is crucial because much of the class is centered on experiential learning right in the classroom.

Experiential learning can be a rather slow process. It can be frustrating, because often we can *mentally* understand something quickly, but the body and mind may need time to embody this knowledge. We acknowledge that each person in class arrives with different backgrounds, skill-sets, curiosities, and bodies/brains/minds. Please be patient with yourself, your classmates, and your professor.

Our class is geared toward heightening your awareness of experiences and to develop your writing about these experiences, building your own well-being toolbox, as well as nourishing intellectual insights about the material. You will have time during each class meeting to encounter a wide variety of experiences in order to generate ideas and to write on the spot. This process of generating text is expected to be exploratory and even messy at times. Know that generative writing stages can provide new insights and you will have opportunities to re-write, edit and compose as necessary. As a Communication Intensive course, students will receive detailed feedback on at least one draft before the final submission of papers. Expect to write often in class and for graded assignments.

We will be reading two books this semester; one fiction and one non-fiction. Although they are on different topics, they are similar in that we will be considering different lived experiences described in both and relating these topics to well-being from different viewpoints. We will be reading other short chapters too. Thinking about our own identities in relation to the world around us as well as the authors and characters in our readings is a key component of our class.

In addition to daily writing in class, students should expect to regularly work in pairs or small groups and engage fully in class (discussions, dialogues, in-class generative and creative projects, etc). Please expect that you will be asked to share your thoughts, questions, and work each class period. Be prepared to contribute to our regular conversations about the material covered in our class as well as the readings. In doing so, you will also gain firsthand experience in a social justice pedagogical approach called Intergroup Dialogue.

We will be using different analog and digital techniques for communication and honing awareness of your well-being. In addition, you will have an opportunity to opt in to an evaluation of your well-being using different methods to track the dynamics of your own well-being.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is mandatory. Attendance will be taken during each class meeting. Any unexcused absences will be reflected by a lower attendance grade. Certain situations do arise that preclude coming to class that are allowable, which would be excused absences. For example, if you are not feeling well or cannot come to class because of other health related issues, it is expected that you contact me and do not come to the classroom; in this case, you can attend via WebEx, but you do need to let me know in advance. If you need to obtain an official excuse for an extended absence, please contact the Office of Student Success success@rpi.edu. Please note: If you are late, not fully participating, or leave early (without prior approval), points will be deducted from your attendance/participation grade.

### Other Course Policies:

***Attendance and lateness*** As unexcused absences will lower your grade so will being late. Late attendance to class (more than 5 minutes) is disruptive to our community: 2 lates = 1 absence. You must attend ***all*** project presentation days, even if you have already presented your own work. If you are absent for a presentation day your grade will be lowered one letter grade.

***Class participation*** To facilitate community building and class participations, please be present and engaged whether you are in physical classroom (or remote learning environment). Participation in class include discussions, in-class and out-of-class non-graded writing, and other expressive projects. You will be expected to participate in each class. *Please: no texting, emailing, cell phone calls, or other mediated communications during class* (or you will lose participation points).

***Lateness policy*** Late assignments will be accepted, but for each day an assignment is late, 3 points will be deducted from the grade. *I will only accept assignments up to one week late.* Please email me in the case of extenuating circumstances and difficulties completing assignments BEFORE they are due.

## Policy on Collaboration and Cheating/plagiarism In this class, all exams and assignments that are turned in for a grade must be the student’s own work and completed for this class only (i.e. do not turn in work you have turned in for any other class). In cases where help was received (applies to graded assignments only, and not exams), a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment or exam that is in violation of this policy will result in penalties. Penalties for a single offense of cheating on an assignment or exam will result in a zero grade for that material. In instances of such cheating over multiple occasions by the same student, this can result in failure of the course. If you have any questions or require clarification of these policies, please see me before turning in the material.

**Professor Walf’s I.D.E.A. Statement** In this classroom, we will acknowledge and aim to respect **i**nclusivity, **d**iversity, **e**quity, and **a**wareness. In our classroom, all students are welcome and will be supported regardless of race/ethnicity, gender identities, gender expressions, sexual orientation, socio-economic status, age, disabilities, religion, regional background, neurodiversity, citizenship status, nationality and the many other diverse identities that we each bring here. Indeed, the diversity that the students bring to this classroom should be viewed as a strength, resource, and benefit.

We will be focusing on the material from primarily a (neuro)science perspective. We must be aware that science is not always objective and unbiased and historically has not included representation of diverse individuals in the experiments or the researchers themselves. For example, individual differences between human subjects in experiments were often minimized or considered confounds because of the added complexity they may have contributed to the analysis of the data. However, in this classroom, we will strive to integrate a diverse set of experiences for a more comprehensive understanding of the material in this course, despite the potential for added complexity. An important part of a college education is learning about the complexity of various issues. We will also expand our understanding by hearing from diverse voices in the field through our readings and discussions of the material. Moreover, students in this class are encouraged to participate during class meetings and all efforts will be made for students to have an equitable opportunity to contribute and succeed in a supportive learning environment. Our classroom will represent a diversity of individual beliefs, backgrounds, and experiences. We all must listen and show care and respect for every other member of this class even if we do not necessarily agree. I will be facilitating our classroom conversations using a social justice pedagogical approach called Intergroup Dialogue, which you will learn more about this semester. Please honor the uniqueness of our classroom community and appreciate the opportunity we have to learn from one another. If you have any questions or concerns, please discuss with me.
