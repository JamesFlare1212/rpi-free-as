## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This class is an introduction to the field of media studies. As a survey of approaches to addressing the cultural impact of mass media—including television, film, radio, games, print, the internet, and social media—this course aims to increase your media literacy through analysis of specific media texts, as well as a discussion of broad topics such as commercialization, ideology, and representation. The course will serve as a ‘sampler plate’ of approaches to media studies for students from all majors, and as a first step/core course for majors and minors in Communication, Media, and Design. As an inquiry class, we will consider the functions media play in shaping our individual and social identities. We’ll think through how they work--on each of us individually, as well as on groups of us collectively--and we’ll take action towards becoming more engaged in media processes.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Course Goals*

1\) You will develop tools for ‘critical media engagement,’ learning how to analyze, interpret and appreciate the capabilities and functions of different media forms. We’ll explore the distinctive features of various media—their inherent strengths or limitations, and their institutional or structural biases. You’ll gain an understanding of the historical and socioeconomic origins of modern media, and their profound impact on contemporary society. A particular emphasis in this version of the class will be on representation in media; here, the concept of ‘representation’ is loosely interpreted, including but not limited to representation of identities (race, ethnicity, gender, sexuality and so on). We’re trying to understand how our ‘re-presenting’ of reality shapes our understanding of it.

2\) As a complement to these critical tools, we will enhance your ‘reflexive media awareness,’ a term that refers to your processual and dialogic understanding of your relationship to media. In other words, you should gain a deeper understanding of how media shapes us as individuals, as groups and communities, and as cultures.

3\) You will hone your communicative/generative skills by articulating and refining your thoughts in oral, written, and presentational forms. If you are so inclined, you’ll have the chance to create media works in various formats as a way of engaging with concepts we encounter.You should leave the class able to more fully articulate your experience of media, contextualize it, and empathize with others’ perspectives.

4\) Finally, an underlying theme of the class is your involvement in a collaborative learning process; the class will be shaped by your interests and prior knowledge as a community, as well as by my disciplinary, personal and pedagogical expertise. Through the use of non-traditional educational strategies, we’ll engage in meta-examinations of the learning process, and we’ll consider how the course itself functions as a ‘medium’ for learning.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

ASSESSMENT: ‘MEDIA POINTS’-BASED ‘GAMEFUL’ GRADING

Introduction:Grading for this class will unfold a little differently than you’re used to. Using what might be called a ‘gameful’ approach, your performance in the class is based on the points that you earn, much like in a game system (a video game, a table-top role-playing game, or what have you). Like in some games, or in many popular films/TV, there’s even a ‘clock’ defined by milestones in the semester.

Everyone begins with 0 points. At the start of the semester, there are 410,000+ points to be earned. As you accomplish tasks in the class, you build up points towards your final grade. There are central tasks for everyone to complete (like the readings, daily assignments, and final projects), but there are also some ‘media awareness’ activities (like a media journal and ‘a day without media’), as well as ‘solo adventures’ and ‘side quests’--opportunities to learn according to your interests, to apply knowledge in new ways, or to create your own explorations. Outside of the final project and the basic participation grades, you aren’t required to do any specific task; however, you will need to do some of those if you want to achieve a high ‘rank’ in the class (which translates to a high grade).

Goal of this approach to assessment:

What I’m looking to do here is to get you to consider a different way of thinking about what it means to participate in a class. This is a class about a topic for which there isn’t a single body of knowledge or point of view, so this grading system is meant to encourage active engagement with the material. I want you to be active in your own assessment. The ranks are not traditional grade percentages, but rather levels of achievement in building knowledge of media studies, and in critical and creative thinking. Think of a grading system as a ‘medium’ for your evaluation/assessment; by using an alternate method, I want to put into relief the nature of the assessment systems you’ve used in the past. I welcome your feedback on the system I’m using.

You can figure out what kind of a rank you are looking for, and work towards that goal by accumulating points. ‘Medal’ Based Scoring

Assignments will be assessed using a ‘medal’ system. For some larger projects, we may establish a more detailed rubric for grading, or reach an understanding through discussion. In many cases, particularly for larger assignments, you may be able to revise an assignment to improve its medal.

Medal Points % Description

Diamond* 120% A sparkling exemplar for the class, full of dazzling insight

Platinum* 110% A perceptive, clever, and resilient exploration of assignment

Gold 100% Weighty, polished, addressing all required aspects of assignment

Silver 90% A response with clarity and strength, but maybe a tiny tarnish

Bronze 80% Relatively solid response; some clarity, some weaknesses

Copper 70% Mostly complete, but may bend under the stress of examination

Nickel 60% Partial or relatively flimsy assignment that needs strengthening

Tin\*\* 50% Dull or lifeless response, crumples in the breeziest of discussions

*To receive a ‘Platinum’ medal requires that you go above and beyond the requirements of the assignment, and the ‘Diamond’ requires even more in-depth work of great quality.

\*\*Below ‘Tin’ means you simply failed to turn in the assignment, or it’s too incomplete to score
