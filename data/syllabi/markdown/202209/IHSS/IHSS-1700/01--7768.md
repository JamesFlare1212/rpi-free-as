## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

From opera arias to indigenous folk tunes to hip-hop, the act of setting words to music has produced some of the world’s most popular and enduring art in the form of songwriting, with many of the world’s greatest songs brought into existence with little more than imagination and a guitar or a beat. This course examines the wide variety of elements present in the creation of a well-written song, while honing a higher sense of critique when analyzing the work of both successful and obscure songwriters. Song recordings, videos, and lively class discussion frame a closer look at multiple lyrical and creative techniques, and students are encouraged to experiment on a grand scale, without fear, when writing at least one song per week.

Students get positive and useful creative feedback along with opportunities for collaboration, while learning how to make their own effective demonstration recordings and communicate well with musicians and recordists in a studio session. Though it focuses primarily on aspects of songwriting as a popular and commercial art form, this course can also be seen as an effective study in creatively effective structure building, in many ways analogous to design forms in architecture, mathematics, chemistry, and computer sciences. Or it can just help you finish that surefire hit you’ve been working on.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students who are songwriters will create a portfolio of best works, and discover a wealth of strategies for creativity in the study of great writers, lyricists, and songwriting teams, while becoming more familiar with the enormous body of great (and not-so-great) works available through streaming and downloading. Students who are more connoisseurs of songwriting will still participate in the songwriting process, and possibly find their appreciation of the artform deepened and enriched by its closer examination, potentially discovering a voice therein through collaboration, creative promotion, and music production.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Students will write 8 songs and one paper, plus one class presentation. Topics of lectures include Song Critique, Lyrics 1, Lyrics 2, Melody, Chords, Chord Progressions, Rhythm, Phrasing, Song Form, Collaboration, Plagiarizing, Copyright, Publishing, Making Demos.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Develop an improved understanding of the art of songwriting, and the works of great songwriters past and present.
- Accumulate key recording techniques to effectively capture and affix their works to a tangible medium.
- Experience in collaborating with other artists, learning how to create with others successfully.
- Improve general writing, presentation, and communication skills.
- Learn to generously and insightfully offer and receive high-quality critique to/from other creatives, with the goal of improving the work.
- Become able to conceive, write, and record a complete new song, on a fixed schedule.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

TEN (10) SONGS: Music file and lyric doc. Can be collaborative (Song 5 & 7 collaboration required). Students must turn in a professional portfolio of their four (4) best songs on the last day of class.  
SONGWRITER PAPER- Students will select a songwriter, and produce 1000-word paper focusing on the individualized processes and methods employed by the songwriter, with music files and lyrical examples provided.  
SONG PRESENTATION- Students will provide a 20-minute analysis and presentation on a chosen “Popular Song” to the class. These will be spread out over the second half of the semester.  
SONGWRITER PORTFOLIO- Students will meet the song deadlines as presented, and turn in a professional portfolio of their four best songs, complete with demonstration recordings and lyrics, on the last day of class.  
PRESENCE AND PARTICIPATION- The success of this class depends on the presence and participation of each student when discussing the reading and critiques.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

The first unexcused absence I’ll let go. Any work due on that date is still expected on time, any missed quizzes score a zero. The second time will make me look askance at you, and wonder why you need to miss this class you signed up for. Third time gets a probationary warning, and the fourth will result in a **half-grade drop off your final grade**, with any further absences another **half-grade drop for each**. Excuses from Student Success are required for illness and extended absences. Communication with the lecturer is key; unexcused absences can be made excused (emergencies, personal difficulties, etc.) with adequate and timely explanation.

### Other Course Policies:

Assignments are due on the dates indicated. **Ten percent of your grade will be deducted for any assignment submitted after the due date** and **ten percent for each day late thereafter**, with none accepted more than five days after the due date. Songs turned in on time and named properly get a score of "100," and score "95" if named improperly. Any missed quiz will result in a score of 0 for that quiz, unless due to an excused absence, in which it is the responsibility of the student to make suitable arrangements with me for making it up.

Students who miss class for extended periods of time without permission or explanation will be reported to the Dean of Students Office or the Department of Public Safety for support and assistance, as needed. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

This class is about creation, so there is wiggle room regarding sources, but don't abuse it. You are welcome to use outside materials for your songs (beats, poems, etc.) as long as you cite them. Songwriting is notorious for accusations of plagiarizing which is really easy to catch with modern technology. If you can get it by me you're good, but good luck with the originator or the Library of Congress! If you are taking this class and NOT involved with writing your own songs, just getting by using others work without citation, well, you suck. I promise you'll hear from me eventually and there could be grade damage, but this will be on a case-by-case basis, just don't please. Grades in this class are only significantly affected by a) attendance, b) submitting assignments on time, and c) participating in class discussion and feedback.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

This course requires being able to record simple music demonstration recordings with vocals and accompaniment, and export them as sound files. Phones, tablets, and computers have all the required tools to achieve this, but it is up to the individual songwriter to decide how they will use their available technologies. Some basic recording application learning will be necessary outside of class.
