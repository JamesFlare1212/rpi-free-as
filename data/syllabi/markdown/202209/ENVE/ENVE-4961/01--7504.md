## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is an undergraduate level course that examines the water reuse technologies and the current legislations and applications of water reuse around the globe. Throughout the course, students will have specific homework assignments with questions about topics discussed in the classroom as well as specific activities in class to develop projects for certain advanced treatment technologies. This course will also have a final project encouraging students to critically evaluate case studies about specific applications of reclaimed water. Besides, the delivery of a technical report about the results and technical feasibility of the case study proposed, students will be encouraged to present their findings and sharing with their classmates, helping to develop not only teamwork and technical skills, but also documentation and communication skills.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The objective of this course is to provide a working knowledge about water use and reuse legislation, water quality requirements, effluent generation, treatment technologies, water conservation, and reuse programs. The course will present technical analysis of projects for water optimization and water reuse and will introduce treatment methods to achieve different levels of water quality, considering the many different final effluent uses. Each method will be presented and evaluated considering the advantages and disadvantages of its application, as well as recommended practices for each situation.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand the basis for state and federal regulations associated with water quality and potential risk contaminants that may compromise the water reuse practice.
- Compare different advanced technologies for treating water and wastewater and choose the most adequate ones to remove critical contaminants for specific applications.
- Identify potential opportunities to apply methodologies for water reuse or opportunities to adapt current operating systems to water reuse practices.
- Distinguish the practices of direct potable reuse from indirect potable reuse and the design and operation of environmental buffers to ensure water quality.
- Recognize the possible applications of reuse water in contexts of urban and non-urban uses.
- Develop critical thinking and analytical abilities to evaluate case studies and present the results of your considerations to an audience.

## Exam Policy

Exams will be closed book in this course. All necessary reference tables and equations will be provided. The use of mobile devices (cellphones, computers, etc.) is prohibited during exams.

A missed exam will result in a grade of zero unless permission is granted before the scheduled exam time and is documented through the Student Experience Office (Academy Hall, x8022, se@rpi.edu). In such cases, the weight of other exams will be equally increased to account for the missing grade.

The midterm assessment for this course will be based on HW assignments and Exam #1 until late October. Students who have concerns with their midterm performance should meet with the instructor to discuss approaches to improving their performance in future work or the possibility of dropping the course before the drop deadline.

## Homework Policy

Students should submit clear and legible paper copies of their homework. Students are encouraged to work in groups, but each student is expected to submit their own work. However, students should not consult online resources that contain solution manuals as it constitutes a violation of the Academic Integrity policies. Reading of course textbook is highly recommended to supplement topics covered in class and for additional practice exercises.

Homework should be submitted to the instructor at the beginning of the class period on the dates specified on the Course Calendar. Thirty percent of the assignment grade will be deducted for lateness for homework submitted within one week after the due date. Homework submitted after 1-week will receive a zero grade.

It is highly recommended that students work diligently on their homework and in-class exercises to master the course material and do well in the exams. Furthermore, solutions to homework questions will be posted on LMS and should be reviewed in preparation for exams.

## Grade Appeals

Grade appeal for each exam, lab report, and homework submission will **ONLY** be accepted up to one week after graded work has been returned to students.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work, although students are encouraged to work with each other by collaborating on assignments (see item 2 under *Homework Policy* above). Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy (e.g., one student copying work done by another student or a student copying from a previously developed solution) will result in a grade of zero for that particular assignment.
