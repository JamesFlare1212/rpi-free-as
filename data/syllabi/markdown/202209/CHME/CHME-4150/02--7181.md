## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Catalogue Description: [The first term of] a two-term laboratory course on experimental analysis of the operations and processes of chemical engineering. Emphasis is placed on planning of experiments, data evaluation, and report writing.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Course Objective: The purpose of this course is to provide senior chemical engineering students with hands-on experience operating and analyzing common chemical engineering unit operations. The unit operations covered in this laboratory course build on the theoretical material covered in previous and concurrent chemical engineering courses. Emphasis is placed on planning and executing the experiments, analyzing data, and dissemination of results via written reports. As a primary goal of this course, you should develop an aptitude for performing unit operations by applying fundamental chemical engineering knowledge to their operations. You will also develop communication skills necessary to work in a team or group.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Lectures and quizzes on:  
1\) Laboratory safety  
2\) Writing of report sections, e.g. abstracts; spelling, grammar, formatting  
3\) Diversity in background, skills, and perspectives (essay assignment)  
4\) Experimental statistics and error propagation  
Experiments on three of the following five topics:

1\) Heat Exchangers  
2\) Pumping & piping systems  
3\) Photocatalysis, reaction kinetics  
4\) Rheology and Fluid Flow  
5\) Virtual reality: chemical reactor design

Experiment Schedule & Report Due Dates  
Each laboratory team will conduct three experiments conducted during the course of the semester, and reports will be due two to three weeks after the date of completion of the experiment.

For reports #1 and #2, your team will complete two of the following physical experiments:

Heat Exchanger

Pumping

Photocatalysis

Rheology and Fluid Flow

For report #3, students have the option of completing Photocatalytic Reactor Design in Virtual Reality or else completing a third physical experiment.

Each experiment will generally consist of a three-week cycle:

**Week 1: Conducting the Experiment**

In-person laboratory experiments will be supervised by a teaching assistant.

**Week 2: Individual Deliverables due. Interim Meeting.**

Following the experiment, all students have seven days to prepare calculations and write sections of the report on their own. Submission of individual contributions through LMS for grading is required by the seventh day, and no individual extensions will be granted. *Please plan ahead!*

An interim meeting with the instructor will take place during the second week. The Interim Meeting Schedule will be posted on LMS once the experiments are assigned to each group. *Students should bring completed calculations, tables of data, and figures to this meeting.* Each group member is to be familiar with the calculations and data analysis by this time. Be prepared to ask any questions about the data or analysis and the instructor’s expectations for the final report and/or presentation, which is due the following week. Generally, these meetings will take place from 5:00 to 6:20 PM in Ricketts 206 (if possible) or via WebEx, and several laboratory teams will be present for each meeting. At least two students from each group should attend the meeting; the meeting will be recorded for those who cannot attend.

**Week 3: Final Report**

Each experiment concludes with a final report due one week after the interim meeting. *Detailed guidelines for preparing reports will be posted on LMS and covered during lecture time.*

The final grade on each report will be divided between **individual contributions (30%),** **technical writing aspects (35 %)** and **scientific content, correctness, and uncertainty analysis (35 %)**. Dr. Hedden will grade each lab report based on technical writing considerations including English grammar, writing style, organization, completeness, presentation of Figures/Tables, and formatting. In addition, each of the three experiments has a supervising instructor or TA responsible for the grading of scientific/technical correctness and uncertainty analysis.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

All students must be present for the three laboratory sessions assigned to their groups; a missed experiment will result in a grade of Incomplete for the course until the experiment is performed.

### Other Course Policies:

**Policy on Laboratory Safety Quiz.**

*Students must score 80 % or higher on the laboratory safety quiz before they are permitted to enter the laboratory. One re-take will be permitted, but a second failure to score 80 % or higher will result in expulsion from the course.* **Policy on Student Laboratory Teams and Division of Labor.**

The experiments in this course require multiple participants for successful completion. Lab groups will consist of four students, though a few groups of three may be required. Students are free to choose their own groups, provided their schedules are compatible enough to permit team-based completion of laboratory reports. *CHME 4150 is a writing-intensive course, and it is essential that everyone contributes to the writing of all three laboratory reports.*

**Policy Regarding Individual Contribution Assignments.**  
After seven days, each team member should have done part of the calculations and completed the writing of at least one report section on their own. Team members are expected to bring substantial calculations and writing to their first team-based writing session. Your individual work submitted through LMS will serve as a record of what you brought to your first team writing session. *No make-up assignments can be given because we cannot turn back the clock and repair harm done to your teammates due to lack of effort on your part.* Individual contribution grades reflect your effort level and the quality of work done to support your team. Failure to attend your afternoon laboratory session and/or poor preparation for the laboratory session may result in a reduction of your Individual Contribution grade.

**Policy Regarding Report Preparation.** Laboratory reports are team-based writing assignments. You will find that your grades on the reports depend to a large extent on whether your teammates communicate well and attend the meetings. *If any teammates fail to attend the group writing sessions, the remaining team members are expected to complete the work on their own.* There will be an opportunity to report poor meeting attendance and/or lack of effort on the peer review form distributed with the final exam. *Teammates' ratings of effort, quality of work, and meeting attendance may be used at the instructor's discretion in determining an individual's course grade.*

Failing to contribute to writing of a given laboratory is justification for receiving a grade of zero on the report, even if you were present when the experiment was being performed. Students who contribute less to the writing of their reports than expected by their teammates may receive substantial deductions on the final report grade; there is no stipulation that all team members will receive the same grade on a given report.

**Policy on Final Examination.**

Please plan ahead and do not schedule job interviews during the final examination. RPI generally will not excuse students from final examinations, and it is unlikely that a conflict final exam will be offered.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

All work in this course is to be done independently by each lab group. No collaboration between different groups is permitted; copying from previous years' laboratory reports is considered cheating and will result in disciplinary action. Questions should be addressed to the Instructor or TAs. Evidence of collaboration or falsification of laboratory data will be dealt with according to the Student Handbook. Additionally, a signed ‘Foreman Report’ must be completed and attached to each final report documenting the contribution of each individual member in the group.  
Plagiarism from textbooks, internet sources, or previous years' laboratory reports is prohibited and may result in a grade of zero for the assignment and/or a grade of "F" in the course at the instructor's discretion.
