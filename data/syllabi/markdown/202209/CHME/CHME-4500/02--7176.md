## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Principles of kinetics, reactor design, and analysis for both homogeneous and heterogeneous (catalytic) systems. Topics include design for multiple reaction networks (optimum selectivity), analysis of simple reactor combinations, and design of isothermal, adiabatic, optimum temperature profile reactor, and thermodynamics for chemical reactor design.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Rawlings, J.B. and J.G. Ekerdt, Chemical Reactor Analysis and Design Fundamentals, 2nd ed., Nob Hill Publishing (2014), ISBN-13: 978-0975937723 --- available at Amazon.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The purpose of this course is to introduce undergraduate students to the theory and practice of chemical reactor analysis and design. The MATLAB package will be used, and many of the homework assignments will require computer-based simulations. The course is often taught in a studio format, combining lectures, discussions and computer-based simulations in a single laptop-based classroom. *Thus, students should plan on bringing their* *laptop computers to each class*.

An agreement for a new student version of MATLAB should be completed and a new license installed: https://itssc.rpi.edu/hc/en-us/articles/360001212511-Available-Licensed-Software-

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate an understanding of the modeling, design and operation of batch stirred tank reactors, continuous stirred tank reactors (CSTR), and plug-flow reactors (PFR), and compare the performance characteristics of the different reactor types.
- Demonstrate an understanding of multiple steady-states, reactor stability, and runaways.
- Demonstrate an understanding of the safety implications of reactor design.
- Use computer packages (such as MATLAB) to solve reactor models.

HOMEWORK ASSIGNMENTS AND PROJECTS

Homework will be uploaded on LMS. The majority of the homework assignments and projects will be performed in groups of TWO students; each student is responsible for understanding the entire assignment. Homework is due at the beginning of class. Late homework, including homework turned in during or after class will be accepted, with a penalty of 20% for each day late. *Although an assignment may have several problems, often two will be selected at random for grading.*

*Groups may discuss the problems with other groups, but are not allowed to share solutions (MATLAB m-files, etc.). Violations will be handled in accordance with the student handbook.*

Note about Showing Work:In this class, the process of getting to an answer is as important (if not more) than the actual answer. Therefore, you *must show and justify* all of your work to receive credit. If you do not show your work, you will receive zero credit (even if the answer is correct).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Late homework, including homework turned in during or after class, will be accepted, with a penalty of 20% for each day late.  
Grade Appeals

A student may appeal a grade by attaching a memo to teaching assistants to the specific exam or homework assignment that is being appealed. This memo must state the specific reason(s) for the regrade. Verbal appeal is not accepted. The student has one week after the grades have been assigned to contest the grade. The regrading process will be based on proper physical and chemical implication(s) of the answer to the given question. Be professional in the appeal process.

Use of Electronic Devices

All mobile devices (cell/smart phones, computers, pagers, etc.) must be stored securely away during lecture and are not be used unless specifically directed otherwise by the instructor. Use of (or ANY interaction with) a mobile device during an exam without explicit permission of the instructor will be interpreted as the illicit transfer of exam data, will be considered an act of cheating and will be treated as such.
