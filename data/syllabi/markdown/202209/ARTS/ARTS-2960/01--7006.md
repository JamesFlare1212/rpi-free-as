## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

From opera arias to indigenous folk tunes to modern hip-hop, the act of putting words and music together in a short form has produced some of the world’s most popular and enduring art in the form of songwriting, with many of the world’s greatest songs brought into existence with little more than imagination and a guitar or a beat. This course examines the wide variety of elements present in the creation of a well-written song, while honing a higher sense of critique when analyzing the work of both successful and obscure songwriters. Song recordings and videos plus lively class discussion frame a closer look at multiple lyrical, musical, and general creative techniques, and students are encouraged to experiment on a grand scale, without fear, while writing (roughly) one song per week.

Students give and receive consistent positive and useful creative feedback, while learning how to make their own demonstration recordings, and how to communicate effectively with other creators, sometimes in collaboration. At the midpoint of the class, we discuss how certain songs have had impacts on societal and even political changes world-wide, especially in the US. Though it focuses primarily on aspects of songwriting as a popular and commercial art form, this course can also be seen as a study in short-form creative structure building in a medium that has, in many ways, design forms analogous to architecture, mathematics, chemistry, and computer sciences. Or, this class can just help you finish that surefire hit you’ve been working on, like, forever.

The class will be split into two groups, which allows for all student works to be heard and discussed every due week, and lectures will be in-person with both groups attending together. Class business will be conducted on Webex, where student song recordings and demos will be turned in and presented to all, papers will be delivered to me to be graded, and all written feedback posted. Students will be expected to create basic demo recordings of their songs using readily available technology on phones, tablets, and computers, and upload them—along with lyrics in a word doc or pdf—to the Webex **before due date class periods**. Please note: these demos do not need to be of any particular quality level, they just need to have understandable lyrics and have just enough music present to be able to effectively teach someone how to play and sing the song, and to “affix” the song for copyright protection.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Students personally select a media resource--book, movie, game, etc.--that is not (necessarily) music-based, to use as an inspiration guide for songwriting throughout the semester.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Student songwriters will create a portfolio of best works, and discover a wealth of strategies for creativity in the study of great music composers, lyricists, and songwriting teams, while becoming more familiar with the enormous body of great (and some not-so-great) works available through streaming and downloading. Students who are more connoisseurs of songwriting than creators can still participate in the songwriting process, and possibly find their appreciation of the artform deepened and enriched by its closer examination, potentially discovering a personal creative voice therein through song production, promotion, and publishing.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Develop improved understanding of the art of writing songs, as well as the works of great songwriters, past and present.
- Accumulate key techniques for recording, and effectively capture and affix their works to a medium.
- Learn how to effectively collaborate with others on a creative project.
- Improve general writing, presentation, and communication skills.
- Develop techniques for giving and receiving high-quality critique to/from other creatives to achieve positive improvements to works.
- Better understand copyright, publishing, and emerging technologies for song collaboration, promotion, and distribution.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

SONG ANALYSIS PRESENTATION- Students will provide a PowerPoint/Keynote presentation on an analysis of a chosen song on the class Webex space.

SONGS AND SOCIETY PAPER- Students will select a song that had a large effect on society, and write a 1000-word paper analyzing the song, the songwriters, the historical context, and how the song impacted people.

8 SONGS/SONGWRITER PORTFOLIO- Students will meet the eight (8) song deadlines as presented, and turn in a well-presented digital portfolio of their four (4) best songs, complete with demonstration recordings and lyrics, on the last day of class.

PRESENCE AND PARTICIPATION- The success of this class depends on the presence and participation of each student when listening to and critiquing everyone’s work. With this class, we will strive to make a community where quality creative feedback can be freely given and taken. Any student in danger of not receiving these points (aka not participating or responding) will be notified discreetly weeks in advance of assessment, and given every chance to reclaim them.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

The first unexcused absence I’ll let go. Any work due on that date is still expected on time, any missed quizzes score a zero. The second time will make me look askance at you, and wonder why you need to miss this class you signed up for. Third time gets a probationary warning, and the fourth will result in a **half-grade drop off your final grade**, with any further absences another **half-grade drop for each**. Excuses from Student Success are required for illness and extended absences. Communication with the lecturer is key; unexcused absences can be made excused (emergencies, personal difficulties, etc.) with adequate and timely explanation.

### Other Course Policies:

Assignments are due by midnight of due date. Songs and assignments that are late receive a 10% grade deduction per day.
