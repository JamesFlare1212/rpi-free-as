## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The purpose of this course is to provide students with a fundamental understanding of audio recording principles and technologies, reinforced with hands-on experience in applying that knowledge to the process of recording in a studio. From the analog world of microphones and speakers, to the digital world of DAWs and VSTs, we will explore the process of audio capture, storage, mixdown, and playback in an introductory format. What makes a sound a “good” sound? How do we make sure we are capturing a “good” sound? And can we make a “bad” sound into a “good” one? We also explore the concept of production, as in: what make someone a “producer” or a recording “produced?” Exploring these questions leads to higher level thinking about what audio is capable of when handled skillfully, and how its principles can be applied to many other disciplines.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Upon completion of this course, students will be able to:

- Demonstrate an understanding of studio ethos and etiquette, and how to set up and tear down an audio recording session safely and efficiently.
- Utilize a fundamental knowledge of analog and digital audio signals, and learn how signal flow operates in a recording studio.
- Proficiently record, edit, and mix audio using a Digital Audio Workstation (DAW), particularly ProTools Ultimate, at beginner to intermediate level.
- Maximize use of the Avid S6 control surface while using ProTools, with appropriate monitoring.
- Identify the properties of different microphones and their ideal placement for desired results.
- Integrate essential concepts of audio engineering, production, mixing, and storage, and using NARAS guidelines for sessions and mixes.
- Achieve Level 3 certification, which allows for more access to the HASS Media Lab, and entry to Sound Recording and Production 2 in the spring.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Introduction to Avid ProTools and using a Digital Audio Workstation (DAW), mono and stereo microphone techniques, microphone properties, basic analog and digital audio concepts, recording most instruments, production vs. engineering, mixing techniques, studio proficiency, mastering and final audio products.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate and understanding of recording studio ethos and etiquette, and be able to set up and tear down an audio recording session safely and efficiently.
- Proficiently record, edit, and mix audio using a digital audio workstation (DAW), prioritizing industry-standard Avid ProTools, at a beginner to intermediate level.
- Identify properties of important microphones, and their ideal placement, positioning, and amplification for desired results.
- Utilize a fundamental knowledge of analog and digital audio signals and their attributes in the creation of audio files, for a variety of formats.
- Integrate essential concepts of audio engineering, production, mixing, mastering, and storage, fulfilling general NARAS guidelines for DAW sessions and mixes.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

LEVEL 1 PROFICIENCY QUIZ: At the outset of the semester, we will make sure you have a clear understanding of general studio etiquette and rules for using the HASS Media Lab for your lab times. This quiz covers the Level 1 guidelines.  
THREE (3) HOMEWORKS: Homeworks are assigned to coincide with reading assignments and lecture content, with up to fifteen questions covering salient points from both.  
THREE (3) LABS: LAB 1 requires students to assemble, edit, and mix six tracks in ProTools. LAB 2 requires students to record an event using a single microphone. LAB 3 requires the student to use a Zoom recorder to do location recording.  
PROFICIENCY TEST-Level 3: Student will demonstrate ability to create a ProTools session, set up and record two microphones, bounce and store a mix, and tear down session efficiently.  
MIDTERM EXAM: Covers salient points of readings and lectures in first 12 weeks of class, plus important studio techniques learned.  
FINAL LAB: Student must create and mix eight tracks in ProTools, with four having been recorded by student using microphones and direct boxes. Selection must be at least two minutes long and reasonably well-mixed at a professional volume level. Sessions and mixes must follow NARAS guidelines for deliverables.
