## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is the second part of a three-semester sequence aimed at cultivating theory, analysis, and musicianship skills that will enhance students’ ability to understand and create music in a diverse range of styles and practices. By considering forms and techniques from the Western “common practice” tradition, African-American musical practices, and non-Western musics, this class will provide students with a baseline of international contemporary musical literacy and basic musicianship skills. Music theory topics include tonal harmony, multi-phrase melody, song forms, species counterpoint, cadences, part-writing, and multiphrase harmonic progressions. Musicianship topics include sight-singing, aural identification, and rhythm exercises. Students will also engage in expanded listening exercises, helping them to develop and articulate their own approaches and responses to a variety of musical and sonic experiences drawn from contemporary music and sound art practices.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to analyze and compose multiphrase melodies and harmonic progressions.
- Students will be able to understand a variety of harmonic notational practices, including roman numerals, figured bass, and jazz chord symbols.
- Students will be able to write two-part species counterpoint.
- Students will be able to do basic formal analysis from a score and by ear.
- Students will gain a fluency in foundational musicianship skills, including solo and ensemble singing, rhythmic performance, melodic dictation, and harmonic dictation.
- Students will develop a fluency in critical listening skills and a vocabulary for articulating what they hear.
- Students will acquire the skills necessary to advance to Music and Sound II.
- Students will develop their own individual creative practice.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Class Participation:** This is a hands-on, experiential course; students are expected to participate in all class activities, including presentations, discussions, musicianship activities, written and creative practice, and other forms of in-class interaction. Students will be graded on effort, integrity of interaction, and improvement over the course of the semester.

**Sound Journals:** Regular sound journals are assigned in an attempt to cultivate an ongoing individual practice of engaging with and reflecting on sonic experience. Each sound journal assignment will include a prompt and/or listening materials along with any needed instructions.

**Written Exams:** A written exam will be given at the end of each unit. These exams will demonstrate students’ grasp of analytical concepts and the application of theoretical principles. They are graded on grasp of the relevant theoretical concepts and the neatness of notation.

**Analysis Projects**: Students will complete an analysis project for each unit in which they will apply their knowledge of the unit’s material to a specific musical work. These projects are graded on grasp of the theoretical concepts and the accuracy of their application.

**Creative Projects:** Creative work provides students the opportunity to apply theoretical concepts and skills to their own creative practice. Depending on the unit, students are invited to strike a balance between the limitations of the assigned topic and their own creative intuitions.

**Musicianship Activities**: Each class meeting, we will do several performance and ear-training activities in which everyone is expected to participate. In addition to these daily activities, which are graded on effort, there will also be some prepared performance assignments.

**Final Project:** The semester’s work will culminate in a final written/analysis project which ties together the material covered throughout the semester. The project will be graded on grasp of the material, clarity of presentation, and critical understanding.

**Attendance:** Music is about collaboration, creativity, and communication.
