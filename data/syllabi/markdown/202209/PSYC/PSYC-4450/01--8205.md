## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course examines the psychology and neuroscience of human learning. A wide range of perspectives on this topic will be considered, including human development, clinical disorders, computational theories, and the role of memory, emotion, and social influences on learning

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Student Learning Outcomes**

Students who complete this course will be able to:

1\. Describe historical and modern theoretical frameworks for understanding human learning.

2\. Understand techniques and methods used to study learning experimentally.

3\. Apply research on learning theory to real-world scenarios.

4\. Utilize critical thinking in order to understand the strengths and weaknesses of current research in learning.

5. Students will demonstrate the ability to provide critical feedback for peers who are involved in team presentations

**Course Assessment Measures/ Learning outcomes**

6-10 Quizzes Recurring; See schedule / 1, 2

Class participation/discussion Cumulative / 3, 4

Midterm writing assignment due October 23, by midnight / 1–4

Team presentations /1–5

Final Project/ 1,3,4

Notes on assessment measures:

- Quizzes

— See section “Course Schedule”, below, for dates of quizzes

— All quizzes are open-book, multiple choice (generally ~15 questions)

— Quizzes will be taken in-class at the start of the scheduled class time and /or online

— Quizzes will cover readings, discussion, and lecture material from the

previous week

- Class participation/discussion

— Time will be dedicated during certain classes for student-led discussion of

assigned readings

— In a maximum of 50 classroom minutes, groups of two or three students (depending on the number of students in the class) will develop PowerPoint-driven workshops on a textbook reading assigned for the date. The workshop will be based upon, *but should not repeat*, the textbook

— See handout, “Theme Specific Workshop Assignment” at the end of syllabus for

expectations

— Class participation/discussion grade will be based on your role as a

discussion facilitator, as well as your participation in discussions

throughout the course led by other students. Documentation at the end of the syllabus provides full details.

*Note: A part of the course grade is peer review of team presentations (See evaluation form at the end of the syllabus)

- Midterm writing assignment

— The topic of the writing assignment will be of the student’s choosing,

building on the content covered in chapters 1–6 of the course textbook.

— The due date for the writing assignment is October 23, by midnight

— See end of syllabus for details

- Final Case Study Writing Assignment

— You will research an Individual that has been studied in the history of Learning and Memory research that has made an impact on our existing knowledge in the field.

This individual will not be one that has been widely studied in the field, like Clive Wearing, but rather an individual that little is known about.

The idea here is to find a unique case. You will select and read 3 empirical journal articles as well as other supporting sources (Textbook, internet, etc.) for this project.
