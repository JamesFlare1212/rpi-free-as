## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

What is the 'nature' of religion and religious experience? What kind of being does the word 'God' refer to? Can the existence of 'God' be rationally demonstrated by way of reason and arguments? What is the problem of evil? What is the relationship between God's existence and morality? Are contemporary scientific beliefs coherently compatible with religious beliefs? How does religion shape our conceptions of death and the meaning of life? These are just some of the questions and problems we shall query as we explore and discuss key readings, both classical and contemporary, within the field of Philosophy of Religion.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The goals of this course are to familiarize students with the different methodologies for studying religions so as to reveal the variegated beliefs and practices that define the identities of world religions. Students will learn how to: (1) analyze and synthesize both primary and secondary sources; (2) conduct an inquiry into the epistemological, metaphysical and ethical issues that define Philosophy of Religion; (3) examine and evaluate theories and arguments, specifically in regard to methodologies, that shape our understanding of religious traditions; and (4) engage in the general practice of hermeneutics so as to deepen their appreciation of the historical/cultural contexts by which religious beliefs and practices present themselves to both practitioners and scholars of religion.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- At the end of this course, students will be able to think critically about a range of beliefs, theories and arguments that are salient to Philosophy of Religion.
- At the end of this class students will be able to evaluate world religions through different methodologies, including: (1) cultural anthropology and sociology; (2) psychology; (3) phenomenology and hermeneutics; (4) and political science.
- At the end of this class, students will be able to demonstrate their ability to evaluate, discuss and debate arguments concerning: (1) the existence of God; (2) the problem of evil; (3) the nature of faith and reason; (4) the relationship between ethics and religion; and (5) the interface between religion and science.
- At the end of this course students will be able to identify and explain key theories, arguments and thinkers that have helped define the field of Philosophy of Religion.
- At the end of this course, students will be able to effectively communicate their perspectives through essays, presentations and seminar discussions.
- At the end of this course, students will be able to conduct and present their research according to The Chicago Manual Style of Format.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

All assignments in this course - discussions, presentations and essays - will be evaluated in light of the following criteria: (1) ability to write clearly by following grammatical rules and conforming to the guidelines of The Chicago Manual of Style; (2) ability to demonstrate critical thinking by assessing theories, methods and arguments that shape our understanding of world religions; (3) ability to effectively reference and examine both primary and secondary sources included in the course reading list.  
**Assignments and Evaluation** *Two Essays (1000 words):* For each short essay assignment, students will be given constructive feedback and comments. After submitting their essays for a preliminary review, each student will be given two weeks to edit and make content changes prior to submitting their work for a final grade. Essay Prompts will be centered around specific theorists/arguments/methodologies/issues that help reveal the philosophical dimensions of religions and religious belief. Essays will be evaluated according to one's ability to: (1) write clearly; (2) apply the methods of studying religions to specific cases and issues; (3) employ the basic skills of critical thinking; and (4) evaluate and create arguments in defense of a hermeneutical perspective.  
*Research Paper (1500-2000 words)*: Students will have the opportunity to conduct their own research upon a specific topic concerning philosophy of religion, and the arguments that undergird our perspectives of such. Students will compose a short 100-word abstract detailing their research thesis; abstracts should be submitted no later than 10/14/2022. All papers will be submitted for a grade no later than 12/19/2022. Research papers will be evaluated according to one's ability to: (1) write clearly; (2) apply the tools of critical thinking to specific theories, arguments and issues; (3) identify and explain their research methodology; and (4) evaluate and create arguments in light of their references (no less than seven academic sources).  
*Research Panel Presentation*: Each student will participate in a panel discussion whereby they will have the opportunity to present their thesis, arguments/methodology and conclusions in regard to their research papers. They will also have the opportunity to engage in a dialogue with other panelists as well as field questions from the audience. Students will be evaluated individually based upon their ability to: (1) effectively and clearly present their research and arguments; (2) engage in a panel discussion with other panelists; and (3) respond to questions from the audience.  
*Weekly Seminar Discussions*: This class is a communication intensive course, which means there will be regular seminar discussions that address specific theories, thinkers and arguments that have defined the field of Philosophy of Religions. Discussion topics will be provided before class meetings so that students can prepare to share their perspective and thereby raise any critical questions they may have. Participation will be evaluated according to one's ability to dialogue with students and articulate arguments that are relevant to the course readings.  
**Evaluation Breakdown:**  
Two Essays: 20% each  
Research Paper: 35%  
Panel Presentation: 10%  
Weekly Seminar Discussions: 15%

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity & Plagiarism Policy**:  
Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute's commitment to 'thoroughness' and 'knowledge' includes the values associated with intellectual honesty and humility. Using the words or ideas of someone else without proper acknowledgement is immoral and will result in a failure for the assignment. If there is a subsequent infraction, then they will receive an F for the course. Students should become familiar with *The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* so that they are aware of the various forms of academic dishonesty.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

**Successful Performance:** Reading religious/philosophical texts can be challenging. Students should get in the habit of setting aside enough time each week to read carefully, and often reread course materials. If a student is having difficulty with course readings, be sure to meet with me during office hours so that we can address any and all concerns.  
Writing essays on religious/philosophical matters is a skill that students are expected to cultivate in this class. I recommend all students to consult Zachary Seech's book, *Writing Philosophy Papers*. Cengage, 2008.
