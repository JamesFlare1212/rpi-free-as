## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Environmental Ethics is an area of applied ethics that explores moral perspectives, duties and responsibilities towards non-human animals, the natural environment and human communities that are impacted by environmental policies and ecological concerns. In this class students will explore a range of ethical issues pertaining to the ethical theories that shape our attitudes about nature, as well as the application of such theories so as to address a range of environmental topics, including: (1) animal welfare and rights; (2) wilderness preservation and conservation; (3) human over-population; (4) green capitalism and land-use policies; (5) sustainability and technology; (6) agriculture and food ethics; (7) pollution; and (8) climate change.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Goals:**  
As we examine key readings in Environmental Ethics, students will have the opportunity to engage the practice of philosophical thinking in order to cultivate a moral imagination for a just and sustainable world. Herein our goals are to: (1) become familiar with dominant ethical theories in Western moral philosophy;(2) clarify the normative differences between various environmental perspectives, including anthropocentrism, biocentrism and ecocentrism; and (3) apply the tools of critical thinking and moral reasoning to specific environmental topics, including, yet not limited to, sustainable land-use policies, pollution and climate change.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Curriculum Content**:  
In this class students will work through the following theories/topics/issues:  
(1) Ethical Theories (virtue theory, deontology, consequentialism, social contract theory and feminisim).  
(2) Moral obligations to future generations.  
(3) Moral obligations to non-human animals.  
(4) Moral obligations to natural environments and ecosystems.  
(5) Environmental perspectives (anthropocentrism, biocentrism, ecocentrism, deep ecology, ecofeminism).  
(6) Environmental issues and topics (human overpopulation, agriculture and food ethics, wilderness preservation, sustainability and technology, pollution and climate change.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- At the end of this course, students will be able to effectively participate in the general practice of philosophical thinking by demonstrating their ability to evaluate arguments in light of complex moral situations and issues pertaining to environmental ethics.
- At the end of this course students will be able to identify and explain the dominant traditions of ethical theory and environmental perspectives.
- At the end of this course students will be able to work cooperatively and empathetically with others in order to entertain multiple perspectives and cultural views.
- At the end of this course, students will be able to demonstrate their ability to evaluate, discuss and debate arguments concerning environmental issues.
- At the end of this course, students will be able to identify and explain key concepts, theories and thinkers that have shaped our understanding of environmental ethics.
- At the end of this course, students will be able to effectively communicate their perspectives through essays, seminar discussions.
- At the end of this course, students will be able to conduct and present their research in The Chicago Manual of Style format.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Learning Outcomes:**  
All assignments in this course - discussions, presentations and essays - will be evaluated in light of the following criterion: (1) ability to write/speak clearly; (2) ability to demonstrate critical thinking by formulating and assessing arguments; and (3) ability to effectively engage and reference key articles and philosophical perspectives included in the syllabus by following grammatical rules and conforming to the style guidelines of *The Chicago manual of Style.* Late assignments will not be accepted for a grade unless a student requests an extension in advance of the submission deadline.  
*Short Essays*: For each short essay assignment (1000 words) students will be given constructive feedback and comments. After submitting their essays for a preliminary review, each student will be given two weeks to edit and make changes to their essays prior to submitting them for a final grade. Essay prompts will be centered around specific theorists/arguments/cases in environmental ethics. Essays will be evaluated according to one's ability to: (1) write clearly; (2) apply ethical theories to specific cases; (3) employ the basic skills of critical thinking; and (4) evaluate and create arguments in defense of a normative position.  
*Research Pape*r (2000 words):  
Students will have the opportunity to conduct their own research upon a normative topic in environmental ethics. Students will compose a short 100-word abstract detailing their research thesis which will be submitted no later than 10/14/2022. All papers will be submitted for a grade no later than 12/19/2022. Research papers will be evaluated according to one's ability to: (1) write clearly; (2) apply ethical theories to specific issues and cases; (3) employ the basic skills of critical thinking; (4) identify and explain their research methodologies; (5) evaluate and create arguments in defense of a normative position; and (6) effectively incorporate no less than seven academic sources.  
*Research Panel Presentation*:  
Each student will participate in a panel discussion whereby they will have the opportunity to present their thesis, arguments and conclusions in regard to their research papers. They will also have the opportunity to engage in dialogue with other panelists as well as field questions from the audience. Grading will be based upon one's ability to: (1) effectively and clearly present their research and their arguments; (2) engage in a panel discussion with other panelists; and (3) respond to questions from the audience.  
Weekly Seminar Discussions:  
This class is a communication intensive course, which means there will be regular seminar discussions that address specific theories, thinkers and issues that have defined the field of environmental ethics. Discussion topics will be provided prior to class meetings in order to afford students time to reflect upon their perspective and normative position. Participation will be evaluated in light of one's ability to dialogue with other students and thereby articulate arguments that are relevant to the course readings.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity and Plagiarism Policy**:  
All students are encouraged to review The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities in order to become familiar with various forms of academic dishonesty. Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute's commitment to 'thoroughness' and 'knowledge' includes the values associated with intellectual honesty and humility.
