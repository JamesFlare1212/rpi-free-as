## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course offers the introduction to network science and review of current research in this field. Classes will interchangeably present chapters from the textbook and the related current research. The emphasis will be on mathematical background of network science: graphs and networks; random networks and various types of scale-free networks; network properties such as assortatitivity, mobility, and robustness; social networks and communities; and dynamics of processes on networks.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

**Author:** Albert Laszlo Barabasi  
**Title:** *Network Science Publisher:* Cambridge University Press, 2016  
On-line version is available at http://barabasi.com/networksciencebook/  
In addition, class notes will be used.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Present and discuss fundamental network science ideas to create models and understand dynamics of networked systems;  
Show how compare, contrast, and describe the similarities and differences of various kinds of networks and processes modeled on networks;  
Give examples of critique and evaluation of the strengths and weaknesses of each of the models and types of networks based on them and these network types performance in diverse network science applications;  
Foster students understanding the principles of applying network science to disciplinary science and design and set up basic models for some specific applications.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

1\. Mathematical background of network science: graphs and networks.  
2\. Random networks and their properties.  
3\. Scale-free networks, small world networks and Barabasi-Alert model.  
4\. Mobility and networks  
5\. Network robustness  
6\. Social networks and communities  
7\. Assortativity of networks  
8\. Dynamic processes on networks

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Being able to apply fundamental network science ideas to create models and understand dynamics of networked systems;
- Being able to compare, contrast, and describe the similarities and differences of different kinds of networks and processes modeled on networks;
- Being able to critique the strengths and weaknesses of each of the models and types of networks based on them and these network types performance in diverse network science applications;
- Understanding the principles of applying network science to disciplinary science and design and set up basic models for some specific applications.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The students' performance will be measured using three different methods:  
(i) programming homework,  
(ii) selection of a current research paper for presentation and its evaluation plan,  
(iii) presentation on the selected paper, including slides content, and evaluation of the scientific results of the paper,  
(iv) contributions to in-class discussions.  
The programming homework and presentation plans will measure the student’s ability to apply concepts of network science to networks analysis.  
The presentation slides and evaluation of the paper results will measure student’s ability to prepare summary material based on fundamental scientific concepts and basic research.  
The presentation will measure student’s ability to communicate the concept and notions related to the research projects in which the student is involved.  
The discussion in class will measure the student’s skills to critically evaluate and objectively assess the presentations of other students.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**One individual programming homework** (40% of the total grade), followed by **one individual presentation of the selected research paper** (50% of the total grade), with **questions and participation in discussions** for at least two student presentations will providing the remaining (10%) of the the total grade.  
**The programming homework** will be handed out approximately after the end of the 4th week, and due in three weeks after that. The homework will require using network analysis tools (or programming) and analysis of the results obtained for the real and synthetic networks. The graded homework will be returned to undergraduate approximately one week after they are handed in. Students will have these grades as their means to determine progress in the course by mid-semester.  
**The research paper to be presented** will be assigned in the 7th week of the course, and the 15 min discussion of the plans for presentation will be due for one of the classes in the 8th week.  
The final **30 min in class presentation the assigned paper** will be scheduled in the last two weeks of the class.  
Grade ranges: A 96, A- 91, B 85, B- 80, C 70, C- 60, F <60.
