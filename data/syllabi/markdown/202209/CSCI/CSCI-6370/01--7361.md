## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

- Data and Society provides a broad overview of how society is leveraging and responding to the social, organizational, policy, and technical opportunities and challenges of a data-driven world. Course themes focus on various aspects of the data ecosystem, data and innovation, and data and the broader community.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Develop greater data literacy o Be able to understand and explain the role that data plays as well as its limitations in various areas of research, commerce and modern life.
- • Develop greater data literacy o Be able to read, understand, assess, and discuss data-oriented professional and popular publications and articles.
- • Develop communication skills around data: o Be able to advance an evidence-based argument about data, data cyberinfrastructure and data-oriented efforts to both knowledgeable specialists within the field as well as non-specialists.
- • Develop policy knowledge about data usage: o Understand the potential impacts of regulation with respect to data regulation and legislation.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

This course is a communications intensive course that was originally designed by Dr. Francine Berman, who is now the Director of Public Interest Technology and Stuart Rice Research Professor at University of Massachusetts Amherst, and thus no longer at RPI. For me to change the course material and/or assignment types/grading would require that the course be reapproved for CI status, and there was not time to do that for this term. However, the material covered will be related, but not identical – so the ‘form’ of the course is unchanged, the ‘material’ of the course will overlap, but include aspects of data and society that were not given as much coverage in the previous course. There will thus be more overlap with the Data Science course, but the instructors have worked to minimize this.

However, students who have not taken data science, or are not taking it as a corequisite, may find themselves at a disadvantage and need to do some extra research on their own to pick up this material. (The professor will help identify sources if requested/needed).
