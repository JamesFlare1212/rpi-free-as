## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course offers a rigorous yet accessible introduction to differential equations, with an emphasis on both analytical techniques and real-world applications. Students will develop a foundational understanding of how to formulate, solve, and interpret differential equations in various contexts.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Mark H. Holmes, Introduction to Differential equations, 3ed, XanEdu Publishers.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Recognize and classify different types of differential equations
- Solve first-order ODEs using methods such as separation of variables, integration factors, homogeneous equations, exact methods, and Bernoulli equations.
- Solve second-order ODEs using characteristic equations, the method of undetermined coefficients and variation of parameters.
- Apply the Laplace transform and systems of equations to solve initial value problems.
- Understand the qualitative behavior of solutions.
- Model real-world problems in physics, biology, and economics using differential equations.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Successful completion of this course should enable you to demonstrate an understanding of fundamental techniques for the solution of first-order and second-order ordinary differential equations.
- Successful completion of this course should enable you to demonstrate an understanding of Fourier series and separation of variables for certain partial differential equations.
- Successful completion of this course should enable you to demonstrate an understanding of solution techniques and the behavior of solutions of systems of first-order differential equations.
- Successful completion of this course should enable you to present written solutions to problems in a clear, concise and coherent fashion.

# Academic Integrity

For the homework, you are encouraged to collaborate by exchanging ideas and brainstorming solutions. You can work in groups in which you discuss the problems and help each other out if you are stuck. You are also allowed to use books and informative websites such as Wikipedia. However, in all cases, what you turn in must be your own work, and copying of any kind is forbidden. Examples include copying from fellow students (this includes partitioning the homework among several students without everybody's participation on every problem), contacting helpers outside of exam rooms using electronic or other means, wholesale copying of solutions from the internet (both recycled problems from past versions of this class or solutions of the assigned problems from any other source), using solvers such as Maple or Mathematica (unless allowed for a specific problem), using cheat sites such as Chegg, or Course Hero, or using AI apps such as ChatGPT.

Pop Quizzes will be “open notes,”; and scheduled quizzes will be closed book. For exams you will be allowed a one-page, double- sided, crib sheet of notes in your own handwriting or your own typing. During taking the quizzes and exams, you are not to discuss the questions with anyone but the instructor and/or the TA. Copying is strictly forbidden, as is using any type of books, notes (except on pop quizzes), calculators, computers, tablets, cell phones, or other electronic aids. Violating this policy will result in a score of zero for the quiz or exam.

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. Acts, which violate this trust, undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. Violations of the above policies constitute violations of student-teacher trust, as well as represent taking an unfair advantage of your fellow students. Therefore, such violations will result in a score of zero for the homework, quiz, or or exam on which they were committed. Penalties for repeat oﬀenses can be quite harsh: you should check the Academic Integrity Policy page provided by the Dean of Students Oﬃce for more information about this: https://info.rpi.edu/dean- students/05/29/2020/notice-student-rights-and-responsibilities.

If you feel that something was overlooked or that a mistake was made in the grading of your work, you need to discuss this with the Teaching Assistant. If such a frivolous request is made, we reserve the right to review and regrade the entire quiz/exam, and this may well result in fewer total points in the end.

If you have an excuse, you should contact the instructor and TA via email as soon as possible. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

In the case of homework assignments, collaboration between students is encouraged. However, each student must write his/her own solutions and submit his/her own problem sets. Copying someone else's solution or using a problem-solving site (such as Chegg) is not allowed.

In the case of exams, there is no collaboration of any kind permitted, and each student will be required to write out and sign an Honor Pledge on each exam confirming this. If cheating is suspected, then an explanation will be requested. If the explanation is not satisfactory, then an exam grade of zero will be given and a report will be made to the Dean of Students.
