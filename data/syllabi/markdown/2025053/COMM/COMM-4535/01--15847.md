## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This ***communication intensive (CI)*** course offers an in-depth, comparative exploration of social media [1] platforms and practices across Eastern and Western cultures. Students critically examine how digital platforms are used for socializing, entertaining, self-presentation, activism, and marketing, while investigating the cultural, politic al, and design logics behind them. Through platform analysis, cross-cultural case studies, and real-time medial tracking, the course highlights how social media reflects and reshapes identity, and power.

Emphasis is placed on the diversity of mediated experience—both global and local—as students engage in hands-on assignments, peer dialogue, and observational research. The course invites students to reflect on their own digital practices while examining trends from East Asia, North America, and beyond. No Chinese language proficiency is required and all instruction is in English.

[1] Here we use a broad definition of social media to refer to “computer-based technology that facilitates the sharing of ideas, thoughts, and information through virtual networks and communities” (Dollarhide, 2020, para. 1)

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course invites students to critically examine how social media platforms are used across Eastern and Western cultures for communication, entertainment, self-representation, activism, and marketing. Students will explore how digital media shape—and are shaped by—cultural values, political systems, and social behaviors. Emphasis will be placed on cross-cultural comparisons, observational analysis, and real-world platform engagement.

By the end of the course, students will:

- Analyze the role of social media in shaping individual identities and public discourse across cultures.
- Compare Eastern and Western platform design, regulation, and user behavior.
- Develop cross-cultural media literacy through case studies and peer discussions.
- Reflect on their own digital practices in light of global media systems.

The course is conducted in English and requires no Chinese language proficiency.

- Students with no Chinese language background will gain foundational insights into Chinese digital platforms and popular culture.

- Students with prior Chinese knowledge will deepen their cross-cultural analytical skills and learn to articulate insights across linguistic boundaries.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- By the end of this course, students will be able to articulate, access, and critically analyze social media use in both Eastern (e.g., China) and Western contexts. Specifically, they will be able to: 1. Recognize Cultural Diversity in Media: Identify and appreciate how mediated messages reflect and are shaped by diverse cultural values and practices. 2. Enhance Cross-Cultural Communication: Strengthen intercultural understanding and communication skills through engagement with global media perspectives and peer interactions. 3. Critically Analyze Social Media Platforms: Compare the functions and social roles of Eastern and Western social media platforms in domains such as social interaction, entertainment, publicity, and marketing. 4. Evaluate Media’s Cultural Impact: Assess how media practices differ across cultures, with a focus on the comparative dynamics between American and Chinese digital cultures. 5. Apply Critical Thinking to Media Discourse: Participate in informed, reflective discussions on social media issues within and across cultural boundaries. 6. Foster Cultural Sensitivity through Media Literacy: Develop a nuanced understanding of how media represents and influences cultural identities, promoting inclusive and culturally aware perspectives.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic dishonesty encompasses various actions, including cheating, fabricating, or falsifying information or sources, improper collaboration, submitting the same paper for multiple classes without permission, and plagiarism. Plagiarism occurs when writers intentionally or unintentionally use someone else’s language, ideas, or materials without giving proper credit and citation. Familiarizing yourself with RPI’s Student Rights and Responsibilities regarding academic integrity is essential.  
Plagiarism is defined as the act of taking credit for someone else’s work, whether done in deliberately or unintentionally. Examples of plagiarism include submitting an essay, in whole or in part, written by someone else (such as a friend or an internet source) and claiming it as your own, as well as incorporating information or ideas from research materials without properly citing the sources.  
In this class, plagiarism is unacceptable. If any instances of accidental or deliberate use of someone else’s words, ideas, or work without attribution are identified, you will first receive a warning. Failure to rectify the situation may result in penalties, such as receiving a failing grade on the paper, a failing grade in the course, and/or being referred to the Dean of Students.
