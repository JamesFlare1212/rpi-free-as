## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides an overview of the art of developing, maintaining, and expanding imagined worlds, with special emphasis on worldbuilding for games. Students will be equipped with tools for collaboratively imagining worlds, presenting those worlds through interactive media, and using worldbuilding techniques to promote positive social change.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Our course runs six weeks, from July 7 to August 15. There are no internal divisions to the time, such as rounds. Each week we will do a recurring cycle of activities together:

| ## Discuss (Mondays) · Each week we will discuss a selection from the textbook and a short story · You will complete the readings before class and take notes in your Cosmographer's Log | ## Play (Tuesdays) · Each week you will play one or more worldbuilding games with your team · You will take notes on these games in your Cosmographer's Log |
| --- | --- |
| ## Watch (Wednesdays) · Each week we will watch a film that demonstrates interesting worldbuilding · You will take notes on the films in your Cosmographer's Log · **Screenings will be held on Wednesdays, but you can also watch the film individually** · | ## Design (Thursdays) · Your team will imagine a world and design a multimodal world guide that presents it · You will document your design process in your Cosmographer's Log · We will usually have guided studio time on Thursdays · |
| ## Critique (Fridays) · You will take notes on critique sessions in your Cosmographer's Log · Starting in Week 3, you will also critique a peer's Cosmographer's Log | ## Write (Always) · You will write an analytical essay that offers an interpretation of a piece of worldbuilding · Some stages of your writing will be done in your Cosmographer's Log · Most writing will occur outside of class |

# Multimodal World Guide

## Description

Over the course of the semester you will collaborate with an assigned team to imagine a world and describe it in the form of a multimodal world guide. Your team will collaborate on the main text of the guide, which should be written such that other people could take up the guide and create new works set in your world.

Each team member will also contribute at least three multimodal elements to the guide. These elements can be in any medium (illustration, photography, 3d modeling, text, sound, game, etc.). They should help a reader of the guide better imagine the world, and will generally represent a diegetic (i.e., in-world) reality. You might design a series of flags, sketch a creature, write a diegetic document like a proclamation or a poem, craft a soundscape, record a diegetic song, or anything else you can think up. Importantly, each team member can only use the same medium twice.

# Worldbuilding Analysis Essay

## Description

Your final paper for this course will be an essay that analyzes an imagined world of your choice through the lens of one of the chapters from our textbook that analyzes another imagined world. In order for us to properly assess your analysis, the the instructor and/or TA will need to be familiar with the world you choose. A list of suggested worlds is attached.

Additionally, you will draw on at least two other academic sources in your analysis. The final paper will be 1,500 - 3,000 words, and you will submit two deliverables along the way.

# Cosmographer’s Log

## Description

Throughout the semester you will record notes and exercises in a composition book. We call this book your *Cosmographer's Log*. It is something between a writer's notebook, a commonplace book, and a lab notebook. Although you will receive feedback on your notebook, we will not assess the notebook for quality, only for completion and format.

Assignments are due by the end of the noted class period, unless otherwise stated. For each day an assignment is late, 25% of the specifications grade will be deducted from the grade for the assignment.

## AI

Any use of AI to assist with work related to the course, whether as a brainstorming aid, editing, content generation, or any other usage must be discussed with the instructor *prior* to utilization. While the expectation of the course is to conduct all work without the use of AI, some limited exceptions may be granted. Use of AI without prior approval will be considered a violation of academic integrity.

Any changes to the syllabus will be collaboratively negotiated between the instructor and students and will be communicated in writing with sufficient time for students to adapt.
