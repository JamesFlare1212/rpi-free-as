## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will focus on understanding the chemical aspects of food composition with emphasis on the functional properties and chemical reactions of the major components of foods: Water, carbohydrates, proteins and fats/oils (lipids). Several food-related concepts such as Gelation, Browning and the Maillard reactions, Meat glue, Food texture and Mouth feel, Emulsions, Sous-vide cooking, Thickening, etc. will be introduced through videos of a lecture series by renowned chefs from The United States and abroad.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Part 1: Carbohydrates in Foods**

- Chemical structures
- Classification: digestible vs. indigestible; monosaccharides, oligosaccharides, polysaccharides
- Functional properties: sweetness, preservation, appearance, texture, fermentation, freezing point change, antioxidant activity
- Browning and Caramelization processes
- The Maillard reactions
- Starch: structure, gelatinization, retrogradation, staling, oxidized starch, and benefits
- Gums and their functional roles in food processing
- Fibers and their roles in human diet

**Part 2: Proteins in Foods**

- Chemical structures
- Essential amino acids
- Sources of food proteins
- Biological value of proteins
- Proteins as food additives: e.g. transglutaminase (meat glue), gelatin.
- Denaturation and coagulation processes and factors that cause them: e.g. frying egg, sour milk.

**Part 3: Water in Foods**

- Forms of water in foods: free, absorbed or bound.
- Moisture content vs. water activity: methods used to measure them.
- Effect of water activity on food spoilage, shelf life and product formulation.

**Part 4: Fats and Oils (Lipids) in Foods**

- Classification, chemical structures, and physical properties
- Essential fatty acids
- Main sources of fats and oils
- Benefits of unsaturated fats
- Emulsions and Foams in Food processing: Factors that affect their stability.
- Trans fat: Origin and health problems
- Cholesterol: HDL vs. LDL, benefits, and drawbacks

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Students will have a better understanding of the chemical aspects of food composition.
- 2\. Students will learn about the functional properties and the chemical reactions of the major constituents of food.
- 3\. Students will gain knowledge of several food-related concepts such as Gelation, Browning and the Maillard reactions, Meat glue, Food texture and Mouth feel, Emulsions, Sous-vide cooking, Thickening, etc.
- 4\. Students will be able to expand their knowledge through a video series on Science & Cooking with practical examples.
- 5\. Students will gain a better understanding of factors that can affect food spoilage during storage and processing.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The following course assessments will be used to measure the student's learning outcomes.  
**1. Attendance**

Attendance at lectures and discussion sessions is mandatory. Since students will be taking the quiz during this period, any unexcused absence will result in a zero grade for the quiz given on that specific day.

**2.** **Quizzes**

A brief quiz will be given at the end of each lecture. Each 15–20-minute quiz will be made up of four or more questions to test the student’s understanding of the materials covered in the previous lecture including relevant information from the video discussion. To that end, the quizzes are designed to help students test their understanding of the materials. Quiz make-up will be arranged if the student has an excused absence.  
**3.** **Discussion**

Each pair of students will be assigned a video on Science and Cooking. Students will extract relevant information from the video, correlate it with the topics covered in lectures, and present the result of their findings during a 20-minute discussion session. Integrating the topics discussed in lectures with practical examples from the video is key to developing a base upon which students will be able to expand their knowledge of food chemistry.
