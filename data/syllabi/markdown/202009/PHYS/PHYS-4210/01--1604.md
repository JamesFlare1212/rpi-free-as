## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Description**: PHYS 4210 Electromagnetic Theory is an intermediate level course for physics majors that emphasizes the fundamental relationship between electric and magnetic fields. Students taking this course should have a good working knowledge of vector calculus and linear algebra. This is a 4-credit hour course.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to solve electrostatic charge distribution and boundary value problems using vector calculus tools including the divergence theorem; line, surface and volume integrals; Laplace and Poisson equations; multipole expansions.
- Students will be able to solve magnetostatic current distribution and boundary value problems using vector calculus tools including Green’s and Stoke’s theorems; line, surface and volume integrals; Laplace equation; multipole expansions.
- Students will demonstrate an understanding of electric and magnetic fields in matter.
- Students will demonstrate an understanding of how electromagnetic waves can be deduced from Maxwell’s equations.
- Students will able to solve boundary value and wave propagation problems including dispersion in materials and reflection at interfaces.
- Students will demonstrate an understanding of and be able to derive the relativistic relationship between electric and magnetic fields.
