## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Sakurai & Napolitano, *Modern Quantum Mechanics*, Second Edition (Addison-Wesley, 2011)

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Teach advanced fundamental concepts, associated mathematical tools, and problem solving skills in quantum mechanics.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

(1) Fundamental Concepts: The Stern-Gerlach Experiment, Bra-Ket Formalism, Operators and Representations, Measurements, Observables, and the Uncertainty Relations, Change of Ba(1) sis, Wave Functions in Position and Momentum Space.

(2) Quantum Dynamics: Time Evolution, Schrödinger vs. Heisenberg Picture, Simple Harmonic Oscillator, Schrödinger’s Wave Equation, Propagators and Path Integrals; Potentials and Gauge Transformations.

(3) Theory of Angular Momentum: Rotations and Commutation Relations, Spin ½ Systems and Finite Rotations, SO(3), SU(2), and Euler Rotations, Density Operators, Pure vs. Mixed Ensembles, Eigenvalues and Eigenstates of Angular Momentum, Orbital Angular Momentum, Schrödinger’s Equations for Central Potential, Addition of Angular Momenta, Spin Correlation Measurements and Bell’s Inequality, Tensor Operators.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students are expected to demonstrate the ability to apply the concepts and theories of Quantum Mechanics in problem solving tasks.
- Students are expected to demonstrate the ability to make use of the physical principles along with necessary mathematic skills to describe quantum mechanical phenomena.
- Students are expected to learn the fundamental concepts and advanced problem solving skills in quantum mechanics.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Collaboration and discussion are allowed and encouraged in homeworks. However, you must write-up your own solution, as the results of synthesizing discussions with others, and not merely copying others’ solutions. On exams, you’re on your own and not allowed to discuss anything with your classmates. Don’t copy someone else’s homework or cheat on exams. If I suspect you of either, I’ll ask for an explanation. If your explanation is unsatisfactory, you’ll be given a grade of **0** (zero) and reported to the Dean of Students. If this happens more than once, you will be given an F for the course, and the entire matter will be forwarded to the Dean of Students.
