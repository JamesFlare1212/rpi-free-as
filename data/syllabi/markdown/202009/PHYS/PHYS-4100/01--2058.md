## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will introduce the fundamental concepts of quantum mechanics, including

the state of a quantum mechanical system, operators, superposition principle, and

measurement postulate. The notion of an operator and the algebra of operators is introduced

with the help of elementary concepts of mathematical analysis. Topics covered

in this course include the general formalism of quantum mechanics, Symmetries and

conservation laws, quantum mechanics in three-dimensions, angular momentum, spin,

perturbation theory, and scattering; to name a few topics.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Homework

The homework is an essential part of the course. You should attempt all the problems

yourself, but you may argue with your colleagues about the basic ideas underlying the

problems. Simply copying each other’s solutions or downloading solutions is, however,

counterproductive for all parties, and contrary to the honor code. Your solutions should

go beyond the usual “just writing down” solution, in that special care should be given

to the presentation and discussion of the logic of the solution. In science, the communication

of arguments and results is nearly as important as the results themselves, so this

should be good practice. Homework solutions will be posted on the course LMS web

site.

While some of the problems in each set will be new, many others will be from the textbook.

Its solution manual is floating around, but it is required for you to work out your

own solution both according to the honor code, and because (of course!) that is the only

way you will learn the material for yourself.

There will be a problem set every week. The main purpose of the problem sets is to

help learn quantum mechanics, not to grade performance. They are included in the final

grade as a safety net in the event exams do not consistently reflect a student’s understanding.

Sitting down by yourself and reasoning your way through a problem will help you learn

the material deeply, identify concepts that are not clear, and develop the analytical skills

needed for a successful career in science. If you can solve the problems by yourself, you

can expect to do well on the exams.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Student will learn the fundamentals of solving quantum mechanical problems with the wave function approach.
- Student will be able to solve the 3D time-dependent and time-independent Schrödinger equation for simple potentials like for instance the harmonic oscillator and hydrogenlike atoms, as well as the interaction of an electron with the electromagnetic field
- Student will be familiar with spin, angular momentum states, angular momentum addition rules, and identical particles
- Students will learn approximate methods for solving the Schrödinger equation ( the variational method, perturbation theory, Born approximations)
- Student will be made aware of the modern application of quantum mechanics: entanglement application, laser cooling, atom trapping
- Student will be introduced to the EPR discussion and quantum spookiness

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Cumulative mastery of homework is intended to be the primary learning tool in preparation

for exams. There will be two 2-hours closed-book test during the normal lecture

time. There will be a three-hour final exam scheduled during the final exam period. All

exams will be administered remotely.

Safety protocol related to Covid-19

Students will be required to wear face coverings at all times while in class and while

transiting between classes within buildings and doorways. Students must comply with

all posted and verbal instructions provided to them by instructional faculty, graduate

TAs, and staff members. Such instructions may include specific information about individual

space cleaning, the directionality of entrances and exits, as well as the times when

students and faculty will enter and leave the classroom. These schedules are designed

to minimize cross-traffic and enhance social distancing. Faculty will review PPE policies

and practices at the start of the semester and when needed.

If students fail to observe COVID PPE (face covering) protocols, they must be challenged

and gently urged to comply with safety policies. Faculty will remind such students

that non-compliance with PPE policies is a violation of classroom requirements

and Rensselaer policy. If the student refuses or cannot comply, the instructor will determine

the cause (e.g., no mask?). The instructor will help fix the problem (there will be

extra masks in classrooms). If the situation escalates, and the student refuses to comply

or to leave the classroom when asked, the class will be dismissed. A report detailing

the student’s non-compliance and the resulting dismissal will be sent to the Dean of

Students office, and the Dean of Science.
