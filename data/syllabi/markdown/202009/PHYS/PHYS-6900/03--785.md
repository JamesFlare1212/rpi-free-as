## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course exposes graduate students, through different colloquia, to multidisciplinary research topics in which physics plays an important role.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

There is no textbook in this course.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students will be exposed to different research areas related to physics.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The course is composed of a series of seminars of different types of research.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be exposed to research carried out by different invited speakers of diverse disciplines in order to provide a broad view on how research in physics or related to physics works.
- Students will learn from different speakers how to present a formal research lecture.
- Students will be able to interact with different speakers from different institutions about research related to physics.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students will submit an assignment form after each colloquium through LMS.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

The grades are Satisfactory (S) or Unsatisfactory (U).

It is expected the student to attend all the colloquia and submit the assignment form after every colloquium through LMS to obtain a satisfactory grade S.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Honor code:** The course does not measure your ability to search the web. Any instance of copying problems that you have not solved on your own or in groups will constitute dishonesty. Dishonesty is punishable with an F in the course. Working in groups and consultation with other students is allowed, but each one should hand in their own work.

**Academic Integrity Policy:**

Academic integrity is one of the cornerstones of RPI. Students taking courses at RPI have a right to expect that their work will be evaluated fairly with respect to other students. They have a right to expect that other students will not attempt to enhance their own grades or the grades of their friends by cheating. Teachers have a right to expect that their students are honest and submit work reflecting their own efforts. In an atmosphere of academic integrity, students and teachers are on the same team trying to achieve the same learning objectives.

Collaboration and discussion **are allowed** **and encouraged** in the homework, the project and in-class activities. However, you must write-up your own solution, as the results of synthesizing discussions with others, and not merely copying others’ solutions. On exams, you are on your own and not allowed to discuss anything with your classmates. Thus, don’t copy someone else’s homework, in-class activities, and quizzes and don’t cheat on exams. If I suspect you of either, I’ll ask for an explanation. If your explanation is unsatisfactory, you’ll be given a grade of **0** (zero) and reported to the Dean of Students. If this happens more than once, you will be given an **F** for the course, and the entire matter will be forwarded to the Dean of Students.
