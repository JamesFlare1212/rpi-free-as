## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

**Text:** There are many good textbooks in Thermodynamics, but none that perfectly matches the content/approach of this course. (1) For the review of classical thermodynamics (~50% of the course), the course will closely follow the book by Gaskell. We will also use homework problems from Gaskell. Thus, it is highly recommended that students have access to the book by Gaskell. (2) For statistical thermodynamics, the course will use various sources. The most suitable reference may be McQuarrie or also Hill, while Chandler is a more compact and possibly more readable to some students.

Thus, the books relevant to this course are (roughly sorted with decreasing importance):

- \- Gaskell and Laughlin, Introduction to the Thermodynamics of Materials, 6th, Good coverage of classical TD with Materials Science perspective. ~$90.
- \- McQuarrie: Statistical Mechanics. Detailed extensive description of statistical TD. Maybe a bit more developed/structured in comparison to Hill. ~$80.
- \- Chandler: Introduction to modern statistical mechanics. A rather compact version of statistical TD, including some advanced concepts. ~$80 (also e)
- \- Hill: Introduction to Statistical Thermodynamics. Detailed extensive description of statistical TD. ~$20
- \- Dehoff: Thermodynamics in Materials Science. This text is primarily covering classical thermodynamics (with one chapter statistical). (good alternative to Gaskell) ~$90.
- \- Hudson: Thermodynamics of Materials. This text has previously been used for this class. Covers well the overall content (both classical and statistical). Some mistakes. Shortcuts in derivations that make it hard to follow. ~$190 (previous students may have a copy to borrow)
- \- Callen: Thermodynamics and an introduction to thermostatistics. Covers both classical and statistical TD, focusing more on classical. Good coverage of concepts. ~$160 (also e)
- \- Zemansky and Dittman: Heat and Thermodynamics. Extensive coverage of classical and some statistical TD. Widely used reference. Broad engineering perspective. $20-80 (also e).
- \- Kittel: Thermal physics. Statistical TD. Physics oriented.
- \- Reif: Fundamentals of statistical and thermal physics. Both classical and statistical TD. Physics oriented.

Sandler: An introduction to Applied Statistical Thermodynamics. Newer book on statistical TD. ChemE perspective.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The goal of the course is to review classical thermodynamics, to provide an introduction to the fundamentals of statistical thermodynamics, to establish an understanding of the relationship between statistical and classical thermodynamics, and to explore selected applications of statistical thermodynamics in Materials Science.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to use thermodynamic concepts and terminology to describe equilibrium and phase behavior of materials.
- Students will be able to apply both classical and statistical thermodynamics formulas and methods to specific materials problems.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Homework:** Homework assignments will be announced in class and/or by email and are typically due within 5-7 days. Due to the hybrid mode of this course, homework assignments will be handled through lms.rpi.edu. Homework is designed to complement the class-room experience. The homework can either be done alone or as a team. If done as a team, turn in only a single homework with names of up to three students on it. Do not turn in two separate nearly identical homework solutions, as this looks like illegal copying. The student name on a homework indicates that the student has done and/or participated in creating the solution and understands how to do the entire homework. All students on the same turned-in homework will receive the identical grade.

**Exams:** The exams are ~1 hour each, and are held during regular class hours. Bring a working calculator. No notes are allowed, if not previously announced by the instructor.

**Final exam:** This class has an oral final exam. Students will be tested individually for 20-30 minutes, each. The audio of these exams will be recorded. The final exams will be held after the last lecture, during the official RPI final exam period.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

The relationship between students and faculty is based upon trust, and the continued maintenance of this trust is necessary for education to be successful. Students need to trust faculty to make appropriate judgments as to the content and structure of the course. Faculty members need to trust that the work turned in by students represents their own effort. Violation of this trust undermines the educational process. In addition, as engineers, you may be designing products that lives depend on. As a result, there is no tolerance for breach of academic integrity such as cheating, plagiarizing, or inappropriate sharing of laboratories or quizzes.

**Anyone caught cheating or plagiarizing for the first time will receive zero points in the assignment or test.**

**Anyone caught cheating or plagiarizing for the second time will receive an F in the course.**

**In addition, cases of academic dishonesty are also reported to the Office of the Dean of Students**, which keeps a report of the incident on file, and may also decide of further corrective measures.

Cheating includes sharing answers, stealing answers, and placing text strings or equations on your calculator or phone. Any interaction with a mobile device during an exam without explicit permission of the instructor will be interpreted as the illicit transfer of exam data, will be considered an act of cheating and will be treated as such. Plagiarism means copying words from someone’s work, even if you “change the sentence a bit.” If you share your homework, you are as guilty as the person copying it. **If you do use any source other than the textbook or your notes from class, you need to ALWAYS reference it, this includes Web-pages.**
