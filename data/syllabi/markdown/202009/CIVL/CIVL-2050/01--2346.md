## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

CIVL 2050 is the introductory course in fluid and flow phenomena applicable to engineering problems. It is also the laboratory course in fluids and flow phenomena. It is intended to draw to student's attention the behavior of real fluids and to expose students to the experimental method.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Fluid Mechanics: Fundamentals and Applications**,** Yunus A. Cengel & John M. Cimbala

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Analyze pressures and forces exerted by fluids at rest and in uniform flow
- Apply the fundamental principles of conservation of mass, energy and momentum to a control volume
- Analyze energy losses in pipe flows
- Use dimensionless number to evaluate pipe friction, pump performance and model/prototype relationships
- Acquire skills in analyzing and solving engineering problems.
- Make measurements of flow phenomena
- Apply the fundamental principles of hydrostatics and conservation of mass, energy, and momentum to a control volume
- Write a succinct report on the experiment and discuss the experimental observations in the context of theory.

### Other Course Policies:

Organization:

The course is structured into two-110-minute lectures per week. Each class will be divided into lecture sessions and in-class participation sessions by the students. Lectures will be conducted on an iPad and lecture notes will be posted on the LMS. **In person lectures will be replaced WebEx meetings. Recordings of the lectures will be posted to LMS for viewing at later dates**.

Labs:

Throughout the course, students will engage with four laboratory experiments where they will become familiar with the experimental method. This may be described as observing, modelling, predicting and evaluating fluid behavior. A theoretical model will usually be available from lecture notes or textbooks and the model will predict results for the experiment, which can then be compared with measured results. In this way the theoretical model can be evaluated for its usefulness. Students will write a succinct report on the experiment and discuss the experimental observations in the context of theory. **A video recording of the lab procedure will be provided for you to watch remotely. Labs data will subsequently be available via LMS with ample time to complete the lab report.**

Experiments (see dates below)

1. Orifice and free jets
2. Flow meters
3. Impact of a jet
4. Energy loss in pipes

Homework:

There will be approximately 11 homework assignments, issued according to the schedule below. Homework assignments will be submitted digitally on LMS as a PDF. Students should scan their assignments (there are many scanning apps available for smart phones) and it is the student’s responsibility to ensure sufficient scan quality such that writing is legible. Solutions will be uploaded to LMS shortly following submission. No late assignments will be accepted.

Some extended answer problems will require the use of software. Students are suggested to become familiar with MATLAB, however, any package (i.e., excel) will suffice. Homework assignments will be graded as follows:

- One question (selected by instructor or TA) graded for correctness (5/10 points);
- The remaining homework questions are complete (5/10 points).

The lowest homework grade will be dropped.

You are encouraged to collaborate with other students on homework assignments, however, each student is expected to submit their own work. **Students found to have copied any part of their homework from a solution manual or from an online database will be considered in breach of academic honesty. Appropriate recourse will** **sort based on the circumstances.**

*Homework presentation*

**1)** It is strongly encouraged that students use engineering “ruled” paper (8 ½ * 11 in.) with a clean left edge (i.e., no perforations). This paper is available at the bookstore. Each problem should begin at the top of a new page with the problem # and text problem # shown.

**2)** The upper margin of the first page of your homework should show your name, the homework assignment number, the date the homework is turned in, and the page number. The upper margin of the first page of each problem should show the problem number and page number. Other pages should include the page number. The page number should be given in the upper right corner in the format A/B where A is the current page number and B is the total number of pages in your homework.

**3)** Computations should be arranged in logical order and each step recorded in full (other engineers should be able to easily follow your work). The order of presentation of work should be as shown below. Any work that does not substantially follow this order of presentation will receive no credit.

\- List given data and diagrams

\- List required results

-------------------------------------------------- (use a line to separate information given to you from your work)

\- Calculations and diagrams

\- Final answer (boxed in)

**4)** Do not make short-cuts. Perform one operation at a time and make a complete record of it. Only experienced engineers can safely take short-cuts. An exception may be made for repetitive or tabular computations, in which case it is enough to provide a complete record of a sample calculation.

**5)** Final results should be clearly indicated by *boxing* them in.

**6)** The correct units should be provided for intermediate results and final answers. A result is of no value if units are not provided. Note that careful consideration of units will often prevent mistakes.

*Clearness and neatness*

**1)** Figures and letters must be neat and large enough to be easily read.

**2)** All work must be done in pencil. Pencil marks must be clear-cut and black so that they are easily readable.

**3)** Erasing must be clean and thorough. Smudgy, dirty work is not acceptable.

Opportunity for extra credit:

After the midterm exams, students may submit a “root cause analysis” (RCA). This brief report requires an analysis of the mistakes made in exams. Students who choose to conduct the root cause analysis may redeem points on their original exam score. See “Root cause analysis guidelines” on LMS.
