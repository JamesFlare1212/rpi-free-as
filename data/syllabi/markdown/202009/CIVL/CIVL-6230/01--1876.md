## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

**About textbooks:**

There is not a single book that could be used as the sole reference for a course of this nature. The students will be provided with guidance on journal articles, reports, and textbooks to get a complete picture of transportation economics, mathematical economics and microeconomic theory.

**Recommended Textbook:**

1. Levinson, D. Gillen, M. Iacono (2013) “Transportation Economics” (free)

http://www.ebooks-for-all.com/bookmarks/detail/Transportation-Economics/onecat/Electronic-books+Economics-and-Business+Microeconomics/1/all_items.html

Litman, T. (2013) “Understanding Transport Demands and Elasticities: How Prices and Other Factors Affect Travel Behavior” https://www.vtpi.org/elasticities.pdf

**Suggested References for Advanced Reading:**

Jara-Diaz, S. (2007) “Transport Economic Theory” Elsevier ISBN 978-0-08-045028-5

Ben-Akiva, M. and Lerman S. R. (2000) *“Discrete Choice Analysis: Theory and Application to Travel Demand.”* The MIT Press. ISBN 0-262-02217-6 (The reference for discrete choice models.)

**Additional bibliography:**

Oum, T.H. and Waters, H.G. Eds. (1998) "Transport Economics" PTT Publishers. ISBN 90-5702-186-2 (A good compilation of original papers on transportation economics.)

Varian, H. (1992) “*Microeconomic Analysis*,” 3rd Edition, W.W. Norton & Company. (Advanced textbook on microeconomics, companion of Varian, 1996)

Yang, H. and Huang, H.J. (2005) “Mathematical and Economic Theory of Road Pricing” Elsevier ISBN 0-08-044487-3

Gujarati, D. (2002) “Basic Econometrics” 4th Edition McGraw Hill. (An excellent introduction to Basic Econometrics.)

Button, Kenneth J., (2002) “*Transport Economics*,” 3rd Edition, Edward Elgar Publishing Limited. ISBN: 1-85278-523-3 (Good reference that emphasizes conceptual aspects and European experience. No mathematical economy.)

Small, K. (1995) “*Urban Transportation Economics*,” Harwood Academic Publishers. (Good book on urban transportation economics that focuses on quantification, and empirical and theoretical issues. It does not discuss microeconomic theory, nor mathematical economy.)

Henderson, J. and Quandt, R. (1980) “*Microeconomic Theory: A mathematical approach*,” McGraw Hill. (The reference in mathematical economy.)

Rasmusen, E. (2001) “Games and Information,” Blackwell Publishers.

Varian, H. (1996) “*Intermediate* *Microeconomics: A Modern Approach*,” 4th Edition, W.W. Norton & Company. (A good introductory textbook to microeconomic theory.)

## Course Goals

### Goals:

To provide the students with a solid understanding of the fundamentals of transportation economics and its interrelationships with microeconomic theory, demand / supply analysis, and the economics of project analysis. In general terms, both the undergraduate and graduate versions of the course have similar outlines though the levels of expectations are different.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

# Outline

# I. Introduction

Economic nature of transportation demand. Transportation and externalities. Transportation and land use. Review of microeconomic theory. The market. Demand and supply. Budget constraint, preferences and optimal choice. Indifference curves. Demand and quantity consumed. Factors conditioning demand. Elasticity: price and income. Classification of goods according to elasticities. Substitution and income effect: Slutsky equation. Consumer and producer surplus

Suggested references: Any textbook in micro-economics

# II. Empirical Evidence

Empirical evidence and issues associated with transportation demand. Factors influencing travel demand. Price elasticities. Market segmentation. Income elasticity. Travel time value.

Suggested references: Oum 2 (handouts)

# III. Estimation of Aggregated Demand Functions

ABC of Multiple Regression Analysis. Difference between OLS, WLS and GLS. Key statistics. Application of OLS to estimation of linear and non-linear models.

Suggested references: Gujarati or any textbook on econometrics, NLOGIT, STATA manual

# IV. Estimation of Disaggregate Demand Functions

Estimation of disaggregate demand functions using discrete choice models. Discrete choice models. Model structures. Random Utility Theory. Components of utility function. Binary models. Multinomial Logit (MNL). Nested Logit (NL). Joint Logit (JL). Basic statistic tests. Practical estimation issues. Aggregation. Sampling implications and other issues.

Suggested references: Ben Akiva and Lerman Chapters 1-10; NLOGIT, STATA manual

# V. Value of time

Definition. Alternative methods to Suggested references: Jara-Diaz 2

# VI. Production and Cost Functions

Review of production function, cost functions and cost theory. Fixed and variable costs. Short term vs long term. Short run average cost, short run marginal costs, short run total costs. Transportation costs. Fixed and variable costs. Short term vs long term. Scale economies. Scope economies. Specific, joint and common costs. Allocation of joint and common costs. User and generalized costs.

Suggested references: Any textbook in micro-economics.

# VII. Transportation Externalities

Types of externalities. Sources of externalities. Valuation techniques. Ways to mitigate or eliminate externalities. Role of the public sector. Review of pricing theory. Congestion pricing.

Suggested references: Jara-Diaz 4, Yang and Huang

# VIII. Welfare Economics

Applications of consumer and producer surplus to economic analysis of transportation projects. Classic definition of consumer surplus vs consumer surplus according to Random Utility Theory.

Suggested references: Jara-Diaz 3

# IX. Game theory.

Applications to transportation phenomena.

Suggested references: Jara-Diaz 3

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- At the end of the course, the students will be able to interpret transportation phenomena from the standpoint of transportation economics.
- At the end of the course, the students will be able to understand the economic foundations of economic analysis of transportation projects

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

In other words, the outcomes will be assessed on the basis of the students’: demonstrated knowledge of the theoretical foundations, ability to solve practical problems and to produce a professional quality report that describes the work done.
