## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a graduate level course oriented mainly towards engineering students interested in learning the concepts of intelligent transportation systems. These include highway and transit systems and commercial vehicle operation. Students will be exposed to both public, private and personal ITS systems.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The primary goal of the course is to prepare students for careers as technical, project or management staff in organizations which plan, design, implement, manage or maintain intelligent transportation systems.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Students will have an understanding of the various ITS applications and technologies in all surface transportation modes.
- 2\. Students will be able to determine the appropriate technology for various ITS applications including communications technology and sensors in vehicles and infrastructure
- 3\. Students will be able to perform an ITS requirements analysis, systems analysis and project evaluation
- 4\. Students will understand the role of the government and commercial sectors of the economy in ITS deployment
- 5\. Students will have an understanding of and appreciation for the environment in which ITS operates which includes concerns about privacy, the role of standards and ITS architecture, and ethical design
- 6\. Students will be able to effectively communicate technical concepts to a broad audience.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

There will be four brief and one major papers in the course.

Brief papers:

1\. **Data paper**: Establishing policies related to autonomous vehicles rely on god data analysis. Your project is the provide background information to inform policy makers about actual transportation characteristics of vehicles and travelers. These are examples of suggested areas of inquiry:

- Estimate the distribution of daily miles of travel of personal vehicles
- Determine the age distribution and the age distribution weighted by number of miles driven for the light duty vehicle fleet in the United States.

2. The second will be a description of some **sensor technology** used in transportation. This may include vehicle presence detection (loop detectors), infrared sensors, GPS, weigh-in-motion sensors, weather (temperature and precipitation) sensors, facial recognition, speed acceleration and direction sensors). Students will make a brief 10 minute presentation on their paper. The paper should include a plain language description of the underlying science of the sensor and some application of the sensor. Presentations on Thursday, September 17

3. The third will be a brief (3-4 page) paper describing in plain language a specific **communications technology** (Bluetooth, RFID, microwave, fiber optic, 802.11 wireless, GMRS, SMS, POTS, optical etc.). As in the previous sensor paper, some description of the underlying science and the attributes of the technology such as range, latency, security and throughput. Students will make a brief 10 minute presentation on their paper. Presentations on Thursday, September 24

4. The fourth will be a brief paper (3-4 pages) on some non-technical ITS issue. This might include privacy issues, standards development, requiring users to install safety equipment etc. This will also include a presentation. Due date in November to be determined.

Final paper:

Student will also be required to prepare a proposed systems analysis design for an intelligent transportation technology of their choosing. The paper will include a concise definition of the problem to be solved, requirements analysis, concept of operations assessment of alternatives, and a conceptual plan including the specific technology for communications, human interface, possible problems and solutions. Students may work in teams of up to 5 people.
