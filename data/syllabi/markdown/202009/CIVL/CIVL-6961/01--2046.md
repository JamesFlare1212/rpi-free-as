## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will introduce the basic data ***acquisition, processing, visualization,*** and ***analysis*** techniques that are important to civil and environmental engineering (CEE) problems. Students will go through the entire life cycle of data analytics and learn to use these techniques to solve CEE problems. This course provides an opportunity to develop the skills of studying civil and environmental engineering issues through data analysis and to gain hands-on experience with python and other data analysis tools. The course will emphasize application, but the underlying theory and limitations will also be discussed to ensure that the methods are fully understood, properly applied and potentially enhanced.

For the problem sets, you may (are encouraged to) discuss with other students but the final written solution should be your own work. The midterm exam is a closed book exam; the final exam is open book and open class notes.

Homework exams are expected to demonstrate that the students: a) fully understand the theoretical foundations of the work they are doing; b) are able to obtain accurate and correct numerical solutions to the assignments; c) are able to produce a professional quality report that outlines the solution procedures, assumptions made, limitations of the approach used and, in general, provide the instructor with a solid understanding of the students' knowledge on the subject of the assignment.

Each homework and design problem is **DUE AT THE BEGINNING OF THE CLASS ON DESIGNATED DAYS;** **LATE ASSIGNMENTS WILL NOT BE ACCEPTED**; unless under extreme circumstances as stated in the Rensselaer Student Handbook.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).
