## Course Goals

### Goals:

To prepare students for the quickly changing landscape of software development from an industry and community perspective. Elucidation of the current state of the art in software development and directions. Specification, design and implementation of a ¾ semester long SD project (individual or team). Advancement in at least two areas in the Software Development Life Cycle. Improve software development project planning, execution and presentation skills.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Plan software development management processes
- 2\. Apply software development management methodologies
- 3\. Become familiar with the software development industry, and use open source to improve working efficiency
- 4\. Develop documents for software development management, such as requirements, project plans, etc.
- 5\. Develop documents for software development, such as function specification, design, and testing
- 6\. Apply advanced programming technologies, such as design patterns, frameworks, IDEs, and build automation

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

This course is designed to be a topic and project course in which students develop the skills to research topical areas of software development, assess the current state and directions, and present to the class. As such, student engagement and participation is a critical part of achieving these outcomes

*Presentations*

Each student will be required to give two presentations during the semester – one topical lecture (30 mins) and one software development project presentation (duration determined based on individual versus team composition). Students sign up for topical lecture presentation dates and will be able to (mostly) choose between topics. Details on presentations will be given in the week 1 and week 2 classes.

*In-class discussion*

Everyone in the class is expected to attend or watch lectures and presentations and to participate in the discussions – by voice/ video or message in Webex Teams using “reply to this thread” mechanism. At present there is no plan to use LMS discussion boards but if the discussions become complex that avenue may be required. Note that discussion participation must be meaningful to count toward your grade, and not vague or uninformed (*e.g. “I didn’t understand” or a thousand thumbs-up – they are welcome but will not count*).

*Post-topic lecture quizzes*

Following topical presentations, every student except the presenter is required to answer a short quiz of ~ 3 questions based on the topical presentations in each class – note this means FOUR quizzes per week for 5 weeks. Each quiz will be developed by the student presenting the lecture and approved by the Instructor. The student developer will not complete the quiz but will receive full grade for the quiz they construct. Quizzes will be submitted through the LMS and will be due by 1159 pm on the Monday after the presentation.

*Attendance Policy*

Attendance is a graded component of this course and, as such, is required. Students can miss one (1) class period without affecting their grade.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Note 6000-level students will not be assigned C- or D grades, i.e. C- or less is an F.

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

***A special note on plagiarism.***

Please see the definition of plagiarism given below, as outlined in The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights. To be clear, any time material that is not the students’ own is used, it should be in quotes and contain a citation. If a student paraphrases work from another, it also requires a citation. Furthermore, even when citations are used, others’ work cannot be used as a student response on an assignment. In the case of software we will address the topic of “copying” which is considered a common practice in software development in relation to ethics and conduct of software development.

*“**Plagiarism** Representing the work or words of another as one’s own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference. For example, using sentences verbatim from a published source in a term paper without appropriate referencing, or presenting as one’s own the detailed argument of a published source, or presenting as one’s own electronically or digitally enhanced graphic representations from any form of media.”* - ACADEMIC INTEGRITY (2014-2016 Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights & Responsibilities, August 2014)

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

***Academic Integrity***

This course will utilize a ***zero-tolerance policy*** for any form of cheating or other academic misconduct. All submitted materials will be subjected to copying and plagiarism checkers. University policy deals with the problem of academic dishonesty, cheating, and plagiarism. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. No violation will be tolerated in this class. The sanctions provided in this policy will be used to deal with any violations.

Academic dishonesty includes, but is not limited to, copying material from another student or use of written notes during an exam, representing others’ work as your own, changing answers on an exam paper after the paper has been graded, direct duplication of materials found in any online form, without suitable attribution/ license acknowledgement, and specifically for any software; scripts, packages, distributions, etc. Any student caught cheating will be assigned an F for the course. Furthermore, violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education for undergraduate and graduate students, respectively).

If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting, please ***ask*** for clarification before submitting.
