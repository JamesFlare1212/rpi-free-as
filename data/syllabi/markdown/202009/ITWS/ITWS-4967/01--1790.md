## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Cybersecurity is one of the fastest growing fields in computer science. Modern Binary Exploitation will focus on teaching practical offensive security skills in binary exploitation and reverse engineering. Through a combination of interactive lectures, hands on labs, and guest speakers from industry, the course will offer students a rare opportunity to explore involved subjects in a rapidly evolving field.

The course will start off by covering basic x86 reverse engineering, vulnerability analysis, and classical forms of Linux-based userland binary exploitation. It will then transition into defenses found on modern systems (Canaries, DEP, ASLR, RELRO, Fortify Source, etc.) and the techniques used to defeat them. Time permitting, the course will also cover other subjects in exploitation including kernel-land and Windows based exploitation, as well as static analysis of binaries.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students will learn x86_64 assembly coding, Unix memory mapping and layout of binaries, development of exploits through reverse engineering, memory corruption, shellcoding, race conditions, and ROP chains. Students will learn how exploit mitigation techniques such as stack coodies, NX/DEP, and ASLR work, and techniques for overcoming these exploit mitigation techniques.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Possess the skills necessary to carry out vulnerability analysis of binary applications.
- Understand executable formats, program control flow at the assembly level, and other low level concepts.
- Understand classic and contemporary vulnerabilities and exploitation techniques.
- Apply both source code auditing and binary reverse engineering to the vulnerability discovery process.
- Be capable of exploiting vulnerabilities found in real world software as defined by MITRE’s Critical Vulnerabilities and Exposures (CVE) system.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Labs: Each lecture will be accompanied by a lab that will solidify understanding of the topic of the lecture. Each lab will contain at least 3 problems, a C, B, and A-level problem. Learning outcomes: 1, 2, 3, 4  
Term Projects: There will be two projects over the course of the semester, one at midterm time and one at the end of the semester. In these projects students will be exploiting a known vulnerability in a realistic piece of software as selected by the instructors. Students will be expected to analyze the vulnerability and produce a working exploit with a writeup detailing the vulnerability and how it can be leveraged to gain privileged information. Students may work in groups of up to four for projects. Learning outcomes: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Labs: 70%, 10 labs equally weighted at 7% respectively, of the final grade

Labs will typically consist of 3 problems, a C, B, and A-level problem with each problem harder than the last. Lab grading works as follows.

- Students who complete only the C problem will receive 75% (C)
- Students who complete both the C and B problems will receive 90% (A-)
- Students who complete all three lab problems will receive 100% (A)

If a student does not complete the C-level problem before the end of the associated lecture period, they will receive a letter grade reduction for the lab. For example, if a student fails to complete the C-level problem during the lecture period, but then goes on to successfully complete the C and B-level problems for the week, the grade received for that week’s lab will be a B- (i.e., A- minus one letter grade).

In order to receive full credit for a problem the student must submit their exploit code, the flag, and may be asked to explain their work upon submission to be checked off. Please leave comments and other supporting materials in your code.

Lab problems will typically be introduced at the beginning of Thursday class each week. The C-level problem will be due in person by the end of the Thursday class for the week. All other problems become homework. It is highly advised that B-level problems be submitted no later than one week from their original assigned date.

Students may collaborate on labs in teams of up to four students. Collaborations must be notated in comments in code turned in, including but not limited to the names of your collaborators, explanations of who worked on what, and an explanation in your own words the techniques and exploit concepts used. Failure to include this information when collaborating will result in half credit (-50% reduction) for the week’s labs. All students are responsible for turning in their own personal labs in order to receive any credit, even if they worked collaboratively.

- All work MUST be submitted no later than the last day of classes (December 11, 2020). Anything submitted later than that will not be accepted.

Term Projects: 30%, two projects: midterm weighted at 10% and final weighted at 20%

- Project specific grading breakdowns will be given when they are assigned.
- Project checkpoints exist to keep you on pace with projects, failing to make checkpoints will result in a -5% reduction to the term project grade.
- Projects submitted late will receive a -10% reduction per day late, and will not be accepted for credit after 5 days late.
- Projects may be worked on collaborative, subject to all the same rules and regulations as collaboratively working on labs.

As the course revolves around the exploitation platform, students will be instantly informed of grades upon successful completion of each lab and will be able to track their progress in real time throughout the semester.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance for lectures is mandatory. It will be exceptionally difficult to keep up without attending lecture classes.

Attendance for labs is required as the first problem must be turned in in-person during the lab classes.

All absences must be communicated in advance to the instructor. If a lab class must be missed, arrangements to make up the missed lab work will be made on a case-by-case basis.

### Other Course Policies:

There will be servers dedicated to hosting the problems to be completed by students for both the lab and projects. The submitted exploits/solutions can be developed in any practical manner, but ultimately must work on the course servers to receive credit.  
The labs and interactive exercises used in lecture will be hosted in a variety of different series on an internal ‘Wargame’ server meant purely for teaching security concepts in a safe and educational environment. Students enrolled in the course will have access to the server and are expected to use it as an aide to the learning process. Attacking parts of the server infrastructure beyond the scope of what is assigned or attempts to disrupt services for the course or other students is not allowed and will be considered a violation of academic integrity and will be grounds for failure of the course.  
Any attempt to use the skills learned in this class to attack any RPI server or any external server will be grounds for immediate failure of the course.
