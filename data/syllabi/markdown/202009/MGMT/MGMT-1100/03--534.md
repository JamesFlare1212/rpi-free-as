### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

***Class Participation and Attendance: 10%***

You will find that complex business decisions often do not have a ‘correct’ or ‘best’ answer. In some areas, such as accounting, there is a correct way to do things, of course. However, much of what we discuss will require informed judgment, points of view, and differing opinions. This makes participation very important – the class is not just about me telling you what to think. Therefore, your contribution to discussions is necessary. Your participation grade is based on your “presence,” as opposed to mere attendance, and your contribution to discussions.

Please make every effort to attend both in class and synchronous sessions, and to contribute to class discussions. Contributions to class discussion can be either through direct communication, or in some instances, posing questions via our chat feature on WebEx or through the discussion board on LMS.

If you are going to miss class, please notify me **in advance** by email. An excused absence does not exempt you from the assignment due for that day. If you are sick and need to take the class asynchronously, you must notify me via email and let me know your situation. **Unexcused absences and excessive excused absences will reduce your class participation grade.**

Note that I will ask you to complete confidential peer reviews of your team members at the end of each project milestone, and again at the end of the term. Thus, your team members will be assessing you as well. Your grade on group assignments ***may*** be impacted by your teammate’s evaluation of your contributions.

***Homework and Case Assignments: (25%)***

Questions for each individual assignment will be given orally in the class prior to the due date and are also posted on the syllabus and LMS. You will submit electronically a professional quality document (proofread, clean copy, with appropriate references) for each assignment by the beginning of the class in which it is due. Your grade will reflect the quality of your analysis, the strength of your reasoning, the quality of your writing, the inclusion of outside research and the appearance of your submission where applicable.

Three team homework assignments (and four team consulting milestones) are listed on the schedule. Questions for each team assignment will be given orally in class one week prior to the due date and will also be posted on LMS. **Teams must submit to LMS an electronic copy of their assignment or presentation slides by the start of class.**

There are Mini simulations on Pearson’s MyManagementLab that are also part of your homework. Decisions made within the simulation earn students’ points. The number of points possible depends on which path the student chooses throughout the simulation. The percentage score they earn from the simulation equals the percentage of assignment points that will be recorded in the grade book.

- Within the simulation, each decision has a point value of 5 points (best choice), 3 points (an ok choice), 1 point (not the best choice) or 0 points (incorrect--usually for quantitative or ethically based questions), based on the level of correctness of each decision made by the student.
- Each decision point contributes to the percentage that has been designated by you as the assignment’s worth. The number of points the student receives for each decision (0 to 5 points), is reflected in the final results page.
- Keep in mind that the maximum number of points for each decision do not equal 100 points, but instead, is only reflective of the level of correctness for each decision. NOTE: the maximum number of ‘in-simulation” points (5 points for each decision) will not add up to 100 points total. For example, if a student selects all of the best choices for each decision, he or she will receive all possible points for that simulation, resulting in the student achieving 100% of the assignment points to be recorded in the grade book.
- These percentages are then applied to the assigned points for that simulation, which is listed in the detailed evaluation chart below.

***Quizzes: (25%)***

There will be three quizzes throughout the semester that will primarily cover the topical content, i.e., finance, accounting, marketing, operations, etc. They may reference the cases, but I am not testing you on detailed case knowledge. We will talk about this more in class ~1 week before each quiz. Each may consist of multiple-choice questions, fill in the blanks, and some calculations.

***Consulting Challenge Mini-Milestones: (16.7%)***

The Consulting Challenge is a research project in which you and your team will analyze one company (from an approved list) in depth. There will be one company chosen for this early in the term and all teams will analyze separate companies. Detailed instructions and evaluation criteria will be posted on LMS and discussed in time for you to do the work.

There are two types of deliverables for the Consulting Mini-Milestones. Each week you are required to submit a company update using business-based news feeds to obtain information. If your company has not been in the news, you will check and report what is happening to your competitors or to the industry in general. Your other main deliverable will be to make an oral presentation (in class or synchronous, depending on the schedule) to the class for each of the four milestones.

***Final Consulting Grand Challenge: (23.3%)***

Your “final” for this class is a team assignment and includes a presentation (synchronous or asynchronous -- TBD) of the company you have researched over the course of the semester. The presentation includes supporting documentation which illustrates the research, planning and preparation done by your team. The overall purpose of the assignment is for you to identify challenges the company is facing, come up with recommendations to improve the business, and provide your best assessment of where this company will be in five years. Your recommendations should be achievable, well thought out, and capable of having a substantial impact on the company.

The project should build off the entire course. Think of it in 4 phases: (1) assess the situation / market / company (using tools from readings/lectures/milestones #1-4); (2) generate ideas for recommendations; (3) select recommendations and present them, and (4) present your assessment of their 5 year outlook. We will discuss your ideas in individual team meetings the last week prior to the presentations.

Your team will be graded on the quality of your analysis, your recommendations, the quality and professionalism of your presentation, your use of concepts from the class, your ability to provide a recommendation(s) with an accompanying detailed implementation plan and your responses to challenges during a question and answer session.

Each team should include references/citations (MLA format), and a spreadsheet that includes pro-forma financials that incorporate investment needed and illustrate the growth projections, such that they cover a number of years.

In addition, the team will submit their presentation as a deliverable. A final written paper is not needed, so be sure the presentation is clear and well written. Any additional research, pro-forma financials, data gathered, supporting materials, etc is to be submitted to the LMS drop box as separate appendices.

**Although this is a team project, the instructor reserves the right to allocate grades individually, based on each individual member’s contributions to the presentation and to the question and answer session.**

Again, note that I will ask you to complete confidential peer reviews of your team members at the end of the term. Your grade on this group assignments ***may*** be impacted by your teammate’s evaluation of your contributions.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
