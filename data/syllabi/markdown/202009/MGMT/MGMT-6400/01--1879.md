## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course introduces master’s students in the Quantitative Finance and Risk Analysis program to the econometric foundations and tools for working with financial data. This course addresses financial modeling as an empirical activity – using different econometric techniques in the analysis of financial markets with actual data. Some of the topics covered in this course include return forecasting and volatility, asset pricing models, event studies, panel data analysis, exchange rate movements, the term structure of interest rates, and international linkages among financial markets. Methods and models are discussed with real-data examples that use R programming. Computers are used extensively both in and outside of class

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Estimate various types of regression models using financial data
- 2\. Understand the strengths and weaknesses of econometric methods used in finance, and business in general.
- 3\. Understand and be able to use various time-series models to analyze financial data
- 4\. Forecast financial data using econometric techniques and test their effectiveness
- 5\. Understand and test asset pricing models 6. Use event-study methodology.
- 6\. Use event-study methodology.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- **Attendance, Preparation, and Participation**: Students are expected to attend all lectures, come prepared by having done the assigned readings, and participate during lectures.

- **Assignments:** There will be 4-5 assignments. Students are responsible for turning in completed assignments by the due date. Students can work in pairs but must turn in individual solutions. Unless the student has made prior arrangements with the Syllabus 3 of 8 8.25.2020 instructor, no late assignments will be accepted. Solutions will be provided immediately after the due dates.

- **Exams**: There will be two closed-book exams. Each exam will cover material discussed in class up to the exam date. The second exam will focus mainly on material covered after the first exam.

- **Exploratory Research Projects:** Students will be divided into groups of 3-4 to work on an exploratory research project. More details will be given after the first exam. Presentations will be scheduled towards the end of the semester. The written report will include the following:

7-10 pages max of text and a max of 25 pages total of text, figures, tables, and references.  
Sections: an abstract, introduction, a short literature review, methodology, results and implications, conclusion, and list of reference. Clean and well documented data files (codes and data)
