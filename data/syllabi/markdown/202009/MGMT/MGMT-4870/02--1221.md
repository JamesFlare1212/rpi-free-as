### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Course Learning Objectives/Outcomes:**

Knowledge objectives:

On completing the course, it is expected that students would have learned and would be able to demonstrate an understanding of the following.

1. The purpose and process of managing the overall business organization (as opposed to managing specific functional areas such as marketing, finance, production or accounting).
2. Difference between corporate and business strategy.
3. The content of strategies and the processes by which they are formulated and implemented in business enterprises.
4. Integrate concepts from diverse functional area courses (marketing, finance, etc.) to device corporate strategy at the overall enterprise level. .

Skills objectives

In addition to knowledge objectives, the course will provide an opportunity to develop or apply the following skills:

1. *Analytical skills: synthesize functional knowledge to develop skills pertaining to achieving and sustaining competitive advantage*
2. *Group work skills: work on conceptual, ill structured problems as a team to come up with solutions*
3. *Business writing skills:* *communicate qualitative and quantitative ideas effectively*
4. *Live presentation skills: verbally communicate abstract ideas to functional specialists and implementers of solutions*

**Course Design:**

The course is designed to achieve the objectives listed above. The Hill, Schilling & Jones text will provide theoretical material. This material will be supplemented with other readings and handouts from time to time. Case studies and other assignments will provide opportunities to apply the analytical tools & frameworks obtained from theory. In addition, the course includes assignments that involve analytical work, group work, writing, and making presentations.

The course will be taught through a discussion of theory and case studies. Therefore, it is imperative that you complete your readings and assignments for each class and be prepared **to discuss the material**.

***Weekly business reports.*** Monitoring the business news helps us see how the course is relevant to the business world. Doing so also helps utilize what we learn in the class to better understand, explain, and critique what goes on in the business world each day. This assignment involves selecting three business events/news items that occurred/were reported on during the week and writing a very brief report on each (not exceeding 3 pages in total for each week’s report). The reports should address the following three questions, 1. What is the story in the news? 2. Why did it interest you? 3. How does it relate to what we are learning in class? As part of your report, provide sufficient information about each news item so I can find the news item if necessary. Also provide a web link to the news item if available. Format for the reports: one page for each news story; type each question and follow that with your answer, with adequate space between each set of question and answer; mention the web link to the news item following your answer to question 3.

These reports are due on or before each Saturday, beginning on 9/12/2020 and ending on 10/31/2020. There will be a place created for each of these reports to be submitted in LMS.

Criteria for grading: (1) Did you follow all aspects of the format for the report (2) Did you pick news items from the week (3) How clearly you related each news item to something specific that we are learning in class, (4) Was the report easy to read and without spelling and grammatical errors.

***Case study reports:*** Each involves a short write-up addressing questions posed by me about the case study. Your response should be clear and backed by your analysis of facts in the case material. The reports must be typewritten and free of spelling and grammatical errors. Additional details will be provided with each case study assignment. While the first case study report is a group project, the other two are individual projects. The group membership opted for the first case study report project will remain for the other group assignments as well.

***In-class case study reports (group)***: These case studies will be provided in class on the day they are due (See course schedule). You will work in your group to analyze the case study and address the question posed by me in a brief written report. The report should be submitted before the end of class. Please ensure at least one group member has a laptop that can be used to write and submit your report in class. If you are attending the course online, the relevant policy for this assignment will be conveyed separately.

***Tests 1, 2 & 3:*** The tests are intended to check your understanding of the material learned in the course. Each test will be over material covered since the previous test.

***Group Presentations:*** There will be two group presentation projects in the semester. Groups formed earlier in the semester (groups formed to do the first case study report) will be responsible for the presentations. The group grade will apply for all members of the group.

I will let you form your own group. This means you will have both the freedom to choose who is in your group and the responsibility that comes with it—i.e. to ensure that everyone pulls together as a group.

All members are required to share responsibility and work on the projects. If a group has problems with a member I need to hear about it as soon as possible. I will convene a group meeting to find resolution. If problems persist, and at least half of the members of the group feel so, I may deduct points from the presentation project grade for the offending group member. There will also be a peer evaluation form that all members of the group will complete. Keep your presentations to 20 minutes, followed by a 5 minute Q&A. The presentations should be made using PowerPoint, and involve active and roughly equal participation from all group members. Submit a copy of the power point files to me. No other written reports are required for the presentation projects. Each of the two presentations is scheduled for three days (to allow time for all of the groups to present). We will decide the presentation day and order in class. However, to ensure that all groups have the same length of time to prepare presentations, all groups should email their respective presentations to me on or before the first day when each of the two sets of presentations (business strategy presentation and corporate strategy presentation) is scheduled.

These presentation projects involve researching relevant information and analyzing the information to address questions about business and corporate strategies of firms. Criteria used to evaluate these projects include (1) whether all questions posed for the project were addressed, (2) whether the appropriate analytical tools learned in the course were used in the appropriate manner to address the questions, and (3) whether it was clear how and why you arrived at your answers/conclusions for the questions.

***Final learning paper and log:*** Write two pages (typed, double spaced, using a reasonable font size) convincing the reader how you will use what you have learned in this class. Also, attach a log of what you personally learned from preparing for, and/or from participating in, each class session. Please make entries on this log after each class session during the semester, rather than doing it all at the end. Index this log by class#, date, and topic from the course schedule.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

***Academic integrity***

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own performance. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty. If found in violation of the academic honesty policy, students may be subject to two types of penalty. The instructor administers an academic [grade] penalty and the student is reported to the Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education as appropriate. The first violation results in 0 grade for that assignment. The second violation results in failure of the course. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

***Collaborative work and plagiarism***

All assignments and tests, except ones explicitly identified in the syllabus as group assignments, should be your own work. You cannot collaborate with anyone else in completing these assignments and tests.

For papers or presentation projects if you have to use arguments or conclusions from experts or sources using their own words, you can do so in a limited fashion (not more than a couple of sentences) but only after clearly identifying these as direct quotes (by enclosing the direct quotes within double quotation marks) and providing a reference to where the quotes were taken from (e.g., Author, year, publication title, page #). If your work is found to be plagiarized you will receive a grade of zero for the assignment in the first such instance. If there is a subsequent infraction, you will receive an F for the course.

For group projects, you should collaborate only with members of your group and not with students in other groups.
