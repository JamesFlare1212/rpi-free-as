## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Main course objective is to introduce students to basic economics principles and establish economics as a managerial decision-making framework. The course will draw on economic analysis to discuss topics that include supply and demand, consumer behavior, firm production, the effect of market structure on firm behavior and market prices, optimal pricing strategies, game theory, and the role of information. The course also introduces some behavioral economics concepts in its approach. The material in this course is relevant to managers of for-profit institutions and non-profit organizations, and to policymakers. After taking the course, the students will be empowered with some of the most important tools necessary to tackle operational business decisions.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Upon completion of the course the students are expected to:

1. Apply theoretical economic frameworks and mathematical approaches to management decisions while knowing the difference between rational economic models and behavioral economics
2. Understand the concept of supply and demand and elasticity and be able to estimate and forecast demand, production, and costs
3. Understand pricing and output decisions under perfect competition, monopolistic competition, and monopoly
4. Evaluate potential investment opportunities in various sectors and understand the concept of real options in capital budgeting
5. Understand the role of government, regulation in the market economy, and international trade.

- **Assignments****:** There will be 4-6 assignments. - **Final Group Project & Presentation****:** Students will be divided into groups of 3-5 to work on a research project. More details will be given after the first exam. The written report will include the following:

o 6-8 pages max of text and a max of 15 pages total of text, figures, tables, and references.

o Sections: an abstract, introduction, a short literature review, methodology, results and implications, conclusion, and list of reference.  
o Clean and well-documented data files (codes and data)
