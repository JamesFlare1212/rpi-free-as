## Course Goals

### Goals:

COURSE GOALS/OBJECTIVES  
Financial Trading and Investing will be divided into two sections, each with four components.  
1\) Introduction to Market Microstructure  
o Describe and examine concepts essential to markets and trading including motivations for trading, components of bid-ask spreads, liquidity, and market transparency.  
2\) World Markets  
o Study the important venues for trading in each security class including stocks, bonds, futures, derivatives, and foreign exchanges as well as examining new emerging marketplaces.  
3\) Trade Execution  
o Contrast the costs and benefits associated with various order types and execution strategies including market, limit, hidden, and algorithmic orders.  
4\) Current Topics in Trading  
o Will be continuously revised based on changing market trends. Prior topics have included high frequency trading, dark pools, and market fragmentation.  
5\) Investment Fundamentals  
o Begin with the CFA Institute Standards of Practice ethical prescription and extend into drivers of investment, performance measurement, risk management, and biases influencing investor behavior.  
6\) Active Investing  
o Explore a variety of investing strategies and vehicles including asset pricing factors, fundamental valuation, technical analysis, mutual funds, hedge funds, and private equity.  
7\) Passive Investing  
o Establish essentials of portfolio diversification, efficiency, and cost minimization then apply these principles into exchange traded funds, market indexing, and strategic asset allocation.  
8\) Current Topics in Investments  
o Will be continuously revised based on changing market trends. Prior topics have included automated investment advisor platforms, current hedge fund strategies, developments in alternative investments, and algorithmic investment strategies.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

COURSE ASSESSMENT MEASURES  
Attendance and Class Participation (25% of Course Grade)  
Attendance for each class will be taken informally using digital imaging and/or WebEx screen capture. A picture of the classroom will be taken at the beginning of each class and following our mid-class breaks. Additionally, students will receive credit for responses to in-class questions (regardless of student accuracy), mandatory Twitter exercises (to be completed within 72 hours after introduce in class), and/or surveys related to the course material and student investment portfolio.  
Assignments (3 Assignments, 10% Each)  
Students will complete three assignments throughout the semester. Each assignment is worth 10% of the course grade. Students will work independently on the multiple choice and Excel portions of these assignments and the instructor will provide review sessions and/or additional office hours before each assignment deadline (see course calendar for tentative assignment deadlines). Data exercises may be worked on in teams of 2, but each assignment will require working with a different student in the course. The assignments will be posted to Blackboard (LMS) at least one week before the assignment due date. The assignments will include a multiple choice (20-30 questions) within Blackboard and a Microsoft Excel component (2-4 questions) that is to be downloaded, completed, and uploaded back into Blackboard before the assignment deadline. Graduate students will be required to complete a separate data exercise component (requiring use of a computer programming package like MATLAB, Python, etc.) for each assignment. Undergraduate students may complete this additional exercise, but no extra credit will be awarded. The instructor will provide feedback through comments within the assignment documents and upload the comments back to Blackboard. Students are strongly encouraged to review this feedback for Microsoft Excel and data exercises.  
At the time each assignment is posted to LMS, a programming skills session will be held by the teaching assistant. This will provide each graduate student with the basic skills (in either R or Python) needed to process the data for the data exercise portion of the assignment. Before the due date for each assignment, the teaching assistant will hold a review session so that students can ask any questions related to any portion of the assignment. Dates and times for the programming skills and review sessions will be announced in class and posted to LMS.  
Final Exam (20%)  
The final exam will have a very similar format to the assignments, but the exam will be administered in-class. Each student will be required to bring a laptop computer with network connectivity and the required software (Microsoft Excel, web browser to access LMS, etc.). No external resources (e.g. non-LMS websites, chat programs, electronic or paper documents, etc.) will be permitted for use during the final examination with the exception of a single page, double-sided paper document with formulas and/or notes of the student’s choosing. There will be a data analysis component of the final exam but no programming will be required for this component, therefore it will be completed by both graduate and undergraduate students.  
Portfolio Performance & Report (25%, Due Monday, December 21st by 11:59 PM)  
Throughout the semester, students will manage a $1M paper portfolio with an individual Interactive Brokers student trading account. At the end of the semester each student will submit a portfolio report via Blackboard that describes the overall strategy of the portfolio, records and discusses the details of every transaction, evaluates the risk-adjusted performance, and reflects on what could have been done differently in the portfolio (if applicable). Grading will reflect both the relative performance (versus a 80/20 mix of VTI/BND ETFs) of the portfolio over the course of the semester and the quality/relevance of content within the report.  
The content of the report will vary based on the strategies selected by each individual student. If you have questions about what content is applicable to your portfolio, please ask the instructor. Suggested materials that may or may not be included in this portfolio report include:  
• Asset Allocation Statistics and/or Summaries (Tactical, Strategic, etc.)  
• Attribution Analysis (Asset, Sector, Region, etc.)  
• Performance Metrics (Sharpe Ratio, Jensen’s Alpha, Max Drawdown, etc.)  
• Pseudo-Code and/or Rationale for Quantitative Strategies  
• Transaction Costs Analysis (Commissions, Liquidity Considerations, etc.)  
• Valuation Methodologies for Holdings (DCF, Relative Value, etc.)  
Graduate students (QFRA) are expected to apply quantitative methods within their portfolio report. Any quantitative strategies using the Interactive Brokers API must include a full reconciliation of each trade, strategy description (preferably including code or pseudo-code), and risk-adjusted performance analysis.  
The report will cover all portfolio activity from the start of the semester through Friday, December 18th. Students should be contributing to this report periodically over the semester as portfolio trading activities occur. Procrastinating until the end of the semester to produce all trade reconciliations, transaction cost calculations, valuation methodologies, and other components of the report will be a strain on student resources during the final examination period! The entire content of the report should be no longer than 15 pages. This does not imply that each student must produce 15 pages of material: shorter reports with sufficient content are perfectly acceptable. If the initial draft of a report is longer, limit the final submission to 15 pages of the most relevant content.
