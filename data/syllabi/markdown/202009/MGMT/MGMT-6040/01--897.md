## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Framework for the Course**

This course will move from the "micro" side of O.B. to the "macro." We will examine increasingly complex levels of social behavior, moving from the individual to the interpersonal to the group and finally to the organizational level.

**The Classroom as a Learning Laboratory**

In this course, we will bring a practice-oriented approach to the material. In addition to analyzing behavior in cases and texts, we will use the classroom situation itself as a source of data to examine organizational behavior. Consequently, we will be use classroom time for simulations, role-playing, and group and intergroup interaction. Considerable work will be done in your learning groups, which will be used for several class sessions and out-of-class work. Ideally, we will be able to examine aspects of organizational behavior in the context of our group exercises. You should be able to develop your analytic skills in this context, as well as gain insights into your own interpersonal styles. Also, if your group is effective, you will be able to create an open, low-risk, non-threatening environment where you can feel free to explore your interpersonal styles and gain valuable feedback from your colleagues.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Regular Chapter Quizzes**  
Quizzes need to be taken by 2 pm of the day their associated chapter is listed on the syllabus. Each quiz will be equally weighted. There are 15 quizzes. The lowest two scores will be eliminated from the calculations.

**Individual Write-Ups and Entries**  
All write-ups and entries should be clearly written and well-organized. They should be submitted by the dates they are due. Late submissions will be penalized by 10 points per session.

In writing them, it is important to analyze as well as describe. Description tells what happened. Analysis assesses how and why it happened. Any description you do should serve the analysis. Carefully think through the issues and present a logically-reasoned argument to support your case.

The write-ups are based on doing the weekly simulation that I assign in MyLabs and writing 100-150 words on your experience with the simulation and connections to the chapter with which it is associated. The write-ups should be analytical and reflective rather than being simply descriptive. That is, they should consider the whys? and hows? rather than simply reporting the whats? They will be submitted on Blackboard as assignments.

The entries are a separate item. Once a week, I will post a question in Blackboard’s discussion section. I will expect each student to post at least one entry of 75-100 words in response to each question. Entries can be in direct response to the question I post, or to any follow-up questions I post, or they can be responses to an entry by another student under the relevant question. It will help if they can incorporate material from relevant classwork, chapters, readings, and/or lectures.  
**Group Topic Paper and Presentation**

Each group will be responsible for writing a paper and making a class presentation. Information on the nature of the presentation will be provided early in the semester.

I will judge the quality of the group presentations and the class will provide input. Relevant criteria for judging the presentations will include:  
(1) Scope - did the presentation cover the relevant material in sufficient breadth and depth?  
(2) Clarity - was the explanation of ideas and concepts clear?  
(3) Quality of analysis - how well did the group analyze the main issues?  
(4) Quality of application – how well did the group connect conceptual material to the topic of the presentation?  
(5) Contribution – to what extent did the group increase our understanding of the topic? and  
(6) Interest – how well did the group hold our interest?

**Final Exam**

We will discuss the nature of this component in detail toward the end of the semester.

**Contributions to the Class**

Class participation is an integral aspect of this course. Please read and be prepared to discuss the assigned readings and cases listed on the syllabus for each session. Quantity and quality will be considered in evaluating participation. If you are interested in participating but have difficulty initiating it, please let me know so that we can try to ease the process.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
