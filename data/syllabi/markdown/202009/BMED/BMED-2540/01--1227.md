## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Application of mechanics to study normal, diseased and traumatized musculoskeletal system. Areas covered include determination of joint and muscle forces, mechanical properties of biological tissues, and structural analysis of bone-implant systems. Case studies are discussed to illustrate the role of biomechanics in design of implants.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. Understand and quantitatively define the role of biomechanics in human physiology and apply it to understand normal, diseased and traumatized musculoskeletal system.
2. Understand the fundamental definition and manipulation of stress and strain and apply them with underlying assumptions to describe uniaxial, torsion, bending and combination of these loading behavior on musculoskeletal system with particular emphasis on long bones.
3. Understand mechanical testing of biological materials and apply it to determine material properties, constitutive models of common biological tissues and the structure-function relationships in skeletal tissues.
4. Use computational tools to set-up and solve biomechanics problems in physiology.
5. Understand the biomechanics of tissue-implant systems and apply it to understand the design and failure of implants.
