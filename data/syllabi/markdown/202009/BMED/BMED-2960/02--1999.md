## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Have a basic understanding of computer hardware and architecture.
- Understand how a computer stores and processes integer and floating point numbers as well as char and string variables.
- Understand binary and hexadecimal number systems.
- Comprehend and apply different programming statements, including if, for, while, else, switch.
- Deal with string operations and basic handling of database operations.
- Solve mathematical problem by translating them into computer codes.
- Write simple programs to solve BME related problems.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Course Assessment Measures  
Homework Assignments: Counts 50% towards the final grade calculation  
There are 12 homework assignments, typically one every week, which contain programming tasks and understanding type-questions based on the material that has been covered in class up to that point. Each homework submission needs to be uploaded on LMS under homework assign-ments on or before the due date (which is typically one week after the homework assignment was made available). Late submissions will not be accepted and receive a grade of zero unless a valid and verified excuse can be produced.  
Midterm Examination: Counts 20% towards the final grade calculation  
There is one midterm examination that includes the design of simple programs and understanding-type questions based on material that has been covered in class up to that point. The midterm exam-ination is online and scheduled for Wednesday, October 28th, 2020 from 6 to 8 pm. If a student is unable to participate in the midterm exam due to a valid and verified excuse, the percentage of the final will substitute the grade for the midterm exam. If no valid excuse can be produced, the missed midterm exam will be graded as zero. There is no makeup for the midterm exam.  
Final Examination: Counts 30% towards the final grade calculation  
There is one final examination that comprehensively assesses the course material. The exam is cumulative and contains simple programming tasks and understanding-type questions about the course material. The final examination is online and scheduled for Wednesday, December 9th, 2020, from 6 to 9 pm.  
Grading Criteria  
Grading is based on:  
• Homework Assignments (50%): There will be a total of 12 homework assignments the include programming tasks, implementation of numerical methods and understanding-type problems and questions.  
• Midterm Examination (20%): Designing simple programs and understanding-type ques-tions based on material that has been covered up including the lecture on Oct. 29th, 2020.  
• Final Examination (30%): Cumulative exam that includes designing simple programs and understanding-type questions based on the material covered up (including) the lecture on Dec. 7th, 2020.

Grading concerns:  
All graded homework submissions and examinations are graded on LMS (feedback will be pro-vided). If there are any questions or concerns, there is a one week window after each homework assignment or the midterm examination has been graded to seek clarification during the TA office hours. It is important to note that any issue that has not been brought to the attention of the TA one week after a homework assignment or the midterm exam was graded is not going to be considered. The grades for each homework assignment and the grades for the midterm and fi-nal examination will be uploaded on LMS.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
