## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is an in-depth study of current papers within the broad field of Molecular Mechanisms of Protein-Ligand Interactions. Students will read and critique primary papers, present current research articles, and lead critical discussions.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

There is no textbook requirement.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The goal of this course is to familiarize students with the current work in the field of Molecular Mechanisms of Protein-Ligand Interactions. Students will learn to analyze and present a primary research paper as well as critically evaluate the material within that paper.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

This course consists of one class per week in which a current research paper in the field is presented and analyzed in detail. Students will choose a current research paper in consultation with the instructor and present the findings to the class.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Deliver a lecture, with an eye toward critical review, on a primary scientific research paper whose topic focuses on analyzing structural, functional and thermodynamics aspects of protein-ligand interactions.
- Analyze primary research papers and defend their review to an audience of their peers.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students will be assessed by participation in class discussion and their presentation. A rubric for participation which will be given to the students on the first day of class. For their presentation, students will choose papers in consultation with their advisor. After each presentation students will meet with the instructor and receive feedback on their performance. Presentations will be graded by the instructor.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Student Grade Allotment:  
Class Participation – 60%  
Presentation – 40%  
Total – 100%

**Grading Criteria for Presentation/Paper reviews**

**A:** The presentation/paper reviews provided background on the scientific question that is raised by the paper. Figures are assessed (e.g. reviewing individual panels important to the findings and removing panels that are not central to the paper). The presenter/reviewer has an in-depth understanding of the motivation, methods, interpretation for each experiment, and how they relate to the over-arching scientific question.  
**A-:** The presentation/paper reviews fell just short in one of the elements described above.  
**B+:** There are significant flaws in one of the described areas, but others are achieved well.  
**B:** There are multiple problems with the presentation/paper reviews.  
**B-:** The presentation/paper reviews had major flaws in many of the above described categories.  
**C:** The presentation/paper reviews had major flaws in all of the above described categories.  
**C-:** The first paper review met standards but the second had the above described issues and needed grammar improvements.

Note: D and D+ grades are not available

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. Acts, which violate this trust, undermine the educational process.** The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Students should familiarize themselves with this portion of the Rensselaer Handbook and Graduate Student Supplement and should note that the penalties for plagiarism and other forms of cheating can be quite harsh.
