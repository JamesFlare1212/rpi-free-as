## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a course team-taught by research faculty members of the NSF Engineering Research Center, CURENT (Center for Ultra-wide-area Resilient Electrical Energy Transmission Networks). The goal is to present the latest development in the CURENT research areas and other related areas to the graduate students and to have them well prepared for research tasks in CURENT. The course is open to all graduate students. Note that course schedule and other course materials, including recorded lecture videos, can be found on the following UTK website, which is set up by the course coordinator Prof. Bai from UTK.

## Course Text(s)

## Course Goals

### Goals:

A student will be able to understand the characteristics and design methods for large power systems and wide-area monitoring and control systems. The course will give a general introduction to a large variety of power system research topics, such that a student after taking the course, will be able to select and start a research topic.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

1. Fundamental knowledge of CURENT
   1. Modelling, simulation and fast computation for power system analysis
   2. Estimation and prediction
   3. Transmission network architecture
   4. Multi-level control architecture
   5. Market structure and social impact
2. Enabling technologies of CURENT
   1. Ultra-wide-area monitoring and measurement
   2. Situational awareness and grid visualization
   3. Ultra-wide-area actuation and control
   4. Communication and cyber security
   5. Voltage stability
   6. Power electronics fundamentals and actuation, power quality,
   7. Power system state estimation and data analysis
   8. Cyber-physical systems
   9. Support vector learning
   10. Power system model reduction
3. Engineered system for CURENT
   1. Large-scale system test bed
   2. Hardware test bed – reconfigurable grid emulation

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- A student who finishes the course in a satisfactory manner will be able to know the latest research results in several topic areas, including: (1) wide-area control,
- 2\) wide-area monitoring and visualization,
- (3) state estimation,
- (4) power electronic control and hardware,
- (5) cyber security,
- (6) power markets and economics.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Homework problems will be assigned by the instructors for selective topics to assist students to familiarize with the key concepts. These homework problems may consist of computation using calculators or MATLAB, and/or simulating and observing power system behavior. In addition, take-home exams will be used for further assessment.
