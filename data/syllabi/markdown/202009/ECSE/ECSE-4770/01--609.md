## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

To familiarize students with basic principles of good digital design and prototyping practices: Practical familiarization with common TTL and CMOS integrated circuits is assumed as common logic gates are combined into digital circuits. Noted is the importance of signal integrity, power conditioning, and noise margins in the presence of switching noise, especially in bus environments. Students will learn FPGA based design, Xilinx and Altera CAD tool suite, Verilog and schematic capture, automatic routing and placement, post layout extraction and simulation, pad assignment, utilization of FPGAs in interfacing applications, synchronous and asynchronous bus design. Also covered in lectures are program transfer, interrupt driven and DMA data I/O, memory management, packaging issues, and heat removal

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Labs are normally team organized. There are only 2 students per team and single-student teams are common with many students opting for remote participation. The schedule for lab-gradable material (exception is the first lab – software installation), involves a brief report documenting what has been accomplished. Design details include I/O and state machine descriptions where appropriate, as well as explanations on how testing was done to verify that the design satisfied the specifications given in the lab. Use block diagrams, schematics, timing diagrams, oscilloscope traces, screen grabs, photos, or anything else to document the results. Include an analysis, discussion, and/or conclusion on both technical aspects and overall impressions. Discussions on any unusual problems that arose and how they were solved are always welcomed. Reports are graded both on content and presentation (i.e. quality of written content and writing style). A portion of the lab grade is based on a combination of the report and TA evaluation of the participation of the students in the debugging process for each project. For remote students an active participation includes a WebEx Teams meeting with the TA demonstrating proper operation and being able to answer questions about the design. There are no exams in the course, and no homework. However there are many activities that may or should be done outside of the lectures and labs, such as writing Verilog code, building circuit schematics, simulations, and debugging. Each lab takes a different number of sessions to implement, test, and document, as can be seen in the lab schedule above. All work is to represent the individual’s independent work and capability, though group discussion and joint debugging effort is encouraged.
