## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Introduces fundamentals in AI-based methods directly applicable to protect and enhance performance of industrial systems. Learning methods for modelling systems with data, model order reduction with deep learning, extreme learning machines, reinforcement learning algorithms and their deep architectures are included. Application examples for modeling & detecting cyberattacks are covered. Learn how to design a discrete reinforcement learning system measured signals in real-time using sensors and without knowing full system dynamics of the control system.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

To systematically learn AI-based methods for securing control systems against cyberattacks. Application examples include medical device(s) and/ control system(s).

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Apply mathematical fundamentals such as linear matrix algebra & calculus, singular value decomposition, principal component analysis, regularization, data normalization and variety of validation methods for designing AI learning algorithms specifically suited for biomedical & industrial control systems. 2. Demonstrate an ability for modeling systems with input-output data, decompose the system into features with autoencoders and/ convolutional neural networks and significantly reduce the order of the dynamic system model to enhance performance. 3. Demonstrate an ability to apply extreme learning machines for detecting cyber-attacks and protecting control systems. 4. Demonstrate an ability to design a discrete reinforcement learning system (an actorcritic control system) with measured signals in real-time using sensors and without knowing full system dynamics of the control system. 5. Demonstrate proficiency of applying deep mathematical techniques utilized in analyzing data, designing learning algorithms (e.g., Extreme Learning Machine or Reinforcement Learning), their architectures and evaluating algorithm performance via simulation & visualization for signal extraction or cyberattack detection or control performance. 6. Demonstrate an ability to write up one of the research problem and analysis results as a short paper or technical report.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Course assessment will be done through homeworks, one midterm exams, one final exam and a final project. Homeworks will mostly consist of quantitative problems, but also include a few matlab-based programming assignments that requires coding simple algorithms. The two exams will be open book, open notes, and time-limited, and will be conducted during the class hours. [This may change if the entire course is taught online]. For the final project, the students can work individually or in teams to apply one of the techniques learnt in the course to a real-life application. The topic of the project needs to be chosen by week 5, and in agreement with the instructor. The final project demo/presentation will be held in the last week of classes. [If the entire course is taught online, each student can submit a short video explaining how the project is completed].

Students taking the course at the 6963 level may be required to do some additional or complex questions in the homeworks that are focused on more advanced mathematical analysis. Additionally, they would have to submit a research report along with the final project demo/presentation. The details of the grading policy are provided below.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Intellectual integrity and credibility are the foundation of all academic work. A violation of Academic Integrity policy is, by definition, considered a flagrant offense to the educational process. It is taken seriously by students, faculty, and Rensselaer and will be addressed in an effective manner. If found responsible for committing academic dishonesty, a student may be subject to one or both types of penalties: an academic (grade) penalty administered by the professor and/or disciplinary action through the Rensselaer judicial process described in this handbook. Three relevant academic integrity violations to emphasize include:

**Collaboration:** Collaboration is defined as deliberately facilitating an act of academic dishonesty in any way or form; for example, allowing another student to observe an exam paper or allowing another student to "recycle" one's old term paper or using one another's work in a paper or lab report without citing it as another's work.

**Copying:** Copying is defined as obtaining information pertaining to a graded exercise by deliberately observing the paper of another student; for example, noting which alternative a neighboring student has circled on a multiple-choice exam.

**Plagiarism:** Plagiarism is defined as representing the work or words of another as one's own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference. Examples include using sentences verbatim from a published source in a term paper without appropriate referencing, or presenting as one's own the detailed argument of a published source, or presenting as one's own electronically or digitally enhanced graphic representations from any form of media.
