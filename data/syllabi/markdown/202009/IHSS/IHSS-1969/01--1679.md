## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Develop knowledge of key design periods in modern history and how they shaped and were shaped by culture & technology.
- Recognize how design and design thinking has been and can be applied to solving cultural, social and economic problems.
- Recognize the relevance of design history in contemporary design practices.
- Recognize how we can apply knowledge of design history to design for contemporary and future challenges.
- Develop an appreciation for how design is a skilled practice rather than mere decoration.
- Develop an understanding for how good design, form vs. function and aesthetics influence purchase decisions.
- Develop and demonstrate team working skills through group interaction, problem solving, presentation and verbal expression.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Assignments/Projects/Presentations: These activities include working in and outside of class on projects, working on individual or group presentations to the class or misc. homework/research assignments.

Class Readings: Readings are assigned for most classes from one of our two text books. Reading assignments are designed to provide the basis for group and class discussions. Most reading assignments will either have a written requirement (Reading Reflection or answers to the Reading Discussion Questions) to be submitted at the end of the class discussion .

Reading Reflections: These are written, one pagers based on three concepts from the reading you choose to write about. See the assignment sheet on the blackboard for more information.

Reading Discussion Questions: These are a set of questions based on specific chapter readings that you will be asked to answer and bring your “brief” written answers to class. See the assignment sheet on the blackboard for more information.

Quizz Games: Quizz Games are played in teams and can include multiple choice questions, matching, true or false and/or short answer questions based on the reading outlined in the class schedule. Quizz Games cannot be made up as you are part of a team. Your team will receive a negative point penalty if you don’t show up, except in the case of illness or family emergency documented by the Health Services, the Counseling Center, or the Dean of Students.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

– Assignments/Projects/Presentations 30% (letter grade)

– Quizz Games 10% (number grade)

– Final Paper 10% (letter grade)

– Written Reading Reflections & Written Reading Discussion Questions 50% - and participation during class discussions related to readings, the ability to formulate a point of view. (10 points each)

Students at mid-term can see me if they want to confirm their grades, absences, and latenesses to date to see if any penalties have occurred. Your final grade will be determined by translating the number grades, points and letter grades for each item into a numerical equivalent based on the QPA scale and applying the percentage it is worth.

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
