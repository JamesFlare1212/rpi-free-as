## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Course Description

This class is an introduction to the field of media studies. It will serve as a ‘sampler plate’ of approaches to media studies for students from all majors, and as a first step/core course for majors and minors in Communication, Media, and Design. As a survey of the historical origins and cultural impact of mass media—including television, film, radio, games, print, the internet, and social media—this course aims to increase your media literacy through analysis of specific media products, and discussion of broad topics such as commercialization, politics and censorship, and representation. As an inquiry class, we will consider the functions media play in shaping our individual and social identities, and we’ll think through how we can take a more engaged role in those processes.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1\) You will develop tools for critical media engagement with media, learning how to analyze, interpret and appreciate the capabilities and functions of different media forms. We’ll explore the distinctive features of various media—their inherent strengths or limitations, and their institutional bases. You should have an understanding of the historical and socioeconomic origins of modern media and their profound impact on contemporary society. A particular emphasis in this version of the class will be on representation in media; here, the concept of ‘representation’ is given a loose interpretation, including but not limited to representation of identities (race, ethnicity, gender, sexuality and so on); we’re trying to understand how our ‘re-presenting’ of reality shapes our understanding of it.

2\) As a complement to these critical tools, working as a community, we will enhance your reflexive media awareness, a term that refers to your processual and dialogic understanding of your relationship to media. In other words, you should gain a deeper understanding of how media shapes us as individuals, as groups and communities, and as cultures.

3\) You will hone your communicative/generative skills by articulating and refining your thoughts in oral, written, and presentational forms, and (if you are so inclined) by creating media works in various formats as a way of engaging with concepts we encounter in this class.You should leave the class able to more fully articulate your experience of media, contextualize it, and empathize with others’ perspectives.

4\) Finally, through the use of certain non-traditional educational strategies, we will engage in meta-examinations of the learning process; the idea here is that the course itself is a ‘medium’ for learning. An underlying theme of this class is your involvement in a collaborative learning process; the class will be shaped through your personal interests and your prior knowledge as a community, as well as by my disciplinary, personal and pedagogical expertise.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

MEDIA AWARENESS ACTIVITIES AND ASSIGNMENTS

\*Character Sheet / A Media Experience Short Essay [20,000] (\*required)

In the first week of class, you will a) create an introductory ‘character sheet’ about yourself, and b) write about a media experience that was very important to you. You will be given a few guidelines on things to think about, but mostly this is meant to be as vivid a recollection of the media experience as you can muster. These activities will help us think through our relationship to media and introduce ourselves.

Media Journal and Reflection [20,000]

By week 4 or week 6 (you have two chances), you’ll create a journal recording all of your media use over a multi-day period, present in written or graphic form, and then reflect on that media use.

‘A Day without Media’ Activity and Reflection [20,000]

By week 10 or week 13 (you have two chances), you’ll read about stories of people who have (for various reasons) engaged in ‘media fasts.’ You will then find a day when you will do your best to abstain from all screens and electronic devices for a day, and then write a reflection on that day (according to guidelines). The object of this exercise is not to convince you to ‘get off your phone’ [that is NOT the point], but to get you to become more aware of your personal connection to media.

Revisiting your ‘A Media Experience’ Short Essay [20,000]

By week 13 or week 16 (you have two chances), you will have the opportunity to revisit the media that you wrote about for your essay in the first week (if applicable), or a similar piece of media, and produce a reflection on that piece of media, considering how you may (or may not) see it differently now.

PARTICIPATION THROUGH NEGOTIATED SELF-ASSESSMENTS

\*Participation - Weeks 1-4, 5-8, 9-12 (3 X 30,000) [90,000] (\*required)

\*Participation Weeks 13-16 & Course Reflection [30,000] (\*required)

You will write four brief self-assessments of your participation using a template; using my gradebook notes, I will be keeping you honest in that self-assessment and give you a medal grade (some discussion or negotiation may occur when necessary). This is also a chance for you to reflect on the class, and give me direct feedback on how the class is going. The self-assessments will be based on the following:

1\) Preparation for Class: This is a measure of how thoroughly you’ve prepared for the classes, including thoughtfulness put into LMS mini-assignments, your participation in online forums, demonstrations of understandings of the reading materials or viewings, and your willingness to research and give evidence for the points you make. For the most part, any item that appears on the semester schedule in all caps as something to ‘READ’ or ‘PREPARE’ or ‘’WATCH’ denotes something that will have an associated mini-assignment falling into this category.

2\) Presence & Professionalism: This includes your full attendance to class, mentally as well as physically, as well as your conscientiousness in communicating with your classmates for group work. This includes various ways you can add ‘contributions’ to the discussion / dialogue / community of the classroom. ‘Contributions’ is left open-ended: Some of you will often offer comments in full-class discussion; some of you will be more active in small break-out groups; some of you will make comments in online forums, or answer with a greater degree of detail than necessary in the mini-assignments (this is where ‘Preparation’ spills over into ‘Presence’); some of you will find other ways to contribute to our community’s examinations of media. Finally, this includes your courtesy/professionalism in corresponding with the professor about potential absences or concerns, and contacting your partners in group work.

Note: The final self-assessment will include a course reflection that asks you to reflect on the final project process and the course itself as a whole—this also serves as a way to provide me feedback that will help me to shape the class in the future.

SOLO ADVENTURES & SIDE QUESTS

Solo Adventures, Side Quests, or Creative Projects

[Up to 80,000 maximum points, at 1,000~20,000 points each, with 9 opportunities]

Beginning in week 4, and continuing through week 12 of the class, you will be given the option of doing solo adventures, side quests, or creative projects that will give you a chance to more deeply delve into media topics that we’ve talked about in class, learn about media concepts that we don’t have time to in class, or critically or creatively apply what you’re learning. You will provide some kind of a report—a written piece, a slides doc, or a link to a ‘product’ in another medium of your choosing—that you will share with the rest of the class through the LMS discussion board. Depending on what your project was and the timing, on Fridays you may be asked to debrief the rest of the class on what you did, or may be able to give the class a presentation on it. The idea behind these is to give you a chance to explore some aspect of media that you are passionate about and have you articulate it for the rest of us, and then to give you ‘credit’ as a part of your learning in the class. Often I’ve found that students will do one of these ‘explorations’ and then find that they are inspired to expand into their final project; this is highly encouraged, and makes for stronger final projects.

I will provide examples below and in class, I will create a folder on the LMS with extra reading materials, and we will create a text channel in the Discord where we can exchange ideas. You are always welcome to direct message me to talk through possibilities. Examples might include:

1\) Applying a chapter’s concepts to a media text of your choosing;

2\) Doing a creative project that explores a chapter’s or an article’s concepts more experientially (like an editing project or mash up inspired by a reading, or a photography project inspired by a discussion);

3\) Reading a substantial critical, historical, theoretical, or economically-based article on media and sharing its main points and significance with the rest of the class;

4\) Watching a media-related documentary and sharing what you learned from it with the class;

5\) Conducting a media-related survey and reporting your findings;

6\) Undertaking a statistical or archival analysis of a body of media, and summarizing those findings.

The possibilities are literally endless. If you come up with something completely different, suggest it to me and I’m sure we can work something out. If you have an idea but aren’t sure how to translate that into a ‘solo adventure,’ talk with your peers or discuss it with me; we can generate or refine ideas together.. Also, keep in mind that we’re coming up to the 2020 election...this could yield some amazing work. NOTE: For the ‘adventures’ and ‘side quests’ assignments, rather than a ‘medal’ grade, you will receive an amount of points that are appropriate for the scope of the project. The more ambitious and engaging the project is (and the more time it likely required of you), the more points it will be worth (up to 20,000).

Tabletop Games or Board Games as Media Activity [20,000]

As an additional opportunity for a ‘side quest’ assignment, in week 13 of the semester (just before and then over Thanksgiving Break), there will be an optional unit on tabletop / board games as media. There has been a vibrant ‘game renaissance’ of both board games and tabletop RPGs (such as Dungeons and Dragons), and this will be a chance for you to explore that in relation to media studies--and may steer you toward RPI games studies classes. You will have some assigned readings and viewings in connection with the subject, we will have a special class where we explore some games together (this may be an in-person or remote class, not sure yet), you will then be asked to play games of your choosing with others over the break, and then you will produce a report on your experiences based on guidelines.

FINAL PROJECTS

\*Final Project Proposal & Group Work [20,000] (\*required)

By the beginning of week 11, you will prepare a proposal for your final project. I will provide a flexible template, and you should be as detailed as you can about your direction; I’ll give you feedback to help shape that project, and hopefully be able to provide you with some resources. Your proposals will help me to determine how to group you into collaborative cohorts for the final project preparation period.

\*Final Project - Individual Aspect [60,000 total] (\*required)

Your culminating final project will be an in-depth engagement with some aspect of media that you are deeply passionate about. I will give you more detailed guidelines for the final project and show you student examples by the midpoint of the semester, but you will generally have three potential approaches:

1\) A theme of “Why not save the world of media?” or “How is media changing the world?”: For this approach, you would be isolating some aspect of media that you feel needs to be challenged or improved, or you would be advocating for an aspect of media that you feel could be encouraged.

2\) A theme of “How am I (or something I care about) portrayed in media?”: For this approach, you would explore and analyze in depth how some aspect of ‘you’ is represented in media; alternatively, you may choose to look at how something that you care about deeply is represented. The notion of representation is fairly broad here, and you have great leeway in what aspect of ‘you’ will be explored.

3\) A collaborative case study around “Critical Binge Watching”: With a partner or in small groups, you will decide on a TV show that you will examine in depth. Each person in the collaboration will take a different analytical perspective on the show, using critical/conceptual lenses we’ve studied in class.

In general, the projects will fit into one of the following three format options:

Option 1: You will write a finely-honed Research Paper or Critical/Rhetorical Essay

Option 2: You will create a brief Essay in a Non-Traditional Format, such as a ‘video essay,’ a ‘videographic essay,’ an elaborate slide show, a graphic design essay, or an essay in another medium.

Option 3: You will engage in a Creative Project, instead of the final paper. This creative project must engage with conceptual/theoretical issues we’ve dealt with in class and will be accompanied by a brief Reflective Essay (2-3 pages).

For all final project options, you must include research sources that you will cite in a proper MLA formatted ‘works cited’ page.

\*Final Project Panel & Presentation [20,000] (\*required)

As a part of the final project process, you will be arranged into 4~6 person cohorts. The cohort members will serve as each others’ sounding boards or collaborators, providing each other feedback during the preparation phase. On the Final Project days, each cohort will have a full class day to: 1) present, describe, or share your projects and 2) engage the class through activities or discussion. You will work as a team to create an experience for us. For these presentations, your project may still be a ‘work in progress’ (particularly for those who are presenting in the first week). In general, except for the ‘Critical Binge Watching’ approach, most students do individual projects…but if you have a particular degree of chemistry and can create a truly collaborative project, that’s great! Please discuss with me this possibility.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

ASSESSMENT: ‘MEDIA POINTS’-BASED ‘GAMEFUL’ GRADING

Introduction:Grading for this class will unfold a little differently than you’re used to. Using what might be called a ‘gameful’ approach, your performance in the class is based on the points that you earn, much like in a game system (a video game, a table-top role-playing game, or what have you). Like in some games, or in many popular films/TV, there’s even a ‘clock’ defined by milestones in the semester; time runs out quickly!

Everyone begins with 0 points. At the start of the semester, there are 400,000+ points to be earned. As you accomplish tasks in the class, you build up points towards your final grade. There are central tasks for everyone to complete (like the readings, daily assignments of the class, and final projects), but there are also some ‘media awareness’ activities (like a media journal and ‘a day without media’), as well as ‘solo adventures’ and ‘side quests’--opportunities to learn according to your interests, to apply knowledge in new ways, or to create your own explorations. Outside of the final project and the basic participation grades, you aren’t required to do any specific task; however, you will need to do some of those if you want to achieve a high ‘rank’ in the class (which translates to a high grade).

Goal of this approach to assessment:

What I’m looking to do here is to get you to consider a different way of thinking about what it means to participate in a class. This is a class about a topic for which there isn’t a single body of knowledge or point of view, so this grading system is meant to encourage active engagement with the material. I want you to be active in your own assessment. The ranks are not traditional grade percentages, but rather levels of achievement in building knowledge of media studies, and in critical and creative thinking. You can think of a grading system as a ‘medium’ for your evaluation/assessment; by using an alternate method, I want to put into relief the nature of the assessment systems you’ve used in the past. I welcome your feedback.

‘Medal’ Based Scoring

Assignments will be assessed using a ‘medal’ system. For certain larger projects, we may establish a more detailed rubric for grading, or reach an understanding through discussion. In many cases, particularly for larger assignments, you may be able to revise an assignment to improve its medal.

Medal Points % Description

Diamond* 120% A sparkling exemplar for the class, full of dazzling insight

Platinum* 110% A perceptive, clever, and resilient exploration of assignment

Gold 100% Weighty, polished, addressing all required aspects of assignment

Silver 90% A response with clarity and strength, but maybe a tiny tarnish

Bronze 80% Relatively solid response; some clarity, some weaknesses

Copper 70% Mostly complete, but may bend under the stress of examination

Nickel 60% Partial or relatively flimsy assignment that needs strengthening

Tin\*\* 50% Dull or lifeless response, crumples in the breeziest of discussions

*To receive a ‘Platinum’ medal requires that you go above and beyond the requirements of the assignment, and the ‘Diamond’ requires even more in-depth work of great quality.

\*\*Below ‘Tin’ means you simply failed to turn in the assignment, or it’s too incomplete to score

NOTE: You can calculate that the total of all the required elements of the class (if you receive a ‘Gold’ medal on all of those elements) is 240,0000--just enough for an ‘Avid Media Fan’ rank (a C+). If you want a higher grade in the class, you will need to do some of the optional assignments, or you will need to go over and above the requirements on the assignments, or some combination of both.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic Integrity and Misconduct:

Educational relationships are built on trust, and acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process; a key component of this trust is that students should not claim the work of others as their own. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty, and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. Submission of any assignment in violation of this policy will result in a grade penalty and disciplinary action. If you ever have any questions about citing sources or are confused about what constitutes a breach of academic or scholarly integrity, please do not hesitate to ask.
