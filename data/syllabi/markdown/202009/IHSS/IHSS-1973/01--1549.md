## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will: 1. analyze, attain, and communicate knowledge of current events as presented in the news media and works of art
- Students will: 2. discuss, write texts, and prepare presentations about current events, the news media, and related work of art.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**REQUIREMENTS:**

**1) Attendance is required.** Three unexcused absences and you fail. (Of course, allowances are made if there is grave illness or something of this nature.)

**2) Contribution to class discussions and short reports based on required readings, assignments, and lectures/presentations. (40% of total grade)**

Discussions will be founded on students weekly reading/listening/viewing of news media sources, additional required readings and screenings, and the course lectures and presentations.

Students will prepare oral reports based on their analysis of news media reading/listening/viewing and required readings and screenings. Readings will be available online or at Rensselaer's Learning Management System (LMS) site: https://lms9.rpi.edu.

**Class participation, notes:** It is expected that students will participate in class and take notes during class, and your attention to this matter may affect your class participation grade. If you have a learning issue where note taking is a distraction, or, for some reason taking notes is a hindrance to your learning, please contact me about this.

**Laptops/Electronic Devices:**The use of laptops, phones, and electronic devices for non-class related activity will result in a lower final grade for the course.

**Summary of class participation guidelines and grading/evaluation**: If you do not contribute to class discuss or regularly use electronic devices for non-class activity, or never take notes: this is an D or F grade for the class participation portion of your grade; very occasional participation or some non-class on line engagement with electronic devices or very occasional note taking is a C-, C, or C+; some participation with contributions to discussion, with some notes, and no online mis-use, is a B-, B, or B+; and full participation with regular contributions to discussions and notes, and no on-line mis-use, an A-, A, or A+.

**3) Four presentations and essays (15% each, which is 60% of total grade).**

Students will give 4 approximately 7 to 10 minute presentations analyzing a contemporary political/social issue and the news media or a contemporary political/social issue and a work of art dealing with these concerns. All topics must be approved by Professor Staniszewski. The presentations will be an opportunity to integrate students’ particular interests into the course.

A 4-page minimum analysis/summary essay, with additional pages of supplementary Chicago Manual of Style “notes-and-bibliography-style” endnotes (not footnotes) and bibliography are required for each presentation. Please note: endnotes are a very specific type of citation method. See sources at the end of the syllabus and the quick guide: https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/tools_citationguide/citation-guide-1.html

The essays should be in Microsoft word, double-spaced with a standard 12 point font, such as New Times Roman or Calibri. Guidelines for each assignment will be given.

It is intended that communication and writing skills will be developed throughout the course with the achievement of improvements and enhancements from the first to the fourth/final presentation/essay.

Checklist: All written assignments must include the course checklist, which is available on LMS.

**Grading/Evaluation of presentations and essay:**

A: Excellent presentations and essays, distinguished by originality of topic, and depth of research. Essays have good grammar, are spell-checked, and formatting requirements are met.

B: Good presentations and essays, with solid research. Essays have good grammar, are spell-checked, and formatting requirements met.

C: Minimal work on presentations and essays. Essays have satisfactory grammar, may have some issues with bibliography, citations, spelling, and formatting requirements.

D: Poor quality work on presentations and essays with the latter having incorrect content, insufficient length, sloppy bibliographies and citations, and incorrect formatting, spelling and grammar.

F: Extremely inadequate work on presentations and essays, or incomplete assignments. Essays with incorrect content, insufficient length, incorrect bibliographies, no citations when necessary, incorrect formatting, spelling and grammar.

Students are not permitted to re-do or re-submit papers or assessments. However, if improvements are made during the semester, there will be a strong emphasis on this improved work when determining the final grade.

4\) Required Materials:

--Instead of textbooks, students are required to order a 3 month subscription to *The New York Times*.

--Students will be also required to order certain films via streaming services for streaming of films during class or as an assignment. Services, such as Amazon, Hulu will be suggested, but you may use whatever service you choose. However, you must be sure to order the exactly correct required film, with the correct director, date, score, version, etc.

**5) Use gender inclusive language in all of your assignments:** For example, please no: “man,” “mankind,” etc. Use “humanity,” “humans,” “humankind,” “people,” etc., unless in a quote from another source.

**6) Email: Please check your email regularly. I will be writing you often about updates on the course, or if there is news or current events related to the course.**

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**ACADEMIC INTEGRITY:**

Academic integrity is described in the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities Handbook (August 21, 2020) as follows:

“Intellectual integrity and credibility are the foundation of all academic work. A violation of Academic Integrity policy is, by definition, considered a flagrant offense to the educational process. It is taken seriously by students, faculty, and Rensselaer and will be addressed in an effective manner.

If found responsible for committing academic dishonesty, a student may be subject to one or both types of penalties: an academic (grade) penalty administered by the professor and/or disciplinary action through the Rensselaer judicial process described in this handbook.” Please see Handbook, page 16, for the entire Academic Integrity statement: https://rpi.app.box.com/s/3f1cl3w1lt1mq8gqla68v6uk3vh1c19f

Among the types of academic dishonesty listed in the Handbook is plagiarism:

“Plagiarism is defined as representing the work or words of another as one's own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference. Examples include using sentences verbatim from a published source in a term paper without appropriate referencing, or presenting as one's own the detailed argument of a published source, or presenting as one's own electronically or digitally enhanced graphic representations from any form of media.” See Handbook, page 16, https://rpi.app.box.com/s/3f1cl3w1lt1mq8gqla68v6uk3vh1c19f

Academic dishonesty, such as cheating or plagiarism will result in a failing grade for the assignment, and can result in a failing grade for this course.

For example, quoting sections of a text without citing the source is plagiarism. All quotes, citations of data, and close paraphrases should be footnoted with a citation to the original source. If you are uncertain about how to properly source material in your assignments for this course, check with the Professor about the proper procedure. Guidelines for proper sources will be reviewed in class and are listed at the end of the syllabus.
