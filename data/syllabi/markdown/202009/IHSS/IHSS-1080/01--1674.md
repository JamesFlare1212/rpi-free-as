## Course Content

### Content Details:

**\*Important Note on Course Content\***  
As this is a history course, please be aware that some of the material we look at will be challenging in many ways, including words and images in some of original sources we will be reviewing that are offensive and dehumanizing, particularly in regard to race and gender. It is important to understand how these words and images were part of the context in which jazz arose, and were daily experiences for the very artists who were struggling against their influence and the embedded power structures which they represented and supported.

Please be sensitive and considerate about how we discuss these issues in class, as this material can provoke powerful feelings and reactions. Feel free to contact the professor directly in regard to the treatment of this material, or with related concerns.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will gain a basic understanding of the arc of jazz history, including its musical predecessors and expanded influence.
- Students will be able to identify major historical figures in the jazz canon and other related forms of improvised music.
- Students will gain an understanding of the role jazz has played in the revitalization of improvisation in American and European music.
- Students will be able to identify by ear different historical periods of jazz and related music.
- Students will gain an appreciation of the social and political condition in which jazz emerged, and the historical roles jazz has had in the struggle for civil rights and racial equality.
- Students will gain an understanding of several important current trends of improvised music.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Music Journals**: Due every other week. These assignments are designed to create a platform for the development of critical listening skills, for engagement with the social and political conditions surrounding music engagement and production, self-reflection in relationship to the material covered, and clarity and concision in writing skills.  
**Quizzes:** Every other week, alternate weeks from the Journal. Quizzes cover material from the previous two weeks, and are designed to aid students in identification of musical era and genre by ear, identification of major historical figures in jazz and related music, identifying surrounding social and political conditions related to jazz and improvised music, the arc of jazz history. Quizzes are delivered online, to be completed within 25 minutes, available during the 2nd week of material covered and to be completed by Friday at 11:59 pm. Students may retake the quiz up to 3 times during the week it is available, with the highest score counted toward their grade.  
**Mid-term paper:** A 1500 word paper submitted in MLA format. This assignment is designed to develop critical listening and research skills, develop the understanding of jazz history and important historical figures in their relationship to the social and political conditions of their era.

**Final paper:** A 1500 word paper due Oct. 30 submitted in MLA format (date may change due to course progress considerations). This assignment is designed to develop critical listening and research skills, develop the understanding of jazz and improvised music developments in the post-Bebop era, identify important current and historical figures in their relationship to the social and political conditions of their era, and gain an in-depth understanding of recent trends in jazz and improvised music.
