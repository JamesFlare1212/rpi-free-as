## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course explores the historical development of modern international human rights through the

lens of American foreign policy. Through lecture and discussion of the assigned readings, this course will guide students in critical analysis of the narratives and national myths surrounding human rights in the history of the United States and the world. Major themes include the origins of human rights, human rights abuses, the human rights regime, human rights institutions, and problems of enforcement.

Students will have the opportunity to learn about the history of human rights through their reading and analysis of primary historical sources including many foundational human rights documents. In addition to the assigned readings, students will have the opportunity to embark upon their own research project, identifying a theme, issue, or subject in the history of human rights that they wish to further explore.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will learn the major themes, events, ideas, and figures in the history of human rights
- Students will learn to analyze original primary historical sources
- Students will learn to conduct original primary source-based research to answer historical questions
- Students will learn to formulate historical questions and compose original research papers that answer them
- Students will learn to constructively edit and comment on their peer’s writing

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Participation is mandatory and counts for 25 percent of your final grade. Participation includes in-class discussion of the readings and course material, individual meetings with the professor, and a short presentation of the research paper on the last day of class. Students are permitted two absences before their participation grade is affected. More than two absences will negatively impact the participation grade.

Over the course of the semester students will research and compose an original research paper on a topic of their choosing in the history of human rights in the United States and the world. In preparation for the final paper, students will submit a primary source analysis, project proposal, and rough draft.

The final paper should be 8 to 10 pages in length.

Students will be required to meet with the instructor individually twice during the semester to receive feedback on their project proposal and first draft. These meetings are intended to give students feedback on their drafts in order to help them improve their final papers. Students will also be given time in class to review a peer’s written work and receive feedback on their own written work.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Intellectual integrity and credibility are the foundation of all academic work. A violation of Academic Integrity policy is, by definition, considered a flagrant offense to the educational process. It is taken seriously by students, faculty, and Rensselaer and will be addressed in an effective manner.

Academic dishonesty is a violation of the Grounds for Disciplinary Action as described in the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities. A student may be subject to any of the following types of disciplinary action should disciplinary action be pursued by the professor: disciplinary warning, disciplinary probation, disciplinary suspension, expulsion and/or alternative actions as agreed on by the student and hearing officer.

Violations of academic integrity include, but are not limited to: academic fraud, collaboration, copying, cribbing, fabrication, plagiarism, sabotage, and substitution. You are responsible for knowing what actions constitute violations of academic integrity. *At a minimum, any student caught violating Rensselaer’s policy on Academic Integrity will receive no credit for the assignment in question and the violation will be reported to the Office of the Dean of Students. The professor may also elect to pursue disciplinary action.* Please see the [Rensselaer Student Handbook](http://doso.rpi.edu/update.do) for further explanation of the university’s policy on academic integrity.
