## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will: 1. analyze and attain knowledge of issues of race in relation to U.S history as represented in US films and in relation to the creation and history of U.S. films.
- Students will: 2. write clearly and succinctly.
- Students will: 3. prepare and communicate effectively in class discussions and in presentations.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**REQUIREMENTS:**

**1) Attendance is required.** Three unexcused absences and you fail. (Of course, allowances are made if there is grave illness or something of this nature.)

**2)** **Contribution to class discussions based on required readings, assignments, and lectures/presentations.****(8%)**

Discussions will be founded on the lectures/presentations, required readings, assignments, and responses to films screened in class. Readings with an "*" indicate that there is a thesis or key ideas presented in this text (as opposed to an emphasis on factual knowledge, definitions, dates, etc.), which you will be expected to learn and address in your assignments and discussions. Readings will be available on line or at Rensselaer's Learning Management System (LMS) site: https://lms9.rpi.edu: 8443

When possible, online links to films screened in the course will be included in syllabus.

**Class participation, notes:** It is expected that students will participate in class and take notes during class, and your attention to this matter may affect your class participation grade. If you have a learning issue where note taking is a distraction, or, for some reason taking notes is a hindrance to your learning, please contact me about this.

**Laptops/Electronic Devices:**The use of laptops, phones, and electronic devices for non-class related activity will result in a lower final grade for the course.

**Summary of class participation guidelines**: If you do not contribute to class discuss or go online regularly using electronic devices for non-class activity, or never take notes: this is an D or F grade for the class participation portion of your grade; very occasional participation or some non-class on line engagement with electronic devices or very occasional note taking is a C-, C, or C+; some participation with contributions to discussion, with some notes, and no online mis-use, is a B-, B, or B+; and full participation with regular contributions to discussions and notes, and no on-line mis-use, an A-, A, or A+. (10% of final grade)

**3) A quiz/short take home essay exam based on introductory classes dealing with the foundational framework and ideas of the course (10% of final grade):** modernity, the invention of film, and "the invention" of race.

**4) Student presentations and summary essay (17% each).** Students will give two 5 to 7 minute presentations dealing with a film, or a film or race topic (one at mid-term, one at the end of the semester). For each presentation, a summary-essay discussing the thesis and key ideas of the presentation is also due. The presentations and summaries are an opportunity to integrate students’ particular race and film interests into the course.

The summary essays should be a minimum of 2 pages, with additional pages of supplementary Chicago Manual of Style “notes-and-bibliography-style” endnotes (not footnotes) and bibliography are required for each presentation. Please note: endnotes are a very specific type of citation method. See sources at the end of the syllabus and the quick guide: https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/tools_citationguide/citation-guide-1.html

The essays should be in Microsoft word, double-spaced with a standard 12 point font, such as New Times Roman or Calibri. Guidelines for each assignment will be given.

Checklist: All written assignments must include the course checklist, which is available on LMS.

**5) Midterm and final exams (24% each):**

There will be a midterm and a final exam based on the topics, films, and required readings of the course. The final exam will be cumulative. These exams will be structured as “take home” essays.

The exam essays should be a minimum of 6 pages, with additional pages of supplementary Chicago Manual of Style “notes-and-bibliography-style” endnotes (not footnotes) and bibliography are required for each presentation.

Please note: endnotes are a very specific type of citation method. See sources at the end of the syllabus and the quick guide: https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/tools_citationguide/citation-guide-1.html

The essays should be in Microsoft word, double-spaced with a standard 12 point font, such as New Times Roman or Calibri.

**6) Required Materials:**

--Most the course materials will be required readings or videos and films that will be available on LMS, or will be accessible on line. Instead of textbooks, student will be required to order certain films that are not “free” and available online via streaming services for streaming of films during class or as an assignment. Services, such as Amazon, Hulu will be suggested, but you may use whatever service you choose. However, you must be sure to order the exactly correct required film, with the correct director, date, score, version, etc.

**7) Use gender inclusive language in all of your assignments:** For example, please no: “man,” “mankind,” etc. Use “humanity,” “humans,” “humankind,” “people,” etc., unless in a quote from another source.

**8) Email: Please check your email regularly. I will be writing you often about updates on the course, or if there is news or current events related to the course.**

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**ACADEMIC INTEGRITY:**

Academic integrity is described in the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities Handbook (August 21, 2020) as follows:

“Intellectual integrity and credibility are the foundation of all academic work. A violation of Academic Integrity policy is, by definition, considered a flagrant offense to the educational process. It is taken seriously by students, faculty, and Rensselaer and will be addressed in an effective manner.

If found responsible for committing academic dishonesty, a student may be subject to one or both types of penalties: an academic (grade) penalty administered by the professor and/or disciplinary action through the Rensselaer judicial process described in this handbook.” Please see Handbook, page 16, for the entire Academic Integrity statement: https://rpi.app.box.com/s/3f1cl3w1lt1mq8gqla68v6uk3vh1c19f

Among the types of academic dishonesty listed in the Handbook is plagiarism:

“Plagiarism is defined as representing the work or words of another as one's own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference. Examples include using sentences verbatim from a published source in a term paper without appropriate referencing, or presenting as one's own the detailed argument of a published source, or presenting as one's own electronically or digitally enhanced graphic representations from any form of media.” See Handbook, page 16, https://rpi.app.box.com/s/3f1cl3w1lt1mq8gqla68v6uk3vh1c19f

Academic dishonesty, such as cheating or plagiarism will result in a failing grade for the assignment, and can result in a failing grade for this course.

For example, quoting sections of a text without citing the source is plagiarism. All quotes, citations of data, and close paraphrases should be footnoted with a citation to the original source. If you are uncertain about how to properly source material in your assignments for this course, check with the Professor about the proper procedure. Guidelines for proper sources will be reviewed in class and are listed at the end of the syllabus.
