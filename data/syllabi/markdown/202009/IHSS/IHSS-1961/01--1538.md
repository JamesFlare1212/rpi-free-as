## Course Goals

### Goals:

- \- Understand climate justice theories and their practices, and how it is different than mainstream environmental movements
- \- Analyze and use strategies for equity in the face of climate change
- \- Learn about various design practices and understand how design enables a different set of knowledges
- \- Be able to use various forms of design and media-making to create new knowledge about climate justice

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1\. Project 1: Designing and Communicating Justice  
This project will be a creative endeavor that might be one of these: annotated slide show, annotated infographic, critical soundscape, short video, or a survival manual. Other ideas will be considered. You will turn in your creative endeavor along with a two-page critical analysis. Details will be given in class.  
Note: No prior knowledge of software/tech is required.  
1\. 2. Project 2: Living Lexicon  
What is a living lexicon, and why is it important? We will begin (with an in-class workshop) to create our own living lexicon for climate justice, and you will write short essays on 3-5 keywords each. In0-clas workshops and feedback o drafts will be part of the assignments.  
Details will be provided in class and on the LMS.  
1\. 3. Project 3: Student-Led Perspectives  
During the first couple of weeks of the semester, students will pick a theme, concept, object or system related to course topics for the weeks March 20-April 14. This can be done alone, or as a small group (must gain prior faculty approval for groups). You will provide research/discussion questions, and/or do an activity or demonstration that  
explores the topic. Discussions with faculty will be are required for developing your ideas and choosing class readings. You will present during one of the assigned classes and will turn in your presentation  
files and a two-page analysis, with citations, one week after your presentation.  
1\. 4. Project 4: Designing Worlds  
For this project you will imagine a different future, or perhaps design a social/technical system. To do so, you might create a five-minute podcast or video, create a 7-10 page survival manual or comic book, design a product catalog or website, a board game, a zine or magazine, or something else of your choosing. Please meet with faculty no later than DATE if you have an idea that is not listed here.  
1\. 5. Assignment: Weekly Reflections  
You will write weekly reflections based primarily upon the readings. The expectation is for you to engage deeply with the ideas or themes in at least one of the readings. You should make connections to class discussions and workshops as well as to your own projects and research interests. As part of making these connections, you may find that methods apart from writing are more useful. You might include annotated sketches, create and annotate media, embed memes, video, or audio (make sure to cite!), or even write poetry or lyrics. The purpose is for you to synthesize and analyze the key themes and questions that we are engaging with on a weekly basis. These will ground your projects and help you formulate your research questions. You will turn these in via the LMS on a weekly basis. Further details are provided there.  
1\. 6. Assignment: Engineering the Anthropocene  
Due: your response is due 1 week after you attend the talk  
Attend an “Engineering the Anthropocene” talk and provide a response to the main ideas in relationship to what w been learning in this course. You should include the name of the speaker, the date of the talk, and the title of the tal response. You might write a 3 page paper, or you might thoroughly design an object or system (and annotate & anal response to what you have learned. You may also make some form of media. Please see faculty for working through ideas. If you cannot attend any of them (they are on Wednesdays), please let me know by DATE and we will create different assignment for you to fulfill for this grade.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
