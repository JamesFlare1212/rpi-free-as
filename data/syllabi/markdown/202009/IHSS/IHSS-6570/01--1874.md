## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This seminar is an investigation of creative, theoretical, and historical research methods in the interdisciplinary arts, humanities and social sciences. Students will be introduced to traditional and practice-based approaches emphasizing qualitative research, and will gain competence in writing and research techniques, conventions, and methods. This course is open to graduate students across HASS, Architecture and ITWS, and is a requirement for all Arts Department doctoral students.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Booth, Wayne C., et.al. 2016. *The craft of research*. Fourth edition. Chicago: The University of Chicago Press. Chapters are provided by section on LMS. Additional readings are also provided in electronic form, and available on LMS.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Gain knowledge of interdisciplinary research methods, primarily in the creative arts, humanities, and social sciences.
- • Demonstrate a capacity to apply the range of research methods studied to personal research and artistic practices.
- • Acquire in-depth knowledge of the form and other technical components of the doctoral dissertation project.
- • Develop a scholarly agenda that integrates creative practice and/or fieldwork

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- Initial Research Description with Research Questions and Keywords **(15%)** due and presented orally Sept 29. 1000-1500 words. Include research questions and 5 well defined keywords. Outline intellectual interests and the *initial* shape and direction of your scholarship and practice. Specify how and to what extent practice is likely to inform your scholarly production.
- Preliminary Literature Review (**20%)**. 1000-1500 words due on Nov. 3.
- Research paper arising out of a question from your own research **(30%).**2500 words. 200 word Abstract/Proposal is due November 10. To be presented orally in the final class and submitted Dec 8.
- Short methods exercises (including CITI certification) as assigned on a weekly basis **20%.**
- Participation and oral presentations **15%**

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity.** Collaboration between students in this course is strongly encouraged. Likewise, students are encouraged—indeed, to some extent required—to exchange ideas, opinions, and information constantly. You are encouraged to help each other with performance, production, and presentation of projects.

Plagiarism of any kind is in direct violation of the University Policy on Academic Dishonesty as defined in the *Rensselaer Handbook*, and penalties for plagiarism can be severe. In this class you will be expected to attribute due credit to the originator of any ideas, words, sounds, or work which you incorporate substantially into your own work. This applies particularly to citation of sources for quotes and ideas included in your compositions. Copying and pasting from published sources or the internet is considered plagiarism and is not acceptable. Plagiarized work will receive an automatic grade of zero for the assignment in addition to other possible penalties. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty. If found in violation of the academic honesty policy, students may be subject to two types of penalty. The instructor administers an academic [grade] penalty and the student is reported to the Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education as appropriate. The first violation results in 0 grade for that assignment. The second violation results in failure of the course. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
