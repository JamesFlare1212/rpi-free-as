## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Course Goals / Objectives** This is an interactive readings and seminar course in which we will discuss current topics in astrobiology and the origins of life through the view of earth sciences, chemistry, biology, and astrophysics. Each week, the entire class will discuss two different articles from the scientific literature, presented by 2 different students. Presentations should highlight the goals and underlying questions of the paper, the overall scientific approach, and the main takeaways. The presentation should also lead off a class-wide discussion of the article and how the work relates to our overall understanding of astrobiology and the origins of life.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Critically read and evaluate scientific literature from multiple disciplines
- Effectively communicate and discuss scientific experiments and outcomes
- Recognize gaps in our current understanding of Astrobiology and the origins of life

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

This course is designed to be a discussion and readings course in which students develop the skills to read, understand, present and discuss the scientific literature. As such, student participation is a critical part of achieving these outcomes. Students are expected to read the articles prior to each class meeting, and come prepared to discuss the material. Therefore, active participation and attendance comprise more than half of the student’s final grade in the course. • Participation: In-class discussion/involvement (25%), Pre-discussion assignment (15%) • Presentations (45%) • Attendance (15%)

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance Policy** Attendance is a graded component of this course and, as such, is required. Students can miss one (1) class period without affecting their grade. Attendance will be assessed by presence in the WebEx meeting, and recorded in the LMS gradebook.

### Other Course Policies:

**WebEx Participation** Class meetings will be conducted synchronously during the listed class time (Mondays 4:45-6:05) using WebEx. Instructions and links to the WebEx sessions will be posted on the Blackboard/LMS course page. All students should install the WebEx Teams application on their devices, as this improves platform performance and enables the full suite of platform options. Students should be prepared to share their screens and keep cameras active during the course meeting. Discussion will occur both in the chat window and via live conversation, and will be monitored as part of the participation grade.  
Class meetings will be recorded in WebEx and posted on LMS following the course meeting. Watching the class meeting recording after the fact does not substitute for attendance (see attendance policy below).  
Please note that we encourage active discussion in the online forums we will be using this semester. It is the responsibility of each student to ensure that this discussion remains on point to the topics of the course and that interactions remain respectful and open.  
**Zotero Group** Articles will be shared with the class using a dedicated group library in Zotero reference manager. To be able to access this library, you must setup an account at Zotero.org using your RPI email address. Once you have an account, the Instructors will add you to the group.  
**Presentations** Each student will be required to give \~2 presentations during the semester on scientific journal articles. Students will be assigned presentation dates and will be able to choose between a few articles selected by the Instructors. Articles will be available \~2 weeks before the presentation date. The content, including experimental design, results, and implications of the results, should be presented to the class via WebEx in shared-screen mode. The presentation of the article and its findings should not exceed 20 minutes to allow ample time for group discussion. Note that the presentation can either be a formal Power Point presentation or a “chalk talk” using a tablet to annotate figures from the article. Showing the pdf of the article will not suffice.  
The presentation should not only cover the goals/questions, approach and major conclusion, but should also lead off the class-wide discussion of the article. Presenters should be mindful of leading the class-wide discussion following their presentation.  
Grades for each student presentation and class-wide discussion will be assigned by the instructors and posted in the LMS in a timely fashion.  
**In-class discussion** Everyone in the class is expected to read the week’s articles and to participate in the discussions. Student’s involvement will be evaluated by the instructors each week and posted on the LMS. Note that discussion participation must be meaningful to count toward your grade, and that vague or uninformed comments (e.g. “I didn’t understand the paper”) will not suffice.  
**Pre-discussion assignments** Prior to each class meeting, every student is required to turn in a prediscussion assignment into the LMS based on the journal articles that will be discussed in class. This assignment will consist of the following for each article

- The important findings from the study
- The implications of this study in the context of astrobiology/the origins of life
- Scientific questions that arose from reading each article

These assignments will be submitted through the LMS and will be due by 11:59 pm on the Sunday prior to class.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

This course will utilize a zero-tolerance policy for any form of cheating or other academic misconduct. All submitted assignments will be subjected to copying and plagiarism checkers. University policy deals with the problem of academic dishonesty, cheating, and plagiarism. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. No violation will be tolerated in this class. The sanctions provided in this policy will be used to deal with any violations.  
Academic dishonesty includes, but is not limited to, copying material from another student or use of written notes during an exam, representing others’ work as your own, changing answers on an exam paper after the paper has been graded, etc. Any student caught cheating will be assigned an F for the course. Furthermore, violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education for undergraduate and graduate students, respectively).  
**A special note on plagiarism.** Please see the definition of plagiarism given below, as outlined in The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights. To be clear, any time material that is not the students’ own is used, it should be in quotes and contain a citation. If a student paraphrases work from elsewhere, it also requires a citation. Furthermore, even when citations are used, others’ work cannot be used as a student response on an assignment. For example, in this class, if the main conclusions are given in an article, they cannot be used verbatim as a response in the postdiscussion evaluation, even if a citation is present.  
“***Plagiarism** Representing the work or words of another as one’s own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference. For example, using sentences verbatim from a published source in a term paper without appropriate referencing, or presenting as one’s own the detailed argument of a published source, or presenting as one’s own electronically or digitally enhanced graphic representations from any form of media.” - ACADEMIC INTEGRITY (2014-2016 Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights & Responsibilities, August 2014)*
