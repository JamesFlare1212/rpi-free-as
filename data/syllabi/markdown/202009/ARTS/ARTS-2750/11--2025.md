1. **Course Description**

Private applied music lessons are offered to provide a fine arts experience for credit.

1. **Course Expectations**

\- Regular attendance at all scheduled lessons (50 min. x 15/semester or, if needed, equivalent in total minutes over the course of the semester).

\- 30 min. daily practice (minimum).

\- Satisfactory progress on repertoire studied, as determined by private instructor.

**III. Required Course Materials**

\- Student-owned harp; or if not possible, student will need to rent a harp (either lever of pedal, depending on preference and budget)

\- Suitable cover(s) for the harp (harp should be kept covered when not in use)

\- Harp cart/dolly (required for pedal harp unless one is provided by the college or instructor; optional for lever harp)

\- Electronic digital chromatic tuner (or suitable phone/latptop/tablet app)

\- Tuning pickup wire (optional but can be helpful)

\- Harp tuning key (will be provided if it’s a rental harp)

\- Metronome (or suitable phone/laptop/tablet app)

\- Spiral notebook for keeping personal lesson notes (if lessons are online, the instructor’s notes will also be emailed after each lesson).

\- Good pencil and eraser for marking music and taking notes

\- Texts and repertoire determined by instructor

**- ONLINE LESSONS ONLY:** Suitable device for using Skype, Facebook Messenger, or Zoom for online lessons – make sure it’s fully charged prior to lesson and that you’re in a strong wifi location.

1. **Grading Components**

\- Admission to the private lesson program may be by audition, depending upon demand.

\- Private instructors will issue 3 progress reports through semester (week 5, week 10, week 15) in the process of determining musical progress and semester grade.

1. **Academic Integrity**

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.

1. **Learning Outcomes**

Students in the private lesson program will improve their ability to read, discuss, interpret, and perform a plethora of music composed for their individual instrument.

Students will be able to expressively and musically interpret a variety of musical works/styles/genres, within their current playing ability.

Students will further mastery of their chosen instrument.

Students will improve listening skills, as well as mature in their ability to work at once independently.

### Prerequisites or Other Requirements:

Student must have a harp on which to practice and take lessons. Please contact the instructor for more information about how to obtain a rental harp.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Beginners will need to buy beginner books and have a way to take notes. An electronic tuner and a metronome will also be needed.  
Students who already play the harp will need to supply their own music, plus a copy for the instructor if the instructor doesn't own a copy. Or, we will work together to find repertoire depending on each student's situation.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

For students who already play the harp: To further your technical skills and work on repertoire that both interests you and helps you continue developing your playing skills.  
For new students: Most new students just want an introduction to playing the harp. We use beginner books to help in this process. If you find you enjoy the harp and want to continue, we'll continue using the beginner books in subsequent semester and possibly adding other repertoire that you enjoy along the way, as appropriate for your playing level at the time.

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
