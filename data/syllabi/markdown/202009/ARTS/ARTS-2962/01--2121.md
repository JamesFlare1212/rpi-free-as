### Other LMS Information:

This topics based course examines popular music in society, considering the ways in which it may express identities, motivate political movements (Black Lives Matter, #MeToo, and Idle No More) and function within various economic, mediated, and technological environments. This course explores popular music representing various genres (country, hip hop, EDM, Top 40 pop) as it relates to social life, the intersections of race, gender, class, and sexuality, and media and technology.

In the fall 2020 semester we also will use our study of popular music to engage with our current moment by exploring these topics in popular music in relation to digital culture. How do we listen to the sounds, music, and messages that constantly scroll and pop-up on the many mobile devices and screens using headphones, earbuds, and device speakers in our daily lives? What are the different formats where we listen to and produce popular music in digital culture (online performances, viral videos, visual albums, social media platforms, games, sample-based practices) and how do we listen to them?

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand and be able to evaluate the sociocultural, political, economic, and aesthetic function of popular music in everyday life.
- Develop writing and rhetorical skills through classroom exercises, media analysis, projects.
- Explore the emergence of popular music and their historical context and cultural implications, both within and beyond Western culture
- Interrogate, explore, and reevaluate what popular music means when it is experienced in a variety of contexts, including, for example, in the home, along city streets, over radio waves and streaming services, and elsewhere
- Critically engage with the diverse ways in which society listens to, creates, and circulates popular music with different technologies on and offline

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

homework  
papers  
small in-class group projects  
discussion & debate  
writing to learn/listening journals  
short written responses and listening analysis

Course objectives include providing students with significant contact with diverse perspectives on and examples of popular music cultures; helping students recognize and appreciate the music of diverse peoples, their fandom, and their music; and introducing students to interdisciplinary scholarship from popular music studies.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty. In the case of major acts of plagiarism (e.g., text copied verbatim from an unidentified source) the student will receive a grade of F for the course. In the case of minor acts of plagiarism (e.g., referencing the findings of others without appropriate citations) the student will fail the assignment and receive a reduction of final course grade by one letter grade. If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
