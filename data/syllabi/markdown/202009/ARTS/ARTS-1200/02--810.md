## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Basic Drawing is an introductory course in free-hand drawing, where practicing and developing an appropriate set of mark-making skills is utilized in the service of producing a believable image. The primary emphasis of this course is the close observation and subsequent understanding of the structure of forms, the physical relationship they have to one another, and to the space they inhabit relative to the viewer.

Studio work is performed from direct observation. Projects involve still life, live model, architectural and landscape sources. The course is a “hands-on” studio experience, supported by studies in the history of art, using examples from the Masters and contemporary artists.

Critical abilities are developed through group discussions and critiques. Homework assignments are given on a regular basis and related directly to the production of work done in the studio for graded projects.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Through regular and consistent drawing practice the student will learn to translate the observation of their immediate surroundings into a lively, interesting, and accurately representational image on a 2-dimensional surface through skilled mark making. Through the term, the student will be presented with and be expected to understand the concepts and vocabulary necessary to successfully communicate an idea through visual means.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The following is a week-by-week guide to topics and themes to be taught in this course:

**Weeks 1-3** Line Quality, Measuring, Perspective Systems, Diagrammatic Line, Space-mapping, Form and Structural Development, Organic Forms, Ovals and Ellipses.

# Introduction

- Discussion of class procedures, materials list, syllabus.

# Line Quality

- WIDTH of line – thick – thin; VALUE of line – light or dark; TEXTURE of line – rough or smooth; FOCUS of line – soft or sharp; SPEED of line – fast or slow

# Line Sensitivity

- Methods for developing line variations, straight-line construction, development of “process” or “adjustment” lines, searching line, identifying different types of edges and an appropriate use of dimensional line to describe these edges.

# Introduction to Sighting

- Sighting for relative proportions, sighting for angles in relation to verticals and horizontals, sighting for plumb lines or alignments between landmarks.
- Significance of establishing a point of reference or unit of measure. Regular (mechanical) and irregular (natural) forms.

# Introduction to Perspective

- Perspective terminology, establishing a perspective environment.
- Introduction to ellipses, geometric solids, inclined planes, scaling methods.
- Varying eye level and foreshortening.

**Weeks 4-10** Light, value, atmosphere, surface and form description.

# Line to Shape to form

- Reinforced line, planar analysis.
- Contour and cross-contour line with variation,
- Parallel and hatching lines.
- Modeled line, drapery

# Introduction to Value/Tonal Structure

- Divisions of light and dark, what to look for in light and shadow. The process of value reduction/addition. Working from general to specific.
- Demonstration of directional light and its impact on form definition.

# Introduction to Composition

- Positive/Negative space relationships, beginning your drawing with a delicate gesture drawing.
- Partial page (thumbnail) composition studies in line and limited value, full page composition studies in line and limited value.

**Weeks 11-16** Complex figure forms, foreshortening, figure in space, architecture and landscape.

# Introduction to the Figure and the Land

- Correct proportioning of the figure
- Figure as structure and mass
- The figure illuminated (value)
- The figure in space, interior and exterior (foreshortening)
- Study of the head
- Landscape and nature
- Architectural forms and the figure in the landscape

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

All grades are based on the following:

- Attendance
- Quality of work produced
- Presentation of finished work
- Timely completion of projects
- Understanding of concepts discussed CLEARLY demonstrated in work
- Interest and participation in discussions and critiques
- Serious and lively approach to work
- Motivated problem solving both in class and outside of class.

**Attendance (either in class or in online video meetings), participation in critiques, and presentation of final portfolio is required.** Regular attendance is mandatory, unless special circumstances have been presented and discussed at the beginning of the term (such as an inability to attend online meetings live because of time-zone differences or a lack of adequate data-streaming services). Under most circumstances, **regular absences which affect the quality of the learning experience and the work produced will negatively affect the final grade.**

Lecture and demonstration times are scheduled at the beginning of class, so arriving for studio or being present online promptly at the beginning of the scheduled class-time is critical. In case of late arrival, be prepared to gather any information you may have missed from your classmates, as lectures are not given at the individual's convenience. Excessive lateness will negatively affect the final grade.

All students enrolled in the course, regardless of their working location or situation, will be required to attend that day’s live WEBEX session. If this is not possible, that student is required to check in with the professor on that day to make sure they have received any pertinent information, and to review the recorded video of the lectures and demonstrations given.

**Being unprepared to work during scheduled class times, either in the studio or at home, and without the necessary tools is the same as being absent, and will be recorded as such.**

Course evaluation is made at the end of the semester through a portfolio review. The portfolio consists of all work done in and out of class during the semester. **Do not discard any of your work**. A portfolio review can be arranged at any point during the semester. Submission of portfolio will take place during the last day of class. **Failure to submit a complete portfolio will result in failure of the course.**

Students’ final grades are based upon the completion of the final portfolio at the end of the term. However, ongoing assessments for individual projects and current progress and standing can be discussed at any time upon request.
