## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

“Cross-Cultural Soundscapes and Sustainable Music Cultures” is designed to develop listening, critical thinking, research and writing skills through selected cross-cultural topics, the soundscapes and communities of world music practices, and themes exploring the relationships among music, culture, and society. The course will synthesize musical communities, traditions, historical facts, cultural trends, and address some of the major themes in global music making and sound practices and integrate diverse perspectives. Also, as a one-semester course, this course will highlight important details regarding global musics and their function in society in the past and present. The emphasis is on critical thinking, synthesis of information, and the ability to think *through* global music making practices, bringing both musical materials and sociocultural ideas into play. Topics addressed include: music and ritual, participatory music practices; music and urban life; music and migration; gender performance and representation; music, dance, and gesture; music, circulation, and mobility; music and communications theory; music and sports culture; issues of place; spectacle and virtuosity; music and new media; recording studio culture; music and identity; collaborations among the visual and performing arts; music and conflict; sonic memory; the global marketplace and consumer culture; intercultural exchange; and the sound cultures of social movements, among others.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Have significant contact with diverse perspectives on and examples of global music cultures
- Become acquainted with major music culture areas of the world
- Develop an understanding of the ecology of music cultures and the factors that impact cultural sustainability
- Recognize and appreciate the music of diverse peoples and their instruments of music
- Survey and engage with scholarship and recordings of traditional and modern music from around the world
- Develop analytic and auditory skills across music genres, global music cultures, and audiovisual media
- Become sensitive and informed listeners and will be able to articulate their listening experiences
- Gain an appreciation of the incredible diversity of contemporary musical practices and traditions and will be able to situate them within a broader historical and cultural context

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty. In the case of major acts of plagiarism (e.g., text copied verbatim from an unidentified source) the student will receive a grade of F for the course. In the case of minor acts of plagiarism (e.g., referencing the findings of others without appropriate citations) the student will fail the assignment and receive a reduction of final course grade by one letter grade. If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
