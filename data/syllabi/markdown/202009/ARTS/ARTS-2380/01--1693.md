## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is the first part of a two-semester sequence aimed at cultivating theory, analysis, musicianship, and ear training skills that will enhance students’ ability to understand and create music in a diverse range of styles and practices. By considering forms and techniques from the Western “common practice” tradition, African-American musical practices, and non-Western musics, this class will provide students with a baseline of international and contemporary musical literacy and basic musicianship skills. Music theory topics include melodic and contrapuntal writing, chord progressions, voice leading, embellishment, and functional diatonic harmony. Musicianship topics include sight-singing, aural identification, and rhythm exercises. Students will also engage in expanded listening exercises, helping them to develop and articulate their own approaches and responses to a variety of musical and sonic experiences drawn from current music and sound explorations and practices.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to analyze and compose multiphrase melodies and harmonic progressions.
- Students will be able to understand a variety of harmonic notational practices, including roman numerals, figured bass, and jazz chord symbols.
- Students will be able to write two-part species counterpoint.
- Students will be able to do basic formal analysis from a score and by ear.
- Students will gain a fluency in foundational musicianship skills, including solo and ensemble singing, rhythmic performance, melodic dictation, and harmonic dictation.
- Students will develop a fluency in critical listening skills and a vocabulary for articulating what they hear.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Class Participation:** This is a hands-on, experiential course. Therefore, students are expected to participate in all class activities, including discussions, singing, rhythm exercises, improvisation, and other forms of in-class interaction. Students will be graded on effort, integrity of interaction, and improvement over the course of the semester.

**In-Class Workshops:** Throughout the term, we will do in-class workshops to apply the theoretical principles we have covered and to explore different ways of interacting with musical and non-musical sound. These will be graded on engagement, open-mindedness, and written work (when applicable).

**Final Project:** The semester’s work will culminate in a final project which ties together the material covered throughout the semester. The project will be graded on grasp of the material, clarity of presentation, and critical understanding.

**Written Work:** Written work will demonstrate students’ grasp of analytical concepts and the application of theoretical principles. It is graded on grasp of the relevant theoretical concepts.

**Creative Work:** Creative work provides students the opportunity to apply theoretical concepts and skills to their own creative practice. Depending on the unit, students are invited to strike a balance between the limitations of a given historical technique and their own creative intuitions.

**Performance Work:** Performance activities are assigned for each unit. It is understood that everyone in the class comes to performance with their own distinctive history and level of experience. Performance work will be graded according to effort and improvement throughout the semester.

**Ear-Training Work:** Ear-Training work allows students to relate a sonic perceptual experience to a conceptual understanding. As with performance, students will come to class with different levels of experience and different levels of inherent aural sensitivity. The work will be graded according to effort and improvement throughout the semester.
