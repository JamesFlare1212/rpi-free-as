## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The second part of a two-semester sequence aimed at cultivating a wide swathe of music theory, analysis, musicianship, and listening skills that will enhance students’ performance and the creation of music. Combining traditional and contemporary approaches, the course focuses on the basic parameters of the western "common practice" tradition (broadly defined) through the study of chromatic harmony to provide students with a unified baseline of international music literacy and basic musicianship skills. Music theory topics include applied chords, modulation, sequences, mode mixture, and form. Musicianship topics include sight-singing, aural identification, and rhythm exercises. Students will also engage in expanded listening exercises, helping them to develop and articulate their own approaches and responses to a variety of musical and sonic experiences drawn from current music and sound explorations and practices.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to harmonize a modulating melody or baseline.
- Students will be able to do basic musical analysis from a given score and by ear.
- Students will gain a fluency in fundamental musicianship skills, such as sight singing chromatic and modulating melodies, and aural identification of intervals, scales, and chords, chord progressions, rhythm, and thematic relationships.
- Students will hone their abilities to create original written musical scores.
- Students will gain a fluency in critical listening skills, and develop a vocabulary for articulating what they hear outside of any specific musical tradition.
- Students will gain a fluency with fundamentals Western tonal musical practices with a focus on the interrelationship between harmony and form.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Written Homework:**  
Written homework assignments address the music theory material in the course and develop compositional skills.  
**Performance assignments:**  
Performance assignments consisit of singing pre-learned melodies either alone or in groups.  
**EarMaster Aural Drills:**  
The aural drills portion of the homework will be conducted through *EarMaster* software. Each student will create a linked *EarMaster* account. New musicianship modules (rhythm, interval identification, singing, and dictation), will be posted each week.  
**Sound Journals:**  
Sound Journals are short written responses of at least 350 words, due every Friday at the start of class. These exercises address critical listening skills in a broad context, applicable to any genre of music and sound art.  
**Final Project:**  
The final project will consist of a creative composition project using the materials we have covered over the semester.
