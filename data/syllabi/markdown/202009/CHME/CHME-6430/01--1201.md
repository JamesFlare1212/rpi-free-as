## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Description, fundamentals, and engineering aspects of processes using microbial cells and enzymes are be covered. Topics include review of biochemistry, basic recombinant DNA techniques, enzyme kinetics, enzyme technology, cell growth, fermentation, bioprocess development and product isolation. Some advanced topics, including mammalian cell culture, protein engineering and metabolic engineering, are also be covered.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The students that finish this course in a satisfactory manner will be able to demonstrate: i) a knowledge of enzyme technology; ii) a knowledge of cell growth and fermentation; iii) a basic knowledge of bioprocess development, iv) the ability to identify, formulate and solve biochemical engineering problems. Students taking the 6000-level version of the course will also gain the ability to propose new research questions and identify methods to test new hypotheses related to biochemical engineering.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Homework - 30%

Quiz - 3%

Midterm 1 - 15%

Midterm 2 - 15%

Final Exam - 20%

Literature Summary, Analysis – 10%

Literature Presentation (group) - 7%

**Homework:** There will be approximately 7 homework sets. Questions will be posted online at least one week before their due date. A random subset of the questions will be graded.

**Quiz:** Short quiz will given approximately once per week via LMS/link, generally at the beginning or end of class with a 10 or 15 min time limit. Students participating asynchronously (remotely) will have 24 hours to complete the quiz.

**Exams:** Midterms and final exam will be open book. All exams will be cumulative.

**Late Policy:** One homework assignment may be submitted up to one week late for full credit. For all other assignments, 10% credit will be deducted each day after the due date. No credit will be granted if assignments are submitted more than one week late or after the homework has been returned following grading (whatever is shorter).

**Collaboration:** Homework may be done collaboratively, but you must submit your own work. All exams must be your own work only. The literature summaries must be your own work, but discussions with classmates to aid in understanding, communicating the key elements is acceptable and encouraged. Presentations will be done in groups. Grading rubrics for presentations and projects will be provided on LMS.

**Grading concerns and appeals:** Please contact Professor Collins in a timely manner to discuss and resolve any issues with grading. Submit the assignment/exam with a note identifying the question(s) you are requesting be re-graded and a brief explanation.
