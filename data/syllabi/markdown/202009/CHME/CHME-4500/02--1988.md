## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Principles of kinetics, reactor design, and analysis for both homogeneous and heterogeneous (catalytic) systems. Topics include design for multiple reaction networks (optimum selectivity), analysis of simple reactor combinations, and design of isothermal, adiabatic, optimum temperature profile reactor, and thermodynamics for chemical reactor design.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Rawlings, J.B. and J.G. Ekerdt, Chemical Reactor Analysis and Design Fundamentals, 2nd ed., Nob Hill Publishing (2014), ISBN-13: 978-0975937723 --- available through Amazon. Course notes will be uploaded to LMS after each lecture.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students who successfully acquired these learning outcomes from this course will be capable of identifying, formulating, and solving complex reactor design problems by applying the reactor design, science, and mathematics principles.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Reaction Stoichiometry for single and multiple reactions  
Reaction Equilibria  
Material balances on major chemical reactor types (batch, CSTR, and PFR)  
Energy balances on major chemical reactor types (batch, CSTR, and PFR)  
Numerical solution of ordinary differential equations for chemical reactors using MATLAB/Simulink  
Simulation of major chemical reactor types (batch, CSTR, and PFR) in Aspen Plus  
Extraction of kinetic parameters from experimental reaction rate dataDesign of packed bed reactors  
Reactor safety and control systems

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students who complete the course should be able to demonstrate an understanding of the modeling, design and operation of batch stirred tank reactors, continuous stirred tank reactors (CSTR), and plug-flow reactors (PFR), and compare the performance characteristics of the different reactor types.
- Students who complete the course should be able to analyze rate data using integral and differential methods to find rate constants and reaction order.
- Students who complete the course should know how to evaluate kinetic rate constants or thermodynamic parameters by fitting of experimental reaction data to kinetic or thermodynamic models
- Students who complete the course should demonstrate an understanding of multiple steady-states, reactor stability, and runaway reactions.
- Students who complete the course should demonstrate an understanding of the safety implications and control strategies for reactor design
- Students who complete the course should know how to use software packages (such as MATLAB and Simulink) to solve reactor models, and be able to model and optimize chemical reactors in Aspen Plus software.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Homework Assignments will include both conventional hand-written problem solutions and some assignments that are programming-intensive. *Homework is due at the beginning of class, not at the end.* Assignments should be submitted via LMS on a week-by-week basis. The teaching assistants may only grade selected parts of homework assignments or check only for completeness of problems at their discretion.

Homework collaboration policy: Individuals may discuss the problems with others, but are not allowed to share solutions (MATLAB m-files, etc.).

**Active Learning Exercises** will be distributed during class time, and students are expected to complete them during class (participation/attendance grade). Class attendance and participation will therefore be measured by completion of ALE, many of which will involve MATLAB or Aspen programming. Online assignment submissions (though LMS) will be required by the end of class time. *Electronic sharing of ALE solutions with students who are not actually working on the assignment is cheating, and will result in grades of zero for everyone involved.*

ALE Grading Policy:

1\) No make-up ALEs will be given for any reason.

2\) Students missing an ALE with an excused absence from Student Success will be given a grade of "X", which means the ALE does not count.

3\) Each student gets to drop (1) ALE at the end of the semester, which may be a missed class due to illness or a job interview. Otherwise, you may drop your lowest ALE score.

4\) No extra time accommodations are offered on ALEs.

5\) Some ALEs may be graded like quizzes; others may be "participation" exercises in which everyone is given a score of 100% for completing the assignment.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

The course grade will be determined using the following components:

Homework Assignments (6-8) 20 %

Active Learning Exercises (12-20) 15 %

Midterm Exams (2) 40 %

Final Exam (1) 25 %

Total 100 %

Course grades will be curved. Approximate letter grade ranges for each exam will be announced after the exams are graded and returned.

Exam average policy: *Students earning an average examination score of < 50 % ("unacceptable") will receive a course grade of "D" or "F" only, regardless of homework and active learning exercise (ALE) scores.* This policy will not be relaxed. Fair warning! Conversely, and exam average > 50 % does *not* guarantee a course grade of C.

Guideline #1: an exam score of 90% or higher indicates a letter grade of at least A-

Guideline #2: an exam score of < 50 % indicates unsatisfactory (failing) performance

Grade Appeals:

A student may appeal a grade by attaching a memo (to the instructors) to the specific exam or homework assignment that is being appealed. This memo must state the specific reason(s) for the re-grade. The student has *one* week after the grades have been assigned to contest the grade; after that time period, *no more re-grade requests will be considered.*
