### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

**Fully Remote Option****:** Students electing to complete the course fully remotely will complete three laboratory experiments consisting of videotaped experiments (Pumping, Heat Exchanger) and Virtual Reality experiments (Process Control, Photocatalytic Reactor Design). Each laboratory team will choose to complete three of the four available experiments.

Note that it is permissible for part of a laboratory team to conduct the experiment in-person while the remaining students participate remotely; streaming video capability will be available.

**Asynchronous Learning:** Students located in time zones different from the RPI physical campus in Troy, NY are assumed to be selecting the Fully Remote Option. These students are responsible for forming teams that can meet to work on report writing at mutually agreeable times.

## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

A two-term laboratory course on experimental analysis of the operations and processes of chemical engineering. Emphasis is placed on planning of experiments, data evaluation, and report writing.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The purpose of this course is to provide senior chemical engineering students with hands-on experience operating and analyzing common chemical engineering unit operations. The unit operations covered in this laboratory course build on the theoretical material covered in previous and concurrent chemical engineering courses. Emphasis is placed on planning and executing the experiments, analyzing data, and dissemination of results via written reports. As a primary goal of this course, you should develop an aptitude for performing unit operations by applying fundamental chemical engineering knowledge to their operations. You will also develop communication skills necessary to work in a team or group. Courses at RPI are generally expected to require 2-3 hours of outside work for each credit hour. You should therefore expect to put in at least 8-10 hours/week outside the lab or classroom.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students who complete this course should be able to communicate results of experiments effectively in written form via laboratory reports.
- Students who complete this course should be able to work together as a team to collect, analyze, and interpret data, draw conclusions, and present results in laboratory reports.
- Students who complete this course should be able to apply the theoretical principles traditionally used by chemical engineers (e.g., fluid flow, heat and mass transfer, reaction kinetics, and mathematics) to analyze and interpret laboratory-scale unit operations.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

LABORATORY REPORTS

Each laboratory team will conduct three experiments conducted during the course of the semester, and each experiment will be conducted approximately over a three**-**week cycle. Laboratory reports will be due on the following dates:

**Report #1:** Due Friday, October 9th by 5:00 PM

**Report #2:** Due Friday, October 30th by 5:00 PM

**Report #3:** Due Friday, November 20th by 5:00 PM

For report #1, your team may choose from: Heat Exchanger, Pumping, and Virtual Process Control. For reports #2 and #3, your team may choose from any of the four experiments (including Photocatalytic Reactor Design).

QUIZZES

Four quizzes will be administered to prepare students for team-based writing of laboratory reports:

Laboratory safety

Experimental statistics and uncertainty analysis

Writing quiz #1 (Abstracts)

Writing quiz #2 (Grammar)

*Students must score 80 % or higher on the laboratory safety quiz before they are permitted to enter the laboratory. One re-take will be permitted, but a second failure to score 80 % or higher will result in expulsion from the course.*

Quizzes will provide students a means to post individual grades early in the semester. Feedback on lab reports is generally received in the 2nd half of the semester due to the length of the experiment cycle and the two-stage grading scheme employed. Our goal is to return three graded laboratory reports to you before the final exam. However, you may or may not get your first graded report back before your second one is due, for example.

FINAL EXAM

A final examination will be held, which covers both the technical content and writing-intensive aspects of the course. Thus, it is imperative that students understand the concepts and calculations within each experiment, as opposed to relying upon one person in the group to do all of the calculations, for instance. The final exam will also include a section in which you will rate your teammates' performance in three categories: attendance, effort, and quality of work. *Teammates' ratings of effort, quality of work, and meeting attendance may be used at the instructor's discretion in determining an individual's course grade.*

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Quizzes (individual) 20 %

Written Reports (group) 50 %

Final examination (individual) 30 %

Total 100 %

The overall course grading scale will be based on a curve. However, *grades of less than 60 % on any individual assessment item are indicative of unacceptable performance.*

The final grade on each laboratory report will be divided between technical writing aspects (50 %) and scientific content, correctness, and uncertainty analysis (50 %). Dr. Hedden will grade each lab report based on technical writing considerations including English grammar, writing style, organization, completeness, presentation of Figures/Tables, and formatting. In addition, each of the three experiments has a supervising instructor or TA responsible for the grading of scientific/technical correctness and uncertainty analysis.

## Course Calendar

### Calendar Details:

**Each experiment will generally consist of a three-week cycle:**

Week 1: Conducting the Experiment

In-person laboratory experiments will be supervised by a teaching assistant. Remote experiments may be done on any day at any time, pending availability of your teammates.

Week 2: Interim Meeting

Following the experiment, an interim discussion with the instructor will take place during the second week. The Interim Meeting Schedule will be posted on LMS once the experiments are assigned to each group. *Students should bring completed calculations, tables of data, and figures to this meeting.* Each group member is to be familiar with the calculations and data analysis by this time. Be prepared to ask any questions about the data or analysis and the instructor’s expectations for the final report, which is due the following week.

Week 3: Final Report

Each experiment concludes with a final report due during the third and final week of the experiment. *Detailed guidelines for preparing reports will be posted on LMS and covered during lecture time.*

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

All report writing is to be done independently by each lab group. No collaboration between different groups is permitted; copying from previous years' laboratory reports is considered cheating and will result in disciplinary action. Questions should be addressed to the Instructor or TAs. Evidence of collaboration or falsification of laboratory data will be dealt with according to the Student Handbook. Additionally, a signed ‘Foreman Report’ must be completed and attached to each final report documenting the contribution of each individual member in the group.

Failing to contribute to writing of a given laboratory is justification for receiving a grade of zero on the report, even if you were present when the experiment was being performed. Students who contribute less to the writing of their reports than expected by their teammates may receive substantial deductions on the final report grade; there is no stipulation that all team members will receive the same grade on a given report. The instructor will use the Foreman report and brief interviews with team members to determine "% effort" contributions when complaints or disputes about effort levels arise.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

**FOREMAN REPORTS AND DISPUTE RESOLUTION PROCESS**  
In cases where a teammate's contributions to report preparation are unsatisfactory, an "individual effort multiplier" system will be employed to adjust their individual report grade. For example, if a student is judged to have done only 50 % as much work as done by their teammates, their grade will be equal to the group report grade multiplied by 0.50. *No make-up assignments can be given because we cannot turn back the clock and repair harm done to your teammates due to lack of effort on your part.*

Procedure for Resolving Disputes Pertaining to Lack of Effort:

1\) One or more teammates must file a written complaint with the instructor via email. If the complaint is consistent with the Foreman Report received, then steps 2-4 may be followed. Otherwise, nothing will be done.

2\) The instructor will interview each team member via WebEx and/or email to determine if one or more team members did not contribute sufficiently to report preparation.

3\) The instructor will generally request that the accused teammate should submit a record of their individual contributions to report preparation, which may consist of calculations and/or written report sections.

4\) The instructor will determine if an effort multiplier is required to adjust one or more individuals' grades.
