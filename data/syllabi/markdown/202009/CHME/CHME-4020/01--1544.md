CHME-4020-01 Transport Phenomena II

**Location: Remote Time: 2:30-4:20 PM Mondays & Thursdays**

The course is a continuation of CHME 4010 (Transport Phenomena I), but in many ways will be totally different from CHME 4010. The focus of this course is on multidimensional problems, coupled heat, mass, and momentum transport, and boundary layer analysis. It will rely heavily on background material that was learned in differential equations and Physics 1. It will also use topics from multivariable calculus.

**Prerequisites:** MATH 2400 (Differential Equations) and CHME 4010 (Transport Phenomena I)

**Required text:** “Transport Phenomena Fundamentals” by Joel Plawsky, CRC Press, 2020 (fourth edition)

**Optional texts:** “Transport Phenomena” by R. B. Bird, W. E. Stewart, E. N. Lightfoot, Wiley, (second edition)

“Fundamentals of Momentum, Heat, and Mass Transfer” by J. R. Welty, C. E. Wicks, R. E. Wilson, Wiley, (fourth edition)

**Office hours:** Tuesday 2p-5p or by appointment (Webex)

**Teaching Assistants:** Somdatta Bhattacharya, bhatts7@rpi.edu

**TA Office Hours:** M 3-5; W 4-5 in Ricketts Coonley Lounge

**LMS:** An LMS page has been created for the course. If you are registered for the course, you should have been added automatically. Students should verify that they have access to the page. Announcements and handouts will be posted to this page.

**Course grading:** Homework (15%), quizzes (10%), two term exams (25% each), and final exam (25%).

**Homework:** Due on Thursdays during class unless otherwise stated. **Download from LMS website and submit the same way.** Late homework will not be accepted.

**Extra Credit:** There will be an opportunity to earn extra points towards your homework scores, as described on the last page.

**Quizzes:** Multiple-choice quizzes (3 questions, 1.5 minutes per question) will be given at the beginning of class on Mondays, unless otherwise stated. The lowest two quiz grades will be dropped. This is done only to provide for sickness, interviews, emergencies, personal problems and other such exigencies that inevitably occur during the semester. No official excuses are necessary.

**Term Exams:** During class. Closed book and notes. You are allowed **two sides of one sheet of paper** (8.5”x11”). Note that you will not be allowed to have cellphones, laptops, tablets, etc. with you at your seat. Students should either not bring these items or place them at the front of the classroom during the exam.

**Final Exam:** Cumulative exam (covering all course material) during Finals Week, date and time TBA. Closed book and notes. You are allowed three (3) one-sided sheets of paper (e.g. the sheets from the two term exams plus one new sheet). **Note that you will not be allowed to have cellphones, laptops, tablets, etc. with you at your seat**. Students should either not bring these items or place them at the front of the classroom during the exam.

**Regrading Policy:** If you feel your work was not accurately graded, you must submit a regrade request *in writing within one week* of receiving the grade. You must cite the posted solutions in your written argument why you believe it was not accurately graded.

**Homework collaboration:** Students are encouraged to discuss the underlying concepts and approaches with their classmates. As part of this, students will submit homework in pairs. Some students may not have a partner if there are an odd number of students or if there are adds/drops to/from the class. Note that the work turned in should reflect only the pair’s efforts.

**Code of Conduct:** Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty, and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty of a zero on the assignment/quiz/exam for the first offense and an **F** grade for the course for any subsequent offenses.

If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

**Note about Showing Work:** In this class, the process of getting to an answer is as important (if not more) than the actual answer, and you need to be able to clearly communicate that process. Therefore, you ***must show, explain, and justify*** all of your work to receive credit. If you do not show your work, you will receive zero credit (even if the answer is correct). For example, a series of equations without explanation will not receive credit even if all equations are correct. If you are algebraically manipulating equations to simplify, you should state that. Unless specifically requested in the problem statement, it is not acceptable to use packages such as MATLAB, Maple, Mathematica, etc. to perform manipulations, integrals, etc.

**Learning Outcomes**

After successfully completing the course, a student should be able to:

- Simplify models based on geometry and values of dimensionless groups
- Construct and solve microscopic balance equations
- Construct and solve macroscopic balance equations
- Solve coupled momentum/energy/species transfer problems

**Extra Credit:** It is possible that there are some typographical errors in the textbook. If you find them, you will have an opportunity to earn extra points towards your homework score. To earn points, you should submit *in writing or by email* a description of the error (including the location in the text), what the correct version of the text should be, and why. For each correct “error”, you will receive 2 points towards your homework score. You will not receive points if the error was already submitted by another student. Submissions should be made either in person or by email. If a group of students submits an error, the points will be distributed equally among the students in the group.
