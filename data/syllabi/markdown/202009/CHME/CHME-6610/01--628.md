### Prerequisites or Other Requirements:

This course will build upon many concepts you learned in an undergraduate differential equations  
course. You should review some of the material from Chapters 1-4 from Greenberg, discussed below.  
Also listed below are some optional problems which you can use as a self-test of your background  
solving differential equations. The course will also build upon many concepts using matrices. You  
should review the following material from Chapters 10 and 11.  
Chapter 1: Modeling  
Read pages 1-16  
Chapter 1 gives an introduction to modeling, which is the process of representing a phenomenon  
with approximate mathematical equations.  
Chapter 2: 1st order ODE’s  
Read pages 18-30,46-48,62-69  
Chapter 2 discusses first order differential equations. The key concepts from section 2.2 are  
how to solve the homogeneous equation, use of an integrating factor to solve the nonhomogeneous  
equation, and use of variation of parameters. Section 2.4 discusses separable equations. Section 2.5  
discusses exact equations and integrating factors.  
Chapter 3: Linear ODE’s of higher order  
Read pages 73-105,117-131,133-144  
Chapter 3 discusses higher order ODE’s, restricted to linear equations. Section 3.1 contains  
a very useful review of the different classes of equations. Section 3.2 discusses linear dependence  
and linear independence of functions. Section 3.3 discusses general solutions and linear differential  
operators. Section 3.4 discusses linear, constant coefficient, homogeneous equations, in particular solutions in terms of exponentials, circular, and hyberbolic equations. Section 3.6 extends 3.4  
to nonconstant coefficients. The equidimensional (or Cauchy-Euler) equation is especially important. Section 3.7 discusses techniques for nonhomogeneous equations. Especially important are the  
techniques of undetermined coefficients and variation of parameters.  
Chapter 4: Power series solutions  
Read pages 173-190,193-209  
Chapter 4 discusses power series solutions to linear, nonconstant coefficient equations that are  
second order and that do not have simple closed-form solutions. Section 4.1 gives an introduction  
and overview. Section 4.2 reviews power series and uses them in the case of analytic coefficient  
functions. Section 4.3 identifies singular points and introduces the method of Frobenius.  
1  
Chapter 8: Systems of linear equations  
Read pages 396-407  
Chapter 8 discusses the solution to systems of linear algebraic equations. Section 8.3 describes  
how to solve these equations using Gaussian elimination. In addition to the case where there is  
a unique solution, you should understand what happens when there is no solution (the system is  
inconsistent) or when there is a non-unique solution (with a p-parameter family of solutions).  
Chapter 10: Matrices  
Read pages 465-479, 481-485  
Chapter 10 introduces matrices. Section 10.2 defines matrices and operations on and between  
them. Section 10.3 defines the transpose of a matrix and useful properties. Section 10.4 defines  
the determinant, discusses properties of it, and how to calculate it. It is important to know how to  
calculate a determinant.  
Chapter 11: Eigenvalue problems  
Read pages 541-551  
Chapter 11 introduces the eigenvalue problem for matrices. Section 11.1 defines the problem.  
Section 11.2 discusses how to solve the problem and applications. It is important to know how to  
solve these problems for 3 × 3 matrices.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Learning Outcomes**

After successfully completing the course, a student should be able to:

- describe the defining characteristics of a generalized vector
- describe the defining characteristics of a basis set and compare and contrast different choices of basis sets
- solve PDE’s using Separation of Variables, FFT, and transform methods
- solve equations using both regular and singular perturbation techniques
