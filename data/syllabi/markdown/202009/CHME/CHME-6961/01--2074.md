## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will cover the theoretical and practical aspects of technical communication in written and oral form. The goal of this course is to teach students the art of clear, concise, and impactful communication. This class is designed to be highly relevant to graduate students who will produce publications, presentations, and thesis documents as part of their degree program. However, undergraduate students who wish to improve their technical communication may also enroll. Key concepts will include logical structuring of information, flow and storytelling, style and grammatical choices, and steps in creating effective technical writing. These concepts will be solidified through practice writing and speaking assignments. This class is geared towards both native and non-native English speakers. This class is NOT an English as a Foreign Language course, and students are expected to have a solid grasp of English language usage.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students who complete the course will gain:  
• Familiarity with the three stages of efficient technical writing  
• Practical experience with writing common forms of technical writing, such as research articles, reviews, and proposals  
• Competence in using grammar and language tools to improve the clarity and impact of technical writing  
• Practical experience in reading and evaluating technical writing  
• Practical experience in structuring, composing, and delivering oral presentations that are impactful and clear  
• Understanding of effective communication with targeted audiences that have varying technical backgrounds

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Unless otherwise noted, all assignments are expected to be individual, original work. All reference materials (including web materials) used in preparation of assignments must be cited appropriately using footnotes and/or a bibliography. One instance of unacceptable collaboration or plagiarism will result in a reduction of the final course grade by one letter grade (e.g. B to C). A second instance of academic dishonesty will result in failure of the course.  
Examples of plagiarism include:  
• Using someone else’s words, ideas, art, or other production as your own  
• Using someone else’s words, ideas, art, or other production in your production without crediting the source (regardless of whether compensation was exchanged)  
• Quoting someone else without putting the quote in quotation marks  
• Providing incorrect source information  
• Copying or quoting enough ideas or words from someone else so that it makes up the majority of your work, regardless of whether you credit the source or not  
• For more information, please see https://www.plagiarism.org/article/what-is-plagiarism  
Plagiarism is taken very seriously in technical communication. Engaging in plagiarism not only robs others of their work, but also has permanent impact on your credibility as a scientist/engineer. Plagiarism can severely affect your academic performance and future career path. In short, it’s just not worth it. If you are uncertain if something qualifies as “plagiarism”, please ask ahead of the assignment’s due date.
