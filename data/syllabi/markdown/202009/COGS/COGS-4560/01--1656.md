## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

In this course we will explore the different strategies used by different languages to fulfill the same needs of human communication. The topics we will most likely cover are: language universals; generative grammar; linguistic relativity; cross-cultural pragmatics; reproducibility in science; cross-linguistic angles of computational linguistics (knowledge-elicitation for low-density languages; natural language understanding systems); and historical linguistics.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Objectives**

Students will understand the principles of influential linguistic theories aimed at modeling the human language capacity.

Students will understand how linguistic theories change over in response to both scientific and extra-scientific factors.

Students will have a better appreciation for the scientific process as it actually plays out in the world, not as an idealized abstraction.

Students will have a better understanding of cross-linguistic diversity based on surveys of many languages.

Students will appreciate the complexity of language-oriented issues in society, such as dying and endangered languages.

Students will improve their oral and written communication skills, including their ability to collaborate with their peers.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will demonstrate that they have understood the principles of influential linguistic theories and will be prepared for advanced study if they choose to pursue it.

They will show the ability to engage in group discussions with appropriate etiquette: not dominating or being silent, and being able to both offer and accept constructive criticism.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance in this course is required. Why?

- This is a communication-intensive course and communicating with yourself doesn’t count.
- A large portion of the material we will cover is not summarized in any written materials that I can point you to.
- The slide shows I will post on the LMS neither cover all material nor are completely self-explanatory.
- I have developed this course with the intention of getting a large portion of the job done during class meetings. (There is homework but my goal is to keep it moderate in both quantity and difficulty.) But in order to get the job done during class meetings, you’ve got to come to class.

**If you are opposed to attending classes, then this course is not a good fit for you and I strongly encourage you to find a different course for which attendance is not required.**

The specific policies associated with attendance are as follows:

- If you miss class and have official documentation excusing you, find out what you missed from someone in class and make up any missed assignments within a week. If there are any issues with this, please be in touch with me.
- You are permitted one free absence for illness without documentation. The indented bullet points below apply only to this case:
  - This absence cannot be taken (a) on an exam day, or (b) on a day when there will be group discussion of papers and presentations. If you are ill on a day like that, you need to be in touch with me in advance to get permission or else get official documentation. Otherwise, the absence will cause a deduction in your grade.
  - If there is a pop quiz on the day you miss, you can either take a 0 for the quiz or you can choose to take a make-up quiz, which will be more difficult.
  - If you miss any assignments, you will be required to find out what you missed from another student, make up the work, and hand it in within a week of the miss.
  - I will not repeat lectures for unexcused misses during office hours. You will need to ask your fellow students to catch you up.
  - Writing me an email to say that you are missing a class is not an excused absence unless I tell you explicitly that it can count as an excused absence.
- If you miss more than one class without an official excuse – we will call this an “excessive miss” – this will result in a reduction of your **course grade (i.e., your course average)** as follows:
  - One excessive miss: subtract 2 from your course grade (e.g., 92 becomes 90)
  - Two excessive misses: subtract 5 from your course grade (e.g., 92 becomes 87)
  - Three excessive misses: subtract 9 from your course grade (e.g., 92 becomes 83)
  - Four excessive misses: subtract 14 from your course grade (e.g., 92 becomes 78)
  - Five excessive misses: subtract 20 from your course grade (e.g., 92 becomes 72)
  - Six excessive misses: you get an F for the course.
- If you are not paying attention during class and/or if you are using technology in any way that I do not specifically assign, this is counted as an absence. (No texting, tweeting, doing email, surfing, programming, doing other assignments, etc.)

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own performance. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. This class will involve some collaborative work, and the nature of allowed collaborations will be explained. **Any assignment, quiz, exam, or presentation that reflects academic dishonesty will result in an F as the course grade for the student.** In addition, the student will be reported to the Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education as appropriate. If you have any questions concerning this policy, please promptly ask for clarification.

**Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism**

I am required to define what is meant by cheating, a task that I believe is practically impossible to do comprehensively: any definition I write will be open to crafty interpretations by individuals who want to cheat. The following is an attempt to fulfill this requirement of defining what cheating is. The list is representative, not exhaustive:

Unless otherwise specified by the professor, you must carry out every assignment and test exclusively using your own brain power. This means that:

- you may not collaborate with, give help to, receive help from, or steal help from anybody during a test or assignment
- you may not use cribs or any outside materials during tests or assignments.

You may not plagiarize. If you convey in writing any ideas that are not your own, you must use proper attribution.

If you are permitted to do work collaboratively, you must name the collaborators and, if applicable, their relative contributions.

You may not look up answers to problem sets or questions on the web, unless specifically permitted to do so by the professor.

You may not reproduce, verbally convey, or otherwise reveal the content of any exam to any individual at any time.
