## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course/internship focuses on data analytics research applied to real-world problems using open health data, inspired by our challenge to create a healthier world through data analytics. In this course/internship, highly motivated students work in multidisciplinary teams on six-week, team based, hands-on data analytics projects. Approximately three hours per week are devoted to learning modeling methods useful in health informatics and in data analytics practice more generally. Datasets and projects will be selected at the start of the semester from a collection of challenges supplied by on- and off-campus clients. Each client provides project and dataset background and assists in setting team objectives. Students actively engage in the process of transforming knowledge to data, gaining critical practical experience applying data analytic methods learned in prior coursework. Students will learn the process of setting project objectives, planning, and management, including the use of best-in-class collaboration tools and methods. Teams will conduct their project based on research goals and workflows they develop to attain these goals.

During the course of the 6 weeks, each team will create an entry into the AHRQ Social Determinants of Health Challenge Phase 2. We have already created and entered our Phase I entry to the challenge. Here is more information

Students will be guided via classroom demonstrations and individualized team coaching by the instructors and teaching assistants in the employment of visualization, analytics and modeling methods on health care data. The demonstrations will be done in the R data analytics environment and will involve the use of R routines, external packages, script building and the use of pre-built scripts provided by the instructors. An R ‘boot camp’ will be held at the beginning of the semester to get those unfamiliar with the environment started.

For Fall 2020, our research projects will focus on using data analytics to understand and combat the Covid-19 Pandemic and will be doing a bioinformatics project on Circadian Rhythms.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The instructional focus will be on problem formulation, data management, preparation, exploration and use of visualization, analysis and modeling methods and their application in health analytics. . Interpretation of results, formulation and evolution of project objectives and work plans will be an important part of the hands-on process. Project execution and the development of management skills, and written and oral presentation skills will also be emphasized.

The projects and examples in this course will examine how data analytics can be used to provide health care solutions and improve the quality and delivery of human health care. Students will become aware of the emerging Healthcare Analytics Careers that use data to drive better care. New careers are opening up for data analytics to work on multi-disciplinary teams to exploit powerful new data analytics technologies with massive electronic health record databases to provide insights to decision makers to improve healthcare quality and delivery.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Students will work in groups on open research questions. Their results will be presented as part of presentations in standups, breakouts, and in a paper prepared for publication.

Students will receive coaching to build their data analytics research skills to address these open research questions including

- Data analytics problem formulation based on project objectives and data
- Problems in health informatics and their potential contribution
- Data set evaluation and selection
- Data preparation and visualization
- Data analytics application and results interpretation
- Data modeling and results interpretation
- Project planning and management
- Presentation and report preparation and delivery

The Lab will hold a virtual mini-conference. Feel free to invite your friends and family to see your research.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will learn to pose data analytics questions needed to find insights into data to meet project objectives and translate these questions to data analytics workflows.
- Students will learn to evaluate data sets for completeness, utility for a given purpose, and presence of errors, ambiguities and other inconsistent content.
- Students will learn to correct data set flaws and deficiencies, and otherwise prepare data sets for use in various analytical processes (workflows).
- Students will apply quantitative analytic methods and learn to interpret the results and translate these into further analysis, modeling and project planning.
- Students will learn about health analytics and its potential contributions
- Students will practice presenting their results in oral and written form.
- Students will demonstrate use of collaboration tools for remote team work.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1. Individual project notebooks: Students will individually document their research contributions and projects using an online, R-based project notebook.
2. Commitment of notebooks, code, and data to R project Github.
3. Group project artifacts (github, including notebooks & github wiki)
4. Participation at class meetings.
5. Participation in class standups and project breakouts
6. Practice Blog Post
7. Actual Blog Post
8. Final group research paper (using Overleaf!)
9. Practice project reviews presentations and demos
10. Final group project presentation/demo in Mini-Conference
11. Getting ready assignments

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Mid-course Group Project Presentation: 10%

Final Group Project Poster Presentation: 10%

Participation in the breakout and standup meetings for each team is required. Students should be prepared to discuss their work at each meeting. A single poor performance or missed meeting will not be penalized.  
All students will be doing different research work so students are assessed on their own individual efforts, progress, communication of results, participation and outcomes. We expect things to go slow at beginning of the semester and that students will accomplish more as they gain experience.

Students/Teams whose project presentation grades are low will be offered outside counseling. Students who appeal their project presentation grade may do so by providing additional documentation to support their appeal.

Students whose work session and Q&A participation grades are low will be contacted for outside counseling. Students who appeal these grades will be given the opportunity to provide additional input.
