## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is an in-depth study of current peer reviewed literature and topics in the field of ecology and the environment. Students will read and critique peer-reviewed papers and present and lead discussions on peer-reviewed articles.  
This course consists of one class per week in which a current research paper is presented and analyzed in detail. Students will choose a current scientific paper in consultation with the instructor and lead discussion in class. Students will send the chosen paper to the instructor one week before their presentation. All students will contribute to the analysis and discussion of the selected peer-reviewed article.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Learning Outcomes for BIOL 6971:**

- Demonstrate competence in reading and critically reviewing primary scientific literature in ecology and the environment by formulating written critiques of articles and summarizing the key points within selected articles.
- Articulate an objective evaluation of selected articles in class discussion, supported by facts in the selected articles and scientifically grounded perspectives.
- Lead class discussion in the evaluation of peer-reviewed papers, and demonstrate understanding of the subject in selected articles, including knowledge of the broader sub-discipline.
- Describe the differences in peer-reviewed article structure, format, journal type, and impact factor in order to evaluate article and journal quality and identify important and impactful peer-reviewed articles.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Assessment:** Students will be assessed by participation in class discussion and leading/presenting discussion. Participation in class discussion will utilize both oral contributions during class time as well as summaries of articles on LMS due the night before class. In leading discussion, students will choose papers in consultation with the course instructor. Discussion presentations will be graded by the instructor.

**Reading and attendance:** This class is intended to be a highly interactive learning course. Attendance will be taken and participation is essential for successfully completing the course. Each missed class after the first two will result in the loss of half a letter grade. All students are expected to read the assigned papers before class and be prepared to discuss them in detail.

**Grading rubric:**

All students will be evaluated based on (1) their leading discussion and presentation (40%) and (2) their contributions discussions and paper analysis (60%). Discussion presentations will be evaluated on a 100-point scale, with points given for the following categories described below. After each discussion presentation, the student discussion leader will meet with the instructor to obtain feedback on their performance. Presentations will be graded by the instructor.

1. **Introduction and knowledge base (30 points)**
   1. Does the discussion leader provide a concise summary overview of the paper to remind the class of the key points (context and results)?
   2. Does the discussion leader bring in outside information (e.g., figures or statistics; to the extent needed and relevant)?
   3. Is it clear that the discussion leader has become an expert on the paper and the topic in general?
2. **Poise and presentation (30 points)**
   1. Is the discussion leader engaging with students? Making eye contact? Confident? Articulate?
   2. Does the student command attention in the room and manage time effectively?
   3. Does the speaker use encouraging language and tone of voice to facilitate student participation?
   4. Does the discussion leader provide adequate time and space for feedback and comments by others?
   5. Does the discussion leader spend a balanced amount of time listening as well as talking?
3. **Synthesis and critical thinking (30 points)**
   1. Does the discussion leader synthesize points that are brought up?
   2. Does the discussion leader ask thoughtful engaging questions of others that orient students to important considerations, issues, and implications?
   3. Does the discussion leader link topics together and back to material previously covered in class?
4. **Intangibles (10 points)**
   1. Does the discussion leader get students excited about the topic(s) or results of our readings?
   2. Does the discussion leader facilitate an overall engaging discussion?

**Evaluation of student participation:**

In addition to the requirement of each student leading/presenting discussion on at least one peer-reviewed paper, students will be evaluated each class based on their contribution to discussion. These evaluations will be graded for each class and averaged across all classes in the semester to generate a grade toward the final grade.

Evaluation will be based on the following contributions during class (i.e., oral contributions to discussion) and submission of written summaries the night before class. All students are required to submit written summaries via LMS. Evaluation of oral and written contributions will be based on the following:

1. Has the student demonstrated that they have read and understood the assigned paper, such as through asking questions, answering a question, or providing an insight into the paper topic? (50%)
2. Has the student demonstrated their comprehension of the topic covered in the assigned reading, such as through the content for their question or a perspective they shared on the assigned reading? (50%)

Students may only appeal any grades received to the instructor within one week of receipt of the grade. Questions about grades or scientific issues must be submitted in writing with the assignment in question to the instructor. Final decisions about grading rest with the instructor. Students may monitor their course progress by checking their grades on RPILMS and at a time to be announced during the semester.
