## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The first part of the semester will be spent working with yeast as a model system for cell biology. Then, we will cover mammalian cell culture skills, and you will use the techniques of Western blotting and immunostaining to obtain information about proteins within cells. While learning the basics of mammalian cell culture, you will be developing the experimental design for an independent project. You will submit a formal proposal for your project along with appropriate protocols, and you will then carry out what you propose. Depending on the work flow, the recitation time will often be used for lab experiments. Time will be taken during some lab periods to introduce or discuss material as a class. You will also be expected to keep accurate notes of your methods, results, and conclusions. **This is a communication intensive course.**

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The course goals are to teach students:

- How to operate independently in a biological research lab.
- How to organize experiments, keep track of results and reagents, and troubleshoot procedures in the lab.
- How to represent and analyze qualitative and quantitative data with statistical rigor.
- How to communicate findings in the lab in written, verbal, and presentation formats.
- Where to find peer-reviewed research articles and interpret their results.
- How to write a manuscript for publication in a peer-reviewed research journal.

- ­ A lab requires its members to show respect to other lab members, their experiments, and their shared equipment. Each infraction of lab policy, including the rules listed under the *COVID-19 Precautions & Restrictions* and *Lab Safety* sections of this syllabus, will result in the deduction of one point. ## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

- You are expected to attend all lab periods and recitation. For instances where labs are held remotely, you will be expected to attend lab via WebEx during the normal lab and recitation meeting times.

For example, if you receive a Dean's excuse or doctor’s note via email, "print" this email as a PDF and submit the PDF to LMS; if you receive physical documentation, you must first scan it and then submit the scanned document to LMS.

If you foresee yourself missing a lab, contact Dr. Klein to coordinate a make-up time if a make-up is even possible; this must be done well ahead of time. Failure to make up lab work will result in the same penalty as missing the lab.

**COVID-19 PRECAUTIONS & RESTRICTIONS**

- **Masks and Eye Protection**
  - ­ Masks are to be worn by all students and faculty in all classrooms, labs, and any other institute buildings. Masks must be worn properly, covering both the mouth and nose. Students who violate this policy by not wearing masks will be reported to the Dean of Students and will be requested to leave the lab and return to their living quarters. The Dean of Students will provide the appropriate sanctions for the students per the code of conduct signed by the students.
  - ­ Safety glasses or goggles should be worn during lab; these will be provided for you, but you are encouraged to bring your own. Resist touching your face, eyes, and nose while in the lab or classroom. For emergency situations where your eyes must be cleaned, eye-wash stations are found in lab - rinse your eyes for at least two minutes and do not touch tem afterwards.
- **Cleaning of Spaces**
  - ­ Students are required to disinfect their work surfaces in the lab before they begin work and after they have finished for the day. 70% ethanol will be provided for disinfection. Disinfecting lab chairs and stools is also encouraged.
- **Traffic Flow and Social Distancing**
  - ­ Students and faculty will respect the need for social distancing to the degree possible by the setting. All personnel will move in and out of labs as per the appropriate instructions of the faculty/administration. Students are expected to follow printed traffic flow statements posted in all rooms and buildings. Specifically, when using common reagents and a shared workstation, please be patient and wait for other students to complete their work.
- **Student Health**
  - ­ If you are ill, under quarantine for COVID-19, or suspect you are ill, you must report this to Student Life. Once faculty notification is made, Dr. Klein will make every reasonable effort to accommodate your absence and will communicate that accommodation directly to you. However, to maintain accurate attendance tracking, **you must submit documentation of your absence through LMS** (see *Lab Attendance Policies*).
- Refusal to comply with any appropriate request to limit the spread of COVID-19 will be treated as a classroom disruption and disciplinary actions and sanctions will be taken through the judicial process in the Student Handbook (request to change the behavior; request to leave the lab; dismissal of the lab and referral to Student Life and the Dean of Students.)

**LAB SAFETY**

- Follow all rules issued in writing and verbally from Dr. Klein.
- Do not engage in any activities that will endanger yourself or those around you.
- **Wear gloves** when interacting with any laboratory equipment and change gloves often. You will be provided gloves.
- **You must wear safety glasses or goggles** at all times during lab except when using microscopes - disinfect eye pieces of microscopes with ethanol before and after use. Eye protection will be provided, but you are encouraged to bring your own.
- Lab coats are required and must be worn at all times in lab; lab coats are not provided.
- You must wear shoes; footwear like sandals and crocs are not allowed.
- Report any spills or accidents to Dr. Klein **immediately**.
- When finished, you must clean your work area and return it to the condition you found it in; **disinfect your work areas with 70% ethanol**.
- 70% ethanol will be used extensively by all members of the lab to disinfect working areas and equipment before, during, and after lab experiments.
- To reduce risk of COVID-19 transmission, maintain a 6 ft (1.8 m) distance from other personnel in the lab; do not cross into other students' work spaces.
- Follow traffic flow guides in lab to help maintain social distancing; do not crowd other students while they are working at a shared location or on shared equipment.
- Always **wear a mask** while in lab or any institute building.
- **Use of cell phones is not allowed in lab**. You will recall that laptops, however, are required - the rational for disallowing cell phone use is that, unlike laptops, cell phones are placed on or near the face, creating a serious COVID-19 transmission risk and increases the risk of contamination by other hazardous reagents used in the lab.
- Book bags, backpacks, and other personal items must be stored under your lab bench, away from other student's belongings. Be sure to avoid making your personal belongings a tripping hazard. It is recommended that you bring as few personal belongings as possible with you to lab.
- Violation of any rules stated here or verbally by Dr. Klein will impact your **Lab Citizenship grade**, and major violations can have you removed from the lab.
