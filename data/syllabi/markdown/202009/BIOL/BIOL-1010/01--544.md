## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Description**: This is an introductory course in biological systems focusing on major topics in evolution, human genetics & medicine, and ecology. It is a course that is intended for non-majors and majors and covers a variety of topics that impact our daily lives and the grand challenges of this decade and your generation. The goal is to provide an understanding of fundamental principles, to develop analytical thinking skills in the context of modern biology, and to develop data dexterity for understanding and solving grand challenges in the life sciences. Note that this is an approved DI-1 course.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate mastery of the foundational knowledge and core concepts of evolution, genetics & medicine, and ecology.
- Apply foundational knowledge and core concepts in these areas to solve novel problems, analyze realistic scenarios, and make predictions.
- Apply reasoning and methods to the analysis and solution of biological problems.
- Utilize information and data from a wide range of sources to construct knowledge and communicate that knowledge effectively.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1. Evaluation: Answer multiple choice questions through exams
2. Evaluation: In-class discussion, homework and online exams
3. Evaluation: homework, online exams
4. Evaluation: In weekly homework, examine a current topic in biology that presents ethical or moral concerns. Evaluate what scientific knowledge/information would be needed to address the problem.

***QUIZZES**

There are 23 lectures and 11 quizzes spaced at approximately weekly intervals. Please pay attention to quiz dates on the schedule because exams shift them as they occur during class. All quizzes will be worth 10 points and consist of two essay-type questions. One essay will be either data interpretation or factual information and the second question will be on the societal/ethical relationship of the biological finding. THE QUIZZES ARE OPEN BOOK, OPEN NOTES, BUT INDIVIDUAL WORK- NO GROUP OR JOINT EFFORTS. As the 12th quiz comes just before the last exam, it is not necessary to do this quiz. However, if it is handed in it will be used as extra credit and points added to your individual score and not to the 350 points for the course.

Students may discuss any grades received for the Lecture Section with Dr. Hanna or your assigned teaching assistant. It is most effective if this is done within one week of receipt of the grade. Students may monitor their course progress by checking their grades on RPILMS during the semester.
