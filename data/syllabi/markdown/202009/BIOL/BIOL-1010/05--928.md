### Other Course Policies:

**Academic Integrity**: Relationships within the academic community are built on trust. For example, students must trust that their instructors have made the appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and the faculty must trust that the assignments that students submit are their own. Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational process. The *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty, and as a Rensselaer student, it is your responsibility to read and understand these. *In addition to what is stated in the Handbook, we provide the following guidelines for BIOL-1010*:

**Class Activities**: We encourage students to discuss all activities in pre- and in-class sessions with fellow students. Learning is interactive, and we encourage this. However, when you are writing pre-class or in-class assignments, you must write your answer in your own words.

**Quizzes & Exams:** You may not give or receive assistance by any means before or during an exam. This is all about you!

**Class Presentation**: This is a team project; therefore, you must work as a team. The expectation is that each person will contribute equally. Further information will be given during the first part of the semester.

**NOTE:** Because of the ability to use the internet as a research tool, the greatest academic integrity problems arise because of copying and pasting electronic words without thinking. It is critical that you learn early to use these tools and then to put the ideas in your words and provide a reference of the source of the information. In addition, while Wikipedia is a wonderful tool, we do expect you to read the literature we assign and get beyond “fast facts”, some of which are incorrect.

The second most common academic integrity issue at Rensselaer is taking advantage of previous students’ papers, essays, problem sets, posters, etc. and submitting them as your own or submitting recycled work of your own. While some may view this as a game and part of the competitive process, your faculty and the Institute consider these as another academic integrity violation. Think carefully about your moral compass and the person you want to be. It is your choice. If you have any doubts or concerns of whether an action is an academic dishonesty, please feel free to discuss it with your instructor or TA.

### Additional Course Information:

**Reflection Posts**: You will be asked to watch a video and then provide

1\. Evolution: HHMI, The Origin of the Species: The Making of a Theory. **Due September 20th.**

https://www.biointeractive.org/classroom-resources/origin-species-making-theory

Throughout history, Charles Darwin is largely credited with the theory of evolution. Yet as we have learned more, it is Alfred Russel Wallace, a British naturalist, who made significant contributions that solidified Darwin’s thoughts and gave Darwin the confidence to publish. Assess the contributions of each, and why you think that Charles Darwin received most of the credit. Do you see this as a repetitive pattern even today and in all fields where there is not appropriate recognition for discoveries? How can perceptions be biased based on the information one receives? In this case, why do you think that the Theory of Evolution is largely attributed to Darwin?

2\. Genetics & Medicine: Human Nature. Movie available after September 9th. See trailer here.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HyrtdNtr8J4 **Due October 18th.**

This movie explores the power of CRISPR and its potential. The questions that arise are the ethics of its use, for example, to fight disease, to enhance certain traits in newborns or animals, to revolutionize agriculture, and/or to accelerate evolution. Write a reflection piece of your thoughts addressing the ethics and where the line falls to use or not use CRISPR. What are the potential benefits and dangers?  
3\. Ecology: There is significant concern about global climate change and its impact on weather, global food supply and fresh water, as well as more frequent pandemics. Yet at the same time there is optimism that we can effect change if we focus our intellect, scientific knowledge, and take small steps on a large scale that involve many. There are a couple of videos below that focus on this approach and well as several on the 20 Million Trees Project. **Due November 23rd**.

Sir David Attenborough. How to Save our Planet. April 5, 2019. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0Puv0Pss33M

Sir David Attenborough. A Reason for Hope. May 9 2019. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BNbq9m72JXE

20 Million Trees Project by 2020: https://teamtrees.org/  
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U7nJBFjKqAY

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7bsuXsxX950

Choose any of these and write a reflection of what you see as your ability to effect change on a local scale that when applied on a global scale can have a high impact. Consider your major and what you and your professional community can do to effect change. In each of these videos, there is a connection between science and technology and potential outcomes. Think about this too, and the advantage and importance of a Rensselaer technological education and research training.

**Guidelines for Class Presentations – Biol-1010-05**

Your talks should be ~10 minutes in length with 5 minutes for questions for a total time of 15 min. You must not exceed these time recommendations because of the number of presentations per class. Presentations will be stopped at 15 min.

Grading will be based on the following considerations:

1. **Content:** Substantive summary of some important recent results (past 5-10 years) from the scientific literature pertaining to your topic. 30 points.
2. **Appearance & effectiveness of slides:** Were the slides organized and easy for the viewers to read and interpret? Did they contain a balance of text and figures that concisely presented key findings and conclusions? 20points.
3. **Actual presentation:** Did all members of the team share equally in the presentation? Did each team member display mastery of the content and the ability to clearly and articulately present that content? Were the slides and their associated content connected, or did the information in each slide seem to stand alone, largely disconnectedfrom the information in the other slides? 25 points.
4. **Ability to answer questions:** Were members of the team able to clearly and politely answer questions from the class? Were they able to engage in a give-and- take discussion when the opportunity arose? 25 points?
5. **Note on References:** While web sites containing information relevant to your topic will help you gather initial information and organize key components of your talk, this is a science course and we expect you to go beyond basic Internet resources and delve into the scientific literature. Google Scholar is a good place to start as well as PubMed and Web of Science. Depending on your topic, the journals *Science*, *Nature*, *Current Biology*, and the *Proceeding of the National Academy of Sciences* (PNAS) are excellent journals to search for some of the most important research results to appear over the past few years. In general, you should utilize and reference at least **5 articles from the scientific literature** for your presentation.
