## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is an introductory course in biological systems focusing on major topics in evolution, human genetics & medicine, and ecology. It is an undergraduate level course. It is intended for non-majors and majors and covers a variety of topics that impact our daily lives and the grand challenges of this decade and your generation. Activities, such as student presentations and written responses to lecture questions enable students to interact, and discuss these topics. The goal is to provide an understanding of fundamental principles, to develop analytical thinking skills in the context of modern biology, and to develop data dexterity for understanding and solving grand challenges in the life sciences.

ASSESSMENT DESIGN:

The assessments are designed to achieve all 4 learning outcomes of this course. However, the assessments are in an open-book test format, and not in a proctored closed-book exam format. The major objective of such a design of assessments is to enable students to learn about biology by engaging in interactive, discussion-oriented exercises. Students achieve this when submitting answers to questions on Lecture Tests on the Master Online Participation Slides (MOPS). This enables students to evaluate topics with examples that are currently in focus, rather than using the same set of examples every semester. For example, learning phylogeny via the coronavirus example during a pandemic is much more powerful in enhancing student learning than the ungulate example used in prior regular semesters. Students gain experience in communicating their newly gained knowledge of basic biological concepts and and thereby develop communication dexterity, via 2 formats (below):

1. Small Group Peer Review (Graded).
2. Faculty and TA Office Hours (Ungraded).

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

ACADEMIC GOALS:

1. Students will be able to grasp, and master introductory biological principles.
2. Students will be able to connect biological concepts, and apply concepts learned in the biology classroom, with those used in the corequisite laboratory course.
3. Students will be able to satisfy prerequisites, in order to take advanced courses in the biological sciences.
4. Students will be able to connect biological concepts, and apply concepts learned in the biology classroom to complex current issues in the outside real world.
5. Students will be able to connect biological concepts, and compare and contrast these principles, in an interdisciplinary manner, with what they are learning in their major line of study.

ACADEMIC TIPS:

How to Study: In studying a subject like biology, which contains an inexhaustible amount of material, it is important to study actively, sorting the material into at least two categories: familiar, understood concepts and facts on the one hand, and new or difficult concepts or facts on the other. Plainly, once you do this, you know what it is you have to concentrate on in order to increase your grasp of the subject. Do the questions and/or quizzes in the Study Area of the textbook’s web site, Mastering Biology, http://www.masteringbiology.com/. It is generally useful to form study groups to help clear up difficult concepts (not to go over what “everybody” knows).
