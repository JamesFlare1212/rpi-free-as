## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Course Goals:** I have four goals for this course:

1. That you develop a mechanistic understanding of how cells function,
2. That you develop the ability to critically read scientific literature,
3. That you develop the ability to find/curate scientific information about a topic and integrate it into a broader mechanistic understanding.
4. That you get experience with a real-world-like team project

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Student will be able to integrate new knowledge into broader mechanistic understanding of cell biology.
- Student will be able to explain cell biology concepts to others.
- Student will be able to interpret, analyze, and evaluate current scientific literature.
- Student will be able to work together with others to design and execute a coordinated project.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

These objectives will be assessed by a team project, paper analyses (top 4 grades of 7 given), a class presentation, quizzes (top 5 grades of 7 given), and participation in class discussions.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Although I will not take attendance, attendance, whether in-person or via WebEx, is expected and you will find it extremely difficult to do well in this class without attending regularly. In addition, 5% of your final grade comes from class discussion and if you are not in attendance, you will forfeit those points. If you have difficulty attending synchronously, let me know and we’ll work something out.

### Other Course Policies:

**Readings/Time Commitment:**

I expect you to watch any videos I post and read the textbook chapters (or find equivalent background info on your own) and assigned papers BEFORE the class in which they are discussed.

*Textbook:* We will not cover everything in the textbook, and we will discuss material that is not covered in the textbook, but there is significant overlap. You will not be responsible for any topics that are covered in the book that we do not discuss in class (for example, we will not discuss photosynthesis, so you can safely ignore that!).

*Papers:* Reading current scientific literature is hard, but very important. You will not succeed in this class if you do not read the papers carefully (and probably more than once!). The paper analysis format is designed to help you work through the paper -- not to be busy work. The more you put into it, the more you will get out of it!

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
