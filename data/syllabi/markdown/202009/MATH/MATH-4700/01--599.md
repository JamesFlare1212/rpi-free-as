## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Mathematical formulation of models for various processes. Derivation of relevant differential equations from conservation laws and constitutive relations. Use of dimensional analysis, scaling, and elementary perturbation methods. Description of basic wave motion. Examples from areas including biology, elasticity, fluid dynamics, particle mechanics, chemistry, geophysics, and finance.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

As this semester takes place under unusual and unprecedented circumstances, the assessment and grading procedure here will be subject to change if a modification is discovered under evolving circumstances to serve better in determining a fair grade.

Your course grade will be determined primarily by the homework grade. The first homework will be due on or about September 21. Homework must be submitted as single PDF files on LMS. Students should expect to be interviewed from time to time over WebEx about their homework to validate the independence of their work. Inadequate performance on these interviews will result in a penalty applied to the score of the homework in question as well as all previous homework scores since the previous interview (or all previous homework scores if there was no previous interview). The final exam, conducted one on one over WebEx, will similarly serve to validate the homework grade. Inadequate performance on the final exam will result in a penalty to the overall homework score, and therefore the final grade. The current plan is to require only the highest scoring students to take the final exam, with the precise cutoff to be determined. Violations or suspected violations of the integrity policy on homework may result in the final exam requirement being imposed on further students whose work is in question, or if widespread integrity violations are observed, on the class as a whole. Regular final exams for the highest scoring students will be administered over WebEx during the final exams period. Final exams imposed due to suspected or determined academic integrity violations will be scheduled between November 30 and December 11.

Your course grade can be enhanced by active and substantive contribution to discussions in class and on the LMS site. You can be awarded as much as 5 percentage points on your final grade for exceptionally insightful and thoughtful input and questions.

Late homework will be penalized 10 points (out of 100) per working day. Once I post solutions, homework will no longer be accepted for credit. If you have need for an extension on your homework, you should ask me in advance of the due date. Homework is due at 4:45 PM on the due date specified on the calendar; submissions later in the class or day will be penalized 5 points.

You may only appeal scores on homework based on factual criteria (a correct answer was marked wrong, or scores were added incorrectly). Any such legitimate appeals will only be accepted within one week of the date on which the graded homework was returned to the class. I will not entertain quibbles about how many points of partial credit you think you should have been given on some problem. Students making frivolous appeals will be given one warning, after which further nuisance appeals will be penalized by as many points as the student was frivolously arguing for.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Beyond these general institute-wide policies, here are some course-specific clarifications

\* If you obtained assistance from anyone outside of the course or any written material beyond the lecture notes and the course text, you must explicitly acknowledge the source. You may under no circumstances query outside sources to solve in substance the homework problems for you. Inappropriate use of external sources will generally result in a grade of 0 for the entire assignment.

\* You should show your work and explain your calculations clearly and coherently in order to achieve most of the credit on homework problems. You may cooperate in small groups on the solution of homework problems, but your homework submission must represent your own understanding, thoughts, and expression. It is not permissible to copy the solutions of other students, even if working together. If there is evidence for copying, all students involved will generally receive zero credit for the implicated work if the collaboration is not acknowledged. If there is evidence for copying from an acknowledged collaborator, a grading penalty will still be applied for copying, possibly as severe as dividing the points among the collaborating parties. Note in particular, students who share their homework solutions with another student, who then copies their work, will suffer the same grade penalty as the student(s) who copied their work.

\* Students whose homework appears not to reflect their independent understanding and expression will substantially increase their likelihood for being interviewed about their homework. Students whose homework interviews do not reveal a level of understanding commensurate with their homework will suffer a grading penalty (as described above), and substantially increase their likelihood to be interviewed regarding future homeworks.

If you have any question concerning these policies before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

Students who violate the spirit or letter of these rules are subject to penalties according to the principles outlined in the Rensselaer Handbook, in addition to the grading penalties described. In addition, I will report all instances of academic dishonesty to the Dean of Students.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

I will direct the class according to the following learning strategy:

\* Students preview the sections of their textbook to be covered in class before the class. It is not necessary to have a thorough understanding of the section after a first reading, but knowing the basic facts and content should help in following the class discussion. In particular, I do not plan on spending much time in class on simply stating facts and definitions which students can more easily read in a nicely typeset textbook. Rather, I will assume students have at least looked over the section enough to have encountered new definitions and classifications, so that I can spend the class time explaining more about the "how" and "why" of the material rather than the "what."

\* Students attend class as much as possible, and obtain class notes from a friend or the LMS website when they are unable to attend. The material covered in class is intended to supplement, and not simply restate, the material in the textbook. Moreover, the class discussion will emphasize those aspects of the material that I find most important.

\* Students work either alone or with a small group of colleagues to solve the homework problems. While collaboration is encouraged to stimulate learning and understanding, each student is responsible for understanding any solution submitted under his or her name. If the assigned homework problems are too difficult, students should work out some of the simpler problems from the related sections of the textbook until they have gained enough confidence and skill to tackle the assigned problems. If students do not feel sufficiently confident with the material through self-study or consultation with colleagues, they should seek further assistance from the professor during his office hours.

LMS will be used for some aspects of course management. You can get started by going to https://lms.rpi.edu/ and consulting the document describing the use of LMS in this class. I also plan to experiment with incorporating piazza; more information about that later.
