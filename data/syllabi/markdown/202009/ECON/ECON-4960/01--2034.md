## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides skills to apply econometrics to diverse economic topics. Econometric methods are developed for tools such as instrumental variables, regression discontinuity, difference-in-differences method, quantile regressions, and spatial regressions. Methods to identify causal effects are emphasized. Students do hands-on data work that puts to use these econometric tools.

## Course Text(s)

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Apply appropriate econometric techniques to data analysis and interpret statistical inference results.
- Understand modern econometric methods and read research paper in economics.
- Write short research paper/report using the econometrics tools taught in this class.
- Gain competence in the statistical software R.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Weekly Assignments:

Unless otherwise noted, one assignment will be given at the end of each lecture. You can work in groups on homework assignments, but each student must turn in his or her own version of the assignment. **Please submit your homework in LMS before each Thursday 8 AM. Late submission will not be accepted.**

All assignments will be graded on a 0-100 scale. Students are allowed to drop a single lowest score before final grades are calculated.

Some problem sets will ask you to estimate econometric models and interpret the results. Your grade will depend on both whether or not you apply appropriate econometric methods, and on how well you explain your models and discuss your results.

This course will also focus on the importance of computational methods and *reproducibility* in econometric research. Thus **you should also submit your electronic code files before the due date of the assignments (if computational analysis is required).** If you have any questions about your submission, feel free to contact me. The recommended software for the computational aspects of the course is R. R is an open source project. It is available without charge from *http://cran.r-project.org/*.

Midterm:

The midterm will be take-home exam **due at 8 AM, October 12**. Note that the midterm date may be changed marginally. I will send you a notification in advance if there are any changes. This midterm will cover all materials up to and including Week 05 (February 18) and must be taken completely alone. **Late submission will not be accepted. The only exceptions include: 1. Illness (must provide a doctor’s note) 2. RPI-approved official activities (must provide documentation in advance).**

Final Project:

Each student will work on a project that involves using econometric estimation and inference methods to solve a real-world problem in a specific setting. The topic of this final project is flexible. You may choose a topic that interests you based on the availability of the data. You may need to collect the economic data related to your topic, apply the appropriate econometric methods taught in this course, articulate your results and construct tables and figures. Your final project report will be more like a mini-research paper. A complete project report must consist of (1) introduction, including literature review, (2) econometric models, (3) data description and statistical analysis, (4) conclusion, and (5) reference. Your project should be no more than 10 pages (excluding reference, tables and figures).

More instructions about the final project will be given after the midterm exam. This final project should be submitted **before 8:00AM, December 13**.
