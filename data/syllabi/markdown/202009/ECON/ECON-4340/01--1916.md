## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course describes how individuals (and firms) make financial decisions, and how those decisions might deviate from those predicted by traditional financial or economic theory. Throughout the semester we will examine how the insights of behavioral economics complement the traditional finance paradigm by introducing some of the main psychological biases in financial decision-making and examining the impacts of these biases in financial markets and other financial settings. This course will also introduce students to behavioral and experimental methodologies used in finance, economics and other disciplines.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Learn, know, and comprehend some of the main psychological biases in financial decision-making
- Become aware of the current research on how these biases impact financial markets and other financial settings
- Gain an understanding of how individuals actually make financial decisions
- Know how to improve financial decision-making in themselves and others

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Attendance and Participation (7%):**

- I expect you to attend all class meetings (either in person or virtually), participate in in-class activities, and to have read the materials that will be assigned. While I will not be grading on attendance per se, students will be graded on their class participation and participation in in-class exercises, which are designed to illustrate the biases discussed.
- I encourage your participation in class discussions, and please feel free to ask questions at any time.
- The above expectations apply to both in-person and online lectures.
- Some students may not be able to participate in certain lectures due to various reasons (e.g., time zone difference). These students will not be penalized; however, this is conditional on them communicating to me their special circumstance at their earliest convenience. If you know that you will not be able to make it to class, email me as soon as you can and explain the reason.

**Homework Assignments (18%):**

***Write-Up Submissions***

- Each student will produce a short write-up (about 300-400 words). This write up must involve i) a short description of a financial situation and ii) a discussion of how one or more of the topics we discussed in class impacted that financial situation.
- The financial situation has to be a real-life example, and the situation can be personal, professional, or something from current events.
- You are required to upload these write-ups to LMS by their indicated deadlines (listed on course calendar).

***Peer-Evaluations***

- You will read and evaluate two write-ups uploaded by other students. These peer-evaluation deadlines are also listed on the course calendar.
- You are expected to evaluate the write-ups based on the following criteria:
  - o Overall Quality
  - o Quality of the financial situation
  - o Appropriateness of the topic for the chosen financial situation
- Make sure to create a sub-section in your peer-evaluation for each of the above criteria and carefully comment on them.

***Homework Grades***

- Your grade for each homework is determined by both your own write-up (60%) and the peer-evaluations (20% each) that you provided.
- There will be a total of 3 homework assignments. Each homework will constitute 6% of your final grade in this class.

**Exams (25% each):**

- Exam-1 will be posted on LMS on October 19th. Exam-2 will be posted on LMS on December 10th. Exams will be posted at 10 am (Eastern Time). The exams will also be emailed to students.
- Once these exams have been posted/disseminated, you will have 24 hours to email your answers to aksoyb3@rpi.edu. 24 hours will be given to ensure that all students, including those in different time zones will have enough time to complete the exam.
- Your answers must be submitted in **pdf** format. If you submit your answers in a format other than pdf, and if this causes you to miss the submission deadline, you may lose 20% of your grade.

**Final Project (25%):**

*For ECON 4340 Students*

- Each student who signed up for ECON 4340 will select one of the topics discussed in class (the topic will require my approval).
- Students will thoroughly read one academic paper recently published on their chosen topic and present their papers to class at the end of the semester.
- The academic paper is required to be
- Published in the last 10 years
- Published in a peer review journal in Economics, Finance or related field.
- During your presentation, I expect you interact with others in the classroom by asking questions and encouraging a discussion.
- Each presentation will be about 15 min long. More instructions on presentations will be provided later in the semester.
- Everyone will be responsible for materials discussed in these presentations in Exam-2. So please attend these classes and engage with the presenter by asking questions.

*For ECON 6340 Students*

- Each student who signed up for ECON 6340 will write a short research proposal and present their proposed research in class at the end of the semester.
- The research proposals will be about 1,000-1,500 words excluding references. They are due by December 1st, 8pm. All proposals must be sent by email to aksoyb3@rpi.edu in word and pdf formats.
- Your topic selection is flexible as long as it is somewhat related to the topics we discussed in class. In general, you will be expected to introduce and motivate your research question(s), provide some literature review, propose a research method to investigate your question(s). Your research method needs to be empirical, either using pre-existing data sets or experiments.
- Each presentation will be about 15 min long.
- More instructions on research proposals and presentations will be provided later in the semester.
- If preferred, students can work in pairs. If you would like to work in pairs, get in touch with me to discuss the details.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
