## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Introduction to data science and machine learning, with case studies in discovery of structure-property relationships and new materials from experimental and computational data. Brief review of required background in linear algebra and statistics with hands-on exercises in Python. Data science topics: model fitting, clustering, dimensionality reduction, ontologies, Bayesian inference and design of experiments.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Outline of topics that will be covered in this course:

- Introduction to material informatics
- Exploratory data analysis: a demo
- Python and NumPy boot camp
- Statistics of single variables
- Relationships between variables
- Basic linear regression
- Bayesian statistics
- Design of experiments
- Databases
- Model fitting, prediction and cross-validation
- Classification
- Dimensionality reduction
- Clustering and density estimation
- Image analysis
- Neural networks
- Genetic Programming
- Case studies in material informatics (student presentations)

The first half of the course will establish familiarity with necessary statistical and computational techniques using relatively simple data sets and techniques, while the second half will survey advanced techniques with more complex data sets.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand the statistical and mathematical foundations of data-science methods
- Apply data-science techniques to research and engineering design problems in materials science
- Understand the reliability and quantify the uncertainty of conclusions derived from data-driven methods

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- Weekly homework with theoretical and computational exercises. (Outcomes 1 and 3; ABET 3.1 and 3.6) [**60%**]
- Term project showcasing a material informatics approach applied to a real-world materials engineering problem, with a written report. (Outcome 2, ABET 3.1 and 3.3) [**40%**]

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
