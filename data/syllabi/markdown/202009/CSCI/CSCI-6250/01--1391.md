## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course offers the introduction to network science and review of current research in this field. Classes will interchangeably present chapters from the textbook and the related current research. The emphasis will be on mathematical background of network science: graphs and networks; random networks and various types of scale-free networks; network properties such as assortatitivity, mobility, and robustness; social networks and communities; and dynamics of processes on networks.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

**Textbooks**

## Course Content

### Content Details:

- Mathematical background of network science: graphs and networks.
- Random networks and their properties.
- Scale-free networks, small world networks and Barabasi-Alert model.
- Mobility and networks
- Network robustness
- Social networks and communities
- Assortativity of networks
- Dynamic processes

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1.\&nbsp;\&nbsp;\&nbsp;\&nbsp; Apply fundamental network science ideas to create models and understand dynamics of networked systems;\<br>\<br>\<br>
- 2\. Compare, contrast, and describe the similarities and differences of different kinds of networks and processes modeled on networks;
- 3\. Critique the strengths and weaknesses of each of the models and types of networks based on them and these network types performance in diverse network science applications;
- 4\. Understand the principles of applying network science to disciplinary science and design and set up basic models for some specific applications;
- 5\. Read, analyze, and critique published literature in the field of network science and social networks;
- 6\. Assess novelty of network science research projects and their relation to the state of the art in the field.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The student performance will be measured using three different methods: (i) selecting a research topic for presentation, (ii) presentation on the selected topics and plans for the proposed project, (iii) final presentation that should include evaluation of research results for graduates, and (iv) contributions to in-class discussions.

The projects and plans will measure the student’s ability to apply concepts of network science to network analysis. The presentation of a research topic willl measure student’s ability to prepare summary material based on fundamental scientific concepts and basic research. The presentation of the project results will measure student’s ability to communicate the concept and notions related to the research projects in which the student is involved. The discussion in class will measure the student’s skills to critically evaluate and objectively assess the presentations of other students.
