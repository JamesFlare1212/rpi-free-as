**Course Description:** This course offers the introduction to network science and review of current research in this field. Classes will interchangeably present chapters from the textbook and the related current research. The emphasis will be on mathematical background of network science: graphs and networks; random networks and various types of scale-free networks; network properties such as assortatitivity, mobility, and robustness; social networks and communities; and dynamics of processes on networks.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Apply fundamental network science ideas to create models and understand dynamics of networked systems;
- 2\. Compare, contrast, and describe the similarities and differences of different kinds of networks and processes modeled on networks;
- 3\. Critique the strengths and weaknesses of each of the models and types of networks based on them and these network types performance in diverse network science applications;
- 4\. Understand the principles of applying network science to disciplinary science and design and set up basic models for some specific applications.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The student performance will be measured using three different methods: (i) projects for the undergrads and research plans for grads, (ii) homework, and (iii) contributions to in-class discussions.

The projects will measure the student’s ability to apply concepts of network science to network analysis. The homework will measure student’s ability to prepare summary of material based on fundamental scientific concepts and basic research and ability to answer basic questions about the properties of the networks. The discussion in class will measure the student’s skills to critically evaluate and objectively assess the presentations of other students.
