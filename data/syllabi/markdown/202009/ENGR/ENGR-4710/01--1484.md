### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

ENGR 4710 - Manufacturing Processes and Systems Laboratory I

Manufacturing theory, laboratory experimentation, and manufacturing systems design and development comprised of selected modern manufacturing technologies. Technologies include robotics and automation, injection molding, computer numerically controlled (CNC) machining, metal forming, inspection, and rapid prototyping. Student teams apply lessons to develop a proposal for a manufacturing system to produce a product requiring multiple coordinated manufacturing processes. Students will prepare appropriate technical documentation for communicating keys aspects of the developed manufacturing system to a separate fabrication group.  
**When Offered:** Fall term annually.  
**Credit Hours:** 4  
**Contact, Lecture or Lab Hours:** 8 contact hours

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The primary purpose of the Manufacturing Processes and Systems (MPS) I, a two-course sequence (i.e., I and II) is to teach, by experience, how to plan and execute efficient, cost-effective and environmentally sound manufacturing operations that produce a useful, high quality, and economical or “affordable” product. Getting hands-on experience with some of the most important manufacturing processes and equipment through a series of laboratory exercises will teach and reinforce manufacturing principles, procedures, methods and analysis. In addition, this course develops technical communication skills through periodic report writing (e.g., Technical Data Package, memos), and oral presentations. Another goal of this course sequence, which begins in MPS I and is completed in MPS II, is for students to experience being on a team that takes an existing engineered product design and delivers a product in small batch manufacturing quantities and in a very short period of time (i.e., 6 months total). In the process, you will learn to plan your work, carry out your plan, integrate tasks, and to effectively communicate with fellow group members and others. MPS I is a prerequisite for MPS II (ENGR-4720), which teaches an integrated project approach to planning and producing a product using the skills developed in MPS I. In general, the MPS faculty and staff expect most students that complete MPS I to continue with MPS II.

Another involves researching various feeders and fixturing methods.

- Computer Numerical Control (CNC) – The write-up for the Mastercam activity will include a Mastercam programming exercise along with an analysis exercise involving a comparison of two different machining scenarios to see which approach is faster.
- Polymer Processing – There will be analysis questions on injection molding including mold and part shrinkage and a separate write-up for the plastic shrinkage lab activity.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Students will demonstrate the ability to use and apply current manufacturing processes and practices including: • Additive manufacturing • Computer Numerical Control (CNC) and conventional machining • Digital manufacturing process simulation • Metal forming • Robotics and automation systems • Plastics processing with injection and forming
- 2\. Students will demonstrate the ability to redesign an existing product concept for sustainable manufacture.

**APPENDIX B –** Template for MPS Company Positions

This appendix discusses an organizational structure guideline for MPS teams; specifically, it outlines examples of administrative and engineering titles, responsibilities, and workload breakdown for various positions. This information will help your team and your advisor develop a final structure including responsibilities for each team member.

**Section 1 - Administrative Positions**

**Project Manager** – The project manager is responsible for the overall project management including personnel management. Specific tasks include organizing meetings, developing a project plan, keeping the project on schedule, managing team priorities, and making final decisions on product design and processes. The project manager also functions as an engineer within the company. The expected workload breakdown for this position is 75% project manager and 25 % project engineer.

**Financial and Procurement Manager** – The financial and procurement manager is responsible for budget projections, component and process estimates, maintaining the company budget for the technical data package, placing orders, and verifying incoming shipments. The financial and procurement manager also functions as an engineer within the company. The expected workload breakdown for this position is 50% financial and procurement manager and 50 % project engineer.

**Information Manager** – The information manager is responsible for coordinating and maintaining the Technical Data Package (TDP) information for the team, formatting the TDP, submitting the TDP at class milestones, verifying drawing formats and revisions, and maintaining the teams share point site. The information manager also functions as an engineer within the company. The expected workload breakdown for this position is 60% information manager and 40% project engineer.

**Manufacturing Manager** – The manufacturing manager is responsible for developing a process and production plan to produce the teams product. This includes process review, setting the manufacturing schedule, overseeing the work of the manufacturing engineers, developing an overall quality plan, tooling review, and interacting with the assembly team as needed. They are responsible for submitting the manufacturing teams work to the information manager for inclusion in the TDP. The manufacturing manager also functions as an engineer within the company. The expected workload breakdown for this position is 60% manufacturing manager and 40% project engineer.

**Assembly Manager** - The assembly manager is responsible for developing a processes plan to assemble and package the team’s product. This includes procedure review, setting the assembly schedule, overseeing the work of the assembly engineers, developing an overall quality plan, tooling/end effector review, computer program review, and interacting with the manufacturing team as needed. They are responsible for submitting the assembly teams work to the information manager for inclusion in the TDP. The assembly manager also functions as an engineer within the company. The expected workload breakdown for this position is 60% assembly manager and 40% project engineer.

**Section 2 – Engineering Positions**

**Note:** The following position descriptions are examples of different types of manufacturing and assembly engineers within a company. Multiple people may have similar titles and responsibilities depending on the workload of the team. Final titles and responsibilities will be discussed with your company advisor. The expected workload breakdown for these positions is 75% as outlined by the position description, 15% assisting in other areas as needed, and 10% administrative (TDP support).

**Engineering – Manufacturing Related**

**Plastics Engineer** - The plastics engineer is responsible for designing and producing drawings for plastic processing tooling including injection molding and vacuum forming. This includes shrinkage calculations, draft angle designs, mold design, Mastercam code generation, cutting tool selection, component drawings, and bill of materials. The plastics engineer is also responsible for a quality control plan for production parts.

**Process Engineer** – The process engineer is responsible for process selection and tooling design for metal forming/bending operations, machining fixtures, jigs, and other processing equipment as outlined by the teams manufacturing plan. This includes tolerance analysis, process calculations, component drawings, bill of materials, and developing Mastercam files as needed. The process engineer is also responsible for a quality control plan for production parts.

**Engineering – Assembly Related**

**Programming Engineer** – The program engineer is responsible for developing a flow chart, preliminary code, and final code for controlling the robots in the team’s assembly process. This includes, interfacing the robots with the parts feeder system, joining systems (ultrasonic welding and/or forming), and special purpose machines.

**Part Transfer Engineer** – The part transfer engineer is responsible to developing a plan and tooling for controlling the transfer of parts within the assembly system. This includes designs for feeders, pallets, developing drawings, bill of materials, and Mastercam files as needed. They are also responsible for tolerance and any calculations associated with assembly issues associated with parts transfer.

**Hard Automation Engineer –** The hard automation engineer is responsible for designing a plan and components for assembling parts requiring special purpose machines and or fixed automation. This includes review of part design, conceptual ideas for assembly, tolerances, calculations, component drawings, Mastercamfiles as needed, and bill of materials.

**Packaging Engineer** - The packaging engineer is responsible for developing the tooling, drawings, and Mastercam files as need for components associated with the packaging system. They are also responsible for tolerance and any calculations associated with packaging requirements.
