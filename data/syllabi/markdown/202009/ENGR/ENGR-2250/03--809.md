- 3 - Be knowledgeable and conversant with the field of thermal fluids (e.g. Biomedical, Civil, Computer and Systems, Electric Power, Electrical, and Industrial and Management Engineering).

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is required during all lectures, and each student is responsible for *all* materials presented and *all* announcements made in class. Please contact the instructor if you have time-zone issues. Note: there is a strong correlation between attending classes, doing in-class problems and homework assignments and one’s overall performance in the class.

### Other Course Policies:

Organization:

The course is structured into two-110-minute lectures per week. Each class will be divided into lecture sessions and in-class participation sessions by the students. Lectures will be conducted on an iPad and lecture notes will be posted on the LMS. **In person lectures will be replaced WebEx meetings. Recordings of the lectures will be posted to LMS for viewing at later dates**.

Lecture notes & lecture summaries:

Lectures will be conducted on an iPad and lecture notes will be posted on the LMS website. In addition, a summary of key ideas and equations from each lecture will be uploaded to LMS. The lecture summaries can be used in exams and quizzes.

Project:

Students will submit a group project for part of their overall grade (15%). The purpose of the project is to focus your attention to a thermal-fluids engineering topic with real world applicability. Students should refer to the “TF1 project description” document in the Project folder on LMS for further details.

Exams:

Three exams (open book) are scheduled on the syllabus calendar.

Exam 1: Friday, October 1st, 2020

Exam 2: Friday, November 6th, 2020;

Final Exam: TBA

Exams and the final exam will be held as timed take home exams. Exams will be posted to LMS and remain available for the duration of the timed period. Exam responses are to be made in your own notebooks/paper, scanned and uploaded to LMS following the end of the examination. Students must login to WebEx during the exam; the instructor will be present for questions, if necessary.

You are permitted to use your notes/textbook etc., but you must work independently. See section on Academic Integrity below for more information on exam expectations.

A missed exam will result in a grade of zero unless permission is granted before the scheduled exam time or a written letter from a physician or the Dean of Students Office is provided. A makeup will be given only where arrangements are made in advance with the appropriate note. Such situations must be documented through the Student Experience Office (Academy Hall, x 8022, se@rpi.edu).

Students must have their cameras on during exams.

Homework:

There will be 10 homework assignments, issued according to the syllabus calendar below. Homework assignments are submitted digitally on LMS and must be submitted as a pdf. Students should scan their assignments (there are many scanning apps available for smart phones) and it is the student’s responsibility to ensure good scan quality such that all writing is legible. Solutions will be uploaded to LMS shortly following submission. No late assignments will be accepted.

Homework assignments will usually involve 2 - 4 conceptual problems and 4 - 5 extended answer problems. Some extended answer problems will require the use of software. I suggest becoming familiar with MATLAB, however, all questions can be completed using your preferred package (i.e., excel). Homework assignments will be graded as follows:

- One question – selected by the instructor or TA – is graded for correctness (5/10 points);
- Remaining homework questions are complete (5/10 points).

Homework assignments are allocated 10% of the total class grade and the **lowest homework grade will be dropped**.

You are encouraged to collaborate with other students on homework assignments, however, each student is expected to submit their own work. **Students found to have copied any part of their homework from a solution manual or from an online database will be considered in breach of academic honesty and will result in a zero grade for homework assignment**.

*Homework presentation*

**1)** It is strongly encouraged that students use engineering “ruled” paper (8 ½ * 11 in.) with a clean left edge (i.e., no perforations). This paper is available at the bookstore. Each problem should begin at the top of a new page with the problem # and text problem # shown.

**2)** The upper margin of the first page of your homework should show your name, the homework assignment number, the date the homework is turned in, and the page number. The upper margin of the first page of each problem should show the problem number and page number. Other pages should include the page number. The page number should be given in the upper right corner in the format A/B where A is the current page number and B is the total number of pages in your homework.

**3)** Computations should be arranged in logical order and each step recorded in full (other engineers should be able to easily follow your work). The order of presentation of work should be as shown below. Any work that does not substantially follow this order of presentation will receive no credit.

\- List given data and diagrams

\- List required results

-------------------------------------------------- (use a line to separate information given to you from your work)

\- Calculations and diagrams

\- Final answer (boxed in)

**4)** Do not make short-cuts. Perform one operation at a time and make a complete record of it. Only experienced engineers can safely take short-cuts. An exception may be made for repetitive or tabular computations, in which case it is enough to provide a complete record of a sample calculation.

**5)** Final results should be clearly indicated by *boxing* them in.

**6)** The correct units should be provided for intermediate results and final answers. A result is of no value if units are not provided. Note that careful consideration of units will often prevent mistakes.

*Clearness and neatness*

**1)** Figures and letters must be neat and large enough to be easily read.

**2)** All work must be done in pencil. Pencil marks must be clear-cut and black so that they are easily readable.

**3)** Erasing must be clean and thorough. Smudgy, dirty work is not acceptable.

Opportunity for extra credit:

After the midterm exams, students may submit a “root cause analysis” (RCA). This brief report requires an analysis of the mistakes made in exams. Students who choose to conduct the root cause analysis may redeem points on their original exam score. See “Root cause analysis guidelines” on LMS.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

This statement is intended to cover most, but not all, forms of academic dishonesty. (You should refer to *The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* for detailed information on Institute policy in this regard.)

Homework:

Collaboration among the students in the class is permitted and, in fact, encouraged. Seeking help from other sources is also encouraged. However, the work you submit should be your own. **Students found to have copied any part of their homework from a solution manual or from an online database will be considered in breach of academic honesty and will result in a zero grade for homework assignment**.

Exams:

Use of Chegg.com or similar online services to obtain solutions to exam problems is explicitly prohibited. The instructor and TA will monitor these services during the exams; evidence of use of one of these products will **be considered in breach of academic honesty** and will result in **a failing grade for the course** and a recommendation to the Dean of Students that **the student(s) in question be suspended**. Furthermore, collaboration during an exam (exchanging verbal or written information or copying via use of email or chat) is prohibited.

Other:

Outrageous offenses such as arranging for a substitute to take an examination, attempting to obtain examination questions or solutions in advance, or attempting to change a grade record will result in a failing grade for the course and a recommendation to the Dean of Students that the student(s) in question be suspended. If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
