## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Explain and apply the fundamentals of thermodynamics, heat transfer, and fluid mechanics (e.g. conservation of mass, momentum, and energy, etc.) to types of problems commonly encountered by a practicing engineer.
- Advance from a solid foundation to more in-depth courses in this field (e.g. Aeronautical, Environmental, Materials, Mechanical, and Nuclear Engineering).
- Be knowledgeable and conversant with the field of thermal fluids (e.g. Biomedical, Civil, Computer and Systems, Electric Power, Electrical, and Industrial and Management Engineering).
- Acquire skills in analyzing and solving engineering problems.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Quizzes and Surveys (individual work):

Short quizzes and surveys will occur throughout the class, typically in every session. Three quizzes will be given at the beginning of class, throughout the semester, as outlined in the schedule below. Quizzes will be graded simply: 1 point for a significant attempt and 1 additional point for substantial correctness; 0 points for minimal or no response (1+1/0 grading). Surveys will be also graded simply: 2 points for a response; 0 points for no response (2/0 grading). There will be no make-up quizzes or surveys, although the lowest quiz grade and lowest survey grade will be dropped. The cumulative quiz and survey grade will comprise 10% of the final grade.

Exams (individual work):

Three online exams are scheduled on the syllabus calendar.

**Exam 1:** **Friday, 02 October 2020**

**Exam 2:** **Friday, 06 November 2020**

**Final Exam: TBA, xx December 2020 (during final exam week)**

The format for exams will be timed exams administered during class hours. The exam will be posted on Blackboard and remain available for the duration of the timed period. Exam responses are to be made in your own notebooks/paper, scanned and uploaded to Blackboard as PDFs before the end of the examination. Please make sure you have access to and know how to use a smart phone scanning app or scanner for this purpose. It is the student’s responsibility to ensure good scan quality such that all writing is legible. While specific permitted resources will be described in advance of each exam, it is anticipated that the ground rules will be open book, open notes, closed internet, closed collaboration with any other people.

In-class Problems (team work):

There will be roughly one in-class problem assignment per class session. In-class problems are completed in teams and submitted digitally, one per team, to Blackboard as a PDF. Please make sure you have access to and know how to use a smart phone scanning app or scanner for this purpose. It is the student’s responsibility to ensure good scan quality such that all writing is legible. Solutions will be uploaded to Blackboard shortly following submission.

In-class problems will be conceptual problems. In-class problems will be graded simply as follows: 1 point for a significant attempt and 1 additional point for substantial correctness; 0 points for minimal or no response (1+1/0 grading). In-class problems are allocated 10% of the total class grade and the lowest in-class grade will be dropped.

Homework (team work):

There will be 11 homework assignments, due according to the syllabus calendar below. Homework assignments are completed in teams and submitted digitally, one per team, to Blackboard as a PDF. Please make sure you have access to and know how to use a smart phone scanning app or scanner for this purpose. It is the student’s responsibility to ensure good scan quality such that all writing is legible. Solutions will be uploaded to Blackboard shortly following submission. No late assignments will be accepted unless an appropriately documented hardship is presented to the instructor.

Homework assignments will involve a mix of conceptual problems and more open-ended problems. Some problems will require the use of software. I suggest becoming familiar with MATLAB, however, all questions can be completed using your preferred package (e.g. MS Excel). Most problems of each homework assignment will be graded simply as follows: 1 point for a significant attempt and 1 additional point for substantial correctness; 0 points for minimal or no response (1+1/0 grading). Open-ended questions requiring more effort than typical conceptual problems will be identified in advance and will receive double weight (2+2/0 grading). Homework assignments are allocated 10% of the total class grade and the lowest homework grade will be dropped.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Each student is responsible for *all* materials presented and *all* announcements made in class. While attendance is **not** mandatory, short quizzes, surveys and in-class assignments in each class will also serve as attendance references. Please note that there is a strong correlation between attending classes, doing in-class problems and homework assignments and overall performance in this class.

For those living in time zones during the Fall 2020 semester that make participation in the live lectures unreasonable, we will make alternative arrangements for quizzes, surveys, problem-solving, exams and other in-class activities. All lectures will be recorded and posted on Blackboard. While we will survey the class members via email regarding ability to participate during regular class hours early in the first week of class, it is the responsibility of each student who is unable to participate during regular class hours to ensure the instructor is aware of your situation and time zone.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

In-Class Problems & Homework:

You may talk about concepts and approaches to problems with anyone inside or outside of class. However, the work submitted by each team for each in-class problem or homework set must be that of you and your team members exclusively. Students found to have copied any part of their homework from a solution manual or from an online database will be considered in breach of academic honesty and will result in a zero grade for homework assignment.

Quizzes & Exams:

Collaboration during a quiz, survey or an exam (exchanging verbal or written information or copying) is, of course, prohibited. In addition, the use of email or the internet during quizzes or exams for any purpose is strictly prohibited. All mobile devices (cell/smart phones, computers, pagers, etc.) must be stored securely away during lecture and are not to be used unless specifically directed otherwise by the instructor. Use of (or ANY interaction with) a mobile device during an exam without explicit permission of the instructor will be interpreted as the illicit transfer of exam data, will be considered an act of cheating and will be treated as such. All parties involved will receive an F in the course.

Other:

Outrageous offenses such as arranging for a substitute to take an examination, attempting to obtain examination questions or solutions in advance, or attempting to change a grade record will result in a failing grade for the course and a recommendation to the Dean of Students that the student(s) in question be suspended.
