### Prerequisites or Other Requirements:

This course requires completion of *Nuclear Phenomena for Engineering Applications* (MANE- 2830) or the equivalent, which should have provided the basics of the nature of matter, energy, and radiation. Some of the concepts involving detection and quantification of radiation build upon the principles covered in *Nuclear Instrumentation and Measurement* (MANE-4410).

### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

An introductory course on the principles of radiation protection (health physics). It provides a fundamental understanding of radiation interactions in matter, the biological effects of radiation, internal and external dosimetry, occupational and environmental radiation protection, health physics instrumentation, regulations, waste management, risk assessment, and radiation shielding.

## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course is an undergraduate level course. The materials for the course will draw largely from the required textbook, covering most of Chapters 2-11 and 13, plus supplementary material consisting of important references and typical industrial standards. A tentative lecture schedule is attached. The topics of the course are organized into three sections:

- Radiation Physics (Chapters 2-6): Underlying physical principles associated with radiation, radioactivity, interactions with matter, and the transfer of radiation energy
- Radiation Protection (Chapters 7-9): Biology and the effects of radiation interactions, measures and quantities of radiation risk, and the principles and standards of radiation protection
- Applications (Chapters 10, 11, 13, and supplementary materials): Radiation protection systems and regulations. Computational methods for shielding design and issues associated with specific industries such as power reactors, medical facilities, and accelerators.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Describe the mechanisms by which radiation interacts with humans
- Prepare shielding design for common radiation fields and situations
- Understand how radionuclides are incorporated into and affect the body
- Calculate and interpret the dose to an individual from exposure to radiation or radioactivity
- Choose appropriate radiation detection equipment for different circumstances or situations
- Be familiar with the regulatory environment common to Federal and State agencies
- Understand the basic components of a radiation protection program
- Apply the principles of health physics to various operational areas, including medical, power reactor, research, and accelerator facilities
- Design radiation shielding to meet dose limits and other constraints

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Three exams: 22% each

Two Projects: 10% each

Homework: 14%

The exam will be closed book and you will be provided with the necessary formulas in the exam, and you can additionally bring a one-page, double-sided crib sheet to the exam (more information will be available later).

Two projects involving the use of Monte Carlo simulations to shielding design/analysis will be assigned during the semester.

Homework will be assigned approximately weekly, and will be due in class one week from the date assigned. Late homework submissions will be accepted until the following class for a 25% grade reduction.

Final grades will be assigned on a standard 10-point scale with modifiers (93-100 = A, 90-92 = A-, 87-89 = B+, etc.). There will not be a curve applied to the final grades. However, if the class average on an exam falls below a C+, the scores may be adjusted upwards to the C+/B- range.
