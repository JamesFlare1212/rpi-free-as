## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Adjoints, also known as dual or costate variables, arise in a variety of science and engineering applications including inverse problems, design optimization, control, and uncertainty quantification. This graduate course will introduce students to the theory and application of adjoints for partial differential equations (PDEs). Topics will include: derivation of adjoint PDEs; continuous versus discrete adjoints; adjoint consistency; sensitivity analysis; error estimation; algorithmic differentiation; and check-pointing for large unsteady problems. Students will be expected to implement algorithms as part of projects, so adequate experience with programming is highly recommended. Students must also have some knowledge of ODEs, PDEs, and suitable discretization methods.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

After completing this course, students should be able to

- derive the adjoint equation for any given primal problem and functional;
- use the adjoint for sensitivity analysis and output error estimation; and,
- implement and solve adjoint problems in software.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Derivation of (steady) Adjoint PDEs:\&nbsp;state Green's identity and extended Green's\&nbsp;identity; derive the adjoint operator for an arbitrary PDE; derive adjoint\&nbsp;boundary conditions for an arbitrary PDE and functional; explain the role of\&nbsp;adjoints in over-determined systems; define the Frechet derivative; derive\&nbsp;adjoint PDEs of nonlinear state equations; describe the characteristics of\&nbsp;adjoints at shocks; describe the qualitative behaviour of adjoints of\&nbsp;hyperbolic, elliptic, and parabolic PDEs.
- Discretization of Adjoint PDEs: explain how to discretize adjoint PDEs directly; derive discrete adjoint equations for arbitrary problems; explain the difference between the continuous and discrete adjoint approaches; define adjoint/dual consistency; show whether a given discretization is adjoint consistent or not; explain the implications of adjoint consistency for functional accuracy.
- Implementation and Solution of Adjoint PDEs: use finite-difference/complex-step approximations to evaluate state Jacobian; describe how coloring can be used to evaluate the Jacobian; use the reverse-mode of algorithmic differentiation to compute vector-Jacobian products; use finite-difference/complex-step approximations to verify vector-Jacobian products; explain how to isolate errors in the Jacobian using selective products; describe how to solve adjoint problems using Krylov iterative methods; describe how to solve adjoint problems using pseudo-time marching; list and explain the possible convergence issues when solving adjoints; explain when and why tight convergence of the adjoint is necessary.
- Using Adjoints for Sensitivity Analysis: explain how to use adjoints to evaluate the reduced gradient; explain how to use adjoints to evaluate Hessian-vector products; explain how to evaluate sensitivities of coupled problems; derive mesh adjoints and describe their use in shape sensititives; explain what incomplete sensititivies are and why they are used; use adjoints to compute sensitivities for simple PDEs.
- Error Estimation and Mesh Adaptation: derive the adjoint-weighted residual (AWR) method for linear PDEs; define the mean-value linearization; derive the AWR method for nonlinear PDEs; list and explain the different ways of approximating the ``fine'' space; explain the role of adjoint consistency in error estimation and control; define the effectivity of an error estimate; describe the steps (in a flowchart) of output-based mesh adaptation; explain how error localization is performed for DG and CG schemes; define Riemannian metric, and explain its use in anisotropic adaptation; describe the entropy adjoint and its advantages/disadvantages for mesh adaptation; implement the AWR on a one- dimensional (nonlinear) PDE.
- Adjoints of Unsteady Problems: derive unsteady adjoint PDEs for first-order time derivatives; derive the discrete adjoint of explicit and implicit schemes; explain the role of the functional in defining the terminal condition; define checkpointing and describe how it is used to solve adjoints; analyze the adjoint consistency of time marching schemes; implement and solve the adjoint for an unsteady, one- spatial dimension PDE.
- Other/Advanced Topics: explain the challenges associated with chaotic PDEs; describe the ensemble adjont methods; describe the LSS adjoint method.
