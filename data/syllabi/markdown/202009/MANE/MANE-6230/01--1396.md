## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

*Advanced Manufacturing Processes and Systems* (AMPS) is a research project-based lecture/laboratory course that teaches graduate engineering students how to develop new manufacturing processes and systems.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

There is no dedicated textbook for this course. All reading assignments, notes, homework problems pertinent to lecture material and lab handouts will be provided electronically through the course LMS website.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

During this semester, the students will:

- Receive in-depth theoretical and practical knowledge (including individual assignment and team-based laboratory exercise) in four selected topic areas in advanced manufacturing processes/systems including: micro/nano manufacturing, additive manufacturing; advanced composites and digital mfg.
- Be organized into one of five student teams that will work on either an advanced manufacturing R&D project for an industrial collaborator or develop a manufacturing lab exercise and
- Write a final report for your team project and present methods, results and conclusions to a review panel made up of course instructors and invited guests from industry.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Learn the underlying theory, technology, and standard industrial practice for selected advanced manufacturing processes and systems
- 2\. Perform a hands-on laboratory exercise pertaining to the selected advanced manufacturing processes and systems
- 3\. Learn how to organize, lead, and manage advanced manufacturing research and development projects using an effective structured approach
- 4\. Design, analyze, and prototype a new advanced manufacturing process and/or system through a team-based project
- 5\. Demonstrate the new process and/or manufacturing system

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. ## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

**REMOTE STUDENTS:**

This is for students who have identified themselves as remote (100% online) this semester. Given the hands-on nature of the projects and the fact that you will NOT be physically present in the spaces running the experiments, the teaching team expects you to :

- Be present and participate online for the lectures since attendance affects your 5% grade.
- Turn in all individual submissions on time - just like the in-class students.
- Clearly take ownership of things on the team deliverables that you can handle/deliver on remotely [ Each project team must divide work taking into consideration their teammate’s remote status]
- Pay attention to any extra requirements that the module instructors may impose on specific deliverables on a case-by-case basis. (This will be done to ensure grade parity)
