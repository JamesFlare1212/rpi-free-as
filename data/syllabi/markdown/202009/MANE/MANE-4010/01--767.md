## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Application of thermodynamics, heat transfer, and fluid flow principles to practical engineering systems, including power generation, HVAC, automotive design, materials processing, etc. Extends and complements concepts introduced in ENGR 2250. Utility of the 2nd Law will be demonstrated and emphasized.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Thermodynamics is the science dealing with energy transformations, including heat and work, and the physical properties of substances that are involved in energy transformations. Heat transfer is the field of study which addresses the flow of energy due to temperature differences through a solid or a vacuum and between a solid and a fluid and the factors that affect the magnitude of this energy flow. Fluid mechanics is the discipline that focuses on static and flowing fluids, and their interactions with solid surfaces. In most applications, ideas and information from these three topics must be brought to bear to develop an effective and reasonable solution. Because of these interconnections, our intent with this course is to present material in the context of applications and problems so that these interconnections are made obvious. In this course we want our students to begin to take an expansive view of these topics rather than imposing a disciplinary view of them. In this way we will encourage our students to develop more insight and “feel” for engineering and to learn how to apply information from different disciplines to a problem solution. This course will build on the material presented in the first course (Thermal and Fluids Engineering I) and will extend that information to new topics and applications.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Student Learning Outcomes**

**On successful completion of this course students will be able :**

- to select simplifying assumptions in analyzing a complex thermo-fluids system.
- to analyze thermo-fluids systems by applying conservation of mass and energy.
- to perform entropy balance in thermodynamic systems.
- to identify thermodynamic states.
- to identify and analyze main thermodynamic cycles, such as vapor-compression refrigeration cycle, Rankine and Brayton cycle (simple and advanced), Otto and Diesel cycles.
- to analyze heat transfer in thermo-fluid systems by identifying heat transfer modes–convection (forced/natural, internal/external), conduction (transient and steady-state) and radiation using the appropriate equations to evaluate them.
- to analyze the performance of heat exchangers and design heat exchangers given a set of specifications.

Assessment Measures of student learning outcomes: 3 Midterm exams + Final exam, Homeworks

### Other Course Policies:

**Organization**

We will meet ONLINE twice weekly during class time for 110-minutes each time. During these meetings, there will be short lectures and a variety of exercises on which the students will work. Class participation is required. (This active involvement in the class is more effective for learning material than by passively listening to a lecture.) Homework will be handed in for grading. Three semester exams and a final exam will be given in addition to in-class work and homework.

**Study Time**

Learning the material will require effort in class and outside of class. The rule-of-thumb for the needed amount of study time for college level classes is approximately **two hours outside for each contact hour**. Some courses require less time; others require more. Likewise, some individuals need more or less time depending on their background, ability, study habits, etc. Several steps can be taken to improve your learning of the material. Studying on a regular basis is more effective than cramming. Studying the material before attempting homework can improve both accuracy and speed. Reading and/or thinking about the topics to be covered in lecture before attending the lecture can result in better understanding; this approach is particularly effective since new material introduced in lecture is then not so overwhelming.

**Homework**

Homework will be distributed and graded on an individual basis. Homeworks are assigned and should be submitted through LMS. Solutions will be posted on LMS after the homework is turned in. Because of the large number of students enrolled in this course, grading of homework will be based primarily on level of effort and a general sense if the solution procedure is reasonable. The solutions must show all steps and be presented in an organized and clear manner. ONLY NEATLY WRITTEN HOMEWORK WILL BE GRADED! Each student is expected to make a serious effort at completing all homework assignments and has individual responsibility for learning the material. LATE HOMEWORK WILL NOT BE ACCEPTED! (Solutions are posted by the TAs immediately after the deadline expires).

**Attendance**

Attendance in lectures is mandatory. Each student is responsible for all materials presented and all announcements made in class.

**Laptop use and in-class work**

This class will at times use MATLAB to solve in-class examples and homework problems. Solutions obtained with other software platforms are also accepted.

**Semester Exams**

Three semester exams will be given according to the schedule presented below. All students will take all three exams. The only acceptable excuses for absence are validated through an appropriate statement from the Dean of Students, presented to your professor in advance of the examination.

All semester exams will be open book and notes. Each exam will tend to concentrate on the material presented in the preceding three or four weeks, but can address any topic to date. Note also that exam problems will synthesize ideas practiced in several different homework problems, examples, in-class assignments etc. ***For all people excused from one of the semester exams, a single make-up exam*** will be given at the end of the semester. Time and location will be arranged as needed. This make-up exam will be comprehensive because it must cover material from all three exams.
