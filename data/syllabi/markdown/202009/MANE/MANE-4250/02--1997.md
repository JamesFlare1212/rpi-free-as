## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Space vehicle design introduces all elements of the spacecraft design process from proposal preparation through detailed specification and prototyping. Students are organized into design teams associated with different subsystems and tasks, to develop a solution to a space vehicle system’s problem of practical interest, by drawing on their background in aerospace engineering science, machine design, and manufacturing methods. Topics include problem definition and requirement analysis, design specifications, concept development, reliability, consideration of alternative solutions, materials considerations, engineering prototyping, mission analysis, and presentation skills. This is a communication-intensive and writing-intensive course.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The course goals include:  
\- To foster design strategies for space vehicles that encourage innovation.

\- Introduce the students to NASA’s process for selecting major space exploration missions, with a particular focus on the NASA Discovery Program.

\- Give the students the opportunity to work on a project that is of real interest in the aerospace engineering and scientific community.

\- Provide the students with the work environment they would find in their future careers as aerospace engineers.

\- Expose the students to the different realities of federal agencies sponsoring various classes of spacecraft.  
\- Practice thinking and acting as an engineer:

- Acquiring skill in working with others as a member of a team
- Developing skills in expressing oneself orally and in writing
- Development of basic project management skills

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Ability to prepare technical documents in aerospace industry
- Ability to give technical presentations
- Ability to work as part of a team
- Ability to design a realistic spaceflight system subject to multiple realistic constraints, with proper consideration of appropriate standards
- A knowledge of launch vehicle standards
- A knowledge of past NASA planetary exploration missions and how to access past flight data through the NASA Planetary Data System (PDS)
- A knowledge of aerospace structures
- A knowledge of aerospace propulsion
- A knowledge of spaceflight mechanics and JPL ephemeris files

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

All assignments will be due at the beginning of the class period on the due date or at a specific date and time specified by the instructor. Late deliveries will lose a full letter grade.

This is a design-focused course. Lecture modules for each spacecraft subsystem will be posted on LMS for students to watch on their own schedule. Class time will primarily be devoted to team meetings and project-specific discussions with the instructor, though there will be occasional in-class (synchronous) lectures. As the semester progresses, focus will shift towards team meetings. Having regular team meetings during the allocated class time ensures that all team members are available and provides dedicated access to the instructor for detailed Q&A.

The formal lectures will typically be brief (30-60 minutes) and will cover aspects of general spacecraft design. All students are expected to watch each spacecraft subsystem lecture.
