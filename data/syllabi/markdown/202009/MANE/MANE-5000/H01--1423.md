**Course Description:**

An introductory graduate course in advanced engineering mathematics. This course provides the knowledge necessary to study engineering applications consistent with our physical laws. Topics include: differential equations, linear systems, vector calculus, series solutions, and partial differential equations. The topic of probability and statistics may be covered as an optional topic.

**Course Objectives:**

- q *To impart an understanding of ordinary differential equations methods for application to several engineering disciplines.*
- q *To familiarize students with linear and Eigen-systems, vector space, and vector calculus as mathematical models for engineering applications**.*
- q *To provide an understanding of concepts and applications of partial differential equations.*

**Learning Outcomes:**

*By the end of the course, students will be able to:*

- q *Understand and solve relevant engineering application problems through use of ordinary differential equations, and linear systems.*
- q *Be able to select series solutions methodologies to address and evaluate various sets of engineering problems.*
- q *Be able to apply partial differential equation models to solve problems in several engineering disciplines*

**Syllabus Appendix:**

- **Organization**

Online content will be delivered through Canvas system. Each week of the Fall semester will be given a module. Every module will be of the same forma, as follows:

(1) Reading assignment (text reading and Module class notes),

(2) Discussion statement (to be addressed and replied, with 1 minimum reply),

(3) Assignments (conceptual questions and homework)

The reading session will include: Module class notes with text chapter sessions to further your understanding of the subject;

The Discussion session will include: an address part/gate which will require answering with simple, one or two sentences, or even a small paragraph; followed by a reply gate to others, with 1 reply minimum for class participation credit.

The assignment will include: conceptual questions and homework;

Lastly, “Ask the Instructor” is an optional discussion board where students can ask questions. Discussions will be open for all students to see and participate.

- **Assignments: Conceptual questions and Homework**

Conceptual questions and homework will be submitted online via Canvas weekly. Both questions and homework answers should be submitted as a pdf document with the following naming convention:

**LastName_FirstName_Discussion_X.pdf**

**LastName_FirstName_Homework_X.pdf**

where X stands for module number (i.e., X=1 for module 1, X=2 for module 2 etc.) Any scanned documents should be correctly oriented and easily read.

- **Discussions Board ( Address and Reply gates )**

Discussion board interactive questions will be found in the “Discussion” folder. Discussion boards will be used to facilitate student-student communication as well as student-instructor communication. It is expected that every student participates in these group discussions. Your participation in the discussion boards will be a major factor in determining your class participation grade, which is 30% of your final grade. The discussion address gate amounts to 15%; whereas, the discussion reply gate amounts to 15%, for a total of 30% for a completed discussion. Remember, the reply gate requires 1 reply minimum for credit.

Participation in discussions may come in many forms. Examples of participation may include, but are not limited to:

- raising a question
- answering the question of a classmate
- explaining a step required to solve the problem
- explaining the process for solving the problem
- commenting on the post (agreeing, disagreeing, adding to, etc.) of a classmate
- guiding your classmates to the proper equations or section of the textbook
- providing a numerical answer to **a part** of the problem
- providing an expression necessary for solving the problem
- providing a schematic to add clarity to the problem
- Simple interactions like: “I agree“ or similar will not be sufficient and will not be considered as relevant participation in discussion

Rules for posting:

- 1\) You may not post a complete solution to the problem, even if it contains only variables.
- 2\) Simple interactions like: “I agree “ or similar will not be sufficient and will not be considered as relevant participation in discussion.
- 3\) Extra postings which are not related to the subject matter are totally prohibited (examples may include propaganda, personal views/comments not related to the subject matter).

Discussion boards will be used to facilitate student-student communication and exposure to different perspectives. The format of each discussion board question will vary. My expectations for your responses (content and length) will be explained for each discussion board. In general, responses should adhere to all standard writing, grammar, and punctuation rules. Abbreviations or “lingo” commonly used in texting is not acceptable

Discussion board questions will not have a single “correct” answer and may intentionally be left vague (open) to allow for variety in the responses. In some discussion board questions, I will try to better understand how you are thinking about the material. Generally speaking, the instructor will provide the path for the best solution coming from different points of view. Thus, you will not lose points for wrong answers. You will only lose points if you do not put forth a good faith effort or ignore the instructions. Otherwise, **I encourage you to think and express yourself freely.**

Your class participation grade will be based on the level and quality of involvement in the class. The goal for the class is that, through participation, students not only have their own learning experience, but can see and be part of the learning experiences of their classmates. **Therefore, I would like to see everyone participate in online discussions, promote a positive learning environment, and work together with your classmates.** Much of this participation will occur in the discussion boards, pseudo office hours, and/or face-to-face sessions. In general, if students actively participate in these activities, they will receive 30%. However, I reserve the right to give less than 30% for students who are blatantly apathetic to discussions and/or the group nature of the class.

- **Projects ( Mid and End-of-Semester )**

To maximize the effectiveness, two projects will be take home assignments to be completed per deadlines. These projects are to be written independently. You are free to use your notes, textbook, and class materials

**Academic Integrity and Rules of Behavior**

Any form of cheating will result in an F for the test. Both types of violations will be reported immediately to the Dean of Students Office.

I do not expect that we will incur any issues with academic integrity. With that being said, if such issues do arise, the proper measures, laid out in the student handbook and discussed in Section I, will be taken.

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust, and this trust is especially critical for blended classes. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments which students turn in are their own effort. Acts which violate this trust, undermine the educational process. An extensive and general discussion of this subject is contained in the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and Graduate Student supplement to the handbook.

Evidence of copying another person’s work is considered a serious violation of professional and academic ethics. Violations will be reported immediately to the Dean of Students Office.

**A special note regarding the use of solution manuals:** The use of solution manuals to solve quiz, HW, or test problems is prohibited. Using solutions manuals is considered plagiarism and/or copying and is in direct violation of the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities, as well as the Graduate Student supplement to the handbook. All quizzes and assignments showing evidence of the use of solution manuals will receive a zero and, under my discretion, may also be reported to the Dean of Students Office.

**Buckley Amendment**

The Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (Buckley Amendment) guarantees privacy to students, particularly in the areas of grades and performance.
