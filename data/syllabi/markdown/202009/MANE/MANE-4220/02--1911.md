## Course Goals

### Goals:

This is a Capstone class. There would be 3 presentations and 3 reports due for different stages of your innovation viz. Concept, EIS (Engineering Innovation for Society), and Final. Templates are provided. Emphasis would be on engineering content, and analysis and synthesis of information collected. Throughout the course students would learn and practice a few “Ideation & Innovation” (I&I) tools, as well as select “tools” from each of the 6 phases of “ Lean Design for Six Sigma – Ideation, Rapid Prototyping & Pilot Manufacturing Innovation” (Lean DFSS-IRPPMI). Ideation Tools are listed as IT. Complete Exercises in Sketch Book mentioned in class. Complete Inventor’s notebook.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Weeks 1-2: Introduction, Syllabus, Grading Criteria, Review Inventor’s Studio 1 / IED tools and processes, Practice Ideation Tools, Leadership Styles, Overview of Patents, Copyright etc.

Review 5 I&I tools. Lean DFSS-IRPPMI process- Define phase – 10 questions, 100 questions. Start ideation for individual innovation. Overview of Additive Manufacturing. IT1: Day in the Life Of. IT2: I wish, IT3: Backward Imaging IT 4: Snowballing HW1: 1 problem definition and 1 solution. Overview of Human Centered Design process. Review of 5 Technologies of the future.

Weeks 3-6: EIS 4 week blitz – Team project, Global Challenges, etc. EIS Rubric, Engineering Ethics, etc. EIS

Review additional 5 I&I tools. Practice Lean DFSS IRPPMI process - Gather & Analyze phases – Trade off Matrix (Pugh Matrix), Failure Modes & Effects Analysis (FMEA) & Quality Function Deployment (QFD). Continue developing individual innovation. Guest lectures. Define rapid prototyping steps for your innovation. Innovation Concept presentation and report submission. Overview of Team forming for Innovation Project teams - Approval, Resource, Members & Informed (ARMI) and Goals, Resources, Process & Interdependencies (GRPI) tools. SWOT analysis – Strength, Weakness, Opportunity, Threat analysis.

Work on global challenges topic in a 4 week blitz. Work in diverse team to come up with an engineering solution to a complex global problem. Student teams learn to write and submit a technical paper for MANE Student journal. Also student teams present a poster to a diverse audience at the end of 4 weeks, along with a prototype of a system/sub-system of their solution. This simulates a real life situation for engineering graduates.

Discussion on Engineering ethics, and how ethics is considered in all phases of engineering solution development.

1\) An ability to identify, formulate and solve complex engineering problems by applying principles of engineering, science and mathematics.  
a) Future technologies HW

c) Concept innovation design  
d) Final innovation design

a) EIS blitz

a) Ad hoc presentation in class while practicing tools.

c) EIS Journal paper. Written communication via Journal paper.  
d) Fill Innovation disclosure form to submit for provisional patent for review by patent disclosure examiners.

4\) An ability to recognize ethical and professional responsibilities in engineering situations and make informed judgments, which must consider the impact of engineering solutions in global, economic, environmental, and societal contexts.  
a) Integration of PD 3 with Inventor's Studio 2 content. Modules on ethical and professional responsibilities are brought up in all mentoring sessions.  
b) Specific content on Engineering ethics and professional responsibilities covered in class.  
c) EIS Blitz rubric has criteria on ethics and standards etc.

5\) An ability to function effectively on a team whose members together provide leadership, create a collaborative and inclusive environment.  
a) EIS Blitz  
b) PD 3 content integrated with entire Inventor's Studio 2 course.

6\) An ability to develop and conduct appropriate experimentation, analyze and interpret data, and use engineering judgment to draw conclusions  
a) EIS Blitz system design and prototype design and build.  
b) Concept innovation system design and prototype design and build.  
c) Final innovation system design and prototype design and build.

7\) An ability to acquire and apply new knowledge as needed, using appropriate learning strategies.  
a) Future technologies HW  
b) EIS blitz "Mindful Reflection" section  
c) Providing feedback to peers in "Discussion" board for Future technologies, EIS Journal paper and concept innovation paper etc.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
