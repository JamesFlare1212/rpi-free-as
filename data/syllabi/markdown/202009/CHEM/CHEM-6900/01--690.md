## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Course Objectives:**

The objective of this course is to expose students to current trends in chemical research as well as provide important professional development content through weekly seminars given by experts in the field. The course will also provide a forum for current graduate students in the Dept. of Chemistry & Chemical Biology to present their own research findings as part of the requirements for the Ph.D. degree.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Chemistry Ph.D. candidates in their first and second year and other students registered in the class must attend scheduled weekly seminars live (either in person or online). Attendance will be taken at each meeting. Students who miss more than two (2) seminars will not receive a passing grade. Since all of the seminars will be attended virtually this semester, we want to promote engagement and discussion with the speakers. Therefore, all registered students will be required to have their web cameras on throughout each seminar.

Furthermore, some of the virtual seminars will be run similar to an “Ask Me Anything” (AMA). In these cases, the speaker will provide us with one or two papers on the work they will discuss. Students should thoroughly read the papers and submit (by noon on Tuesday):

- 3 bullet points on the implications of the work presented
- 3 bullet-point scientific questions that you had while reading the studies.

These seminars will have ample time for discussion of the presentation and papers. Students are encouraged to ask questions they submitted or new ones that came up. These seminar speakers will often have additional interactions with students (e.g., lunch-time meetings to discuss career paths, etc.) that all will be invited to attend.

Additionally, Ph.D. candidates in the Dept. of Chemistry and Chemical Biology must present a seminar on their thesis material during their third or fourth year in the graduate program. If you are nearing your defense (within a year) and you have not given a departmental seminar, contact the course coordinators to schedule a seminar date.

**Grading Criteria and Breakdown:**

This course is graded as Satisfactory or Unsatisfactory based on attendance at weekly seminars. Missing more than two of the live seminars will result in an “Unsatisfactory” grade for the semester.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

The *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. In this course, the primary concerns for academic honesty is in each student’s graduate research presentation. In doing so, students must present their own work, in the context of properly cited foundations from the scientific literature and collaborative efforts. In addition, students must exhibit professional participation and respectful behavior during seminars. This should include preparation for seminars by reviewing relevant literature. This will foster valuable student participation, including asking questions of the speakers.
