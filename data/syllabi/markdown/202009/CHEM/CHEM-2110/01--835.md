## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Course Goals / Objectives:** This course presents principles of equilibrium chemistry (acid-base and redox equilibria, nonideal equilibrium, etc.) as well as introductory concepts important to quantitative chemical analysis and analytical chemistry. This course will also cover statistical analysis, classification and propagation of error, and various calibration methods. It also deals with basic electrochemistry, electro-analytical, and spectroscopic methods of quantitative analysis.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate a proficient knowledge of acid-base and redox equilibrium chemistry
- Distinguish between different measures of performance in analytical measurements such as sensitivity, detectability, accuracy, and precision.
- Explain the basic principles of and approach to performing spectroscopic and electrochemical measurements
- Effectively analyze and process data with various statistical methods

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance Policy:** Live attendance of online lectures is expected. Since exams are based on material presented in lectures, attendance is necessary to know what material has been covered and to better understand the presented information. Students can join the lectures via WebEx though a link in the LMS. Students should have their web cameras turned on throughout the class period.

### Other Course Policies:

**Exams:** A total of four exams will be given during the semester (three in-class exams and a final exam). Students will have 50 minutes to complete the in-class exams and the online final exam. Exams will be administered through SaplingPlus (and can be accessed through the LMS). The in-class exams will not necessarily be comprehensive, but understanding of earlier material will often be necessary as concepts in this course will build upon each other. The instructor reserves the right to change this policy if certain key concepts are not being fully understood by the class. The final exam will be comprehensive.

**Make-up Exams:** Make-up exams are available only to those students who both (i) miss an exam for legitimate reasons (e.g., personal illness, death of a family member, or participation in a sanctioned University event), AND (ii) provide acceptable documentation. A valid medical excuse is a letter or note from a physician which specifically excuses you from attending classes. If you miss an exam, it is your responsibility to inform the professor ***within 24 hours,*** via email, of the reasons for missing the exam, preferably prior to the exam.

**Calculator:** Only ***non-graphing*** calculators will be permitted during exams (scientific calculators are acceptable).

**Homework:** There will be one homework assignment each week throughout the semester that will be due at the beginning of each week. Homework assignments will be managed through SaplingPlus and can be accessed directly through the LMS (Blackboard Learn); students will have about 1 week to complete each assignment. The instructor reserves the right to alter this homework policy during the course of the semester. Late homework assignments will NOT be accepted without a legitimate excuse.

**In-class Worksheets and Pre-class Activities:** A significant amount of this course involves detailed calculations (e.g., non-linear equilibria, statistical analysis), which are best learned through experience. Therefore, there will be regular in-class worksheets and out-of-class activities that will allow students to practice these skills prior to homework and exams. **Note**: worksheets will NOT be thoroughly graded, but checked for completeness.

The “LearningCurve” pre-class activity is an adaptive game-like quiz system that helps students practice skills and recognize areas where more emphasis is needed. Students answer multiple-choice questions to increase their score trying to reach a target score. Students must reach the target score by the due date to receive full credit for the assignment.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

This course will utilize a ***zero-tolerance policy*** for any form of cheating or other academic misconduct. University policy deals with the problem of academic dishonesty, cheating, and plagiarism. None of these will be tolerated in this class. The sanctions provided in this policy will be used to deal with any violations.

Academic dishonesty includes, but is not limited to, copying material from another student, use of written notes or internet resource during an exam, changing answers on an exam paper after the paper has been graded, etc. Any student caught cheating will be assigned an F for the course. Furthermore violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the Dean of Students.

If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ***ask*** for clarification.
