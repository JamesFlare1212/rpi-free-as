## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Discrete Event Simulation is a ***design*** tool for industrial and service systems. It builds detailed computer models that mimic the operations of real systems, so that the designer can “play out” ***design alternatives*** on the models and analyze their performances before constructing or altering the real systems. Performance data are collected from the simulation to identify the optimal design through rigorous statistical analysis. In this sense, simulation is to design of enterprise systems as the CAD method is to the design of physical parts/products. Unlike many other IME methods that focus on obtaining analytical results in closed form, a simulation model is developed to reveal the complex nature of enterprise ***operations***, thus it has to include realistic details of the target systems. This focus on realism rather than simplification makes it a unique tool for enterprise systems analysis and design. This course adopts a ***studio mode*** of learning to develop your modeling skills from hands-on exercises, while gaining proficiency in one of the leading industrial software programs, ***Arena***. Attendance is required where possible, and self-efficacy is emphasized. A studio consists of three basic learning processes: 1. the student studies the pertinent studio models in the designated Episode and does his/her best to reproduce the models before the online classes (homework); 2. the instructor reviews the concepts, techniques and subtle details of these studio models and demonstrates their execution (online class); and 3. the student finalizes these models and finishes the studio report as required (online class). The third process may continue after the online class if necessary (homework). Each studio is an intensive modeling laboratory in which the instructor coaches and the student practices to develop a rewarding learning experience through self-efficacy.

The student should also explore the learning resources provided in the textbook and Arena web sites. Visit www.mhhe.com/kelton (also at http://arenasimulation.com/arena_Home.aspx) to install, learn, and operate the free student version of Arena software. A rich library of user manuals, examples, and technical documentation pertaining to both the concepts and techniques of simulation with Arena is included in the software. The instructor will build the required intellectual foundations for you, but cannot provide any technical support for the software since he does not possess any such means. However, many students around the world have used ARENA without hand-holding.

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

**The Remote Mode of instruction** *This class is taught in the **remote mode**. Since it adopts the studio pedagogy, please read the following explanation carefully in its entirety to understand how the pedagogy works.*

Approximately 10 studios will be taught. Each studio teaches you a particular case of simulation study, complete with Arena models and analyses. All Arena models in the studios are completely documented in the posted class notes, **Episode I to Episode VIII**, suitable for self-efficacy learning. These Episodes are available from **YouTube** as well as **Blackboard/LMS**. You are required to reproduce, implement and analyze these models for each studio.

A studio includes three components: 1. Your pre-class study of the studio models and self-efficacy effort to create them according to the instructions in the Episode, **before each studio** **starts**. 2. The regular classes where the instructor lectures on the models and reviews your modeling work. 3. Your written report about your modeling work that the studio requires. You should treat the first and the third components as **homework assignments** to be completed outside the regular classes.

**Remote classes** will be conducted via WebEx Teams, which you should download it before school starts. The software is available at https://www.webex.com/downloads.html. Download it using your RPI credentials (email address, RCS name, and password). Enter the Space named ISYE 4290/6620 Discrete Event Simulation. Based on the Institute’s policies, the CDC guidelines, and the viral situation at the time, the instructor may decide to turn some of the review classes into **in-person classes**. All in-person classes, if any, will be announced at least one class ahead of the class time, by the instructor via email, including detailed instructions. Those not able to attend can watch recordings at the LMS/Blackboard site.

The **social distancing rules** for in-person classes follow strictly the Institute policy. Each in-person class, if used, will be split into two 50 minutes sessions. You will be assigned to one of these two groups according to the alphabetic order of your surname. The first group will attend the first session, and the second group the second session. You are expected to work on the review materials at a suitable place during the other class session that you are not assigned. Students must wear a **face mask** and observe the Institute regulations on social distancing.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

1. This course adopts an **honor system**.

(1) Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust

that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of

the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments which

students turn in are theirs. Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational

process and damage its purpose. Rensselaer places great importance on the integrity of its students and faculty. Faculty commit to fairness and care in the grading of assignments. Students commit to honesty in submission of graded assignments. Together, these commitments insure that grades on Rensselaer transcripts are an accurate assessment of student accomplishment. You are asked to sign an honor pledge on the final exam, quizzes, and all other assignments of the studios that require individual works, as designated by the instructor.

(2) What constitutes "cheating?" ***You are encouraged to learn from your peers.*** However, the contents of all studio models and reports must be developed, created and written by you alone. The assistance provided by your peers must be restricted to the learning of concepts and techniques, short of actual implementation. If any person(s) assisted you in producing any part of the results that you submit for grading, then you must clearly credit such part(s) to such person(s). The same requirement applies to any sources that you used. ***Violations, such as copying any piece of work from any other person’s work, or having other person(s) work out results that you submit as your own for credit, constitute plagiarism.*** Plagiarism will be strictly prosecuted as cheating according to the Institute policy.

(3) The attendance of classes is required. However, this is adjustable by your actual medical, residence, and family conditions. If your conditions dictate that you must miss a class, or you must take the classes on an asynchronous basis, then you could use the posted PPT files and videos to catch up your coursework and prepare the written studio report. The final exam requires you to include a signed written statement: “On my honor, I pledge that I have neither given nor received assistance during the exam.” The exam will be emailed to you at the official exam time and you must complete it and return it in a digital form by email to the instructor within the allotted exam time. The clock time for starting and ending the exam is adjusted by you according to your time zone.

1. ***Off-Class work at home using the posted Episodes to build your own models is required.*** The studio pedagogy, or learning by hands-on examples, is the best way to learn the art and science of simulation. The instructor has completely revised the class notes to help you reproduce the class models for each studio. You should do your best to build the assigned models before each class that teaches/reviews them, and then complete the modeling work to make the models run correctly during the class, where the instructor discusses the concepts and subtle details of the model. Then, you should follow up with writing a complete studio report that you will submit to the instructor via email. You should use the posted Episodes as a “TA” to make up any missed work. The Episodes have been shown to be sufficient to guide a serious student reproducing the models on a self-efficacy basis.
