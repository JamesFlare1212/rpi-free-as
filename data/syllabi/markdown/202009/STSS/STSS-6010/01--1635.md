## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

I will be providing all texts via our course LMS. Some suggestions for “Further Readings” are listed for some weeks, and might be interesting “forward reading" suggestions, though are not required.  
In addition to the assigned reading, you are asked to obtain and read at least one of the “Introductions” to the field of STS listed below which, you will notice, are all written by white male authors. I therefore also ask you to obtain and read one of the edited volumes that add an additional layer of critique to “classic” STS approaches, also listed below.  
Introductions to STS:  
• Massimiano Bucci (2004). Science in Society: An Introduction to Social Studies of Science.  
• Harry M. Collins and Trevor Pinch (1998). The Golem: What You Should Know about Science.  
• David Hess (1997). Science Studies: An Advanced Introduction.  
• Sergio Sismondo (2003). An Introduction to Science and Technology Studies.  
Making STS Bigger (some readers/edited volumes/special issues):  
• Ruha Benjamin, editor (2019). Captivating Technology: Race, Carceral Technoscience, and Liberatory Imagination in Everyday Life.  
• Cyd Cipolla, Kristina Gupta, David A. Rubin, and Angela Willey, editors (2017). Queer Feminist Science Studies: A Reader.  
• Kelly Fritsch, Aimi Hamraie, Mara Mills, and David Serlin, editors (2019). Special Section of Catalyst 5 (1), on Crip Technoscience.  
• Sandra Harding, editor (2011). The Postcolonial Science and Technology Studies Reader.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Analyze and challenge commonsense ideas about everyday concepts such as “science”, “objectivity”, and “nature”.
- Describe the scope of the broad and diverse intellectual terrain of Science and Technology Studies.
- Identify and trace out the scholarly conversations in the field to understand the context of selected readings.
- Write and present a lecture and lead a discussion on concepts in the field of STS.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty. If found in violation of the academic honesty policy, students may be subject to two types of penalty. The instructor administers an academic [grade] penalty and the student is reported to the Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education as appropriate. The first violation results in 0 grade for that assignment. The second violation results in failure of the course. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
