## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Science, Technology and Society (STS) is a multidisciplinary social scientific and humanistic field that examines science and technology in their social, cultural, and political contexts. This course will cover central ideas in STS, and invite you to experiment with a wide variety of conceptual tools. You will apply these tools to real-world situations, in order to better understand the politics of science and technology.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Ursula K. Leguin’s The Lathe of Heaven (1971).

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students will learn to:

1. Employ concepts from science and technology studies to describe science and technology in their social, cultural, and political contexts.
2. Critically engage with academic and non-academic writing on science and technology, evaluating reasoning and assumptions.
3. Communicate about complex social issues relevant to science and technology in a way that engages multiple perspectives and respects others’ beliefs.
4. Reflect on their own perspectives on science and technology, understanding the unique limitations and insights of their social vantage points.
5. Identify, interrogate, and assert their own political, moral, and ethical values as they relate to science and technology, and discuss how these values relate to current events.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- Supportive participation (20%): Students will show up, pay attention, and contribute thoughts and questions about course materials. While engaging critically with course concepts is a great way to participate, supporting your classmates and making the classroom more comfortable for everyone will also count as good participation.
- In-class writing (10%): Students will write or draw brief answers to prompts given during some lectures.
- Queries (20%): For Fridays, students will prepare a question or comment based on the readings. Students will share these questions and comments on LMS by 8PM on Thursday night, so that instructors have a chance to read and reflect on everyone’s queries.
- Presentation and essay (20%): At the beginning of the semester, students will select one Friday to lead discussion. Students will work in groups to present their responses to a given prompt that relates the reading to contemporary issues (10 points). The following week, students will turn in a 1,000-word essay that reflects on other students’ ideas from the presentation and discussion (10 points).
- Midterm exam (15%): The midterm will be a take-home (keep home?) essay test. You will choose from a set of essay questions pertaining to the readings and class discussions.
- COVID-19 Diaries (15%): Throughout the semester, students will observe the ways that science, technology, and medicine shape their lives during this phase of the COVID-19 pandemic. At the end of the semester, students will work together to develop a social account of COVID-19 as a scientific, technological, and public health problem.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance: If you are unable to attend a class section, you may make up for it without penalty by completing the steps outlined under “asynchronous participation” (listening to class recordings and posting on Webex Teams (for discussion sections) before the following class period). If you do not make up for a missed class session, this will count as an absence. You may miss two lectures, or one lecture and one discussion section without having to make up for missed participation.

Absences count as excused if you have an official note, or if we talk about it and it seems like a good reason. Absences count as unexcused if you do not talk to me about it, or if you don’t have a good reason. If you have more than two excused absences, we can talk about how best to make up for missed participation. If you have more than two unexcused absences, each unexcused absence will result in a reduction in your overall grade (5% for sections, 2% for lectures). Please talk to me if anything is making it difficult to participate; I would much rather you make up for missed participation, rather than take points off your final grade. With both excused and unexcused absences, you will still be responsible for making up in-class exercises.

### Other Course Policies:

Course structure: This course will be available entirely online, with both synchronous and asynchronous ways to participate. In addition, there will be three optional in-person meetings spread across the semester. The online portions will take place on the following platforms:

- Blackboard LMS: All readings and resources will be available on LMS, aside from Lathe of Heaven. Students will post queries on LMS discussion boards, and use LMS to submit some assignments.
- Webex Meetings: Lectures and sections will be held in Meetings. Links to lecture and sections will be available on LMS and in this document. You can join a meeting in most browsers, though it will be easier if you download the application.

- Webex Teams: You will join two Teams spaces: one for the full class and one for your section. These spaces will be used for announcements and clarifications.

- Google Drive: Some important class documents (syllabus, schedule, etc) will be housed on Drive. You will also use Drive for collaborating with classmates on the COVID-19 Diaries assignment.

There will be assigned readings that everyone will read before Tuesday and Friday classes. Tuesdays we will meet as a full class and there will be a lecture from one or more instructors. There will be no assignments due on Tuesdays. For Friday classes, students will post queries on the LMS discussion board. Each Friday, up to four students in each section will prepare a brief presentation that builds on the reading and poses thoughtful discussion questions. On Fridays, there will be student presentations and a discussion led by the section instructor.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

Asynchronous participation: Synchronous participation is strongly recommended; it helps to create space for connection, communication, and mutual understanding. In general, the more perspectives in a classroom, the more interesting the discussion will be. However, I recognize that many students are attending from a different time zone, in which case synchronous participation will be difficult.

Asynchronous participation is possible if necessary. For Tuesday classes, watching the recorded lecture is sufficient for asynchronous participation, though you will also be responsible for any in-class exercises that happened that day. I would also recommend posting questions and comments in your section’s Teams space, in order to shape the direction of class conversations. However, this is not necessary. For Friday classes, asynchronous participation includes (1) listening to the recorded presentations and discussion and (2) posting a question or comment in your section’s Teams space. If you do both of these things before the following class session, you will not be counted as absent.

If you are going to miss a few classes, participating asynchronously can allow you to make up for missed material. Beyond three missed classes, please let me know what is going on. If you will never (or rarely) be able to attend class sessions—due to time zones or other reasons—that is totally fine, but please let me know at the start of the semester. You will need to record your presentation, and find ways to communicate with group members for group assignments.

Technology: The online nature of this course means that we will be reliant on a variety of technologies. For this course, you will need: a computer, high-speed internet access, and an updated web browser. If you will have a difficult time accessing any of these at any point in the semester, please let me know and we’ll work together to develop accommodations.

Statement of professorial goodwill: Your instructor wants you to do well in this class. If you ever have any problems in this class, never be afraid to talk to your instructor about it. We will work with you to figure out a way through any issues that might come up.
