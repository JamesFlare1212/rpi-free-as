## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Recall and analyze the history of practice and profession of medicine, public health, disease, historical memory, education, the state, policy, institutions, and race, class, and gender in the history of medicine from the 18th century through the present
- Historically interpret the practice and profession of medicine
- Develop familiarity with prominent historical debates and problems surrounding the history of the practice and profession of medicine
- Construct and defend a position on major historical debates and problems in the history of medicine
- Compose compelling interpretive analyses of historical literature, debates, and ideas

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students are expected to come to each class prepared to discuss the assigned readings. The class will run as a seminar. The assigned readings for each day will serve as the basis of our class discussion. Daily participation is necessary in order to achieve a good participation grade. Please see me early in the semester if you have any concerns about your participation or ability to participate. Students are permitted *two absences* before their participation grade is affected. More than two absences will negatively impact the participation grade. The goal of the seminar is to create a collegial environment in which all participants feel comfortable offering a contribution and asking critical questions of the readings and one another.

In addition to daily participation in seminar, students will lead one or more sections (dependent upon course enrollment) over the semester by preparing a brief presentation (approximately 15 minutes) of the assigned readings for that day, and submittinga 500 word written analysis of that day’s readings (about two pages) in preparation for the final paper. The presenter for the day will proceed with a series of questions to facilitate class discussion of the readings. Following the student-led portion of the class we will take a brief break and will resume discussion of the readings.

Also in preparation for the final paper, students will submit two analysis papers over the course of the semester. These will be a minimum of 750 words each (about three pages) and will respond to a prompt that requires students to make an interpretive argument about the history of medicine based on one of the books we will read this semester.

For the final paper, students will prepare a 2000 word analysis (about eight to ten pages) that will analyze a problem or debate in the history of medicine by marshaling the reading completing during the semester. This paper is an opportunity to put forward a unique and original interpretation of the history of medicine.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

Intellectual integrity and credibility are the foundation of all academic work. A violation of Academic Integrity policy is, by definition, considered a flagrant offense to the educational process. It is taken seriously by students, faculty, and Rensselaer and will be addressed in an effective manner.

Violations of academic integrity include, but are not limited to: academic fraud, collaboration, copying, cribbing, fabrication, plagiarism, sabotage, and substitution. Please see the [Rensselaer Student Handbook](http://doso.rpi.edu/update.do) for further explanation of the university’s policy on academic integrity.

If found in violation of the academic honesty policy, students may be subject to two types of penalty. The instructor administers an academic [grade] penalty and the student is reported to the Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education as appropriate. The first violation results in 0 grade for that assignment. The second violation results in failure of the course and request for disciplinary action.

All students will be required to complete an online tutorial on academic dishonesty. Students are required to submit the signed contract found at the end of the syllabus stating that they have completed the tutorial, have read the Rensselaer Student Handbook on academic integrity, and promise to abide by the institute’s policy.

If you have any question concerning this policy, please ask for clarification before submitting your work.
