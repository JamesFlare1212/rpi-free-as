## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course surveys the history of the United States from the colonial era through the present. The course introduces major themes and tensions in United States politics, society, and culture. Topics will include encounters between American Indians and colonial peoples, independence, the formation of the American government, slavery, immigration, citizenship, rights, social movements,colonialism, war, and the changing identity of the United States in the world.

Through lecture and writing, students are encouraged to think critically about the historical process, historical narrative and memory, and will be invited to explore history from the perspective or primary sources rather than secondary interpretations of historical evidence.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

First Primary Analysis: 20%

Midterm Exam: 20%

Second Primary Analysis: 20%

Final Exam: 20%

Participation: 20%

You will be required to complete an online module in LMS for each class period. The modules will consist of either a recorded or composed lecture, a brief comprehension test for you to gauge your retention of the material, and discussion questions based on the day’s assigned readings. Students must complete the module and comprehension test, and answer the reading questions in LMS order to receive participation for that day. Participation is mandatory and graded. (The score you receive on the comprehension tests will not be counted toward your participation grade, but are there for your own reference. Only uncompleted quizzes will count against you.) Students are strongly encouraged to complete the module and questions during the scheduled class time and are required to complete them by the following Sunday at midnight, Eastern standard time. *Each week, the modules for that week will be released on Monday. You will have until the following Sunday to take notes and complete them before the become* *un**available.* Uncompleted LMS modules will be counted as an absence. Students are permitted *two absences* before their participation grade is affected.

There will be an online written midterm exam at the middle of the semester as well an online (non-cumulative) final exam to be held on the last day of class. The exams will include identification, short answer, and essay sections. Both exams will be open-note, however the exams will be timed and students are strongly encouraged to have a strong command of the course material and identifications before beginning the exams. The exams will be timed and you will have 48 hours to complete them once they have been posted. Lists of exam identifications will be posted to LMS at the start of the semester. These will serve as your study guides for the exams and you are strongly encouraged to reference them as you complete the online modules. Each exam will be checked to verify that no part of the exam has been plagiarized from other sources. Plagiarism results in a failing grade for the assignment and you will be held accountable for ensuring that your ideas and writing are entirely your own.

In addition to the exams, students will be required to submit two primary source analyses of three to four pages in length that offer in-depth original analyses of a selection of the required reading for the class. The instructions for the analyses are available on LMS.

All written work is to be submitted by the end-of-day Eastern standard time on the due date. Late work will be accepted but will be marked down by one-third of a letter grade for each day that it is late. (For example, an “A” paper submitted three days late will receive a “B.”)

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

Intellectual integrity and credibility are the foundation of all academic work. A violation of Academic Integrity policy is, by definition, considered a flagrant offense to the educational process. It is taken seriously by students, faculty, and Rensselaer and will be addressed in an effective manner.

Violations of academic integrity include, but are not limited to: academic fraud, collaboration, copying, cribbing, fabrication, plagiarism, sabotage, and substitution. Please see the [Rensselaer Student Handbook](http://doso.rpi.edu/update.do) for further explanation of the university’s policy on academic integrity.

If found in violation of the academic honesty policy, students may be subject to two types of penalty. The instructor administers an academic [grade] penalty and the student is reported to the Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education as appropriate. The first violation results in 0 grade for that assignment. The second violation results in failure of the course and request for disciplinary action.

All students will be required to complete an online tutorial on academic dishonesty. Students are required to submit the signed contract found at the end of the syllabus stating that they have completed the tutorial, have read the Rensselaer Student Handbook on academic integrity, and promise to abide by the institute’s policy. This form may be found in LMS and is due no later than Friday, June 5 to [sohask@rpi.edu](mailto:sohask@rpi.edu). It may be signed electronically.

If you have any question concerning this policy, please ask for clarification *before* submitting your work.
