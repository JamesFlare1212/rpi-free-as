## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Advancing environmental sustainability depends on many kinds of expertise. In this course, students will explore the evolving terrain of “green” jobs and diverse sustainability-oriented career pathways that leverage the expertise they have developed through their undergraduate studies. Simultaneously, we will question what “green” means, critique the limits of “sustainability” discourse, challenge the injustices that are woven into contemporary “environmentalism”, examine our assumptions around “careers”, and question our notions of “success”. We will situate all of these terms in broader contexts and examine their histories, connotations, and connections.

This course provides students the opportunity to conduct sustained research that examines where sustainability-oriented work is being done and how people with varied expertise are making a contribution. Students will practice applying their expertise and interests to real-world situations. We will have many guests throughout the course of the semester and students will grow their networks and practice communication and coordination skills by doing much of the outreach to invite these guests. Students in this class will work together to compile resources that can be helpful for other students seeking careers in sustainability.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students will learn to:

1. Demonstrate understanding of sustainability concepts and analyze sustainability problems to inform practical action.
2. Describe and evaluate the roles of diverse organizations and domains in which work for sustainability takes place.
3. Describe and evaluate the diverse skills and forms of expertise that are needed to address key sustainability challenges.
4. Identify their personal strengths, values, and priorities in order to pursue appropriate and desirable career pathways.
5. Effectively use a variety of sources to investigate employment opportunities and skillfully prepare job applications, cover letters, resumes, grant proposals, and other career development materials.
6. Better articulate the complex forces (historical, economic, cultural, psychological, technological, scientific, etc) shaping employment and sustainability.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- Supportive Participation (20%): Your participation grade will be based on the degree to which you contribute to the production of a productive, supportive, convivial “classroom” environment. This work may happen during class-time (participating in discussion, asking questions, making jokes) or outside of class-time (sharing resources, helping classmates with work, posting discussion questions).
- Short Assignments (30%): There will be a variety of short assignments throughout the semester that will contribute to class discussions. These will be submitted via LMS. With many of these assignments, you will be asked to share selections of your work on Google Drive as well.

- Grant Proposal (20%): In partnership with a community organization, you will identify a funding opportunity and prepare a grant proposal based on the actual submission guidelines of the granting organization. Your grade will be based on seeking out a community organization and a grant (5%), writing the grant itself (10%), and your reflections on this process (5%).

- Outreach to Guests (10%): In order to make this class speak to your own interests, you will reach out to professionals working in your fields of interest and invite them to speak with the class via video chat. Your grade will be based on your outreach efforts (5%) and your reflections on this process (5%).

- Career Fair Contributions (10%): While an in-person JobFest is not in the cards this year, students will contribute to a career resources page for sustainability-oriented RPI students.

- Alumni Profiles (10%): Students will interview an alum from their degree program who is working in a sustainability field, and submit a reflection on this interview.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Asynchronous participation: Synchronous participation is strongly recommended; it helps to create space for connection, communication, and mutual understanding. In general, the more perspectives in a classroom, the more interesting the discussion will be. However, I recognize that many students are attending from a different time zone, in which case synchronous participation will be difficult.

Asynchronous participation is possible if necessary. Participating asynchronously includes (1) listening to the recorded discussion and (2) posting a question or comment before class, in response to readings and/or after class, in response to discussion. If you do both of these things before the following class session, you will not be counted as absent.

If you are going to miss a few classes, participating asynchronously can allow you to make up for missed material. Beyond three missed classes, please let me know what is going on. If you will never (or rarely) be able to attend class sessions—due to time zones or other reasons—that is totally fine, but please let me know at the start of the semester. You will need to record your presentation, and find ways to communicate with group members for group assignments.

Attendance: If you are unable to attend a class section, you may make up for it without penalty by completing the steps outlined under “asynchronous participation” (listening to class recordings and posting on Discord before the following class period). If you do not make up for a missed class session, this will count as an absence. You may miss three classes without having to make up for missed participation.

Absences count as excused if you have an official note, or if we talk about it and it seems like a good reason. Absences count as unexcused if you do not talk to me about it, or if you don’t have a good reason. If you have more than two excused absences, we can talk about how best to make up for missed participation. If you have more than two unexcused absences, each unexcused absence will result in a 3% reduction in your overall grade. Please talk to me if anything is making it difficult to participate; I would much rather you make up for missed participation, rather than take points off your final grade.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
