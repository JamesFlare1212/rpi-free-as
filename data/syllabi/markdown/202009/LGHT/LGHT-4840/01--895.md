## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand how the visual system works.
- Analyze the visual requirements of various visual tasks; make and critique lighting recommendations.
- Specify the stimulus presented to the visual system under various conditions.
- Recognize and predict lighting conditions likely to cause discomfort, generate specific impressions, and/or modify behavior.
- Conduct assessments in terms of the lighting effects on people.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Student assessment will be based upon a review of assignments, exams, and papers submitted; evaluation of the quality and preparation of any in-class presentations; and the degree to which the student participates in class discussions.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Your grade will be calculated based on the average of your grades for all of the assignments for the course, weighted as follows:

- Review Assignment = 10%
- Critique Assignment = 10%
- Laboratory Assignment = 20%
- Examination 1 = 25%
- Examination 2 = 25%
- Class Participation = 10%

In the review assignment, students will review a published article describing a human factors study, evaluate the methods and results and suggest future research. In the critique assignment, students will review and critique a published recommendation for lighting in a specific application and suggest improvements. In the laboratory assignment, students will carry out a simple human factors experiment and report on the results and implications for lighting practice. In the examinations, students will demonstrate their knowledge of course concepts by providing short answers to a broad range of questions. Class participation includes attending all class sessions and active involvement in team assignments.  
Your final grade for the course will be one of: A, A-, B+, B, B-, C+, C, C-, or F. (Undergraduate students may also receive grades of: D+, D, or D-.) You may appeal any grades given by discussing your concerns first with the course instructor and ultimately with the Head of Graduate Education and the Graduate Education Committee. Full attendance and participation in class discussions are essential parts of Class Participation. Late assignments turned in one day late will be penalized a full letter grade (e.g., an A- will be given a B-). Assignments later than one day will be assigned a grade of F; if there is an emergency please discuss with the instructor.
