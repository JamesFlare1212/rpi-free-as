## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This undergraduate course will enable students to gather information about the science of astronomy and its mathematical and physical underpinnings. After taking this class, students will be able to use basic astronomical jargon in context; perform simple physical calculations of orbital motion, the structure of stars, and the expansion of the Universe; and will be able to describe the evolution of the cosmos.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Foundations of Astrophysics, by Ryden and Peterson

## Course Goals

### Goals:

(1) Students will be able to use Kepler’s Laws to calculate the properties of planetary and binary star orbits.  
(2) Students will be able to describe the components of the Solar System  
(3) Students will be able to explain the electromagnetic spectrum, and its importance to the study of astronomy.  
(4) Students will be able to describe and to calculate the relationships between magnitude, distance, color, temperature, and the Hertzprung-Russell Diagram.  
(5) Students will be able to describe the physical processes that govern stellar evolution as a function of initial stellar mass.  
(6) Students will be able to list the types of stars, galaxies, and compact objects (e.g. - pulsars, black holes, and AGN) that have been classified, and describe their characteristics.  
(7) Students will be able to describe the formation and evolution of the Universe.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Week 1: Course Orientation, Position and Time in the Solar System  
Week 2: Kepler’s laws  
Week 3: The Earth-Moon System  
Week 4: Radiation and Matter  
Week 5: The Solar System, Exoplanets  
Week 6: Properties of stars – photometry and distances  
Week 7: Midterm exam  
Week 8: Atmospheres of stars – stellar spectra  
Week 9: The ISM, Formation and Evolution of Stars  
Week 10: Stellar Interiors  
Week 11: Stellar Remnants, Lagrange Points, and Roche Lobes  
Week 12: The Milky Way Galaxy, Galaxies, Active Galaxies, and Large Scale Structure  
Week 13: Cosmology  
Week 14: The Big Bang and the History of the Universe  
Week 15: Review

Grades are made available on LMS, with statistics for the class.

All quizzes and exams will be taken online, through LMS. They may contain sections requiring problem solving or free response that will need to be scanned and uploaded. Students may use a calculator for all exams and quizzes, including the final exam. The calculator must have nothing in its memory. You may also use a standard crib sheet. Lecture quizzes and reading quizzes are open everything and are untimed. For weekly quizzes, midterm exam, and final exam, no sources of information besides the calculator and crib sheet are allowed. These exams will be during class hours and will be randomized and timed. Students who reside in a time zone that makes class time difficult may request to take the exam/quiz at an alternate time, to be determined after a survey taken the first day of class.

Students are allowed and encouraged to converse on the homework. However, all assignments which are turned in are expected to be the individual work of the student who turns it in. For example, all of the work on the assignment must follow from the preceding data or equations, and should not require consulting another student’s methodology to understand how the solutions were derived. Scanned homework assignments will be turned in through LMS.

All work must be clear and legible. The grader will not puzzle over assignments that are poorly presented. If you feel that you have received a grade in error, see the TA (HW) or the instructor (exams, quizzes, and unresolved disagreements with the TA). Requests for re-grades must include a written explanation of what was graded incorrectly, and include the whole assignment to be re-graded. The instructor reserves the right to re-grade the entire submitted assignment.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Dishonesty:** Students who cheat will receive a failing grade for the course. Cheating includes: copying, or allowing another student to copy, a test or assignment; altering a graded test or assignment before requesting re-grading; copying submitted solutions from published solution manuals or previous years; using materials other than those that are allowed on an exam; misrepresenting the work of another group or individual as your own. It is considered cheating to help another student cheat. Because of the online format, it is unusually easy to cheat on exams and quizzes, and the expectation is that you will not consult each other or unallowed sources even though it might be easy for you to do just that.
