## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Age and origin of the Earth, internal constituents and energy sources; how plates move, oceans develop, and mountains rise. The course aims to give a quantitative picture of the Earth’s major processes and the ways in which they interact.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Read and understand graphical representations of data
- Express some evidence for the theory of plate tectonics
- Know the age of the Earth and how it is measured
- Know the structure of the Earth and some of the ways that geologists measure it
- Understand that rocks record the processes (and events) that operate on Earth
- Describe, analyze and assess the geologic features that impact your daily lives
- Participate as informed citizens in decision-making processes on issues that will affect yourself, your family, your community, country and the world

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Quizzes:** After you complete your readings and videos you will take a comprehension quiz on BlackBoard. These assignments will be mostly multiple-choice questions. You may submit as many attempts as you would like, and your grade on each assignment will be the average of all attempts. *Quizzes are due by 11:59 pm Thursday*. No late work will be accepted for these quizzes, as they are used to gage comprehension before Friday class meetings. However, the lowest quiz will be dropped at the end of the semester.

Quizzes will be available approximately 2 weeks prior to due date. It is possible that due to schedule changes, you may do readings and take a quiz that we do not discuss in class (not all learning is for exam preparations!). Nevertheless, the assignment is still connected to the material and will enhance your understanding of course concepts.

**Minute Papers:** After each Webex class, you will be given a writing prompt to respond to on BlackBoard in the form of a minute paper. A minute paper is a short (1/2 page) writing assignment designed to take no more than 5 minutes in order to determine the extent to which you comprehend important concepts from class. These are used to discover common “sticking” points and assess information that was unintentionally vague or difficult to understand—this is a low-stakes way to allow misconceptions to be addressed before you get to a high-stakes exam question. It is also an opportunity for you to evaluate what you recall and allow you to self-assess (for this reason, you should not answer these with your notes-use just what you retained and can recall). Minute papers will be graded based on effort and completion, as opposed to accuracy as follows:

- 0 points-not submitted
- 1 point-Incomplete response (not addressing prompt)
- 2 points-Partial response (addresses prompt but is unclear)
- 3 points-Full response (fully addresses prompt, and is clear)

*Minute papers are due by 11:59 pm Mondays.* One minute paper will be dropped at the end of the semester.

**Exams:** There will be 3 exams throughout the semester. Each exam will cover the topics preceding that exam (**not cumulative**-however, keep in mind that we will be building upon prior knowledge). For each exam, you will have ~ 1 week to take the exam. You can take the exam at any time that works for you within the timeframe. The exam will be timed-once you start the exam you will only have 60 minutes to complete the exam (unless you have an exam accommodation). You will need to complete each question before you can proceed to the next and you will not be able to go back and revise answers to a previously taken question. Exams should be taken independently without the use of notes, textbook or the internet. Sharing exam questions with students who have not taken the exam yet is not allowed. If this is found to happen, both students will receive a zero.

Make-up must be requested in writing **prior to the exam** and include documentation of extreme hardship.

**Project:** After the Thanksgiving break, class time will be spent applying the information you learned over the semester and applying it to learn about the geology of your town. This project will be divided into three sections:

- Part 1: Describe the geologic environment you live in (i.e. tectonic setting, depositional environments)
- Part 2: Find a rock, take a photo with it, and describe the rock in as much detail as you can (i.e. type or rock, minerals, texture, weathering)
- Part 3: Interpret the history of the rock (i.e. where was it formed? Any indication when? How did it get to where it is today? Is it aligned with current tectonic history? Consider erosion, uplift, weathering, transport, deposition)

During class meetings dedicated to working on the project, Dr. Cadieux will set up small group meetings to discuss the project with students.

You can begin working on the project anytime. More information will be provided in November.
