## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is an in-depth study of papers addressing key aspects of both ancient and modern carbon cycles. Students will read and critique primary papers, present current research articles, and lead critical discussions.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Integrate previously constrained reservoirs and fluxes of organic and inorganic carbon to explain observed variations in carbon cycling through geologic time scales.
- Interpret and synthesize carbon isotope records to explain global environmental and biogeochemical conditions (e.g., biological evolution and redox state).
- Assess the impacts of carbon cycle perturbations within the Earth System as evidenced by geologic records and explore the implications for modern climate change.
- Evaluate and compare the net effects and uncertainties associated with anthropogenic activities which alter modern organic and inorganic carbon budgets.
- Explore the combined effects of positive and negative feedback loops and propose how these may result in the net release or sequestration of carbon within the context of future climate change.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Paper presentations** – Student-led paper presentations will be *evaluated individually* using a rubric that assesses content, organization, depth of understanding, preparedness, and communication. Student-led collaborative discussion of assigned papers will be *evaluated as a group* using a rubric that assesses synthesis, facilitating discussion, preparedness and participation, and audience engagement.

**Participation** – Student audience members will complete a *pre-discussion assignment* that describes key findings, contains a global context statement, and lists questions or comments that arose from the readings. Audience member discussion contributions will be *evaluated individually* using a rubric that assesses depth of understanding and general engagement.

**Research proposal** – Students will be asked to *write a* *research* *proposal* on a carbon cycle-related topic of their choosing. The research proposal will undergo *peer review* by one of your classmates before the *final version* is due. Specific research proposal instructions and grading rubric will be shared with students early in the semester.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

**Course format** – This course consists of one class per week in which current research papers are presented and analyzed in detail. All students will contribute to the analysis and discussion of all peer-reviewed articles throughout the semester. Students will be assessed by class participation (35%), leadership of paper discussions (40%), and on the content, critique, and presentation of a written research proposal (25%). This class is intended to be a highly interactive learning course. All students are expected to read the assigned papers before class and be prepared to discuss them in detail.
