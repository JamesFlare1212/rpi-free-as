Course Overview

This course will encompass marine, freshwater, and terrestrial microfossils (with emphasis on foraminifera), including taxonomy, evolutionary history, ecology and paleoenvironments, and a broad spectrum of micropaleontological applications, such as sea-level and climate reconstructions, geochemical analyses, oil industry research, and forensic geology. This course will also cover concepts of biostratigraphy and micropaleontological applications to stratigraphic correlation and time scale construction, which are fundamental to the “deep time” nature of Earth Sciences. Related topics will include sequence stratigraphy, cyclostratigraphy, and chemostratigraphy.

Course format will include lectures and labs, with a focus on hands-on microscope work, relevant readings, and lab exercises that will familiarize students with microfossils of different ages and from a variety of paleoenvironments. Extensive hands-on microscope and labwork will be required. Several one-day field trips on weekends may be required. Fundamental overviews will include multiple phyla of marine and freshwater phytoplankton, zooplankton, micro-metazoa, pollen, benthic foraminifera, and testate amoebae; the emphasis will be on foraminifera. Detailed labwork will concentrate both on developing an understanding of the key morphological features that are used in identifying foraminifera, and on learning to recognize the species and genera that are most often used in geochemical analyses for different time periods. In addition, students will learn about the potential pitfalls to watch for, such as morphologically-similar benthic foraminiferal taxa, downslope transport, and downhole contamination. Students will be expected to become proficient in lab techniques and applied micropaleontology, and master lecture and reading material to develop the working knowledge that is necessary to interpret their labwork.

By the end of the course, students will have completed a variety of lecture and lab exercises, and will have the fundamental tools necessary to identify, describe, interpret, and use microfossils in a range of geologic applications.

Student Learning Outcomes

Students who successfully complete this course will be able to:

1\) identifty lab specimens from multiple phyla of marine and freshwater phytoplankton, zooplankton, micro-metazoa, pollen, benthic foraminifera, and testate amoebae

2\) identify key indicator foraminifera to the species and genus level, and use this ability to reconstruct paleobathymetry and paleoenvironments based on lab samples

3\) explain how microfossil assemblages are used to study and reconstruct climates and climate change through geologic history

4\) use a working knowledge of geochemical proxies in foraminifera to interpret past ocean and climate conditions, transitions, and events

5\) explain the role of microfossils in biostratigraphy, time scale construction, sequence stratigraphy, cyclostratigraphy, and chemostratigraphy.

# Honesty and Academic Integrity

As a student, you must have confidence that the instructor made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of this course, and that the instructor will make a strong effort to accommodate the varying needs of a diverse group of students. The instructor, in turn, must have confidence that the reports, assignments, and exams that you complete are your own work.

The Rensselaer Handbook defines various forms of academic dishonesty and procedures of responding to these. All forms are violations of student-teacher trust. Students should familiarize themselves with the appropriate portion of the Rensselaer Handbook and note that the penalties for plagiarism and other forms of cheating are quite harsh.

The evaluation of student performance is a service provided by Rensselaer. Attempts to undermine this service lower Rensselaer's reputation. Therefore, it is essential that academic honesty be preserved. Unless otherwise indicated, you should cooperate with one another in and outside of class on the solution of problems. You may collaborate with partners on reports *with instructor permission*. However, you may not collaborate on examinations or misrepresent another person's work as your own on examinations. You may not bring crib sheets to examinations, and you may not write on or alter examination materials that you submit for regrading. Students who violate the spirit or letter of these rules are subject to penalties as stipulated in the Rensselaer Handbook.

Penalties for violating Honesty and Academic Integrity Policy

Dishonesty on an exam will result in a 0 on that exam.

For exercises that are specified to be worked on as individuals, copying another student’s lab or field report will result in a 0 point grade for that report.

Attendance policy

Attendance is required – each missed class, laboratory, or field trip once without a legitimate reason will result in a 1% lowering of the final grade. With a legitimate excuse, you will be required to make up the class, laboratory, or field trip with an additional assignment at the discretion of the instructor.
