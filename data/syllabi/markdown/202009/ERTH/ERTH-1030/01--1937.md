## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- This course is intended to provide students who are not majoring in a science or engineering field a comprehensive and integrated introduction to the natural sciences. For our purposes, the term "natural sciences" is taken to mean the sciences of the natural world, as distinct from the man-made (or artificial) world, although it will be clear the two are related.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Grades will be determined according to the following weights:

| Class Participation | 5% |
| --- | --- |
| Homework | 20% |
| Exams | 75% |

***Class participation:***

This is an online class with "live" lectures during normal class hours. "Attendance" in class is not required - and will not be monitored on a day-to-day basis - but this class really works a lot better if everyone actively participates. Also, if you participate in class you will find the exams and homework much easier. At the same time, I understand that not all of you have great internet connections, so I can't hold you accountable for "live" interactions. Nevertheless it will always work to your advantage at the end of the semester if I have some reason to conclude that you have been taking the class seriously. Consider the "participation" percentage as a potential penalty if is appears you made litte to no effort to communicate.

NOTE: If you really do have trouble with connecting eletronically, or maybe you just are a better student in 3D, we can look into having "in-person" meetings with small groups (<3 people at a time). We need to be sure we can stay within current health protocols - at the moment I'm not sure how that will work but I'll see what I can do.

***Homework:***

There will be about 7 homework assignments during the term (roughly one every two weeks). Questions and problems will be taken from the text and other sources; their purpose will be to get you to think about material presented in lecture and give you practice in problem-solving.

Homeworks will have definitive due dates. **Late homeworks** will be penalized by subtracting 20% of the total value per day (including holidays and weekends). Thus, any homework more than 4 days late gets no credit. Note that a "day" is considered over at 5PM. If your homework is not delivered to me or in my mailbox by that time it will be docked an extra day.

***Exams:***

There will be 3 exams during the regular term (see the schedule). There is no "final" exam. The second and third exams will be cumulative: expect about 10% of the point value on the second exam to come from material covered on the first exam, and 20% of the point value on the third exam to come from material covered on the preceding two exams. The exams will include short-answer (possibly involving simple calculations, multiple-choice and true/false questions).

**Missed exams** may be retaken only if the instructor is satisfied with the reasons given by a student for being absent from the exam. If a student has an unavoidable conflict of significance which he/she knows about in advance of an examination he/she MUST notify the instructor prior to the day of the exam. In cases of emergency (e.g., health problems) the student MUST provide sufficient evidence of the emergency (e.g., a note from a doctor) to the instructor before any action will be considered. Exams will be retaken only at the discretion of the instructor.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grading weighting scheme:  
Class Participation5%  
Homework 20%  
Exams75%

How to determine your grade at any time durng the semester:  
Given the weights shown above, you can figure your standing at any point first by computing your current percentage. To do so, let

| SUMQ | be the total sum of all possible points on Homeworks/Quizzes given thus far |
| --- | --- |
| MYSUMQ | be the total sum of all points that you have accumulated on the Homeworks/Quizzes. |
| SUME | be the total sum of all possible points on Exams given thus far |
| MYSUME | be the total sum of all points that you have accumulated on the Exams. |
| SUML | be the total sum of all possible points on class partcipation. |
| MYSUML | be the total sum of all points that you have accumulated from class participation. |

Roughly, you might estimate MYSUML/SUML to be the fraction of lectures you have attended.

Then, your current percentage can be calculated from

MYPERC = 100\*[(MYSUMQ/SUMQ)\*0.50 + (MYSUME/SUME)\*0.45 + (MYSUML/SUML)\*0.05)

Your current grade is estimated from the following binning:

| **MYPERC** | **Estimated Letter Grade** |
| --- | --- |
| Greater than 95% | A |
| Between 90% and 95% | A- |
| Between 87% and 90% | B+ |
| Between 83% and 87% | B |
| Between 80% and 83% | B- |
| Between 77% and 80% | C+ |
| Between 73% and 77% | C |
| Between 70% and 73% | C- |
| Between 65% and 70% | D+ |
| Between 60% and 65% | D |
| Less than 60% | F |

The final grade will be base on a curve, and that curve *ALWAYS* lowers the cutoffs on the letter grades. Where exactly that cutoff will eventually wind up will depend on the performance of the class throughout the term (along with some consideration of the performance of previous classes), BUT you can be sure that your grade will NEVER be LOWER than what is indicated in the table above. For example, if you wind up with an 88% score, you are guaranteed a B+, but that may eventually be rounded up to an A- or even an A.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
