## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Study of hydrologic, geologic, and other factors controlling groundwater flow, occurrence, development, chemistry, and contamination. Groundwater flow theory and aquifer test methods are introduced. Interactions between surface and subsurface hydrologic systems are covered. Some field trips are possible.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course will prepare you to understand the role of groundwater in the hydrologic balance of lakes and rivers; systems for water supply, irrigation, drainage, dewatering, and remediation; assessing environmental impacts. Darcy’s Law is developed as a framework for understanding how soil properties influence transmission of water by aquifers. Darcy’s law is employed as the basis for describing flow in porous media, and direct application to flow, infiltration, flow nets, and aquifer pumping is illustrated. The incorporation of Darcy’s Law within other, more sophisticated groundwater flow models is developed, including non-steady radial flow to wells. Students will become proficient in predicting the effects of pumping on the piezometric surface and analysis of aquifer test data under ideal and non-ideal boundary conditions. The geological processes that lead to the formation of aquifers will be introduced.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

***Specific Course Topics:***

1. Describe components of the hydrologic cycle, including mechanisms of precipitation, evaporation, streamflow, and groundwater recharge, and how these mechanisms are influenced by the properties of water. Apply measurement techniques, averaging techniques, models and mass balances (hydrologic budget) to quantify components of the hydrologic cycle.
2. Apply basic concepts of fluid mechanics to flow in pipes, rivers, and porous media: discharge, continuity, hydrostatic pressure, head, Bernoulli’s Equation, Reynolds number and its use to characterize flow regimes, weir flow, Manning’s Equation, and chemical tracer techniques.
3. Distinguish confined and unconfined aquifers based on their properties, including the mechanisms of water storage and release. Apply the concept of effective stress to aquifer compressibility and the effects of pumping on subsidence. Compute aquifer soil properties including porosity and bulk density and describe the effects of packing, particle size, and particle size distribution. Compute and plot grain size distribution from raw laboratory data. Define and distinguish among total porosity, effective porosity, specific yield, and specific retention.
4. Apply the principles of potentiometric surface mapping to prepare maps and identify groundwater features such as flow direction, flow gradients, recharge areas, and discharge areas. Construct flow nets for complex geometries in homogenous isotropic media and use them to estimate flow. Apply the Bernoulli Equation to compute values of head from potentiometric maps and flow nets.
5. Describe important features of Darcy’s Law including hydraulic gradient and intrinsic permeability. Predict how fluid and media properties influence hydraulic conductivity. Estimate hydraulic conductivity using correlations and laboratory measurements (constant and falling head test). Apply Darcy’s Law to simple flow geometries, and heterogeneous (layered) media.
6. Employ the concepts of mass balance and flux to derive the groundwater flow equation in rectilinear and radial coordinates. Solve the flow equations for steady flow in confined and unconfined aquifers with and without recharge (discharge) in rectilinear and radial coordinates. Apply solutions to the unsteady groundwater flow equation in radial coordinates to compute aquifer response to pumping or recharge for confined, leaky, and unconfined aquifers. Apply the principle of superposition to model the effects of changing pumping rate, recharge and impermeable boundaries. Determine aquifer properties from drawdown data and slug test data using Theis, Jacob-Cooper, and Hvorslev methods.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Quantify components of the hydrologic cycle to assess groundwater/surface water interactions, perform hydrologic balances, and estimate groundwater contributions to surface water bodies.
- 2\. Identify and characterize aquifer properties, geologic boundaries (type of confinement), and mechanisms of water storage and release.
- 3\. Prepare groundwater maps to identify features of the potentiometric surface and quantify the distribution of head and flow in cross-section.
- 4\. Employ material balances to develop and calibrate groundwater flow models and use them to estimate flow quantities and predict potentiometric surface profiles, including aquifer response to pumping or recharge.
