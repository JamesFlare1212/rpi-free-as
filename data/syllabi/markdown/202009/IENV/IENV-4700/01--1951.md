## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

A course that focuses on the Hudson River Basin as an environmental microcosm and a vehicle through which to illustrate the natural science of river systems with particular attention to human influences. This interdisciplinary environmental science course is for environmentally oriented junior, senior, and graduate students.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

1. History and State of the Hudson: Read a range of papers on issues facing the Hudson River to understand the science and implications
2. Future work on the Hudson: Write a proposal to the Hudson River Foundation

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand basic watershed scale biogeochemistry through detailed study of the Hudson.
- Synthesize the knowledge you’ve gained in your many courses and use that knowledge to address real world environmental and natural problems
- Perform independent research on a topic in a well-organized, well-documented manner using scientific literature and popular media
- Use evidence based arguments to support your conclusions
- Further develop your ability for oral and written persuasive argument
- Practice editing and critiquing other’s work
- Deepen your understanding about challenging environmental science issues.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Paper summaries:** Each week, you will work in groups of 3-5 students to read a paper and discuss. Each group needs to create a 1-2 page paper summary by *Monday at noon*. You can choose to work together in-person (while socially distant) or virtually. Friday class periods are designed as a time for you to work with your groups to discuss your paper and compile your summary sheet. Your summary needs to include key points from the paper, including describing important figures. *No late submissions for paper summaries will be allowed as it is important for all students to review before class discussion.*

**Discussion participation:** Each group will be responsible for introducing and summarizing the key parts of the paper they read, as well as answering questions about the material—you will need to have figures ready to share on screen. During the discussion, you are encouraged to ask questions, draw connections between papers or other sources, and contribute information. If you are unable to make it to a synchronous class meeting you can make contributions or ask questions on LMS discussion boards asynchronously.

Each week your participation will be graded on 3 points:

- Rare or uninformed contribution to discussion (either in class or on discussion boards) = 1 point
- Occasional useful contributions to discussion (either in class or on discussion boards) = 2 points
- Frequent useful contribution to discussion (either in class or on discussion boards) = 3 points

During Webex discussions, I would like to encourage you to have your video on and speak audibly if you feel comfortable in order to have a discussion like we would if we were in person. If you would prefer not to have your video on, you can add a photo of yourself to your Webex account so we can visualize who is speaking. If you are unable to have audio on due to your working environment o technical difficulties, the chat function can also be used.

**Discussion summaries:** After each discussion, you will write a summary that incorporates all the papers and synthesizes the discussion. This summary should include information about the issue, history, and implications. Discussion summaries are due by *Friday at 11:59.*

**Grant Proposal Project:** Over the semester, we will discuss a number of environmental issues occurring along the Hudson River. In order to improve water quality and restore ecological conditions, money is needed. A number of private and federal organizations have grants available to improve water quality and protect natural recourses in the Hudson River watershed. For this project, you will work on writing a proposal to the Hudson River Foundation. The Hudson River Foundation supports scientific and public policy research, education, and projects to enhance public access to the Hudson River. The Hudson River fund supports scientific research on all aspects of the Hudson River ecosystem, with emphasis on sties that bear on human uses of the system. This project can be completed in groups of 1-3 students.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
