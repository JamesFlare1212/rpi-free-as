## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is the second in a two-semester sequence on research methods and statistics in the behavioral sciences. It covers approaches to statistical modeling and inference that lie within the null hypothesis significance testing (NHST) framework as well as alternative frameworks. Students will learn how to analyze various kinds of data using linear regression and analysis of variance and gain proficiency with the R programming language and software environment for statistical computing. Each student will carry out a research project and prepare a formal report that comports with the guidelines from the American Psychological Association.

## COURSE POLICIES

***Attendance and participation.*** Attendance and participation are an important part of this course and contribute to 5% of your final grade. Students who skip class will have to spend a great deal more time outside of class to keep up with the material. If you must miss class for a legitimate reason, you should contact the instructor beforehand by e-mail. Students who are taking the course remotely are expected to attend in real time via Webex unless they have a legitimate excuse for not attending (e.g., major time zone differences, poor internet connection). It is the student’s responsibility to discuss these issues with the instructor at the beginning of the semester.

***Recording of lectures.*** Lectures will be recorded and available on LMS. Although the recordings will be available to everyone, students who are able to attend (either in-person or via Webex) in real time are expected to do so. In general, portions of classes spent working on assignments will not be recorded.

***Email.*** For the purposes of this course, email should be used as a tool for scheduling a time to meet (virtually) with the instructor or TA if office hours conflict with your schedule. Please include times during which you are available to meet and a brief (1-2 sentence) description of the purpose of the meeting. In general, we will not respond to emails asking for further explanation of material covered in class or the textbook, or for clarification on assignments. This policy encourages you to ask questions about the assignment during class time and to seek help by meeting with the instructor or the TA in person, which is far more effective than trying to get help via email.

***Use of electronic devices.*** Students should bring their laptops to class since they will be needed to complete quizzes and in-class assignments. However, use of laptops, tablets, cell phones, and other electronic devices during lectures and discussions for any purpose other than to virtually attend via Webex is strongly discouraged. These devices interfere with learning and can be a distraction to you and other members of the class.

***Academic integrity.*** Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.

In this class, you will be given a number of assignments. You may consult with other class members about general statistical issues and procedures, use of statistical software, etc., but you are expected to work individually on all assignments. All analyses and write-ups should be your independent work.

Problems will be sufficiently open-ended that identical solutions or write-up language (especially if the results are incorrect!) will be viewed as possible plagiarism. To be formal about it, plagiarism means passing off the statistical designs, software commands, or written results of others as one's own work. Any student who engages in plagiarism or any other form of academic dishonesty will receive zero credit for the assignment or exam, and will be reported to the Dean of Students for further disciplinary action. The Rensselaer Handbook defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All of these forms are violations of trust between students and teachers. Please familiarize yourself with this portion of the handbook. If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

***Gender fair language.*** Because the way we speak and write affects the way we think, everyone in this course is expected to use gender-fair language in all discussions and writing. A guide to gender-fair language is available from the Writing Center and from the Library.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

**COURSE FORMAT**

The course format will be a combination of lecture and in-class assignment. For some topics, lectures will be short and will only serve to introduce a topic, with the rest of the class period being used to start the assignment. For other topics, I will lecture for most of the class period. After class, you will be expected to read the corresponding chapter of the textbook. A short quiz assessing your understanding of the reading material will be given at the beginning of the next class period. Occasionally, class time will be used for discussion about the reading assignment. There will also be some time set aside for working on the research project.

**EXAMS**

There are two exams and both are worth 12½ % of your final grade (25% total). Exam #1 will take place near the middle of the semester and Exam #2 will take place during finals week. Exam #2 is not cumulative -- it will be based on material covered since the last exam. However, most topics build upon material covered earlier in the semester. Details about the exam, including topics covered, format, and guidelines, will be discussed at least one week before each exam. Exams may be rescheduled only under special circumstances and with prior permission. If you miss an exam without prior permission, you will automatically receive a grade of zero.

**QUIZZES**

Short quizzes will be given via LMS at the very beginning of class and will assess your understanding of the material in the reading assignment. Therefore, it is important that you complete the reading assignment and arrive at class (or, if taking classes remotely, be logged in to LMS) on time. You may refer to your notes and the textbook during quizzes but due to the time limit, you will not have much time to search for answers. If you do not complete a quiz before the window closes, you will receive a zero for that quiz. No exceptions. Quizzes may not be rescheduled but your lowest quiz grade will be dropped. Students who are unable to attend in real time should discuss their situation with the instructor in advance so that accommodations can be made.

**ASSIGNMENTS**

Learning how to analyze and interpret data requires extensive practice working with a wide variety of data sets. As such, you will need to spend significant time both in class and outside of class practicing the statistical techniques that you read about and using R to explore, analyze, and interpret data. In general, students who read the textbook and come to class prepared to work should be able to complete most of the assignment before the end of class. If you are not able to complete the assignment in class, you may work on it outside of class and submit it no later than the end of the day before the next class period. Extensions beyond the due date will be given only under exceptional circumstances and with prior permission. Assignments that are submitted after the due date will incur a 10% penalty per day.

**RESEARCH PROJECT**

Each student will complete a research project on some topic of his or her interest related to psychology. Class time will be set aside for students to work on this assignment, but most of the work will be done outside of class. Each student will submit a written report (in APA format) summarizing the rationale for the study, previous research, methods, results, and conclusions and give a short presentation in class. More details about the research project, including a project milestones and timeline, will be provided at a later point in time.
