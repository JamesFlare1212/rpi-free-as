## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course, the first in a two-course sequence, provides an introduction to basic methods of behavioral/social science research, including experimental, quasi-experimental, single-subject and non-experimental research designs, psychometric considerations (e.g., reliability, validity, control, importance, levels of measurement), approaches to data collection and analysis, and communication of results. Ethical considerations when carrying out behavioral/social science research will be explored, including coverage of ethical principles described the Belmont Report and American Psychological Association (APA) ethical guidelines. Students will demonstrate their understanding of the topics covered in the course by writing a detailed research proposal formatted according to American Psychological Association (APA) guidelines. Students will also gain experience in using a selection of parametric and non-parametric statistical procedures. Data analysis will be carried out using the open source *R-Studio* package. This is a communication-intensive course.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will demonstrate a conceptual understanding of basic methods of behavioral/social science research, including experimental, quasi-experimental, single-subject and non-experimental research designs.
- Students will demonstrate their knowledge of psychometric considerations, including reliability, validity, control, importance, levels of measurement).
- Students will demonstrate their understanding of different approaches to data collection and analysis.
- Students will demonstrate their ability to develop a formal research proposal on a topic of their choosing.

Students will demonstrate their understanding of basic methods of behavioral/social science research through their performance on three online exams and through the required research proposal.  
Students will demonstrate their knowledge of psychometric considerations through their performance on three online exams and through the required research proposal.  
Students will demonstrate their understanding of different approaches to data collection and analysis through their performance on three online exams, through the required research proposal, and through their performance on five R-Study lab write-ups.  
Students will demonstrate their ability to develop a formal research proposal through the final document they submit for grading. The document must meet the formatting guidelines of the American Psychological Association.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
