## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

The instructor has developed a good sense of the standards that should be met to earn grades (I have been teaching university-level courses for more than 50 years). Consequently, the goal is no longer to establish standards, but to work toward all students meeting the standard of earning an A.

The course consists of two segments. One is learning the content of Chapters 1, 2, 3, 4 and 16 (contents of Test 2). These chapters provide the general background for consideration of specific diagnosis and treatment of behavioral disorders. Students are expected to read and study these chapters and take a test whose results support the conclusion that students have read and studied the content. Additionally, the professor will give lectures on selected diagnoses specified in a common diagnostic manual , e.g., schizophrenia. What is written in the textbook on those topics and the professor’s presentations will be the subjects of Test 1. These lectures will be made available prior to Test 2. The lectures will be on schizophrenia, alcoholism (relevant to addictive problems), Alzheimer’s disease (most common disease leading to dementia), chronic insomnia, and phobias (topics for which the prof has done research).

The other major segment: a student writes a review on a mental disorder (a diagnosis). Before the advent of Covid-19, this segment of the course was a period in which there were no class-meetings, hence giving students the time to prepare a lecture of their selected topic to be given, via PowerPoint slides, to the class. With the advent of COVID-19, class-meetings were suspended and the requirement to attend to a single diagnosis was changed to writing an essay (a scholarly review of what is known of the disorder) on the diagnosis and treatment of a disorder rather than presenting a lecture. I discovered that the written essays were far superior to the lectures with assistance of PowerPoint slides. Consequently, written reviews on a diagnosis (any diagnosis that the student choses to be the topic of their written work and is discussed in the textbook). Note, previously lectures were given by students to the assembled students of the course. That necessitated a choice of when to give the lecture. Some students wished to lecture very early in the semester, therefore, leaving time for such as a project that would be finished at the end of the semester. Others chose times other than early or just before the deadline. Most students chose to write and submit their review early leaving time to study for their other courses’ final exams. I like that, because reviews will be posted for all to read (among the enrollees in the course) and when posted at various times will likely to be attended to by fellow classmates.

There is the requirement: all Test-questions be answered correctly. Therefore, a Test can be taken multiple times, with a day in between testing, until all questions are answered correctly. Given that all students eventually have demonstrated that they have learned sufficient detailed information on the topics of the tests, it follows that all students will have earned an A on the testing segment of the course (constitutes 50% of course-grade). Professor’s judgements on the quality of the written work will constitute the other 50% of the grade. Given that considerable time usually devoted to scheduled class meetings is allocated to writing a review of a diagnosis, it is expected that the written reviews will be of high quality. Given that this semester’s students do as previous students, it is likely, but not surely, that a A-grade will be assigned to the written work, hence an A is earned for the course thereby meeting a goal of the course.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

The course is about the complex issues associated with the definition of abnormal behavior. With respect to most textbooks, abnormal behavior is ultimately defined as behavior that is problematic to the society (i.e., asocial behavior) or is troubling to the individual or both.

Once there is a decision about what constitutes abnormal behavior, there are the issues associated with how to prevent and treat the behavior in question. Not all ideas of how to prevent and treat what is called abnormal behavior are compatible with a democratic government.

The psychological science of abnormal behavior draws on a variety of academic disciplines (e.g., neuroscience and experimental psychology) and should be a basic science for the applied disciplines of clinical psychology, psychiatry, psychiatric nursing, social work, and other health-care professions. The course is useful as a liberal arts course because of its relevance to many daily concerns.
