## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The Course is a Graduate Level Seminar.

The purpose of this Course is twofold. On the one hand, it introduces students to the History of the Concept of Environment as it pertains to the topics of Natural Environment, Ecology, Sustainability as they relate and are influenced to problematics and frameworks of design.

As well, the course discusses theories, architectural design drives and attitudes towards the word Environment as a conceptual framework - a way to address the complexity of the subject matter of designing inhabitable cultural spaces in the context and awareness of the Anthropocene era.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Introduction to concepts, structures and case-studies emblematic of the cultural conditions that gave rise to Environmental and Ecological conscience. Introduction to examples that relate the Environmental and Ecological movements to design agendas, frameworks, methodologies and results.

Develop critical understanding of the different theories of design per their relationship to concepts of Environment.

Explore and Develop ways to create active comparisons and formulate arguments on the topics, towards producing relevant questions for discussion.

Use learned concepts, structures and examples to structure, describe and expand on an argument which is preparatory and declares a thesis and potential work proposal on the topic.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Learning Outcomes

This seminar engages analytical and synthesis opportunities. It engages the articulation and development of environmentally and ecologically sensitive built systems from a variety of vantage points, within design practice and ambition.

A major paper (minimum 7000 words) will be required of graduate students that will provide the theoretical material for the Master’s thesis and or Master’s Project.

In this course students will:

1. Learn how to combine technical and theoretical understandings of the subject matter into coherent thought.

2. Learn how to combine work from other disciplines and discuss its significance for the discipline of Architecture

3. Learn how to effectively collect bibliography relevant to their area of interest.

4. Learn how to articulate (in the form of a written paper) their own research agenda situated within a community of other researchers within their area of interest.

5. Outline of Course Work

   Classes will engage lecture, seminar discussion and proposal discussion formats.

   Students are required to participate in class, by producing the assignments and engaging the discussions. Students will have the option to work in teams (2-3 people).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

The *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process. Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust will merit a failing grade in this course and the violator(s) will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.
