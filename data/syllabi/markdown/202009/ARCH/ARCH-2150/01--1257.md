### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

Architecture is many things. We call it a profession, a discipline, and a practice. Each of these terms presumes in some way to define architecture and each term has different connotations. Sometimes the differences may appear slight or non-existent -- distinctions without a difference. At other times, the differences seem to be separated by an unbridgeable gap. What are these differences? One of the things you will be doing throughout your education and certainly during this semester is trying to discern what these differences may be. Trying to understand these differences, as ambiguous as they might seem sometimes, is just the tip of the iceberg regarding the complexity, the purposes, and the role of architecture in our contemporary culture and its civilization as well as its role in other cultures and civilizations. Understanding these differences and their various shades requires asking questions.  
We also call architecture an art, a science, a technology, and a craft. Which is it? Or is it some part of or all of these things? We humans, especially in our highly rationalized western civilization, like to put things in well-defined compartments and we are also habituated to think in straight line progressions. These habits of mind help us make sense of the world but such compartmentalization and linearity of thought can sometimes blind us to larger truths and deeper understandings of life, our world, and the nuances of their liminalities. Thus, as educated, thinking, and thoughtful beings, we constantly have to think against thought. We also have to become accustomed to understanding that thought and action are not two divergent and perhaps polarized things but are intimately related to one another in the same way that our bodies and our minds are connected to one another. Can our minds transcend our bodies? Can our bodies transcend our minds?

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
