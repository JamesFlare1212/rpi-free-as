## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

In the doctoral seminar within the graduate programs in lighting students will undertake activities that will assist them to develop skills necessary to pursue careers in research and/or academia. This includes writing technical papers and funding proposals, review and critique of scholarly work, and teaching.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

In the doctoral seminar the student will:

- Research, develop, and write a proposal for funding for a research topic from a local, national, or international funding organization
- Research and write a scholarly article for a trade publication or journal within their field of interest
- Peer review and critique a technical journal manuscript submitted by another researchers within their field of interest
- Develop and teach a portion of a graduate-level course under the supervision of the main course instructor.

Two of the objectives listed above will be selected for each 2-credit section of the doctoral seminar course. Students will take the seminar twice for a total of 4 credits to fulfill all four objectives listed.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Assessment**

The assessment of each student will be based on input from the following sources in the proportions stated for each 2-credit doctoral seminar completed by the student. The student must take two, 2-credit sections of the doctoral seminar as part of their plan of study for the Ph.D.

***Course section 1:***

Development and writing an article for a trade publication or journal – 70%

Review and critique of a technical manuscript that has been submitted to a peer-reviewed journal – 30%

***Course section 2:***

Development and teaching of a portion of a graduate course – 30%

Development and writing of a proposal for funding to a local, national or international funding agency – 70%

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
