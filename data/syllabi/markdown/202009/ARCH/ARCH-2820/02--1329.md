### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

ASSIGNMENT 01: Interrogating a Work of Sculpture  
Each studio section critic will curate a collection of sculptures that parallels his or her own architectural interests. Each student will examine, analyze, and reconstruct one of these works. The analysis will introduce students to particular discourses within sculpture and the three-dimensional and material modes of operation used to engage these discourses physically. Students will be responsible for accurately re-constructing and re-presenting one of these sculptural works as well as interpreting the formal, spatial, tectonic, political, and paradigmatic contexts in which that sculptural work exists.  
The most critical phase of ASSIGNMENT 01 is PART 03, the generative phase. The student will not only demonstrate that he/she understands the process and implications of the particular discourse that produced the sculpture but also can intervene in that discourse to author new unanticipated variants of the specific sculptural work that he/she is studying. These variants should amplify, multiply, and extend certain aspects of the discourse. Less important is that the variants “look” like the original work [we don’t want to produce replicas] and more important they “perform” like the work.  
ASSIGNMENT 02: Pin-up Summary  
Following the pin-up via WebEx and Miro of representative responses to ASSIGNMENT 01, PART 03 from each studio section, students will write a review of each section’s work.  
ASSIGNMENT 03: Research & Programming  
Given the general guidelines for the Tang Museum’s ancillary facilities as providing accommodations for Schaulager spaces, studios, a fabrication workshops, a small auditorium, and classroom spaces, each studio section will work as a team and…  
1\. …develop a preliminary program that defines critical parameters for each of the spaces included in the project. Each team may elect to add additional program as long as it is consistent with the Tang Museum’s mission and the Skidmore College context. Each team will present its preliminary program to their studio critic for advice and recommendation. It is imperative that students understand design as a critical practice. Hence, in performing the work of ASSIGNMENT 04, which will use a program that amalgamates all the programs devised by each studio section as a guideline and deliberative tool, that program must necessarily be open to critique and adjustment during the course of the design process.  
2\. …each student team will select two examples from a group of existing museums curated by their faculty critic and develop a case study of those museums.  
3\. …produce three distinct and significantly different site plans that each includes generic volumetric and planimetric distributions of form and space on the site in the vicinity of the existing Tang building. There must be no attachment of any of the new program to the existing building. However, the new site plan and the order of the existing building and its site should respond to one another in terms of the exterior spaces they create.  
ASSIGNMENT 04: Tang Ancillary Facilities  
In pursuing the work of this part of the semester, students must consider carefully and thoughtfully the many different parameters associated with this project in both theoretical and practical terms. A few relevant theoretical concerns were outlined in the THEORETICAL QUESTIONS section above. Students will have to consider these questions in the context of three general concerns that are intrinsic to this project. Embedded in ASSIGNMENT 04 are two exercises related to the planning and development of the site.  
1\. Landscape  
A crucial aspect of the work of ASSIGNMENT 04 is being able to read, analyze, and design within a variable topographical context such as the terrain surrounding the existing Tang building presents. Each student will analyze and assess the appropriateness for building of all terrain immediate and proximate to the existing Tang building. In order to preserve the larger flat areas of the site for new permanent or temporary installations, all new ancillary building[s] defined by the program must occupy only areas of the site that have discernable topographical variation. Many different partis can be created to organize this topographical space and accommodate the program and its implications.  
Intervening into this context will require students to negotiate and manipulate its intricate topography two- and three-dimensionally in analogical and digital realms. Any new built form cannot simply be “dropped” onto the topography. The very presence of something new within an existing context changes both that context and the motivating intentions for creating the newly proposed intervention. Such intentions are usually defined by the program. It is the architect’s responsibility to control how those changes occur and positively exploit them. Therefore, adjustments to the topography must occur during the design process; these adjustments must respect the conceptual premises of the building design and everything that comes with them AND yet respect the basic integrity of the site and its topography. Integral to the design of this project is for each student to develop a comprehensive architectural, i.e., formal and spatial, strategy for the entire site. THE SUCCESS OF EACH STUDENT’S WORK WILL BE JUDGED ON THE DEGREE TO WHICH HE/SHE HAS BEEN ASSIDUOUS AND THOUGHTFUL IN CONSIDERING THIS REQUIREMENT.  
2\. Structure  
A long span building, the Schaulager, to exhibit, store, and showcase any exhibited work. At critical junctures during ASSIGNMENT 04, we may seek the input of Dominic Pilla as a consultant on issues of long span spaces.  
3.Education  
The Tang Museum is as much of an educational institution as it is a place for exhibition. This philosophy will also be intrinsic to the purpose of the ancillary facilities. Thus, an important consideration is how does one design spaces, both interior and exterior, which promote learning and teaching? One concern in particular is visibility and transparency. Another is defining spatial order and sequences that encourage curiosity and propinquity to the art being displayed as well as the creation and fabrication of art. Of special importance in any enterprise where works of art are exhibited, stored, and created is the introduction as well as the judicious control of light, natural and artificial. Natural light in particular can be detrimental to certain kinds of art works. On the other hand, natural light can greatly enhance not only the characteristics of a work of art but also its appreciation. Light in any form can also be either advantageous or deleterious to the production of art. Therefore, the control of light is essential to the educational mission of any aspect of the Tang Museum’s ancillary facilities. Likewise, acoustics and the ease of movement, i.e. circulation, are essential adjuncts to any educational process. Ultimately, the predicate for these concerns is good communication. Thus space, movement, and activity are dependent on how you control the low-entropic flow of information in your design.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Environmental Hazards [for students on campus]  
THE USE OF SPRAY ADHESIVES, PAINTS, AND AEROSOLS ARE NOT PERMITTED IN OR ON THE GREENE BUILDING. The faculty strongly urges you not to use such materials. If you must, use the school’s spraybooth or use these materials outside away from windows and doors and against a backdrop to protect lifeforms and building materials from overspray. THESE MATERIALS CAN BE HARMFUL TO YOUR HEALTH, THE HEALTH OF OTHERS, AND THE ENVIRONMENT. FOLLOW ALL DIRECTIONS ON THE LABELS. ADHESIVES AND OTHER MATERIAL USE THESE GUIDELINES: IF IT SMELLS BAD, IT IS BAD. DON’T USE IT. IF IT DOESN’T SMELL BAD, IT STILL MAY BE. READ AND FOLLOW THE LABEL. Epoxies and resins absolutely must not be used in the Greene Building and if used, should comply with all safeguards and not be returned to the Greene Building until adequately off-gassed. Acrylic and other materials used in the laser cutter or any other fabrication process that will create dangerous off-gassing must be adequately off-gassed in the shop off-gassing chamber. This material must be left in there for the appropriate time as listed in the shop’s standards or taken from the Greene Building until the proper time has passed. Violaters of this policy may be subject to:  
1\) being charged for the cost of cleaning / repairing any damage to the building and its surroundings.  
2\) the material worked on will be excluded in that project’s review and disqualified from consideration in the project’s evaluation.
