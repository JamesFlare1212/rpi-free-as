## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The Integrated Design Schematic Studio is placed at a critical juncture in the curriculum. A most  
important element is indicated by the term ‘Integrated’ in the title of the course. In addition, as will  
become clear below, the course requires the projects undertaken to address a broad range of design  
criteria required by National Architectural Accrediting Board because they are essential to the successful  
proposition of real building projects. Buildings require the synthesis of user requirements, compliance  
with building and zoning codes, accessibility requirements, structural necessities, and energy standards  
as proscribed by regulating bodies and authorities, in addition to program, site, and context issues. This  
list may seem both daunting and burdensome. But consider that all built projects have met, and in many  
cases exceeded, these requirements. Many are driven forward by their consideration.  
As one develops skill and confidence as a designer is is often by developing knowledge and experience  
in managing constraints and requirements in a way that facilitates innovation. This should be obvious  
from the quotations above. Many other examples can be drawn from literature, music, and other creative  
fields. But just as it’s critical to understand that constraints and creativity have no opposition, it is equally  
important to realize that the constraints do not produce the creativity. The designer does that. It is done  
by constantly probing and pushing in order to understand what is possible and to be able to realize the  
properties of the constraints, to realize how the various requirements operate together, and ho  
sometimes one constraint can be used together with other ones to produce the uncommon or even the  
new.  
This semester is the schematic phase of a two semester studio sequence that will finish with the  
Integrated Design Development Studio in a subsequent semester. In order to be effective in IDD, projects  
in IDS must achieve a level of clarity and integration that precludes any substantial redesign in a later  
semester. Therefore the primary goal of the semester is to develop a schematic design that resolves  
code and zoning issues, has a clear structural proposition, environmental strategy, and lucid program  
and circulation distributions. This is only accomplished by having a strong architectural concept in terms  
of which decisions regarding a myriad of issues can be made. The results of these decisions should be a  
mutually supporting set of decisions that operate across technical, environmental, and social parameters.  
These must be clearly set forth in the documents produced by the end of the semester.  
Projects will be done individually this semester

## Course Goals

### Goals:

IDS (integrated Design Schematic Phase) will concentrate on developing a design project that integrates cultural, climatic and urban context, a complex building program and technical requirements including structural, environmental, zoning, and code compliance.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to research salient features of the context, historical, cultural, environmental, and infrastructural. They will record and analyze these factors and prepare a report
- Students will be able to document and analyze salient precedent building projects in order to understand strategies and building organizations that have been successful on the past.
- Students will undertake and analysis of the received building program and elaborate the requirements of spaces and the logical adjacencies required for a successful project. This work will be documented in a report format.
- Students will prepare a series of proposals for the project, analyze, and critique each in order to find a viable scheme that can be chosen for additional development.
- Students will develop a schematic design proposal over the course of the semester that fulfills the program requirements, integrates, building systems, structural, programmatic and organizational needs and responses to climate, regulations, and the urban condition.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Health, Safety, and Welfare in the Built Environment [NAAB SC.1] — students will understand the  
impact of the built environment on human health, safety, and welfare at multiple scales, from  
buildings to cities.  
Professional Practice [NAAB SC.2] — students will understand professional ethics, the  
regulatory requirements, the fundamental business processes relevant to architecture practice  
in the United States, and the forces influencing change in these subjects. [partial fulfillment]  
Regulatory Context [NAAB SC.3] — students will understand the fundamental principles of life  
safety, land use, and current laws and regulations that apply to buildings and sites in the United  
States, and the evaluative process architects use to comply with those laws and regulations as  
part of a project. [partial fulfillment]  
Technical Knowledge [NAAB SC.4] — students will understand the established and emerging  
systems, technologies, and assemblies of building construction, and the methods and criteria  
architects use to assess those technologies against the design, economics, and performance  
objectives of projects. [partial fulfillment]  
Design Synthesis [NAAB SC.5] — students will develop the ability to make design  
decisions within architectural projects while demonstrating synthesis of user  
requirements, regulatory requirements, site conditions, and accessible design, and  
consideration of the measurable environmental impacts of their design decisions.  
Building Integration [NAAB SC.6] —students will develop the ability to make design decisions  
within architectural projects while demonstrating integration of building envelope systems and  
assemblies, structural systems, environmental control systems, life safety systems, and the  
measurable outcomes of building performance. [partial fulfillment]

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

GRADING / ASSESSMENT  
Course grades will be determined as follows:  
10% – Phase 1A & 1B deliverables, Research (Site & Program)  
15% – Phase 2 deliverables, Conceptual Design and Building Organization  
50% – Phase 3 deliverables, Schematic Design  
10% – Attendance and engagement with the studio virtual desk crits, virtual pin-ups, and  
reviews  
15% – Digital session work  
Grades will be established according to the following policy:  
A (EXCELLENT) The design work is exceptional in every way. It has been undertaken with  
uncommon insight and energy and the ideas have been pursued with criticality and creativity.  
The project shown a strong ability to integrate systems and exceed the functional  
requirements. The results demonstrate the ability to innovate.  
B (GOOD) Project is a creative and energetic response to the program, the work is timely,  
complete and well presented. Performance above the norm; accurate and complete; beyond the  
minimum requirements of the course; work demonstrates marked progress and initiative.  
C (AVERAGE) Satisfactory work that meets minimum requirements and demonstrates  
satisfactory comprehension, communication skills, and effort; demonstrates little initiative to  
investigate the problem without substantial input from the instructor; work shows little  
improvement. Building systems are treated in a default manner and are not significantly  
transformed with respect to other systems.  
D (INFERIOR) Unsatisfactorily meets minimum requirements; demonstrates minimum  
comprehension, communication skills, and effort at an inferior level; initiative lacking;  
improvement not noticeable. The work is not produced and submitted on time, or is  
incomplete. The various building systems show minimal integration.  
F (FAIL) Does not meet minimum requirements; fails to adequately demonstrate comprehension,  
communication skills, and effort. major project presentations may be missed or have  
inadequate or missing components or the work does not demonstrate the care and  
thoroughness befitting a professional education. Systems show no integration.

### Other Course Policies:

Virtual Pin-ups  
Periodically students will be required to present their work publicly in a formal review process. These  
presentations are a critical part of the architectural education. It is an opportunity for the student and the faculty  
to judge the project and critically evaluate the process of design. It may be appropriate at points in the  
semester to evaluate only selected projects in order to convey information relevant to the entire class. In this  
situation it is important for each student to witness and participate in the review process. Even if your project is  
not being reviewed, the criticism and discussion should inform the evaluation of your own progress and  
design.  
Virtual Desk Critiques  
The design studio environment involves two-way communication. The role of the studio critic is to evolve and  
strengthen the student’s design process. Every time the student meets with the faculty, they must have new  
iterations of work based on previous conversations. If the student has not produced new work, the faculty  
reserves the right to skip the critique and continue with other students who have produced new material.  
Archiving  
At the conclusion of the course, all students will be required to submit all final digital works to Publications.  
Publications will assign a liaison to the second year studio; all students will be responsible for submitting work to  
the liaison. In addition, selected physical material will be collected and stored between semesters so that it may  
be photographed or scanned by Publications. Grades for the semester will not be released until this material is  
received.  
Environmental Hazards
