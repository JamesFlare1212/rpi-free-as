### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

This vertical studio, premised under the title “Stim and Dross: From Chemical Production to Content Production,” will delve into an urban seam between Queens and Brooklyn in New York City that was once home to the petrochemical industry on the east coast and that now has creative content creators aggregating in neighborhoods along its post-industrial banks. The title references Lars Lerup’s 1995 essay “Stim and Dross: Rethinking the Metropolis,” published in *Assemblage 25*, argued that stims (points of stimulations caused by human activities) and dross (ignored, undervalued areas) constitute the contemporary metropolis. Though his essay focused on Houston, it may surprise many that the principles apply to cities like New York which are often considered *traditionally* urban. Once outside of Manhattan and older parts of Brooklyn, the urban landscape becomes significantly less coherent as various street grids collide and 19th century infrastructure - in the form of railroad lines and canals - slices through the outer boroughs. The insertion into this context of a Center for Art & Media Technology will test architectural and urban propositions on land ripe for remediation and reinvention, perhaps an important stake put down before more mundane developer-driven projects emerge. The course will mine creative media content, those arts with a temporal dimension such film, music, video art, digital art, to develop an alternative vocabulary of architectural space. This will serve as both an inspiration for exploring architectural affect/effect as well as generator of the architectural agenda.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1\. Students will demonstrate the ability to analyze and explain a complex urban context, including both physical and intangible forces.

2\. Students will develop and articulate a comprehensive architectural program for a publicly accessible building

3\. Students will research techniques from parallel arts disciplines to de􀂦ne architectural ordering strategies.

4\. Students will demonstrate conceptual, formal, organizational, and environmental principles to develop an architectural proposal uniquely informed by the program and media which the project will house.

5\. Students will synthesize a site proposal that complements the architectural program and interfaces with the immediate urban context.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process. Cheating, plagiarism (de􀂦ned as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity.
