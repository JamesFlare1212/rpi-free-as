## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The relationship between light, projection and drawing existed since antiquity. This seminar unpacks it through literary and physical explorations; testing different ways light can draw and sculpt environments. Operating as a mini-lab seminar that concludes in a physical construction of light elements and their impact on architectural space.  
This seminar introduces the students to designing with light. The first part of the semester will include include lectures, discussions and small experiments with materials and light. In the second part of the semester students will develop a project that addresses light projection as a spatial light construct in physical form and related drawing.  
**Class structure**  
**Part 1 -** Week 1-6 topic lectures (posted on LMS weekly), Scheduled synchronous WebEx Session including - reading discussions and small experiments.  
Lectures will be posted weekly on Friday prior to relevant class. Students are required to view lectures and read the relevant readings and respond in min 100 words to an aspect of the lecture or readings.  
**Part 2 -** Week 8-12 Final project development from one of the mini-labs and draft of paper (500 words deepening investigation into topic)  
**Part 3 -** Week 13-15 - Final review and paper - testing project 2 in new location or extending it. Writing a 1000 word paper (under-graduates) 1,500 word paper (graduates)  
Since the course is conducted online students are encouraged to create a darkened environment in which they can conduct ‘experiments’ when they present during class time and photograph independent work for submission weekly.  
You will be asked to use your phone light, and purchase a color changing bulb/strip light for class experiments. The third part of the semester will be conducted off campus and may require a spatial adjustment of lighting device/ spatial exploration.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Student learning objectives**

- To introduce students to the relationship between a light source and spatial elements and their impact on the environment.
- To introduce students to different aspects of light control and the effects they may yield.
- To introduce students to principles of architectural projection and their origin relationship to light and optics.
- To introduce students to literary aspects of light foundational in western thought, and have them reflect upon that in their individual projects.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will demonstrate the ability to use the relationship between a light source and spatial elements to impact on the environment through designing a 1:1 scale elements that project light onto their environment and impact the perception of the space.
- Students will demonstrate strategic use of at least one light control to reach a required affect.
- students will demonstrate understanding of the relationship between light and drawing through class participation, writing, drawing and project.
- Students will create a project with light and relate it to the classes literary and theoretical foundations through a written essay.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students will be expected to produce one main constructed light element based on developed ideas and materials studies introduced in class. This construct will be represented and analyzed for the effects it creates as an object and the effect it has on its surroundings. Students are also expected to write one 1000 word paper, relating to readings and lectures and relevant to their final project. Students are expected to participate in class conversations and mini-labs.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

This undergraduate research is built as a team work and based on collaboration between student and directing instructor. This team is built on the trust that students produce the work agreed upon themselves. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process. Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust, the student be warned. The second instance will merit a failing grade in this course and the violator(s) will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.
