## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

The textbook for this course is Design Basics by David Lauer and Stephen Pentak. There will be additional readings from Data Visualization: A Successful Design Process by Andy Kirk.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course will take a theoretical and “hands-on” approach to visual design. The course will include topics such as creative problem solving, visual perception, design theory and research, visual semiotics, and data visualization. The course provides a foundation in design that will help you improve your ability to *interpret* *and design* visual information, as well as understand the importance of effective visual design in different forms of communication.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will apply concepts in creative problem solving to the design and development of visual design projects.
- Students will apply concepts in visual perception and Gestalt psychology by analyzing and creating visual designs that incorporate these principles.
- Students will analyze target audiences and objectives for visual designs, and then create projects that meet those objectives.
- Students will discuss problems/issues in which data is a relevant tool, including writing about, presenting on, and visualizing data. Students will critically evaluate the pros/cons of different types of data visualizations to determine the most effective ways to visualize specific data relationships. Students will create a design document for an interactive data visualization. They will identify 1) a problem to address with data analysis, 2) identify the audience and objectives for the project, 3) decide which data relationships meet the objectives, and 4) determine the best forms of data visualization and interaction design to show specific data relationships by applying research and principles in visual design, information and data design, visual perception, and human-computer interaction (HCI).

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Design Basics Assignment**.  
This is an individual assignment. You will answer a series of questions and create visual designs based on the readings in the Design Basics textbook. There is no grade for this assignment. You will be expected to refer to the design elements in this assignment in other written analyses that you submit for class projects throughout the semester.  
The assignment is due Sept. 25 (via email by 10:10 AM ET). If you do not complete this assignment, you will receive an Incomplete for the course.  
**Assignments and Projects**  
Two of the projects in this class are team projects. Two projects are individual projects. Brief descriptions of these projects are listed below. More details will be distributed later.  
**Creative Problem Solving Project (Team Project) 25% of final grade**  
You will use creative problem solving techniques to develop a visual design for a specific concept. You will also design a logo and poster for that concept. This project will include a class presentation and a written paper.  
**Analysis of Political Ads (Individual assignment) 20% of final grade**  
You will analyze ads from two opposing political candidates in this year’s national elections. This assignment includes a class presentation and a written paper.  
**Data Visualization Project (Team Project) 25% of final grade**  
You will use visual design principles to visualize and communicate data relationships for a specific data analysis project. You will identify the audience(s) and objectives, and explore ways to visualize various data relationships using different types of data charts, filters, and interaction design to determine the most effective forms of data visualizations. This project will involve a class presentation and a written paper.  
**Final Design Project (Individual Project) 30% of final grade**  
You will create a logo and a poster for a product, organization, company, event, research project. For a research poster, the logo can represent the research concept or research group.  
**Grading**  
Your final grade will be determined as follows:  
Creative Problem Solving Project 25%  
Political Ad Analysis 20%  
Data Visualization Project 25%  
Final Design Assignment 30%  
I will assign letter grades for projects, and your final grade will be determined by translating the letter grade for each project into a numerical equivalent based on the QPA scale. The QPA scale is as follows: A=4.0, A-=3.67, B+=3.33, B=3.0, B-=2.67, C+=2.33, C=2.0, etc. Your average for the course must be at least 3.67 for an A-, at least 2.67 for a B-, etc. Your average for the course must be a 4.0 for an A.  
All the projects require class presentations. You must do a presentation or your grade for that project will be dropped one letter grade.  
All projects must be completed in order to receive a passing grade.  
If you have questions about your grade at any time during the semester, you should see me as soon as possible.  
**Late Projects**  
All projects are due on the designated due date. Because there are several projects in this class, it is very important that you submit projects on time to avoid a backlog of work. Late projects will be dropped one letter grade for each day they are late. Saturdays, Sundays, and holidays count as late days. Since technical problems always seem to occur the night before an assignment is due, I strongly suggest that you aim to complete your projects a few days before the due date. In general, extensions will not be given because of hardware or software problems.  
If you feel that a personal or academic problem is going to prevent you from meeting a project deadline, you should speak to me several days before the due date (not the day the project is due) to see if an extension is possible. At that time, if I grant an extension, I will also set another due date. That deadline must be met in order to avoid a grade penalty. Projects that are handed in after the extended deadline will automatically receive an F for a grade.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
