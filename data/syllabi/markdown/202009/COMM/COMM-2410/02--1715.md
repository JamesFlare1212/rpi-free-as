## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Students will be able to analyze photographs in terms of their formal/expressive, psychodynamic, and social/political components in ways that integrate both the students' knowledge and feelings.
- • Student will be able to discuss theoretical photography concepts and apply this knowledge to the analysis of photography

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students will be evaluated on the basis of Formal Papers and an Oral Presentation, in addition to

Class participation/preparation/Study Questions.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Evaluation: Students will be graded in two categories:

**First Category**: Formal Papers/Oral Presentation

#1. Paper. 26%

#2. Paper. 32%

#3 Paper. 32%

Oral Presentation 10%

**Second Category:** Class participation/preparation/Study Questions

This category, as a whole, will be graded in terms of excellent/very good; good; adequate; inadequate

To receive a course grade **within the A level**, students must achieve both

- total score 90 or above in Category (1) Paper/Study Question/Oral Presentation/Study Questions.

A is 93-100; A- is 92-91

- **and** an excellent/very good in Category (2) Participation/Preparation/Study Questions

To receive a course grade **within the B level**, students must achieve both

- at least a good in Category (2) Participation/Preparation/Study Questions
- **and** a Paper/Study Question/Oral Presentation score of 80 or above in Category (1).

B+ is 88-89; B is 87-85; B- is 84-80

To receive a course grade **within the C level**, students must achieve both

- at least an adequate in Category (2) Participation/Preparation/Study Questions
- **and** a Paper/Study Question/Oral Presentation score of 70 or above in Category (1).

C+ is 78-79; C is 77-75; C- is 74-70

D course grade will be given in either of two cases:

if there is inadequate participation/presentation or if the student has a Paper/Study Question/Oral Presentation grade below 50

F course grade will be given if the student has inadequate participation/presentation and misses more than 60% of the classes or if the student has a Paper/Study Question/Oral Presentation grade below 50

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
