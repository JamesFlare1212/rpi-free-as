## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will introduce students to the literary genre of creative nonfiction. The primary objectives of this course are to write thoughtful and effective short experiments in the genre, engage meaningfully in an artistic community of peers, practice listening and nuanced thinking, and become more creatively fearless. Assignments will guide students to refine their powers of observation, model their work from published memoirs and essays, and practice tools for lively writing. Through reading and discussion, short writing exercises, and participation in a writing workshop, students will learn to recognize and practice the various subgenres of creative nonfiction, including memoir, literary journalism, personal essay, and literary art or science writing. Every student will leave the course with a toolkit of ideas for creative nonfiction and a final portfolio of creative writing.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will demonstrate knowledge of creative nonfiction writing in general and the subgenres in particular by utilizing appropriate terminology to analyze and discuss published essays.
- Students will demonstrate craft-based reading strategies through annotations and discussions/LMS posts.
- Students will establish a consistent creative practice by regularly producing short creative writings.
- Students will demonstrate engagement in the writing process through observing, drafting, peer critique, and revision.
- Students will actively engage in a creative community of peers through participation in workshop and discussion.
- Students will construct a learning portfolio that showcases their skills and development in the field of creative nonfiction writing over the course of the semester.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Reading.** In this course, you will learn to read like a writer, which is substantially different than the reading you may be used to in English classes. The expectation is that as you engage with these texts you are “looking behind the curtain” to determine how the artistic effects were achieved and preparing to discuss your theories with others. Reading should be an active endeavor.  
**Short Exercises (SE).** These are short creative writing prompts designed to give you practice in one particular skill. There is no minimum length so long as the prompt is thoughtfully addressed. Maximum length is one double-spaced page. Short exercises should be posted in the student's online journal.  
**Craft Discussion Boards.** These approximate the kinds of thoughtful, initial comments about a text you might make if we were all in the same room. The purpose is to help you organize your thoughts before class and to get discussions started in a remote environment (which can be awkward). **Please post by class start time (EST) on the date listed on the assignment schedule.** Discussion boards will be regularly assessed as Provocative (P), Substantial (S), Emergent (E), or Unsatisfactory (U). You will earn grades in this category based on the quality of the majority of posts: Majority P = A; Majority S = B; Majority E = C; Majority U = F  
**Workshop.** Instruction in this course follows the guidelines established by the Association of Writers and Writing Programs (AWP) for the teaching of creative writing, which include a “challenging writers’ workshop” as a hallmark, and includes:

- Uploading your story drafts to the LMS Workshop Discussion board on time.
- Posting your end comments to the Workshop Discussion board *before* the workshop.
- Participating in workshops during class time.

-Note: In order to receive credit for workshop draft submission, you must also attend your workshop. Students who miss their own workshops, therefore, do not earn draft credit even if a draft is submitted (barring emergencies).

**End Comments.** Good feedback can be short or long, so I won't impose an arbitrary length on yours. Your stance should be that of a careful and interested reader. You're making suggestions or observations the writer can take into the next draft or into other pieces. In my feedback, I will assume your work is a draft in progress and gear my comments toward improvements you can make in future drafts, even though you may not actually choose to rewrite that particular piece. Feedback is most helpful if it makes specific points. It's least helpful when it deals in generalizations.  
**Observation Journal.** This is simply a place for your to record your observations about the world around you, which includes your internal landscape. You will include observation journal entries in your final portfolio.

**Creative Portfolio**

FINAL DRAFT DUE December 15th via Blackboard as ONE MS Word or PDF document and run through SafeAssign (plagiarism detection software).

Rather than producing a major new work at semester’s end, you will instead compile and revise a portfolio of work that you feel best represents your learning this semester, as reflected in the learning outcomes listed on this syllabus.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

This is a small seminar class, the success of which is entirely dependent on active student participation. Therefore, I expect all students to participate to the extent of their abilities. Students unable to attend or participate due to illness, instability, or irresolvable conflict should reach out to me as soon as possible so we can problem-solve together. The expectation for synchronous Zoom classes is that students will have video turned on so that we can better engage with one another as a community. However, every student is welcome to take two audio-only days during the semester, in which they can listen in to a synchronous class with video off and not be called upon to participate. Exemptions to the video policy will be made for students that require accommodations. For accommodations to this policy, please see me within a week of joining the class.

### Other Course Policies:

## Late Work Policy

Due to the nature of workshop, late workshop submissions cannot be accepted (in case of an emergency, contact me). On-time workshop submissions have a domino effect on your grade, since these on-time submissions are a prerequisite for completion of your final creative portfolio. **Do not miss workshop submission deadlines.** For all other assignments, there is a twenty-four-hour grace period for submissions to be made, after which time the assignment will close for anyone who has not requested an extension. If you need more than an extra day to complete an assignment, simply email me for an extension. I rarely have a problem working with students around deadlines. That being said, do your best to stick with the schedule to avoid work accumulation.

## Final Grading Policy

Students are expected to monitor their grades throughout the semester via Blackboard’s grading feature, and I am happy to help you navigate that. If you have any questions about your grade at any time, please do not hesitate to respectfully inquire. All inquiries should be made as soon as possible and no later than the last day of class. Please review the late work policy; students may not submit late work to better a grade at the end of the semester. All grades on SIS are final, barring computational error.

## Technology Policy

Technology is absolutely essential to success in this course. At a minimum, you will need reliable access to the internet, Zoom, LMS, MS Word, and a slide presentation program, such as PowerPoint. Technology fails are inevitable, but you are expected to do your best to insulate yourself from tech tragedies by testing your hardware, software, and connections before class, ensuring adequate bandwidth, regularly backing up files, and reading up on assignments in advance in case LMS goes down when you are meant to be working on something (plan for LMS outages). While I will do my best to make reasonable accommodations for technology mishaps, I cannot guarantee these accommodations. You are ultimately 100% responsible for your technology and for reaching out to me or to your peers in advance if you anticipate a problem.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

All assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work completed for this class in particular. If you have a compelling reason for wanting to use previous original work for the class or work being co-created in another class, it must be substantially and fundamentally revised, and you must get approval from the instructor first. Any ideas, quotes, images, and language borrowed from any source must be properly cited with a Works Cited page using MLA citation style. This includes information from other courses as well as any information whatsoever that is not considered common knowledge. This means students may have to find a source for information that is known to them, but not yet cited. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty of a zero for the assignment and the potential to fail the course. Second offenses will result in automatic failure of the course and the potential for disciplinary action by the institution.If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
