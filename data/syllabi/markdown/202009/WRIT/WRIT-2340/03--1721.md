**Proposed Learning Outcomes:**

The objective of this course is to provide students the opportunity to:

- create and present five kinds of speeches well (an introductory speech, informative speech, persuasive speech, progress report, and proposal),
- choose good subjects and invent good content,
- organize talks well,
- use appropriate style,
- approach memory well,
- develop excellent delivery capabilities,
- improve small-group communication skills through discussion, and
- learn some virtual communication techniques.

**Course Requirements:**

1. Students learn from each other and help each other with feedback. They are expected, then, to attend class unless they are ill or have some other valid excuse. They cannot pass unless they attend class regularly (no more than five unexcused absences, each three occasions of tardiness counting as an absence) and complete all requirements.

2. I will negotiate the dates for speeches with students. We will try to avoid having students give speeches on the days they have exams, for instance. Once speeches are negotiated, unless there is a valid excuse, students should present their speeches at scheduled times, or grades can be adversely affected. Two unexcused failures to present your speeches at scheduled times will result in a “C.” Three such unexcused failures will result in an “F.”

3. Classes will all be virtual using “Zoom Meetings.” I will send a link to all class members, and they must be visible in the gallery so that I can evaluate the reasonable dress and appearance of everyone at all times. Scheduled speakers will deliver their speeches live, and each speech will be followed by full oral evaluation by everyone else present. Before the next class period, I will also send a written evaluation to speakers. Speeches and class discussion will be recorded on Zoom.

4. Students need a functioning computer with consistent access to high speed internet. They need a good video camera, good audio, and appropriate lighting. To deliver a speech, they will use a podium or something like it, and their cameras should be at their eye levels. They should be visible at least from the waist up, and they will not wear masks. They can download Zoom at the basic level free to practice speeches. They should record speeches to check their video, audio, and lighting before presenting speeches live. They should practice their speeches on and off Zoom many times to improve quality.

5. Students can download Zoom by going to https://zoom.us. They should download the Zoom Meetings Client. They can find guides in abundance online and should begin to familiarize themselves with them. I would appreciate hearing from any students already very familiar with Zoom as soon as possible. In the evaluation that follows speeches, all students should participate. Toward that end, students should familiarize themselves with the “raise hand” function. I will do my best to call on students with and without raised hands to ensure that everyone participates in discussion every class period.

**Assignments and Grading Formula**

6. Introductory speech. 0% of grade.

7. Informative presentation. 15% of grade.

8. Persuasive presentation. 15% of grade.

9. Progress report. 20% of grade; possibility of being eliminated as an assignment.

10. Proposal. 25% of grade.

Higher grades on later speeches may result in higher overall grades than the grading formula warrants.

*The Rensselaer Handbook* defines various forms of academic dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Students are responsible for familiarizing themselves with these policies and should note that the penalties for plagiarism can be quite harsh.

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
