## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course presents students with a variety of texts from a range of historical periods loosely organized to explore four related thematic trajectories: myth, mystery, magic, and monsters. While introducing students to the basic ideas and terminology used to discuss fiction, poetry, drama, and literary criticism, the texts will also facilitate discussion on the relationship between the real, the unreal, and the uncanny in various literary traditions. Assignments include discussion, reading reflections, four short critiques, an extended critique paper or exam (student choice), and a final learning portfolio in which students may choose (optionally) to include creative work. Student participation is the heart of this course.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

- To foster enjoyment of literature.
- To help students recognize that literature does not occur in isolation, but is part of a complex dialogue within cultural and historical contexts.
- To demonstrate the broad nature of the field of literature by studying a variety of literary elements and genres.
- To draw student awareness to reading practices.

- To facilitate team-building, small group-dynamics, and interpersonal communication skills by fostering a learning community.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will demonstrate understandings of the generic conventions of literature utilizing appropriate terminology.
- Students will reflect on their own critical reading practices.
- Students will demonstrate careful reading, including differentiation among reading strategies for a variety of genres.
- Students will produce strong analyses, through multiple drafts, of a variety of literary texts through a particular critical lens.
- Students will construct a learning portfolio that showcases reflective learning and improvement in understanding and writing about literature over the course of the semester.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Reading/Viewing:** Students' primary assignment in this course is to read and view course texts actively and critically.

**Participation:** Active participation means first and foremost maintaining a positive attitude and open mind. It includes reading carefully, taking notes, and engaging in class activities regularly, prepared to interact with your peers.

**Short Critiques.** These papers should be between 2.5 and 4.5 double-spaced pages in 11- or 12-point standard font with one-inch page margins and no extra spaces between paragraphs. In the poetry unit, you will work with peers to instead make a brief critique presentation to the class.

**Quote Analyses:** All students will sign up a number of times over the course of the semester (exact number depends on how many students are enrolled in the class) to do a brief oral presentation on a relevant passage from a literary text.

**Discussion Preparation Boards.** These approximate the kinds of thoughtful, initial comments about a text you might make if we were all in the same room. The purpose is to help you organize your thoughts before class and to for us to get discussions started in a remote environment (which can be awkward for some). Students should be prepared to elaborate on their post in class. Discussion boards will be regularly assessed as Provocative (P), Substantial (S), Emergent (E), or Unsatisfactory (U). Letter grades will be issued based on the quality of the majority of a student's posts, as follows: Majority P = A; Majority S = B; Majority E = C; Majority U = F

**Exam.** An exam will be available on LMS during class time (EST—see me if this is not feasible for you due to your time zone) on Tuesday, November 24th. The exam will ask for both knowledge and skills demonstrations related to units 1-3. Students who feel an examination would not be conducive to their learning style may opt to write a 7-10-page essay instead, which will be due at the time of the exam. **Students wishing to take the essay option must be approved to do so one month in advance, by October 24th.**

**Final Learning Portfolio**

FINAL DRAFT DUE Tuesday, December 15th via Blackboard as ONE MS Word or PDF document and run through SafeAssign (plagiarism detection software). Rather than producing a major new work at semester’s end, you will instead compile and revise a portfolio of work that you feel best represents your learning this semester, as reflected in the learning outcomes listed in this syllabus. There is a detailed assignment sheet for this project. In summary, you are required to include: a brief reflective statement, a table of contents, and revised short critiques (2). Students should also include at least one additional evidence of engagement with close reading and analysis in a form(s) of their choice, for example: creative and/or multimedia works, a bibliography for future reading, write-ups of optional literary events attended, annotated pages, discussion board reflections, or any other demonstrations you would like to include. The page count will vary from student to student, but the portfolio should be substantial enough to reflect a semester’s learning.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

This is a small seminar class, the success of which is entirely dependent on active student participation. Therefore, I expect all students to participate to the extent of their abilities. Students unable to attend or participate due to illness, instability, or conflict should reach out to me as soon as possible so we can problem-solve together. The expectation for synchronous Zoom classes is that students will have video turned on so that we can better engage with one another as a community. However, every student is welcome to take two audio-only days during the semester, in which they can listen in to a synchronous class with video off and not be called upon to participate. Exemptions to the video policy will be made for students in circumstances that require accommodations. For accommodations to this policy, please see me within a week of joining the class.

### Other Course Policies:

## Technology Policy

Technology is absolutely essential to success in this course. At a minimum, you will need reliable access to the internet, Zoom, LMS, MS Word, and a slide presentation program, such as PowerPoint. Technology fails are inevitable, but you are expected to do your best to insulate yourself from tech tragedies by testing your hardware, software, and connections before class, ensuring adequate bandwidth, regularly backing up files, and reading up on assignments in advance in case LMS goes down when you are meant to be working on something (plan for LMS outages). While I will do my best to make reasonable accommodations for technology mishaps, I cannot guarantee these accommodations. You are ultimately 100% responsible for your technology and for reaching out to me or to your peers in advance if you anticipate a problem.

## Final Grading Policy

Students are expected to monitor their grades throughout the semester via Blackboard’s grading feature, and I am happy to help you navigate that. If you have any questions about your grade at any time, please do not hesitate to respectfully inquire. All inquiries should be made as soon as possible and no later than the last day of class. Please review the late work policy; students may not submit late work to better a grade at the end of the semester. All grades on SIS are final, barring computational error.

## Late Work Policy

For all assignments except the exam, there is a twenty-four-hour grace period for submissions to be made, after which time the assignment will close for anyone who has not requested an extension. If you need more than an extra day to complete an assignment, simply email me for an extension before the due date. I rarely have a problem working with students around deadlines on an occasional basis. That being said, do your best to stick with the posted schedule to avoid work accumulation. This policy supports students in exercising agency. Therefore, I will not accept late work beyond the grace period if an extension is not requested before the due date.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

All assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work completed for this class in particular. If you have a compelling reason for wanting to use previous original work for the class or work being co-created in another class, it must be substantially and fundamentally revised, and you must get approval from the instructor first. Any ideas, quotes, images, and language borrowed from any source must be properly cited in text and with a Works Cited page using MLA citation style. This includes information from other courses as well as any information whatsoever that is not considered common knowledge. This means students may have to find a source for information that is known to them, but not yet cited. The exam must be taken independently with no help from any other person or from the internet. The exam is open book, and all course materials may be used. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty of a zero for the assignment and the potential to fail the course. Second offenses will result in automatic failure of the course and the potential for disciplinary action by the institution. If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
