## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Students are invited to apply positivity practices to their own lives, contexts, and experiences to ensure understanding of the material from the book and from class. From your textbook authors: The text examines how positive psychology applies to stressors and health within such traditional research areas as developmental, clinical, personality, motivational, social, and behavioral psychology. Furthermore, the text offers perspectives on positive emotional states, research and theory on positive traits, coverage of positive institutions, and a look at the future of positive psychology.

We will be journaling in class to reinforce the concepts taught in lecture.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1. **Quizzes (100 points total)**

- 6 multiple choice quizzes (non-cumulative)
- Hints will be provided during class time
- No final exam

2. **Well-Being Journal (100 points)**

- Choose: notebook or eJournal
  - o Notebook option: Please buy a notebook and dedicate it to this course.
  - o eJournal option: create a PowerPoint or Word document and bring to class daily
- Personalize your notebook (go old school/grade school and decorate/individualize your notebook to you: cover, pages, add pics, etc)
- We will have specific Journal Entries during lectures that I will assign throughout the semester.
- If you have exercises that are very personal to you and you do not want to share, simply tape another sheet of paper over that entry if Notebook option, or on your submission just write “ too personal to share” and delete your entry

3. **Gratitude Journal OR Letters To My Daughter Review OR**

**OR** **6 Week Mindfulness Meditation (40 points)**

**Gratitude Journal**

- 5 days/week write a brief description of 3 things you are grateful for and explain why.
- Length is not important. I want you to think about things that happened in the day that you are glad happened. It can be as simple as the sun was out and you took a walk, or you received a call from your grandmother and she made you happy, or you completed an assignment ahead of time, you thought about the sacrifices you and/or your parents are making to pay for your tuition, or whatever you were grateful for. If you are having trouble getting started, then start with one thing per day, and increase over time. Try not to repeat the things stated on previous days.
- Do this for 6 weeks.
- Describe what you are grateful for and **WHY**
- LMS will have 6 entries for the gratitude journal. Submit your entries for the week (your 5 days of journaling). You must write daily gratitude summaries for a minimum of 5 days per week.
- What your Friday submission should look like:
  1. Monday: I was grateful that my mom took me and the kids out to dinner. It was a busy day and going out to dinner reduced my stress level. I was grateful that it snowed today so that I can go skiing tomorrow with fresh snow. I was also grateful for the snow because it was beautiful to see the snow in the trees.
  2. Tuesday: Glad my kids did all their homework on their own…quieter for me! Thankful my oldest was such a good big brother to his sister when he was helping her skiing today. Thankful for a good pair of skis!
  3. Wednesday: Thankful for Lanzi’s salad dressing because it makes a salad really taste awesome. Grateful the man I love is in my life even though he is so far away.
  4. Thursday: Thankful I have a good job so I can provide a good life for my kids. Grateful for my parents…they are the kindest, most thoughtful people I know. Thankful Smartwool was developed so I can stay warm skiing.
  5. Friday: Grateful my kids like broccoli and spinach and carrots and all kinds of fruit so they can get good nutrients. Thankful for garlic because it makes broccoli and spinach taste really good! Thankful for my friend Gina because we have such a great time skiing, exercising, and hanging out together.

**Letters To My Daughter Review by Maya Angelou**

- Only do this if you do not do the gratitude or mindfulness journal.
- Read the book (obviously!)
- Angelou: Discuss the positive psychology themes mentioned throughout her letters. Discuss what resonated with you, what you found interesting, or just touched your heart.
- Paper should be 5-7 pages double spaced. Submit on LMS
- **Due: June 27**

**6 Week Mindfulness Meditation**

- Only do this if you did not do the gratitude journal nor Angelou reading.
- Must complete 10min mindful meditation daily for 6 weeks
- Highly recommend starting with Korumindfulness.org
- Submit weekly reports on LMS describing your experiences including what types of mindfulness meditation you did, the struggles, epiphanies, challenges, and successes
- Your weekly summary must be one page double spaced
- Apps and websites to help you learn how to do mindful meditation: korumindfulness.org, Headspace, Calm, and thousands of free meditations on insighttimer.com

4. **The Last Lecture (15 pts) Discussion Thurs May 30th**

- Read the book because there will be a quiz
- You will receive discussion questions
- Choose one person from your group to write up a summary of your discussion and all students submit that same document on LMS

5. **The Five People You Meet In Heaven (15 pts) Discussion June 14th**

- Read the book
- You will receive discussion questions
- Choose one person from your group to write up a summary of your discussion and all students submit that one document on LMS

6. **Leadership and Self-Deception (15 pts) Due June 21st**

- Read the book and then think of an instance where you were in the box. What was the situation and outcomes? Now consider at time you were out of the box (a different situation from your in the box situation)
- There will be a short quiz
- Write a summary of your in the box and out of the box examples. You will submit on LMS and also use to discuss in class
- Use the Template provided in LMS

7. **Attendance/The Meaning of Life (50pts)**
   - This course has the potential to positively impact your well-being, and your presence and attention can benefit you greatly. Therefore, I really want you to be in class.
   - 5 days during the semester I will be taking attendance by way of asking you thought provoking questions relating to the meaning of life
   - If you have an excused absence, please email me prior to your absence

8. **Happiness Calendar (50 points) Due each Friday (except Week 6 is Thurs)**

- Every class you will be given a topic from Greater Good Science Center’s Happiness Calendar. You will be prompted on what to write about each class.
- Some activities you will be able to do in class. Some you will do outside of class and then write about what you did in your journal.
- Weeks 1-5: 9pts and Week 6 is 5pts
- Due dates: Every Friday by 8p

9. **Presentation: Good News Fridays (15 pts)**

- Fridays of Weeks 2, 3, 4, and 5, there will be 5 students who present a Good News Story. Search online for a feel good story that you can share with the class.

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
