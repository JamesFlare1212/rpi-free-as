### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

Environmental Economics strives to put into practice an objective data driven framework for understanding and solving problems of the environment caused by human actions of production and consumption. We will highlight important aspects of managing and mitigating damage in the environment and the analytical tools of economics for modeling environmental problems and solutions. A wide variety of environmental issues will be surveyed as we apply economics to major cases, such as air, water, solid wastes/ toxic substances, and global environmental management. This course is a studio (not lecture) course, meaning that the course depends heavily on student interaction and participation. This course also will satisfy requirements for Professional Development II and Communication Intensive criteria. Students will be responsible for textbook readings, interactive assignments, submission of a portfolio of written work, and a verbal presentation. Environmental Economics is a four credit course which spans a six week condensed summer term and the workload should be expected accordingly. It is critical that students keep on top of work (readings, assignments, lessons, and writing progress) and not get behind.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grade Component: PORTFOLIO (80%): A portfolio of written work will be compiled by each student; there are separate and specific instructions for portfolio content. A draft of the portfolio will be due in Week #4 of the term and the final draft will be due during the last week of the term. Mid-way through the course the rough draft will be submitted for an editing workshop in which all students will participate. The finished portfolio must be 15 pages minimum in length (double-spaced, 11 pt. font) in order to satisfy the communication intensive criteria. However, it is very likely that, by following the instructions for the portfolio, the length will exceed the 15 page minimum and you are encouraged to not skimp on writing in order to keep to the 15 page minimum. It is imperative that students begin writing their portfolio at the start of the semester; this is not an assignment that can be pulled together during the last week. It is also expected that you will revise and edit your writing as needed so as to submit a polished and professional-looking final document. Credit will be deducted for spelling and grammar mistakes, awkward wording, and for the use of language translation software. If you are at all concerned about your writing skills, you are urged to contact the Rensselaer Center for Communication and Design; see the section of this syllabus titled, ‘Communication Intensive Criteria’.

Grade Component: PRESENTATION (20%): Students will present their Portfolio at the end of the term. Each presentation should be ten minutes in length and highlight only the parts of the portfolio you find most interesting. In addition, each student will be assigned one section of the Analytics (see portfolio description) to describe in the presentation. You may choose to give your presentation live or to produce a video presentation. If you choose a video presentation, you must be present in class to show your video.

Final Grades: Final grades will be broken down as follows, rounded to the nearest whole number: A 94 to 100; A- 90 to 93; B+ 87 to 89; B 84 to 86; B- 80 to 83; C+ 77 to 79; C 74 to 76; C- 70 to 73; D+ 67 to 69; D 60 to 66; F 59 and below.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

All work submitted for a grade must be each student’s own work. Violation of the policy on academic integrity involves activities such as copying from another student’s work or some other external source (plagiarism), submitting written work that has been written for another class or by a former student during a prior run of this course, and any activity that is meant to undermine the teaching and learning process. There is a zero-tolerance policy towards violations of academic integrity. If any incident of violation is discovered, the involved student(s) will receive a failing grade for the course.
