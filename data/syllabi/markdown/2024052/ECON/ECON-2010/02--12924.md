## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course combines demand, derived from consumer preferences, and supply, based on firms’ production functions, to establish market prices for goods and services. The course applies the microeconomic theory of the firm to price, cost, and output decisions of business enterprises under different market structures. Product pricing strategies are examined. Calculus-based techniques are used to minimize costs and maximize utility and profits across differing industry structures. The course also introduces topics such as game theory, externalities, public goods, asymmetric information, and behavioral economics.

The grading scale is 90-100 A, 80-89.9 B, 70-79.9 C, 60-69.9 D, <60 F. Plus/minus grading will be used in this course with plus grades will be assigned for scores in the top 3 points of a grade range while minus grades will be assigned for scores in the bottom 3 points of a grade range. For example, 82.6 points out of 100 is a “B minus” while 89.6 is a “B plus.” Your overall score is determined by the summation of your performance on exams, assignments, and participation.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Weeks 1 and 2 - What is economics, how is math used in economics, markets, budgets, preferences, utility, choice, demand, consumer surplus, equilibrium.  
Weeks 3 and 4 - Exchange economies, costs, profits, firm behavior, supply, monopoly, market structure, price discrimination.  
Weeks 5 and 6 - Oligopoly, game theory, externalities, public goods, asymmetric information.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- To be able to explain to others why economists use mathematics and formal modeling to analyze situations of constrained choice.
- To be able to identify how market structures (perfect competition, monopoly, oligopoly) affect resource allocation decisions, equilibrium market prices, economic efficiency, and the effectiveness of policy.
- To be able to apply microeconomic concepts to evaluate real-world policy issues including taxation, government spending, regulation, externalities, and price controls.
- To be able to critique the limits of economic reasoning and modeling, including identifying what separates economic models from the real-world choice situations that they are designed to represent.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

**How to be successful in this class:**

- Come to class. The material builds continuously so missing a class can and probably will lead to confusion later. It will take several reviews of the material before it begins to sink in.
- Ask questions. **Your questions are not an imposition**; they are welcome and make class more interesting. If you are confused, other people probably are too. If you feel uncomfortable asking questions in class, please ask during office hours or by email.
- **The time to be concerned about your grade is throughout the semester**, not just in the final week. Contact me if you feel you are falling behind.
- I recommend that you form a study group. Review your notes, study until you feel you thoroughly understand and can apply the material. Then, try to teach what you do understand to those who do not. If you cannot *communicate* a concept to others, you do not understand it.
- The key to mastering economics is, like anything, **practice**. The internet is full of economics practice problems and video explanations (YouTube is your friend!). If you are struggling with the material, you need more practice.
