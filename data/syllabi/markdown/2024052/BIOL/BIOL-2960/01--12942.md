## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is at the undergraduate level. It explores the status of our current knowledge on Human Evolution. The goal is to provide active learning in a collaborative environment, to seek the causal factors leading up to our own emergence. *What makes a human?* Data collection is from laboratory specimens (phenotypic traits), as well as from public databases (genomic/protein datasets). Data analysis is accomplished with simulations. Data analysis utilizes the supporting evidence, while tracing the evolutionary relatedness amongst primate/mammalian species. Interactive discussions allow students to generate hypotheses, and design tests of their hypotheses. The impact of disease pathogens, lifestyle/culture and environment on human populations is discussed.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. Principle Concepts: Students will be able to master introductory concepts underlying Human Evolution.
2. Predict and Propose: Students will formulate and test hypotheses, for example, compare their Phylogenetic predictions with the Fossil Record, and apply Phylogenetic principles to Human Epidemic Diseases such as HIV and SarsCoV2.
3. Pre-requisites: Students will be able to take more advanced courses in Evolution and Bioinformatics in future semesters.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate mastery of the foundational knowledge and core concepts of Human Evolution by answering graded multiple choice questions and/or short answer questions in class workbooks and in the Final Exam, and/or by delivering oral presentations to peers in class.
- Apply foundational knowledge and core concepts to make predictions by answering graded multiple choice questions and/or short answer questions in class, and in the Final Exam, and/or by delivering oral presentations to peers in class.
- Propose, predict and present a model on the Future of Human Evolution by gathering information from research articles, texts, the web, and other relevant established sources, and by using the RPI Library, assessment is by oral presentations to peers in class.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Compositions/Interactives (INT) and Quizzes (QINT) are each worth 5 points, for a total of 10 points each class day starting from the second day. Simulations (SIM) are worth 20 points each. Maximum possible points is 100 [= 6 INT X 5 points each + 6 QINT X 5 points each + 2 SIM X 20 points each] points.

Your Interactive Assessments are worth a total of **60 points**:

- INT (5 points each) X 6
- QINT (5 points each) X 6

There will be interactive discussions in labs. Submit your reflections, individually or as a team/group on any aspect (announced in class) of Human Evolution discussed in labs. Your reflections/discussions, in the form of oral debate/presentations/Q&A sessions, on any topic such as human paleoclimate, language, tools, art, culture, technology, AI or any other will reinforce your learning experience.

Your Simulations are worth a total of **40 points**:

- EVI (20 points)

There will be specimens in labs that you will analyze to generate your hypothesis (cladogram). You are encouraged to partake in Field Trips. The Field Trips, such as to the NYS Museum in Albany will introduce you to Big Data stored in public archives for use by scientists.
