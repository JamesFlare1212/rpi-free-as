## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Course Description

This is an introductory course in Biology. It is intended for undergraduates, both non-majors and majors. It serves as the first course for life science majors and for students interested in the health professions.

Laboratory activities include scientific computing in interactive virtual environments using Python & R for data processing, analysis, and visualization. Students generate hypotheses via simulations, and share their ideas in interactive in-person mini-groups. Hands-on wet labs impart basic cell and molecular biological skills.

The goal is for students to gain biological literacy by applying the scientific method. Students will use quantitative tools, develop data dexterity, and analytical thinking skills and learn basic laboratory skills. This is a D1 Data Intensive Course.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. Principle Concepts: Students will be able to grasp and master introductory biological principles.
2. Protocols: Students will be able to connect and link core concepts, and apply them to the bases of protocols used in the corequisite laboratory course.
3. Peer Interaction: Students will be able to discuss and analyze topics in the classroom with peers.
4. Pre-requisites: Students will be able to satisfy prerequisites, in order to take advanced courses in the biological sciences.
5. Plan of Study: Students enrolled are mostly non-biology majors, and after taking this course will be able to connect biological concepts, and compare and contrast these principles, in an interdisciplinary manner, with what they are learning in their major line and plan of study.
6. Present Problems: Students will be able to connect biological concepts, and apply concepts learned in the biology classroom to presently occurring complex current issues in the outside real world.
7. Predict and Propose: Students will be able evaluate solutions to complex issues, predict fates of current plans, and propose possible alternatives or improvements to avoid any negative plan outcomes.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The laboratory course content is divided into different types of laboratory experiments:

1. In vitro Labs
2. In vivo Labs
3. In silico Labs

Each laboratory draws from fundamental principles from one, two or all of the units, at once, that taught are in the corequisite BIOL 1010 coursework, such as those listed below, but also additional research work from the real world:

1. Ecology
2. Evolution
3. Genetics and Medicine

Thus, each experiment encourages students to find connections between disparate biological concepts and fundamental principles in the textbook, classroom and the real world.

## Course Content:

The Scientific Method is applied to understand fundamental concepts in:

- Cell Biology - Mitosis, Meiosis, and the Cell Cycle.
- Molecular Biology - DNA replication, Protein Synthesis, and Regulation.
- Microbiology - Prokaryotic (versus Eukaryotic) cell size, shape & morphology.
- Virology and Protein Structure - SarsCov2 genome & spike protein data visualization using PDB.
- Biochemistry - Structures of amino acids, nucleotides, atoms and bonds using an Artificial Intelligence Learning Platform, and PDB.
- MolBio Techniques/Skills - Wet Lab extraction of DNA from plant/animal samples collected via RPI campus Field Trip, Gel electrophoresis & PCR preparation.
- Medicine - Drug/vaccine design and genetic engineering using Simbio’s simulations.
- Genetics - Mendelian Patterns, probabilities, ratios, Chi Square Analysis using corn purple/yellow plump/wrinkled mutant kernels or Fruit Flies.
- Ecology, Biodiversity and Conservation - Ecosystems, Biodiversity, Conservation, Epidemiology, eg., R0, vaccine critical threshold, and or Hardy Weinberg Allele frequency and other calculations, R Scripting.
- Evolution, Phylogeny - Cladistics, Bioinformatics, and basic Computational Data Analytics using Python scripting.

ACADEMIC TIPS: How to Study: In studying a subject like biology, which contains an inexhaustible amount of material, it is important to study actively, sorting the material into at least two categories: familiar, understood concepts and facts on the one hand, and new or difficult concepts or facts on the other. Plainly, once you do this, you know what it is you have to concentrate on in order to increase your grasp of the subject.

N = total number of individuals in population.

n = sample size

df = the number of categories that are free to vary; df = n-1 in a single sample.
