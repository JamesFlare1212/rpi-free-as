## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Demonstrate knowledge of concepts and theories relevant to communicating health information.
- • Evaluate the appropriateness of a variety of communication strategies for a given topic/issue/population.
- • Identify elements of campaign design important for influencing individual and population health.
- • Evaluate the effectiveness of health campaigns.
- • Demonstrate spoken and written communication competence

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Course Assignments:**

1. **Reading response: discussion leader (20%) and discussion participant (20%)**

- ü Each student will sign up for two reading assignments, and lead the discussions in class. Students should prepare 8-15 slides to help lead the discussion. The discussion leader should 1) provide a summary of the article, 2) challenge or support the authors’ methodology, reasoning, or thesis, 3) identify exciting/interesting points raised by the author(s), 4) include two memorable quotes from the paper, and 5) raise two or three thought-provoking questions to prompt the discussions in class.
- ü Students who are not the discussion leader of the day will be assigned a role in the discussion activity. You should effectively play the role, actively respond to discussion questions, and participate in the discussion activity.

1. Presenter/discussion leader: the main presenter of the day
2. Facilitator: keeps discussion move forward by asking the other group members questions/or providing explanations.
3. Devil’s Advocate: introduces alternative ideas and respectively raises constructive disagreements.
4. Connector: articulates any connections between what other classmates have said/between the current topics with previous topics or explore how the discussion can be potential applied in the real world.
5. Wildcard: participates in various roles as needed.

\* Your 1st reading response (slides to lead the discussion) should be completed by the end of ***5/31***. Your 2nd reading response (slides to lead the discussion) should be completed before the end of ***6/24***.***Your slides should be submitted before the noon of the day when you lead the discussion.***

\* Students’ participation performance will be self-assessed and confirmed/challenged by the instructor.

2. **Module Exercises 20%**

Module Exercises will be used to help you understand the topics. These will include, but may not be limited to, writing/speaking assignments, paired assignments, or group assignments. Please be prepared to participate in these activities as well as class discussions. The assignments missed due to absence can't be made up, unless in case of emergency that can be proved by official documents. Please do not ask for an excused absence unless there are exceptional circumstances.

3. **Health Communication Campaign Plan &Presentation (group) 30%**

Students will work in a group and write up a health communication campaign plan (targeting college students) including the following components:

1. Planning and strategy development: research on a health problem, why a communication program can help, intended audiences, objectives, and communication strategies.
2. Developing materials: design activities or messages (poster slide, graphics, video, etc.) and pretest the messages.
3. Implementation: discuss how you will distribute messages to your intended audiences.
4. Assessments: evaluate your health promotion message and identify refinements.

4. **Attendance and Participation 10%**

Attendance/participation grade is not “free” points automatically given just for showing up. It must be earned. This grade is calculated based on the quality and quantity of your responses to discussion questions (e.g. in-class assignments), and your attendance to the class meetings requested by the instructor.

- A grade: Student always arrives to class on time and stays for the duration of class; is always engaged and attentive throughout the class period; regularly and actively responds to questions and participates in class discussions; never displays disruptive and/or disrespectful behavior during class; and consistently arrives to class prepared to discuss the assigned readings.
- B grade: Student sometimes arrives to class late and/or does not stay for the duration of class; is sometimes disengaged and/or distracted during class period; responds to questions and participates in class discussion, but not regularly; rarely displays disruptive and/or disrespectful behavior during class; and usually arrives to class prepared to discuss the assigned readings.
- C grade: Student often arrives to class late and/or does not stay for the duration of class; does not always listen to others’ ideas and opinions; rarely participates in class discussion; occasionally displays disruptive and/or disrespectful behavior in class; and rarely arrives to class prepared to discuss the assigned readings.
- D grade: Student rarely arrives to class on time and/or stays for the duration of class; often interrupts when other students speak; never participates in class discussion; almost always displays disruptive and/or disrespectful behavior during class; and almost never arrives to class prepared to discuss the assigned readings.
- E/F grade: Student always arrives to class late and/or does not stay for the duration of class; always interrupts when other students speak; shows obvious lack of willingness to participate in class; always displays disruptive and disrespectful behavior during class; and never arrives to class prepared to discuss the assigned readings.

5. **Extra Credit 1%:** Schedule an appointment with COMM+D to discuss a speaking or writing assignment (e.g. case study presentation, theory application paper, or final presentation). For more information about scheduling an appointment, please click on the link: https://rpi.mywconline.com/ . For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own.
