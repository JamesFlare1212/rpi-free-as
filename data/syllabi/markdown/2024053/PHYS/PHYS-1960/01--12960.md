## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a short, focused non-credit course that is intended to strengthen students’ tools and knowledge to address mechanics problem solving. There are ten on-campus intensive phase classes in which students practice group problem solving and laboratory activities. The intensive phase of the course is then followed by weeks of remote guided study and practice.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

University Physics, 15th Edition by Young and Freedman, Pearson Publishers

Required license: Modified Mastering Physics with association to Young and Freedman 15th edition.

This is also the text that will be used in the regular PHYS 1100 (Physics I) course.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. Students will demonstrate the following development of skills and knowledge:
   1. Development of conceptual understanding through observation of physical phenomena.
   2. Reasoning about physical phenomena on the basis of available evidence.
   3. Use of experimental data in the development, testing, and refinement of theoretical models.
   4. Evaluation of datasets containing extraneous information and or noise in regard to identifying relevant/important information.

2. Applying course material to improve thinking skills through quantitative problem solving.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Measurement and analysis.

Problem solving approaches.

Motion and Newtonian mechanics in one and two dimensions.

Applications of energy and momentum conservation laws.

A selection of topics from rotational motion, and vibrations and waves.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are required to attend classes when on-campus. Students are required to have weekly meetings with mentors and/or instructors and TAs during the remote off-campus phase of the course.

### Other Course Policies:

## Homework:

Homework problems and tutorials will be due on a regular basis. Due times will be posted for every problem set. Most of the homework will be assigned through the online homework and study system, MasteringPhysics. Written homework problems will also be assigned to be handed in for grading; they will be graded by class staff.

**Deadlines for all assignments will be posted for each assignment**. Keep your work for later reference and for discussions during Office Hours.

Both online MasteringPhysics homework and written homework problems will also be assigned during the guided phase of the course. Those will be due on a weekly basis, with due times indicated for every assignment. Submission of the written assignments during the guided phase will be done using Gradescope, with scores linked to the class LMS.

## Topical Activities:

Topical activities/labs will also be assigned. **The due dates for those will be posted,** and they will be submitted in class during the on-campus intensive phase portion of the course, and online during the off-campus guided phase either using the LMS assignment facility or Gradescope. Assignment scores will be recorded in the LMS grade book. Activities may be worked in groups – this, in fact, is encouraged – but each student must hand in their own written assignment sheets.

## Exams:

You will be evaluated on your performance on two course exams. Students will have the opportunity to prepare on one side of a single sheet of 8.5″ by 11″ paper as an aid for calculations. Students will be coached on the preparation of this note sheet. Activities and worksheets are open-book, open-source.

Exams may consist of multiple-choice questions and/or open-ended work problems. Multiple-choice questions usually do not receive partial credit. Programmable calculators are allowed but you will not need any features beyond those found on a simple scientific calculator. You may not store additional materials such as formulas, homework solutions, etc. on your calculator.

## Participation:

**Successful completion of the Physics Bridge class (and qualification for scholarships associated with the Bridge Scholar Program) requires that you participate actively in the class, for the intensive and guided phases of the course. This includes regularly attending lectures and mentoring sessions, working and completing all activity assignments and homework (online and written) by their designated due dates, and taking the course exams.** To encourage your participation, points will be awarded each week for your participation in mentoring sessions and for the completion of class activities and homework each week (“classwork participation points”). **If enough participation points are not accrued by the end of the course, we will not be able to recommend to the Program administrators that a scholar has successfully completed the Physics portion of the Bridge Scholar Class.**

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

The development of teamwork skills is a core objective in this course. Hence, all students are expected to participate actively in a collaborative group when working on their class activities. However, each student must turn in her/his own individual activity write-up containing only work to which she/he contributed. In other words, we expect you to participate and not just copy other people’s work.

When you take a quiz or examination, any type of collaboration is considered cheating. Copying answers from another student’s exam without his or her knowledge is considered cheating only for the one who copies. For online quizzes and exams, using aids external to those that are explicitly allowed (e.g., formula sheets, calculators) such as internet searches, Discord channels, etc. is the same as collaboration and is therefore cheating.

Discussing homework problems and getting help with them before class is permitted and encouraged. Looking at someone else’s solution to learn how to do it before class is permitted, but direct copying is not permitted.
