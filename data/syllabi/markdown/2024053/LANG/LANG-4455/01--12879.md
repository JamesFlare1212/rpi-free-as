*Required Materials:*

You basically only need one Chinese writing brush (app. 1 ¼ inches tip), some rice paper, and liquid ink or ink with an ink stone set. You can use scratch paper or newspaper for daily practices and rice paper for projects.

We will be using a free textbook at no cost to you. However, it is important for you to have your own writing tools, such as ink and brush, for practice.

Additionally, for your art show project, you will need special paper. I recommend holding off on purchasing the special paper initially, because you may come up with new ideas once the class starts. However, you do need to prepare ink and brush for routine practice.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- By the end of this course, students will be able to: 1. Demonstrate fundamental skills: Acquire basic techniques and skills in Chinese calligraphy, including brush handling, stroke order, and ink application. 2. Explore aesthetic and intellectual values: Analyze and appreciate the aesthetic and intellectual values embodied in Chinese calligraphy, including the relationship between form and meaning, the expressive power of strokes, and the balance of composition. 3. Create projects based on artistic interpretation: Apply learned calligraphy techniques and concepts to create original projects that reflect their artistic interpretation and understanding of the art form. 4. Recognize the wellness benefits of calligraphy: Understand the wellness benefits associated with calligraphic practice, such as improved mental health, concentration, posture, and spiritual calmness. 5. Develop cultural awareness by exploring Chinese calligraphy's historical and cultural context. 6. Cultivate openness to diverse art forms: Develop an open and receptive attitude towards different art forms and artistic expressions.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic dishonesty encompasses various actions, including cheating, fabricating, or falsifying information or sources, improper collaboration, submitting the same paper for multiple classes without permission, and plagiarism. Plagiarism occurs when writers intentionally or unintentionally use someone else’s language, ideas, or materials without giving proper credit and citation. Familiarizing yourself with RPI’s Student Rights and Responsibilities regarding academic integrity is essential.

Plagiarism is defined as the act of taking credit for someone else’s work, whether done in deliberately or unintentionally. Examples of plagiarism include submitting an essay, in whole or in part, written by someone else (such as a friend or an internet source) and claiming it as your own, as well as incorporating information or ideas from research materials without properly citing the sources.

In this class, plagiarism is unacceptable. If any instances of accidental or deliberate use of someone else’s words, ideas, or work without attribution are identified, you will first receive a warning. Failure to rectify the situation may result in penalties, such as receiving a failing grade on the paper, a failing grade in the course, and/or being referred to the Dean of Students.
