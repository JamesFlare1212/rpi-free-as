## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Being one of the most revered arts in China, Chinese calligraphy (the art of writing – *书法 shufa*) is considered both a top visual art form and self-expression and cultivation. This course is an introduction to the importance of calligraphy in Chinese culture as well as its principles and techniques. It provides an insight into the fundamentals of ancient art and examines the aesthetic values and intellectual metaphors that Chinese calligraphy embodies. Students will learn (1) how Chinese calligraphy has been used as a visual and narrative art form, (2) gain essential knowledge and skills of Chinese calligraphy through hands-on practice with brush and ink, and (3) create calligraphy projects based on their artistic interpretation. This course is taught in English. No knowledge of Chinese is required.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students in this course will have knowledge of Chinese calligraphy from theory to practice. Lectures, presentations, and in-depth discussions introduce the history and aesthetic principles of Chinese calligraphy, then expand to the evolution of Chinese calligraphy and development across cultures and beyond traditional formats. Daily step-by-step practices and projects guide students from holding a brush to interpreting and creating their own artwork. This course encourages students to develop a deeper interest and understanding of the Chinese language and its culture.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Understand and appreciate the art form of Chinese writing – calligraphy 2. Differentiate major scripts of Chinese calligraphy 3. Demonstrate techniques and skills in writing Chinese calligraphy 4. Interpret aesthetics of Chinese calligraphy and its role in Chinese culture 5. Apply the knowledge and skills and design one’s own artwork

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic dishonesty includes cheating, fabricating or falsifying information or sources, improper collaboration, submitting the same paper for different classes without permission, and plagiarism. Plagiarism occurs when writers deliberately or unintentionally use another person’s language, idea, or materials and present them as their own without properly acknowledging and citing the source. Familiarize yourself with RPI’s Student Rights and Responsibilities.

Plagiarism is defined as the act of taking credit for someone else’s work whether deliberately or unintentionally. This includes but is not limited to turning in all or part of an essay written by someone other than yourself (a friend, an internet source, etc.) and claiming it as your own, and including information or ideas from research material without citing the course.

Plagiarism in this class is *unacceptable*. Accidental or willful use of someone else’s words, ideas, or work without attribution will first be warned. If not corrected, will then be penalized by a failing grade on the paper, a failing grade in the course, and/or referral to the Dean of Students.
