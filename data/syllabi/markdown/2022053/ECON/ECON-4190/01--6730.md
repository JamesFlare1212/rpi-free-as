## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course investigates the significance of economic globalization, covering the following topics: international trade and financial flows, technological innovation and intellectual property, technology transfer, national government and transnational corporations, natural resources, health and the environment, impacts on selected industries and countries, and roles of the world trade organization and international monetary fund. The major controversies surrounding globalization are identified, and alternative arguments are evaluated based on available evidence.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

“International Economics: Theory and Policy” by Krugman, Obstfeld, Melitz, 11th edition. MyEconLab, Pearson.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- demonstrate knowledge of the major economic theories in international economics.
- use formal economic models to make prediction of the outcomes of trade and government trade policies.
- clearly explain how international economics and globalization affects output, incomes, and welfare.
- demonstrate an understanding of major economic events, government policies, and international institutions that have affected the patterns of international trade and globalization.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Online Homework:  
A total of 6 to 8 online homework assignments will be given throughout the semester. Each homework is designed to provide students with an opportunity to practice the concepts learned in class. Homework assignments are available on MyEconLab and must be submitted online on the scheduled due date. It is your responsibility to check MyEconLab frequently for any update on the due dates. Late assignments are not accepted (NO EXCEPTIONS). To mitigate this policy, I will drop the lowest homework grade, or alternately, you may skip one online homework during the semester. Any questions on the online homework should be addressed to the TA.  
Writing Assignment:  
A total of 5 writing homework assignments will be given throughout the semester which are designed to give students the opportunity to work through the models discussed in class. At the end of some lectures, I may give you some hints on the questions corresponding to the lecture included in the writing assignments. These assignments will be available on LMS and are due at 11:59 pm on the scheduled due dates. You can either type your answers or scan your handwritten results and upload it on LMS (use a PDF version). Make sure your answers are clear and readable in case of choosing a handwritten version; you may receive no credit if the answers are not easy to read. Late assignments are not accepted (NO EXCEPTIONS) and will receive a zero grade. Any questions on the writing homework assignments should be addressed to the TA.  
Exams:  
The two exams will be given on MyEconLab during our normal class hours. They will include multiple choice as well as short answer questions and it's your responsibility to submit them at the specified times. In case you can’t make an exam as scheduled due to health or family issues (which could typically be considered as acceptable excuses and are at the discretion of the instructor to decide on a case-by-case basis), you should let me know in advance to get it approved. I will then make arrangements for you to take the exam early, depending upon the circumstances of your absence. There is also a take-home portion of the exam which will be posted on LMS after the due time of the online version and you will have 24 hours to finish it. The submission of the take-home portion would be similar to the submission of writing homework assignments.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic Dishonesty:  
As a future financial professional, your most valuable asset will be your personal integrity. Cases of academic dishonesty are viewed as a serious violation of the University’s student code of conduct. Examples of academic dishonesty include, but are not limited to:  
• copying or sharing answers on graded homework assignments or exams  
• doing assignments for other students  
Academic dishonesty will not be tolerated and penalties can include canceling a student's enrollment without a grade or receiving an F for the course. While problems of academic dishonesty rarely come up, feel free to bring them to my attention, and I will work with you to address them appropriately.  
Students with Learning Disabilities:  
Any student with a learning disability needing academic adjustments is requested to provide me with a letter explaining the necessary accommodations as soon as possible to arrange for timely and appropriate adjustments or accommodations. The letter should come from the Director of Disabilities Services.
