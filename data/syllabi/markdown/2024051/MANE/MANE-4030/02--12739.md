## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Homework:** All homework assignments will count towards the final grade except the lowest one.

Homework is meant to be a learning experience and is not meant to be a vessel of just simple reiteration previously learned concepts. Problems sets will help learn and understand the fundamental theory that is difficult to convey in lectures and students should pay close attention to the patterns and concepts that arise in homework that are not discussed in lectures. Homework assignments will be posted on LMS and Gradescope. Submission of homework assignments is via Gradescope only. Homework assignment submissions are due by 11:59 PM on the day listed. The instructor will allow up to 8 hours of late submission without any penalty.

**Exams:** All three exams count towards the final grade. The three exams will be held in-class. Absence from an exam without a prior documented valid medical excuse will yield zero for that exam. Without a prior documented valid medical excuse, a replacement exam will not be provided.

Absence from any two or more exams will automatically result in a F grade. The nature of the topics in this class are comprehensive so students are expected to be familiar with fundamental concepts taught in the earlier part of the semester which will be required for exams held in the later part of the semester.

During exams, you are permitted your class notes (including homework assignments) and a physical copy of the textbook (Mechanical Design of Machine Elements and Machines, Second Edition, by Collins, Busby, and Staab), a pocket calculator, and a straight edge. You are not permitted to use any other electronic devices such as laptops, tablets, phones, smartwatches, etc.

Under special circumstances such as covid exposure, other sicknesses, natural disasters, unforeseeable events etc., exams may be offered online so students have the best possible chance to complete exams on time. Instructions regarding such exams will be posted in class materials and on LMS.

Replacement exams will be offered if a student is absent from a scheduled exam for prior documented valid excuse. Students need to inform the instructor if they are unable to take an exam as soon as possible, so a replacement exam can be scheduled in due time. Beyond the last day of classes for this class, no exams or replacement exams can be held.

The format of the replacement exam is at the discretion of the instructor. The instructor will consider the number of students who missed the exam, available time remaining for satisfactory progress in class before offering replacement exams. Due to the large class size and the remaining time in the semester, replacement exams will generally be held orally and recorded. Note that for any replacement exams, the instructor would still need a formal written excuse from the student before the replacement exams can be conducted so a record of excuses can be maintained. Replacement exams for replacement exams will not be offered.

**Grading Policy:** Following the course catalog, A = excellent, B = good, C = average, D = passed, and F = failed. Typically, the top 15-20% of the class earns A, and if you have at least a 93% average you will definitely earn an A, the next 20-25% earns B and if you have at least an 83% average you will earn at least a B, and the next 30-40% earns C and if you have at least a 73% average you will earn at least a C. At least a 50% average is needed to pass.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course is designed to introduce the student to the design process through a thorough study of mechanical design. Problem definition and solution requiring both analysis and optimization are emphasized, while studying both the fundamentals of mechanical design and specific mechanical components.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Introduction to the design of mechanical components and integrated assemblies. Loads, stresses, and strains. Failure phenomena and material selection. Mechanical components including shafts, couplings, bearings, gears, springs, clutches, brakes, screws and fasteners, and bonded joints. (4 credit hours)

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Demonstrate an ability to apply knowledge of mathematics, science and engineering to analyze the stress and deflection in a variety of machine elements; Predict failure in machine elements subjected to static, cyclic, and impact loads; Design and analyze a variety of commonly used machine elements.
- • Demonstrate an ability to identify, formulate, and solve engineering problems involving stress and deflections in machine elements, identification of potential failure modes, and performing the appropriate analysis.
- • Demonstrate an ability to use the techniques, skills, and modern engineering tools necessary for engineering practice such as drawing free-body-diagrams, relating loading conditions to stresses, identifying potential failure modes and appropriate analysis techniques.

The Class Dean will communicate the receipt of the note (with expected end date) to the relevant faculty.”
