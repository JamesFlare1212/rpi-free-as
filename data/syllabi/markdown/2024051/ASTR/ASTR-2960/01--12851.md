## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The study of cosmology is currently at the forefront of modern astrophys- ical research. As technology continues to advance, we continue to gain insights into questions about the Universe long thought unanswerable. At its roots though, Cosmology began as a philosophical quest towards explaining some of the most fundamental questions of nature. How did the Universe begin? Did it have a beginning in the first place? Is there an end? What is the nature of the Universe? Do we, as humans, play any role in it? Over the course of this semester we will pon- der these questions ourselves, explore the musings and observations of the early astronomers, and discuss the modern quests towards finding some answers to those fundamental questions. We will also explore some of the more exotic aspects of modern physics and astronomy such as: special and general relativity, black holes, the nature of space and time, and the Big Bang. We’ll take a more conceptual, rather than mathematical, approach to these topics and so this course will be suitable for majors and non-majors but knowledge of algebra and an understanding scientific graphs, tables, and equations is required.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- An ability to apply the scientific method, learned through the lens of its applications in cosmology.
- Knowledge of the history of cosmological thought that led to today’s understanding of the Universe.
- Gain an appreciation of the content and implications of Einstein’s theories of special and general relativity
- Knowledge of the major elements of the Big Bang theory of the Universe.
- An ability to connect foundational knowledge of cosmology to give context to modern unsolved problems.
- An awareness of the modern tools being used to address cosmological inqueries.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Homework:

There will be a homework assignment every week. Homework assignment are due on Mondays at the beginning of class time. Homework solutions are to be submitted in whatever hard copy format you prefer as long as it is legible. Late homework will be marked zero. The homework will account for 25% of the final grade.

The lowest homework score will be dropped to compute your final grade in the course. This means you miss one homework assignment without penalty.  
The homework assignments are crucial in mastering the course material. Therefore, all homework assignments should be prepared by the student. AI-generated submissions are not permitted and will be treated as plagiarism. Ethics still matter and plagiarism is a serious academic integrity offense. The university’s policy on plagiarism still applies to any uncited or improperly cited use of work by other human beings, or submission of work by other human beings as your own.

Quizzes

In order to give you credit for doing the reading and attending, there will be quizzes given randomly throughout the semester during the class meeting. The quizzes will be in form of multiple choice questions and/or short answer questions. The quizzes may be given at the beginning of class on previous material or at the end of class on material of that lecture. In the latter case, you may use the notes you took during the lecture.

The lowest two quiz scores will be dropped in calculating the average score. The quiz average will account for 25% of the final grade. No makeup quizzes will be given.
