## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course covers structure, processing, and property relationships for industrially important plastic materials. Fundamentals of fluid flow, rheology, heat transfer, and transport phenomena are applied to analyze polymer processing operations including extrusion, multi-phase mixing, calendering, blow molding, thermoforming, fiber spinning, compression molding, injection molding, and recycling.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students who successfully complete this course will be able to:  
1\. apply mathematical methods to solve problems related to the theory of polymer rheology and processing operations.  
2\. identify techniques, skills, and modern engineering tools necessary to manufacture plastic products such as pipes, bottles, automobile dashboards, etc.  
3\. apply a commercial finite element simulation package to design an injection molding operation for mass production of a plastic part.  
4\. explain contemporary technical, economic, social, and political aspects of the global plastics industry.  
5\. describe emerging trends in materials and methods, illustrating the dynamic nature of the plastics industry.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Introduction to polymers & processing, glassy and semicrystalline polymers; single-screw extrusion, twin screw extrusion, thermoforming, polymer melt rheology, mixing operations, molding processes, secondary shaping processes, plastics recycling, micro/nano lithography.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Grading:**  
Quizzes: approximately five to seven, 25 % of course grade  
Design project: one, 25 % of course grade  
Exams: two, 25 % of course grade each

**Homework**. Approximately 7 to 10 homework problem sets will be assigned during the semester. Homeworks should be finished on the assigned date, but will not be collected or graded. Quizzes of up to 20 minutes duration will follow selected homeworks, after we have reviewed the solutions in class. Exact repeats of homework exercises may appear on quizzes. No make-up quizzes will be given.

**Exams**. Two midterm exams will be given in class time. It is the student's responsibility to attend or arrange for a make-up with the instructor prior to the exam date in case of illness or emergency. Exams typically contain a mixture of essays, multiple choice questions, short answer questions, and numerical problems.

**Design Project.** Students will use Autodesk Moldflow Plastics Adviser to simulate a plastics injection molding process and optimize processing conditions for a given set of material properties. Students will submit their final projects and a brief term paper describing their findings.
