## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course is arranged such that the basic techniques of restriction digests, ligations, PCR, transformations are covered in the first few weeks of the semester. In addition to learning techniques, you will also learn a few features of Benchling, such as restriction mapping, making virtual plasmids and translating segments of DNA to make sure you will engineer the correct fusion construct. Most laboratory work actually takes place on a computer where the DNA molecule is designed for biomedical research. Once you learn the basic techniques, you will use these techniques to make a GFP fusion protein using the promoter of a *C. elegans* gene to drive GFP expression as a major part of an independent project in the second half of the semester. Realize that GFP will only be synthesized if the constructed plasmid is injected into the worm gonad.  
Students should work in groups of 2 for the duration of the semester. Lab assessment grades are based on attendance AND effort, attention to detail, analysis, knowing the lab assignments, efficient planning of experiments, fluency in using the Benchling software program and evidence of increasing skill and knowledge with the basic laboratory techniques used in this course. Your laboratory notebook must also be complete with conclusions after each step in the cloning of your DNA fragment and gel pictures.

At the beginning of this course a list of *C. elegans* genes will be provided. You will select one gene from this list that is of interest and attempt to PCR and make a GFP expression plasmid in the second half of the semester. You will design primers to amplify the promoter region of this gene and a small portion of the coding region and put it in frame with green fluorescent protein (GFP). If time allows, the construct will be injected into *C. elegans* to see if the construct works and examine the GFP expression pattern. Most of the genes on this list have already been cloned and expression patterns have been described. However, other have not and you can hypothesize protein localization in humans by seeing if the homologs of these genes have been studied in the fly, mouse, or human cells to learn more about the gene using online databases.

Your final paper is focused on your gene of interest: design of your primers, describing all the molecular techniques used to make a GFP expression construct, and using BLAST analysis to compare the *C. elegans* gene to homologs in another species.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

- Students will gain experience in basic molecular biology techniques, including pipetting, bacterial transformation, plasmid isolation, restriction digests, ligations and primer design.
- Students will gain knowledge of experimental procedures used in cloning DNA and initiate a corrective course of action if an experiment fails.
- Students will be efficient in the use of certain features of the molecular biology software program, Benchling.
- Demonstrate the ability to work independently to complete a scientific project.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Proficiency in content knowledge concerning molecular biology protocols including primer design and DNA cloning steps (e.g. calculations for volumes of enzymes, bacterial transformations and DNA quantitation)
- 2\. Interpret and paraphrase genomic and gene expression data from online databases and illustrate proficiency in using the molecular biology software program, Benchling.
- 3\. The ability to develop and maintain a laboratory notebook and reporting results using well-labeled graphs and pictures and conclusions (success or failure) at each step in the procedure.
- 4\. The ability to produce laboratory reports in a primary journal article format through independent learning and writing about familiar subjects at a high level of competence.
- 5\. The ability to communicate research goals, purpose, results and conclusions in a clear and concise manner
- 6\. Professional behaviors in all lab work and develop independence including the planning and managing of time to complete an independent project.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

- This course requires that students work together, in groups of two or three. Lab work and data analysis must therefore be shared within each group. Consultation between groups on lab procedures and data handling is also allowed; however, **each student is required to keep an independent** **and detailed notebook**. Copying of data from one notebook to another **is only permitted during lab time** unless explicit permission to do otherwise is obtained from the professor in *writing*. A verbal agreement does NOT count. The notebooks are considered the property of the course until the semester is over.
- Unless otherwise indicated, you should cooperate with one another in and outside of class on the solution of problems. You may collaborate with your lab partner regarding experimental results such as a PCR reaction and sequencing data. Each student’s research report and final lab report must be **independently** written. You may **NOT** collaborate on examinations or misrepresent another person's work as your own on examinations and written lab reports. You may not bring crib sheets to examinations unless specifically stated by the professor in an email or a written announcement, and you may not write on or alter examination materials that you submit for re-grading. The minimum penalty for cheating is an F on the assignment in question; clear cut or persistent cheating will result in a grade of F overall.
