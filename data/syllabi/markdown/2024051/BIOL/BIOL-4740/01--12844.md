## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The first part of the semester will be spent learning about mammalian cell biology and associated cell culture techniques. You will use techniques that include PCR, immunocytochemistry, lipofection, and confocal fluorescence microscopy to obtain information about cellular activity, cell structure, and gene expression. You will develop the experimental design for an **independent project** during the first part of the semester and submit a formal proposal for your project along with appropriate protocols. In the second part of the semester, you will carry out the research you proposed. At the end of the semester, you will present your findings in a formal presentation to the class and write a publication-quality manuscript. You will also be expected to keep accurate notes of your methods, results, and conclusions. Recitation time will be used for extended lab experiments. Time will be taken during some lab periods to introduce or discuss material as a class. **This is a communication intensive course.**

## Course Goals

### Goals:

- Upon successfully completing this course, students will demonstrate the following:
  1. Proficiency in content knowledge concerning various cell biology research techniques
  2. Ability to design experiments and develop laboratory protocols necessary to complete these experiments
  3. Ability to collect, organize, and analyze data through the maintenance of a laboratory notebook that clearly documents laboratory procedures and presents results using well-labeled figures
  4. Ability to communicate experimental findings through a presentation and a publication-quality manuscript and class presentation
  5. Ability to critically analyze scientific literature
  6. Professional behavior and independence in a laboratory environment

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Lab Notebooks**

- ­ Your electronic lab notebook will be provided to you as a Google Docs file that you can edit; physical notebooks will not be used. Therefore, you **MUST bring your laptop to all in-person labs** in order to record that day's work. Do not work from a local copy of your notebook or keep a separate document as a working notebook – all notes, data, and calculations should be kept in your Google Docs notebook!
- ­ Good record keeping skills are an important part of your professional development. You must keep complete notes during every lab since most of the work we do builds on previous work throughout the semester. Further information can be found under the *Lab Notebook* section on LMS.
- ­ Klein will check lab notebooks twice this semester. These dates will **not** be announced in advance, so **your lab notebook should be ready at all times.** When your notebook is checked, you will be asked to export your Google Docs notebook as a PDF file and submit it to LMS. More detailed instructions are given on LMS.

**Presentations**

- ­ Students will be expected to a give **journal club** style presentation of a peer reviewed scientific research article on the date marked on the *Lab Schedule*. This type of presentation is largely informal but should include a background section, an analysis of the article's findings, and critiques of the article. This presentation is given by either individuals or as groups; Dr. Klein will announce the policy.
- ­ Each group of students will present their **independent project** work at the end of the semester as part of their Project Presentation.
- ­ All presentations are due at the **start of lab** on the days specified in the *Lab Schedule*.

**Written Assessments**

- ­ Written assessments, including quizzes, are due on LMS on the dates/times specified in the *Lab Schedule*. Due date times can be checked on LMS.
- ­ Collaborating with students on non-group activities like quizzes is **NOT** See *Grading Policies & Academic Dishonesty* for more details.

**Lab Citizenship**

- ­ A lab requires its members to show respect to other lab members, their experiments, and their shared equipment. Each student is required to be a participating lab citizen and demonstrate their participation by (1) completing their part of shared tasks appropriately, (2) keeping all supplies and experiments in their appropriate locations, (3) discarding all waste appropriately, especially biohazards, (4) maintaining a clean and safe work space, and (5) maintaining the lab for our use and the use of other courses in the same room.
- ­ Every student is entitled to full participation in class without interruption. Therefore, all students are expected to be in lab and **prepared to begin on time**. Disruption of class, whether by latecomers, disruptive devices, or inconsiderate behavior, is not acceptable.
- ­ Each infraction of lab policy will result in the **deduction of 1% from your final grade**. You can earn multiple deductions in a single day if your behavior is poor enough.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

- You are expected to attend all lab periods and recitation. If a lab session is held remotely, you will be expected to attend lab via WebEx during the normal lab and recitation meeting times.
- You will be working with a lab partner, and your engaged participation is critical to your ability to collect meaningful results. It is expected you will delegate this responsibility equally among your group members.
- Missing a lab will result in a **deduction of 5%** from your grade and additional deductions for incomplete or missing assessments and lab work.
- If you miss a lab, you must obtain a Dean’s excuse. Students who need to report an illness should contact the Student Health Center via email or call 518-276-6287. Then, you must submit this documentation to LMS under the ***Excused Absences*** section. For example, if you receive a Dean's excuse or doctor’s note via email, "print" this email as a PDF and submit the PDF to LMS; if you receive physical documentation, you must first scan it and then submit the scanned document to LMS.
- For any excused absence from lab, your lab work must still be made up. **If you foresee yourself missing a lab, contact Dr. Klein to coordinate a make-up time if a make-up is even possible**; this must be done well ahead of time. Failure to make up lab work will result in the same penalty as missing the lab.

**LIMITING THE SPREAD OF INFECTIOUS DISEASES**

- **Masks:** Wearing masks is **encouraged** for all students, faculty, TA’s, and UM’s in lecture. If you feel like you have a cold or other respiratory illness, please do your fellow classmates the courtesy of wearing a mask.
- **Traffic Flow and Social Distancing:** Students and faculty will respect the need for social distancing to the degree possible by the setting. All personnel will move in and out of rooms as per the instructions of the faculty/administration. Students are expected to follow any printed traffic flow statements posted in all rooms and buildings.
- **Student Health:** If you are ill, suspect you are ill, test positive for COVID-19 within the last 48 hours, or under quarantine for COVID-19, report this to the Student Health Center at (518) 276-6287.
- Refusal to comply with a reasonable request to limit the spread of disease will be treated as a classroom disruption and disciplinary actions and sanctions will be taken through the judicial process of the Student Handbook. This entails: request to change the behavior, request to leave the room, dismissal and referral to Student Life and the Dean of Students.

**LAB SAFETY**

- Follow all rules that Dr. Klein issues.
- Do not engage in any activities that will endanger yourself or those around you.
- **Eating and drinking are not allowed in lab**. Do not bring water bottles to the lab; if you do, you will be asked to leave the bottle in the hallway.
- **Wear gloves** when interacting with any laboratory equipment and **change gloves often**. You will be provided gloves.
- **You must wear safety glasses or goggles** at all times during lab except when using microscopes - disinfect eye pieces of microscopes with ethanol before and after use.
- **Lab coats** are required; you must purchase your own lab coat. Wear clothing that **entirely covers the legs** and torso.
- If you are sick and especially when working in close proximity of others (e.g., next to someone in a biosafety cabinet), you should wear an N95/KN95 mask or surgical-style mask. **Masks help to keep you and others from getting sick**, and under certain conditions they will also help prevent contamination of your cultures.
- You must wear **shoes that enclose** **the entire foot**; footwear like sandals and crocs are not allowed.
- Report any spills or accidents to Dr. Klein immediately.
- When finished, you must clean your work area and return it to the condition you found it in; **disinfect your work areas with 70% ethanol**.
- Follow traffic flow guides in lab; do not crowd other students while they are working at a shared location or on shared equipment.
- Use of **cell phones** is not allowed in lab except when indicated by Dr. Klein.
- Do not adjust valves or any components connected to **pressurized vessels** like liquid nitrogen storage tanks or CO2 tanks.
- Dispose of **biohazardous material**, **chemical hazards**, **glass**, and **metal sharps** in the designated locations.
- Book bags, backpacks, and other personal items must be stored **under your lab bench**, away from other student's belongings. Be sure to avoid making your personal belongings a tripping hazard.
- Violation of any rules stated here, in your lab manual, or verbally stated by Dr. Klein will count as deductions for poor lab citizenship, and major violations can have you removed from the lab.
