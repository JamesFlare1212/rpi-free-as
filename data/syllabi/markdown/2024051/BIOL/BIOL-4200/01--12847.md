## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

An introduction to the concepts and techniques of modern statistics. Computer-based, in-class sessions will emphasize hands-on application of statistical techniques using data and examples drawn from the biological and medical sciences. Pre-class, online exercises will prepare students for in-depth classroom explorations. Topics covered will include sampling, regression, analysis of variance, and factor analysis, MANOVA, maximum likelihood methods, discriminant analysis, time series analysis, and forecasting. The final project requires analysis of a major research-related data set and write-up of results in the format of a scientific paper.

We will be "flipping the classroom" for a portion of this course. Please see the FAQs below.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

What is a flipped classroom?

Briefly, a flipped classroom design changes how students engage with the instructor during class time. Traditionally, class time is reserved for lecture. In a flipped classroom students review the lecture before coming to class. I will upload lecture videos to LMS for you to watch before class. During class time we will work together to review material in an active learning atmosphere.

Why are we doing this?

Historically biology students in Biostatistics are intimidated by the fact that they must learn computer programing to perform statistics. Most biology students have little to no coding experience before taking this course. Besides the required programming skills, students must also learn about the application of various statistical techniques. Traditional Biology students who excel in the lab or field are typically apprehensive of the new required skills in math and computer science. I sought to redesign my course by flipping the classroom to give students more time to apply statistical theory and programming in an active learning flipped classroom setting.

Pedagogical transformation is especially important during the ARCH semester when classes are condensed. The Office of Undergraduate Education and the Teaching and Learning Collaboratory (TLC) designed a fellowship program for instructors to redesign their course for the summer semester.

My goal is to create a learning atmosphere that is more engaging. I want students to not only passively hear the material, but to engage with the learning process on a deeper level. One way to achieve this is to use our time together to focus on problem solving, which more closely emulates the way in which you will use these skills throughout your career. This will be a hands-on experiential learning class setting.

Is the entire class going to be flipped?

No. One learning unit will be flipped (Classes 10 through 15) while the other sections will remain taught in a more traditional format (see class schedule). Note this is the first time this course is being flipped. We may face obstacles, but we will work through them together. Student feedback is crucial to any new pedagogical implementation. Feel free to express your thoughts throughout the course. We will build in time to discuss this change as we approach the flipped class time.

What is required of students during the flipped class? How are the requirements different than other no flipped Biostatistics' classes?

Whether the class is flipped or not, students have homework assignments. In a flipped classroom, the homework will be to watch lectures videos created by the instructor. It is crucial for students watch the videos before coming to class. In class students must be willing to work together and participate in discussions and teamwork. The only way for students to fully participate is by being prepared, and it starts by reviewing the lecture videos. As you watch the videos, I do not expect you to fully understand everything. Please take notes and bring questions with you to class.

How will I be graded? Is this similar to the non-flipped classes?

Each class students will have homework assignments. Homework in non-flipped classes consists of programming modules through data camp, while in flipped classes students need to review materials. Data camp assignments are submitted to LMS for credit. Students also earn attendance credit for each class. Students submit a data challenge for attendance during a traditional class. Students will submit their work they created in groups for attendance during the flipped class.

Quizzes: We will have short quizzes at the beginning of every flipped class to ensure that students are reviewing the material and for the instructor to identify where students may be confused. The quizzes are not designed to me particularly hard. They are designed to ensure that we all come to class with the same baseline knowledge from the lecture videos. Homework for both class styles will be weighed similarly.

Exams: Each unit has two exams: an in-class multiple choice exam and a take home exam. These assessments are the same for flipped classes and traditional classes.

What if I miss a flipped class?

Students should submit the missed class submission on LMS. There will be an opportunity to earn credit for the day, but there is no substitute for an in-person flipped classroom learning experience.

What if I don't watch the videos?

Failure to prepare for flipped class will reduce your ability to participate in class and limit your learning. Students will likely perform poorly on the quiz. Please complete the homework on time.

What will the schedule be for a typical flipped classroom?

Instructor will post lecture videos for students to review before class.

Classes are 2 hours and 5 minutes long 10:30 am to 12:35 pm

From 10:30 am to 12:15 pm students will work in teams. The Instructor and Teaching Assistant will move around to various groups to track progress, ensure students are on task, and answer questions.

10:30 am to 10:45 am: Students will have a quick assessment at the beginning of the class.

10:45 am to 11:30 am: Large group work: Material Creation / Gathering. Three large groups will be created (approximately eight students each group):

Group 1: Research the statistical technique covered today and create a 10-minute presentation including: the type of data required, statistical hypotheses, assumptions, types of graphs, and caveats.

Group 2: Will thoroughly review the computer code required to perform the statistical analysis and will become student experts on this code. This will consist of creating a full explanation of the code including comments, all arguments, assumptions, functions, providing code to visualize the data graphically, and explanation of help files provided in the software and any associated examples within the help files.

Group 3: Will focus on finding online examples and application of the statistical technique. This will include finding online tutorials, creating their own example for the class to work through, a list of common issues, best practices for trouble shooting, and at least one reference in the scientific literature using the statistical technique.

Students will need to upload their material to LMS to receive an attendance grade for each class.

11:30 am to 12:15 pm: Small group sharing: Eight groups of three students (each from a different large group) will share their material with other students.

12:15 pm to 12:35 pm: Instructor wrap up and data challenge: Instructor will give summarizing remarks or clarification as needed. The instructor will provide a challenge example data set for students to try individually. This is a self-assessment for students to determine if they understand the content or to identify any additional questions.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Class attendance is crucial for this type of course. So much happens in each class that you will quickly find yourself dazed and confused if you start missing classes. In-class assignments will be assigned every class meeting.

Excused Absences: There will be times during the semester when you may need to miss a class because of other commitments, unexpected events, or illness. If you are unable to attend class, please complete the absence notification submission on LMS with your excuse for requesting an absence that day.

You are responsible for making up the missed material. I suggest you first reach out to another student in the class to obtain any code that you may have missed. Then review the lecture and the code, and submit the in-class assignment. Please email me with any specific questions after you review the material - we can then find a time to meet if necessary. Missed In-class assignments must be turned in to LMS within one week of returning to class to be eligible for credit.

### Other Course Policies:

## On-line Content & Homework:

To prepare for the hands-on classes, it is essential that you complete all of the on-line, pre-class exercises. The BlackBoard site for Biostatistics is managed by RPILMS. Course-related LMS problems should be referred to the TA and your instructor. General LMS problems should be referred to the Help Desk in the VCC. Homework Late Policy: Due dates will be announced ahead of time (check LMS assignment due dates). Data Camp late work may be submitted up to 1 week late for 50% credit. Assignments submitted after 1 week of the due date are not eligible for credit. Students may not complete late homework for the flipped classes.

## In-Class Sessions:

Most sessions will involve introductory discussion of the topic at hand followed by exercises and hands-on work with the computer. As you are well aware, computers can be a serious distraction in the classroom. In order to create an environment where we can all learn and receive the maximum advantage from the course's computer intensive learning environment, please refrain from texting and surfing the web during class.

## Exams:

There will be 2 quizzes and 3 exams, each covering ~1/3 of the course. Each exam will consist of a take-home component involving analysis of data sets with R using the techniques we have learned about, and an in-class part that will be oriented towards concepts and problems similar to the homework and the examples we go over in class. With a handful of exceptions that you will know about in advance, the formulas needed for the in-class exams will be included as part of the exam. We will discuss the date, the topics, and the format of each exam in-class. There will be no final exam. There will be a data analysis presentation that will be discussed during the course.
