## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Age and origin of the Earth, internal constituents, and energy sources; how plates move, oceans develop, resources accumulate, and mountains rise. Gives non-specialists a picture of the Earth’s major processes and the ways in which they interact to provide the world’s citizens with adequate material resources

## Course Goals

### Goals:

In this course, you will learn how to solve research questions of geologic and societal importance:

1. What geologic disasters is a region likely to experience and what are their risks?
2. What geologic resources in a region?
3. What is the geologic history of a region?

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Identify and classify common rocks and minerals.
- Describe the role off plate tectonics in shaping Earth’s surface features, including mountains, volcanoes and earthquakes.
- Interpret geologic maps to analyze geologic features.
- Apply critical thinking and problem-solving skills to analyze geologic data.
- Demonstrate effective communication skills in presenting geologic concepts and findings though written reports, oral presentations and visual representations.
- Recognize the importance of geology in everyday life, including its relevance to natural resources and hazards mitigation.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

***Class Engagement and Participation****:* The best way to learn about rocks is to hold and study rocks! Come to class prepared to learn to identify rocks and minerals, analyze data, read maps, and get your hands dirty! Because active participation will be a major component of the course, what we do in the class meeting will determine a large portion of your grade. Of course, I understand that sometimes emergencies or other unexpected circumstances arise that make attendance that day impossible. If this is the case, please talk with me as soon as possible so we can make arrangements to get you caught up (this will not apply to non-emergencies-like oversleeping). If you will be absent from a class for a university-sponsored activity, please make arrangements with time – beforehand – regarding any work you might miss.

***Tasks*****:** In order to be able to answer the research question, you will need to learn skills. These will be your tasks. Sometimes these tasks will be completing background readings before class, or competing an analysis started in class.

***Projects*****:** Each unit we will be working towards a project (i.e. What geologic disasters could Troy experience in the future?). Classes will be dedicated to contextualizing the problem and learning about how to interpret relevant data to answer this, and similar questions. You will be given the project at the beginning of the unit, so you can work on it. At the end of the unit, you will have a week to use what you learned to finish your project. Your TA and I will be available to assist. You also can revise and resubmit the first 2 projects.

***Geology Journal:*** The geology journal is designed to help you be reflective about what you are learning in class and connect course material to your everyday life. These will include both directed entries and independent entries. You will need to bring your journal to class for directed entries. Journals will be collected randomly, but you can expect a collection every 2 weeks. ***There should be at least 1 independent entry/week.*** Additional information on journals is available on LMS.

***Herkimer Diamond Mine Field Trip**:* On Wednesday July 17th we will take a field trip to Herkimer Diamond Mines. We will leave at 8:00 am. Lunch will be provided. If you are unable to attend the fieldtrip, an alternative assignment will be available
