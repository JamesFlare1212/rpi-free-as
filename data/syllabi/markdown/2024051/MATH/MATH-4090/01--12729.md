## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course provides an opportunity for the development of theorem-proving skills  
in the field of mathematical analysis. Expansion of a knowledge base comes as a by-product of energy  
expended in theorem proving and subsequent exposition. Analysis topics included are: sets, functions,  
the real numbers, cardinality, induction, decimal representations of real numbers, Euclidean spaces, ab-  
stract vector spaces, and metric spaces. This is a communication-intensive course. Prerequisites: math-  
ematics major and MATH 2010. (from the Rensselaer Catalog)

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

3 Exams, 7 quizzes, 8 Homeworks, 2 presentations and 10 Worksheets (for extra points) are the *course* activities that are used to *measure student* performance.  
LaTeX: Learning LaTeX is part of the course. Students must use LaTeX in writing homework. Students  
will be required to submit the original LaTeX code on LMS for some homework sets. This is to help pro-  
vide feedback to students about the appropriate ways of writing LaTeX to make it a useful system. In ad-  
dition, there may be questions about LaTeX syntax on exams. Students should review log files from the  
LaTeX compiler to make certain that the LaTeX syntax is correct. (LaTeX compilers sometimes overlook  
incorrect syntax which may produce correct looking output but may eventually lead to intractable diffi-  
culties as the document grows.) Sample LaTeX files for homework will be provided for download and  
usage.  
LaTeX is free to download. While the download and installation is time consuming, the advantage of  
downloading LaTeX is that you can edit files locally on your own machine without connection to the In-  
ternet and you can easily control your own source files. Most downloads come with a LaTeX editor, but  
LaTeX may be edited with any text editor. There is a choice of downloads with two major ones being  
TexLive and MikTex. Either will suffice for this class.  
Alternatively, students may use an online LaTeX editor such as “Overleaf”. While this makes it a little eas-  
ier to get started, it requires Internet connectivity and it controls your files. Note that the homework rules  
above explicitly forbid sharing LaTeX files in any fashion and thus prohibit using any of the sharing facili-  
ties of online programs such as “Overleaf”.  
Homework is due at the times posted on LMS and Gradescope.  
Up to 48h late your homework will receive a 20% deduction of total points. It will not be accepted after  
that. An excused absence, will not automatically result in an adjustment to the late penalty as these are  
out of class assignments with 1 week allowed for completion. Late penalties on homework will only be  
adjusted or waived at the discretion of the instructor. This is generally reserved for extended illness and  
when notification of the absence/illness was done in a timely manner.  
Make-up exams without penalty will only be given to students with an excused absence documented by  
the Student Success office or students who make arrangements with the instructor at least 24 hours prior  
to the start of the exam. Missing an exam should not be taken lightly. Thus, students missing an exam  
due to an excused absence should notify the instructor to make arrangements for a make-up exam  
Section Day/Time Location Activity. If a student with an excused absence fails to inform the instructor within 24  
hours of the exam, it is at the discretion of the instructor to allow the student to take a make-up exam  
with possibility of a penalty applied. If a student misses an exam due to an unexcused absence (e.g., did  
not know there was an exam, feeling ill the day of the exam, overslept, etc.), then the student must  
inform the instructor within 24 hours of the exam. The student may elect to take a make-up exam at a  
time arranged by the instructor and a 30% penalty will be applied to the score.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the  
Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams  
and quizzes must be the student’s own. A student may not change an answer on an exam or quiz before  
submitting it for an appeal. Additionally you will be asked to “sign” an honor code that indicates your  
work is your own and you have not given or received any help on exams and quizzes. The minimum aca-  
demic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the  
assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also  
be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders  
will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
