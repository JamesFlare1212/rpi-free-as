## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Topics:**

**Differentiation of functions of several variables:** Partial derivatives, total derivative, tangent plane, linear approximation, chain rule, directional derivatives, gradient and steepest ascent, tangent lines and planes for implicitly defined curves and surfaces, mixed partial derivatives, Taylor’s formula for functions of several variables, free extrema, constrained extrema, implicit and inverse functions.

**Vector-valued functions:** Spatial curves, arc length, unit tangent, vector fields, gradient, divergence, curl, two fundamental results of vector analysis, vector operations in orthogonal curvilinear coordinates.

**Multiple integrals:** Double integrals, repeated integrals, integrals over general domains, triple integrals, change of variables, double and triple integrals in polar, cylindrical, and spherical coordinates, applications including average, center of mass, and moments of inertia.

**Line integrals:** Scalar integrals, vector integrals.

**Surface integrals:** Parameterizations of surfaces, tangents and normals to parametric surfaces, tangent planes, surface area, scalar surface integrals, vector integrals on oriented surfaces.

**Integral theorems of vector analysis:** Green’s formula, area of a planar figure as a line integral, Stokes’ and divergence theorems, physical definitions of curl and divergence, divergence form of Green’s formula, applications in fluid mechanics and electrodynamics, conservative vector fields, potentials and path independence, solenoidal vector fields and vector potentials.

**Calculus of variations:** Functionals and their extrema, Euler's equations, extrema with side conditions, geodesics, Newtonian mechanics, Lagrange's equations, Lagrangian and Hamiltonian functions, Kepler's problem.

**Cartesian Tensors:** Transformation properties under rotation, rank 0, 1, and 2 tensors, tensor algebra, tensor form of vector product, tensor invariants, tensor gradient, divergence, and curl, integral theorems for tensors, index notation and repeated-index summation, transport theorems and balance laws in continuum mechanics.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

For the homework, you are encouraged to collaborate by exchanging ideas and brainstorming solutions. You can work in groups in which you discuss the problems and help each other out if you are stuck. You are also allowed to use books and informative websites such as Wikipedia. However, in all cases, what you turn in must be your own work, and copying of any kind is forbidden. Examples include copying from fellow students (this includes partitioning the homework among several students without everybody's participation on every problem), contacting helpers outside of exam rooms using electronic or other means, wholesale copying of solutions from the internet (both recycled problems from past versions of this class or solutions of the assigned problems from any other source), using solvers such as Maple or Mathematica (unless allowed for a specific problem), using cheat sites such as Chegg or Course Hero, or using AI cheat sites such as ChatGPT.  
For the exams, you will be allowed a one-page, two-sided crib sheet of notes in your own handwriting. When taking the exams, you are not to discuss the questions with anyone but the proctoring instructor and/or TAs. Copying from fellow students is strictly forbidden, as is using any type of books, notes, or electronic aids.
