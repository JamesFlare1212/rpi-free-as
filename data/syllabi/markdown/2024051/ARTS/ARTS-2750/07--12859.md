## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Private applied music lessons are offered to provide a fine arts experience for credit and are focused on vocal performance and assessment in both private and group settings.

- \- Regular attendance at all weekly lessons (50 min. x 14/semester).
- \- 15 min. daily practice using the warm-up routine and techniques from class.
- \- Satisfactory progress on repertoire studied, as determined with each student and the instructor.
- \- Music should be printed, notated, and provided to the instructor as requested. Please provide a copy to the professor of any new music, double sided and hole-punched with breaths marked in pencil. Bring a pencil and scores to each lesson.
- \- Students should keep a vocal journal (Google doc) to record lesson notes and progress.
- \- **Note regarding cancellations, illness and COVID safety**:
  - o Please opt to join class via Webex if you have any symptoms of any illness, fever, nausea, etc. If you have laryngitis (voice loss), you must attend if not contagious because we can continue to do valuable vocal work.
  - o Make-ups: At this time, make ups will not be available for missed course time unless absences fall under the categories listed online at Student Life.

This course creates an explorative environment for students to develop their voice and vocal technique with the student’s vocal identity at the center. Introductory ideas to voice science/vocal pedagogy, breathing and singing technique, languages/diction, and phonation, with the aim of making a healthy, efficient acoustic sound across a variety of vocal genres in an energizing, encouraging experience. This is an exploration of student’s unique vocal identity, guided by technique and the instructor’s insight as tools for individualized progress.

In this course, students will gain the skills to give and receive technical feedback, gain performance experience, access information on voice resources, workshops, and vocal connections across campus, and engage in periodic activities throughout the semester. Certain dates are TBD.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Grading**

Grades and assessment of student progress is made based on individual pace, effort, and improvement over the course of the semester.

- \- In-class singing: 40%
- \- Work at home / journal: 20%
- \- Self-assessment and in-class feedback: 20%
- \- Final reflection: 10%

Regular in-person assessments and feedback will be provided during workshops, along with a warmup routine and repertoire selections that are student-led and pedagogic.

**Assessment**

Students will be evaluated on the following:

- Preparation and attendance
- Balance of preparation of material with openness to new experience and risk-taking
- Progression and application of feedback across the semester
- Tracking of daily practice and goalposts throughout semester
- Investment in rehearsal and research prior to workshops to aid preparation
- Collegiality and positive collaboration with colleagues

**Learning Outcomes**

Students in the private lesson program grow their sense of vocal identity and understanding of the voice and its assessment and to musically interpret a variety of musical works and styles/genres within a dynamic that is encouraging, challenging, and aimed at improving students’ ability to self-assess and diagnosis areas of growth in each other’s voices (breath flow, support, diction, expression, and character).

- \- Students will actively assess and support fellow vocalists.

- \- Students will have the opportunity to experience an immersive visual and acoustic space at the Craive Lab (date TBA) and voice science tools including FonaDyn and the electroglottograph (EGG).

- \- Students work on listening skills and their ability to work independently on appropriate warm-ups and music explored in class.

- \- Students commit to their own daily technical work to create space for greater individual expression and vocal exploration, in pedagogically appropriate solo, chamber, choral, and various musical contexts.
- \- Students will be encouraged to question the musical canon, and structures and systems of organization in vocal/musical history.
- \- Students track progress with note-taking and weekly updates via a shared Google Doc.
