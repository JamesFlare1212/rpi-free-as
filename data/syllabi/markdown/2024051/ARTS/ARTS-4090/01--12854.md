## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course delves into creative coding as a practice of using computational techniques, such as programming, for artistic expression. It will introduce open-source programming libraries and tools used by artists and programmers to create interactive experiences and artworks. Through hands-on projects, students will develop intermediate skills of programming while also learning the strategies of generating art through coding and pushing the aesthetic boundaries of code.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Cultivate a perspective that views coding as an expressive tool akin to a pencil for sketching or a paintbrush for painting.  
Gain the experience of conceptualizing and bringing artwork to life through computational methods.  
Explore the relationship between art and technology, recognizing how they intersect and complement each other in creative endeavors.  
Enhance art critique skills by reflecting on the aesthetic, cultural, and social significance of code-based artworks.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Compositions  
Due: May 30  
Create three distinctive and static images using p5.js with mostly geometric shapes. The goal is to make visually compelling compositions with an emphasis on structure and form. Artist references: Piet Mondrian, Agnes Martin, Mark Rothko, Frank Stella, Ellsworth Kelly.  
Patterns  
Due: June 6  
Create three distinctive, static, and procedurally generated images using p5.js. The goal is to create patterns using the method of procedural generation. Artist references: Casey Reas, Jason Salavon, Tyler Hobbs, Bridget Riley, Sol LeWitt, Carlos Cruz-Diez.  
Motion  
Due: June 13  
Animate the three patterned images from your last assignment (or create new ones) using p5.js. Your goal is to make fluid animations that visualize motion within the patterns. Artist references: Zach Lieberman, Refik Anadol, Markus Heckmann, Olia Lialina.  
Particles  
Due: June 27  
Create a particle system using p5.js. It can be flames, fountains, flies, and flakes, or any other realistic or abstract elements. This is the LAST assignment where you are required to use p5.js. Artist references: Daniel Shiffman (TheCodingTrain), Robert Hodgin, Memo Akten.  
Procedural Generator  
Due: July 18  
Create a generator that generates objects based on user-defined attributes, for example, a kitten generator that generates kittens with customizable features such as eye color, fur color, size, and shape. Use any tools you like. Artist references: Everest Pipkin, Max Bittker, Daniel Temkin.  
Interactive Simulation  
Due: August 15  
Create an interactive simulation that displays a dynamic system where user interaction can influence its behavior which can be observed in real-time. Artist references: Max Bittker, Rafaël Rozendaal, Daniel Rozin, Golan Levin, Matt Deslauriers.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

For beginners, it is acceptable and understandable to learn through tutorials, but it is unacceptable to directly copy other people’s work.  
If you have used a tutorial, please cite the source in your code. Additionally, to demonstrate your comprehension of the code, it is highly recommended to write comments.
