## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course objective is to add economic consideration to the engineering decision-making process. Mathematical models and economic analysis methods will be presented in this course. Financial functions will be used in MS Excel spreadsheets for analyzing complex problems. This course will provide students with an integrated systems perspective on the practice of their chosen engineering field. After completion, students will be able to use computing and related engineering tools when performing engineering economic analysis in the business and the public sector, will have the knowledge to pass the engineering economics portion of professional licensure exams, and will have the skills and tools to include economic considerations in design decision-making.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Comprehend, formulate, and solve engineering economics problems with financial transactions or cash flows.
- Evaluate alternatives based on equivalent worth, rate of return, cost-benefit analysis, etc.
- Implement concepts of replacement, depreciation, taxation, and inflation in engineering decision-making.
- Evaluate the probabilistic nature of engineering problems by quantifying sensitivity and risk involved.
- Analyze portfolios for optimal resource allocation scenarios under budget constraints.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Collaboration with other students enrolled in the course is permitted on homework assignments, but it is important to ensure that all work is original and  
not copied from any source, including other students’ work. If you worked with other people on a problem, include their names as “Collaborators” within the specific problem section. It is expected that you present your own solutions in a way that makes sense to you. Plagiarism, or copying the work of others, is a violation of the RPI Community Standard and will not be tolerated.  
Except for the first exam, all exams will need the use of MS Excel for computations. Using a computer on an exam is a privilege, not a right. If abuse of academic integrity occurs by any student on any exam, the privilege of computer use will be withdrawn for that student for all remaining exams. In addition, the penalties for academic dishonesty stated later in this syllabus will be imposed on the offending student(s). Abuse includes the use of software solutions to problems from others including the use of software developed by students in prior years and sharing software with another student. Sharing software for problem solutions is academic dishonesty and will be pursued as such.  
For undergraduate students, the standard punishment for the first offense is a 0 on the assignment and/or a full letter grade reduction on the final semester grade. Students whose violations are more flagrant will receive a higher penalty. Undergraduate students caught a second time will receive an immediate F in the course, regardless of the circumstances.
