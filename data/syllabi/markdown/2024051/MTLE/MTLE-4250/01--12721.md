## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a required departmental course but is also appropriate for biomedical engineers and other engineering disciplines as an elective. This course teaches the mechanical properties of metals, ceramics, and polymers from both the macroscopic and atomistic or micromechanical viewpoints. An introduction to three-dimensional stresses and strains, elastic behavior, plastic behavior, strengthening mechanisms, fracture, creep, and fatigue are all addressed. Includes laboratory component.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

M. A. Meyers and K. K. Chawla, “Mechanical Behavior of Materials,” Cambridge University Press, 2nd edition, 2009.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Upon successful completion of the course, students will have a basic understanding of several mechanical properties and the use of ASTM standards to complete valid tests.
- Upon successful completion of the course, students will be able to use quantitative equations to describe observed mechanical behavior of materials (such as hardening laws) and predict failure (von Mises Criteria, Paris Law).
- Upon successful completion of the course, students will have a basic understanding of the relationship between atomic and microscale structure and mechanical properties.
- Upon successful completion of the course, students will have a basic understanding of strengthening mechanisms in metals and the role of dislocation motion and structure.
- Upon successful completion of the course, students will have a basic understanding of time and temperature dependent behavior in all materials with an emphasis on metals and polymers.
- Upon successful completion of the course, students will have a basic understanding of stress tensors and transformation of stresses.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Anyone caught cheating or plagiarizing during an exam will receive a zero grade for that particular exam. Cheating in homework assignments or laboratory reports will be punished by a 0% grade for the whole homework grade (normally 10% of the total grade) or laboratory grade (normally 30% of the total grade). Cheating can include sharing answers, as well as stealing answers. Plagiarism means copying words from someone’s work, even if you “change” the sentence a bit.

During the laboratory sessions, you will work as teams, sharing data and in many cases collectively analyzing the data within the group. Each group is expected to collaborate on writing up the introduction, experimental procedure, background information, conclusions and analysis. However, the requirements for academic integrity still apply. Please properly reference any and all graphs, equations and/or sentences that are copied from another source. Please properly cite all references (including online sources) used in your reports. Do not use the data obtained by another group.

All examination work is strictly individual. Students are not allowed to have any electronic equipment (such as smart phones) with them during examinations unless it is explicitly permitted by the instructor.
