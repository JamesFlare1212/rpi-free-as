- Gain experience in developing reasonable arguments and to enhance critical thinking and communication skills.

- Learn how to analyze and evaluate public arguments in legal, political, social, and governmental contexts.

- Learn how to structure informative and persuasive communication for successful outcomes.

## • Learn about the history and development of speech communication in our society.

• Develop an awareness of the role that speech communication plays within a variety of fields and our daily lives.

- Improve writing, analytical and strategic decision-making skills.

- Gain confidence in speech communication abilities.

**General Requirements:**

**Attendance** is required and will be monitored. Please be on time.

If late, you are responsible for correcting the attendance record at the end of class. Repeated and unexcused tardiness will reduce your final grade.

You are allowed 2 absences without any penalty. You will be penalized 5 points for your 3rd absence and every absence that follows. These points will be taken off of your final grade for the class.

If you are absent, for whatever reason, you are responsible for obtaining information on any announcements or assignments made in class. Please ask another student who is taking the class for this kind of information. Please do not email me to request written summaries of class.

Late assignments will not be accepted. Makeup assignments are not allowed.

**Quizzes** are listed on the class schedule above and the dates are subject to be changed with sufficient notice. All quizzes will be open book. A minimum of 48 hours will be allowed to complete each quiz. Questions will be in the form of multiple choice, true false, or fill in the blank. Make-up quizzes are not allowed. A grade of 0 will be given for any missing quiz.

**All assignments** will be assigned with specific instructions and objectives. You will be graded on how well you follow instructions and meet the required objectives. Late or make-up assignments will not be accepted. A grade of 0 will be given for any late or missing assignment.

**Class Participation:** Group discussion plays a large role in this class.

- Come prepared to participate in all class discussions, lectures, and group activities.

- Ask questions, share your opinion, and participate in constructive debate of controversial issues.

- Come prepared to answer questions with a thoughtful and intelligent response when called upon to do so.

- Follow the daily news. Be prepared to participate in discussions about national and global headline news events.

**Class Participation Grade #1 & 2:**

Please review the sections “Class Participation” above. Your ability to follow these guidelines will be assessed twice during the semester. Each assessment is worth 100 points.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.
