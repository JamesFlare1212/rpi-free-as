## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Age and origin of the Earth, internal constituents, and energy sources; how plates move, oceans develop, resources accumulate, and mountains rise. Gives non-specialists a picture of the Earth’s major processes and the ways in which they interact to provide the world’s citizens with adequate material resources

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Upon successful completion of this course, you will be able to:

- Extract information from geologic materials (graphs, rocks, maps, etc.) and use your observations to synthesize reasonable conclusions
- Understand how understanding plate tectonics can help society
- Understand the processes that give rise to natural disasters
- Apply knowledge of sedimentary environment, climate and sedimentary processes to infer potential types and locations of natural resources

**Attendance:** Attending class regularly is really the only way you’ll get the most out of this course and you should attend if you are able. ### Other Course Policies:

**Late Policy:** If an assignment is due in class, it will be due at the start of class. Scores on late assignments will be penalized 10% each day they are late. *After 1 week, the grade becomes a zero.* IF you need additional time to complete an assignment, let me know prior to it’s due-date and an additional 48 hours will be given.

Because active participation will be a major component of the course, what we do in the class meeting will determine a large portion of your grade. Of course, I understand that sometimes emergencies or other unexpected circumstances arise that make attendance that day impossible. If this is the case, please talk with me as soon as possible so we can make arrangements to get you caught up (this will not apply to non-emergencies-like oversleeping). ## How to Be Successful in this Course

Once upon a time, I was also a student in an Introductory Physical Geology course because it was the only science course that fit into my schedule. It was a hard course and I had to work my butt off in that class! There was lots of new terminology and quantitative reasoning and I often felt lost and overwhelmed. In fact, the grade I received in the course was the lowest I received in college! However, it ended up being my favorite class and resulted in me switching my major to Geology (originally, I was a Pre-Med student)! I am not smarter than you, I’ve just been thinking about geology longer than you. You may not think you are a “science person”, but you have all the skills to be successful in this class. Plus, you have me here to help you!

Based on experience, here are some suggestions for success:

1. Read before and after class. By introducing yourself to key terms, it will make following class easier.
2. If you have a question at any point, ask it!
3. Focus on concepts. How does information build on each other? How are concepts related to each other?
4. Make sketches as notes!
5. All powerpoint notes will be posted after class. Instead of focusing on getting down all the words on the slide, focus on key phases, questions, and concepts. Your notes should be in our own words, not mine!
6. Don’t forget to take notes on class discussions, activities and videos

Allow yourself to have fun!
