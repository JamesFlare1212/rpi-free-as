## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Use a lab notebook and construct lab reports to retain and evaluate environmental observations
- Formulate stock solutions and serial dilutions for calibration and measurement of unknown samples
- Conduct fieldwork activities while adhering to leave no trace principles
- Deploy in situ sensors and multiprobes for the collection and interpretation of continuous data
- Recover, analyze, and preserve grab samples for subsequent laboratory-based analyses
- Demonstrate proficiency in various laboratory and instrumental techniques for assessing water quality
- Synthesize analytical data to understand the overall condition and ecological health of local waterways

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Student progress will be evaluated based upon the following:  
ACTIVE PARTICIPATION – This course is hands-on and 100% in person. To get the most out of this course, it is critical that you attend each class in person and actively participate. Roughly half of class periods will be spent in the field, where you will get wet, sweaty, and dirty. The other half of class periods will be spent in the laboratory, where you will be handling samples, chemicals, and using various analytical instrumentation. You are expected to participate in all of these course-related activities. If you happen to miss a class, you are responsible for obtaining any material, announcements, or assignments covered during the class you missed. The active participation grade (90 total points; max 30 points per category) will comprise 25% of your final course grade.  
WRITTEN ASSIGNMENTS – Nearly all written assignments will be typed and submitted via Gradescope and include lab reports and written reflections. The length, degree of detail, and format of each lab report will vary depending upon the research activity. Examples of what may be included in a lab report include: objectives and hypotheses, graphs and data summaries, statistical analysis, interpretation of data, and describing study limitations. Written reflections will give you the opportunity to consider how your personal experiences and observations shape your thinking and encourage you to explore your own ideas about class activities. Lab reports and reflections will be written and submitted independently, not as a group. The weighted average written assignment grade will comprise 40% of your final course grade.  
LAB NOTEBOOK – As part of this course, you will learn how to keep a lab notebook as a useful reference for yourself and others. While there is no one “correct” way to keep a lab notebook, efficient and effective notetaking is an essential part of conducting well-rounded and robust environmental research. At the end of the semester, you will be graded on your effectiveness in keeping a lab notebook. The lab notebook will be submitted to Prof. Wagner near the end of the semester. The lab notebook grade (50 total points; max 30 points for Content; max 10 points each for Organization and Readability) will comprise 5% of your final course grade.  
INDEPENDENT FIELD STUDY – Students will take what they have learned over the course of the semester to formulate and carry out an independent field study. While the study site will be the same for everyone (Vanderheyden Preserve), each student will devise or select their own research objective and hypotheses, sampling plan, and analytical methods (from a list of methods covered in this course). This is your opportunity to be creative! Once all data is collected, each student will summarize and interpret the results, describe their findings, draw relevant conclusions, and describe study limitations. On the last day of class, students will present their field study in the form of a short PowerPoint presentation and submit a formal written report via Gradescope. Further instructions on the formal report and presentation will be provided later in the semester. The final field study grade (40% presentation; 60% formal report) will comprise 30% of your final course grade.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Attendance and tardiness – Due to the hands-on and immersive nature of this course, it is essential that you arrive on time and prepared each day. Since most of this course takes place in the teaching lab or in the field, we will not be in the classroom most days. If you expect to be late or absent, you must email, Webex, call, or text Prof. Wagner by 9am that day (email address and cell phone number are listed at the top of the syllabus). If you call and Prof. Wagner does not answer immediately, please leave a voicemail or send a text instead. If you are late, we may not be able to wait for you, but we can tell you where we will be if you are able to drive or find a ride to the field site on your own. Repeatedly being late or absent will impact your active participation grade (see above) and could impact other components of your grade since being present for field and lab work are essential to the course learning objectives.  
Late policy – Lab reports, independent field study components, and other assignments will not be accepted late without the express permission of the instructor. Should the student encounter a situation (e.g., illness, family emergency, etc.) in which it will not be possible to submit an assignment on time, they should email the instructor at least 24 hours in advance so appropriate accommodations can be made. If the student notifies the instructor less than 24 hours before the submission deadline, then the assignment will incur a grade penalty (25% drop in grade per day late).  
Accommodations – If you require special accommodations (e.g., learning challenges, religious holiday conflicts, planned classes you will be missing), please schedule a meeting with Prof. Wagner during the first week of class. If you have concerns about fieldwork (see “lab and field safety” below), please contact Prof. Wagner as soon as possible.  
Lab and field safety – On days where we plan to be in the lab, you must wear closed-toe shoes to protect against spills and broken glassware. On days where we plan to be in the field, expect to get sweaty, wet, and dirty – There is also a good chance that we will be walking on uneven ground, in muddy areas, and/or wading into shallow water. Please dress accordingly and in a manner that is most comfortable for you! On field days, it is recommended that you wear shorts or pants that can be rolled up, and water-compatible boots/shoes/sandals. For example, Prof. Wagner tends to wear comfy summer clothes that can get wet/dirty and Teva- or Chaco-like sandals because she doesn’t mind getting wet, they protect the bottom of her feet, and stay on easily. You are welcome to bring a back-up change of shoes or clothes if you desire. Prof. Wagner will probably remember to bring sunscreen and bug spray on field trips, but you are encouraged to bring your own just in case she forgets. Given the long class period, please being water and snacks/lunch with you and we will have a break mid-class.
