## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course addresses classical and contemporary issues in the theory of money and banking. Topics covered include: financial markets and interest rates, commercial banking, the Federal Reserve System, theories of money demand and money supply, and the theoretical and practical aspects of monetary policy and economic stabilization. ECON-4130 will meet in the classroom two times per week during the scheduled times for lecture, discussion, and problem-solving. All assignments and tests will be delivered and submitted online. Office hours are available via Webex video conference.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- to gain a factual knowledge of money, banking, and the financial system
- to practice interpreting data in the monetary context
- to understand monetary policy and its implications for national and world economies.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Tests: There will be three tests administered online via MyLab. The basis for tests will be all assigned textbook readings, all MyLab assignments, and all lecture material. Each test will be made available for a period of seven days; see MyLab for availability dates. Test format is open book/note, multiple choice, and numerical answer. Each student will receive a unique test. Tests must be completed in one session and will be timed at 110 minutes. There is no late window for tests, as is the case for assignments. Practice work for tests is available in MyLab and will be reviewed in class; you are expected to make use of this resource prior to taking an actual test.

**MAKEUP TEST POLICY:** *Makeup tests will only be allowed for absences having an official Rensselaer excuse issued by the Health Center or the Dean of Students, and submitted to the professor by two days after the test due date. The makeup test is scheduled during the week after the test. If you miss a test and do not have an official Rensselaer excuse, you must take the optional final exam to improve your test average.* Beware: wifi connection is not reliable and you are encouraged to take your test from a wired internet connection. If you cannot wire your laptop, then you are encouraged to take your test in any campus computer center, such as VCC, where you can use a wired desktop computer.

MyLab Assignments: Assignments using the Pearson MyLab online system will be due on three dates. These assignments include: 1) three sets of Homeworks and Quizzes which pertain to the assigned readings, and 2) one set of monetary data analysis problems, called the Fed Project. All assignment sets, except for the last one due, have a 7 day period of time following the due date during which assignments may be completed late for a 10% penalty on all questions unanswered by the due date. After the 7 day late period, you must contact professor with an explanation of extenuating circumstances. The last assignment set due allows a late submission period of only 1 day. All MyLab Homeworks, Quizzes, and the Fed Project are available from the start of the semester through their due date/time. Due dates are cut-off dates for the submission of work. Due dates are not meant to be dates for you to begin or even finish work. It is expected that all students will complete work in a time efficient manner according to the outline described below. This means that work should be completed on a weekly basis and in advance of the due date. Any student who saves work to complete on the due date must expect that technical difficulties will prevent the finishing of said work by the cut-off time and credit will be lost.

FOMC Essay: Students will submit a written analysis of the most recent meeting of the Federal Open Market Committee, to be submitted in LMS by the due date specified; specific instructions for content are posted to LMS and will be explained in class; there is no revision opportunity. The policy for academic integrity with regards to plagiarism from any source including from former students is in full effect for this and all work completed. Any student who is not confident of their writing ability is urged to contact the campus center for student writing development, called “Comm+D Center”, located in the lower level Folsom Library. Late Policy: essays are accepted late only by permission of professor and only if permission is requested within 2 days after the due date; there is a 25% late penalty if professor can accept the late essay. If an excuse can be provided by the Rensselaer Health Center or Dean of Students, then the late penalty will be waived.

Optional Final Exam: An optional final exam will be offered as a way for students to attempt to improve their grade. The final exam will cover all course material. If you take the final exam, the score you obtain on the final exam will be compared with the average of your mid-semester tests. You will be awarded the higher of these scores as your grade for the test portion of the final grade, 45%. *If you miss any of the three regular* *test(s) and do not have a proper excuse (see policy on makeup tests above), then plan on taking the final exam to improve your grade.*

Final Grades: Final grades will be broken down as follows, rounded to the nearest whole number: A 94 to 100; A- 90 to 93; B+ 87 to 89; B 84 to 86; B- 80 to 83; C+ 77 to 79; C 74 to 76; C- 70 to 73; D+ 67 to 69; D 60 to 66; F 59 and below.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

By enrolling in this course, all students are committed to absolute agreement to and understanding of the course policy on academic integrity. MyLab assignments allow teamwork. It is expected that students will adhere to a code of honor and do their own work to complete tests; tests are open book/open note and each student receives a unique and timed test. Written work submitted will be monitored for plagiarism from any source, including present and former students. In general and to a great extent, there is a degree of “you get out of it what you put into it.” Professors cannot possibly monitor all dishonest activity, nor can they force students to learn. It is your responsibility to get your money’s value out of this and any college class. Any student found to engage in dishonest activity in any aspect of this course will receive a failing grade for the course.
