## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Introduction to modeling and control of dynamic chemical processes. Topics include the development of first-principles models, linearization and state space form, input/output (transfer function) form, design and tuning of PID controllers, model-based control, frequency response for robustness analysis, case studies in multivariable control, numerical analysis, and simulation.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Required textbook: Bequette, B.W. Process Control: Modeling, Design and Simulation, Prentice Hall (2003)  
Required software:  
The SIMULINK simulation package (within MATLAB) is used for dynamic process simulation and control system development. Many of the homework assignments require MATLAB coding and computer simulations. The course combines lectures, discussions and computer-based simulations in a laptop-based environment. Please install MATLAB R2022a on your laptop computer before attending the second class period. When installing MATLAB, be sure to install Simulink, the Control System Toolbox, and the Symbolic Math Toolbox, which we will need in this course.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The purpose of this course is to introduce undergraduate students to the theory and practice of chemical process modeling and control. The emphasis is on a model-based approach to control system design.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Be able to formulate differential equations describing unsteady-state mass and energy balances to describe chemical process dynamics.
- Be able to solve systems of ODEs and plot results using MATLAB.
- Be able to linearize ODEs and formulate state-space models.
- Be able to recognize systems with 1st or 2nd order responses to step inputs.
- Be able to develop empirical transfer function models to fit data from step response functions.
- Be able to formulate feedback and IMC control strategies for chemical processes.
- Be able to apply MATLAB/SIMULINK to simulate process dynamics and control schemes.
- Be able to perform a Routh stability analysis to find controller tuning parameter values that cause the closed-loop system to lose stability.
- Be able to design a PID controller using the IMC-based PID design procedure.
- Exhibit basic knowledge of modern topics in process control.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Course grades will be curved. Approximate letter grade ranges for each exam will be announced after the exams are graded and returned.

Guideline #1: an exam score of 90% or higher indicates a letter grade of at least A-.

Guideline #2: an exam score of < 50 % indicates unsatisfactory (failing) performance.

Exam average policy: *Students earning an average examination score of < 50 % ("unacceptable") will receive a course grade of "D" or "F" only, regardless of homework and ALE scores.* This policy will not be relaxed. Fair warning!

Homework Assignments will include both conventional hand-written problem solutions and some assignments that are programming-intensive or involve experimentation in a Virtual Reality (VR) environment. *Homework is due at the beginning of class, not at the end.* Instructors will advise whether hard (paper) copies or soft copies of assignments should be turned in on a week-by-week basis. The teaching assistant may only grade selected parts of homework assignments or check only for completeness of problems at his discretion.

Homework collaboration policy: Individuals may discuss the problems with others, but are not allowed to share or copy solutions (MATLAB m-files,etc.).

**Active Learning Exercises** will be distributed during class time, and students are expected to complete them during class. Class attendance and participation will therefore be measured by completion of ALE, many of which will involve MATLAB programming. Online assignment submissions (though LMS) may be required in some cases.

ALE Grading Policy:

1\) No make-up ALEs will be given for any reason.

2\) Students missing an ALE with an excused absence from Student Success will be given a grade of "X", which means the ALE does not count.

3\) Each student gets to drop (1) ALE at the end of the semester, which may be a missed class due to illness or a job interview. Otherwise, you may drop your lowest ALE score.

4\) No extra time accommodations are offered on ALEs.

5\) Some ALEs may be graded like quizzes; others may be "participation" exercises in which everyone is given a score of 100% for completing the assignment.

**Exams** will be given during normal class time, and will involve hand-written problem solving

exercises. If a laptop is required for an exam, the instructors will notify students beforehand.

A single crib sheet, 8.5 x 11 ", double sided, in your own handwriting and not photo-reduced, will be allowed. The crib sheet must be turned-in with the exam solution. *There will be no make-up exams given without an excuse letter/email from Student Success*.
