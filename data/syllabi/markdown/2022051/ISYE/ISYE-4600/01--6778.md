## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides an introduction to commonly used methods of deterministic and stochastic Operations Research (OR). Topics include linear programming, simplex algorithm, duality, sensitivity analysis, linear networks, integer programming, exact and heuristic solution procedures for integer problems, queuing theory, Markov chains, game theory, multi-criteria decision making, and decision analysis.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

- Wayne L. Winston. *Operations Research – Applications and Algorithms*. 4th edition, Thomson Learning, 2004.
- Frederick S. Hiller. and Gerald J. Lieberman. *Introduction to Operations Research,* 9th Edition. McGraw-Hill Science, 2009.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course aims to provide students with:

- An ability to apply commonly used OR methods to aid decision-making in a variety of contexts; this includes a conceptual understanding of these methods and how to use computer software to implement them to provide solutions and analysis.
- An ability to formulate real-world problems as OR models.
- An ability to assess the applicability and limitations of an OR model to a practical problem.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Lab Assignments:** There will be five in-class lab assignments, which will be released the week prior to class through Gradescope. The student will work in teams during class to complete the work. Yet, the assignments should be submitted individually each one should upload their own solutions. Students should submit all five homework assignments on-time. A valid excuse might allow for a late delivery. Otherwise, each day a homework is late, has 25% reduction of the grade received.

**Class exercises:** The schedule table shows the set of review problems from Winston’s textbook for the students to solve after each lecture. These review problems are the ones that appear at the end of each chapter. The scanned pages of the review problems will also be posted in LMS. The student should work all of them throughout the course. At the end of the course the student must submit their work to the instructor through Gradescope, as it is worth 10% of the final grade (as part of the Participation).

**Project:** Students will be in charge of presenting an Operations Research topic (from a list a topics posted in the project instructions) during the second part of the course, and must be done in groups of 1-2 people. Detailed instructions about the project will be published in LMS.

**Exams:** Midterm exam is in-class and final exam is take home, and should be done individually. More information about the format of the exam and rules are given prior to the exam. Sections of the exams might be online, and these will be released in LMS and will be available for a specific time frame.

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

**Missing Tests or Lab work:** Missing tests are only allowed if there is an emergency verified by the Dean of Student’s office. There are *no* makeups for tests. Missing assessments without official excuses will receive a zero.

**Excusal from Course Assignments and Examinations:** If you know ahead of time that you will miss an exam/assignment deadline, then you must make arrangements before the assessment is due. Failure to do so will result in 25% off for each 24-hour period the assessment is late.

If an emergency arises and a student cannot submit assigned work on or before the scheduled due date or cannot take an exam on the scheduled date, the student (or the Student Experience Office) MUST give notification to the instructor NO MORE THAN 48 HOURS AFTER the scheduled date. If you have an emergency, the campus is here to help, please coordinate with the Student Experience Office – 4th floor of Academy Hall, x8022, se@rpi.edu

**Re-Grading Policy:** Document in writing which part you want re-graded and why, and turn in this document with your original assignment. You will be given one chance (per assignment) to request a re-grade. No re-grade will be accepted more than one week after an exam or an assignment has been returned to the student. Re-grades made after this period has elapsed will NOT be considered.
