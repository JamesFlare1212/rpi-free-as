## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course provides an opportunity for the development of theorem-proving skills in the field of mathematical analysis. Expansion of a knowledge base comes as a by-product of energy expended in theorem proving and subsequent exposition. Analysis topics included are: sets, functions, the real numbers, cardinality, induction, decimal representations of real numbers, Euclidean spaces, abstract vector spaces, and metric spaces. This is a communication-intensive course.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Write high quality mathematical proofs.
- Read mathematical proofs and verbally communicate how each sentence contributes to deducing the assertion.
- Solve problems in analysis.
- Write LaTeX syntax for mathematics and mathematical equations.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Homework:** There will be 10 homework assignments due on Mondays at 11pm (RPI time). Each homework must be formatted with LaTeX and submitted to Gradescope. Some homework assignments will also require the LaTeX code to be submitted as well to get full credit.

**LaTeX:** Learning LaTeX is part of the course. Students must use LaTeX in writing homework. Students will be required to submit the original LaTeX code on LMS for some of the homeworks.

**In-Class Quizzes:** There will be 10 in-class quizzes held during the end of lecture on Mondays (excepting the first Monday of each half of the course). Each quiz will be based on the problems of the corresponding recitation worksheet.

**In-Class Exams:** There are 2 in-class exams on the following dates.  
• Exam 1: Thursday, June 30  
• Exam 2: Thursday, August 18

**Class Lectures:** On Mondays and Thursdays, there will be “lecture” sessions. Typically an unmarked version of the notes will be available 24 hours before class and a marked up version of the notes will be available shortly after class.  
**Recitation Worksheets:** On Thursdays, there will be a“recitation” session during the second half of class. During these sessions, students will work in groups to complete worksheets and get feedback on their proofs and solutions. The grades will be based on active participation and the completion of certain worksheet problems or submission of worksheets. The problems on the worksheets will be the basis for the in-class quizzes and will be part of the study guides for the exams.  
Parts of the worksheets will be posted on LMS in advance. To get full credit for active participation in recitation, students must engage in dialogue with at least one member of the teaching team during the recitation period. Dialogue that meets the criteria of participation includes asking questions about proofs (structure, content), or explaining a proof from the worksheet.

**Proof Portfolio and Reflection:** Before finals week student will create and submit a proof portfolio. The proof portfolio will consist of six proofs, three from each half of the class, that represents the student’s best work during the course. These proofs may come from Homework, Exams, or Worksheets. They should be revised based on any comments received. The portfolio must also have a 1-2 page reflection of the course and your growth as a mathematical writer. Please see LMS for more information.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:** Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. Exams must be done individually and cheating will result in a grade of zero and a report to the Dean of Students. Students may work together on homework provided they follow the rules in the preceding section on homework. Students must work individually, without assistance on exam and quizzes. If rules are violated, students will receive a zero on the assignment, exam or quiz and a report will be made to the Dean of Students. Repeat offenders will fail the class.
