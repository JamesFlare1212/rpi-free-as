## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The theory underlying vector spaces, algebra of subspaces, bases; linear transformations, dual spaces; eigenvectors, eigenvalues, minimal polynomials, canon- ical forms of linear transformations; inner products, adjoints, orthogonal projections, and complements. Prerequisite: MATH 2010 (from the Rensselaer Catalog).

Other topics: Determinants, complex vectors and matrices, singular value decomposition.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Introduction to Linear Algebra, Fifth ed., by Gilbert Strang, Wellesley-Cambridge Press, 2016, ISBN: 978-09802327-7-6.  
Other sources will be used for some of the topics. Notes or references will be provided.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- The student should be able to demonstrate knowledge and understanding of the basic ideas of Linear Algebra, including concepts of linear systems, independence, theory of matrices, linear transformations, determinants, vector spaces and subspaces, bases and dimension, eigenvalues, eigenvectors and diagonalization, inner products, etc.
- The student should be able to write clear and accurate proofs using the concepts of this course.
- The student should be able to apply Linear Algebra concepts of this course to solve problems.

### Other Course Policies:

**Classroom Decorum:** Students must be on time for class and should refrain from leaving and reentering the classroom during class. If a student has a legitimate reason for arriving late to class or being excused early from class, then they should discuss this with the instructor before class. Phones may not be used during class and should be silent. Computers and tablets may not be used for anything other than taking notes or accessing notes. Students who are unable to courteously comply with these policies may be asked to leave the room.

**Disability Services for Students (DSS):** Students with a memorandum from the Disability Services for Students (DSS) office identifying accommodations for exams must present the memorandum to the instructor as soon as possible. Students will not be granted accommodations unless a memorandum from DSS has been received. Providing the accommodations requires advance planning. Students may not be granted accommodations if a memorandum is received on the day of an exam.

**Calculator Policy:** Calculators will not be allowed on exams.

**Academic Integrity:** Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams must be the student’s own. In addition, no calculators, computers, tablets, books, notes, phones, headphones, or smartwatches may be used during exams. Talking or communicating with other students in any way during exams is also prohibited. Students may discuss homework with other students, TAs, and the instructor, but the work submitted must be the student’s own. Submissions with glaring similarities will violate academic integrity polices.

The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.

**Health and Safety Protocols:** Students are required to follow the health and safety protocols of RPI, which are subject to change throughout the semester. Students who are not cleared for campus access are prohibited from attending class. While masks are required in classrooms, eating and drinking in class is prohibited. Failure to comply with health and safety protocols will be met with a request to comply, a request to leave the class, or a dismissal of the entire class and referral of the non-compliant student to the Dean of Students.

**Visitors:** Visitors are prohibited during all class meetings. Only students who are registered for the class should be present in the classroom. Students may only attend class meetings at the time and place listed on their schedule on SIS.

**Grade Appeals:** If upon reviewing the answer key and solutions for an exam a student feels that their work was graded incorrectly, then the student may submit a grading appeal directly on Gradescope. Students have three days after the scores of exams are released to submit an appeal. You may only appeal scores based on factual criteria (a correct answer was marked wrong, or scores were added incorrectly). Grade appeals should not be used to request more partial credit.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).
