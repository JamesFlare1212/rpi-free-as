## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

GSAS Capstone serves as the culminating experience for GSAS students. Students work in large-scale, self-directed teams to produce and publish a polished game or interactive media project. As a part of the production process, students will work to develop and execute a game pitch, game design document, business plan and formation, work plan, playtesting plan, and marketing and social media strategy. Students will also be responsible for meeting team-determined and instructor-determined milestones.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Learn to manage a project from conception to completion.

- Learn to accommodate feedback.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Grades are assessed 3 times throughout the semester.

Grading includes quality of work, quantity of work, in-discussion contributions, and industry-oriented performance throughout the course.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grading is assigned based on agreed upon milestones per student schedule AND/OR Individual project allocation. Some students will have their entire grade based around their group capstone work, while others will have an agreed upon weight assigned to their group work. This is a project-based course, and all projects are likely highly distinct and have their own unique features. Grading is achieved through assessment of the work done by specified dates in line with the project objectives, bearing in mind that these projects might be in a variety of formats, of differing degrees of complexity, from differing start-points, with some being solo developer projects, and others being projects for large teams, and many being somewhere in between. Therefore, criteria are not set, but tailored specifically to each project.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

Trust: Student-Teacher relationships are built on trust. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. And, teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts, which violate this trust, undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

Plagiarism: All work produced in this course must be original and created by the student. Incidents of academic dishonesty or attempted academic dishonesty will result in grade penalties and may be referred to the Dean of Students. While the creation of digital artwork may utilize prior works for inspiration, reference, and source material, assignments in this class are expected to represent the student’s originality and technical ability through the creation of substantially new works. The use of AI generated content is forbidden except in cases where the project centers the use or critique of AI tools in its design. In such cases, plans for appropriate use and crediting of AI tools will be explicitly planned in consultation with the course instructors. When in doubt, ask your instructor.
