## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Our work together has two primary threads: interpreting narratives in existing games and developing our own creative practice for crafting narrative-driven games. To do this we will:

- **Play:** *We will play a curated list of analog and digital games in and out of class. Our shared experiences with these games will give us common ground for discussion.*
- ***Write******:*** *We will write regularly in writer’s notebooks, and more formally in two major projects; an analytic essay and a creative project.*
- **Read:** *We will use our course text to develop a shared language for our craft and analysis.*
- **Workshop****:** *Peer review and workshopping are essential elements of developing our creative and academic practices in community together. We build our skills when we read each other’s work, and work is improved through the insights and questions of our peers.*

## Writer’s Notebook

As you progress through the course you will develop a writer’s notebook. This will compile all of your notes, writings, and reflections related to the course. At times you may be asked to share samples from your notebook with peers for workshopping or discussion

### Entries in the Miscellany

- A running list of required entries will be posted on the course site
- Some required entries, like notes from class sessions, will be specified
- Others will be required, but can be anything that helps you think with the class. Examples and suggestions will be given in class
- Additional entries can be made for extra credit, earning up to 125% for the Notebook Grade

### Notebook Reflection Report

You will fill out a reflection on your developing notebook practice using a template that will be provided.

### Note Specifications

A main them of the course is to explore possibility within structure. As such, meeting the following specifications is important for your log grade.

- Notebook is actually a college-ruled composition book
- Name and “Writer’s Notebook” on the cover
- The first page says "Table of Contents" at the top
- The first 5 pages of are reserved for the table of contents
- The table of contents is up to date with a title for each entry and page numbers in a column on the far right
- The bottom right of every odd numbered page has a page number (if you have used the page)
- New entries have a title at the top

Assignments that don't meet all specifications will have 5% deducted from their grade. (90%-->85%) Those that fail to follow multiple specifications will have 10% deducted from their grade. (90%-→80%.). A random selection of notebooks will be periodically spot-checked in class

## Game Narrative Review (GNR)

The Game Narrative Review is a format for “detailed structured analysis of the narrative elements of a game” developed by the Game Developer’s Conference. Leading game design programs from around the country use this format the resulting papers are submitted to the GDC writing board for review. The most promising submissions are invited to create a poster as a visual synopsis of their review. Successful poster submissions are declared winners and exhibited at the GDC. **RPI has had 9 gold winners in the past 5 years.**

The GNR uses a template structure, which we will complete in stages:

- **September 25**: Basic Information, Overview, Characters, Critical Reception
- **October 13**: Breakdown and Highlight
- **November 10**: Successful/Unsuccessful Elements, Lessons, Summation
- **December 16**: Final Draft

At each stage we will workshop your draft. You will receive drafts from peers and provide comments using a template. Your final submission will incorporate the changes from the workshops.

## Final Project

Throughout the semester you will apply what you’ve learned from the course to creating a narrative-driven piece of analog game design. This will not be a standalone game but a component compatible with an existing game. Format options will be provided in class. This project will also be completed in states:

- **September 11**: Concept pitch
- **October 8**: Design brief
- **November 13**: Playtest draft
- **De****cember 13**: Final draft
