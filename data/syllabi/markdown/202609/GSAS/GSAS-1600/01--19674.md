## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Our work together will focus on developing practical skills for making sense of games in their cultural and historical context. The specific content will depend on themes and questions that emerge from this semester’s specific combination of people, places, and times. The key verbs of our work are:

- **Play:** *It is impossible to understand games without playing them. We will play a variety* *of them* *in and out of class.*
- **Observe*****:*** *Participant Observation is an essential method for the ethnographic approach of this course. We w**il**l spend time with other people who are playing games and train* *our* *observational skills*.
- **Read:** *Careful reading is an irreplaceable discipline for connecting with the thoughts of others. Having a shared concrete reference point is foundational for thinking in community. Each of us will be developing our capacities in this essential discipline as we work through key texts and each other’s writing.*
- ***Record****: To succeed in this work you must develop a habitual practice of recording experiences, observations, insights, and questions.*
- **Communicate****:** *Because our goal is to think together, we will need to exercise our capacity to communicate about complicated ideas, ask questions, and listen for the best insights of others.*

## Miscellany

As you progress through the course you will develop a miscellany, sometimes called a commonplace book. The miscellany is an active tool which you will use to think with by compiling your thoughts, observations, and notes relevant to the course.

### Entries in the Miscellany

- A running list of required entries will be posted on the course site
- Some required entries, like notes from class sessions, will be specified
- Others will be required, but can be anything that helps you think with the class. Examples and suggestions will be given in class
- Additional entries can be made for extra credit, earning up to 125% for the Miscellany Grade

### Miscellany Synthesis Report

The Friday before Exams 2 and 3, you will submit a report on what threads and insights you are finding in your Miscellany. A template will be provided at the beginning of each phase, and you will develop the report over the course of the phase.

### Miscellany Specifications

A main purpose of the course log is to explore possibility within structure. As such, meeting the following specifications is important for your log grade.

- Notebook is actually a college-ruled composition book
- Name, section, and “Miscellany” on the cover
- The first page says "Table of Contents" at the top
- The first 5 pages of are reserved for the table of contents
- The table of contents is up to date with a title for each entry and page numbers in a column on the far right
- The bottom right of every odd numbered page has a page number (if you have used the page)
- New entries have a title at the top

Assignments that don't meet all specifications will have 5% deducted from their grade. (90%-->85%) Those that fail to follow multiple specifications will have 10% deducted from their grade. (90%-→80%.). A random selection of miscellanies will be periodically spot-checked in class

## Homework

- Assigned on Friday, due in class the following Friday

- Will include varied work, such as playing and analyzing a game, taking notes on a reading, recording ethnographic observations, or writing a guided reflection.

## Examinations

At the end of each segment of the course we will have a written examination. The emphasis in each examination will be on practical application of concepts and techniques practiced in the course. This may involve responding to a new case study or drawing on data gathered in your course log. The class session before each exam will be a review of relevant concepts and techniques.

## Quizzes

Each Tuesday we will have a short quiz in class. The format of the quizzes will vary, but will be a mixture of factual information recall, reflection, and analysis. If memorization of facts, definitions, or the like, is expected, you will be given advance notice.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
