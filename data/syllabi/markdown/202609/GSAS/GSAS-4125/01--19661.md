## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The goal of this game art course is to introduce students to the core concepts, software, and workflow of character modeling for a video game pipeline.

Students will be concepting and mocking up a character of their own design either from the waist up, or a full character. Depending on the weight you'd like to place on the class.

Students will then be moving to Zbrush for the bulk of the course where we will learn anatomy, hair, cloth, hard surface, detail, and various sculpting techniques for completing your character.

Students will then take the character and UV and retopologize it, in order to prepare it for texturing. Some remodeling or optimization may need to be done in order to fully prep it for texturing.

Students will be texturing in Substance Painter in combination with Material creation in Unreal in order to render the final model.

The final rendered shots will be lit and rendered in Unreal.

Students should follow a stylized or realistic workflow. Anime, cartoons, chibi characters will be too simple for the scope of the class.

### Text Details:

- Maya

- Wacom Tablet or Cintiq access
- Wacom tablets can be checked out from the Sage checkout space and used in the game design lab.
- There is also a Graphics Lab which has 4 large Cintiqs. The pens can be checked out as well.
- Optional: Marmoset Toolbag 2

The following books, videos, and sites are not required but encouraged to help development as an artist and modeler.

- **Anatomy for Sculptors, Understanding the Human Figure**
- [Uldis Zarins with Sandis Kondrats](http://www.amazon.com/s/ref=dp_byline_sr_book_1?ie=UTF8&text=Uldis+Zarins+with+Sandis+Kondrats&search-alias=books&field-author=Uldis+Zarins+with+Sandis+Kondrats&sort=relevancerank), 2014
- Great book that highlights human anatomy and makes it easy to understand and sculpt from a digital perspective.
- **ZBrush Characters and Creatures**
- Kurt Papstein, 2015
- Highlights a lot of good individual techniques for getting results like musculature, skin folds, scales, and other organic visual cues. This is an intermediate level book to get once you feel comfortable working in ZBrush.
- **ZBrush Hard Surface Techniques**
- Mike Jensen, 2013
- Great for hard surface oriented sculpting in ZBrush for guns, mechs, robots, vehicles, etc. Also has a great part about compositing images out of ZBrush. This is a DVD series.
- **The Game Artist's Guide to Maya**
- Michael McKinley, 2006
- Older but excellent book that walks beginners through creating and animating a character step by step. Great for getting a feel of what the character pipeline feels like.
- **Autodesk Documentation**
- When it comes to understanding and learning Maya I don’t recommend buying a book because of how fast Maya changes. With updates releasing every year books quickly become obsolete so Autodesk’s documentation is really the place to get accustomed to when looking for help with Maya.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students can expect to have the following skills upon completion of the course:

- Concept drafting
- Basic modeling in Maya
- Advanced sculpting in ZBrush
- Texturing in ZBrush, Substance, and Photoshop

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Assessments

- Students are graded on a milestone basis with regards to a character modeler’s pipeline.
- Milestone 1: *Concept Art Package*
- **Due: end of week 2**
- This milestone is due at the end of week 3 and is comprised of a small PDF of a character bio, concept art, references, and influences detailing the character the student plans to make over the semester.
- Submission: PDF, JPG
- Topics Covered: Design and concept art. Research gathering, presentation and communicating ideas.
- Milestone 2: *Low Poly OBJ*
- **Due: end of week 5**
- At this milestone students will be submitting a low polygon version of their model complete with all clothing, weapons, accessories, etc. Each piece of the model should be complete enough to be moved to ZBrush for high detail sculpting.
- Submission: OBJ or MB
- Topics Covered: Working in Maya, MSR, symmetry, extruding, poly/edge/vertex, human anatomy, merging, poly counts.
- Milestone 3: *Practice Sculpts*
- **Due: end of various weeks, classes**
- At various points in the class students will be submitting practice sculpts to demonstrate their progress in various sculpting techniques like anatomy, hair, cloth, hard surface, etc.
- Submission: OBJ, Images
- Topics Covered: ZBrush sculpting
- ~~Milestone 3:~~ *~~Custom UI~~*
- **~~Due: beginning of week 6~~**
- ~~This milestone marks where students should have a custom UI and shortcuts prepared inside of ZBrush. Students can either show the professor quickly their custom UI or submit the preferences and brushes file.~~
- ~~Submission: Visual credit or submit brushes and preferences file.~~
- ~~Topics Covered: Students will be making their own UIs and shortcuts in order to dramatically speed up workflow inside of ZBrush and make the program more comfortable for their working style.~~
- Midterm
- **Due: M or W of Week 8**
- This will be a test of the students retained knowledge of the techniques and software that have been used to date in the course. It will be administered in class and due at the end of the lecture session.
- Submission: JPG
- Milestone 4: *High Poly*
- **Due: end of week 10**
- This milestone should mark where students have completed sculpting or have neared the point of having to break symmetry for their base character mesh. Students should be ready to begin applying alphas, UVs, and textures on the base character mesh.
- Submission: Base Mesh w/o Accessories-Front, Back, Side, Extra JPGs
- Topics Covered: Working in ZBrush, dynamesh, subdivision levels, brushes, human anatomy, masking, subtools, lazy mouse, deformations.
- Milestone 5: *Retopology*
- **Due: end of week 11**
- This milestone should mark where students have taken their base mesh out of ZBrush and retopologized it in either Maya or Topogun as a cleaner low poly mesh. Students should also have reimported the low res mesh back into ZBrush.
- Submission: Base Mesh w/o accessories- OBJ
- Topics Covered: Working in Topogun or Maya’s quad draw. Poly count management, symmetry, edge loops, edge flow, UVs, vertex density.
- Milestone 6: *UVs*
- **Due: end of week 12**
- This milestone marks where students should have their base model UV’d and laid out.
- Submission: Base Mesh w/o accessories – OBJ
- Topics Covered: UVing in Maya, Zbrush, and Topogun. UV shells, reading UVs, seams, stretching, overlapping UVs.
- Milestone 7: *Maps*
- **Due: beginning of week 15**
- This milestone marks where students should have their base mesh completely textured, detailed, and have maps exported from ZBrush and be game ready.
- Submission: At least 4 maps (depending on workflow) in a single JPG.
- Topics Covered: Painting textures using spotlight in ZBrush, adding detail via alphas. Substance Painter.

- Milestone 8: *Final Shots & Presentation*
- **Due: end of semester**
- The final milestone will mark the end of the class where students must submit a bust of their character, textured, detailed, and posed. Students are NOT required to UV and retoplogize every part of their character i.e. weapons, accessories, clothes, etc. Students should also have the scene lit and render settings adjusted appropriately for a final shot.
- Students should also have a small presentation to show the class at the end of the semester highlighting the work they have done.
- Submission: Front, Back, Side, Top/Bottom/Quarter (A-pose)
- 3-5 Beauty shots w/ posing, lighting, post process, splashes, etc.
- You may submit a short turntable video of your model OR
- A Marmoset model viewer link.

- Software: ZBrush
- **Week 7:** Dynamesh, masking, selections, clipping, trimming, anatomy, MSR bar breakdown.
- Software: ZBrush
- **Week 8:** Brush, stroke, alpha, materials, deformation. Midterm.
- Software: ZBrush
- **Week 9:** Hyper detail, IMMs, VDMs,
- Software: ZBrush
- **Week 10:** Critique
- Software: N/A

- Software: Maya, Photoshop, ZBrush, Substance Painter.

- **Week 15:** Polishing models. Using layers. Hard surface workshop. Lighting in ZBrush. Post Process. Turntable. Marmoset model viewer.
- Software: ZBrush, Photoshop
- **Week 16:** Critique/ Final presentations.
- Software: N/A

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

Policies

- Late Work
- There is NO Late work. Every assignment can be submitted unlimited times, so always submit what you have when you have it. But there is no late work, if its 1 minute late its a 0. There are no exceptions.
- Better to submit something 90% complete and finish it later for your portfolio than it completely done a few days late.
- Attendance
- Attendance accounts for 5% of your grade.
- Class lectures will be very important for completing your model on time and will save you hours/days of research later.
- There is no group work in this class.
- If any of these policies conflict with your schedule/needs please contact me.
- **Purposeful Ambiguity -** Creative fields can be frustrating when feedback is not specific, but it is an unfortunate part of the industry. You must acclimate yourself to working with limited feedback at times. The instructor will always step in with more directed feedback or paint over work when progress has stalled, but be prepared to deal with times where you must creatively work through ambiguity.
- **Sensitive Topics -** Like with any creative/artistic course there may be a discussion of sensitive topics. These topics will always be discussed neutrally, and from an academic perspective. The instructor will always do their best to handle them elegantly and use them as learning opportunities.
- **AI & Plagiarism** - AI usage is considered plagiarism and will be treated like any other plagiarism case and will be reported to the University for disciplinary action**.**

Course Procedures

- Each class in between Milestones will also have an optional critique for students looking for feedback on their models.
- Classes will consist of the professor walking through the next part of character development on a large screen so students can follow the techniques, concepts, and softwares used for character modeling.
- A persistent character model that the professor is building will act as an example so students can see the modeling process in real time.
- The .mb, .obj, or .ztl will be made available after every class session.
- Each class will have an outline of important techniques and topics covered so students can review them later on documentation sites, with the professor, or the optional readings.
- Assignments will be distributed and turned in via Blackboard
