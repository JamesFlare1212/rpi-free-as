## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Concept Art is a design course oriented towards quickly bringing ideas into being. Ideating and visualizing worlds, props, environments, characters, creatures, motion, and interaction for games and evolving experiential media.

Level: Intermediate (assumes basic drawing and digital painting literacy)

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

\- Scott Robertson & Thomas Bertling, \*How to Draw\* and \*How to Render - Andrew Loomis, \*Figure Drawing for All It's Worth\* — free, public domain, still unbeaten on proportion - James Gurney, \*Color and Light\* — the best practical book on light logic - Published art-of books for any shipped game (studio library) —as design documents, not picture books - FZD School and Trent Kaniuga process videos — watch for workflow and decision-making, not for style - GDC Vault art talks — free tier, especially pipeline and art-direction sessions

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Essentially students learn how concept artists work, what skills to develop, what a professional design brief package is, how to create an original design brief, and how to work from others’ IP/ design briefs.

creating an original design brief: Developing short, descriptive text of the general look and feel of the project. -environmental narrative development: quick thumbnails/ notans, editing/picking the best, imaging ethics, mood boards, photo bashing, 3d blockouts, digital painting -props: quick sketches, 2 & 3d development, texturing, uv mapping, exploded views -vehicles: concept sketches, for spaceships, cars, or giant robots that focus on how they move and function. Include exploded views -character development: quick sketches, trait sheet, silhouette, turnaround, 2d, 3d, texturing, uv mapping -Communicating through design briefs so others in the pipeline can work well from this blueprint.

The course focuses on building foundational art skills (perspective, lighting, anatomy, color) while applying design training for games or films, including character, environment, and prop design. Key components include ideation, rendering, photo bashing, 3D modeling, portfolio development, and translating creative briefs. The course helps students move from mastering art fundamentals (the "how" of drawing) to design principles (the "why" and "what") of creation. Students solve design problems for a production team under constraints of time, style, and technical feasibility — and then communicate that solution clearly enough so that someone else can build it. This course teaches this discipline: generating ideas at volume, filtering them ruthlessly, and finishing them to a standard a modeler, animator, or art director can use. Fundamentals (perspective, value, shape, color) are treated as tools in service of design decisions, not as ends in themselves. **Format:** 16 weeks two 2-hour sessions per week, 6–9 hours of studio work outside class

*Session A* — **Design Challenge Overview Launch and Critiques** of the previous assignment (30-40 min), then the week's design concept, case studies, class research and discussion. Ends with the brief for the week's assignment. *Session B* — **Studio & Technique** Demos of the tools and methods that solve the week's problem, then supervised lab time. Students leave with the assignment underway, not blank.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Students Generate 30+ distinct thumbnails on a brief and defend a shortlist. 2. Students design an environment, prop, vehicle, and a character that read clearly in silhouette at 10% scale. 3. Build and use a 3D blockout as an under-layer for a painted environment. 4. Deliver a design package a third party can build from without asking questions. 5. Produce a design in a style not their own, matching a supplied style guide. 6. Take art direction, revise without ego, and articulate reasoning in critique. 7. Assemble a portfolio showing process and not just finishes including best works from the semester and original design package

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Teardowns** (detailed assessment and critique) of published concept art from your 3 favorite games. Analyze focal point, "anomaly" principle, lighting & atmospheric staging such as backlighting, rim lighting, atmospheric bloom **2 points**

**30 Notans** showing form and composition using black and white digital or traditional technique (Select 5 best original ones and defend why) **3 points**

**Original 2d environment concept art** showing composition, atmosphere, perspective, value grouping, location, mood, scale anchors in the service of gameplay architecture: identifying clear paths, cover points, and visual choke points that support the game's mechanics and style **5 points**

**Original 3d environment concept art** showing composition, atmosphere, perspective, value grouping, location, mood, scale anchors in the service of gameplay architecture: identifying clear paths, cover points, and visual choke points that support the game's mechanics and style **5 points**

**Props and Prop & Hard-Surface Callouts:** Detailed blueprints or close-up drawings of everyday items, magical artifacts, or armaments that include material swatches utilizing real-world photo references (e.g., rusted corrugated iron, brushed carbon fiber) to define surface roughness and texture behaviors for shading artists. Include exploded views that show how a weapon, mechanical device, or door hinges open to allow VFX and animation teams to build functional moving parts. **5 points Vehicles and Technology:** Design concept sketches, for spaceships, cars, or giant robots that focus on how they move and function. Include exploded views **5 points**

**Full character sheet** — front/side/back, turnaround, callouts, color variants — plus one rendered in-world hero image of the same character, with descriptive text of characteristics **10 points Original Design brief and package: your** original style and design as indicated in a well thought out and executed design brief consisting of concept art for a cohesive environment, character development with descriptive text of characteristics, and prop/vehicle. Include highly technical, granular orthographic drawings (front, side, back views), silhouette at scales, turnarounds. Include technical information on movement, exploded views, **45 points**

**Design package for a 3rd party IP** create a new concept branch of an existing game using their style and IP **10 points**

**Work Habits**

Always back-up your work frequently; that is, every time you make something you think is worth keeping. Systems crash when least expected and you could lose all your work. It is a good idea to make three backups (on different media), as storage media are sometimes unstable. Always save onto your own media or into your account as files left on hard drives will be removed.

Try not to harm or deface any equipment, environment or software in any way or lose files and folders belonging to our class or other classes.

Also, please keep in mind the highly addictive aspects of working with computers. Many people lose track of time and later wonder why they have severe back, neck and eye problems. It is a good idea to take a rest every 15 to 20 minutes. Look up or beyond your computer or, better still, at a long distance to relax your eyes. Take a walk or stretch. Fatigue can lead to frustration. Stay in touch with your body's needs. All the best for a productive semester.
