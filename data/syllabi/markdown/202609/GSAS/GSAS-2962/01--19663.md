## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course explores creative coding as a practice of using computational techniques for artistic expression. It will introduce open-source programming libraries and tools used by artists and programmers to create interactive experiences and artworks. Through hands-on projects, students will develop intermediate skills of programming while also learning the strategies of generating art through coding and pushing the aesthetic boundaries of code.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

- Cultivate a perspective that views coding as an expressive tool akin to a pencil for sketching or a paintbrush for painting.

- Gain the experience of conceptualizing and bringing artwork to life through computational methods.

- Explore the relationship between art and technology, recognizing how they intersect and complement each other in creative endeavors.

- Enhance art critique skills by reflecting on the aesthetic, cultural, and social significance of code-based artworks.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to use creative coding tools p5.js and processing.
- Students will be able to implement procedural and generative techniques (noise, randomness, cellular automata) to create patterns, textures, and generative systems.
- Students will be able to build particle systems and physics-based simulations.
- Students will be able to design and implement interactive experiences using event-driven input (mouse, keyboard, sliders, buttons).

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

ASSIGNMENTS

Compositions

Due: 9/10

Create three distinctive and static images using p5.js with mostly geometric shapes. The goal is to make visually compelling compositions with an emphasis on structure and form. Artist references: Piet Mondrian, Agnes Martin, Mark Rothko, Frank Stella, Ellsworth Kelly.

Patterns

Due: 9/17

Create three distinctive, static, and procedurally generated images using p5.js. The goal is to create patterns using the method of procedural generation. Artist references: Casey Reas, Jason Salavon, Tyler Hobbs, Bridget Riley, Sol LeWitt, Carlos Cruz-Diez.

Motions

Due: 9/24

Animate the three patterned images from your last assignment (or create new ones) using p5.js. Your goal is to make fluid animations that visualize motion within the patterns. Artist references: Zach Lieberman, Refik Anadol, Markus Heckmann, Olia Lialina.

Particles

Due: 10/08

Create a particle system using p5.js. It can be flames, fountains, flies, and flakes, or any other realistic or abstract elements. This is the LAST assignment where you are required to use p5.js. Artist references: Daniel Shiffman (TheCodingTrain), Robert Hodgin, Memo Akten.

Procedural Generator

Due: 10/22

Create a generator that generates objects based on user-defined attributes, for example, a kitten generator that generates kittens with customizable features such as eye color, fur color, size, and shape. Use any tools you like. Artist references: Everest Pipkin, Max Bittker, Daniel Temkin.

Interactive Simulation

Due: 12/10

Create an interactive simulation that displays a dynamic system where user interaction can influence its behavior which can be observed in real-time. Artist references: Max Bittker, Rafaël Rozendaal, Daniel Rozin, Golan Levin, Matt Deslauriers.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Plagiarism

Plagiarism is defined as representing the work or words of another as one's own through the omission of acknowledgement or reference. Examples include using sentences verbatim from a published source in a term paper without appropriate referencing, or presenting as one's own the detailed argument of a published source, or presenting as one's own electronically or digitally enhanced graphic representations from any form of media.
