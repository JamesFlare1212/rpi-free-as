## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Student will be able to Analyze Modularity Politics (Taxonomy: Analyze/Evaluate): Deconstruct how software modularity, standardization, and architectural active transport shape development workflows, industrial labor, and power dynamics across game production ecosystems.
- Student will be able to Critique Sensory & Cultural Encoding (Taxonomy: Critique): Examine the social, cultural, and political dimensions of codecs and compression technologies, evaluating how technical standards construct psychovisual regimes and mediate human perception.
- Student will be able to Investigate Network Infrastructure & Power Dynamics (Taxonomy: Analyze): Interrogate netcode, server architectures, and networking protocols as political infrastructures that structure online sociality, governance, and platform control.
- Student will be able to Evaluate Economic & Labor Impacts of Abstraction (Taxonomy: Evaluate): Assess the social, economic, and organizational friction introduced by software abstraction layers, engine monopolies, and proprietary middleware runtimes.
- Student will be able to Examine AI Middleware & Algorithmic Ethics (Taxonomy: Evaluate/Synthesize): Critically analyze the ethical, cultural, and labor implications of stochastic AI runtimes and automated asset schemas within media production pipelines.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Artificial Intelligence (AI) Policy: Contextual Integration & Academic Integrity

In this course, Generative AI tools (e.g., LLMs, visual/audio generators, code assistants) are recognized as modern game design and research tools. However, because this is an advanced graduate seminar focusing on empirical methods, human-centered design, and academic scholarship, the use of AI is context-dependent.

1\. Permitted Uses (AI as a Production & Evaluative Co-Pilot)

You are permitted and encouraged to use AI tools for:

- Synthetic Personas & Heuristic Pre-Testing: Generating AI-driven user personas or using LLM agent simulations to conduct rapid early-stage usability walk-throughs, stress-test dialogue/narrative trees, or identify obvious UX friction points prior to human playtests.
- Production & Prototyping: Accelerating code development (e.g., debugging, optimizing scripts in Unity/Unreal) and generating preliminary visual or audio assets for prototypes.
- Ideation & Research Preparation: Exploring design concepts, drafting candidate survey questions, or structuring paper outlines.
- Grammar & Editing: Refining prose and technical writing for clarity.

2\. Restricted & Prohibited Uses (Preserving Empirical Rigor)

To preserve the theoretical and empirical integrity of graduate-level user research:

- No Substitution for Empirical Human Data: While synthetic personas and AI-simulated responses are valid for preliminary design evaluation, they cannot replace actual human participant data in your final study results. All empirical findings, user metrics, and primary research conclusions in your Midterm Study Plan and Final Paper must derive from genuine human testing.
- No Uncritical Framing: Synthetic data or persona output must be critically analyzed for bias, hallucinations, and limitations—never presented as an uncritical proxy for real human behavior.
- No Uncritical Synthesis: AI tools may not be used to write the primary theoretical analysis of your case studies or literature reviews.
- Privacy Restrictions: Do not input confidential participant data, human subject interview recordings, or proprietary materials into unvetted public models.

3\. Student Accountability & Disclosure Requirements

100% Intellectual Ownership: You are fully accountable for the factual accuracy, code stability, academic integrity, and ethical implications of everything you submit. Presenting unverified or hallucinated AI output (e.g., fake citations or flawed code) constitutes an academic integrity violation.

Mandatory AI Disclosure: Every major deliverable (Case Studies, Midterm Study Plan, Final Research Paper) must include an AI Disclosure Appendix detailing:

1. Tools Used: Specific models/platforms (e.g., Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Midjourney, GitHub Copilot).
2. Scope of Application: Exact sections, features, code modules, or persona simulations assisted by AI.
3. Prompting Strategy & Verification: A brief statement on how synthetic data or AI output was verified, edited, or methodologically separated from human subject data.
