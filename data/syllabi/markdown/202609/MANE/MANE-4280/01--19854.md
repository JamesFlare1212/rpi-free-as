## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This (co-listed) course introduces the theory and practical use of numerical design optimization methods. Topics include: gradient-based methods for unconstrained and constrained nonlinear optimization; numerical evaluation of derivatives; polynomial and kriging-based surrogate models; gradient-free optimization methods; optimization under uncertainty; multi-objective and multi-disciplinary optimization. Projects require the use of computer programs to generate numerical results; therefore, experience with programming is highly recommended.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

- Identify and apply appropriate optimization algorithms/software for engineering design problems that are amenable to optimization.
- Formulate design problems as optimization statements, selecting suitable mathematical models for the physical systems as necessary.
- Prepare for relevant industrial positions that use, or could use, design optimization.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Unconstrained Optimization of Smooth Objectives: explain local optima, global optima, and stationary points; state and explain the first-order necessary and second-order necessary/sufficient optimality conditions for a generic, unconstrained smooth objective; describe how the contours of an objective are related to its gradient and why the gradient is the direction of steepest descent; implement step computations for the steepest descent, Newton, and BFGS methods; describe the relative advantages and disadvantages of the steepest descent, Newton, and quasi-Newton methods; analyze the relative performance of two or more unconstrained optimization algorithm based on given data, and; formulate and solve unconstrained design optimization problems.
- Computing and Analyzing Derivatives: state the advantages and disadvantages of 1) the finite-difference method; 2) the complex-step method, and; 3) algorithmic differentiation in the context of computing derivatives numerically; implement first- and second-order finite-difference formulae and the complex- step method in Matlab; apply algorithmic- differentiation software in both the forward and reverse modes; verify derivative accuracy using, e.g., simplified problems, and; identify the parameters that impact a design the most (sensitivity analysis).
- Constrained Optimization of Smooth Objectives and Constraints: define the Lagrangian, and derive the first-order necessary conditions for a generic, constrained smooth problem; interpret the importance of a constraint based on its Lagrange multiplier; define and explain the feasible/infeasible spaces; implement and use penalty methods to find approximate solutions to constrained problems, and; use SQP/interior-point methods and assess their convergence.
- Surrogate Modeling: state the advantages and disadvantages of surrogate models in the context of design optimization; in particular, explain the curse-of-dimensionality; employ sampling methods (e.g. Latin-hypercube sampling) to create the initial data for a surrogate model; find interpolants/fits based on linear and quadratic polynomials in n-dimensions; create Kriging-based (or GPR) surrogates for optimization, including the determination of parameters via maximization of the concentrated log-likelihood function, and; use surrogates in design optimization problems, including some form of adaptive sampling.
- Gradient-free Optimization Methods: explain when gradient-free methods are appropriate, and when they are not; describe the Nelder-Mead simplex algorithm, the genetic algorithm, and the particle-swarm algorithm; apply gradient-free methods to solve optimization problems, either directly or on surrogate models; analyze the performance of two or more gradient-free optimization algorithms on the same problem, and; compare gradient-free and gradient- based algorithms on both smooth and non-smooth problems.
- Optimization Under Uncertainty: explain the risks of using deterministic models in the context of design optimization; list sources of uncertainty that can impact design optimization, and explain the difference between aleatory and epistemic uncertainties; model random inputs using probability density functions, and apply Monte-Carlo and stochastic collocation methods for forward propagation of uncertainties, and; formulate and solve robust optimization and reliability-based optimization problems.
- Other/Advanced Topics: select appropriate design-space parameterizations; select appropriate mathematical and/or computational models for engineering design problems, and implement them; explain Pareto optimality in the context of multi- objective optimization; explain multi-disciplinary analysis, and why it is critical in many fields of engineering design optimization, and; distinguish between high- and low-fidelity models, and when each is appropriate.
- MANE 6710 Outcomes: In addition to the above, students enrolled in MANE 6710 are expected to; identify and read appropriate academic literature; write software for engineering analysis and/or combine analysis software with optimization software; present methodology and results in an academic-style report.

### Important Grading Policies

****Project Weightings:****

Student project grades will be calculated as the product of their raw project grade and their grade on the project section of the corresponding test. For example, a student with a raw grade of 90% on Project #1 and a grade of 75% on the project section of Test #1 will receive a grade of 90% x 75% = 67.5% on Project #1. The questions on the project section of the tests are intended to be straightforward provided a student wrote their own code and report.

****Project Oral Exam (MANE 6710 only):****

Students will be given a short (5-10 minute) oral exam at the end of the semester to verify their understanding of their independent study, including any software that they wrote. The questions on this exam are intended to be straightforward provided a student wrote their own code and report. The oral exam score will then weight the project score. For example, suppose a student obtains 80% on their raw project grade. If the student answers all oral exam questions correctly, their project grade will remain 80%. However, if they achieve 50% on the oral exam, their project grade will be 0.5 x 80% = 40%.

****In-class Assignment Drops (MANE 4280 only):****

The two in-class assignments on which a student scores lowest --- including assignments not submitted --- will not be included in a student's assignment grade. For example, a student who scores 100% on all assignments except for two will obtain a 100% assignment score.

### Artificial Intelligence (AI) Policy

Students are permitted to use AI tools --- such as ChatGPT and other large-language models (LLMs) --- **provided they do not copy data into the prompt or copy data out of the output response.** In this context, data includes (but is not limited to) copied text, code, and images. Examples of acceptable uses of AI tools include the following:

- Asking questions to clarify concepts;
- Asking an AI to improve the grammar of a sentence, or;
- Asking for help translating a snippet of Python code to Matlab.

Students are reminded that LLMs may provide incorrect information. Examples of unacceptable uses of AI include the following.

- Using AI to write a significant fraction of your code;
- Using AI to generate data for results or figures, or;
- Using AI to write your report.

Think of AI tools like a classmate. It is acceptable to get help from a classmate to clarify a concept or get their help with Matlab syntax. It is not acceptable to copy their code or their report text. *If a student is unsure if a particular use of AI is acceptable or not, they should contact Prof. Hicken for clarification.*
