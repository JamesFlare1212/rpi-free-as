### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

**Course Description:**

This course provides a graduate-level treatment of heat transfer, with primary emphasis on the mathematical analysis of heat conduction. Analytical and numerical methods for solving the heat diffusion equation are developed for steady-state, transient, and multidimensional problems. Selected topics in convection and thermal radiation are also introduced to provide a broader understanding of heat-transfer processes, including multimode heat transfer, and to establish a foundation for more advanced coursework in convection and radiation.

## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Objectives as the Instructor:**

The primary objective of this course is to develop a rigorous understanding of heat transfer, with particular emphasis on **conduction and thermal diffusion**. Students will learn to formulate heat-transfer problems from fundamental conservation laws, identify appropriate assumptions and boundary and initial conditions, and develop mathematical models for steady-state, transient, and multidimensional conduction. Emphasis will be placed on developing the ability to decompose complex thermal systems into tractable subproblems and to select appropriate analytical or numerical methods for their solution.

Students will develop familiarity with analytical techniques used in solving the heat diffusion equation in **Cartesian, cylindrical, and spherical coordinate systems**. Depending on the nature of the problem, these methods may include separation of variables, Fourier series, eigenfunction expansions, Bessel functions, Legendre polynomials, superposition, and Duhamel's theorem. Particular attention will be given not only to obtaining mathematical solutions but also to **interpreting their physical significance**, identifying characteristic length and time scales, and understanding the influence of material properties, geometry, boundary conditions, and heat generation on thermal behavior.

The course will also introduce **selected topics in convection and thermal radiation** to provide students with a broader understanding of the principal modes of heat transfer. These topics will emphasize the physical mechanisms and governing principles of convective and radiative transport and their interaction with conduction in multimode heat-transfer problems. This coverage is intended both to provide a well-rounded graduate-level foundation in heat transfer and to prepare students for more specialized advanced courses in convection and radiation.

Analytical methods will be complemented by **computational approaches** for problems that are difficult or impractical to solve analytically. MATLAB will be used for numerical calculations, evaluation of series solutions, visualization, and interpretation of results. Students will also be encouraged to use computational tools such as ABAQUS, ANSYS, or COMSOL to investigate complex geometries, boundary conditions, and coupled thermal problems. Through these activities, students will develop an understanding of the complementary roles of analytical and computational approaches and the importance of verifying and physically interpreting numerical solutions.

Finally, the course will incorporate a **research-oriented project** in which students investigate a thermal transport problem of current scientific or engineering interest. Through project development, technical analysis, written reports, and presentations, students will gain experience in applying the concepts and methods learned in the course to open-ended problems. The overall objective is to develop the analytical, computational, physical, and communication skills needed to address advanced heat-transfer problems encountered in research and engineering practice.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Course Objectives:**

1. Develop a fundamental understanding of heat-transfer principles, with primary emphasis on conduction and selected concepts in convection and thermal radiation.
2. Formulate and use analytical methods to solve steady-state, transient, and multidimensional heat-conduction problems.
3. Use numerical and computational methods to solve complex heat-transfer problems for which analytical solutions are impractical.
4. Analyze heat-transfer problems involving conduction, convection, radiation, and combinations of these modes, and interpret the resulting thermal behavior.
5. Conduct, document, and present an independent investigation of a current research or engineering problem involving thermal transport.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Course Outline:**

This course will cover selected topics in **graduate-level heat transfer**, with primary emphasis on **conduction heat transfer**. Analytical and computational methods for the formulation and solution of heat-transfer problems will form an important component of the course. Selected topics in **convection, thermal radiation, and other areas of current interest** may also be introduced to provide a broader perspective on thermal transport and complement the primary focus on conduction.

The topics listed below represent a **tentative course outline**. The depth and sequence of coverage may vary depending on the background and interests of the class and the time available. Some topics may be treated in greater depth, while others may be abbreviated, replaced, or supplemented with additional topics in conduction, convection, radiation, computational heat transfer, or other areas relevant to contemporary heat-transfer research and engineering applications.

1. **Fundamentals of Heat Conduction**

- Fourier's law and thermal conductivity
- Derivation of the differential heat-conduction equation
- Cartesian, cylindrical, and spherical coordinate systems
- Initial and boundary conditions
- Steady and transient conduction
- Lumped-capacitance analysis
- Variable properties and anisotropic media

2. **Analytical Methods for Heat-Conduction Problems**

- Mathematical formulation of conduction problems
- Orthogonal functions and eigenfunction expansions
- Sturm–Liouville problems
- Fourier-series solutions

3. **Separation of Variables: Cartesian Coordinates**

- Basic concepts
- Steady-state problems
- Transient problems
- Multidimensional conduction
- Method of superposition

4. **Separation of Variables: Cylindrical Coordinates**

- Basic concepts
- Steady-state problems
- Transient problems
- Bessel functions and their application to conduction problems

5. **Separation of Variables: Spherical Coordinates**

- Basic concepts
- Steady-state problems
- Transient problems
- Relevant eigenfunction solutions

6. **Selected Advanced Analytical Methods**

- Time-dependent boundary conditions
- Duhamel's theorem
- Treatment of discontinuities
- Superposition and related solution techniques
- Other analytical methods as appropriate

7. **Selected Topics in Convection Heat Transfer**

- Physical mechanisms of convection
- Governing principles and dimensionless parameters
- Selected topics in forced and/or natural convection
- Conduction–convection and other coupled heat-transfer problems

8. **Selected Topics in Thermal Radiation**

- Fundamental concepts of thermal radiation
- Radiative properties of surfaces
- Radiation exchange and selected applications
- Combined conduction, convection, and radiation problems

9. **Selected and Contemporary Topics in Heat Transfer**

- Topics drawn from current research and engineering applications
- Advanced or emerging heat-transfer phenomena
- Topics associated with student research projects, as appropriate

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Homework (25%):**

Homework is meant to be a learning experience, not a regurgitation of previously learned concepts. Problems sets will elucidate the finer points of applying the fundamental theory that is difficult to convey in lectures, and students should pay close attention to the patterns and concepts that arise in homework that are not discussed in lectures.

**Class Participation (5%):**

Your class participation grade will be based on the level and quality of involvement in the class. The goal of the class is that, through participation, students not only have their own learning experience but can see and be part of the learning experiences of their classmates.

**Exams (2 @ 20% each):**

The exams are to be completed independently of anyone else. You can use notes, textbooks, and any class materials; however, **you may not communicate with anyone else regarding the exam’s content**. This includes coworkers, friends, fellow students, and even other professors. I do not expect that we will incur any issues with academic integrity. If such issues arise, the proper measures in the student handbook will be taken. Any form of communication with others during an exam is prohibited and will result in an F for the test. Both types of violations may be reported immediately.

**Final Project (30%):**

The final project will consist of an in-depth conduction analysis completed with finite element (FEA) software. Students will propose their projects and may use any available FEA software after approval from the instructor. Work on projects will begin during the course. A project proposal (5%) is the first related assignment and will describe the physical problem, background, objectives, and expected results. In order to ensure continued progress, two progress reports (intermediate-5% and final-10%) should be submitted describing the progress (and outlining the remaining steps in the case of the intermediate report). In addition to the final report, students will present their results in a formal presentation (10%). The presentation (15 minutes total) will be graded based on the peer (5%) and instructor (5%) evaluations.
