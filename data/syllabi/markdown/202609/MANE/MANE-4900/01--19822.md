## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Analysis of free and forced vibration of continuous structures (bars, beams, rods). Natural frequencies and mode shapes. Exact solutions of PDEs, approximate solutions based on energy methods (Rayleigh-Ritz and Finite Element methods). Introduction to static and dynamic aeroelasticity including phenomena of divergence, control reversal and flutter.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Optional: Dewey H. Hodges and G. Alvin Pierce, Introduction to Structural Dynamics and Aeroelasticity, Second Edition (2011), Cambridge University Press.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to analyze the natural frequencies of a vibrating string
- Students will be able to estimate the natural frequencies of a torsion rod using analytical and numerical methods.
- Students will be able to identify the natural frequencies of a bending beam, using analytical and numerical methods.
- Students will be able to describe and analyze static aeroelastic phenomena such as divergence and aileron reversal
- Students will be able to describe and analyze wing flutter.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

5 homework assignments will assess the course learning outcomes (one assignment for each CLO)  
3 midterm exams will assess student mastery of the CLOs. Additionally, an optional final will be administered.  
Final exam functions as a retake of up to two midterms. Students are judged based on the better of the final and the associated midterm(s).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Honesty is the cornerstone of an education. All work that you submit must be your own. The exception is on homework, which may be performed collaboratively with your classmates but must be disclosed at the beginning of any assignment.

A student found to be in violation of this policy will receive a grade of zero on the assignment for a first offense. A second offense will result in a grade of F for the course.

Work submitted after the posted due date is considered “late.” Any late work will have a 50% penalty applied to it after it is graded. For example, a late assignment that would have scored an 80/100 if submitted on-time will be scored as a 40/100. Exceptions will be made according to Institute policy, and may also be granted at my discretion.
