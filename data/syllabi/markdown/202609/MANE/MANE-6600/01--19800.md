## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course trains students in basic state-space theory for analysis of linear systems and design of linear state-feedback controllers. Broadly, the course covers (1) state-space models and representations of finite dimensional linear time-invariant systems, (2) controllability & observability, and (3) controller design through pole placement. The course also reviews linear algebra and matrix theoretic concepts that are relevant to the state-space theory, with applications. Some computation tasks will be done with MATLAB.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand the basic concepts of vector spaces, linear operators, basis, and linear algebra fundamentals.
- Understand the concept of state variables and how to derive them from different linear system representations.
- Know how to solve a system of linear differential equations in state space form
- Understand the geometric aspects of linear systems including eigenvalues decomposition and singular value decomposition
- Understand the concepts of controllability and observability of linear systems
- Be able to design state-space controllers for stabilization and pole placement for linear systems

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

***Assignments – Homework and Enrichment*****:**

**Homework:** 6 homework assignments will be given to provide opportunities to practice and master the concepts in this course. Students will have 1 to 2 weeks to complete each assignment. They are to be *individually* and professionally done and submitted before the beginning of the class when due to receive credit. You are encouraged to collaborate with others and to judiciously use outside resources as learning tools (see section on AI usage). Homework assignments will be reviewed for integrity and scored for completion. The lowest homework assignment score will be dropped. The grader may provide some feedback on mistakes, and solutions will be posted after the due date for self-review. You are highly encouraged attend office hours to follow up on any parts of the solution you may not have understood.

**Enrichment:** 4-6 special assignments will be included that are meant to stimulate higher-level thinking about the relevance and implications of the knowledge from this course. They will often occur as in-class questions and are not intended to be time intensive. These assignments will be graded for completion and relevance rather than correct/incorrect.

***Midterm Exams:***

There will be 3 (three) closed book, closed notes exams throughout the semester. ***No collaboration is permitted.*** Exams 1 and 2 will each be worth 35% of the grade, and will include opportunities for reworking solutions to earn back partial credit. Exam 3 will be worth 20% of the grade.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

***Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI)*****:**

AI tools such as large language models are increasingly used as a sophisticated way to search and synthesize information. As such, you may find AI to be a useful tool to help you better understand some of the abstract concepts in this course. Appropriate ways to use AI in this manner may include:

- providing explanations of concepts
- creating additional example or practice problems

- generating ideas, questions, or summaries

- editing text

If you use AI to prepare any written material, be sure to revise the output yourself and properly cite the work if you are ultimately submitting it as your own product.

Students should be aware of the potential limitations of using AI as a tool for learning and research. It is NOT helpful to your learning process to use AI to solve a homework problem for you[1]. Even if you study or review the AI solution, since you did not struggle through the thought processes yourself, your learning is undermined[2]. Futher, since AI outputs are probabilistic predictions, they are not always reliable or accurate[3]. Students should critically evaluate the sources, methods, and outputs of AI systems. Finally, it is in violation of Academic Integrity to present work that was produced by AI or other sources as your own.

If you have any questions about this policy or if you are unsure whether a particular use of AI is acceptable, please do not hesitate to ask for clarification.

[1] H. Bastani, O. Bastani, A. Sungu, H. Ge, Ö. Kabakcı, & R. Mariman. “Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics.” *Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci.* U.S.A. 122 (26). 2025.

[2]André Barcaui. “ChatGPT as a cognitive crutch: Evidence from a randomized controlled trial on knowledge retention.” *Social Sciences & Humanities Open,* Volume 12, 2025.

[3] Bender, Emily M., et al. "On the dangers of stochastic parrots: Can language models be too big?🦜." *Proceedings of the 2021 ACM conference on fairness, accountability, and transparency*. 2021.
