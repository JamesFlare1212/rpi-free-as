- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work (Use of tools of such as ChatGPT or similar are a violation. ChatGPT is effectively automated plagiarism and often yields incorrect equations, disastrously poor designs, garbage explanations and fictitious references). Intelligent web-based tools which can include AI, however, may be used to check spelling, grammar and word smithing on documents which have already been written by the student, but these tools cannot be used to adjust any engineering content. If these tools have been use, then the student MUST indicate which tools were used and how in the form of a citation which includes but is not limited to the LLM and the prompt input. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

AI generation & plagiarism can now be detected with high reliability. Assignments will be checked to determine the likelihood that the assignment was generated using AI tools. The instructor will ultimately decide whether misconduct has occurred. Papers assessed to be AI generated will receive a zero grade.  
Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy may result in a zero for that assignment, and will result in a penalty of an additional 10% of the final course grade. A second infraction will result in failure of the course. Each dishonesty instance will likely show on the student's academic record. Such records can be accessed by potential employers for job interviews.
