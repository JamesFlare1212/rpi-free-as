## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a Capstone class. There would be 3 presentations and 3 reports due for different stages of your innovation viz. Concept, EIS (Engineering Innovation for Society), and Final. Templates are provided. Emphasis would be on engineering content, and analysis and synthesis of information collected. Throughout the course students would learn and practice a few “Ideation & Innovation” (I&I) tools, as well as select “tools” from each of the 6 phases of “ Lean Design for Six Sigma Ideati on, Rapid Prototyping & Pilot Manufacturing Innovation” (Lean DFSS IRPPMI). Ideation Tools are listed as IT. Complete Exercises in Sketch Book mentioned in class. Complete Inventor’s notebook.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Course goals are:  
1\) Students learn and practice tools and processes to ***ideate, innovate, design and develop new and innovative*** products, technology etc. that have an impact on society.  
2\) Students learn and practice how to assess patentability of their invention, learn how to protect their Intellectual Property (IP), and practice writing a disclosure for a provisional patent.  
3\) Students learn a combination of tools and processes using 1) engineering design process, ***2) human centered design process 3) lean design for six sigma process, and Disney's imagineering process etc.,*** which enable them to get Lean Design for Six Sigma - innovation and product design and development certificate.  
4\) Students learn and practice ***Model Based Systems Engineering*** using Mathworks's Matlab, Simulink and Simscape modules.

## Course Calendar

### Calendar Details:

Weeks 1-2: Introduction, Syllabus, Grading Criteria, Review Inventor’s Studio 1 / IED tools and processes, Practice Ideation Tools, Leadership Styles, Overview of Patents, Copyright etc.  
Review 5 I&I tools. Lean DFSS IRPPMI process Define phase 10 questions, 100 questions. Start ideation for individual innovation. Overview of Additive Manufacturing. IT1: Day in the Life Of. IT2: I wish, IT3: Backward Imaging IT 4: Snowballing  
HW1: 1 problem definition and 1 solution. Overview of Human Centered Design process. Review of 5 Technologies of the  
future.  
Weeks 3-6: EIS 4 week blitz Team project, Global Challenges, etc. EIS Rubric, Engineering Ethics, etc. EIS  
Review additional 5 I&I tools. Practice Lean DFSS IRPPMI process  
Gather & Analyze phases Trade off Matrix (Pugh Matrix), Failure Modes & Effects Analysis (FMEA) & Quality Function Deployment (QFD). Continue developing individual innovation. Guest lectures. Define rapid prototyping steps for your innovation. Innovation Concept presentation and report  
submission. Overview of Team forming for Innovation Project teams Approval, Resource, Members & Informed (ARMI) and Goals, Resources, Process & Interdependencies (GRPI) tools . SWOT analysis Strength, Weakness, Opportunity, Threat analysis.  
Work on global challenges topic in a 4 week blitz. Work in diverse team to come up with an engineering solution to a complex  
global problem. Student teams learn to write and submit a technical paper for MANE Student journal. Also student teams  
present a poster to a diverse audience at the end of 4 weeks, along with a prototype of a system/sub system of their solution.  
This simulates a real life situation for engineering graduates.  
Discussion on Engineering ethics, and how ethics is considered in all phases of engineering solution development.  
Weeks 7-8: Individual Innovation, Concept Presentation & Report, Additional 6 I& I tools.  
Continue to build a MVP (Minimum Viable Product) of an innovation, either individually or in a group.  
Lectures on “How to write patents”. Overview of “Patents”, “Copyright” and “Trademarks” given, and students taught how to  
write patents.  
Lean DFSS IRPPMI process Design, Optimize and Validate phases, Multi generation product design ( MGPD) and versioning and upgrading methods learned, Customer Critical to Quality (CTQ), “Must Haves vs. Nice to Haves”. Finalize innovation design and rapid prototyping process. Overview of Dimensioning & Tolerancing. Design for Manufacturing & Assembly (DFMA), Design for Assembly (DFA) and Design for Manufacturing (DFM). IT7: 5/7 Whys. Six Sigma Overview Xbar / R chart, I / MR Chart, Process Capability, DPMO (Defects per million opportunity calculation, SIPOC etc.. Lean Overview 7  
types of waste, 5s, VSM,  
Weeks 9-13: Final Project Individual or Teams, Final presentation and report completed. Iterate/Refine/Ideate on group innovation project. Continue working on Lean DFSS IRPPMI process. Continue completing rapid prototyping Innovation system or sub system. Patent submission.  
Week 14: Final Project Presentation & Report, Provide Feedback to team members, etc.  
Final individual, or group innovation presentation and report.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
