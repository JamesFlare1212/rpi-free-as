## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides an introduction to the subject of structural vibrations. Topics include: free and forced linear vibrations of damped and undamped mechanical of n-degree of freedom; continuous system vibration; manual and computer methods of finding natural frequencies; self- and nonself-adjoint problems; eigenfunction expansion; integral transforms perturbation techniques; stability criteria. Vector algebra and calculus are used throughout in developing the theory and in problem solving. Required use will be made of *MATLAB/ Python.*

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course provides an introduction to the subject of vibrations. Topics include: free and forced linear vibrations of damped and undamped mechanical and electrical systems of n-degree of freedom; continuous system vibration; manual and computer methods of finding natural frequencies; self- and nonself-adjoint problems; eigenfunction expansion; integral transforms perturbation techniques; stability criteria. Vector algebra and calculus are used throughout in developing the theory and in problem solving. Required use will be made of *MATLAB/ Python.*

## Learning Outcomes

Students are expected to be able to:

- Formulate complex vibration situations,
- To linearize occasional nonlinear equations about a point of equilibrium,
- To test the system stability,
- To determine resonant frequencies and damping characteristics,
- To set up and to compute the overall short time and long-time solution,
- To determine approximations for those resonant frequencies.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Details of topics are as follows:

- ­ The conservative and nonconservative, homogeneous single degree of freedom oscillator with examples in mechanical systems.
- ­ The nonhomogeneous single degree of freedom oscillator solved by: (a) Method of undetermined coefficients, (b) Convolution.
- ­ The multi-degree of freedom Lagrange's equations of motion with damping, constraints, and generalized forces with selected examples.
- ­ Inertia and stiffness matrices via both the direct and analytical methods
- ­ Free vibration of n degree of freedom conservative systems (eigenvalues, eigenvectors principal coordinates, normalized principal coordinates, orthogonality, computer solutions, repeated eigenvalues).
- ­ Forced vibration of n degrees of freedom conservative systems (with the use of principal coordinates to obtain the net solution).
- ­ Vibration with damping (use of Rayleigh dissipation function and Hamilton's canonical form).
- ­ Proportional damping with n degrees of freedom.
- ­ Vibrations as with damping (use of Laplace transform technique).
- ­ Approximate and numerical techniques.
- ­ Distributed Vibration Systems.
- ­ Finite elements.
