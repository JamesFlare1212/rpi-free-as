## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This laboratory course offers a culminating experimental experience in the field of **thermal and fluids engineering** for undergraduate MANE students. The theoretical background gained in previous courses is now consolidated and expanded by hands-on experiments and analysis of thermal and fluids systems. This lab course is intended to familiarize students with a variety of thermodynamic, heat transfer, and fluid flow phenomena in the context of the operation of major pieces of equipment. Both fundamental concepts as well as practical/applied ideas will be explored.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

There are no required texts for this class. However, it will be beneficial to have at least one of the books below as an authoritative source to reference in reports and/or presentations.

- *A Heat Transfer Textbook* by Lienhard and Lienhard is available online for free and covers many of the topics discussed in the course.
- Other textbooks which cover the fundamentals of heat transfer, thermodynamics, fluid mechanics, and experimental methods:
  - *Fundamentals of Thermal-Fluid Sciences* by Çengel and Turner
  - *Brief Introduction to Fluid Mechanics* by Young, Munson, and Okiishi
  - *Fundamentals of Engineering Thermodynamics* by Moran and Shapiro
  - *Fundamentals of Heat and Mass Transfer* by Incropera and DeWitt
  - *Fluid Mechanics* by White
  - *Theory and Design for Mechanical Measurements* (2nd Ed., 1995, Wiley) by Figliola & Beasley

Materials specific to the experiments performed in this class will be provided through handouts and lecture notes posted on LMS. Extensive handouts have been prepared on the first three experiments. These handouts include an introduction to the project, theory, specific objectives, equipment description and operating instructions, and lab report requirements. Another handout has been prepared to provide information on uncertainties in experimental data. It should be studied as carefully as the ones on individual experiments.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1\. To demonstrate thermodynamic, heat transfer, and fluid flow principles;

2\. To integrate theory and practice through the operation of experiments and through the acquisition and analysis of experimental data;

3\. To familiarize students with complex, realistic systems and the interactions among thermodynamics, heat transfer, and fluid flow;

4\. To introduce some new concepts and techniques in the three topic areas;

5\. To develop abilities to understand, experiment with, and analyze thermal and fluids systems, including analysis and decision making in the face of experimental uncertainty;

6\. To exercise technical communication skills in a variety of modes.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Be able to integrate theory and practice through the operation of experiments and through the acquisition and use of experimental data with uncertainty
- Be able to perform experiments in the topic areas and report the results in a professional manner
- Become familiar with complex, realistic systems and the interactions among thermodynamics, heat transfer, and fluid flow

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students will work together in teams, and there are a total of four laboratory experiences. Three weeks will be spent on each laboratory experience.

Students start preparations a week ahead of a scheduled experiment by reviewing the theoretical background and studying the experimental setup and procedures. Students will be given a prelab assignment to complete, which will be due before the lab section start time on the day they perform the experiment.

Each laboratory experience consists of experimental investigations. Students will be given a homework assignment of analyzing the data from your experiments and preparing results. Students may collaborate with fellow group members, but will be required to submit their own work individually.

One week after conducting the experiment, groups will meet in a studio-style discussion with the section instructor to review results and talk about details of the lab. The "Results Homework" is due at the start of this discussion session, and students should come prepared to (individually) explain how they obtained the values submitted.

The rest of the discussion session time is available to begin writing an extensive laboratory report which will be due after another week, at 11:59 PM on the day of the assigned lab section. The laboratory reports will be similar to technical reports produced by practicing engineers. Reports will be graded as a group. Prelabs, homeworks, and reports will be evaluated by the lab section instructor.

Throughout the semester (see course schedule), there will be in-person quizzes on topics covered during lectures and in the laboratory. The primary intent of each quiz is to draw attention to details in the laboratory handouts, and ensure that students understood the underlying physical principles of the experiments. The quizzes will be in-person and closed-notes, held during the weekly lecture time. The quizzes will be timed and may contain a mix of multiple-choice and short answer problems.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

- Activities for labs 1-3: 50%
  - Prelab (individual, 15% of lab grade)
  - Homework (individual, 25% of lab grade)
  - Report (individual, 30% of lab grade)
  - Report (group, 30% of lab grade)
- Design of Experiments (DoE) project: 25%
  - Planning presentation (individual, 20% of project grade)
  - Final presentation (individual, 80% of project grade)
- Quizzes: 15%
- Peer assessment: 10%

The report will be graded principally on its technical content, but also on the quality of the writing, clarity, appearance, and format. Students will be provided with a grading rubric for the report and more report writing instructions during lecture. A style guide will be provided on the LMS. Each lab will have different requirements regarding expected sections, check the lab handouts for details.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is expected for the weekly lecture. Attendance is mandatory for the lab sessions. Attendance in the studio discussions is expected for all group members. You must attend the lab scheduled for your section and your group. Alternative arrangements should be extremely rare and only with the approval of your section instructor.

Absence from the lab session without an instructor-approved excuse will result in a grade of zero for the report. Absence from the discussion without an instructor-approved excuse will result in 30% reduction in the homework grade for that discussion. Please communicate with your section instructor if you need an accommodation.

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Rensselaer's policy on academic dishonesty is explained in the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities. You are expected to review and understand that information. In addition, the following policies apply specifically to this course.

- Committing, or attempting to commit an act of academic dishonesty, or assisting in the commission or attempt of such an act, is a flagrant offense to the process and culture of education; expect our reaction to it to be harsh.
- You are expected to refrain from any academically dishonest act, and to be vigilant in protecting your work from others who might attempt to commit an act of academic dishonesty.

In this course, academic dishonesty - cheating - can take the following forms.

- Submission of a lab report that, except for short phrases or properly referenced material, consists of any content not created by and original to the report authors listed. Plagiarism may be penalized according to the severity and deliberateness of the act, from reduction of report grade to failure of the course and referral to the Dean of Students for dismissal from Rensselaer.
- Taking the quizzes with any assistance from another person, regardless of who they are, or the medium of communication for assistance.
- Using any materials that are not explicitly permitted during the quizzes.
