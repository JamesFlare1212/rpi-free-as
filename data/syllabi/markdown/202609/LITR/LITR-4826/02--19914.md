## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Using a storytelling framework for learning, students engage with the history of the moving image. In a format not unlike a table-top roleplaying game, students create characters that were born sometime in the early 19th century and became vampires shortly after the invention of the photographic image. Individual vampires come together as small groups called covens, and then witness the evolution of motion pictures up to the present day. From the perspective of their vampire characters, students recount the story of their undead lives through culture and history as they encounter various films, broadcast TV shows, and streaming digital media--spanning 200 years. The semester culminates in a memorialization of the history of images and motion pictures, commemorating the experiential unlife of images as related to cultures and histories.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Overarching Goal of the 200 Year Old Vampires Game-As-Class: As much about historiography as history, this class encourages you to see that how you view any cultural artifact (in this case, images and motion pictures) is rooted in a historically-situated perspective.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Articulate and critically discuss the history of photographic imagery and motion pictures, tracing major artistic, industrial, cultural, and historical trends of that history.
- Individually and collaboratively develop fictional characters and groups, and generate stories using those characters that are rooted in historically-researched times and places.
- Produce in character artifacts and writing that analyze, reflects on, and engage with images and motion pictures throughout history, comparing and contrasting those analyses with their personal perspectives
- Collaboratively curate, as small groups and a whole class, a comprehensive overview of the history of photographic images and motion pictures.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

ADDITIVE ASSESSMENT:

Rather than an exercise in knowledge absorption, this course should feel like a process of exploration, articulation, reflection, and re-articulation through presentation. You’ll be carefully considering histories of places and cultures, technological developments, and of course images and motion pictures, but you’ll be reacting to them both as yourself but also imagining how your historically situated character might have. This work will be informed by readings and amateur research on history, culture, and technologies, and by interacting with the work of scholars and writers that have come before you. Just as importantly, you will be expected to work creatively as well as critically and reflectively. Assessment in this class will primarily be an attempt to measure your depth of engagement with all of these activities.

To that end, we will use an ‘additive assessment’ strategy where everyone starts with zero points and then accumulates points based on assignments and activities. You won’t receive points based on whether or not you agree with my perspective on the materials, or whether your answers are ‘right’ or ‘wrong’; you’ll receive points based on whether or not you’ve really thought it through, and whether or not you have genuinely engaged. In general, if you follow the guidelines for assignments and activities, and authentically work towards applying the concepts, you’ll get most or all of the points for assignments.

Note that there are baseline activities (repeated regularly), as well as required activities.

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

Paper Formatting

For academic papers, use academic formats and correctly cite references. As a standard, MLA format will be used. If you have a personal/professional reason for wanting to use a different format—APA, Chicago, etc.—I am perfectly open to that, as long as it’s a standard format. The Purdue University’s OWL website is an excellent resource for both MLA and APA formats and general academic writing practices: \<https://owl.english.purdue.edu/owl/>

HOWEVER…For many of your writings in this class—since they are intended to be period writings of vampires—you will be welcome to use non-traditional format, fonts, etc.. What I’d like you to do is try to ‘sneak in’ (or label in a header or something) the actual information that we need to keep track of your points for the class – i.e., give us your real name and the actual date somewhere, for example. The same goes with citing images or stills from motion pictures – maybe you can sneak that in at the end.

Use of Images in Assignments

In this class, of course you should include images on any assignments and papers. If you’re using stills from motion pictures or audio, in your references you should give a citation for the media text itself (not the website where you found it), and when possible give a minute/second notation (just as you would give page numbers for a book) so that someone can find the cited material in the original text.

Late assignments\*/\*\*

For most required assignments we need you to do them ahead of class so that we can ‘play the game’ of the class. If you do the regular assignments late, you may receive half the points (see above). For ‘artifacts’ there aren’t specific due dates (except the convocations), but rather you are highly encouraged to align when you turn in the activity with the ‘timeline’ that it should correspond to in game.

*NOTE: I am always open to discussing extenuating circumstances—but will be much more lenient if you communicate with me promptly and professionally about what those circumstances are/were.

\*\*NOTE: Vampiric Hypnosis (the Power to Erase My Memory)—I realize that we all have crazy and unpredictable lives, and that things come up. So…each of you can also imagine that your vampire used the power of hypnotism to make me forget that you didn’t turn in one required daily or minor assignment and/or have 1 absence…let me know when you’d like to use this ability during the semester.

Revisions of Assignments

If you feel that the points weren’t as high as you think you could have achieved on an assignment, we can make arrangements for a resubmission to improve the points attribution of that assignment. Note that this will NOT be possible for the final projects due to time constraints on grading, and if you turn things in late and then want to revise them they may only receive half points total…
