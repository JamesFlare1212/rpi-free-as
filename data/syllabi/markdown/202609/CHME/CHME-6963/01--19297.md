## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Upon completion of this seminar course, students will understand the milestones associated with the PhD program in Chemical and Biological Engineering at Rensselaer.
- Upon completion of this seminar course, students will understand expectations and strategies for successfully navigating the transition from undergraduate study to PhD study.
- Upon completion of this seminar course, students will know where to go for help when programmatic, research and personal challenges arise during the course of their PhD studies.
- Upon completion of this seminar course, students will understand ethical standards and expectations associated with PhD studies, research, and professional conduct.
- Upon completion of this seminar course, students will understand how to interact with scientific and technical literature.
- Upon completion of this seminar course, students will understand high-level strategies for successfully executing research and analyzing results.
- Upon completion of this seminar course, students will understand components of, and strategies for preparation of, compelling scientific and technical presentations and written documents.
- Upon completion of this seminar course, students will be able to begin constructing a personal model for themselves as independent researchers-in-training, informed by the experience and advice of more senior scientific and technical researchers.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students will complete occasional short assignments that may include reading materials to be discussed in class, writing a short summary on a research topic, giving a short presentation on a research topic, and completing programmatic processes/forms.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:
