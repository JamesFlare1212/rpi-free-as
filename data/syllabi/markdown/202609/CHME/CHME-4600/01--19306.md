### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

**Course Objectives:**

1\) Familiarize the student with major modern semiconductor manufacturing technologies and how they fit into the overall semiconductor industry. The student should be able to discuss these technologies in an industry technical employment interview and present themselves as familiar with the broad ideas behind them.

2\) Bring the perspective of transport, reaction, balances, data collection, and process control to bear on understanding and designing these technologies.

3\) Familiarize the student with job roles and career paths within the industry.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Familiarize the student with major modern semiconductor manufacturing technologies and how they fit into the overall semiconductor industry. The student should be able to discuss major modern semiconductor manufacturing technologies and how they fit into the overall semiconductor industry in the equivalent of a technical employment interview and present themselves as familiar with the broad ideas behind them.
- Bring the perspective of transport, reaction, balances, data collection, and process control to bear on understanding and designing these technologies.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

There are **three exams**, which cumulatively cover all the topics of the course. These exams can be considered to be Volume 1, 2, and 3 of a single comprehensive exam. **75% of your grade** is determined by the sum total of the points earned on all the questions contained in all volumes. My exams are exhausting to take and more exhausting to grade.

There will be **three quizzes**, for **10% of the grade, dropping the lowest quiz grade.** These are short, in-class quizzes midway through the three course “volumes”. Everything in a volume is in-scope for a quiz.

There is **one paper analysis presentation project for 15% of your grade**. You will be expected to choose a paper from a semiconductor industry archival journal, read and do all the research necessary to understand it in detail, and then present the contents of the paper to the class. Your choice of paper must be approved by me, and I can guide you in this process.

Note) Class attendance and participation are requirements for successful understanding of the course material. As such, no attendance requirement goes explicitly toward assessment, but implicitly without full attendance, you will not acquire the necessary knowledge to succeed on exams and the paper analysis listed above. Exams will not be rescheduled without either 10 days’ notice and a good explanation or a note from the dean’s office. Quizzes are not rescheduled.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
