## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is an unusual course in that it enrolls both undergraduate and graduate students from multiple disciplines. Nevertheless, the course is meant as an introduction to the principles of biochemical engineering. It is appropriate for students with little or no formal training in the life sciences and will focus on the engineering aspects of biotechnology. Such issues include enzyme technology, protein/enzyme engineering, metabolic engineering and synthetic biology, microbial and mammalian cell growth and product formation, bioreactor design and mass transfer, and biomanufacturing for various industries.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

On successful completion of the course, the student should be able to perform the following:

- Understand where biochemical engineering plays a key role in the broader field of biotechnology
- Understand basic concepts and computational tools in protein engineering, metabolic engineering, and synthetic biology
- Understand the use of fermentation and mammalian cell culture to create industrially relevant products
- Understand elements of typical bioprocessing workflows (e.g. upstream and downstream processing).
- Utilizing group discussion, peer review, and other active learning methodologies to enhance learning.
- Communicating technical information via oral and written forms of communication.
- Exemplify the knowledge acquired in this course and extrapolate its applications to translational challenges addressed by the field of Biochemical Engineering (6430 level only).

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

This course uses an active learning approach, where students are experientially involved in the learning process. Studies have shown that actively learning yields improved information retention and comprehension for nearly all students. Lectures in this course will incorporate in-class activities, group discussions, and peer teaching exercises. Additionally, each lecture will have a short quiz regarding the topics covered in the prior lecture. Attendance is critical and will be a component of your final course grade.

Excused absences will be granted on a case-by-case basis and can include reasons such as medical or family emergency, job interview, pre-scheduled participation in a sports game (only for varsity athletes). Excused absences should be requested from the instructor as far in advance as possible. If an excused absence is granted, you will be expected to review the missed lecture, which will be posted online for asynchronous learning. No make-up quizzes will be given and the in-lecture quiz will be waived for an excused absence.

### Other Course Policies:

**AI Use for Preparing Assignments:** AI platforms, such as ChatGPT and Claude, can be a useful tool for professional technical writing and idea generation. They can also be a demise to your learning and a significant detriment to your grades. AI can help you gather information, proofread your reports, improve your use of professional and concise language, and stimulate ideas. However, AI cannot think for you. AI does not know how to evaluate information supported by years of peer-reviewed science vs speculative pseudoscience with little support or logic. AI can sometimes hallucinate, creating fake references and information, so anything gathered from AI needs to be independently verified. AI cannot think on-the-spot for you in class, and you (not Claude) will bear the responsibility of knowing the course material.

While usage of AI tools is not strictly forbidden in this course, you must proceed with caution if you choose to use AI. Verify any information you obtain from AI with a trustworthy expert source (such as your instructor or a reputable textbook). You must append an AI Usage statement at the end of written assignments which describes how (if) you used AI in preparing the assignment.

**(Re)Grading Policy:** Grading decisions are the sole responsibility of the instructor. I do not grade capriciously, but grading mistakes are inevitable. If you wish to contest a grade, please provide specific reasons for contesting the grading in a clearly written explanation to me within two business days of receipt of your graded assignment. After I re-evaluate your grade, it is final and will not be further re-evaluated.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity – Plagiarism Policy:** Unless otherwise noted, all assignments are expected to be *individual, original* work. All reference materials (including web materials) used in preparation of assignments must be cited appropriately using footnotes and/or a bibliography. One instance of unacceptable collaboration or plagiarism will result in a reduction of the final course grade by one letter grade (e.g. B to C). A second instance of academic dishonesty will result in failure of the course.

Examples of plagiarism include:

- o Using someone else’s words, ideas, art, or other production as your own
- o Using someone else’s words, ideas, art, or other production in your production without crediting the source (regardless of whether compensation was exchanged)
- o Quoting someone else without putting the quote in quotation marks
- o Providing incorrect source information
- o Copying or quoting enough ideas or words from someone else so that it makes up the majority of your work, regardless of whether you credit the source or not
- o For more information, please see https://www.plagiarism.org/article/what-is-plagiarism

Plagiarism is taken very seriously in this course. Engaging in plagiarism not only robs others of their work, but also has permanent impact on your credibility as a scientist/engineer. Plagiarism can severely affect your academic performance and future career path. In short, it’s just not worth it. If you are uncertain if something qualifies as “plagiarism”, please ask ahead of the assignment’s due date.

**Academic Integrity – Quiz/Exam Policy:** No communication between students or use of written, typed and/or electronic information in any form is allowed during the quizzes/exams unless by explicit written consent from the instructor. Sending messages/emails/texts/etc. via mobile devices as well as accessing information online or stored on any electronic device (other than information explicitly allowed by the instructor) is not allowed during quizzes/exams and will result in a grade of zero on the quiz/exam for the first offense and failure of the class on the second offense. The only allowed use of mobile/cell/smart phones is to receive incoming text messages for purposes of campus safety. Finally, please ask the instructor for clarification if you have any questions concerning this policy before taking a quiz/exam.
