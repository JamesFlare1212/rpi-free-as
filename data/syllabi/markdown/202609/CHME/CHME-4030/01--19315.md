### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

The purpose of this course is to introduce undergraduate students to the theory and practice of chemical process modeling and control. The emphasis is on a model-based approach to control system design. The SIMULINK simulation package (based on MATLAB) is used for dynamic process simulation and control system development. Many of the homework assignments will require computer-based simulations.

Courses at Rensselaer are generally expected to require 2-3 hours of outside work for each credit hour. *You should expect, therefore, to put in roughly 8-12 hours/week outside the classroom.*

The course is usually taught in a *studio format*, combining lectures, discussions and computer-based simulations in a single laptop-based classroom. Please install MATLAB on your laptop computer before the first class period.

Students are expected to read the course material before each class and to watch the assigned screencasts from youtube (Bequette Process Control Education) and learncheme.com https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCgCo9hVaUrDILwBsla7_tLg/videos and http://www.learncheme.com/screencasts/process-controls before class. Also, there will often be on-line quizzes related to the screencasts and videos.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- From step response data, students should be able to develop empirical transfer function models to fit the data.
- For a low-order process transfer function, students should be able to perform a Routh stability analysis to find controller tuning parameter values that cause the closed-loop system to lose stability. For a proportional-only controller, students should be able to confirm that Routh stability analysis results are consistent with a Ziegler-Nichols closed-loop stability test.
- For a given transfer function, students should be able to design a PID controller using the IMC-based PID design procedure. Students should be able to relate the IMC filter factor (closed-loop time constant) and the PID controller parameters (controller gain, integral time, and derivative time).
- For a multivariable steady-state process gain matrix, students should be able to perform a relative gain array (RGA) calculation and recommend input-output pairings for control system design. Students should perform a failure sensitivity analysis to predict if the closed-loop system will remain stable if one of the control loops is “opened” or if one of the manipulated inputs (controller outputs) saturates.

**Homework Assignments**

Homework is due at the beginning of class. Late homework, including homework turned in during or after class will be accepted, with a penalty of 20% for each day late. Although an assignment may have several problems, often one will be selected at random for grading.

*Individuals may discuss the problems with others, but are not allowed to share solutions (M**ATLAB* *m-files, etc.). Violations will be handled in accordance with the student handbook.*

Before most classes there will be a brief on-line quiz that must be completed before class begins. These will often be multiple-choice, true-false, or short-answer questions related to the reading assignment (from the textbook and other sources).

## Course Calendar

### Calendar Details:

Week **Tentative** Material Covered:

1 Incentives for process control, fundamental models, introduction to MATLAB. Chapters 1 and 2. Module 1.

2 Fundamental models, state space models, transfer functions. Chapters 2, 3 and 4. Modules 2 and 4.

3 Dynamic behavior. Chapters 3 and 4.

4 Empirical models and digital filtering -- Chapter 5.

5 Introduction to feedback control. Routh stability – Chapter 6. Module 5.

6 Model-based Control. Direct Synthesis and Internal Model Control (IMC) - Chapter 7. Module 5.

7 IMC-based PID - Chapter 7. Modules 6 and 7.

8 PID controller tuning – Chapter 8. Digital PID. Module 5.

9 Cascade control. Chapter 10. Module 9.

10 Feedforward, PID enhancements. Chapter 11. Module 10.

11 Split-range, selective and over-ride strategies. Chapter 12. Module 11.

12 Control loop interaction, multivariable control. Chapters 13 and 14.

13 Multivariable control, plantwide control. Chapters 14 and 15.

14 Model predictive control (MPC). Statistical process control. Chapter 16

15 Optimization, linear programming
