### Prerequisites or Other Requirements:

Undergraduate level differential equations. Background in linear algebra and multivariable calculus is useful.

### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

The course is intended for chemical or other engineers and will focus on advanced mathematical tools and methodologies often used in chemical engineering research. The course is open to graduate students and, with permission, talented undergraduates.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

“Advanced Engineering Mathematics” by Michael D. Greenberg, Prentice Hall, 1998 (second edition).

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- describe the defining characteristics of a generalized vector
- describe the defining characteristics of a basis set and compare and contrast different choices of basis sets
- solve PDE’s using Separation of Variables and transform methods
- solve equations using both regular and singular perturbation techniques

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Homework, Quizzes, and three Term Exams

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Homework (15%), quizzes (10%), three term exams (25% each).

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

**Note about Showing Work:** In this class, the process of getting to an answer is as important (if not more) than the actual answer, and you need to be able to clearly communicate that process. Therefore, you *must show, explain, and justify* all of your work to receive credit. If you do not show your work, you will receive zero credit (even if the answer is correct). For example, a series of equations without explanation will not receive credit even if all equations are correct. If you are algebraically manipulating equations to simplify, you should state that. Unless specifically requested in the problem statement, it is not acceptable to use packages such as MATLAB, Maple, Mathematica, etc. to perform manipulations, integrals, etc.  
**Regrading Policy:** If you feel your work was not accurately graded, you must submit a regrade request *in writing within one week* of receiving the grade. You must cite the posted solutions in your written argument why you believe it was not accurately graded.
