## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

In addition to providing a comprehensive overview of the main theories, concepts, and subject areas within sociology, this course aims to provide you with an understanding of various aspects of medicine, health, and illness from a sociological standpoint. Throughout the duration of the course, sociological research will be utilized to emphasize the methods and perspectives employed in medical sociology. Recognizing that a person's health and life expectancy are more strongly influenced by their zip code than their genetic makeup, it becomes evident that health and illness cannot be comprehended solely by examining biological factors. It is essential to also consider a range of social, political, economic, and cultural factors that contribute to the production and interpretation of health and illness. Throughout the semester, we will explore the key subjects in this field, with a particular focus on understanding the following themes: the structural and cultural dimensions of health, health disparities, the medical profession, the experience of illness, and efforts to reform healthcare. An overarching theme that will be addressed throughout the semester is social inequality and its connection to health disparities, which are shaped by factors such as social class, race, gender, culture, and environment, ultimately impacting access to healthcare. We will also conduct cross-national comparisons of healthcare systems. Additionally, we will examine the historical and socio-political factors that shape the current healthcare delivery system in the United States, critically analyzing and questioning its framework. Lastly, we will explore the quality and cost of healthcare and discuss potential approaches to healthcare reform.

Although this course is accessible to students from all majors and has no prerequisites, it is designed to support undergraduate students who are interested in pursuing careers in medicine, public health, health sciences, and epidemiology. The Association of American Medical Colleges recognizes the significance of sociology in comprehending health, to the extent that 30% of the social and behavioral section of the MCAT now includes sociological content. With pre-medical students in mind, the course incorporates carefully selected readings, lectures, and assignments covering topics such as social relationships, cultures, institutions, stratification, inequalities, demographics, and social change.

This course is:

- *Introductory:* Students are not expected to have prior experience in sociology.
- *Comprehensive:* This course offers the full range of concepts, terms, and theories typically presented in a general introductory sociology course.
- *Targeted:* Most readings and discussions incorporate topics likely to be encountered by physicians and other health care professionals.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

In this course you will

1. Develop the skills necessary to read and evaluate social science research in an intelligent and critical manner.
2. Understand the role of theory in the construction of sociological inquiry.

3. Understand different theories about human culture, social identity, economic entities, political systems, and other forms of social organization.
4. Summarize sociological concepts and theories as they apply to medicine and healthcare.

1. Identify sociological research methods used to define and measure health outcomes.
2. Apply epidemiological principles and methods to health-related outcomes.
3. Describe how health and illness behavior are socially constructed.
4. Describe the impact of social and economic influences on health.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Weekly Reflections/Quizzes (20%):**

Frequency: 10x2pts Length: 150-200 words, handwritten, no laptops allowed during this time At the beginning of selected class sessions, students will complete a brief in-class reading reflection. These reflections are designed to support careful engagement with the assigned readings and to prepare students for discussion.

Each reflection should address:

1. One key argument or concept from the reading that stood out to you
2. One question, tension, or point of confusion you would like to discuss in class
3. (Occasionally) a brief connection between the reading and a contemporary health issue or lived experience

**Research Proposal (10%):**

**Format:** Group assignment (5-6 students per group) **Length:** 2 pages, double-spaced **Due:** Sept 18h **Grading:** Group grade (I will check in with each group to make sure every member of the group participated equally)

**Description** In this assignment, student groups will develop a short research proposal focused on a **health-related issue affecting *the RPI campus and its surrounding community***. The proposal should apply sociological concepts and research methods introduced in the course to a real and observable problem.

Possible topics include (but are not limited to):

- Mental health and stress
- Access to healthcare or counseling services
- Disability and accessibility
- Food insecurity
- Substance use
- Sexual health
- Care, stigma, or institutional support systems

**The proposal should include:**

1. **Research question** – What health issue are you studying, and why does it matter sociologically?
2. **Sociological framing** – How does this issue relate to concepts from the course (e.g., inequality, institutions, stigma, care, medicalization)?
3. **Methods** – What methods will you use (e.g., interviews, surveys, auto-ethnography, observation, media analysis)?
4. **Data sources** – Who or what will you study?
5. **Ethical considerations** – How will you protect participants’ privacy and well-being?

This proposal will serve as the foundation for the final poster project.

**Social Determinants of Health Infographic (10%):**

**Format:** Group assignment (same groups as proposal/poster) **Deliverable:** One-page visual infographic (PDF) **Due:** Nov 13th **Grading:** Group grade

**Description** For this assignment, student groups will create a **visual infographic** analyzing the **social determinants of health** related to the campus health issue they selected for their research project. The infographic will present the results of the literature review you conducted for your research project. Imagine you are a health provider or a public health expert and you are about to attend a meeting with different stakeholders to discuss an ongoing outbreak. Because the schedule is tight, you are only given 5 minutes to explain your case and you decide to use visual help that condenses all the points you wanted to make in a single representation. This assignment will allow you to apply the information and concepts you learn in the first half of the course.

The infographic should move beyond individual behavior and instead highlight how **social, institutional, economic, and environmental factors** shape health outcomes on campus. Groups should use course concepts to explain *why* the problem exists and *who* is most affected.

**Final Poster Presentation (15%):**

**Format:** Group poster + in-class poster session **Due:** Week 15th **Grading:** Group grade ***(group members will grade each other based on their contributions to the project)***

**Description** For the final project, groups will conduct a small-scale sociological study based on their approved research proposal and present their findings in the form of a **research poster**.

Groups are expected to **collect original data** using one or more methods discussed in class, such as:

- Interviews
- Surveys
- Participant observation
- Media or document analysis

Students may also draw on **their own experiences**, provided these are analyzed sociologically and situated within broader institutional or social contexts.

**Poster components should include:**

- Research question and background
- Methods
- Key findings
- Sociological analysis
- Implications for campus health, policy, or care

Posters will be presented during an in-class poster session, where students will engage with one another’s work and discuss their findings.

**Final Exam (20%):**

This will be a multiple choice, cumulative exam, covering not only the lectures but also the readings assigned throughout the course.

**Attendance and active participation (10%):**

In a discussion-based course, student attendance and active participation is required and crucial to creating a successful learning community. This means coming to class ready to discuss the assigned material with observations, questions, and comments, as well as participating fully in any small and large group activities. Our class time is specially designed to prepare you for successful completion of the other graded components of the course. Thus, your full engagement and participation during our class time will directly and positively influence your grade more broadly. I expect each of you to come to class having finished all the readings and assignments for the day. I acknowledge some of you may be uncomfortable in large group discussions, and so I recognize engaged listening and note taking as valid forms of participation. Still, consider preparing one specific point about the readings to address in class each day, or challenge yourself to speak up in class once a week in some way.

During class discussion, you should be intentionally aware of your participation, if you find you are speaking repeatedly, step back and allow others the chance to speak up; conversely, if you notice that you are not speaking during class discussions, find ways to contribute to the conversation e.g. draw upon your notes for questions or previously formed thoughts.

Unless you miss more than 3 classes without a valid medical or similar excuse, you will receive full grade for attendance (5 pts). If you miss more than 3 classes, I will meet with you at the end of the semester to discuss your attendance grade.

**Reading Facilitation (10%):**

**Frequency:** Once per group during the semester **Length:** 15–20 minutes

**Description** Each group will be responsible for **facilitating discussion for one assigned reading** during the semester. The goal of this activity is not to lecture or summarize the text, but to help the class think sociologically about the reading’s key arguments, concepts, and implications.

Groups should approach facilitation as **conversation-building rather than presentation**.

**Facilitation should include:**

1. **Brief reflections** on the reading (What stood out? What was challenging, surprising, or troubling?)
2. **Key ideas or concepts** the group thinks are most important for understanding the text
3. **2–3 discussion questions** that invite interpretation, critique, or application
   - o Questions should be open-ended and sociological (not factual recall)
4. **Optional activity** (e.g., small-group discussion, polling question, short writing prompt, case example)

Groups are encouraged to connect the reading to:

- Contemporary health issues
- Campus life or everyday experiences
- Themes that recur across the course

**What this is NOT**

- A formal presentation
- A summary of every section of the reading
- A test of whether classmates “did the reading”

If you are not able to meet during my office hours or physically in my office, please talk with me about making other arrangements. I will happily do all that I can to accommodate. If for any reason I have to reschedule my office hours, I will do my best to announce changes in advance.

**Technology:** While we are all responsible for showing up to class fully engaged, participation will look different for each of us. You may use personal technology at your discretion in the ways that work best for your learning. This is subject to change should technology begin to cause distraction to the instructor and/or your peers. Please, be comfortable, but respect that the classroom is a space for communal learning.

**Access & Accommodation:** We all learn in different ways, and we all work to enable our own learning process under various circumstances that may be beyond our control. If for any reason you become concerned about your ability to fully participate in our course due to the structure of the activities or assignments, please talk to me immediately. I am personally committed to providing all students equal access to learning opportunities in my courses. If you are registered with the Disability Resource Center, please notify me at the beginning of the semester and we will set up a meeting to discuss what is needed to ensure your success in the class. If you are not (yet) registered with Disability Services, but know that your learning style requires specific attention, please talk with me as soon as possible regarding your experience. If you are a non-native English speaker/writer and need extra assistance or accommodation with the reading and writing assignments please talk with me as soon as possible. If you are a parent, do not let struggles with childcare stop you from coming to class! Your child is welcome in the classroom as needed. Similarly, if you are working one or more full-time jobs and struggling with course deadlines, be in communication with me as soon as possible so we may find a way to keep you engaged in the class.

Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute strives to make all learning experiences as accessible as possible. If you anticipate or experience academic barriers based on a disability, please let me know immediately so that we can discuss your options. To establish reasonable accommodations, please register with The Office of Disability Services for Students. After registration, make arrangements with the Director of Disability Services as soon as possible to discuss your accommodations so that they may be implemented in a timely fashion. DSS contact information: dss@rpi.edu ; +1-518-276-8197; 4226 Academy Hall.

**Attendance:** Students are allowed two excused absences, no questions asked. Following University policy, any documented absence due to University sanctioned events, family emergencies, religious holidays, or illness will be excused. To have an absence excused for any of these reasons, simply email me prior to our class meetings. Staying in communication with me is key. Beyond excused absences, I will subtract 5 pts per additional absence out of your attendance grade. Students will be marked absent if they do not sign the sign in sheet.

If you miss a class, you are responsible for making up the material and should contact a classmate for discussion notes and class announcements. If absent, I encourage you to spend extra time on the class Moodle Forums for days that you are gone. As an access measure, I will post my class PPT each day after class so that you may see our class work. You are welcome to do in-class activities that you may have missed and turn that work in when you return for participation credit. I cannot go over class material via email, but I will happily answer questions and go over assignments and notes during my office hours.

**Use of AI and ChatGPT:** You can use artificial intelligence and language l models to (1) brainstorm your ideas and (2) get feedback on your writing. Don’t forget that ChatGPT is a language model, not a factual model. You *cannot* use such services to (1) fact check; (2) write a paper; (3) or replace critical thinking. Your instructors are experienced faculty, and they will notice when/if you submit an assignment created by AI. This will automatically result in 0 points for the assignment, and you will fail the course should you repeat the same mistake. If you have found other creative ways to use AI to assist with your learning, feel free to come to my office hours to discuss it with me.

**Communication:** I will be easily accessible for one-on-one meetings if you need any help. I expect you to check your University email for class updates and be responsible for the information provided to you. You may use email to ask for clarification or instructions on how to complete an assignment, but please check the syllabus and LMS first for answers to your questions. When sending an email, please be sure to include the course number, your name, and the topic of the email in the subject line. The body of your email should contain specific questions and information as well as professional communication etiquette. Out of respect for your instructor’s time and effort outside of class, allow up to 48 hours for email responses—**please no urgent emails.**
