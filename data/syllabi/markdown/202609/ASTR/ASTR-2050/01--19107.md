## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will enable students to gather information about the science of astronomy and its mathematical and physical underpinnings. After taking this class, students will be able to use basic astronomical jargon in context; perform simple physical calculations of orbital motion, the structure of stars, and the expansion of the Universe; and will be able to describe the evolution of the cosmos.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Textbook: Foundations of Astrophysics, by Ryden and Peterson.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course will enable students to get understanding of Kepler’s Laws; properties of stars, galaxies and the solar system, cosmology. The students will be able to gather information about the science of astronomy and its mathematical and physical underpinnings. After taking this class, students will be able to use basic astronomical jargon in context; perform simple physical calculations of orbital motion, the structure of stars, and the expansion of the Universe; and will be able to describe the evolution of the cosmos.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The topics that will be taught in the course are Kepler’s Laws, components of the Solar System, Electromagnetic spectrum and its importance to the study of astronomy,  
stellar properties such as magnitude, distance, color, temperature, and the Hertzprung-Russell Diagram; stellar evolution as a function of initial stellar mass. Types of stars, galaxies, and compact objects (e.g. - pulsars, black holes, and AGN). Formation and evolution of the Universe.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- (1) Students will be able to use Kepler’s Laws to calculate the properties of planetary and binary star orbits. (2) Students will be able to describe the components of the Solar System. (3) Students will be able to explain the electromagnetic spectrum, and its importance to the study of astronomy. (4) Students will be able to describe and to calculate the relationships between magnitude, distance, color, temperature, and the Hertzprung-Russell Diagram. (5) Students will be able to describe the physical processes that govern stellar evolution as a function of initial stellar mass. (6) Students will be able to list the types of stars, galaxies, and compact objects (e.g. - pulsars, black holes, and AGN) that have been classified, and describe their characteristics. (7) Students will be able to describe the formation and evolution of the Universe.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Exams will be held in the usual classroom during normal class hours (see the attached schedule for dates). A review for each exam will be held during the preceding class. You may take these exams at a different time ONLY with a prior notice of absence or an official excuse.

Activities are graded for correctness and count toward the overall grade. Attendance on days with in-class activities is **required** to complete the activity. If you are unable to attend the lecture you will be able to make up the activity *only if you provide notice of an excused absence or discuss it with the professor beforehand*. Unexcused absences from these assignments will be reflected in the student’s grade.

Classes will include power-point presentations, videos, examples to work through, and interactive simulations for activities. Powerpoint slides will be available for download in LMS. Attendance and participation on days when activities and exams are scheduled **is required**. While attendance is not an absolute requirement on other days, it is *stronger encouraged* as active engagement with the material historically correlates with overall class performance. Those students that do not attend lecture are expected to independently review the powerpoint lectures and come to office hours if they have questions. It is the student’s responsibility to reach out to the professor or TAs to ask questions regarding material covered in class and/or attend appropriate office hours. General knowledge and topical items discussed in class will not necessarily be included in the materials available for download.

### Other Course Policies:

Homework: Students are not only allowed, but are encouraged to converse about the homework. However, **all assignments which are turned in are expected to be the individual work of the student who turns it in.** For example, all the work on the assignment must follow from the preceding data or equations, and should not require consulting another student’s methodology to understand how the solutions were derived. Any submitted Homework from a student that shows only the answers will not be considered individual work and receive a zero grade. Scanned homework will be turned in through Gradescope. Weekly homework assignments will be posted in LMS. All homework must be clear and legible. The grader will not puzzle over assignments that are poorly presented or illegible, this includes presenting answers that do not show clear work of derivation or argument of how the result was obtained.  
Re-Grading: If you feel that you have received a grade in error, the TA is the point of contact for homework and in-class activities. The instructor is the point of contact for Exam re-grades or unresolved disagreements with the TA. Requests for re-grades must include a written explanation of what was graded incorrectly. The instructor and TA reserve the right to re-grade an entire submitted assignment.

Any interaction with a wireless device (cell phone, iPad, laptop, etc.) during an exam (except when explicitly indicated otherwise by the instructor) will be considered an illicit data exchange and result in a zero for the entire exam.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

When you place your name on a submitted piece of work, you are claiming that you are the original author of that work, excepting appropriately cited material. If you turn in work that is not your own, you are stealing credit for that work from the original author. Additionally, you are ignoring the steps that the instructor feels are necessary for you to understand the material, and interfering with a fair assessment of your abilities.

**Please always produce honest, original work.** If you turn in dishonest work, you are implicitly undermining your education and the system of fairness that exists.program as your own. It is considered cheating to help another student cheat.

Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a zero on that assignment, and subsequent violations may result in an F grade in the course.

Examples of cheating include, but are not limited to:

- Copying, or allowing another student to copy, an assignment or exam
- Altering a graded test or assignment before requesting a regrade
- Copying solutions from published solution manuals
- Using materials other than those that are allowed on an exam
- Misrepresenting the work of other students or groups as your own
- Submitting work generated by an AI program as your own original work

If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

**Statement on the use of AI and Academic Integrity**

You may use AI programs e.g., ChatGPT to help generate ideas and brainstorm. However, you should note that the material generated by these programs may be inaccurate, incomplete, or otherwise problematic. Don’t trust anything it says. If it gives you a number or fact, assume it is wrong unless you either know the answer or can check in with another source. You will be responsible for any errors or omissions provided by the tool. It works best for topics you understand.  
Beware that use of AI tools may also stifle your own independent thinking and creativity. You may not submit any work generated by an AI program as your own. It would be considered automated plagiarism. AI is a tool, but one that you need to acknowledge using. If you include material generated by an AI program, please include a paragraph at the end of any assignment that uses AI explaining what you used the AI for and what prompts you used to get the results. Failure to do so is in violation of the academic honesty policies. Be thoughtful about when this tool is useful. Don’t use it if it isn’t appropriate for the case or circumstance.
