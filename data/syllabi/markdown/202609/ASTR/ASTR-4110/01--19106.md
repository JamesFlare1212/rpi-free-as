## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Cosmology is the study of the Universe as a whole. In this course we will try to answer the following questions (among others): What are the contents of the Universe? How has the history of the Universe looked until now? How will the Universe evolve from here? How come one location in the Universe is not more special than another? And how come one direction is not more special than another?

## Course Content

### Content Details:

By the end of the course, students will have an elementary understanding of the hot big bang cosmology and the inflationary paradigm.

(1) Students will be able to derive the Friedman equations.

(2) Students will be able to explain how the contents of the universe influence its overall dynamics..

(3) Students will be able to use methods of statistical mechanics to analyze equilibrium processes such as neutrino decoupling in the early universe.

(4) Students will be able to explain the concept of the WIMP miracle.

(5) Students will be able to explain the formation process of the light element during Big Bang Nucleosynthesis.

(6) Students will be able to identify and explain the problems with standard hot big band cosmology.

(7) Students will be able to describe how cosmic inflation solves the problems with standard hot big band cosmology.

(8) Students will be able to explain concepts related to the theory of reheating.

(10) Students will be able to quantitatively analyze the cosmological perturbations that lead to structure formation.

(11) Students will be able to explain how physical processes and quantities determine the shape of the CMB power spectrum.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Learning will be assessed using a combination of homework assignments, in-class participation, and exams. Students at the 6000-level will also be assessed based on a final paper.

*Homework:* There will be homework assignments based on the content seen in class. The homework assignments will be due at the beginning of class on the due date. Homework solutions are to be submitted on paper. In general, late homework will not be accepted.

The homework assignments are crucial in mastering the course material.

***Exams****:* There will be 2 midterm exams.

***Project Assignment (****For the ASTR/PHYS 4110 only):* Students will submit an essay of a topic of their interest related to a topic of this course and that is current research in the field. The essay can have the form of an article if desired. The total length should be 8 pages long. It should contain enough material that can show the student has done an in-depth search on the topic, establishing clearly and with their own words what the topic is about, why it is new, etc.; it should show the students’ understanding of the concepts by explaining clearly the topic and relevance, include a discussion on what the status of the topic is, include figures or diagrams if necessary, and their conclusions and references. The paper can be typeset in Word or LaTex using a full page (8.5”x11”) physics or astronomy journal style, it should include references and must be formatted as required in the selected journal. Note that while AI is allowed for interpreting papers generating ideas, and proofreading, it is forbidden to use it to write any portion of the essay. In particular, it is not allowed to ask an AI to suggest text which is then adapted into the paper. I want to see your own way of thinking.

***Final project (ASTR 6110 and PHYS 6110 only)****:* Students will submit a topic proposal that includes a list of relevant peer-reviewed literature on the subject on which the project will be based (10%). The students will use those resources (and any others that are identified later) to write a review paper on the subject. The paper should be 7000-8000 words and typeset in LaTex using a full page (8.5”x11”) physics or astronomy journal style (70%). References must be formatted as required in the selected journal. Figures should be self-made and could combine data from multiple papers. Note that while AI is allowed for interpreting papers generating ideas, and proofreading, it is forbidden to use it to write any portion of the paper. In particular, it is not allowed to ask an AI to suggest text which is then adapted into the paper. Students will give a short presentation of their topics to the class and answer questions about their paper as an oral exam (20%). The Final project will account for 15% of the total final grade, as shown in the Grading criteria.

**Statement on the use of AI and Academic Integrity**

You may use AI programs e.g., ChatGPT to help generate ideas and brainstorm. However, you should note that the material generated by these programs may be inaccurate, incomplete, or otherwise problematic. Don’t trust anything it says. If it gives you a number or fact, assume it is wrong unless you either know the answer or can check in with another source. You will be responsible for any errors or omissions provided by the tool. It works best for topics you understand. Beware that use of AI tools may also stifle your own independent thinking and creativity. You may not submit any work generated by an AI program as your own. It would be considered automated plagiarism.

AI is a tool, but one that you need to acknowledge using. If you include material generated by an AI program, please include a paragraph at the end of any assignment that uses AI explaining what you used the AI for and what prompts you used to get the results. Failure to do so is in violation of the academic honesty policies. Be thoughtful about when this tool is useful. Don’t use it if it isn’t appropriate for the case or circumstance.

For students that required to do the final project, read the additional terms for the use of AI mentioned in the previous section of the syllabus.

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own.
