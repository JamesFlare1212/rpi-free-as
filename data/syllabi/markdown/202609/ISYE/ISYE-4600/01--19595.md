## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is an introduction to deterministic mathematical modeling for common decision problems in industrial and management engineering including supply chain management, production planning, and public policy. Students will learn to formulate and solve linear and integer programs, evaluate robustness of solutions via sensitivity analysis, and apply duality and other fundamental techniques in deterministic optimization.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Student Learning Outcomes:  
By the end of the course you should be able to:  
1\. Solve linear and integer programs in the AMPL mathematical optimization soft-  
ware.  
2\. Formulate mathematical models from written/spoken language descriptions of a  
problem.  
3\. Solve and evaluate quality/robustness of solutions via simplex, sensitivity analysis,  
and duality.  
4\. Understand, model, and apply other deterministic optimization models outside of  
LP and IP.  
5\. Communicate solutions in terms of the problem description such that it is imple-  
mentable by a non-engineer/non-mathematician.  
6\. 6000-level students: demonstrate an ability to incorporate other concepts to en-  
hance the applicability of the material covered (see section on Grading Criteria for  
an example).

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

I care that every student has the opportunity to learn. To do well in this class, you should capitalize on that opportunity – participate during class time, re-watch videos, complete practice problems, review notes, attend office hours and ask questions. Whether you seize that opportunity is entirely up to you. As such, I do not take attendance. If life ever conflicts with this schedule, then I encourage you to contact me.

My primary requirement is that you you shall not do anything to remove the learning opportunity from other students, e.g., snoring/drooling while sleeping, talking on the phone, consuming loud/smelly food (in person) etc.

### Other Course Policies:

Notes on Exams:

The exams and quizzes are open notes, but closed book. Why closed book? Because many students have a pdf of the book and not a physical copy, but interaction with computer/tablet is prohibited, and I don’t want students to “have to” print whole book chapters. I encourage each student to make their own neat sheet(s) instead relying on the book—this will actually help you to learn the material. My intention is not to limit the information you have available; my intention is to normalize the information between students with physical/electronic copies of the book.

In general, it is good practice to prepare a concise note sheet containing the information you need to succeed even if your class does not allow notes on the exam. If you are able to identify would you need to be successful, then you are probably fairly well prepared for the exam. I would be more than happy to go over note preparation with individual students prior to each exam.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Submission of any assignment in violation of this policy may result in the following:

• a 0 on the assignment/exam if you notify me of the issue.  
• an F in the course if you do not come forward on your own regarding the issue.

What Constitutes an Academic Violation for This Class:

For exams, outside collaboration or any communication with any other student during the exam is forbidden.

For assignments, I freely allow, and encourage, you to collaborate with others so long as you provide proper citation. I’m not worried about proper MLA/Chicago style as long as you provide proper credit. Examples:

– Acceptable: You work with Phyllis from class on problem 3 and, for problem 3, you write “Phyllis [last name] and I collaborated on this problem”.

– Acceptable: You copy solutions from Chegg and write, “these solutions come from [insert url]”. Note: I do not recommend this because (1) it doesn’t help you learn and (2) many modeling solutions on Chegg are sadly incorrect.

– Unacceptable: You use part of Phyllis’s code or solution without giving her credit.

In general, be professional (and ethically safe) and provide credit to anyone that contributes to your work. Please do not photocopy someone’s assignment and write “I photocopied Phyllis’s assignment” (someone has done this in the past and, no, they were not reported for academic misconduct). This policy should not deter you from working with others or seeking outside help. While I have caught quite a few instances on academic dishonesty on exams in the past, I rarely have issues for assignments. Please help maintain this trend.
