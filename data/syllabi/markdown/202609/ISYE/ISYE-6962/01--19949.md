## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides students with the modeling skills, methodological background, and computational  
tools required to solve large-scale optimization problems in supply chain management, finance,  
statistics, and other engineering and business domains. Students will learn to design, implement, and  
evaluate classical algorithms alongside state-of-the-art optimization methods using real-world data and  
industry-relevant problem settings. Students will also examine the emerging landscape of quantum  
optimization, comparing quantum and hybrid quantum-classical approaches with traditional methods.  
The course integrates modern Artificial Intelligence (AI) and large language model (LLM) tools to  
accelerate model development, algorithm implementation, and analytical workflows.

## Course Text(s)

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Learning Goals  
By the end of the course, students should be able to:  
• formulate large-scale optimization problems and identify exploitable mathematical structure;  
• implement and evaluate decomposition, relaxation, and diagram-based optimization methods;  
• explain the basic computational principles behind quantum optimization approaches;  
• compare classical, quantum, and hybrid quantum-classical approaches for optimization;  
• use AI/LLM tools responsibly to support modeling, coding, experimentation, and communication;  
• communicate computational results clearly in written and oral form.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The final grade in the course is based on homework assignments.  
• There will be approximately 5 computational assignments. There might also be some reading,  
conceptual, or theory components.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

• Each assignment grade has two components:  
– Written report (50%): solution methodology, explanation of datasets, experimental conclusions,  
and future work.  
– Presentation (50%): presentation of the homework solution to the class. Discussion of the  
different approaches used by each group is expected. Students should demonstrate that they  
understand their solution methodology.  
• Students will work in groups of approximately 3 people.  
• Use of LLMs is permitted and encouraged. However, students must show that they know what  
they are doing and must follow the AI-use policy below.
