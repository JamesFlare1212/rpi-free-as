## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course explores modern statistical and machine learning approaches for quality control and process monitoring in industrial and systems engineering. The 4000-level component emphasizes fundamental statistical quality control methods, including the construction and interpretation of various control charts (e.g., Shewhart, CUSUM, EWMA), graphical methods, process capability indices, acceptance sampling, and experimental design principles such as Taguchi methods.

The 6000-level component extends these concepts by integrating advanced machine learning techniques for data-driven quality improvement. Topics include active machine learning for real-time monitoring, data cleaning techniques for quality assurance, unsupervised learning for anomaly detection, and supervised learning for predictive quality analytics. Students will explore the intersection of statistical methods and modern computational techniques to enhance decision-making in complex manufacturing and service systems.

Students in the 6000-level course will complete additional coursework focusing on theoretical foundations, algorithmic implementation, and case studies involving large-scale, high-dimensional quality control datasets. Prerequisites include a working knowledge of probability, statistics, linear algebra, and algorithms.

Both sections share the same lectures. The course keeps a classical statistical quality control backbone; machine learning and AI topics enter at the natural quality-control junctures where they change practice, and every such topic is evaluated against a quality-control task (monitoring, capability, improvement, inspection).

## Course Goals

### Goals:

By the end of the course, students in the 4000-level course will be able to:

- **Understand Fundamental Quality Control Methods** - Learn statistical quality control techniques, including control charts (Shewhart, CUSUM, EWMA), process capability indices, and acceptance sampling.
- **Apply Graphical and Statistical Tools** - Use graphical methods and statistical process control tools to monitor and improve manufacturing quality.
- **Evaluate Process Performance** - Analyze process capability and determine tolerance limits for quality assurance.
- **Implement Experimental Design for Quality Improvement** - Understand Taguchi methods and other design of experiments techniques to enhance product and process quality.
- **Interpret Quality Control Results** - Develop the ability to assess and communicate quality control findings for decision-making in industrial and systems engineering.

In addition to the 4000-level objectives, students in the 6000-level course will also:

- **Integrate Machine Learning with Statistical Quality Control** - Extend traditional quality control techniques with modern computational tools to improve decision-making.
- **Implement Advanced Machine Learning Techniques** - Utilize supervised and unsupervised learning for predictive analytics, anomaly detection, and data-driven quality control.
- **Develop Data Cleaning and Preprocessing Skills** - Learn methods to detect and correct corrupt or inaccurate records in industrial datasets.
- **Analyze Complex and High-Dimensional Data** - Use information theory, active learning, and sequential design techniques for intelligent quality monitoring.
- **Apply Physics-Driven and Data-Driven Models** - Explore hybrid approaches that incorporate domain knowledge into machine learning models.
- **Work on Research-Oriented Projects** - Conduct in-depth case studies applying advanced ML methods to real-world quality control problems.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is necessary for success in this course, which will take 5% of the end-of-semester grade. There are three instances of no-excuse absences (not on the two exam dates) granted for each student. It is mainly designed to accommodate those emergent personal issues that do not leave enough time to receive official approval. Beyond the no excused absences, missing classes are only allowed if there is an emergency verified by the Dean of Student's office. Any absence beyond the three times of no excused absences and without an official excuse will cause a 0.5% penalty on the end-of-semester grade.

### Other Course Policies:

- Late homework submission will not be accepted. However, an extension of the deadline may be allowed if the extension request (should be made at least 2 business days before the due date) is approved by the instructor.
- Requests for make-up of exams will NOT be granted unless the student has a valid medical or instructor-approved excuse. The request should be sent and approved at least 2 business days before the exam day.
- Document in writing which part you want re-graded and why you are advocating for a regrade. Turn in this document with your original assignment (wangy88@rpi.edu). You will be given one chance (per assignment/exam) to request a re-grade (except for the final exam). No re-grade will be accepted more than one week after an exam or an assignment has been returned to the student. Re-grades made after this period have elapsed will NOT be considered.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

- Collaboration on the course homework and project is encouraged. However, the student must complete the homework and project independently. AI assistants are permitted for coding help, concept explanation and checking work, and are not permitted for wholesale solution generation; any AI assistance and any human help must be noted on the assignment.
- No discussion or collaboration is allowed for the midterm and final exams. These are closed-book, AI-free assessments; no AI tool of any kind may be used.

**Penalty in violation of academic integrity:**

Violations of academic integrity, including plagiarism, cheating, unauthorized collaboration, fabrication of data, submission of unverified AI-generated work as one's own, or any form of dishonest conduct, will result in strict disciplinary actions in accordance with RPI's Academic Integrity Policy.

**First Violation**

- A zero on the assignment or exam in question.
- A possible reduction in the final course grade.
- Required academic integrity remediation is required, such as attending an ethics seminar.

**Second Violation**

- Automatic failure (F) in the course.
- A formal academic integrity report filed with the Institute.
- Referral to the Academic Integrity Board, which may impose further disciplinary actions, including probation, suspension, or expulsion.

Students are responsible for understanding and adhering to RPI's Academic Integrity Policy. If unsure about permissible collaboration or citation practices, students should seek clarification from the instructor before submitting work.
