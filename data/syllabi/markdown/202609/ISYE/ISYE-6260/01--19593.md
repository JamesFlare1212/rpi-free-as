## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides a comprehensive exploration of human performance, focusing on how sensory and motor functions interact within various system designs. Students will delve into the principles of human-machine interfaces, examining how visual and auditory displays, control devices, tools, and workspaces can be optimized to enhance performance. The course emphasizes the critical influence of cognition, perception, human error, fatigue, and sleep on decision-making and task execution, offering insights into how these factors impact overall system safety and user satisfaction.  
Building on this foundational knowledge, students will learn to evaluate human performance through the use of advanced sensory devices and physiological response measurements. The course introduces wearable sensors e.g., eye-tracking systems, which provide valuable data on stress levels, attention, and cognitive load during task performance. These physiological metrics are integrated into models that help predict human responses in complex tasks, thereby improving situational awareness and system design.

The course also covers statistical techniques like ANOVA, MIXED-ANOVA, RM-ANOVA, and Mixed-effect Linear Regression models, alongside machine learning applications on large physiological datasets. Through these methods, students will predict performance outcomes, identify patterns of fatigue or cognitive overload, and develop strategies to optimize system performance and anticipate human error.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The goal of this course is to equip students with the knowledge and skills to effectively model, evaluate, and analyze human performance in complex systems. By integrating principles of human-machine interaction with advanced statistical techniques and machine learning, students will learn to predict performance outcomes, mitigate human error, and design systems that enhance safety, efficiency, and user satisfaction.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

- Sensory and Motor Functions
