## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This graduate-level course examines emerging technologies and their applications in transportaiton, with an emphasis on understanding transportation problems from a systems perspective. Through contemporary applications, real-world case studies, class discussions, and project-based learning, students will explore how advances in sensing, connectivity, automation, electrification, artificial intelligence, communication, and computing are reshaping transportation systems and services.

Rather than focusing primarily on the technical details of individual technologies, the course emphasizes the ability to identify and frame transportation problems, understand interactions among technological and non-technological system components, evaluate existing solutions, identify and learn appropriate tools, and critically assess proposed solutions and their limitations. Students are encouraged to actively explore emerging tools, including AI, machine learning, large language models, intelligent agents, simulation, and other computational technologies, and to evaluate when, why, and how such tools are appropriate for a particular transportation problem.

The course also examines the broader implications of ITS deployment, including interactions among travelers, vehicles, infrastructure, service providers, public agencies, and communities, as well as issues related to implementation, reliability, safety, privacy, equity, public acceptance, and unintended system impacts.

Major application areas include connected and autonomous vehicles, electric vehicles, advanced parking management, bike-sharing, car-sharing, ride-sourcing, air mobility, digital twin cities, and other emerging intelligent mobility systems.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The goals of this course are to:

1. Introduce students to emerging Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS) technologies and applications.
2. Develop students’ ability to examine transportation problems from a systems perspective, considering interactions among technologies, infrastructure, travelers, operators, institutions, and society.
3. Foster active learning and critical thinking by encouraging students to independently identify transportation problems, explore appropriate knowledge and tools, evaluate existing and proposed ITS solutions, and question their assumptions, limitations, and potential unintended impacts.
4. Prepare students to make thoughtful use of emerging technologies and tools, including machine learning, simulation, and other computational resources, to support transportation problem solving while maintaining independent professional judgment.
5. Provide opportunities for students to communicate, discuss, critique, and refine ITS ideas through case studies, collaborative seminars, peer review, and project-based learning.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The course is organized into four major modules. Rather than focusing primarily on the technical details of individual technologies, each module uses representative ITS applications to examine how transportation problems are identified, how intelligent systems are designed and operated, how emerging tools can be used to support decision making, and how proposed solutions should be critically evaluated from technical, societal, and policy perspectives.  
**Module I.** *Connected, Automated, and Cooperative Transportation Systems*

This module introduces the evolution of transportation systems from conventional human-operated systems toward increasingly connected, automated, and cooperative environments. Connected and autonomous vehicles (CAVs) are used as a primary case to examine how sensing, communication, infrastructure, human behavior, and system-level control interact.  
**Module II.** *Smart Infrastructure and Electrified Transportation*  
This module examines how physical infrastructure, information systems, energy systems, and transportation operations increasingly interact. Advanced parking management and electric vehicles provide two complementary examples of transportation problems in which infrastructure capacity, information availability, user behavior, pricing, and system management must be considered together.  
**Module III.** *Shared, On-Demand, and Platform-Based Mobility*  
This module examines the transition from individually owned and operated transportation toward increasingly shared, on-demand, and digitally coordinated mobility services. The emphasis is on understanding how platforms connect travelers, vehicles, infrastructure, operators, and information, and how individual mobility decisions collectively affect the transportation system.  
**Module IV.** *Emerging and Integrated Intelligent Mobility Systems*  
The final module looks beyond individual ITS applications toward increasingly integrated transportation systems. Air mobility and digital twin cities are used to explore how future transportation may connect multiple modes, physical infrastructure, virtual representations, real-time data, artificial intelligence, and decision-support systems.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Explain the fundamental concepts and system components of ITS
- Observe, identify, and frame transportation problems from a systems perspective
- Analyze interactions among transportation system components
- Critically evaluate existing ITS technologies and applications
- Conceptualize and compare alternative ITS solutions to real-world transportation problems
- Assess ITS solutions from multiple professional and societal perspectives
- Question, defend, and refine proposed solutions through evidence-based reasoning
- Communicate and collaborate effectively in the analysis and design of ITS solutions

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance and Absences:**

You are expected to attend class promptly and regularly. If you are unable to attend class, it is your responsibility to obtain class notes from a classmate. If you arrive late to class, or must leave early, please be considerate with your classmates, and do so as quietly and unobtrusively as possible. If you are unable to submit a homework assignment or take and exam or quiz due to an absence, you will be granted a waiver of the late homework policy and be given the opportunity to make up for the missed exam or quiz only under special circumstances. These include 1) illness or personal injury, 2) quarantine/isolation, and 3) university-related extracurricular activities. Illnesses and personal injuries include you or your children or spouse, and extracurricular activities include athletics. There is no need for a medical excuse to be provided for absence of up to a week (five days); otherwise, students missing more than a week due to illness or personal injury will be required to provide a doctor’s note or other supporting documentation to the Student Success Office to be granted these special considerations. In the case of athletics, a letter from your coach or trainer must explain why you had to miss class.

### Other Course Policies:

**AI Usage Policy:**

Students are encouraged to make thoughtful and responsible use of generative AI and other emerging AI tools, such as ChatGPT, Google Gemini, AI coding assistants, and intelligent agents, to support their learning and problem-solving activities. Appropriate uses may include exploring unfamiliar concepts, identifying possible approaches or resources, improving writing and communication, generating or debugging code, organizing information, and critically examining alternative solutions.

AI tools are intended to support, not replace, students’ own thinking and judgment. Students remain fully responsible for all submitted work and must be able to explain, evaluate, and defend any content, analysis, code, or recommendations produced with AI assistance. AI-generated information should be critically evaluated and, when appropriate, verified using credible sources. Students should not assume that AI-generated information, references, calculations, or conclusions are accurate.

For any homework assignment, lab, seminar report, or course project in which AI tools are used, students must include a brief **AI Use Statement** identifying the tool(s) used and describing how they contributed to the work. For example:

*“In this assignment, I used ChatGPT to refine the writing of the report, explore alternative approaches to the problem, and assist with debugging a data-processing function. I independently reviewed and verified the resulting content and remained responsible for the final analysis and conclusions.”*

Failure to disclose substantive use of AI tools may be treated as an academic integrity concern.

If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. All course work, including tests and homework, will be assigned to students individually. Where collaboration among students in groups is permitted and encouraged, this will be made clear in the assignment.
