## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Access data from multiple original sources, both internal and external to the organization, to appropriately represent the hypothesized relationship.
- Use visualization tools to determine clustering and degree of relationship between multiple variables.
- Use computational tools (Python) to determine statistical validity of hypothesized relationships.
- Use data mining techniques to establish statistical relationships to evaluate degree of relationship between data.
- Explore topics like Bias-Variance trade-off, Dimensionality reduction, Decision tree techniques and Random Forest algorithm.
- Develop multivariate representations, dummy variables (one-hot encoding), and proxy variables to strengthen the explanatory power of available data and to represent interdependency of data.
- Utilize data within a ML framework to provide classifications and/or predictions. Utilize structured data within a ML framework to provide classifications and/or predictions.
- Utilize ML where appropriate. Utilize Supervised ML where appropriate
- Demonstrate use of data to train, evaluate, and deploy ML models to gather insights, moving towards systems that can make predictions.
- Enhance the models using different boosting techniques (like Gradient boosting, Xgboost etc.).
- Demonstrate use of computation tools (Python) to analyze data and to produce meaningful and representative results.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

## Academic Integrity

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Dean of Students office website defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty in the [RPI Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement.](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs) Please make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. Of course, learning with and collaborating with others in the class is encouraged – work submitted must be completely your own and original. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy may result in a penalty of failure of the assignment or of the course, depending on the severity. A grade of zero will be given on the first assignment where a violation of the policies described above is detected, and a referral to the Dean of Graduate Education. If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of “F” for the course and referral to the Dean. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask your faculty mentor for clarification.
