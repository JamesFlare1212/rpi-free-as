## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Course Description and Course Structure  
Introduction to Engineering Design is an introductory course that allows students to experience the engineering design process in a team setting. Students will apply steps of the design process and learn about team development models. Students will learn how to understand user needs, design specifications, concept ideation, design refinement, fabricating a proof-of-concept prototype, working in a team, and other design related topics. This course is project and team based and encourages students to apply knowledge they have learned in other classes combined with additional research to understand a problem area and develop solutions. Students are expected to come to every class and work as a part of a team.  
Asynchronous Professional Development 1 (PD1)  
The course includes professional development objectives to help students develop necessary skillsets to work effectively and successfully in diverse teams. PD1 is delivered through asynchronous Blackboard modules and selected in-class activities, and consists of competency-based learning designed to foster effective teamwork and communication skills through the Four Gates of Peak Performance team development model (Andreatta, 2018). Coursework and assignments are designed for students to gain content specific knowledge, as well as provide opportunities to apply professional development knowledge and skills while working in their IED team throughout the engineering design process. Professional Development content meets specific ABET accreditation requirements and is integral to the course.  
Continuous engagement with the asynchronous PD1 course material and completion of PD1 assignments is necessary to be successful in IED. All the PD1 content, activities, and assignments support IED teams’ development processes, as well as students’ individual development towards being able to effectively work in teams. It is expected that students will review the required material and complete the outlined activities and assignments weekly on LMS.  
PD1 instructors will be in regular communication multiple times a week with students to provide reminders, check-ins, and feedback on completed assignments and coursework. In-person consultations are available by request for teams and individuals needing assistance on team development issues. Consultation request forms are available through LMS and the PD1 Instructor.  
The Engineering and PD1 sequences are coordinated as follows:  
Classes 1–6 | MS1: Problem Definition | Gate 1: Safety  
Classes 7–13 | MS2: Concept Development Proposal | Gate 2: Purpose / Sense of We  
Classes 14–22 | MS3: Subsystem Demonstration | Gate 3: Belonging  
Classes 23–28 | MS4: System Integration; MS5: Proof of Concept Report | Gate 4: Peak Performance / Adjourning

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The goal of this course is for students to learn about the design process and how to work in teams. Understanding the design process, starting from finding a problem area and understanding user needs to creating a proof of concept will allow students to understand the practicality of their engineering skills and how they can use their skills to improve peoples’ lives.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

- Student Learning Objectives  
  Students will demonstrate an ability to  
  1\. understand and implement the engineering design process and engineering principles to solve complex problems  
  2\. identify engineering problems, determine stakeholders, and assess user needs  
  3\. develop design specifications based on user needs  
  4\. integrate professional, societal, and ethical considerations and the role diversity, equity, and inclusion as part of the engineering design process  
  5\. communicate engineering concepts with a variety of audiences using different forms of communication  
  6\. work as part of a team and assume various leadership roles within the team  
  7\. perform background research to acquire and apply new knowledge relevant to their project area  
  8\. create a psychologically safe team space by developing shared community agreements and detailing/reflecting on their team formation process in relation to team development models.  
  9\. engage in activities that allow for reflection upon their experiences that promote diversity, equity, and inclusion to create strategies that can be used for future team development opportunities.  
  10\. learn and apply leadership competencies in conflict management and giving effective feedback.  
  11\. reflect on their team’s evolution to improve performance.  
  Engineering Milestone Overview  
  MS1: Problem Definition | A team presentation that communicates the problem context, users and interpreted needs, existing approaches, unresolved gaps, opportunities to innovate and a concise Problem Statement.  
  MS2: Concept Development Proposal | A team report documenting systematic concept generation and selection, target and refined specifications, comparison of sub-team alternatives and the integrated concept selected for development.  
  MS3: Subsystem Demonstration | Individual and sub-team evidence that key subsystems have been designed, built and tested as proof-of-concept elements against relevant specifications.  
  MS4: System Integration Demonstration | A team demonstration of an integrated proof-of-concept system and the coordination of subsystem interfaces and dependencies.  
  MS5: Proof of Concept Report | A team report documenting the proof-of-concept design, testing evidence, design decisions, limitations and design-for-manufacture considerations.  
  Engineering Homework Overview  
  HW1 | Individual | Problem identification  
  HW2 | Team | Problem opportunities, screening and selection  
  HW3 | Sub-team | User research and interpreted needs  
  HW4 | Sub-team | Concept generation and selection with target specifications  
  HW5 | Team | Design-review preparation and integration of alternatives  
  HW6 | Individual | Sketchbook evidence from MS1 and MS2  
  HW7 | Team | Subsystem demonstration preparation  
  HW8 | Team | System-integration preparation  
  HW9 | Individual | Final sketchbook evidence after system integration  
  Assignment instructions and rubrics are posted in Blackboard. Engineering submissions and grading are managed through Gradescope; PD1 submissions are managed through Blackboard. ## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will have the capacity to identify, formulate and solve complex engineering problems by applying principles of engineering, science and mathematics, while instilling the importance of creativity in developing innovative solutions.
- Students will know how to apply the engineering design process to produce solutions that meet specified needs with consideration for public health and safety, and global, cultural, social, environmental, economic factors
- Students will exercise and improve important design skills of visualization, calculation, experimentation, and modeling.
- Students will have skills in organizing people and ideas for successful design. Skills include teamwork, project management, verbal and written communication, and documentation.
- Students will be able to function on a team whose members together provide leadership, create a collaborative and inclusive environment, establish goals, plan tasks, and meet objectives. Teams will have diverse backgrounds, skills or perspectives.
- Students will develop the ability to communicate effectively with a range of audiences.
- Students will develop their ability to recognize ethical and professional responsibilities in engineering situations and make informed judgments, which must consider the impact of engineering solutions in global, economic, environmental, and societal contexts.
- Students will have the ability to acquire and apply new knowledge as needed, using appropriate learning strategies

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance and Participation  
Attendance and active participation are required because substantial engineering and PD1 work occurs in teams during class. Attendance contributes 5% of the course grade. Students remain responsible for communicating absences promptly, completing missed work, and coordinating with their Engineering and PD1 instructors when a required in-class activity is missed.  
Attendance will be recorded for each class meeting in Blackboard. A student marked Present receives full attendance credit for that meeting, a student marked Late receives half credit, and a student marked Absent receives no credit. An approved Excused absence is excluded from the attendance calculation. Attendance is recorded as a graded item in Blackboard.

### Other Course Policies:

Lab Safety  
All students and projects must comply with the School of Engineering General Safety Rules and Operational Policies for Manufacturing and Prototyping and Materials Testing Labs (full policies are available on LMS).  
AI Use Policy Statement  
AI tools may be used only as supplementary resources, like a textbook or tutor, and not as substitutes for original student work. Any permitted AI use must be documented using the course AI Use Disclosure Form. Students must evaluate, revise and integrate AI contributions and remain responsible for the submitted work. AI use may not replace an assignment’s core learning outcomes.  
Permitted uses  
• Early brainstorming, alternative concepts, and preliminary sketches or block diagrams.  
• Sample code for non-critical parts of a larger system, with full student review.  
• Background summaries of standards or regulations and grammar or clarity checks.  
Prohibited uses  
• Submitting AI-generated final deliverables without substantive student work and review.  
• Producing engineering analysis, calculations, test results, or concept-selection decisions that students present as their own work.  
• Submitting undocumented AI contributions.

• Between 1 and 24 hours after the deadline: 20 percentage-point deduction.  
• More than 24 hours after the deadline: 50 percentage-point deduction.  
No submissions will be accepted after the late submission date specified on Gradescope/Blackboard. Exceptions may only be granted in advance for documented circumstances such as illness, family emergencies, or official university travel.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Course Policy on Collaboration and Cheating/Plagiarism  
Plagiarism involves using another’s work and claiming it as one’s own. Cheating includes plagiarism, and includes additionally, but is not limited to, using or facilitating another to use work-product for a graded deliverable which is misrepresented as to authorship of the solution/ concept/ approach/ answer, or involves a breach of rules stated as part of the protocol for the assignment or exam. Collaboration on assignments, quizzes or exams is permitted only when explicitly described as permitted or required by the instructor for specific occurrences. The student will receive at least a full grade penalty, up to and including an F for the course, the full deduction being at the instructor’s discretion.

### Additional Course Specific Inclusive Excellence Information:

Diversity, Equity and Inclusion  
Please familiarize yourself with RPI’s policy toward Diversity, Equity and Inclusion (https://info.rpi.edu/diversity). School of Engineering policy is that no students, faculty and staff shall be excluded on the grounds of gender, race, ethnicity, class, religion, sexuality, disability, etc. The free exchange of ideas, as well as diversity of background, experience and views contributes to the learning experience, and further broadens and adds strength to the design team. Having a diverse, inclusive, equitable and respectful design team brings benefits for the businesses, customers, and employees. Decades of real experience and data has shown that diverse, inclusive, respectful teams are better at innovation, recognizing and stopping exclusion, and attracting and retaining top talent. All participants in this course are encouraged to recognize the diversity around them and are expected to treat their classmates with respect. Disrespectful, harmful, offensive, bigoted, or violent language or behavior will not be tolerated.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

Academic Integrity  
The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own performance. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.  
Academic Accommodations  
Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute is committed to providing equal access to our educational programs and services for students with disabilities. If you anticipate or experience academic barriers due to a disability, please contact the Office of Disability Services for Students (DSS) (dss@rpi.edu; 518-276-8197) to establish reasonable accommodations. Once you have been approved for accommodations, please provide your Faculty Memorandum (a letter provided to students by DSS) to all faculty members for this course.
