## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will introduce students to the processes and operations associated with computer numerical controlled milling, drilling, and turning. All these processes will be controlled by code written by the students. Students are expected to apply their knowledge of computer-aided engineering and manufacturing processes to class exercises, homework assignments, and tests. Over the course of the semester, students will program numerically controlled machine tools to machine parts. This course meets twice a week and is a combination of lecture and laboratory.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

- Machining Processes and Tooling
- Metal Removal Rates
- Computer Aided Machining Systems
- Computer Numerical Control (CNC) Overview
- Milling and Turning Processes for an Example Part
- General CNC Machine Setup for both Milling and Turning Machines
- G-code
- Mastercam

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will demonstrate a basic understanding of machining fundamentals including speed and feed calculations, tooling systems, and work-holding systems for CNC milling and turning equipment.
- Students will demonstrate a basic understanding of numerical controlled (NC) programming strategies.
- Students will demonstrate an ability to set-up, program, and operate CNC milling and turning equipment.
- Students will demonstrate an ability to generate NC code, both by hand and using Mastercam, to machine parts to specifications.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance for each class is mandatory, but students may leave early after the assignment due for that week has been checked off by the teaching assistant or an instructor. For each unexcused absence the student will incur a 10% reduction in their grade for the assignment due that day. Excuses must be emailed to the teaching assistant before the start of the morning class, or they will not be accepted. Unforeseen circumstances will be handled on a case-by-case basis by the teaching assistant and the instructor.

### Other Course Policies:

- Safety in the laboratory is the number one priority of all involved in this class. Anyone who violates safety and/or facility policies will be warned of the violation and depending on the severity of the violation, may fail the course. The class manager and the Associate Dean of Engineering deal with all situations on a case-by-case basis.
- Safety Quiz – all students must review the SoE Core Engineering safety policies and take the safety quiz titled “Rensselaer Manufacturing and Prototyping Laboratories – Safety Orientation” on Percipio before they can use the equipment in the lab. The training should show up in your Assignments on Percipio. If it does not, please inform the instructor.
- Immersive modules are to be completed by the end of class on the day they are due according to the schedule. It is recommended that students begin the modules on their own outside of class to ensure the module(s) is finished by the end of the class.
- It is MANDATORY for every student to take the tests and participate in all demonstrations. If you miss any one of these evaluation criteria, you will not receive a passing grade for that portion of the course, and this will most likely result in a failing grade for the class.
- Errors in programming and machine operation resulting in mistakes in machined parts will affect your grade. You are allowed two attempts to machine a part. Each try after the second attempt at machining a part will reduce your grade for that part by 10 points.
- Students will not run any CNC program on the machine tools before it is reviewed and approved by an instructor.
- Immersive modules related to milling and lathe work must be completed before students operate the CNC equipment associated with the modules.
- All computer code is to be generated by individual students on their own personal laptop and not obtained from any remote source (including fellow students) or the campus network.
- Some cooperation on homework assignments is acceptable, but do not duplicate answers word for word. Think for yourself.
- Submit all homework and other assignments on time. Late homework and quiz grades will be reduced by one letter grade per day.
- Unless noted, homework can be written in pen, pencil, or a word processor may be used. Just be sure it is neat.
- Check all your work for correct spelling and grammar. Submit all hand calculations and work with all assignments.
- Mobile Devices: Mobile devices are not allowed to be used in the Haas Technical Center without permission of the instructor or teaching assistant. All mobile devices (cell/smart phones, computers, pagers, etc.) must be stored securely away during lecture and are not to be used unless specifically directed otherwise by the instructor. Use of (or ANY interaction with) a mobile device during an exam without explicit permission of the instructor will be interpreted as the illicit transfer of exam data, will be considered an act of cheating and will be treated as such.”
