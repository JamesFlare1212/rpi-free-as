## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is for undergraduate students. An integrated development of modeling-and problem-solving techniques for particles and rigid bodies emphasizing the use of free-body diagrams, vector algebra, and computer simulation. Topics covered include the kinematics and kinetics of translational, rotational, and general plane motion, energy and momentum methods.

**Homework**

Please carefully read below:

- Only single-sided hard-copy homework on letter-sized paper will be accepted. Electronic copies or homework on non-letter-sized papers, including papers with spiral-binding parts, will be returned without review.
- Your name and RIN must be at the top of the first page of homework. If not, your homework will be returned without review.
- Only properly stapled homework will be accepted. Non-stapled or paper-clipped homework will be returned without review.
- Your answer must be in the order of the questions. If your answer is not in the order of the questions of the given homework problem set, your homework will be returned without review.
- Your answer must be legible, and your work should be in the order of the logical developments for your final answer. You must provide a reasonable explanation regarding the development of your answer, as shown in the sample problems in the textbook or homework keys. Non-legible homework or answers without logical justifications will receive no points or a significant penalty.
- Late homework will receive a 25% penalty to the score for each day it is late.
- Note that well-prepared reports are a critical component in your career development.

Please note that you can discuss homework questions with your colleagues, but your homework must be your own work. If any sign of non-your-work in your report is noticed, you will be significantly penalized on your final grade. Repeated offenses will result in failure of this course. Note that homework problems are provided to offer you some opportunities to understand the key ideas and practice technical skills of the course subject, which you can achieve only through your own work.

- Up to two (2) students may form a homework group. Students who want to form a homework group submit a homework group request form by 8 AM Tuesday, September 1st, 2026.

**Grade Appeal**

A student may appeal a grade only by attaching a memo to the specific exam or homework assignment that is being appealed. This memo must state the specific reason(s) for the regrade. The student has one week after the grades have been assigned to contest the grade. The regrading process will be based on proper physical implication(s) of the answer to the given question. Be professional in the appeal process.
