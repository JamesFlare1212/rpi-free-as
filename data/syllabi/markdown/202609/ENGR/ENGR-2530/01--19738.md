## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Concept of stress and strain, generalized Hooke’s law, axial load, torsion, pure bending, transverse loading, transformation of stress and strain components in 2D, design of beams and shafts for strength, deflection of beams, work and energy, columns.

## Course Text(s)

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The goal of this course is for students to learn how materials and structural members respond to loading and how to predict and analyze that response. By working from internal forces and stress through strain, deflection, and stability, students will develop the ability to evaluate the strength and safety of simple structures and apply these skills in later design and analysis courses.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The course develops the analysis of deformable bodies under load and the tools used to design safe structural members. Topics are covered in the following sequence:

1. Static equilibrium, internal forces, and the concept of stress
2. Normal and shear stress, allowable stress, and factor of safety
3. Axial loading: deformation, statically indeterminate members, thermal stresses, and stress concentrations
4. Torsion of circular shafts: shear stress, angle of twist, and statically indeterminate shafts
5. Centroids and moments of inertia of cross sections
6. Pure and non-uniform bending: flexure formula, composite and unsymmetric sections
7. Design of beams: shear and bending-moment diagrams, section selection
8. Shearing stresses in beams and thin-walled members
9. Transformations of stress: principal stresses, maximum shear stress, and Mohr's circle
10. Thin-walled pressure vessels
11. Design of transmission shafts and stress under combined loading
12. Deflection of beams by integration and superposition
13. Stability of columns and Euler buckling

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\) Evaluate normal and shear stress, strain, principal stresses and directions for simple problems.
- 2\) Apply basic mechanics of materials concepts and properties, such as yield strength, ultimate strength, and failure criteria.
- 3\) Predict the expected magnitude and location of maximum stresses in beam structures subjected to simple loads (axial, torsion, bending).
- 4\) Calculate simple beam deflections.
- 5\) Apply course material in the solution of short, well-defined problems in a professional manner.
