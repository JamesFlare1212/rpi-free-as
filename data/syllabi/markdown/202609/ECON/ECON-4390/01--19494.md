## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course combines economic theory with interdisciplinary literature to explore how markets, laws, regulations, and communities shape the production, distribution, and consumption of energy. The course also addresses the ways in which the extraction and use of energy resources may produce and reproduce broader social and political inequalities.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Filippini, M., and Srinivasan, S. An Introduction to Energy Economics and Policy.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

(1) Describe the economic and societal impacts of using markets to supply and allocate energy.  
(2) Recognize and explain market failures that warrant energy policy interventions.  
(3) Evaluate the benefits and costs of different energy policies.  
(4) Engage in meaningful and productive discussions on energy policy issues.  
(5) Communicate key concepts in energy and environmental economics effectively through oral presentation and written analysis.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

We begin the course with a close examination of the fossil fuel industry, and how oil and gas operations affect the communities in which they are located. We turn next to the development of key environmental regulations in the U.S. and how these regulations have shaped energy production. To better understand the sources and distribution of emissions from electricity generation, we then explore the structure of U.S. electricity markets and how they have evolved over time. The course concludes with renewable electricity generation, the rise of data centers and their impacts on society, and the role of climate policy.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

In every class class, students complete in-class worksheets on economic models of energy industry and regulation.  
Students present papers on energy policy, and engage in in-depth discussions on the assigned papers. Students also complete in-class reading quizzes to assess their understanding of the papers. There are 8 papers in the reading schedule throughout the class.  
There are four homework assignments to ensure students understand models covered in class.  
There are 2 in-class exams that test the students' knowledge on both the models covered in class, and policy applications from papers.  
Students submit a final paper on an energy policy issue of choice in three stages where the instructor provides detailed feedback.
