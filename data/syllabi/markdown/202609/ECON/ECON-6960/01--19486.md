## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides an introduction to econometric methods for empirical research in management and related fields. It focuses on the specification, estimation, prediction, and evaluation of empirical models used in academic research. Particular emphasis is placed on developing the econometric foundations and practical implementation of widely used research designs that enable credible causal inference, including instrumental variables, regression discontinuity, and difference-in-differences approaches. Additional topics such as panel data models, time series analysis, quantile regression, and machine learning methods are also introduced. Throughout the course, students examine how these econometric tools can be applied to address important research questions in management and to design rigorous and impactful empirical studies.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Develop econometric intuition and foundations for specifying and interpreting empirical models commonly used in management research.
- 2\. Estimate and evaluate econometric models using appropriate techniques, including linear regression, panel data methods, and time series analysis
- 3\. Apply causal inference frameworks by implementing research designs such as instrumental variables, regression discontinuity, and difference-in-differences.
- 4\. Critically assess empirical research by identifying strengths, limitations, and threats to causal identification in academic studies.
- 5\. Utilize modern empirical tools, including quantile regression and introductory machine learning methods, to enhance prediction and analysis.
- 6\. Design and execute an original empirical research project that applies econometric methods to a relevant question in management or related fields.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Weekly assignments and participation:

- o Problem Sets
- o 1-Pager Research Design for Research Proposal
- o Critic a Research Paper / Write a Referee Report
- o Presentation: Discuss a Paper
- o Research Proposal
- o Reading Assignments

In-Class Exams: two in class closed book exams covering the material covered up to the exam date.

Research Paper: Each student is expected to prepare an empirical term paper (with data analysis) focusing on a research question of his or her interest. The term paper must include the following components: (1) an introduction section describing the objective of the research, key research question(s) and motivation for the work; (2) a review of relevant background literature; (3) a research model; (4) a “methods” section outlying the chosen research methods and steps you plan to take in developing and testing fundamental constructs; econometric data analysis section describing identification and methodology (5) a discussion of results or preliminary results, and (6) a conclusion including future steps.
