## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course addresses classical and contemporary issues in the theory of money and banking. Topics covered include: financial markets and interest rates, commercial banking, the Federal Reserve System, theories of money demand and money supply, and the theoretical and practical aspects of monetary policy and economic stabilization. Subject matter classifies as macroeconomics, but also draws from microeconomic fundamentals.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- to gain a factual knowledge of money, banking, and the financial system
- to practice interpreting data in the monetary context
- to understand monetary policy and its implications for national and world economies.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Tests: There will be a total of FOUR tests: three tests administered online via MyLab and one paper test. The basis for tests will be all textbook readings and all lecture material. **MYLAB TESTS** are taken out of the classroom and are available for a period of three to four days; refer to MyLab for specific availability dates. Details of availability are from 12 AM the class session prior to the due date TO 11:59 PM the day of the due date. There is no late window for tests. Test format is take-at-home, open book/note/resource, and multiple choice/numerical answer. Each student will receive a unique test. Tests must be completed in one session and will be timed at 110 minutes. **THE PAPER TEST** is given in the classroom and is closed book and proctored. **MAKEUP TEST POLICY:** *Makeup tests will only be allowed at the discretion of professor. If allowed, the makeup test is scheduled during the week after the test.*

Optional Final Exam: An optional final exam will be offered as a way for students to attempt to improve their grade and is given in MyLab as a take-at-home test. The final exam will cover all course material. If you take the final exam, the score you obtain on the final exam will be compared with the average of your mid-semester tests. You will be awarded the higher of these scores as your grade for the test portion of the course. It cannot hurt you to take the optional final exam. The optional final exam is it is available for a period of 24 hours and must be completed within that time period. It is timed at 150 minutes. *If you miss any of the mid-semester* test(s)*, then plan on taking the final exam to improve your grade.* **MAKEUP TEST POLICY:** The optional final exam cannot be made up.

MyLab Assignments: Assignments using the Pearson MyLab online system will be due on three dates. These assignments include Homeworks and Quizzes which pertain to lecture/readings, and a data analysis problem set, called the Fed Project. All MyLab Homeworks, Quizzes, and the Fed Project are available from the start of the semester through their due date/time. Homeworks allow three attempts at each question; quizzes allow two attempts at each question. Due dates are cut-off dates for the submission of work. Due dates are not meant to be dates for you to begin or even finish work. It is expected that all students will complete work in a time efficient manner according to the Course Calendar; this means that work should be completed on a weekly basis and in advance of the due date. Any student who saves work to complete on the due date must expect that technical difficulties will prevent the finishing of work, resulting in lost credit. All assignments will be available for late completion until the end of the semester; a 10% late penalty will apply for assignments completed after the due date. See Course Calendar on the last page of this syllabus for the final “late” due date for all assignments.

FED Essay: Students will submit a written analysis of the most recent meeting of the Federal Open Market Committee, to be submitted in LMS by the due date specified; specific instructions for content will be posted to LMS and discussed in class. The policy for academic integrity with regards to plagiarism from any source including from former students is in full effect for this and all work completed. Any student who is not confident of their writing ability is urged to contact the *Center for Global Communication+Design* called “Comm+D Center”, located in the lower level Folsom Library; *If you would like help with writing assignments, visual design projects, or oral presentations, please visit the Center online at* *https://info.rpi.edu/comm-d* *to find helpful resources or to schedule an appointment.* *Comm+D is a FREE resource for all members of the Rensselaer community.* Late Policy: essays are accepted late only at the discretion of professor.
