## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course describes how individuals make financial decisions, and how those decisions might deviate from those predicted by traditional financial or economic theory. Throughout the semester we will examine how the insights of behavioral economics complement the traditional finance paradigm by introducing some of the main psychological biases in financial decision-making and examining the impacts of these biases in financial and other economic decisions. This course will also introduce students to behavioral and experimental methodologies used in finance, economics and other disciplines.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- By the end of this course, students, in their written and verbal assessments, will demonstrate proficiency in identifying some of the main behavioral financial economic concepts (i.e. psychological biases) in economic decision-making.
- By the end of this course, students, in their written and verbal assessments, will demonstrate proficiency in assessing data and methods, and current research on how these biases impact financial and economic decisions.
- By the end of this course, students, in their written and verbal assessments, will demonstrate proficiency in knowledge of how individuals make financial and economic decisions and how these differ from rational behavior.
- By the end of this course, students, in their written and verbal assessments, will demonstrate proficiency in policies and other ways to improve individuals’ financial decision-making.
- By the end of this course, students at the 6K level will also articulate promising research questions in behavioral finance and produce a research proposal to build specialized knowledge in the chosen research area.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

***General Participation:***

- In general, you are expected to attend all lectures, be on time, and actively participate. If you cannot join lectures for any reason, you are expected to communicate this to me as soon as possible. I expect you to participate in in-class activities such as MobLab, participate in and contribute to in-class discussions (by asking questions, providing answers or comments etc.), and to have read the materials that will be assigned.
- I will keep track of each student’s active engagement weekly. I will determine your final grade based on the quality of in-class participation and general classroom citizenship.
- **No electronics!** Please bring your laptops. However, you are expected to have all your electronics put away until they are needed for class. If you need your electronic device to take notes etc., you must obtain my prior approval.

***Leading Paper Discussion:***

- During each of the paper discussion days, we will discuss two papers in class. Each paper discussion will be led by 1-3 students (i.e. leaders) (depending on the class size). These papers will be chosen from a list of papers (provided by me) by discussion leaders.
- Discussion leaders will read their assigned paper very carefully and lead a discussion of the paper in class. They will come to class prepared with slides and a list of questions that you want to ask to class that will guide the discussion of the paper.
- In addition to their assigned paper, leaders are responsible to read the introduction of the other paper to be discussed and participate in the discussion.
- **On the use of AI:** You are encouraged to use AI to clarify specific concepts or terms you find confusing, but don’t substitute an AI summary for reading the paper itself. Reading the original paper means you follow the authors’ actual reasoning, not just their conclusions; skipping that step means the material won’t stick, and it will show in discussion. Do not rely on AI to read the paper for you.

***Grades:*** Your grade will be determined based on i) how well you understood the paper, and ii) your ability to foster an active and engaging discussion environment.

***Discussion Participation:***

- During each of the paper discussion days, we will discuss two papers in class. Half of the class will be assigned to one of the papers and the other half will be assigned to the other one. You will be responsible for reading your assigned paper very carefully. Additionally, you are also expected to read the introduction section of the other paper.
- Whether it is your assigned paper or not, all students are expected to participate in the discussion of both papers. Students are expected take an active role by asking leaders questions, sharing their thoughts, and making comments.
- **On the use of AI:** You are encouraged to use AI to clarify specific concepts or terms you find confusing, but don’t substitute an AI summary for reading the paper itself. Reading the original paper means you follow the authors’ actual reasoning, not just their conclusions; skipping that step means the material won’t stick, and it will show in discussion. Do not rely on AI to read the paper for you.

***Grades:*** Your grade will be determined by the quality of your participation in discussing of both papers.

***Quizzes:***

- At the end of each paper presentation, we will randomly determine whether there will be a quiz (with a 50% chance). Each quiz will be about 5 min long and contain one or two questions about the paper presented and its in-class discussion.

***Exams:***

***For ECON 6340 Students: Research Proposal and Presentation:***

- Each student who signed up for ECON 6340 will produce a research proposal and present their proposed research in class at the end of the semester.
- Your topic selection is flexible as long as it is somewhat related to the topics we discussed in class. In general, you will be expected to introduce and motivate your research question(s), provide a literature review, propose a research method to investigate your question(s). Your research method needs to be empirical, either using pre-existing data sets or experiments.

***Proposal Ideas Discussion Meetings***

- Each student is required to schedule at least two meetings with me. It is your responsibility to email me to schedule these meetings before their due dates. Come to these meetings prepared.

- First Meeting:
  - This meeting must take place during week of October 12th. It is your responsibility to email me the week before (or earlier) to schedule your meeting.
  - You should have a list (at least 3) of potential research questions. Make sure that you explain to me i) why each of these questions are interesting and ii) a very tentative plan on how to study each question. We will discuss each of these potential research questions and help you determine which one(s) you should focus on.
- Second Meeting:
  - This meeting must take place during week of October 26th. It is your responsibility to email me the week before (or earlier) to schedule your meeting.
  - We will have a more focused discussion of your already determined research question(s). During this meeting, you should be able to explain the importance of your research and how you plan to investigate it.
  - If needed, you should schedule follow up meetings.

***Presentations***

- Each presentation will be about 35-40 min long. A presentation template will be provided. I will elicit peer-feedback at the end of each presentation which will help me determine your grade for the presentation portion of your grade.

***Proposal Submission***

- The proposals should be about 1,500-2,000 words. A proposal template will be provided.
- You are expected to incorporate the feedback you may receive during your presentation.

***Grades***

- Your grade will be calculated based on i) how responsible you have been in terms of meeting the deadlines and their requirements (10%), ii) quality of your initial research ideas (15%), iii) the quality of presentation (30%), and iii) the quality of your proposal write-up (45%).
- University approved excuses will be evaluated on a case-by-case basis with the student.

### Additional Course Information:

**Use of AI Tools in Written Assignments**

Students are permitted to use AI tools, such as ChatGPT, for editing purposes to enhance clarity and grammar in their written assignments. However, the content of all submissions must be original and reflect the student’s own perspectives and ideas. Students should exercise caution to avoid potential plagiarism issues that may arise from over-reliance on AI-generated text. Misuse of these tools, resulting in non-original work, will be treated as a violation of academic integrity policies.
