## Course Content

### Content Details:

The topics below correspond to chapters 1-13, 14 (a little), and 15-16 in the text, with roughly one week per chapter or pair of chapters.  
1\. Introduction (ch. 1)  
2\. Review of (or introduction to) probability (ch. 2)  
3\. Review of (or introduction to) statistics (ch. 3)  
4\. Linear regression with one regressor (ch. 4-5)  
5\. Linear regression with multiple regressors (ch. 6-7)  
6\. Nonlinear regression functions (ch. 8, brief coverage of ch. 14)  
7\. Assessing studies based on multiple regression (ch. 9)  
8\. Regression with panel data (ch. 10)  
9\. Regression with a binary dependent variable (ch. 11)  
10\. Instrumental variables regression (ch. 12)  
11\. Experiments and quasi-experiments (ch. 13)  
12\. Introduction to time-series regression and forecasting (ch. 15)  
13\. Estimation of dynamic causal effects (ch. 16)  
Topics could be adjusted if needed during the course.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Discuss key methods of econometric data analysis and the assumptions and benefits of each method
- Explain key mathematical concepts behind these methods, and do some simple mathematical analysis or proof related to these key mathematical concepts
- Apply econometric methods to data analysis
- Use the Stata statistical software to carry out kinds of econometric analysis covered in the course

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

For context, *weekly readings* are required. Readings are best done in advance, so that in lectures you can review and solidify key concepts and skills. A schedule below lists the readings, which are textbook chapters. *Lectures* take place in the first of the two class days scheduled for a topic. Advance reading is recommended. If you are not strong on probability or statistics from prior coursework, definitely study the chapter in the textbook before the probability or statistics lecture, and ask questions as needed.  
*Chapter practice exercises* and perhaps *in-class activities* occur after each lecture, as take-home exercises or in class. These have proven to be important because they help solidify your knowledge and skills. Chapter practice exercises and in-class activities are informal, and unless you are told not to, you may use your book and notes and may discuss with other students during the exercises. The *in-class activities* might include graded or individual or group work if it seems important to solidify learning for review session activities. In-class activities are not generally announced in advance.  
*Review quizzes* occur in most weeks. These are like mini-exams that could review any content in the course to date. There will be a time limit, typically 10 or 15 minutes. They are closed-book, closed-notes, with no use of computers or other materials, and you must do them on your own on paper.  
*Labs* about once per week give experience with state-of-the-art econometric software as you analyze actual data, as well as practice reading published literature. You will write up your lab work, creating a report/paper called an *empirical write-up*. Empirical write-ups should be brought to class and handed in two class sessions after the corresponding lab (i.e., usually the next lab date). You may work with other people when doing the lab work, but it is crucial to exercise your skills on your own so you can do similar work on exams and in future. You must write your own empirical write-up, even if you work on the content with other students. (Grades may sometimes be assigned for lab activities, on an individual or group basis, and counted with in-class activities above.) For the lab on “binary dependent variables (& related issues),” if you are registered for graduate-level credit, you will need to complete an extra lab assignment “Empirical Exercises for Maximum Likelihood Methods,” and the extra lab report will count as one of your empirical write-up grades (if you are registered for ECON-4570 this extra lab is optional for extra credit).  
*Problem sets* should be brought to class and handed in two class sessions after the corresponding lecture (i.e., usually the next lecture date). You may work with other people when doing the problem sets. However, exercise your skills on your own so that you can solve similar problems. You must write out your own answers to each assignment. Problem sets are likely to be graded based on whether you tried not outcomes, with answers to check your own work (the TA will not have time to check all or any of the problem sets, but ask if you have a question).  
A *mid-term exam* will take place on Thursday October 15 during class. A *final exam* will take place during the usual final exam period (date TBA). The final will emphasize material after the mid-term but will cover some material predating the mid-term. Exam questions must be answered by yourself, and exams are closed-book and -notes.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grades consist of 10% chapter practice exercises and in-class activities, 25% review quizzes, 15% mid-term exam, 25% final exam, 15% empirical write-ups, and 10% problem sets. Problem sets will probably be checked for the percentage completed, or may be graded based on content sometimes if teaching assistant resources permit. Late assignments will have their grades multiplied by 0.8 if handed in no more than 1 class late, or by 0.5 if handed in up to two weeks late, but cannot be credited if more than two weeks late or after the last day of classes in the term. If answers to assignments are provided, please check them to identify any problems you are having and learn how to deal with these problems.  
Grade results will be made available as the semester progresses. The lowest two chapter practice exercise grades or in-class activity grades, and also the lowest two review quiz grades, will be dropped to allow for necessary absences. However, attendance is generally required and may affect your grades. For midterm or final exams missed because of major illness, death in the family, etc., you may contact the Student Experience Office, Class Dean, or other RPI support resource to inform me so I can arrange a replacement for you. Intellectual dishonesty, specifically cheating on quizzes or exams or plagiarism, will generally result in a Fail grade and referral to the Dean of Students’ office for possible further action.

**Software**  
Stata, used throughout the semester, is the leading commercial statistical program used by economists. (Free software R is also common.) I have arranged a license for *undergraduates* in this class for Stata BE.* Graduate students can purchase access at https://www.stata.com/order/ . The license extends through February 19, 2027. I will send installation information, or ask if you haven’t gotten it.

An extensive economist-oriented two-volume overview of Stata is: A. Colin Cameron and Pravin K. Trivedi, *Microeconometrics Using Stata,* 2nd ed. (Stata Press, 2022). Other books that show how to use Stata are listed at: https://www.stata.com/bookstore/books-on-stata/ .

\* BE stands for Basic Edition, with the numbers of variables and independent variables restricted to 2,048 and 798 respectively (versus 32,767 and 10,998 in the Standard Edition or 120,000 and 65,532 in the Multi Processing edition). See https://www.stata.com/products/which-stata-is-right-for-me/ and https://www.stata.com/products/comparison-of-limits/ .
