## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Discuss key methods of econometric data analysis and the assumptions and benefits of each method.
- Explain and derive proofs related to key mathematical concepts behind these methods.
- Apply econometric methods to data analysis.
- Use the statistical software, R, to carry out econometric analyses covered in the course.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Problem Sets (20% of grade):** I will assign four problem sets over the course of this semester. The problem sets will comprise questions on deriving theoretical results and interpreting empirical findings based on the concepts covered in lectures. The questions will mainly be from the back of each chapter, unless otherwise stated. You may work with other students when doing the problem sets. However, it is crucial to also exercise your skills on your own so that you can solve similar problems on exams and in future. You must write out your own set of answers to each assignment. Do not just find answers online and copy them – I will know when you’ve done this and then you get in big trouble (see Academic Integrity Policies below). Instead, make your own response to each question as best you can, ask if you have questions, and remember problem sets are not graded harshly and are mainly a way to learn. Please make sure written assignments are legible.

LO1-2

**Midterm Exams (40% of grade):** We will have two midterm exams. In case of any conflicts, please inform me well in advance (see tentative schedule below). If a student fails to attend the exam on the scheduled date, I will offer a make-up exam only after the student obtains a letter from the Dean of Students providing evidence of a legitimate conflict or at my discretion.

LO1-3

**Term Paper (40% of grade):** Students will conduct empirical analysis and present a write-up on a topic of their choice. I will provide you with detailed guidelines on the paper about one-third of the way through the semester. The project would comprise following components—a description of the issue and why it is interesting, summary of existing literature and other articles analyzing the issue, data description, empirical strategy, results, and interpretation of results based on econometric concepts covered in class. Students can work in groups of up to 3.  
LO1-4

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
