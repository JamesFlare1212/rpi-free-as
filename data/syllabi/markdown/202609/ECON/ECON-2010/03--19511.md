## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course combines demand, derived from consumer preferences, and supply, based on firms’ production functions, to establish market prices for goods and services. The course applies the microeconomic theory of the firm to price, cost, and output decisions of business enterprises under different market structures. Product pricing strategies are examined. Calculus-based techniques are used to minimize costs and maximize utility and profits across differing industry structures. The course also introduces topics such as game theory, externalities, public goods, asymmetric information, and behavioral economics.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Learning Goals:** By the end of the semester students will be able to:

1. Apply the mathematical tools of algebra and calculus to determine the optimal allocation of resources for a given economic agent’s preferences and constraints.
2. Identify how market structures (perfect competition, monopoly, oligopoly) affect resource allocation decisions, equilibrium market prices, economic efficiency, and policy effectiveness.
3. Apply microeconomic concepts to evaluate real-world policy issues including taxation, government spending, regulation, externalities, and price controls.
4. Critique the limits of economic reasoning and modeling, including identifying what separates economic models from the real-world choice situations that they are designed to represent.
