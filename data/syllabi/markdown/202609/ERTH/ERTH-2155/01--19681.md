## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

# Course Assessment Measures

Student progress will be evaluated based upon the following:

**Comprehension questions** – Questions will be given during the class period, make sure to bring loose-leaf paper and something to write with! You will be expected to turn these in upon completion. The questions are not supposed to be tricky but are designed to reinforce key points covered in the lecture and assigned readings. They are intended to gauge your understanding of the material covered *before any exam*. Consequently, in addition to the questions, I will ask you to assess how well you think you understand that material (or how well it has been covered): Green = well understood; Yellow = spotty understanding; Red = do not really understand it.

**Homework** – Homework will cover key elements of the readings and lectures. The problem sets will be a mixture of quantitative and qualitative questions. Working together (discussing, sharing hints) on homework assignments is fine (*and in fact encouraged!*) but I expect everyone to turn in an assignment in their own words with their own work. Homework will be due in my mailbox by 5 pm on the date assigned, unless otherwise noted.

Homework expectations:

1. Be neat. I won't give credit to anything I can't read.
2. Be clear. It is much more important to understand how you got to an answer than simply the answer itself. Define any symbols that you use. Make sure that you include appropriate units. Clearly state assumptions. *A bad assumption that is logical and clearly thought out can get you more credit than the ‘correct’ answer based on little else.*

**Exams** – There will be three exams, two midterms and a final. The final exam will not be comprehensive, and only covers material not addressed from after the second midterm (except for key concepts that thread throughout the course). For the most part, the exams will be short answer with maybe the occasional multiple-choice question. No questions will require the use of a calculator. The emphasis is on how well you understand and can explain the concepts. In this regard, consider the comprehension questions to be “practice questions” for the full exams.

**Group Presentations** – Each group will give a short presentation on a facet of planetary science related to the course. This assignment will demonstrate additional mastery of the course material and provide an opportunity to explore a topic we have discussed in greater depth. Students will be divided into groups of approximately 4, with no fewer than 3 students per group. There will be 12 group presentations, each limited to 15 minutes: 10 minutes for the presentation and 5 minutes for questions. The time limit will be strictly enforced. Each student is expected to speak for at least 3 minutes, with approximately equal speaking time among group members, and all students are expected to participate in answering questions. The presentation topic can be anything related to planetary science that interests the group, provided that it connects to material discussed in the course. The group will submit a short paragraph describing the proposed topic, along with an initial list of references, to receive early feedback and ensure that the topic is appropriately scoped. References must come from peer-reviewed journals and may argue for or against the topic being explored. There must be at least 4 references, preferably published within the last 10 years, and each reference should come from a different author or group of authors. Each student will become the “expert” for one reference, meaning that they will be responsible for developing a deeper understanding of that paper and explaining its contribution to the group. However, every student is expected to understand and synthesize all of the references used by the group, not only the paper for which they are the “expert”.

**Project Writeup** – Each student will complete an individual 5-page paper related to the group's presentation. This assignment will provide an opportunity to examine one aspect of the presentation in greater depth. **Each student must submit their own paper.** For the project writeup, each student will summarize and critically review the paper for which they were the “expert” during the group presentation. The paper should not simply summarize the assigned reference. You should frame the paper within the broader context of the group presentation and within the current state of planetary science. This includes understanding how other scientists have built on, used, or evaluated the work in your assigned paper and what the broader literature says about the topic. Consequently, you are expected to continue accumulating references beyond those included in the group's reference list. You will submit a short paragraph describing your proposed paper topic, along with some additional references, to receive early feedback and ensure that the topic is appropriately scoped. The final paper will be due on the last day of class. More information will be provided later in the semester.
