### Content Details:

## Big Questions we will Explore

Earth systems influence the resources we depend on, the hazards we face, and the environmental decisions that will shape our future. In this course, we will use geology to investigate three big questions that connect Earth science to your everyday life:

1. Where do we find the resources we need?
2. Are we moving towards another supercontinent?
3. Are you prepared for the geologic hazards and environmental challenges of the future?

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Explain where Earth’s resources come from by identifying minerals, rocks, and the processes that form them
- Explain how energy resources form and evaluate the benefits and challenges of different energy sources.
- Use plate tectonics to explain how Earth works, including the movement of continents, the formation of resources, and the location of geologic hazards.
- Evaluate geologic hazards such as earthquakes, volcanoes, and landslides using scientific data and evidence
- Assess the environmental impacts of resource and energy use and evaluate potential solutions to environmental and energy challenges using scientific evidence

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Pre-Class Reading:** Before most class meetings, you will complete a short activity designed to introduce key concepts and prepare you for in-class discussions and activities. These assignments may involve reading an article or textbook section, analyzing data, or watching a video. Completing these activities will help you develop the background knowledge needed to participate fully in class and connect new concepts to previous learning.

**Mini-Assignments**: Throughout the semester, you will complete short assignments that allow you to apply geological concepts to real-world problems, interpret scientific evidence, and explore topics in greater depth. These activities are designed to help you practice the skills used by geoscientists and prepare for exams. One assignment will be dropped.

**note—there will be either a pre-class reading or mini-assignment due before every class!*

**In-class engagement:** Class time is devoted to activities that help you practice geological thinking, apply course concepts, interpret data, and collaborate with your classmates. Engagement activities may include guided investigations, data analysis, concept checks, discussions, group problem-solving exercises, and short written reflections. Most class meetings will conclude with a brief **exit ticket** that asks you to summarize a key concept, interpret evidence, apply an idea, or reflect on your learning. These activities are graded primarily on participation, effort, and completion rather than correctness. The goal is to encourage exploration, discussion, and learning without the pressure of high-stakes grading. Because occasional absences are unavoidable, the lowest **three** engagement scores will be dropped.

**Journal Entries:** The geology journal is designed to help you reflect on what you are learning and connect course concepts to your everyday life, current events, and societal challenges. These entries encourage you to think about how geology influences decisions involving natural resources, hazards, energy, and the environment.

**Geologic Feature Brochure** Create an informative travel-style brochure showcasing a geologic feature or location of your choice. You will explain how the feature formed, connect geologic processes to the landscape, evaluate its environmental or societal significance, and communicate scientific information to a general audience. Your goal is to "sell" your location while remaining scientifically accurate.

**Exams:** The three exams provide opportunities to demonstrate your understanding of the major questions explored in each unit of the course. Exams are not cumulative, although concepts learned earlier in the semester may help you understand later topics. Together, the exams assess your ability to explain geological processes, evaluate evidence, and apply course concepts to real-world situations.
