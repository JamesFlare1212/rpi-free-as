## Course Content

### Content Details:

How healthy are the natural and human systems of Troy, New York, and what actions could improve environmental sustainability and resilience?

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Identify and recall the key biological and physical systems that influence the Earth's environment
- Develop transferable skills: a. Critical Thinking: Gather, analyze, synthesize, evaluate, and apply information. b. Interpersonal: Interact collaboratively to achieve common goals. c. Quantitative and Empirical Reasoning: Apply mathematical, logical, and scientific principles and methods. d. Technology: Use appropriate technology to retrieve, manage, analyze, and present information.
- Describe the impacts of humans on Earth’s systems and resources and how diminished ecosystems impact humans.
- Apply scientific principles and methodologies to analyze ‘wicked’ environmental challenges
- Generate strategies to address environmental challenges in personal contexts and broader societal issues

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Homework and Reflections:** There will be a number of out-of-class assignments to complete throughout the semester. These assignments are designed to help you prepare for class, deepen your understanding of environmental concepts, connect course material to real-world issues, and reflect on field experiences. Some assignments will focus on data analysis, environmental observations, or current environmental challenges, while others may involve reflections following field trips, guest speakers, or class activities. Depending on the assignment, submissions will be due either at the beginning of the next class session or one week after they are assigned.

**Lab activities:** Environmental science is a field-based observational science, and as such, laboratory sessions provide an excellent opportunity to learn environmental concepts and methods through applied activities. Most of the labs will take place outside-expect to be outside even if conditions are uncomfortable and dress appropriately. Material covered in labs will appear on your exams, including plant identification.

On rare occasions, illness, family emergencies, and certain school-sponsored events may make it necessary for a student to miss a lab session. You must notify me BEFORE the day of the absence in all but the most extreme emergencies. Typically, it will NOT be possible for you to make up the lab, as many of the labs are conducted off-campus. I will do my best to have an alternate assignment available, but it will not always be possible and should not be counted on.

**Field/Lab Notebook:** Throughout the semester, you will maintain a field/lab notebook documenting your participation in field activities, laboratory investigations, class discussions, and environmental observations made in Troy and the surrounding region. Your notebook should include notes, sketches, data, questions, reflections, and connections between course concepts and our investigations. Rather than being graded on perfect organization or artistic ability, notebooks will be assessed for completeness, effort, and thoughtful engagement with course activities. Notebooks will be collected periodically throughout the semester, and material recorded in your notebook may appear on exams, projects, and other course assessments.

**Exams:** In this course, there will be two exams. Exams will be cumulative and will include both new material and concepts from earlier in the semester. Questions will assess your understanding of lecture content, laboratory activities, field experiences, data interpretation, and environmental problem-solving. Rather than emphasizing memorization alone, exams are designed to evaluate your ability to apply scientific concepts, analyze evidence, and make connections among environmental systems and challenges. This cumulative format encourages continuous engagement with course material throughout the semester.

**Troy Environmental Assessment Project:** Throughout the semester, you will work in a team to investigate an environmental challenge affecting Troy, NY. Using data collected through course activities, field observations, scientific literature, and other sources, your team will assess the issue and develop evidence-based recommendations for improving environmental sustainability and resilience. Project topics may include biodiversity, water quality, pollution, climate resilience, waste management, environmental justice, or other environmental challenges facing the region. The final project will consist of an ArcGIS StoryMap, an executive summary written for decision-makers, and a presentation during our Troy Environmental Sustainability Symposium. The project is designed to help you apply scientific methods, analyze environmental data, communicate findings to different audiences, and propose realistic solutions to complex environmental problems.
