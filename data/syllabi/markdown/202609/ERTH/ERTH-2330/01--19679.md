## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

There are many excellent mineralogy textbooks. I have chosen for this course to use a book written by Dexter Perkins (University of North Dakota). Dexter has been involved in pedagogic improvements to the teaching of mineralogy and petrology for decades. He has created an on-line, open access (i.e. “free”) textbook that is very nicely illustrated with many, many diagrams and photographs of minerals. The URL for the book is:

https://opengeology.org/Mineralogy/

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Scope of course**

(1) To learn the fundamental principles of mineral symmetry, crystallography and crystal chemistry.

(2) To learn analytical methods and techniques for the identification of minerals.

(3) To learn to identify common minerals by their distinctive properties

(4) To learn where, how, why and when minerals form

(5) To examine in detail selected topics related to minerals in the environment and critical mineral supplies.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- (1) Identify a substantial number of mineral specimens in hand sample
- Identify select group of rock-forming minerals in thin section through the microscope
- Discuss the paragenesis of common rock-forming and ore minerals
- Understand the concepts of symmetry as it relates to crystal structures

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Collaboration in the form of discussion and sharing of ideas is permitted and encouraged between students working on problem sets, laboratory exercises and when studying for examinations. However, the final write-up of a problem set, laboratory exercise or paper must be a student’s individual work. That is, under no circumstances shall a student turn in a problem set or a laboratory exercise that is a copy or paraphrase of another student’s work. Collaboration of any sort (oral, written or otherwise) during an examination period is prohibited.
