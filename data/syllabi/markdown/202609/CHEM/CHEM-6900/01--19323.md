## Course Goals

### Goals:

- To expose students to diverse current trends in chemical research through weekly seminars given by experts in the field.
- Provide important professional development content through several presentations by faculty or guest speaker.
- Provide a forum for current graduate students in the Dept. of Chemistry & Chemical Biology to present their own research findings as part of the requirements for the Ph.D. degree.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

A diversity of seminar speakers presenting on their own research work or on a professional development topic.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Through being exposed to a diversity of seminar speakers and/or by presenting a seminar on their own work, graduate students will communicate scientifically at the level of a professional chemist, consistent with one of the outcomes of our Ph.D. program. Attendance to seminars, engagement in the Q&A session, and presenting their own graduate work will serve as evidence students have achieved this learning outcome.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Chemistry Ph.D. candidates in their first and second year and other students registered in the course must attend all seminars in-person. Attendance will be taken at each meeting.

Additionally, Ph.D. candidates in the Dept. of Chemistry and Chemical Biology must present a seminar on their thesis material during their third or fourth year in the graduate program.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

This course is graded as Satisfactory or Unsatisfactory based on attendance at weekly seminars.  
Missing more than two seminars or evidence of academic dishonesty (described below) will result in an “Unsatisfactory” grade for the semester.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance at the scheduled weekly seminars is ***required***. The seminar class will only be held when there is a seminar scheduled. The schedule for the class will be distributed to all members of the department and registered students at the beginning of the semester and whenever there are updates or changes. Potential conflicts with the seminar dates must be resolved with the class instructor at least one week in advance of the date. Except for verified illness or family emergencies, it will not be possible to make up the class.  
Missing more than two seminars or evidence of academic dishonesty (described below) will result in an “Unsatisfactory” grade for the semester.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Policy regarding academic dishonesty**:

The *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. The following is considered academic dishonesty:

- Signing the attendance sheet for another student.
- Multiple cases of signing the attendance sheet and leaving early missing most of the seminar.
- Multiple cases of arriving towards the end of the seminar and signing the attendance sheet.
- A student giving a seminar presents the work of others as their own work.

Students involved in academic dishonesty will receive a “U” grade for the course.
