## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Course Goal:**

To develop foundational chemical literacy and problem-solving skills by introducing students to core principles of chemistry, with emphasis on conceptual understanding, quantitative reasoning, experimental techniques, and scientific communication. This course aims to prepare students for further study in science, engineering, health, and related disciplines.

**Your Textbook & ALEKS: Keys to Preparation and Success**

Your textbook is more than just assigned reading—it is your primary resource for mastering course concepts, reviewing worked examples, and supporting your progress in ALEKS online homework. Many ALEKS problems closely mirror examples or explanations found in the textbook, so learning to navigate it efficiently will make problem-solving faster, more accurate, and less frustrating.

You are not expected to read the textbook cover to cover. Instead, use it strategically and regularly: consult it while working through ALEKS topics, review relevant sections before class, and revisit it when concepts feel unclear. Aim to complete the assigned ALEKS modules *before* class by working through the corresponding textbook sections. This preparation will make lectures more meaningful, help you ask better questions, and allow you to actively participate in discussions and activities.

Reading is the foundation of learning chemistry—not only the textbook, but also lecture slides, handouts, pre- and post-lecture assignments, and lab materials. Skipping these resources means missing essential content that is critical for success.

If you find lecture material confusing, it is often a sign that you have not prepared adequately. To encourage consistent preparation, your instructor will periodically review your ALEKS progress pie chart. Staying up to date is not optional—it is an essential part of succeeding in this course. Remember: you are aiming for understanding and competency, not perfection, but preparation is the key to both.

**Expected Student Learning Outcomes:**

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

1\. **Demonstrate foundational understanding** of the major sub-disciplines of chemistry— analytical, inorganic, organic, and physical chemistry—and explain how they interconnect within scientific and engineering contexts.

2\. **Apply fundamental concepts** of atomic and molecular structure, chemical bonding, stoichiometry, thermochemistry, and chemical reactivity to solve quantitative and conceptual problems.

3\. **Communicate chemical ideas clearly**, both orally and in writing, using appropriate scientific language and representations, tailored to audiences including peers, instructors, and professionals.

4\. **Perform basic laboratory techniques** safely and accurately, including mass and volume measurements, solution preparation and dilution, titration, separations, and introductory synthesis.

5\. **Access and interpret chemical safety information** (e.g., SDSs, chemical databases) and apply appropriate safety practices during laboratory work.

6\. **Collect, organize, analyze, and interpret experimental data**, including estimation of uncertainties and discussion of sources of error, to draw valid scientific conclusions.

7\. **Locate and evaluate scientific literature and credible data sources** and appropriately cite them in written and oral reports.

8\. **Identify major functional groups** in simple organic compounds using infrared (IR) spectroscopy and relate spectral features to molecular structure.

9\. **Develop intellectual curiosity and scientific habits of mind**, including persistence, creativity, and an appreciation for chemistry’s role in real-world applications and interdisciplinary problems.

**Active participation is a critical component of success in this course**. Please note that all classes are conducted in-person. There will be **no recordings available for later review**, so it is important to attend each session and actively participate. Your online ALEKS homework assignments associated with each chapter covered in lecture are posted and can be accessed through our course Blackboard page. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:**

The foundation of the relationship between students and faculty is built on mutual trust. For education to be effective, this trust must be maintained. As students, you are expected to trust faculty to design and structure the course appropriately, while faculty must trust that the work you submit represents your own effort and abilities. Any violation of this trust undermines the educational process and the integrity of the academic community.

In this course, maintaining academic integrity is of the utmost importance. As future professionals, many of you will be responsible for designing critical systems—such as aircraft, medical devices, and infrastructure — where the consequences of failure can be severe. In the same way, academic dishonesty in this course can have significant consequences. Therefore, **any form of cheating, plagiarism, or inappropriate sharing of work will not be tolerated.**

Examples of academic dishonesty include, but are not limited to:

- **Cheating**: This includes sharing answers during exams or assignments, using unauthorized materials (such as searching for answers online), or stealing answers from others.
- **Plagiarism**: This is the act of copying someone else’s work, even if you modify the wording slightly. If you use someone else’s ideas or results, you must properly cite the source.
- **Inappropriate sharing of work**: If you share your laboratory reports, assignments, or answers with others, you are just as guilty as the person who copies your work. This includes submitting work from previous years or copying results from past lab reports.

If you use external sources for your work, be sure to cite them appropriately. Failure to do so is considered plagiarism, even if the source is widely available or not specifically mentioned in the assignment instructions.

The **Rensselaer Student Handbook** and the **Dean of Students Office Webpage** (link) provide detailed descriptions of what constitutes academic dishonesty. Please take the time to familiarize yourself with these guidelines, and do not hesitate to ask if you are unsure about what constitutes a violation.

**Policy on the Use of AI in Chemistry Class and Laboratory**

AI tools can be valuable resources to enhance your learning in this course, but their use must adhere to academic integrity standards. While AI may assist with tasks such as data analysis, literature research, or visualizing chemical concepts, it is crucial that you use these tools to support—not replace—your own critical thinking, problem-solving, laboratory work, and written communications. Relying too heavily on AI can undermine your understanding of the material, hinder your learning process, and negatively affect your long-term academic and professional success. Furthermore, **your grade in this course is based largely on your own performance, including exams where AI tools will not be permitted**. You are expected to engage actively with the course content, complete experiments independently, and develop a personal understanding of scientific principles. Any use of AI in assignments, reports, or lab work must be transparent, with appropriate citations where applicable (see https://style.mla.org/citing-generative-ai/ for citation examples in MLA format). Plagiarism, misrepresenting AI-generated work as your own, or using AI to complete tasks designed to assess your own abilities will not be tolerated and may result in disciplinary action. If you are unsure about the appropriate use of AI in any aspect of the course, please consult with the instructor.

In this course, students must give credit to AI tools whenever used in the laboratory portion of the course, even if only to generate ideas rather than usable text, illustrations, or to partially or completely solve a problem. Again, **students cannot use AI tools during in-class examinations**. Overall, AI tools should be used wisely and reflectively with an aim to deepen understanding of subject matter. When using AI tools on any graded lab assignments, add a statement including:

- o a description of precisely which AI tools were used (e.g. ChatGPT, OpenAI)
- o an explanation of how the AI tools were used (e.g. to solve a specific problem, generate ideas, elements of text, long stretches of text, illustrations of key concepts, etc.)
- o an honest account of why AI tools were used (e.g. to save time, to surmount writer’s block, to stimulate thinking, to handle mounting stress, to clarify prose, to translate text, to experiment for fun, etc.)

**Consequences of Academic Violations**:

Any student caught cheating during an exam will receive an immediate **"F"** for the course. One strike and you are out. Academic integrity is a core value, and violations will be handled swiftly and decisively to maintain a fair and honest learning environment for all. Furthermore, plagiarism, misrepresenting AI generated work as your own, or using AI to complete tasks designed to assess your own abilities will not be tolerated and may result in disciplinary action. If you are unsure about the appropriate use of AI in any aspect of the course, please consult with the instructor.
