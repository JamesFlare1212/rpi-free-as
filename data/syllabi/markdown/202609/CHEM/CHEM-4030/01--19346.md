## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course will provide practical illustrations of some aspects of Physical and Analytical Chemistry and is related to the theory courses in these areas. Experiments involve individual and partnership exercises; specifics about the experiments assigned are given in a separate manual, but you will find that proper preparation and good planning for managing your time in laboratory are essential. This course also is communications intensive, meaning that a significant portion involves both written reports, which will be a major part of some experiments, and oral presentations. Experimental results in science are of little value if they are not communicated effectively, and skills in doing this are essential for a successful professional career.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. to illustrate some aspects of physical chemistry 2. to illustrate some aspects of analytical chemistry 3. to provide practice in communication in the style of a chemistry professional 4. to provide experience in laboratory team work, time management and organization 5. to provide experience in finding information in the chemical literature

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

***Attendance***

This course expects students to show maturity in planning their time and completing the assigned work according to the schedule provided. Students should be present in lab whenever they are scheduled to perform an experiment. This is particularly important on partnership experiments. Absences that involve missing an experiment may be made up if time is available for instrument use (students on-schedule have priority over make-ups on instruments). It is the student’s responsibility to complete the experiments and reports by the end of the semester.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:**

The relationship between students and faculty is based upon trust and the continued maintenance of this trust is necessary for education to be successful. Students need to trust faculty to make appropriate judgments about the content and structure of the course. Faculty members need to trust that the work turned in by students represents their own effort. Violation of this trust undermines the educational process. In addition, as professionals, you may be designing products upon which lives depend, as a result, there is no tolerance for breach of academic integrity such as cheating, plagiarizing, or inappropriate sharing of laboratories, data or related materials (past or present).

Chat-GPT or similar AI software are prohibited. Any use of this AI software will be an automatic failure. Detection software will be employed if work submitted is questionable.

**The penalties of a breach in academic integrity for this class are the following:** First offense: a zero for the work in question, (i.e., report, data sheets, etc...) and a drop in the course letter grade one level (for example, an A would become a B). Second offense: a failing grade in the class, with no opportunity to drop the course.

The Rensselaer Student Handbook and the Dean of Students Office Webpage https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-conduct defines various forms of academic dishonesty. Please familiarize yourself with these and ask us if you have questions about what constitutes academic dishonesty.
