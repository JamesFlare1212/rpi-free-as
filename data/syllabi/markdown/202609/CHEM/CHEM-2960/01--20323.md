## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Time: 12:00 - 12:50 pm on Wednesdays Subject Area:** Computational Chemistry Basics  
**Course Title:** ***Computational Chemistry Basics*** **- *Lab-Based Introduction***  
This 1-credit introductory computational chemistry course is a **lab-style workshop** that meets once per week for 50 min. Students gain hands-on experience generating, editing, and visualizing molecules, running basic calculations, with an emphasis on application and use of computational chemistry methods and techniques rather than deep theory or derivations.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

These texts are widely utilized across higher education curricula to bridge foundational quantum mechanical methods and applied laboratory calculations:

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Learning Objectives:**

- Run basic computational chemistry, electronic structure, and DFT calculations.
- Identify and understand key elements of computational output (e.g., total energies, optimized geometries, spectroscopy and molecular properties).
- Perform simple interpretations of analysis of computational chemistry results.
- Gain basic knowledge of emerging technologies like Quantum Computing.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Phase 1: Quantum Chemistry & Small Molecules**

**Week 1: Foundations of Modeling**

- ***Topic overview***: Introduction to Computational Chemistry, Potential Energy Mapping, Potential Energy Surface (PES), and HPCCs.
- ***Lab overview***: Coordinates (cartesian vs. internal) and building basic molecules.

**Week 2: Molecular Visualization**

- ***Topic overview***: Classical energy expression, total molecular energy, bonded interactions (bond stretching, angle bending, torsions/dihedrals), and nonbonded interactions (van der Waals, electrostatic).
- ***Lab overview***: Generating small molecules in Avogadro visualizer, and viewing bond lengths, angles, and dihedrals.

**Week 3: Energy Minimization & Geometry Optimization**

- ***Topic overview***: Energy minimization algorithm for finding minimum on the PES, gradient descent, and stationary points.
- ***Lab overview***: Performing classical geometry optimizations, and tracking energy convergence.

**Week 4: Hartree-Fock Theory & Basis Sets**

- ***Topic overview***: The Schrödinger equation, Born-Oppenheimer approximation, the variational principle, Slater determinants, the self-consistent field (SCF) method, the physical meaning of HF orbitals, atomic orbitals, Slater-type (STOs) vs. Gaussian-type orbitals (GTOs), split-valence sets, polarization functions, and diffuse functions.
- ***Lab overview***: Setting up single-point energy calculations using varied basis sets, e.g., 6-31G, 6-31+G(d,p), SVP, cc-pVDZ, to benchmark convergence and analyze basis set dependency.

**Week 5: Density Functional Theory (DFT)**

- ***Topic overview***: The Hohenberg-Kohn theorems, Kohn-Sham equations, exchange-correlation functionals (LDA, GGA, hybrid functionals like B3LYP).
- ***Lab overview***: Comparing the optimized geometries and dipole moments of a set of organic and aromatic compounds calculated via HF vs. DFT.

**Week 6: Electron Correlation (Post-HF Methods)**

- ***Topic overview***: The physical nature of electron correlation, configuration interaction (CI), Møller-Plesset perturbation theory, e.g., MP2, MP4, and Coupled Cluster theory, e.g., CCSD, CCSD(T).
- ***Lab overview***: Calculating the dissociation energy of diatomic molecules using HF, DFT, and MP2 to analyze where mean-field methods fail.

**Week 7: Vibrational Frequencies & Thermodynamics**

- ***Topic overview***: Vibrational frequency calculation, geometry optimization and Hessian matrix, thermodynamic functions, and temperature effects.
- ***Lab overview***: Computing Δ*H*, Δ*S*, and Δ*G*, and comparing computed results to experimental data.

**Week 8: Emerging Technologies (Mid-Term Case Study)**

- ***Topic overview***: Introduction to Quantum Computing in Chemistry e.g., mapping Hamiltonians to qubits, Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE).
- ***Lab overview***: Executing a simple Jupyter Notebook tutorial to find the ground state energy of hydrogen molecule.

**Phase 2: Macromolecules & Molecular Dynamics**

**Week 9: Biomolecular Structure & Databases**

- ***Topic overview***: Protein Data Bank (PDB) file formatting, parsing errors, missing loops, and protonation states.
- ***Lab overview***: Downloading a protein structure, stripping ligands, and cleaning the file for simulation.

**Week 10: Spectroscopy & Properties**

- ***Topic overview***: Population analysis, excited state calculations, electrical and spectroscopy properties calculations.
- ***Lab overview***: Excited state modules, electric moments and polarizabilities, and magnetic resonance inputs.

**Week 11: Overview of Multiscale Simulations (QM/MM)**

- ***Topic overview***: Multiscale simulations of molecular systems, overview of ONIOM methods, and electrostatic interactions.
- ***Lab overview***: Integrating multiscale features with ORCA methods, required settings for multiscale simulations, and defining regions.

**Week 12: Solvation Models & Boundary Conditions**

- ***Topic overview***: Explicit vs. implicit solvation, Polarizable Continuum Model (PCM), and the impact of solvent environment on molecular properties.
- ***Lab overview***: Calculating the UV-Vis absorption spectrum (TD-DFT) of a dye molecule in the gas phase vs. in water using an implicit solvation model.

- **Week 13: *Thanksgiving break – No RPI Classes***

**Week 14: Overview of Molecular Dynamics (MD)**

- ***Topic overview***: Integrating Newton’s equation of motion, *Ab initio* MD, timesteps, and thermodynamic ensembles/thermostats (e.g., NVE, NVT, NPT).
- ***Lab overview***: Basic MD modules, thermostats, velocity initialization, constraints, and restraints.

- **Week 15: Final Presentations**
  - ***Lab overview***: Student presentations on an independent modeling project, e.g., formulating a computational hypothesis, data visualization, and error analysis in quantum and classical workflows.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will gain hands-on practice building molecules, optimizing geometry and total energy.
- Students will gain hands-on practice running basic scans of stationary points on potential energy surface (PES).
- Students will learn to analyze molecular properties, spectroscopy, and computed total energies.
- Student will gain hands-on practice performing vibrational frequency and thermodynamic calculations.
