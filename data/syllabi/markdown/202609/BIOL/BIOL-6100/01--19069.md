## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

A detailed survey of important topics in the neurosciences. Some of the topics to be covered in this class are: basic mechanisms of neural signaling (neurophysiology, synaptic transmission, and molecular signaling); understanding of sensation/perception and in particular how it relates to neuroanatomy; neurodevelopment and how the mature brain can change (plasticity); complex brain functions and neurological disease. May be used to fulfill the Culminating Experience requirement in Biology. BIOL 4100 is an undergraduate course taught together with the BIOL 6100 graduate section.  
From Neuron to Behavior is a lecture course and assignments are comprised of major exams and article critiques. Students enrolled in the 6100 graduate section have an additional paper and presentation assignment.

### Goals:

Upon completion of this course, students should have acquired a solid foundation of knowledge for the major topics relevant to the field of neuroscience.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 4100/6100 - Describe the basic electrophysiological and biochemical properties of brain cells by the conclusion of the course.
- 4100/6100 - Discuss the development of the brain by the conclusion of the course.
- 4100/6100 - Discuss how cells of the central nervous system communicate with other cells in the central nervous system and with the peripheral nervous system.
- 4100/6100 - Describe how the brain interprets its surroundings through the senses (sight, hearing, taste, touch, and smell).
- 4100/6100 - Describe the role of the brain in higher, complex behaviors (i.e., cognition).
- 4100/6100 - Describe the pathophysiology of neurological, neurodevelopmental, and psychiatric disorders.
- 4100/6100 - Apply basic concepts in neurobiology to challenging questions in the field of neuroscience by the conclusion of the course.
- 6100 - Apply basic concepts in neurobiology and be able to analyze and interpret neuroscience data in research papers by the conclusion of the course.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Exams and article critiques will be use to assess content-based (i.e., neuroscience knowledge) student learning outcomes for all students enrolled in the 4100 or 6100 sections.  
A review paper and classroom presentation will be used to assess graduate student's (6100) ability to analyze and interpret neuroscience data.

### Other Course Policies:

**ELECTRONIC DEVICES**

Turn off cell phones or set them to silent unless you have made prior arrangements. Students may not photograph or record the instructor, other students, or class material.

**TIME COMMITMENT:**

Students are expected to read the assigned material before class and spend a MINIMUM of 3 hours studying outside of class for every 1 hour of class time. This means that students should spend at least 12 hours per week working on the material for this course outside of classroom contact time. This time should include reading the assigned portions of the textbook, reviewing and studying the textbook and class notes, and preparing for exams.

**LECTURES:**

Lectures are not designed to cover everything in a textbook, and no textbook is comprehensive. Most lectures will focus on selected topics from the book. Not all textbook material will be covered in class, but students are still responsible for all material in the assigned readings. Some material covered in class will not be in the textbook, so students must attend class and take good notes. Students are encouraged to ask questions and will be held responsible for knowing all assigned readings and lecture information.

**ARTICLE CRITIQUES / CRITICAL REACTION PAPERS**

The course is divided into four sections corresponding to the four major exams. Students must locate and read a peer-reviewed empirical/research journal article relevant to material covered in each section and write a critical reaction to the selected article.

Each critical reaction is 700–750 words, excluding the title and reference pages, and is worth 25 points. A citation for the article must be included. Students should be prepared to provide a copy of the article if requested.

These assignments are critiques, not summaries. Students should critically examine issues such as the study's hypothesis, strengths and weaknesses, interpretation and generalization of findings, statistical analyses, data presentation, and the appropriateness of experimental techniques or procedures.

Direct quotations are prohibited. Information from the selected article should be paraphrased in the student's own words. AI technology is not permitted on these assignments, and using AI results in a grade of 0.

Critical Reaction Papers are submitted through the LMS by 11:59 p.m. the day before the scheduled exam. Papers submitted within 24 hours after the deadline incur a 25% deduction (5 points); papers received between 24 and 48 hours late incur a 50% deduction.

**When composing each critical reaction, students might consider one or more of the following:**

- How does the information in this article enhance or expand your knowledge of a topic covered in the class?
- Is the hypothesis for each experiment clearly stated, and do you think that it is an important issue to study? Why or why not?
- What are the strengths of the study, and why do you consider them strengths?
- What are the weaknesses of the study, and why do you consider them weaknesses?
- Do the authors under- or overgeneralize the impact of their results? Please explain.
- Do the authors use appropriate statistical analyses of their data and/or appropriately present their data in tables and/orgraphs?
- Are the authors’ findings correctly interpreted? Please explain.
- Are the techniques, methods, and/or procedures utilized in the experiment(s) appropriate for the purpose of the study?

All critiques in your assignment should be clearly explained, and whenever relevant, citations of other sources should be provided to support your critique.

Critiques that do not fall within the word limit for the assignment or that use quotations from the article will receive additional point deductions.

Review the Critical Research Paper Rubric located at the end of the syllabus for detailed expectations and grading criteria.

**EXAMS**

There are four major exams. Each exam is worth 100 points and consists of 50 multiple-choice questions worth 2 points each. Exams cover assigned readings, lectures, and presentations occurring since the previous exam. Students are responsible for assigned textbook material even when it is not discussed in class. Exams must be completed independently.

**MAKE-UP EXAMS**

Students who miss an exam and provide a legitimate, written, and verifiable excuse may be permitted to schedule a make-up exam. Valid excuses may include serious illness, death in the family, an uncontrollable emergency, or participation in a legitimate RPI-related activity. Students should notify the instructor immediately. Students without an adequate written excuse may take a make-up exam with an automatic 25% deduction. Make-up exams may use a different format and must be completed within one week of returning to campus.

**GRADE APPEALS**

Graded exams will not be returned. However, upon request, Dr. Cheeseman will permit students to review their exam performance under direct supervision. Students may not take notes, photos, or recordings of any kind of the exam questions. Students who wish to review their exam performance should make arrangements directly with Dr. Cheeseman.

**GRADE CURVES/ADJUSTMENTS**

Exams may be subject to a curve. Whether a curve is implemented, and how large it is, will depend on overall class performance and grade distribution. Curves will take the form of raw points added directly to the student’s test score and will be applied equally to all students in the class. A student’s grade will never be lowered as part of a curve.

**GRADE DISCLOSURE**

Exam grades will be entered in LMS and made available within 7 business days of the exam. Article critiques will be graded and posted in LMS within 7 business days of their due date.
