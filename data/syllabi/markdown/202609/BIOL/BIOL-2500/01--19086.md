## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is an introductory course in genetics. It is intended primarily for Biology majors and those in related fields, and covers both classical and molecular genetics.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

*Genetics Analysis & Principles, 7th edition*, (2021) by Robert J. Brooker, McGraw-Hill Education.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Explain/analyze relevant concepts, observations, data sets, and problems in evolution (e.g., natural selection, artificial selection, formation and accumulation of mutations), inheritance, gene expression, gene interactions, systems-level gene function/expression, and other course topics using appropriate vocabulary and justifications.
- Explain how the structure of DNA, RNA, and proteins influences the storage, transmission, mutation, and expression of genetic information and apply this understanding to address relevant observations, data, and problems.
- Design/describe hypotheses, experimental approaches, and data that could address relevant observations/questions, as well as appropriately describe limitations/advantages of experimental methods and interpret experimental results.
- Communicate scientific research/information in a clear, accurate, and concise manner in a written format.
- Identify topics to enrich the scope of the course and to illustrate connections between genetics research and society, as well as develop informed/justified opinions regarding societal aspects of genetics research.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Assessment:**

In-class exams. Four in-class exams will be given on the dates noted on the schedule. Your grade will be calculated based on the best three exam grades (lowest exam grade will be dropped). If an unforeseen emergency prevents a student from taking more than one examination, it is the student’s responsibility to contact the instructor as soon as possible to explain the situation and to provide an official excuse (see above link under attendance). A make-up exam may be scheduled at the instructor’s discretion once the official excuse is received. An exam missed without an official excuse will be counted as a zero. Students who start the exam late will not receive extra time. **Students that arrive after the first exam is handed in will not be allowed to take the exam and will receive a grade of “0” for that exam**. You must bring their RPI ID and may also bring a scientific calculator to exams.

If during the semester circumstances dictate an Institute-wide change in policy and class/exams should be remote, exams will administered via LMS and take place during class hours. Students can request accommodations to account for different time zones or limited internet/streaming capabilities.

**Exams will not be handed back**. Exam viewing sessions for you to review your exam in-person will be scheduled within one week after the exam grades are posted. During this review, copying of any exam by any means, including but not limited to digital photography is NOT allowed. Any copying of an exam will result in a grade of ”0” for that exam.

Homework. Homework will be assigned periodically throughout the semester and the due dates will be provided at that time. Completed homework must be submitted through LMS. Homework submissions will be graded for effort not accuracy. Points can be lost if not all questions are attempted and/or answers are not a reasonable attempt. In other words, only legitimate attempts to complete the problems will receive full credit. Solutions to the problems will be posted on LMS after the due date.

Student Choice Topic. You are expected to submit a suggestion for an additional topic that we could discuss during the semester. You must briefly (200 – 250 words) explain why you think it is important and how it relates to the course. Topic suggestions will be penalized if they are unclear, not a very good fit for the scope of this course, or simply a restatement of a main topic of a chapter from the textbook. If you are unsure of whether your topic is specific enough, then mention why you want to learn more about it as a way to clarify what you want to know. You can suggest going into more detail on a topic that will be discussed during the semester or a topic not covered in the textbook. Include a reference (journal article, news article from a reputable source or book chapter, NOT simply a web site!) so that I may learn more about the topic. We may not be able to address all suggestions, but topics will be prioritized based on the number of times they are suggested.

Learning Objective:

This assignment aims to enhance the learning experience by encouraging critical thinking, broader exploration, and deeper engagement with course concepts.

Article Summaries & Comparisons. In this assignment, you will practice the art of summarizing technical review articles and comparing your summaries with those generated by ChatGPT, an AI tool. The goal is to learn how to convey complex scientific concepts effectively to a broad audience. You will be assigned specific articles to summarize, focusing on distilling the main topic, relevant background, novel insights, and potential future directions within 400 to 500 words. Additionally, you will utilize ChatGPT to generate summaries of similar length and then compare the differences between your summaries and those generated by the AI. The comparison should be between 150 to 250 words. This exercise will enhance your science communication skills and deepen your understanding of AI tools in scientific writing. For the first summary assignment, constructive feedback will be provided. Any summary that represents a legitimate attempt to complete the assignment will be given full credit. The second summary will be graded. The assignment will be assessed based on the quality of your summary and the clarity of your comparisons. A rubric outlining the expectations will be provided.

Learning Objective:

This assignment aims to enhance your ability to effectively communicate complex scientific findings to diverse audiences. By practicing summarization and comparing your work with AI-generated summaries, you will gain valuable insights into the nuances of science communication and the capabilities and limitations of AI in this context.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:** Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts, which violate this trust, undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.

In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. Anyone giving or receiving assistance on an exam or assignment will be considered cheating. Anyone found cheating on an exam or assignment will be given a 0 for that exam or assignment and reported to the Dean of Students at the instructor’s discretion. A second incident will result in a grade of F for the course and reported to the Dean of Students and possibly judicial action.

Some academic integrity definitions and can be found here: https://www.rpi.edu/dept/doso/resources/policies/Fillable2015-2016Version2AcademicIntegrityBrochure.pdf)

Some examples of cheating are giving or receiving assistance during an exam, the use of books, notes, crib sheets or other written material. This includes the utilization of any Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools for exams or writing assignments unless permission is specified.

If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. A complete statement on academic dishonesty can be found in the current Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities.
