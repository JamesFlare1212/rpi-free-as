## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course is arranged such that the basic techniques of restriction digests, ligations, PCR, transformations are covered in the first few weeks of the semester. In addition to learning techniques, you will also learn a few features of Benchling, such as restriction mapping, making virtual plasmids and translating segments of DNA to make sure you will engineer the correct fusion construct. Most laboratory work actually takes place on a computer where the DNA molecule is designed for biomedical research. Once you learn the basic techniques, you will use these techniques to make a GFP fusion protein using the promoter of a *C. elegans* gene to drive GFP expression as a major part of an independent project in the second half of the semester. Realize that GFP will only be synthesized if the constructed plasmid is injected into the worm and this requires 1 – 2 weeks of your time coming in everyday to check the worms.

At the beginning of this course a list of *C. elegans* genes will be provided. You will select one gene from this list that is of interest and attempt to PCR and make a GFP expression plasmid in the second half of the semester. You will design primers to amplify the promoter region of this gene and a small portion of the coding region and put it in frame with green fluorescent protein (GFP). If time allows, the construct will be injected into *C. elegans* to see if the construct works and examine the GFP expression pattern. A few genes on this list have already been cloned and expression patterns have been described. However, the remaining have not been cloned and you will have to identify homologs from human, fly, or mouse to learn more about the gene using online databases.

Lab assessment grades are based on attendance AND effort, attention to detail, analysis, knowing the lab assignments, efficient planning of experiments, fluency in using the Benchling software program and evidence of increasing skill and knowledge with the basic laboratory techniques used in this course. Your laboratory notebook must also be complete with conclusions after step in cloning your DNA fragment and gel pictures.

Your final video is focused on your gene of interest: design of your primers, describing all the molecular techniques used to make a GFP expression construct, and using BLAST analysis to compare the *C. elegans* gene to homologs in another species. Guidelines will be provided.

There will be at least 3 other projects for you to work on in case you run into problems with the worm project. You must finish a minimum of 2 projects to be eligible for credit full credit in Lab Assessment. Success in more projects will be considered extra credit. “Success” is determined by the professor.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Proficiency in content knowledge concerning molecular biology protocols including primer design and DNA cloning steps (e.g. calculations for volumes of enzymes, bacterial transformations and DNA quantitation)
- 2\. Interpret and paraphrase genomic and gene expression data from online databases and illustrate proficiency in using the molecular biology software program, Benchling.
- 3\. The ability to develop and maintain a laboratory notebook and reporting results using well-labeled graphs and pictures and conclusions (success or failure) at each step in the procedure.
- 4\. The ability to produce laboratory reports in a primary journal article format through independent learning and writing about familiar subjects at a high level of competence.
- 5\. The ability to communicate research goals, purpose, results and conclusions in a clear and concise manner
- 6\. Professional behaviors in all lab work and develop independence including the planning and managing of time to complete an independent project.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

- This course requires that students work together, in groups of two or three. Lab work and data analysis must therefore be shared within each group. Consultation between groups on lab procedures and data handling is also allowed; however, **each student is required to keep an independent** **and detailed notebook**. Copying of data from one notebook to another **is only permitted during lab time** unless explicit permission to do otherwise is obtained from the professor in *writing*. A verbal agreement does NOT count. The notebooks are considered the property of the course until the semester is over.
- Unless otherwise indicated, you should cooperate with one another in and outside of class on the solution of problems. You may collaborate with your lab partner regarding experimental results such as a PCR reaction and sequencing data. Each student’s research report and final lab report must be **independently** written. You may **NOT** collaborate on examinations or misrepresent another person's work as your own on examinations and written lab reports. You may not bring crib sheets to examinations unless specifically stated by the professor in an email or a written announcement, and you may not write on or alter examination materials that you submit for re-grading. The minimum penalty for cheating is an F on the assignment in question; clear cut or persistent cheating will result in a grade of F overall.
- Students who violate the spirit or letter of these rules are subject to penalties according to the principles outlined in the Rensselaer Handbook.

**Penalty for Late Assignments** Assignments turned in late will be penalized half a letter grade for each day or fraction thereof that the papers are tardy. If a paper is more than 3 (3) days late I will send an EWS. A late penalty of half a letter grade is applied for each day late. Papers will not be accepted (7) seven days after the due date or when the papers are returned the students. If you cannot hand in the final paper by the end of the semester, it is up to the student to get the forms for an Incomplete (I) for the course. Once a new due date has been agreed upon the paper must be handed in before that time or I will give a zero (0) for the assignment and your final grade will be determined as such.

**Grading Appeals** Appeals of grades must be made to the instructor within one week of the return of graded material to the student. Since grades are assigned both in consideration of the instructor's absolute standards and relative to the performance of the rest of the class, it is unlikely that grades will be changed unless a clear error on the part of the instructor/TAs can be demonstrated.

**Attendance**

Timely attendance at both laboratory and recitation sessions is mandatory. If you must miss a class due to an extenuating circumstance, you must contact the professor as soon as possible. In most cases, a missed laboratory session cannot be made up. Missing one lab will result in a grade reduction. You must email me a Dean’s excuse explaining why you missed lab and additional work will be provided to you to make up the time lost.
