## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course covers the theory and practice of biological sequence analysis, including algorithms for pairwise sequence alignment, multiple sequence alignment, phylogenetic analysis, and database searching. Concepts covered include homology, sequence similarity, parsimony, mechanisms and metrics of molecular evolution, biological databases, database search algorithms (BLAST), and statistical significance.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- At the end of the course a successful student will be able to: describe distinguishable methods for performing pairwise sequence alignment, multiple sequence alignment and phylogenetic analysis perform database searches and analysis genomic and protein sequences across multiple species analyze genomic and protein sequences on the basis of homology, sequence similarity and parsimony describe the mechanisms and metrics for measuring molecular evolution perform phylogenetic analysis of sequences across multiple species discuss methods used for performing genome wide association studies and disease association mapping describe mechanistic drivers of epigenetic changes to genomic DNA and the heritability of these changes demonstrate the ability to communicate sequence analysis-related topics to different audiences (e. g. general public vs upper-level undergraduate biology majors) in written and /or oral form demonstrate the ability to independently research scientific literature on current topics in sequence analysis, synthesize the material and effectively communicate the results in oral and written form

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

At the end of the course a successful student will be able to:

- describe distinguishable methods for performing pairwise sequence alignment, multiple sequence alignment and phylogenetic analysis
- perform database searches and analysis genomic and protein sequences across multiple species
- analyze genomic and protein sequences on the basis of homology, sequence similarity and parsimony
- describe the mechanisms and metrics for measuring molecular evolution
- perform phylogenetic analysis of sequences across multiple species
- discuss methods used for performing genome wide association studies and disease association mapping
- describe mechanistic drivers of epigenetic changes to genomic DNA and the heritability of these changes
- demonstrate the ability to communicate sequence analysis-related topics to different audiences (e. g. general public vs upper-level undergraduate biology majors) in written and /or oral form
- *demonstrate the ability to independently research scientific literature on current topics in sequence analysis, synthesize the material and effectively communicate the results in oral and written form*
