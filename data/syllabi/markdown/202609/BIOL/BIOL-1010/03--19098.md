## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Introduction to biological systems. Discussion of problems associated with biological organization, scaling, and hierarchy. Major topics covered include evolution, genetics, molecular biology and biotechnology, and ecology. The course considers the biological components of various societal and individual problems. Taught in web-based, interactive studio mode with emphasis on biological simulations, problem solving, and peer teaching methods. Includes a one-credit laboratory.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

To provide an understanding of fundamental principles, to develop analytical thinking skills in the context of modern biology, and to develop data dexterity for understanding and solving grand challenges in the life sciences

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate mastery of the foundational knowledge and core concepts of evolution, genetics & medicine, and ecology.
- Apply foundational knowledge and core concepts in these areas to solve novel problems, analyze realistic scenarios, and make predictions.
- Apply reasoning and methods to the analysis and solution of biological problems.
- Utilize information and data from a wide range of sources to construct knowledge and communicate that knowledge effectively.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

These Learning Outcomes will be assessed by:

- Exams and Quizzes that assess foundational knowledge and core concepts through multiple choice, short answer, and true/false questions.
- In-class discussions and writing assignments that apply the foundational knowledge to analysis of problems, scenarios, and predictions.
- A team presentation of an integrated topic which shows the ability to conduct research on a topic, organize it effectively, and communicate it to peers.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the Dean of Students. If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

Here are some specific guidelines for BIOL-1010:

- **Homework and In-Class Assignments:** We encourage students to discuss the readings and materials with fellow students. Learning is interactive, and we encourage this. In-class discussions will build on this interactive learning. However, for in-class writing assignments, **you must write your answer in your own words**. You may not copy from any other source, including AI programs (such as ChatGPT & other AI chatbots). ***If you are caught copying, plagiarizing, or using text generated by AI programs, you will receive a 0 for that assignment and you will NOT be allowed to drop that score.***

- **Exams & Quizzes:** You may not give or receive assistance by any means during these. You must be present (in the classroom) while taking these. ***If you are caught cheating, you will receive a 0 on that quiz or exam and will not be permitted to take the optional 4th exam. A second violation of academic integrity will result in immediate failure of the course. Cheating on the 4th optional exam will result in a 0 that will be used as one of your scores.***
- **Class Presentation:** This is a team project; therefore, you must work as a team. The expectation is that each person will contribute equally. Further information will be given during the first part of the semester.
