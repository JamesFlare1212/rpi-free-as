## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Level: This is an introductory course in Biology. It is intended for undergraduates, both non-majors and majors. It serves as the first course for life science majors and for students interested in the health professions. This is a D1 Data Intensive Course.

Course Content: Topics within the broad fields of Ecology, Evolution and Genetics will be covered.

Activities: Laboratory activities will include in silico, in vitro and/or in vivo experiments. Students generate hypotheses via in silico simulations, and share their ideas in interactive in-person mini-groups in the classroom. Hands-on in vitro labs impart basic laboratory skills. Field data using AI tools enhance student understanding of RPI Campus and Regional Biodiversity. Data analyses are based on scientific computing.

Goal: The aim is for students to gain biological literacy by applying the scientific method. Students will use introductory quantitative tools, develop data dexterity, and analytical thinking skills and learn basic laboratory skills. Students who wish to progress further in their learning of wet laboratory techniques are encouraged to take BIOL 2125 [no prerequisites], instead of or after taking BIOL 1015 labs.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The course content is broadly divided into three modules:

1. Ecology
2. Evolution
3. Genetics and Medicine.

The laboratory course content is generally divided into different types of laboratory experiments:

- In vitro

- In vivo

- In silico

Using Simulations you will learn:

1. UED - How do you apply the Scientific Method?
2. MIT - How do Living Cells replicate?
3. MEI - How are Gametes [eggs and sperm] produced?
4. DNA - How does DNA Replicate? Molecular Biology - DNA replication, PCR principles [for eg., Otzi’s genome]; Genetic Engineering; DNA extraction, PCR, Gel Electrophoresis.
5. TTE - How is Protein Synthesis accomplished? Transcription and Translation. Effect of Toxins, Mechanism of Protein Synthesis. Drug Design.
6. REG - How are Genes Regulated? - lac operon, trp operon, GFP reporters.
7. EPI - How do Diseases spread? - R0, vaccine critical threshold, Hardy Weinberg Allele frequency and other calculations/topics.
8. KP - How do Ecosystems maintain healthy community interactions? Trophic levels, competitive dominance.
9. IR - Why are some of our North American Species endangered?
10. FER - How do Allele Frequencies fluctuate within populations? Biodiversity, Conservation.

Each laboratory draws from fundamental principles from one, two or all of the units, at once, that are taught in the corequisite BIOL 1010 coursework, such as those listed below, but also additional research work from the real world. Hence, you will not find a weekly correspondence between labs [BIOL 1015] and lecture [BIOL 1010]. Therefore, each [BIOL 1010 and BIOL 1015 Labs] course is a separate course.

The lab goals are achieved through each weekly experiment, which encourages students to find connections between disparate biological concepts and fundamental principles in the textbook, the classroom and the real world, and apply them to become “interdisciplinary” problem-solvers.

The Scientific Method is applied each week to understand fundamental concepts in Biology.

- CYCL - Cell Biology - Mitosis, Meiosis, and the Cell Cycle.
- CELL - Microbiology - Prokaryotic (versus Eukaryotic) cell size, shape & morphology.
- AA - Biochemistry - Structures of amino acids, nucleotides, atoms and bonds, polymers.
- PDB - SarsCov2 genome & spike protein; Computational Data Visualization using PDB.
- GEN - Genetics - Mendelian Patterns, probabilities, ratios, Chi Square Analysis using corn purple/yellow plump/wrinkled mutant kernels
- FLY - Applying principles of genetics to study the passage of in vivo traits in the Lab Model Organism, Fruit Flies.
- CLAD - Bioinformatics: Phylogeny using Ungulate Morphological Traits
- TREE - Phylogeny using Geneious Software, Public Databases and NCBI Bioinformatics Software. Medicine - Drug/vaccine design and genetic engineering using Simbio’s simulations.
- STATS - Biostatistics - Excel, Introduction to Jupyter Notebooks with R Statistical Language and Python using free Supercomputer access.
- BMI - Ecology, Biodiversity and Conservation - Aquatic Ecosystems by computing EPT, H, J indices; Student's TTEST.
- DIV - RPI Campus Biodiversity Field Trip using AI tools for species identification.

Students will study data in silico, in vitro or in vivo, in situ or ex situ. Students will use Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools, iNaturalist, LLAMA, Merlin, Envision, and others. Some labs may be announced as optional during semesters impacted by supply chains/resource limitations/TA support/100th Anniversary or for other reasons announced in labs.
