## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Course Description  
Data and Society provides a broad overview of how society is leveraging and responding to the social, organizational, policy, and technical opportunities and challenges of a data driven world. Course themes focus on various aspects of the data ecosystem, data and innovation, and data and the broader community.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will develop greater data literacy. Be able to understand and explain the role that data plays as well as its limitations in various areas of research, commerce and modern life.
- Students will develop critical thinking skills around data. Be able to read, understand, assess, and discuss data-oriented professional and popular publications and articles.
- Develop communication skills around data. Be able to advance an evidence-based argument about data, data cyberinfrastructure and data-oriented efforts to both knowledgeable specialists within the field as well as non-specialists.
- Students to develop policy knowledge about data. Understand the potential impacts of regulation with respect to data regulation and legislation.

Other Course-Specific Information  
This course is a communications intensive course that was originally designed by Dr.  
Francine Berman, who is now the Director of Public Interest Technology and Stuart Rice  
Research Professor at University of Massachusetts Amherst, and thus no longer at RPI.  
This was then taken by Prof. James A. Hendler, who is the co-chair of Tetherless World  
Constellation Chair, Professor of Computer, Web, and Cognitive Sciences, and Director of  
both Rensselaer Future of Computing Institute and RPI-IBM Artificial Intelligence Research  
Collaboration. For me to change the course material and/or assignment types/grading  
would require that the course be reapproved for CI status, and there was not time to do that  
for this term. However, the material covered will be related, but not identical – so the ‘form’  
of the course is unchanged, the ‘material’ of the course will overlap, but include aspects of  
data and society that were not given as much coverage in the previous course. There will  
thus be more overlap with the Data Science course, but the instructors have worked to  
minimize this.  
However, students who have not taken data science, or are not taking it as a corequisite,  
may find themselves at a disadvantage and need to do some extra research on their own to  
pick up this material. (The professor will help identify sources if requested/needed).
