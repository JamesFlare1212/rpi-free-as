## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Introduction to computer architecture, assembler and C programming, and operating systems. Coverage includes numeric representations, processors, memory, I/O, digital logic gates, digital logic circuits, machine-level instruction formats, addressing modes, instruction types, and flow of control. Operating systems coverage includes memory management, processes, interprocess communication (IPC), system calls, synchronization, parallel programming, and network programming. Programming projects are required.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Use the C programming language on a Linux platform to implement moderately complex programs, encompassing systems programming, parallel programming, and synchronization
- Construct moderately complex assembly language programs, including the correct and efficient translation of a given C program into assembly
- Compare, contrast, and apply concepts of both classic and modern operating systems
- Convert integers and floating-point numbers into machine-readable binary formats, and vice versa
- Simplify Boolean expressions using Boolean algebra rules and K-Maps
- Show in written form a multi-cycle datapath, including the processing steps that different classes of instructions require as they move through datapath and control hardware structures

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1. There will be 4 individual homework assignments (20%)
   1. Homeworks will be in C and MIPS and will primarily be auto-graded via Submitty
   2. Due dates are typically Thursdays (by 10:00PM)
   3. Assignments will be made available at least one week before the due date
2. There will be 11 individual lab assignments (20%)
   1. Labs will be coding problems in C and MIPS and some written problems
   2. Labs are due by the end of the lab section you are registered for
   3. Labs will be made available at latest by Sunday evenings
   4. The lowest lab grade will be dropped, i.e., your top 10 labs will count
3. There will be 4 in-class quizzes (8%)
   1. Quizzes will be during lecture and cover the most recent material
   2. Quizzes have a narrow make-up window; an official excused absence is required
4. There will be 6 in-class pop quizzes — these will be a surprise! (10%)
   1. Pop quizzes will be during lecture and cover the most recent material
   2. Pop quizzes do not have a make-up
   3. Solutions to pop quizzes will be posted shortly after class (typically by 3PM)
   4. The lowest pop quiz grade will be dropped, i.e., your top 5 pop quiz grades will count
5. There will be 3 in-person exams (location TBD) (42%)
   1. Exams will be in our Wednesday 4:00-5:50PM test block
   2. A make-up exam will only be given with an official excused absence
6. There will be no final exam
