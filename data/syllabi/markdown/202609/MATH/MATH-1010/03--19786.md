## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance:** Students are responsible for all material covered in lectures whether they are present or not. Attendance is expected at all class meetings and will be recorded on Tuesdays via quiz submissions and on Fridays via Lab Exit Slips.

### Other Course Policies:

**Homework:** Achieve homework assignments will be due weekly on Mondays and Thursdays at 11:59 PM and will be graded automatically by the system. Students may submit these assignments late up to three days after the original deadline, but a penalty will be applied to the score. A 10% penalty will apply to assignments submitted the day after the deadline, a 20% penalty will apply to assignments submitted two days after the deadline, and a 30% penalty will apply to assignments submitted three days after the deadline. **No other due date extensions will be granted for any reason.** To compensate for this, several homework assignment scores will be dropped at the end of the semester.

Additional problems will be assigned from the textbook and will be posted on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/). Though these textbook problems will not be collected nor graded, they should not be viewed as optional. Success in the course depends on successful completion of all homework, both Achieve homework and textbook homework. Exam problems are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples. Full solutions to odd-numbered textbook problems can be found on Achieve. When logged in to our Achieve course, view content by assignments and scroll to the bottom of the page. The student solutions manual for chapters 1-6 may be found below No Due Dates. (Tip: you can download and save these files on your computer.)

**Quizzes:** Quizzes will be given in person at the beginning of Tuesday lectures during most weeks of the semester. Each quiz covers material presented during the previous week. A detailed quiz schedule will be available on LMS in the Course Infomation folder. No make-up quizzes will be given for any reason. To account for unavoidable absences or an off day, **one in-person quiz score will be dropped** at the end of the semester. Students missing a quiz due to a pre-scheduled absence (e.g., an official excused absence documented by Student Success to attend a conference or athletic competition) must notify the instructor in advance. Additionally, if a student's score on the corresponding midterm exam is higher than a quiz score covering corresponding material, **the exam score will replace that quiz score** prior to dropping the lowest grade.

**Active Learning Labs:** Most Fridays during the last 30 minutes of lecture, students will work in small collaborative groups (2–3 students) on problem-solving labs. Labs are graded on participation and completion via a physical Exit Slip submitted at the end of class. Labs are not a repeat of lecture — they are where you find out whether you actually understand the material, as opposed to recognizing it when someone else does it. The problems are designed so that you will need to think, discuss, and sometimes get stuck. Getting stuck productively is the point.

- **Standard Format (Track A — AI-Assisted Socratic Audit):** After giving it a first try on paper, groups use structured Socratic prompts on Google Gemini as a personalized real-time coach to guide problem-solving strategies. Gemini will ask you questions and push back on your reasoning — it will not simply solve problems for you. You will then perform a brief audit of your own handwritten work using an AI image check, receiving instant targeted feedback to identify algebraic errors, line-by-line logical gaps, and notation missteps. A device (phone, tablet, or laptop) and a google account (to log into Gemini) are required for Track A. Gemini is completely free with any google account.
- **Alternative Format (Track B — Pen & Paper Analytical):** Available upon request for students who prefer an analog experience using only textbook resources and manual solution breakdowns.

Both tracks complete identical challenge sets and submit a physical Exit Slip at the end of class for lab credit. The goal, the mathematics, and the Exit Slip are the same regardless of which track you choose.

**Important:** AI tools are permitted during labs strictly within the Track A Socratic protocol. Using AI to generate answers rather than to interrogate your own reasoning defeats the purpose of the lab and constitutes an academic integrity violation.

**Mentoring:** Mentoring sessions meet on non-exam Wednesdays during the test block (12:00–1:50 PM) in small groups of approximately 8 students, led by upperclass undergraduate mentors trained by subject-matter faculty and student life staff. Sessions focus on small-group problem solving, peer discussion, and targeted review of recent course material. Students will receive an email with the time and location of their specific mentoring group; each group follows its own brief syllabus distributed by the mentor, though all groups follow the same attendance, participation, and checklist requirements.

Attendance and active participation are mandatory and count for **5% of your course grade**: 4% for weekly attendance/participation, and 1% for completing the Mentoring Resources Success Plan Checklist (16 required signatures from approved academic-support activities, completed over the semester — see the separate checklist document for details). If you must miss a session, contact your mentor **on the day you miss it** to arrange a makeup by attending your mentor's drop-in office hours within one week. Makeups are limited to 3 per semester (exceptions will only be made in case of a serious emergency), and one missed session is automatically dropped from your grade at the end of the semester.

You will receive more information about Mentoring in the next few days and will be contacted by your mentor. Contact Dr. Traldi if you have have questions. .

**Exams:** Exams will be given during test block with the exception of students with accommodations as outlined below. Attendance will be taken at all exams using sign in sheets. Details about each exam, including the material to be covered on the exam and time limit, will be announced on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/) when the date of the exam is near. The dates for Exams 1–4 are listed above in the section **Grading.**

Students with approved accommodations will be taking their exams at the testing center and are responsible for booking their own testing appointments with the testing center following the guidelines that the instructor provides you. Note that these appointments **must be made well in advance** of scheduled exam dates. If a student fails to schedule a testing appointment with the testing center more than 4 days prior to the scheduled exam date, it is possible that their accommodation can not be met and they may have to take the exam as scheduled with their classmates.

A make-up exam is scheduled for each in-semester exam as noted above in the **Grading** section. Any student who misses an exam, must take the make-up exam at the time scheduled. If a student misses both the scheduled exam and the make-up exam dates, the student will receive a score of 0 on the exam. The only exception to this policy is a student who has an excused absence (e.g. official excused absence documented by Student Success to attend a conference, athletic competition) on the day of the exam AND on the day of the make-up exam. In such a case, the student must have notified the instructor of said absences as soon as they learn of it and no later than one class period prior to the regularly scheduled exam. Documentation excusing the student from class at the time of the regularly scheduled exam and make-up exam ([**excused absence documented by the Student Success office**](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/current-students/academic-and-personal-support/requesting-excused-absence)**, similar documentation directly to instructor or documentation from a coach with game departure time noted)** must be received by the instructor before the day of the regularly scheduled exam. Note that if you know ahead of time that you are going to miss an exam, for any reason, you must discuss this with the instructor no later than one class period prior to the exam. Note that the policy outlined here is for in-semester exams only. The policy for the final exam is outlined below.

**Final Exam:** The Final Exam will be cumulative and will be administered during the finals period December 16-22. **All students must take the final exam at the time scheduled by the Office of the Registrar. Exceptions will only be made if there is a scheduling conflict as defined** [**here**](https://registrar.rpi.edu/services/class-scheduling/final-exam-schedule-conflicts)**, or if a student is granted a NE grade by the Student Success office.** Please note that the Student Success office will only issue an NE for final exams for extreme circumstances, such as hospitalization (Read more here.) All students **must** take their exam at the assigned time for MATH 1010 Sections 01 - 04. You may NOT take a MATH 1010 final exam given by a different instructor. ***Do not make arrangements for travel at the end of the semester until the Final Exam Schedule from the Registrar’s Office is published or schedule your travel after the last day of final exams - December 22.***

**Access and Neurodiversity Services for Students (ANSS):** Students with a memorandum from the [ANSS](http://studenthealth.rpi.edu/disabilityservices) office identifying accommodations for exams must present the memorandum to the instructor, ideally by Friday, September 12. Providing the accommodations requires advance planning. Students will not be granted accommodations unless a memorandum from DSS has been submitted to the instructor **and** the student has followed emailed instructions for signing up for a testing appointment through the Testing Center. Students who fail to make their testing appointments more than 5 days in advance may forfeit their accommodations.

**AI Policy Note:** Generative AI tools (such as Google Gemini) are permitted during Friday Active Learning Labs for students opting into **Track A**, strictly following the Socratic prompting protocol. AI tools remain strictly prohibited during all quizzes and exams.

**Grade Appeals:** If upon reviewing the answer key and solutions for an exam a student feels that their work was graded incorrectly, then the student may submit a grading appeal to the instructor via Gradescope. Regrade requests will only be accepted during the grade appeal window specified on LMS once the grades are released. Regrade requests made outside of Gradescope will be ignored. Please note that regrade requests will not be available for the final exam.

**Calculator Policy:** Calculators will **not be allowed** on exams or quizzes. Problems will be designed so that this does not create an issue. Occasionally, a calculator may be required to complete a homework problem, but students should not rely on calculators for simple arithmetic or trigonometric calculations.

**Health and Safety:** Students are required to follow the health and safety protocols of RPI, which are subject to change throughout the semester.

**Visitors:** Visitors are prohibited during all class meetings. Only students who are registered for the class should be present in the classroom. Students may only attend class meetings at the time and place listed on their schedule on SIS.

**Classroom Decorum:** Students must be on time for class and should refrain from leaving and reentering the classroom during class, except for legitimate reasons. Phones may not be used during regular class and should be silent. Computers and tablets may not be used during regular class for anything other than taking notes. Students who are unable to courteously comply with these policies may be asked to leave the room. Phones, computers and tablets can be used during the active learning labs following the approved lab protocols.

**Extra Credit:** No extra credit opportunities will be available during the course. Thus students are encouraged to give their best effort on exams, quizzes, and homework assignments throughout the semester. Also make sure to attend and participate in your mentoring sessions!

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:** Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://rpi.app.box.com/s/d4ndpn3f7qdwxh9jaz5n1hkkr0mnd25e) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams and quizzes must be the student’s own. In addition, no calculators, computers, tablets, open books, open notes, phones, headphones, or smartwatches may be used during exams or quizzes. Talking or communicating with other students in any way during exams or quizzes is also prohibited. Students may discuss homework with other students, mentors, TAs, and instructors, but the work submitted must be the student’s own.

The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
