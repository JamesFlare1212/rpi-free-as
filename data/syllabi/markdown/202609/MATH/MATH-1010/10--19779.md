## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate basic and advanced symbol manipulation skills.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to convert between calculus concepts and their graphical, numerical, and symbolic representations.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to apply calculus concepts to selected problems in science and engineering.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to apply certain fundamental theorems, basic concepts, and rules from calculus to solve symbolic and graphical problems.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to present problems related to the theory of calculus and its applications to science and engineering disciplines.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are responsible for all material covered in class and recitation, so attendance of every lecture and recitation is strongly recommended and encouraged. If a student is absent, they should get the class notes posted each week on LMS and keep up with assignments as assigned on Achieve, during lecture, and during recitation. The best way to stay on top of the course, besides submitting all assignments on time, is to come to class and recitation, attend mentoring, and think about the material as it is presented.

### Other Course Policies:

Exams: Exam problems will consist of problems for which the students must show all their work, as well as multiple-choice, multiple-select, true/false, and fill-in-the-blank questions. Many exam problems are similar to review questions, homework assignments, and lecture/recitation examples. You must be able to correctly solve and show all your work, as well as fully understand your answer to these to consider yourself well-prepared.

Missed exams may only be made up if a student has notified the instructor prior to the exam (in the rare case of an emergency, students may have until midnight of the exam day to notify the instructor) or receive a grade of 0. Makeup exams will be administered during the recitation time slots following the exam. There is no final exam for this course.

Exams will be uploaded to Gradescope by the instructor, where students will be able to check their grades and feedback a week after the exam is administered. It is the responsibility of the student to review their exam for any discrepancies between their work and points awarded as described by each rubric item. Solutions to the exams are posted after grades are published on Gradescope.

Mentoring: Mentoring sessions meet during the Wednesday block (10:00–11:50 AM) in small groups of approximately 8 students, led by upper-class undergraduate mentors trained by subject-matter faculty and student life staff. Sessions focus on small-group problem solving, peer discussion, and targeted review of recent course material. Students will receive an email with the time and location of their specific mentoring group; each group follows its own brief syllabus distributed by the mentor, though all groups follow the same attendance, participation, and checklist requirements.

Attendance and active participation are mandatory and count for 5% of your course grade: 4% for weekly attendance/participation, and 1% for completing the Mentoring Resources Success Plan Checklist. If you must miss a session, contact your mentor on the day you miss it to arrange a makeup by attending your mentor's drop-in office hours within one week. Makeups are limited to 3 per semester (exceptions will only be made in case of a serious emergency), and one missed session is automatically dropped from your grade at the end of the semester.

Quizzes: Short quizzes lasting approximately 15-20 minutes will be administered during the recitation sessions per the class schedule. Similar to exams, they will be graded using Gradescope and will test understanding of lecture material and examples. You must notify your TA or the instructor PRIOR to the quiz to schedule a makeup quiz and avoid receiving a zero.

In-Class Problems: Each class, I will assign some problem(s) to check understanding and get extra practice. Students can work on these in groups or individually, then submit them using Gradescope (take a picture and upload your submission). Make sure your submission goes through; many students like to download the Gradescope App which notifies you when your submission goes through successfully. However, you can use any device by going to www.Gradescope.com. Students can submit their work from the time we start working on it in class until midnight the day after (on Wednesday and Saturday nights). Submissions will be accepted up to one day late subject to a 50% grade penalty. After that, submissions will not be accepted for any reason. Submissions will be graded for correctness as they would be on exams. If you miss lecture, you are expected to find the in-class problems in the notes posted on LMS and still submit them on time.

Homework: Achieve homework assignments will be due twice weekly on Tuesdays and Fridays at 11:59 PM and will be graded automatically by the system. Students may submit these assignments late up to two days after the original deadline, but a penalty will be applied to the score. A 10% penalty will apply to assignments submitted the day after the deadline and a 20% penalty will apply to assignments submitted two days after the deadline. No other due date extensions will be granted for any reason.

Getting help: don’t hesitate to ask and get help. You can ask math questions during class and office hours. You can also use email. When asking questions, it helps to be as precise as possible and to have a picture of the work you have tried. For example, if you ask a homework question by email, please attach a picture of your work for better and faster assistance. There is also tutoring and support offered by Student Success and our mentors.

*Student Success Drop-in Tutoring hours are Monday-Thursday 7pm-9pm and Sundays 3-5pm in the Success Center

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://rpi.app.box.com/s/d4ndpn3f7qdwxh9jaz5n1hkkr0mnd25e) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams must be the student’s own. A student may not change an answer on an exam before submitting it for an appeal. In addition, no calculators, computers, tablets, open books, open notes, phones, headphones, or smartwatches may be used during exams. Talking or communicating with other students in any way during exams is also prohibited. Students may discuss homework with other students, mentors, TAs, and instructors, but the work submitted must be the student’s own.

The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

Calculator Policy

Calculators will not be allowed on exams. Problems will be designed so that this does not create an issue. Occasionally, a calculator may be required to complete a homework problem, but students should not rely on calculators for simple arithmetic or trigonometric calculations.

Classroom Decorum

Phones may not be used during class and should be silent. Computers and tablets may not be used for anything other than taking notes. Students who are unable to courteously comply with these policies must note that they are probably missing most of the lecture, including practice questions, common errors, or hints that may be given about graded assignments or exams.

Health and Safety Protocols

Students are required to follow the health and safety protocols of RPI, which are subject to change throughout the semester. Please be courteous to others when feeling sick. Consider wearing a mask, covering your face when sneezing and/or coughing, and washing your hands more frequently.

Visitors

Visitors are prohibited during all class meetings. Only students who are registered for the class should be present in the classroom. Students may only attend class meetings at the time and place listed on their schedule on SIS unless cleared by the instructor.

THERE ARE NO EXTRA CREDIT ASSIGNMENTS GIVEN AT THE END OF THE SEMESTER. All course credit comes from the homework, in-class assignments, mentoring participation, recitation quizzes, and exams administered regularly throughout the semester. Successful completion of the class indicates an in-depth understanding of the content AND the ability to correctly and precisely communicate this understanding by solving relevant problems using symbolic representation. If you cannot do the actual math, you cannot pass the course.
