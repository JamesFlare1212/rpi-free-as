## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate basic and advanced symbol manipulation skills.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to convert between calculus concepts and their graphical, numerical, and symbolic representations.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to apply calculus concepts to selected problems in science and engineering.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to apply certain fundamental theorems, basic concepts, and rules from calculus to solve symbolic and graphical problems.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to present problems related to the theory of calculus and its applications to science and engineering disciplines.

Assessments in this course will consist of Exams (4), Final Exam, Achieve Homework (online homework system), and Math Mentoring participation.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are responsible for all material covered in lecture whether they are present or not. Attendance is expected, but will only be checked when there is an exam or quiz.

### Other Course Policies:

**Exams:** The material covered on each exam will be announced on [LMS](https://lms.rpi.edu/) when the date of the exam is near. Exams will be given in person during the Wednesday test blocks in DARRIN 330. **Students who receive testing accommodations from the** [**Access and Neurodiverse Student Success**](https://studenthealth.rpi.edu/list-services/access-and-neurodiverse-student-success) **(formerly Disability Student Services (DSS)) office who submit their accommodations memorandum to the instructor will be notified via email regarding the specific arrangements for their exams. This may include taking the exam in a small classroom at their regularly scheduled exam time or taking the exam in the testing center in DARRIN 345.**

If a student has a pre-scheduled absence (e.g. an athletic competition, official excused absence to attend a conference) on the day of an exam, then they must notify the instructor via email as soon as they learn of the absence and **no later than one week before the regularly scheduled exam**. Documentation excusing the student from class at the time of the regularly scheduled exam must be received by the instructor before the day of the exam. This documentation must be either an [excused absence documented by the Dean of Students Office](https://doso.rpi.edu/student-support/excused-absence) or, if the absence is due to an athletic competition, documentation from a coach. Provided the above conditions are met, the student will then take a conflict exam at a different time.

If a student misses an exam for any other reason (e.g. was ill on the day of the exam, overslept, did not know there was an exam, did not know where the exam was), then the student will receive a zero on the exam. This score will be dropped as the lowest score at the end of the semester. The remaining 3 exam scores plus the final exam score times two will count toward the student’s course grade. Thus, **the final exam serves as a make-up exam for the missed exam**. If a student misses more than one exam, then they will need to email the instructor within 24 hours of missing the additional exam(s) to make arrangements to discuss options, if any.

Note the policies in this section do not apply to the final exam. See relevant policy under **Final Exam** below.

**Final Exam:** The final exam will be cumulative and will be held on a date and time determined by the [Office of the Registrar](https://registrar.rpi.edu/services/class-scheduling/final-exam-schedule). All students must take the final exam at the time scheduled by the Office of the Registrar. Exceptions will only be made if there is a scheduling conflict as defined [here](https://registrar.rpi.edu/services/class-scheduling/final-exam-schedule-conflicts), or if a student is excused by the Dean of Students Office. Please note that the Dean of Students Office will only issue an excuse for final exams for extreme circumstances, such as hospitalization, and not for travel. (Read more [here](https://doso.rpi.edu/student-support-deans/missing-final-exam).) Thus, students should not make end-of-semester travel arrangements until the final exam schedule is finalized, or should only schedule travel for the time period after the last day of final exams, which is Tuesday, December 22.

**Quizzes:** Quizzes will be given most weeks during lecture. A quiz schedule for the semester will be posted on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/). Students must take quizzes in their assigned lectures in order to receive credit for the quiz.

**No make-up quizzes will be given for any reason.** To compensate for this, the lowest two quiz scores for each student will be dropped at the end of the semester. If a student misses more than two quizzes for valid reasons, then they will need to email the instructor within 24 hours of missing the additional quiz(zes) to make arrangements to discuss options, if any.

**Homework:** Achieve homework assignments will be due once or twice weekly on lecture days (Mondays and Thursdays) at 11:59 PM and will be graded automatically by the system. Students may submit these assignments late up to two days after the original deadline, but a penalty will be applied to the score. A 10% penalty will apply to assignments submitted the day after the deadline and a 20% penalty will apply to assignments submitted two days after the deadline. These extensions are granted by Achieve automatically to all students and do not need to be requested. **No other due date extensions will be granted for any reason.** To further compensate for this, five homework assignment scores will be dropped at the end of the semester.

Additional problems will be assigned from the textbook and will be posted on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/). Though these problems will not be collected nor graded, they should not be viewed as optional. Success in the course depends on successful completion of all homework, both Achieve homework and textbook homework. Exam and quiz problems are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples. Full solutions to odd-numbered textbook problems can be found on Achieve. When logged in to our Achieve course, view by Course Content. The student solutions manual for all chapters of our textbook may be found there. Tip: download and save these files for use in future courses!

**Mentoring:** Mentoring sessions meet on non-exam Wednesdays during the test blocks (10:00–11:50 AM or 12:00–1:50 PM) in small groups of approximately 8 students, led by upper-class undergraduate mentors trained by Mathematical Sciences faculty and Student Experience staff. Sessions focus on small-group problem solving, peer discussion, and targeted review of recent course material. Students will receive an email with the time and location of their specific mentoring group. Each group follows its own brief syllabus distributed by the mentor, though all groups follow the same attendance, participation, and checklist requirements.

Attendance and active participation are mandatory and count for 5% of your course grade: 4% for weekly attendance/participation and 1% for completing the Mentoring Resources Success Plan Checklist (16 required signatures from approved academic-support activities, completed over the semester — see the separate checklist document on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/) for details). If you must miss a session, contact your mentor on the day you miss it to arrange a makeup by attending your mentor's drop-in office hours within one week. Make-ups are limited to 3 per semester (exceptions will only be made in case of a serious emergency), and one missed session is automatically dropped from your grade at the end of the semester.

Upperclassmen registered for Calculus I may choose to participate in mentoring or to opt out. Those who opt out will have their course grade determined differently to substitute for the 5% of the grade allocated to mentoring.

For questions about mentoring, contact your mentor or Dr. Traldi, the Math Mentor Supervisor, at [zerbee@rpi.edu](mailto:zerbee@rpi.edu).

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://doso.rpi.edu/policies/academic/academic-integrity). All work submitted on exams and quizzes must be the student’s own. In addition, no calculators, computers, tablets, open books, open notes, formula sheets, phones, headphones, or watches may be worn or used during exams or quizzes. Talking or communicating with other students in any way during exams or quizzes is also prohibited. Students may discuss homework with other students, tutors, mentors, the TAs, or instructor, but the work submitted must be the student’s own.

The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of zero on the assignment. This zero will not be able to be dropped at the end of the semester. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
