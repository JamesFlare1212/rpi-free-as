## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course description:** Provides students with comprehensive coverage of the subject. Topics: vectors and matrices, operations with vectors and matrices, solving systems of linear equations, inverse matrix, LU decomposition, vector spaces and subspaces, independence, bases and dimension, affine spaces, orthogonality, projections, least squares approximation, orthonormal bases, determinants, eigenvalues and eigenvectors, diagonalization, symmetric matrices, positive definite matrices, Cholesky factorization, singular value decomposition, linear transformations. (from the [Rensselaer Catalog 2026-2027](http://catalog.rpi.edu/)); **Prerequisite:** MATH 1020: Calculus II

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

**Recommended Textbook:** Introduction to Linear Algebra, Fifth ed., by Gilbert Strang, 2016.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Apply results and definitions in Linear Algebra.
- Work examples in Linear Algebra.
- Explain topics in Linear Algebra.
- Demonstrate knowledge and understanding of the basic ideas of Linear Algebra, including concepts of linear systems, independence, theory of matrices, linear transformations, determinants, vector spaces and subspaces, bases and dimension, eigenvalues, eigenvectors and diagonalization, inner products, etc.
- Apply Linear Algebra concepts of this course to solve problems arising in applied sciences.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

3 exams and weekly quizzes

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance:** Students are responsible for all material covered in lectures whether they are present or not. Attendance is expected in lectures, but will only be checked when there is a quiz.

### Other Course Policies:

**Exams:** Exams will be given during our test block. Attendance will be taken at all exams using sign in sheets. Details about each exam, including the material to be covered on the exam and time limit, will be announced on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/) when the date of the exam is near. The dates for Exams 1–3 are listed in **Grading** above. This course has **no final exam**.

**Missing an exam should not be taken lightly.** If a student has a pre-scheduled absence (e.g. an athletic competition, official excused absence to attend a conference) on the day of an exam, then they must notify the instructor via email as soon as they learn of the absence and no later than one week before the regularly scheduled exam. Documentation excusing the student from class at the time of the regularly scheduled exam must be received by the instructor before the day of the exam. This documentation must be either an [excused absence documented by the Dean of Students Office (DOSO)](https://doso.rpi.edu/student-support/excused-absence) or, if the absence is due to an athletic competition, documentation from a coach. Provided the above conditions are met, the student will then take a conflict exam at a different time.

Students who miss an exam due to an unforeseen absence (e.g. illness on the day of the exam) must make every effort to notify the instructor **before the exam begins** to avoid grade penalties.

- If a student misses an exam due to an unforeseen absence and notifies the instructor before the exam begins, then the student is eligible to take a make-up exam without penalty.
- If a student misses an exam due to an unforeseen absence and notifies the instructor after the exam begins but before 8:00 AM the morning after their scheduled exam, then the student is eligible to take a make-up exam and a 20% penalty will be applied to the make-up exam score. A higher penalty may be applied to a student with repeated late notifications for missing exams.
- If a student misses an exam due to an unforeseen absence and **fails to notify the instructor before 8:00 AM the morning after their scheduled exam, then the student is** **not** **eligible to take the make-up exam and receives a score of 0 on the exam.**

The make-up exam may differ in format from the regularly scheduled exam. Only students eligible to take the make-up exam (see conditions above) will be given a make-up exam. The make-up exam must be taken within one week of the missed exam. Details regarding the make-up exam will be sent to eligible students the day after the missed exam. There are no make-up exams for the make-up exam.

**Quizzes:** Quizzes will be given typically once a week during lecture on Fridays. Material to be covered on each quiz will be posted by end of day Monday the week of the quiz under Quiz Schedule on [LMS](https://lms.rpi.edu/). Students must take quizzes during the lecture time that is listed on their schedule on SIS. **No make-up quizzes will be given for any reason.** To compensate for this, any missed quiz, or low quiz score, will be automatically replaced with the score on the corresponding exam containing the material if better. Additionally, the lowest two (2) quiz scores will be dropped at the end of the semester.

**Homework:** Homework will be assigned regularly. Though these problems will not be collected nor graded, they should not be viewed as optional. Success in the course depends on successful completion of all homework. Exam and quiz problems are frequently based on homework assignments and lecture examples.

**Attendance:** Students are responsible for all material covered in lectures whether they are present or not. Attendance is expected in lectures, but will only be checked when there is a quiz.

**Calculator policy:** Calculators will **not be allowed** on exams or quizzes. Problems will be designed so that this does not create an issue. Occasionally, a calculator may be required to complete a homework problem, but students should not rely on calculators for simple arithmetic or trigonometric calculations.

**Access and Neurodiverse Student Success (ANSS):** Students with a memorandum from the [Access and Neurodiverse Student Success](https://studenthealth.rpi.edu/list-services/disability-student-services) office identifying accommodations for exams must [email](mailto:browne10@rpi.edu) the memorandum to the instructor, ideally by **Friday, September 11**. In the email you send to your instructor, make sure to include your name, RIN and the course name or number. Providing the accommodations requires advance planning. Students will not be granted accommodations unless a memorandum from ANSS has been submitted to the instructor **and** the student has followed the instructions emailed by the instructor. Students who fail to submit their letter of accommodations and/or fail to follow the instructions submitted by the instructor may forfeit their accommodations.

**Gradescope:** Gradescope will be used for grading and returning all quizzes and exams. At the start of the semester, you should receive an email that you have been added to the course. You will also be able to access Gradescope by clicking on the Gradescope tab on LMS.

Specific dates will be given for regrade request periods for exams at the time that scores are released. Regrade requests for quizzes will also be available on Gradescope for one week after the quiz scores are released or until the last day of classes, whichever comes first. You may only appeal scores based on factual criteria (e.g. a correct answer was marked wrong, or incorrect rubric item checked in Gradescope). Grade appeals may not be used to request more partial credit or for disagreement with the point values on the rubric.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic integrity:** Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://info.rpi.edu/sites/default/files/Rensselaer%20Handbook%20of%20Student%20Rights%20&%20Responsibilities%20%C2%BB.pdf) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams and quizzes must be the student’s own. A student may not change an answer on an exam or quiz before submitting it for an appeal. In addition, no calculators, computers, tablets, open books, open notes, phones, headphones, or smartwatches may be used during exams or quizzes. Talking or communicating with other students in any way during exams or quizzes is also prohibited. The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
