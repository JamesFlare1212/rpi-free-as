## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to demonstrate that they can be able to rigorously prove basic results from mathematical axioms, definitions etc.
- Students will be able to demonstrate that they can be able to to write coherent and concise mathematical proofs.
- Students will be able to demonstrate that they can demonstrate their understanding of how the results in calculus follow from the principles of mathemat- ical analysis.
- Students will be able to demonstrate that they can demonstrate their ability to use tools from mathematical analysis in providing rigorous solutions for problems from calculus and its applications.
- Students will be able to demonstrate that they can be able to choose the appropriate techniques from mathematical analysis to generate mathematical proofs.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Course Overview:** The course will be comprised of the following components:

Lectures

Problem sets

Exams

Descriptions of how each of these components will be utilized in the course is described below.

**Lectures:** Attendance will be required to obtain a full version of the notes. An outline of the notes will typically be available the afternoon before lecture. For best results, please review before attending the notes before attending class.

**Problem sets:** There will be 10 homework assignments. They will be released each non-exam week on Friday and due the following Friday by 11:00 pm EDT. The majority of the problems will require the student to construct a formal proof. Students will be required to use LaTeX to typeset their solutions and failure to do so may result in a 10% deduction. Students will submit their work to Gradescope and may download the .tex file from LMS.

The rules for collaborating on homework problems with others are as follows:

Before working with anyone, each student must read the **entire** homework problem set and (minimally) do the following

Look up and memorize all relevant definitions and Theorems.

Make an outline of what needs to be shown.

Make an attempt at solving the problem.

Once students have satisfied the above criteria, students may seek help and collaborate with others (inside and outside of office hours) on figuring out how to write, refine and improve proofs using the following guidelines:

Students may get help on problems from an instructor, TA or another student currently taking the course.

Students may not get help on problems from anyone outside of the class without explicit permission from the instructors.

## Students may not consult the internet for help unless all information used is properly cited.

Students are responsible to independently write their own submission from their own notes taken during any above interactions.

Submissions with glaring similarities will violate academic integrity polices. The penalties for violating academic integrity policies are shown in a subsequent section on this syllabus.

Students are responsible for protecting their work and preventing it from being copied by others. In the event of an academic integrity policy violation, all students involved will be held accountable and penalized.

**Exams:** The 2 exams will be administered in person during the following weeks –

Exam 1: 10/7-10/11

Exam 2: 12/2-12/6
