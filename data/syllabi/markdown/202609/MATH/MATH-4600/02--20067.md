- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

For the homework, you are encouraged to collaborate by exchanging ideas and  
brainstorming solutions. You can work in groups in which you discuss the problems  
and help each other out if you are stuck. You are also allowed to use books and  
informative websites such as Wikipedia. However, in all cases, what you turn in must  
be your own work, and copying of any kind is forbidden. Examples include copying  
from fellow students (this includes partitioning the homework among several students  
without everybody's participation on every problem), contacting helpers outside of  
exam rooms using electronic or other means, wholesale copying of solutions from the  
internet (both recycled problems from past versions of this class or solutions of the  
assigned problems from any other source), using solvers such as Maple or Mathematica  
(unless allowed for a specific problem), using cheat sites such as Chegg,  
or Course Hero, or using AI apps such as ChatGPT or Claude (unless allowed for a  
specific problem).  
For the exams, you will be allowed a one-page, two-sided crib sheet of notes in your  
own handwriting or typing. When taking the exams, you are not to discuss the  
questions with anyone but the proctoring instructor and/or TAs. Copying from fellow  
students is strictly forbidden, as is using any type of books, notes, or electronic aids.
