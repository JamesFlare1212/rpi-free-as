## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Your course grade will be weighted as follows:

- Homework/Quizzes: 20%
- Midterm: 25%
- Project: 25%
- Final exam: 30%

The first homework will be due on or about September 18. Homework assignments will be assessed via grading of submitted homework, an in-class quiz concerning the homework, or a combination of the two. Homework that is to be directly graded must be submitted as single PDF files on gradescope. Some problems and especially the project will involve working with jupyter notebooks and/or computational code in MATLAB. The project will be assessed via a written report and possibly an oral defense.

Your course grade can be enhanced by active and substantive contribution to discussions in class and on the piazza site. You can be awarded as much as 5 percentage points on your final grade for exceptionally insightful and thoughtful input and questions.

Late homework or project milestones will be penalized 10 points (out of 100) per working day, and will not be accepted for credit after the late submission deadline posted on gradescope. If you have need for an extension on your homework or project milestone, you should ask me in advance of the due date. Homework is due at the specified time and date; submissions later in the class or day will be penalized 5 points.

Students with excused absences for classes involving a homework quiz will be offered a makeup oral quiz.

The standard grading scale is:

Graduate students are graded in the same way for scores of 70% and above, but they will receive a grade of F for scores between 0-69%.

You will be able to monitor your cumulative score and standing in the class throughout the semester via the Gradebook tool on LMS.

Your grade is based solely on performance, not effort. I do sometimes adjust the final grade in your favor if I see a compelling reason. Such grade adjustments are given purely at my discretion, and are not motivated by external matters such as your need to maintain a certain GPA for financial aid, domestic tranquility, etc. In particular, there is no point in asking for a grade adjustment; I will ignore any such requests. I view grades simply as a means of reporting, as accurately as possible, the demonstrated level of your mastery with the material.

You may only appeal scores on homework, quizzes, or exams based on factual criteria (a correct answer was marked wrong, or scores were added incorrectly). Any such legitimate appeals will only be accepted within one week of the date on which the graded work was returned to the class. I will not entertain quibbles about how many points of partial credit you think you should have been given on some problem. Students making frivolous appeals will be given one warning, after which further nuisance appeals will be penalized by as many points as the student was frivolously arguing for.

If you think you have not been meted due justice by me, your next step is to present your concern to the Dean of Science (as the instructor is also the head of the Department of Mathematical Sciences).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Beyond these general institute-wide policies, here are some course-specific clarifications

- For homework that is not directly graded but rather assessed via an in-class quiz, you are welcome to freely collaborate with others and use any resource you wish. Of course the in-class quiz has restrictions on collaboration and technology use, as described below.
- On graded homework and projects: If you obtained assistance from anyone outside of the course or any written material beyond the lecture notes and the course text, you must explicitly acknowledge the source. Use of artificial intelligence should be documented via a screenshot of the prompt and the response. You may under no circumstances query outside sources to solve in substance the homework problems for you. Inappropriate use of external sources will generally result in a grade of 0 for the entire assignment. Artificial intelligence products, especially large language models, are an example of inappropriate use of external sources when used as a basis, rather than supporting tool, in solving a problem. Illustrating the use of artificial intelligence as an adjunct to a properly and independently reasoned solution to a problem is welcome when explicitly signposted as such. Using artificial intelligence to help your learning and understanding is fine so long as it does not serve as a shortcut to solving graded problems. Students may be called for interviews with the instructor to verify their homework represents their own thinking and understanding.
- Unless otherwise indicated, you may cooperate in small groups on the solution of homework problems. You are strongly encouraged to work with a partner on homework, and may turn in one solution set per pair. While you may work together in groups larger than 2, each homework submitted should be written essentially independently once the ideas are hashed out. More generally, you should show your work and explain your calculations clearly and coherently to achieve most of the credit. It is not permissible for one pair to simply copy the solutions of another pair, even if they are working together. If there is evidence for copying, all students involved will generally receive zero credit for the implicated work if the collaboration is not acknowledged. If there is evidence for copying from an acknowledged collaborator, a grading penalty will still be applied for copying, possibly as severe as dividing the points among the collaborating parties. Note that in particular, students who share their homework solutions with another student, who then copies their work, will suffer the same grade penalty as the student(s) who copied their work. The worst penalty for not making your own independent and serious effort on the homework is, of course, a disappointing performance on the exams. Don't let your partner or your friends do your thinking for you!
- For quizzes and exams: You may under no circumstance collaborate on examinations or misrepresent another person's work as your own on examinations. You may not make use of external sources including books, notes, AI, or electronic instruments on quizzes and examinations. Limited notes in your own handwriting are allowed on the exam and the final exam, but not for quizzes. Violation of academic integrity on a quiz will result in a 0 grade on that quiz and all subsequent quizzes. Violation of academic integrity on an exam can be penalized by a 0 score for the exam.

Students who violate the spirit or letter of these rules are subject to penalties according to the principles outlined in the Rensselaer Handbook, in addition to the grading penalties described. In addition, I will report all instances of academic dishonesty to the Dean of Students.

If you have any question concerning these policies before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
