## Course Content

### Content Details:

The course will broadly cover the main topics in all six chapters of the textbook, supplemented

by selected related topics and applications. While the textbook will serve as the primary reference, the

organization, emphasis, and presentation of the course material may differ from those of the textbook.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- To understand the mathematics behind fundamental techniques and algorithms in numerical linear algebra, as well as related concepts and methodologies.
- To carry out standard mathematical and numerical analysis and derivations involving vectors, matrices, and algorithms in numerical linear algebra.
- To implement and evaluate standard algorithms; to interpret and analyze numerical results and observations, extract useful information, and draw meaningful conclusions.
- To be able to select, adapt, or design appropriate numerical algorithms for a given problem.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Exams, Homework, In-class activities are designed to support the objectives and learning outcomes

### Other Course Policies:

**-Policy on collaboration and use of external sources** (e.g., papers, books, and non-AI

resources): You are encouraged to discuss homework problems with others and to consult appropriate

online or library resources. However, the work you submit for grading must be written by you and

reflect your own understanding of the material. If your work involves significant assistance from other

people or substantial use of external sources, you should explicitly acknowledge this in your report.

**– Policy on the use of AI tools:** For a course focused on the mathematical and computational

foundations of numerical linear algebra, it is essential that you develop the ability to formulate and

analyze algorithms mathematically, implement algorithms through computer programming, interpret

numerical results and observations, and connect computational findings with theoretical results.

∗ Using AI tools to complete the analytical or theoretical portions of your homework, or to write

the findings, observations, discussions, or summaries related to the numerical components of your

work, is not allowed and will be treated as a violation of the Academic Integrity Policy. Limited use

of AI tools is permitted solely to improve the clarity of non-mathematical writing (e.g., grammar,

phrasing, or the organization of a report).

∗ Building computer codes is crucial to your learning and mastery of the subject. Using AI tools as

“code-vending machines” is not allowed and will be treated as a violation of the Academic Integrity

Policy. Responsible use of AI tools is permitted when they assist rather than replace you, such as

helping to debug code or explore ideas for improving code structure or performance.

∗ If you use AI tools, you must explicitly acknowledge their use in your report, including which tools

were used and how they were used.

**-Policy on late submission:** Each part of a homework assignment is due by its specified time

and date. Homework reports must be submitted as single documents via Gradescope (directly or via

Late submission is strongly discouraged. For each submission, the following grade reductions will apply

based on the time elapsed after its specified deadline:

5%: up to 8 hours late;

10%: more than 8 hours and up to 24 hours late;

20%: more than 24 hours and up to 48 hours late.

No submission will be accepted more than 48 hours after its specified deadline. Requests for an

extension without penalty must be made at least 24 hours before the relevant deadline.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
