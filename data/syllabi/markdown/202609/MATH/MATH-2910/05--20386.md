## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The math mentor program is an effort to try to help students improve their basic

mathematical skills and mathematical accuracy while making Rensselaer academics

friendlier as mentors help Freshmen make the transition to college academics.

The blend of mentoring aspects and mathematics aspects is an important feature in

this program.

Math Mentors will be able to blend these so as to help two groups of students:

1\. Some Freshmen may relate more easily to the social and mentoring

aspects of the session than the mathematics and these sessions may

help them get more involved in the mathematics.

2\. Some Freshmen may relate more easily to the mathematical aspects of

the session than the social and mentoring aspects and these sessions

may help them become more social and recognize the importance of

what the mentoring has to offer.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Calculus mentors will demonstrate the ability to present basic Calculus and Pre-Calculus problems to their classes.
- Calculus mentors will demonstrate the ability to lead a group of Freshman in discussions about mentoring.
- Calculus mentors will demonstrate the ability to assist peers and faculty members.
- Calculus mentors will help their mentees become good Rensselaer students with the ability to succeed in mathematics courses.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Grading will be based on preparing and leading assigned mentoring sessions, holding office hours, and participating in weekly supervisory meetings

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Mentors are expected to lead 2 weekly mentoring sessions, 1 hour of office hours every other week, and attend a weekly supervisory meeting.  
In a situation a mentor cannot attend one of the above, they are expect to communicate with a senior mentor and the faculty and try to get a replacement.  
Half letter grade will be deducted for every absence without a replacement (in normal circumstances).

- ### Academic Integrity

For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
