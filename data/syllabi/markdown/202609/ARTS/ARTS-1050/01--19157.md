## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

A thematic investigation of art in social, cultural, and historical contexts. This course explores how artists, art collectives, artworks, and institutional structures interact with their respective cultural moment(s) and are received by audiences and artists in the present. This course examines the ways in which artists, artist assistants, audiences, patrons, curators, galleries, public spaces, inventors, artists, and technologies shape artistic practices, and how these artistic practices in turn shape our identities.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**CLASS STRUCTURE**

On Tuesdays, expect the course period to be broken up into a one-hour lecture by the instructor and a break followed by a short discussion and/or presentation by a guest artist or theorist specializing in the topic of the week. Friday class periods will focus on student presentations in which students present for 5 minutes on an artist or art topic mentioned in the previous Tuesday’s class.

Lecture topics may include:

Animation

Western Art

Comics

Punk

Graphic Arts

Art and the Machine

Art of Protest

Utopias and Art

Formalism/Conceptualism, Modern/Post-Modern

Moving Image and/or time-based art

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will identify movements in contemporary art and place them in a historical context
- Students will be able to research art and artists and give presentations based on their individual interests.
- Students will place art movements in a social and political context and draw connections between artists and art movements.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Expect to give two 5-minute presentations on a Friday over the course of the semester on any artist or artistic concept covered in the week’s Tuesday lecture. Two non-cumulative exams will be assigned.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic Dishonesty

*The Rensselaer Handbook* defines various forms of academic dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Students are responsible for familiarizing themselves with these policies. Students should note that the penalty for plagiarism and other forms of cheating is a grade of “F” for the course.

In this class “cheating” consists of plagiarism or representing the work of others as your own. This can include the deliberate or unintentional omission of acknowledgments or references. Cheating also consists of copying graphics, created by others and submitting that work as your own.

The use of AI generated work is prohibited in this course, and will constitute as cheating, resulting in an “F” for the course.
