## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course in an introductory studio course in acrylic painting techniques and concepts and is designed to help students develop skills and aesthetic sensibilities related to artistic expression in acrylic mediums. Students will be taught the properties of acrylic paint as well common materials and processes used. Through assignments that reinforce the use of color and composition, we will explore a range of subject matter and themes that explore both representation and abstraction. This course is also focused on developing your critical eye as an artist. This skill will be learned through lectures, discussions, readings, and critiques.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This studio-based course emphasizes hands-on painting activities, comprising a substantial portion of class time. You can expect a majority of the class being in the physical act of painting, but you will also be presented with slide lectures and be required to participate in discussions. The initial phase of the term will concentrate on material handling, color theory, and compositional exercises. The subsequent weeks are structured to progressively guide you towards expression and experimentation.

Throughout the semester, there will be five main projects, each demanding dedicated out-of-class work. However, your final project will be worth significantly more that the other projects, the equivalent of 2 assignments due to it’s size being larger. In addition, there will be homework between assignments to reinforce your grasp of presented concepts. Evaluation encompasses both the homework and the assigned projects, with each task building upon the preceding one. Attendance is crucial, as missed classes pose challenges in catching up with classwork.

Expect periodic in-class critiques, necessitating preparation to discuss your work and engage in dialogues about your peers’ work. Light reading materials are part of the course, and will be provided electronically. Please sign and date all your work, and save it for portfolio reviews at Midterm and Final assessments.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**GRADING BREAKDOWN:**

Assignments: 65%

Homework: 25%

Class Participation: 10%

Each assignment will be graded on both your effort (how much time and care given to project) and process (following instruction, demonstrating understanding of concepts, showing investigation)

Each assignment will be graded with the following criteria: (\_\_\_/100)

Composition and Design (care/intention in the arrangement of compositional elements ): 25 pts

Technique (care/intention toward the application of materials in relation to desired outcome): 25 pts

Color: (demonstration of color theory concepts and experimentation): 25 pts

Invention/Creativity: (pushing investigation of ideas and concepts to find personal expression): 25 pts

If your assignments and homework are not hanging at the beginning of class on crit day then it will be considered late. Unfinished work will not be critiqued. You will only receive a grade for finished work. Late work will suffer in grade by 10 points.

**Assignment check points:** Students will be often be required to show “progress” check points on major assignments, usually due inbetween crits. These are referred to as painted sketches or underpaintings. This is a helpful point in the project to take pause and see how the structure, compostion and other project concepts are progressing, If these are not completed or presented on day asked, you will recieve five points off of your overall grade.

**Class Participation** means you are actively contributing to discussions and class critiques by showing interest in what others make, asking questions about the assignment goals and outcomes, and using learned vocabulary. It also applies to the explanation and presentation of your own work. At the beginning of each critique, we will have small “peer to peer discussions” before we begin where you will get the opportunity to discuss with a classmate (one on one) their work at close looking. If you miss this segment of the class, your participation grade will suffer.
