## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

*Scientific literacy empowers everyone who possesses it to be active contributors to their own health care, the quality of their food, water and air, their very interactions with their own bodies and the complex world around them… We are building on the work of the cyberpunks who came before us to ensure that a widely dispersed research community cannot be shut down… When we work it is with the betterment of the community in mind, and that includes our community, your community and the communities of people that we may never meet.*

– Meredith Patterson, *Biopunk Manifesto*.

*Biopunk* is an upper-level art and science seminar combining lectures, critical readings, hands-on workshops and DIY methodologies that teach the language, aesthetics and techniques of collaboration with biological materials. This course emphasizes current indigenous, feminist, trans /queer, science / speculative fiction theory and new materialist ecological approaches to thinking about our bodies, our environment, multispecies, weediness, ecosystems, environmental justice, the so-called Anthropocene, and the species with whom we co-habit this earth.

*Biopunk: Arts Lab Practice* introduces students to bioart, microbial life and resilient ecological practices. We use punk, queer and DIY biological laboratory techniques to creatively produce science, design and art works. Students will be required to complete readings, experiment, and create their own visions of speculative biofutures.

Using the lens of “biopunk” we think of hacker and DIT (do-it-together) approaches to manipulating materials. Influenced by the “punk” era and the beginnings of punk resistance, as well as the biohacker community, emphasis will also be placed on developing critical and transgressive thought around ethical issues, resilience, bioremediation, and cross-disciplinary experimentation in art – such as art/science collaborations, and art as research. **All readings will be online in a shared google doc. Other DIY lab resources will be made available to you in class.**

## Course Goals

### Goals:

- Draw upon and design current practices and techniques used in ‘biological art,’ biodesign, critical design, and ecological arts. Through readings, class discussion, and hands-on laboratory protocols students will be able to demonstrate this knowledge through written and visual assignments.
- Understand the possible benefits of “citizen science” and DIY science practices.
- Analyze and ask ethical questions concerning microbial life art works and ecological issues.
- Conduct research into the history and contemporary cultural investigations of the microbiome and working with living materials.
- Conduct research and comment upon contemporary new media art and speculative art that deals with living art.
- Create artworks from an interdisciplinary perspective around microbial life.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

The instructor administers an academic (grade) penalty and the student is reported to the Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education as appropriate. The first violation results in 0 grade for that assignment. The second violation results in failure of the course.

### Additional Course Specific DEI Information:

**Respect and Safe space:** In addition to common courtesy and respect toward your peers, students are asked to maintain a safe working space. If you disagree with classmates, do so respectfully, with the intention of pushing discussion forward. As well, you are strongly discouraged from using language that is racist, sexist, homophobic, transphobic, fatphobic, ableist, or otherwise hateful. Using language that is intended to hurt other students or deny their experiences (name-calling, misgendering, and in-person trolling) will not be tolerated. If people say things that make you feel unsafe (including me), please speak with me.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

**AI Class Policy:** Students should be transparent about all AI used to produce any material for this class. You are asked **not** to use AI to fully produce *any* assignment. Please also **do not** use AI to help you generate original ideas for a project. Users should disclose when AI technologies are being used.

**Center for Student Success:** If you would like help with writing assignments, visual design projects, or oral presentations, please visit the Center online to find helpful resources or to schedule an appointment. Available to all members of the Rensselaer community.
