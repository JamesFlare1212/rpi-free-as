### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

1. **Course Description**

Private applied music lessons are offered to provide a fine arts experience for credit and are focused on vocal performance and assessment in both private and group settings. This course creates an explorative environment for students to develop their voice and vocal technique with the student’s vocal identity at the center. Introductory ideas to voice science/vocal pedagogy, breathing and singing technique, languages/diction, and phonation, with the aim of making a healthy, efficient acoustic sound across a variety of vocal genres in an energizing, encouraging experience. This is an exploration of student’s unique vocal identity, guided by technique and the instructor’s insight as tools for individualized progress. In this course, students will gain the skills to give and receive technical feedback, gain performance experience, access information on voice resources, workshops, and vocal connections across campus, and engage in periodic activities throughout the semester, including shared assessment in other singers’ lessons. Certain dates are TBD. **Note: Innovative pedagogy techniques (flip the classroom, group activities, new events, attendance of others’ lessons) will supplement this year’s course offering**. Repertoire across the vocal spectrum and students new to singing are welcome. Previous singing experience is not a requirement, though the pedagogy in this course interfaces with voice ensemble participation in Concert Choir ARTS 2310.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students in the private lesson program will study to improve their sense of vocal singing identity and to musically interpret a variety of musical works and styles/genres.
- Students will commit to their own daily technical singing work to create space for greater individual expression and vocal exploration, in pedagogic solo, chamber, choral and various musical contexts.
- Students will explore application of learnings in group voice / performance settings.
- Students will improve listening skills, as well as mature in their ability to work at once independently on appropriate warm-ups, ideas, and music. as assigned and discussed in class, as well as guided self-direction.
- Students will learn to question the musical canon, and structures and systems of organization in vocal/musical history, and to explore elements of social justice in their repertoire and application of knowledge.
- Students will successfully track their progress, developing appropriate note-taking skills and complete weekly updates via a shared Google Doc.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Attention to weekly lesson notes via a shared Google Doc  
Weekly lessons per semester  
One final performance  
Required group voice sessions  
Three written assignments

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

1. **Grading and Assessment**

Grades and assessment of student progress is made based on individual pace, effort, and improvement over the course of the semester. Regular in-person assessments and feedback will be provided during workshops, along with a warmup routine and repertoire selections. Grading is based upon:

- \- In-class singing/feedback application 50%
- \- Attendance: Group Events/Lessons 20%
- \- Assignments (3) 15%
- \- Final Performance 15%

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.
