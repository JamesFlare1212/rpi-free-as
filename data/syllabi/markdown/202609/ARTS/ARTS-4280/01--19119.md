## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

In 3D Animation, the technical director covers roles including character rigging, lighting setup, custom tools, and other parts of the pipeline that involve the technical work needed to support artistic creativity. In this course we will begin with a focus on traditional rigging, covering basic and intermediate rigging techniques for characters in Maya including bipeds, facial rigs, motion capture and basic scripting in MEL and Python. We will use this knowledge as a jumping off point to explore non-standard rigs and procedural tool creation.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand the fundamentals of joints, controls, hierarchies and skin-binding
- Create original animator-friendly rigs for props and characters using best-practices workflows.
- Create rigs with facial animation and lip-sync controls
- Retarget motion capture data onto rigs
- Understand the technical needs and process of rigging and character setup for game engines
- Use scripting to enhance rigs and develop custom tools in Maya/Houdini

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Since this is a project-based course, students will be graded shortly after the conclusion of each project. The grade will be accompanied by a breakdown of how student performed according to the criteria laid out in the project guidelines.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**GRADE BREAKDOWN**  
\* This course is primarily project based. This means that you will be working across multiple class sessions on a project. 70% of your final grade will be the average grade of these projects. The remainder will be based on in-class participation. Therefore, while a large percentage of your grade is based on the outcome of your work, attendance and participation is critical.

\* Criteria for project grading will include, but is not limited to, composition, creativity, concept, craftsmanship, and presentation.

\* Completed projects are due at the beginning of class on the stated date and time.  
\* Assignments not completed on due date/time will be lowered one letter grade.  
\* Participation includes being prepared to work on projects in class, participating in discussions and critiques, and being open to constructive suggestions from the instructor and classmates.

\* Students have the option to redo or revise any project for an improved grade.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

While deciding who the "author" is in artistic production can be a thorny process, in this class, all work must be made WHOLLY by you. If there are any exceptions to this rule, I will inform you in advance.  
This means:  
\* If a model is required for a project, **you and you alone** will be doing that modeling.  
\* If a rig needs to be constructed, **you and you alone** will be the one constructing it.  
\* If a script needs to be written, **you and you alone** will write that script.  
The first infraction against this policy will result in failure of the project.  
The second infraction will result in failure of the course and a report to the Office of the Dean.
