## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is a hands-on introduction to the primary building blocks of music and musicianship in a 21st-century context. The course will explore acoustics and psychoacoustics, rhythm, pitch, harmony, melody, timbre, improvisation, composition, and music notation through engaged listening practices and creative projects. No formal musical background is expected. This course may be taken by those who need a stronger musical foundation before enrolling in Music and Sound I and II.

Students will be expected to sing, clap, and otherwise make noise. Be prepared!

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

*The Essential Dictionary of Music Notation,* Gerou T. & Lusk L., Alfred Music (1996)

## Course Goals

### Goals:

- understand fundamental concepts of acoustics and auditory perception.
- have a foundation in critical listening skills, and have a language for articulating what they hear.
- gain an understanding of the variety of roles musical notation can be used to accomplish musical goals in a variety of musical traditions.
- learn to comfortably interact with others in musical contexts, both spontaneously and with direction.
- gain an appreciation for the of the great variety of contemporary musical practices, including the acoustic, electronic, and electroacoustic realms.
- gain a familiarity with the fundamentals of Western musical practices so that they may progress on to Music and Sound I.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Music Theory topics:**

- Music & Listening frameworks
- Sound: simple and complex sounds, transients
- Rhythm & Meter
- Staff notation
- The piano keyboard
- Intervals and interval quality
- Major & Minor scales
- Triads

**Musicianship Topics:**

- singing intervals
- sight reading: rhythm
- sight reading: melody
- ear training: rhythm & meter idenitification
- ear training: interval identification
- ear training: scale identification
- ear training: dictation

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Identify the interval number and quality of any two pitches within an octave both visually and aurally.
- Analyze basic rhythmic and melodic patterns in both simple and compound meters.
- Construct and identify the four types of triads in root position from a given starting pitch.
- Construct and identify major and minor scales across treble, bass, and alto clefs using correct accidentals and key signatures.
- Transcribe into Western music notation simple rhythms and simple diatonic melodies.
- Sing diatonic intervals, and major and minor scales using scale degree numbers.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

#### **Class Participation:**

This is a hands-on, experiential course. Therefore, students are expected to participate in all class activities, including discussions, singing, rhythm exercises, improvisation, and other forms of in-class interaction. Making mistakes, struggling with the material, creative approaches, and taking risks activities is strongly encouraged and supported. Students will be graded on effort, integrity of interaction, and improvement over the course of the semester.

#### **Weekly Homework Assignments:**

Homework assignments will typically be available on LMS on Mondays at the start of class.

#### **Ear Training Exercises:**

Each week will have a number of online ear training exercises to be completed by the end of the week. See the section **Online Exercises** below for more details.

**Written Assignments:** The nature of written assignments will vary, and may consist of theory questions, melodic transcriptions, etc. They will be relatively easy if you have done the drills. They will typically be due Mondays at the beginning of class of the week following their availability. For example, Written Assignment 3 will be due on the Monday at the beginning of Week 4.
