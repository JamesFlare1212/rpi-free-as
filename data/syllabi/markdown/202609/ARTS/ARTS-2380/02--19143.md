## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is the second part of a three-semester sequence aimed at cultivating theory, creative, and musicianship skills that will enhance students’ ability to understand and create music in a diverse range of styles and media. Each unit is focused around a general technical principle which is explicated and explored through written analysis, dictation, improvisation, performance, and creative practice. Topics include speech rhythm, rhythmic feels and beats, melodic structure, modal harmony, 12-bar blues, diatonic functional harmony, partwriting, accented non-chord tones, sequences, chord extensions, and simple forms. Students will also engage in expanded listening exercises, helping them to develop and articulate their own approaches and responses to a variety of musical and sonic experiences drawn from contemporary music and sound art.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

- All course texts and materials will be provided by the instructor. Students are required to have a pencil and blank manuscript paper to use every class.
- Notated creative projects should be completed using notation software. The best notation programs are Sibelius and Dorico, but free programs like MuseScore (https://musescore.org/en) are also perfectly acceptable.
- A simple digital audio program will be necessary for some of the creative projects. A reasonably good free option is Audacity (https://www.audacityteam.org/).
- We will use MIDI keyboards in class. In order to make use of the keyboards, it is necessary to have some kind of program that can take MIDI input and produce sound. A free program that can be used for this purpose is bitKlavier (https://bitklavier.com/). A pair of closed-ear headphones or earbuds are also required.
- For extra musicianship practice, students can use programs like Earmaster (https://www.earmaster.com/).

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to do basic rhythmic, melodic, and harmonic dictation.
- Students will be able to analyze speech rhythms, melodies, chord progressions, part-writing, and simple forms.
- Students will be able to improvise and compose using the theoretical topics we cover.
- Students will be able to perform rhythms, melodies, simple keyboard parts, and multi-part textures.
- Students will develop a fluency in critical listening skills and a vocabulary for articulating what they hear.
- Students will acquire the skills necessary to advance to Music and Sound II.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Class Participation:** This course depends on engaged and active participation in all class activities, including lectures, discussions, workshops, exams, and listening exercises.

**Written Homework:** There is one written homework assignment given each unit. These will be checked in class the day they are due and are graded based on effort.

**Listening Responses**: Throughout the semester, there will be six listening exercises, each of which will be accompanied by a written response to be turned in through LMS. We will also try to discuss your responses during class time.

**Analysis/Dictation Projects**: Each unit, students will complete a take-home project with analysis and dictation components to assess their ability to apply the unit’s concepts and skills to specific repertoire.

**Creative Exercises and Projects:** Creative work provides students the opportunity to apply theoretical concepts and skills to their own creative practice. Given the scope of this class, these projects will be simple creative tasks that will be evaluated through a clearly defined set of criteria. If students want to develop their creative practice beyond these projects, they are welcome to share their work in office hours.

**Performance Checks:** For each unit, performance and improvisation skills will be evaluated during individual or group meetings. Students may be asked to prepare a rhythm or melody, rehearse with a small group of others, or improvise.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance:** Students are expected to attend all class meetings. Over the course of a semester, many unexpected conflicts and distractions can arise. If a student must miss a class meeting for any reason, they should provide the instructor with advance notice and arrange for an opportunity to catch up on missed work and material. Students may miss two classes without any penalty. Beyond that, each unexcused absence will incur a penalty of 2.5% to the final course grade.

### Other Course Policies:

**Email:** During the workweek, I will answer all course-related email within 24 hours of receipt.

**Late Work:** This course moves quickly through material and each unit’s work builds upon previously introduced topics. In order to stay on top of the course topics, it is essential that students do their work and turn it in on time. Late work can be turned in up to two weeks after it is due with a penalty of 5% per day it is late. If a student is aware of extenuating circumstances that will require them to miss an assignment’s due date, they should communicate in advance to avoid the late penalty.

**Devices:** Electronic devices, including phones, laptops, tablets, and smart watches, are ubiquitous in contemporary society. In furtherance of the learning goals of this course and in tandem with a robust participation in classroom activities, their use is encouraged. However, if at any point a student’s use of electronic devices becomes counterproductive to their learning or distracting to their fellow students, they will be asked to discontinue use and could face penalties to their participation grade.

**Accessibility Statement:** Students with diverse learning styles and needs are welcome in this course. In particular, if you have a physical, psychological, medical, or learning disability or health consideration that may impact your coursework and/or require accommodations, please feel free to approach me and/or the Disability Services for Students (dss@rpi.edu). DSS will work with you to determine what accommodations are necessary and appropriate. All information and documentation are confidential.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
