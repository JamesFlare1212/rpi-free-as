### Additional Information for the Instructor Section:

**Instructor Note**

Welcome to Managing Dynamic Organizations. In this course, you will get a sense of what it is like to enter a new work environment and begin to evaluate “what is going on here.” This is where managing and leading organizations begins. This may also be the beginnings of your Certificate to Managing Dynamic Organizations. If so, we look forward to going even more deeply into leading and managing in today’s challenging environments.

The intention of this course is to provide you with some valuable insights and tools to begin or continue your journey in management. You will explore your own leadership behaviors and practices—and use that think through how to manage your “unit” most effectively—your project, your team, your product—within the context of the organization and market around you. You will take on one of the most important tasks for managers: setting performance criteria, and then making decisions about how to allocate the resources of your unit to ensure those performance criteria are optimally met.

You will have opportunities in this course to refine your management capacities - in analyzing, in communicating, in effectively challenging and mentoring those you manage. In essence, this course is all about you building your management capacities to the fullest. Management is hard yet very rewarding—and you will have opportunity to explore the behaviors and practices that will make you the most effective manager possible. I look forward to spending the semester exploring this subject with you.

Before you get started, review the information in this document to familiarize yourself with the course, the course structure, expectations, outcomes, and the flow of work. I will be here throughout the course to help guide and mentor. Reach out to me via the Canvas Inbox at any point along the way.

## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Description**

In this course, students will review organizational alignment and assess organization health based on metrics and goals. Students will learn how to identify problems and potential root causes based on data and to make recommendations for change that will improve organizational performance. Students will also learn how to identify issues within the managed unit and take steps to improve performance.

Prerequisites: None  
Credits Allocated: 3 semester hours  
**Synchronous Sessions**  
As part of the RPI at Work model, this course offers synchronous sessions to provide you with the opportunity to connect with your instructor-mentor and classmates in a live setting. Please refer to the RPIStudio to view synchronous session dates and Zoom links.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- On successful completion of this course students will be able to: Map the organization’s value chain and evaluate components within that chain in terms of communication flow.
- Clearly articulate the role of the unit in the realization of the organization’s mission and vision.
- Express unit requirements as performance criteria in manners that can be quantified and analyzed.
- Determine Core Competencies within an organization.
- Evaluate relevant information and data within the context of the problem and ascertain relevance and degree of relevance.
- Synthesize information to draw a sound and actionable conclusion, decision, or solution to the problem, decision or research question.
- Frame a hypothesis relevant to a problem that appropriately and accurately represents the system under consideration.
- Communicate conclusions to appropriate audiences, giving compelling insight behind the reasoning and conclusions.
- Communicate with purpose and clarity in written and oral formats.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

This course is comprised of four overall projects. You will complete each project by completing multiple deliverables. The scenario, the challenge, the approach, and the deliverables for each project are detailed up front, as are the criteria (rubric) that will be used to evaluate your work. You then interact with provided learning resources and materials that will help guide you through the completion of the project. Synchronous sessions via Zoom will also assist as you complete each project. In these sessions, you will have the opportunity to discuss options and ideas with other students as well as your Mentor.

*NOTE: Unlike many other courses, this course focuses on topics for which there may be more than one right answer. I encourage you to discuss your ideas with other students in the class and leverage their experiences and insights. In managing people and organizations, it is critical to be able to listen actively and incorporate others’ ideas into presented solutions; just remember to acknowledge those that are helping you along the way and to give credit where appropriate.*

As you complete each project, you receive feedback according to the associated rubric for the project deliverables. You can use that feedback to revise your deliverable, or if you have “met the standard” for the deliverable, you can move to the next deliverable. When you have completed all four (4) projects in a successful manner, you have completed the requirements of this course.

The following list indicates the value of each project in the calculation of the overall course grade:

- **Project 1: You as a Manager– Worth 25% of overall course grade.**
- **Project 2: You, the Manager, in an Organization – Worth 25% of overall course grade.**
- **Project 3: Setting Performance Criteria – Worth 25% of overall course grade.**
- **Project 4: Diagnosing Performance – Worth 25% of overall course grade.**

## Academic Integrity

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Dean of Students office website defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty in the [RPI Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement.](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs) Please make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy may result in a penalty of failure of the assignment or of the course, depending on the severity. A grade of zero will be given on the first assignment where a violation of the policies described above is detected, and a referral to the Dean of Graduate Education. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask your faculty mentor for clarification.
