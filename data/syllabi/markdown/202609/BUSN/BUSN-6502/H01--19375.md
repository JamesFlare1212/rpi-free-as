## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Students complete the program governance started in BUSN6501 by establishing the RIO (risks, issues, and opportunities) management, stakeholder analysis, RASIC, and escalation prioritization. They are then immersed in the difficulties of program execution management and are challenged with using leadership and management skills to resolve program issues and keep a program on track, the customers satisfied, and the cost on target. Students also practice negotiating and resolving conflicts that commonly arise as costs increase, scope changes, and contracts require modification

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Lead as a program manager through behaviors and skills that can contribute to superior performance in leading a program to success.
- Deploy the organization's strategy by linking the strategic vision of the organization to the program and effectively communicating this to all members of the program teams. This strategic vision guides day-to-day decision making.
- Plan programs and evaluate their performance through the creation of program plans that utilize operational concepts and risk assessments.
- Manage program execution through the daily operations and activities to help ensure it runs smoothly, and work is completed efficiently towards the goals.
- Manage program finances and contracts through the creation, execution, and/or analysis methods of operational RIO that enhance financial performance of the program for the organization, while reducing its risk.
- Manage customer interactions through behaviors and habits that build effective relationships with the customer(s), especially when the customer(s) are external to the organization.
- Use systems thinking capacity to address complexity. Through systems thinking, the overall coordination, management, and success of a program are benefited

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

#### Evaluation Criteria

This course comprises four overall projects. You will complete each project by completing multiple deliverables. The scenario, the challenge, the approach, and the deliverables for each project are detailed upfront, as are the criteria (rubric) that will be used to evaluate your work. You then interact with the provided learning resources and materials that will help guide you through the completion of the project. Synchronous sessions via ZOOM will also assist as you complete each project. In these sessions, you will have the opportunity to discuss options and ideas with other students as well as your Mentor.

- **Project 1:** Governance **– 21%**
- **Project 2:** Stuff Happens **– 79%**

The first project is the foundation for the scenarios given to the students in the second project mimicking execution in a program. The goal is for you to successfully complete all eight (8) execution scenarios while receiving feedback along the way. Guidelines and further information about re-submissions will be covered in the details of your deliverables.

For each project and milestone, you will be given a "rubric" which simply translates to a list of the criteria your Faculty Mentor will use to evaluate your project. You can also use this list to self-evaluate your work before you submit. You will receive feedback from your Faculty Mentor if your submission can be improved.

If there are 10 elements in the rubric for a particular project, you must work toward a final deliverable that shows appropriate mastery of all 10 items - if you are good on 9, but not so good on 1, you must still achieve appropriate mastery on that project. Each milestone builds on the prior, so you want to make sure you are at the appropriate level before you move to the next. Otherwise, you may have problems with the next milestone.
