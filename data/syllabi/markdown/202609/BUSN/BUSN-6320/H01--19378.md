## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

In this course, students evaluate the formal and informal hierarchical arrangement and culture of the organization relative to its mission and vision and given its geographic dispersion. Students use evaluative tools to make recommendations that improve the arrangement of the organization and influence culture. Students model the desired culture and develop plans and policies that value the human element of the organization as a key catalyst to attaining desired organizational direction. Students learn to coach and mentor those they lead as a model to develop accountability, recognition, and respect.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Leadership: Influence and lead individuals and teams to achieve organizational objectives.
- Talent Management: Ensure achievement of organizational goals with strategic human resource policies and initiatives.
- Ethical decision-making skills: Evaluate ethical perspectives and utilize an ethical and legal framework for decision-making in multiple contexts and domains.
- Communicating effectively: Communicate with purpose and clarity to present ideas in written and oral formats.
- The RPI Analytic Framework of decision making: Use data to evaluate and respond, in context, to a problem, decision, or research question.
- Change Management and Innovation: Diagnose, prescribe, plan, and execute change (product and process) to ensure the organization achieves its objectives.
- Global Environment: Make strategic decisions in a continuously evolving global framework.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Evaluation Criteria**

This course uses a series of projects that serve as the context in which students learn and then demonstrate that learning. You’ll begin this journey by taking on the role of the VP of HR within your organization in Projects 1 - 3, eventually transitioning into the role of Chief Human Resources Officer (CHRO) in Projects 4 and 5. An overview of each project can be found below.

- **Project 1 Developing Talent**: In this project, you’ll take what you’ve learned about your EQi and apply it to mentoring an individual of your choosing. You will engage in a five-week mentorship engagement, utilizing best practices in coaching/mentoring and conducting effective meetings.
- **Project 2 - EQ Leadership: 360 Perspective**: As the VP of HR, you’ve been asked to spearhead a new 360 program within your organization that invites leaders to examine their own leadership practices. You’ll be taking an enhanced version of the EQi assessment you took as part of your first MBA course, and then selecting a few colleagues, peers, and direct reports to serve as “raters” to reflect on their perception of your EQi.
- **Project 3 - Analyzing Organizational Culture**: In Project 3, you’ll apply your insights from the EQi and mentorship exercises to closely examine the culture that exists within your organization. Note that you will begin this project concurrently with your ongoing mentorship engagement in Project 2.
- **Project 4 - Examining Rewards and Recognition Processes**: With an understanding of the elements of organizational culture at play within your workplace, you’ll then examine different rewards and recognition processes. You’ll analyze a sample dataset, then reflect on these insights as they relate to your own organization’s policies.
- Project 5 - Planning for Success(ion): In the final project, you’ll incorporate the work you’ve done throughout the course to create a succession planning framework for your organization that prioritizes retaining and cultivating talent.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

## Academic Integrity

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Dean of Students office website defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty in the [RPI Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement.](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs) Please make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. Of course, learning with and collaborating with others in the class is encouraged – work submitted must be completely your own and original. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy may result in a penalty of failure of the assignment or of the course, depending on the severity. A grade of zero will be given on the first assignment where a violation of the policies described above is detected, and a referral to the Dean of Graduate Education. If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of “F” for the course and referral to the Dean. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask your faculty mentor for clarification.
