### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

### How to Approach This Course

This course will provide you with tools, techniques, and knowledge to become an effective change agent. To accomplish this objective, you will be completing 4 projects designed to guide you through change management, discovering your and other’s leadership styles, applying your leadership style when problem-solving, and implementing a change management model. Use the following approach to this course:

1. We will have synchronous sessions in which we discuss and scope the approach. They are:
   1. **Course Introduction**: conducted during the first week of class to discuss expectations & ground rules, review course layout, and review weekly deliverables
   2. **Leadership Styles**: a simulated team kick-off meeting will be held where you will apply your learnings to identify the leadership style of each participant based on their characteristics
   3. **Design Thinking and ADKAR Change Management Model**: review of a problem-solving tool using double diamond design thinking methodology, review of ADKAR change management model, and application of ADKAR
   4. **Course Learnings**: review of course learnings and how each project contributes to leading change
2. The digital classroom will walk you through a series of steps – the approach – and provide you with the learning resources you will need right when you need them.
3. The course is broken down into projects, and each project has multiple milestones.
4. As you progress through the projects, you will prepare a series of 'deliverables' for each milestone. You will submit these deliverables in the digital classroom.
5. When you have completed the milestone, your Mentor will evaluate it according to the pre-established rubric. Your Mentor will also provide you with feedback when evaluating your deliverables.
6. Your Mentor is available to assist you at any point where and when you need it. Just reach out via email and we will schedule a time that is mutually convenient.

My role in the course is to serve as your Mentor as you work through the project. Rensselaer hires us to be Mentors based on our academic background as well as our years of being a practitioner in the field – and I still continue to actively practice in the profession as a full-time endeavor.  
Finally, I know that you are a working professional with many draws to your time and energy. I also know how big of a commitment it is to work on a graduate degree while employed – I did it myself. So, please, the key to success is communication. If you need additional time for your deliverable, please let me know. If you are struggling, do not wait until you are at the end of the milestone; reach out – we can schedule a mutual time to discuss your issue and get back on track. If you have a commitment and cannot attend a synchronous session, let me know – I will be recording and posting all sessions so you can review them prior to submitting your deliverable for the week.  
The syllabus is posted – please review and plan ahead for the synchronous sessions and the project milestones as laid out. Remember, I am here to mentor and guide you through this journey, so take advantage in developing your mastery in leaders as change agents. I hope you have an outstanding semester. And most of all, I hope you apply your learnings to your workplace!

## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Develop abilities associated with change-agent leaders, evaluating and implementing change within complex and dynamic organizations. Learn to lead high-performing teams, engage stakeholders, and communicate effectively with different stakeholder audiences. Develop and inspire others to engage in change initiatives that align closely with the organization’s desired vision and goals. Learn to engage in environmental evaluation to assess capacity for change, fostering cultural change that allows the organization to sharpen focus and perform as envisioned.  
Credits Allocated: 3 semester hours

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Effective Communication – communicate with purpose and clarity to present ideas in written and oral formats
- Critical Thinking Skills – analyze and solve complex managerial and organizational problems
- Leadership Capacities – use leadership tools to facilitate the inquiry and change processes within the organization.
- Implementation and Management of Change – use change processes to put the change(s) in place.
- Plans for Change – use project management techniques and the change management process to develop achievable plans that deliver change on time, on budget, and on specification.
- Reinforcement and Sustainment of Change – measure, evaluate, and sustain a successful change so that it becomes permanent
- Innovation Skills – practices and processes that move new ideas and inventions into a good or service that creates value for the business and its employees, including solving problems, making decisions, improving product or services quality, increasing customer loyalty, identify competitive advantages, defining long term strategies, and contributing to the success of an organization.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

## Academic Integrity

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Dean of Students office website defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty in the [RPI Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement.](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs) Please make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. Of course, learning with and collaborating with others in the class is encouraged – work submitted must be completely your own and original. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy may result in a penalty of failure of the assignment or of the course, depending on the severity. A grade of zero will be given on the first assignment where a violation of the policies described above is detected, and a referral to the Dean of Graduate Education. If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of “F” for the course and referral to the Dean. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask your faculty mentor for clarification.
