## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course is designed for students who want to gain comprehensive knowledge of Chinese calligraphy, from theory to practice. Through lectures, hands-on practices, presentations, and in-depth discussions, students will be introduced to the history and aesthetic principles of Chinese calligraphy. The course will also explore the evolution of Chinese calligraphy and its development across cultures, including contemporary and non-traditional formats.

Daily step-by-step practices and projects will guide students through the process of holding a brush and interpreting and creating their own artwork. By actively engaging in these activities, students will develop a deeper interest in and understanding of the Chinese language and its rich cultural heritage.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- By the end of this course, students will be able to: 1. Demonstrate Fundamental Skills: Execute basic techniques and skills in Chinese calligraphy, including brush handling, stroke order, and ink application. 2. Interpret aesthetic and intellectual values: Analyze and appreciate the aesthetic and intellectual values embodied in Chinese calligraphy, including the relationship between form and meaning, the expressive power of strokes, and the balance of composition. 3. Create artistic interpretation: Apply learned calligraphy techniques and concepts to develop original projects that reflect both classical traditions and personal artistic vision. 4. Understand wellness benefits of calligraphy: Articulate the physical and mental health benefits of calligraphy, including enhanced concentration, posture, mindfulness, and emotional regulation. 5. Recognize Cultural and Historical Contexts: Identify key historical periods, influential calligraphers, and the broader cultural significance of calligraphy within and beyond Chinese society. 6. Cultivate Appreciation for Artistic Diversity: Develop a respectful and inquisitive approach to diverse artistic practices, positioning calligraphy within a global artic dialogue.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic dishonesty encompasses various actions, including cheating, fabricating, or falsifying information or sources, improper collaboration, submitting the same paper for multiple classes without permission, and plagiarism. Plagiarism occurs when writers intentionally or unintentionally use someone else’s language, ideas, or materials without giving proper credit and citation. Familiarizing yourself with RPI’s Student Rights and Responsibilities regarding academic integrity is essential.

Plagiarism is defined as the act of taking credit for someone else’s work, whether done in deliberately or unintentionally. Examples of plagiarism include submitting an essay, in whole or in part, written by someone else (such as a friend or an internet source) and claiming it as your own, as well as incorporating information or ideas from research materials without properly citing the sources.

In this class, plagiarism is unacceptable. If any instances of accidental or deliberate use of someone else’s words, ideas, or work without attribution are identified, you will first receive a warning. Failure to rectify the situation may result in penalties, such as receiving a failing grade on the paper, a failing grade in the course, and/or being referred to the Dean of Students.
