## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Starting with the introduction of the probability distribution of one or two continuous random variables, basic concepts of mean, variance, covariance and correlation coefficient are covered. Description techniques discussed include stem-and-leaf plots, histograms, box plots, and probability plots. Confidence intervals for a single sample and for two samples are constructed on means and variances. The procedure of hypothesis testing is introduced conceptually followed by solving real-life biomedical problems. The design and perform engineering experiments involving a single factor are discussed, where the analysis of variance is used to extract information from data. The course also shows how to determine situations where nonparametric procedures should be applied. Several nonparametric statistics are introduced.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Student Learning Outcomes:** Successful students will be able to

4000-level

1. Compute and interpret basic descriptive statistics, summarize data effectively, and extract information from figures and tables produced by statistical software.
2. Evaluate probabilities using both discrete and continuous distributions.
3. Identify the appropriate probability distribution for a given problem and justify the choice.
4. Construct confidence intervals and perform various statistical tests without the use of software; apply fundamental techniques in power analysis; and determine when nonparametric methods are more appropriate than t-tests or ANOVA.

6000-level

Independently conduct statistical analyses on datasets, produce supporting figures and tables using statistical software (R and R studio), and effectively communicate findings in academic settings.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**A list of topics:**

- Continuous random variable: probability density function and cumulative distribution function; mean and variance; determine probability.
- Distributions: Normal, Chi-square, T, and F.
- Covariance of two continuous random variables; determine mean and variance of linear function of variables.
- Descriptive statistics and different ways to show data on graphs.
- Sampling distribution, central limit theorem, and point estimator.
- Confidence interval on mean and variance.
- Hypothesis testing on mean and variance for both one-sample and two-sample, and proportions.
- Power analysis, ANOVA, and Nonparametric tests on median.
