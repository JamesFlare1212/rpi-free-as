## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course covers the dynamics of fluid flow and transport phenomena in human physiological system. Engineering principle, fluid dynamic and bio-transport concepts will be taught in the context of cardiovascular system. Topics include: fundamentals of fluid mechanics, pulsatile flow in arteries, vascular compliance and wave propagation, impedance, cardiac mechanics, dynamic coupling of ventricle and systemic circulation, blood flow in vein, coronary circulation, microcirculation, blood flow at complex geometries, imaging techniques in clinical hemodynamic assessment, fluid mechanics in designing and testing circulatory implants, mass transport in biological systems.

**AI use policy**

This course is designed to develop students’ **critical-thinking skills, analytical abilities, and creativity**. Therefore, reliance on artificial intelligence (AI) tools to perform critical thinking, analysis, and/or creative exploration on a student’s behalf may adversely affect the learning process and educational experience.

When used appropriately, AI tools can enhance learning. They may help students identify and explore course-related content beyond materials provided in class, organize notes and course materials, and serve as a study companion by suggesting connections, explanations, or interpretations related to course concepts.

Students should recognize that generative AI tools can produce inaccurate, misleading, fabricated, or incomplete information, often presented with a tone of confidence and apparent authority. Therefore, AI-generated information should be treated as a **suggestion rather than an authoritative source** and should be independently verified using textbooks, peer-reviewed literature, course materials, or other reliable scholarly sources.

Use of AI in this course is limited and should **NOT** be used:

1. During classroom instruction sessions, unless explicitly permitted or requested by the instructor.
2. To directly generate or write text for any paper, assignment, report, project deliverable, or examination response, unless explicitly permitted by the instructor. Submitted work should reflect the student’s own thinking, analysis, and writing.
3. For class projects, assignments, examinations, or other activities for which the instructor has explicitly stated that AI use is not permitted.

Use of AI in this course is **permitted** for:

1. Identifying additional materials, references, or content relevant to classwork, projects, and/or assignments. Any sources identified through AI should be independently verified, consulted directly, and appropriately cited.
2. Translating a non-English source to assist with reading and understanding it. The **original source**, rather than the AI tool, should be appropriately referenced.
3. Creating **illustrative diagrams, schematics, conceptual figures, or images**, if they do not misrepresent experimental results, measurements, or scientific data.
4. Assisting with the **plotting or visualization of data measured, collected, or calculated by the students**. AI may be used as a tool to plot or format such data, but it may **not generate, fabricate, estimate, alter, or otherwise make up data or results** that are presented as experimentally measured or student generated.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and students should familiarize themselves with these definitions. Adherence to Rensselaer’s policy on Academic Integrity is required of all students at all times. Infractions will not be tolerated and will be prosecuted under institute policy, as described in the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities. All infractions will be reported to the Dean of Students Office.

For assignment (quiz, homework, term paper and exam) designated as “individual”, students must work independently. Students are allowed to seek help from TA and instructor, but not allowed to discuss or work with others. All work must be original and not copied or written in collaboration with other students. For assignment designated as “team”, students can work together (discuss, gather resources, share materials). Copying and pasting from published sources or the internet is considered plagiarism and is not acceptable. Computers, books, and class notes are not allowed during exams. Penalties for academic dishonesty in this class can range from a grade of zero, on the assignment of question, to failure of the class as a whole.
