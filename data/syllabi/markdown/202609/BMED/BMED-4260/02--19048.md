## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Students are introduced to the major biomedical industries, markets, and stakeholders, with an emphasis on biomedical devices. Market drivers and consideration for public health, safety, and economic factors are covered including those of regulatory, intellectual property, and reimbursement, including global considerations. The process of product development in biomedical markets is presented. The interactive class format includes case studies and examples from a practical perspective. Students select topic areas for design projects as preparation toward the goal of developing functional design solutions to practical biomedical problems.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Topics are presented as class lectures with associated readings and in-class discussions. Lecture and reading materials will provide the foundation of understanding the business of commercializing biomedical technology. Students will also work in teams, emulating the activities of a corporate product development team, focusing on a realistic commercial biomedical problem or unmet clinical need.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate an understanding of the process and considerations for bringing biomedical technologies to market.
- Demonstrate an ability to identify the major markets and drivers for development of biomedical technology and products.
- Demonstrate an understanding of the regulatory process (both US and OUS) for safely bringing a biomedical product to market.
- Demonstrate the ability to use tools such as FMEA which contribute toward safe and ethical design of biomedical products; and analyze and interpret data, and use engineering judgments to draw conclusions.
- Demonstrate an ability to create and apply a stage-gate biomedical product development program.
- Demonstrate the ability to function effectively on a team whose members together create an inclusive environment, collaborate, establish goals, organize, and plan tasks to meet common objectives.

**Course Policy on Collaboration and Cheating/Plagiarism**  
Plagiarism involves using another’s work and claiming it as one’s own. Cheating includes plagiarism, and includes additionally, but is not limited to, using or facilitating another to use work-product for a graded deliverable which is misrepresented as to authorship of the solution/ concept/ approach/ answer, or involves a breach of rules stated as part of the protocol for the assignment or exam. Collaboration on assignments, quizzes or exams is permitted only when explicitly described as permitted or required by the instructor for specific occurrences. The student will receive at least a full grade penalty, up to and including an F for the course, the full deduction being at the instructor’s discretion.  
**Mobile Devices:**  
All mobile devices (cell/smart phones, computers, pagers, etc.) must be stored securely away during lecture and are not be used unless specifically directed otherwise by the instructor. Use of (or ANY interaction with) a mobile device during an exam without explicit permission of the instructor will be interpreted as the illicit transfer of exam data, will be considered an act of cheating and will be treated as such.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

**AI use policy**

This course is designed to develop students’ **critical-thinking skills, analytical abilities, and creativity**. Therefore, reliance on artificial intelligence (AI) tools to perform critical thinking, analysis, and/or creative exploration on a student’s behalf may adversely affect the learning process and educational experience.

When used appropriately, AI tools can enhance learning. They may help students identify and explore course-related content beyond materials provided in class, organize notes and course materials, and serve as a study companion by suggesting connections, explanations, or interpretations related to course concepts.

Students should recognize that generative AI tools can produce inaccurate, misleading, fabricated, or incomplete information, often presented with a tone of confidence and apparent authority. Therefore, AI-generated information should be treated as a **suggestion rather than an authoritative source** and should be independently verified using textbooks, peer-reviewed literature, course materials, or other reliable scholarly sources.

Use of AI in this course is limited and should **NOT** be used:

1. During classroom instruction sessions, unless explicitly permitted or requested by the instructor.
2. To directly generate or write text for any paper, assignment, report, project deliverable, or examination response, unless explicitly permitted by the instructor. Submitted work should reflect the student’s own thinking, analysis, and writing.
3. For class projects, assignments, examinations, or other activities for which the instructor has explicitly stated that AI use is not permitted.

Use of AI in this course is **permitted** for:

1. Identifying additional materials, references, or content relevant to classwork, projects, and/or assignments. Any sources identified through AI should be independently verified, consulted directly, and appropriately cited.
2. Translating a non-English source to assist with reading and understanding it. The **original source**, rather than the AI tool, should be appropriately referenced.
3. Creating **illustrative diagrams, schematics, conceptual figures, or images**, if they do not misrepresent experimental results, measurements, or scientific data.
4. Assisting with the **plotting or visualization of data measured, collected, or calculated by the students**. AI may be used as a tool to plot or format such data, but it may **not generate, fabricate, estimate, alter, or otherwise make up data or results** that are presented as experimentally measured or student generated.
