### Additional Course Description :

Designed for first-semester senior Communication, Media, and Design majors, Pro-Seminar is the capstone course for the development of the Senior Project. Students will create a professional portfolio piece that showcases their creative work in the diverse disciplines of communication and media and may reference and incorporate the dual.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Role of the professor and student responsibilities  
The Proseminar professor will act primarily as a facilitator, guiding students through the process and evaluating each milestone. Additionally, students will be required to form at least one project “partnership” with a faculty mentor whose work is most closely allied with the student and with whom the student has already studied. Students and their mentors must meet every two weeks for consultation and mentors should attend mid-term and final critiques.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance at classes and participation in discussions is mandatory, and your grade will reflect these conditions. You’ll see that many sessions are designated as “Production.” These are open-ended for students to work in class or elsewhere. But all classes will start in Low 4040 and attendance will be taken. More than three missed classes (unexcused) during the semester will lower a student’s grade by one full letter grade. The instructor must be notified in advance of a possible need to miss class, with a valid excuse. You are expected to complete all milestones on time -- making deadlines is required in professional life.

### Other Course Policies:

**Participation**: Attendance is crucial in this course because much of the class is centered on dialogue, collaboration, production, and sharing within our class. Thus, participation consists of your contributions to the class, i.e., your willingness to accept and give constructive critique; and your willingness to collaborate with others to help realize both their goals as well as your own. Brainstorming and providing references, resources, and suggestions, can occur in class, outside of class, and posted in folders on the class Google Drive.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Definition of plagiarism. What is plagiarism, exactly?  
Plagiarism is representing the work of others as your own through deliberate or inadvertent omission of acknowledgments. Plagiarism and copyright infringement include copying text, graphics, video, sound or music, created by others and submitting that work as your own. Any ideas not your own (paraphrased or quoted) must be accompanied by the proper citations of your sources, which include books, articles, websites, films/videos, and  
personal communications. Standard ways of acknowledging sources include footnotes/endnotes or in-text citations (APA, Chicago, or MLA style) and full  
bibliography. These are the forms of plagiarism:  
• Direct Plagiarism: the verbatim copying of an original source without acknowledging the source  
• Paraphrase Plagiarism: the paraphrasing of ideas from another without attribution,  
causing a reader to mistake these ideas for the writer’s own  
• Plagiarism Mosaic: the borrowing of words, ideas, or data from an original source and blending this original material with one’s own writing, without acknowledging the source  
• Insufficient Acknowledgment: the partial or incomplete attribution of words, ideas, or data from an original source  
Note: ChatGPT is an especially pernicious form of plagiarism and should not be used  
for substantive composing.
