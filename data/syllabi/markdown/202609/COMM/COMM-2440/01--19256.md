## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- An important goal of this course is to educate students in “documentary literacy” – enabling them to interrogate “natural” or “commonsense” assumptions about the world by recognizing the various techniques by which documentary media construct and signal their voice and authority. At the end of the course, students will be able to: • identify various modes, genres and traditions of documentary • understand the historical development of the form. • be conversant with the major theories and debates within the field • apply the written texts to original analysis of the film texts • read the cultural and political intentions of specific documentary films and images

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

**What is plagiarism, exactly?**

Plagiarism is representing the work of others as your own through deliberate or inadvertent omission of acknowledgments. Plagiarism and copyright infringement include copying text, graphics, video, sound or music, created by others and submitting that work as your own. **Any ideas not your own (paraphrased or quoted) must be accompanied by the proper citations of your sources, which include books, articles, websites, films/videos and personal communications.** Standard ways of acknowledging sources include footnotes/endnotes or in-text citation (APA or MLA style) and full bibliography. These are the forms of plagiarism:

- Direct Plagiarism: the verbatim copying of an original source without acknowledging the source
- Paraphrased Plagiarism: the paraphrasing of ideas from another without attribution, causing a reader to mistake these ideas for the writer’s own
- Plagiarism Mosaic: the borrowing of words, ideas, or data from an original source and blending this original material with one’s own writing, without acknowledging the source
- Insufficient Acknowledgment: the partial or incomplete attribution of words, ideas, or data from an original source
