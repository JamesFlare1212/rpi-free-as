## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Provides students an opportunity to enact communication practice from a strong foundation of textual and visual communication theory. Focuses on theories of text-making and distribution across media, qualitative and quantitative approaches to communication research, and applied rhetoric.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Dainton, M., & Zelley, E. D. (2022). Applying communication theory for professional life: A practical introduction (5th Ed.). Thousand Oaks, CA: Sage.

Blackboard-Other readings required for the class will be posted on blackboard.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to articulate, evaluate, and apply communication theories.
- Students will be able to determine the contextual utility of diverse communication theories.
- Students will be able to critically analyze mediated forms of communication.
- Students will demonstrate the ability to work in a small group context.
- Students will collaboratively produce an interactive project that demonstrates communication competence.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Below is a brief description of the assignments for the course. These are intentionally brief, there are assignment sheets posted under the assignments tab on blackboard which provide more extensive detail about expectations and grading criteria.

Theory Application Proposal (10 points): Students will propose a theory discussed inside (or potentially outside) of the textbook that they will use to analyze an artifact of communication. This can include but is not limited to: speeches, advertisements, television shows, or movies. Grade will be assigned based on completion of the expectations outlined in the assignment sheet. The aim of this assignment is to assist students in developing projects.

Theory Application Paper (20 points): Students will write a paper no shorter than 1500 words that will provide a description of a theory, what it illuminates about an artifact, and what greater meaning or significance the reader should be taking from the analysis provided. More details are provided in the assignment sheet.

Theory Application Presentation (20 points): Students will prepare a brief presentation where they outline the important findings of their Theory Application Paper. Expectations are outlined in greater detail in the assignment sheet.

Campaign Messaging Group Project (50 points): The equivalent of a term project to be completed and graded collectively. The group will share the same score, so students are encouraged to work collectively on the project. Expectations for the assignment are discussed in greater detail in the assignment sheet.

Campaign Messaging Group Presentation (30 points): Students will provide a group presentation on their project. Grades will be assigned both individually and collectively.

Campaign Messaging Group Peer Evaluation (20 points): Students will submit a 500 word reflection on their personal experience working in the group and the performance of their peers in the group. Grades will be assigned individually. This assignment and its expectations are explained in greater detail in the assignment sheet.

Quizzes (50 points): Quizzes will be assigned using blackboard, and will cover materials assigned during the preceding week of courses. The quizzes can include material from the readings as well as application questions, asking you to apply the content of the class to an example of communication.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Grading Procedure:**

Each assignment will receive a proportion of points possible based on the instructor’s evaluation of student success at demonstrating knowledge/skill in keeping with course objectives. Grades will be determined based on the total number of points cumulatively earned on the assignments for the duration of the semester. Grades will not be subject to a curve or rounding, as the grade should reflect individual student knowledge and effort.

**Grade Disputes:**

Grading is a challenging form of labor and mistakes are possible. If a student believes that a mistake has been made they are asked to contact the instructor for a meeting (in person or over zoom). In the meeting students must identify specific errors in the grading process which merit a specific change in the number of points assigned. Appeals rooted in the performance of other students or past work will not be successful. Students should note: simply asking for a modification does not mean one will be made. If the instructor feels confident that the original grade was correct then no modification will be made. If this process does not provide student satisfaction, there is a further procedure for appealing grading choices which is outlined in the student handbook.

**Late Work:**

Late work will be accepted for most assignments, excluding any presentations, which must be completed on the date assigned. Late work will receive a 10% deduction per class period which it is turned in late. For example, if a student has an assignment due on Tuesday they may turn it in at any time after that class is completed until Friday when class meets again, and they will still receive up to 90% of the points available for that assignment. When an assignment is worth less than 50% of it’s original value the instructor will no longer accept it.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Absences:**

This class does not take attendance and attendance is not graded. That said, students who do not attend class regularly will not be able to effectively participate in many of the assignments and assessments, making it unlikely that a student who is frequently absent will successfully pass the class. If you cannot make class during the regularly scheduled times you are strongly advised to withdraw from the class. Below are the expectations for how occasional absences should be handled.

Foreseen absences: If a student knows of a forthcoming absence they are expected to contact the instructor of the forthcoming absence and make arrangements for the material to be made up. In some circumstances the instructor may not approve the absence because it is not in line with RPI policy for excused absences. For materials covered during excused absences, not all materials can be made up in a pedagogically equivalent manner, and circumstances may be such that the student will receive a more challenging substitute assignment.

Unforeseen absences: If a student experiences an unforeseen absence for reasons which fall in line with RPI’s policy for excused absences they will be allowed to make up the missed work with the same conditions outlined in the previous paragraph. If the unforeseen absence does not meet those criteria students are expected to contact the instructor to discuss what a reasonable accommodation and outcome would be. If the absence is both unforeseen and unexcused the student should be aware that the faculty member is under no obligation to provide an accommodation.

This policy is understood to be in keeping with RPI’s policy for absences. This policy is described in greater detail here: http://catalog.rpi.edu/content.php?catoid=13&navoid=306
