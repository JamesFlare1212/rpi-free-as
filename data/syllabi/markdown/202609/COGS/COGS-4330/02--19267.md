## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • compare and contrast the techniques (direct and indirect measures of brain activity) that are used in Cognitive Neuroscience (cognitive tasks, clinical-pathological correlations, to imaging).
- identify brain regions that are involved in the basic aspects of cognition (sensation/perception, attention, memory, emotion, executive processing, social cognition).
- communicate in scientific language about important questions in Cognitive Neuroscience.
- describe findings and applications of research in Cognitive Neuroscience through articles published in professional journals as well as other media.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Course Assessment Measures:**

***Quizzes & Exams***

LMS Quizzes (30%): There will be seven LMS quizzes consisting of multiple-choice or true/false questions from assigned readings and the lecture materials that were covered in the previous weeks (not cumulative). They will be available on LMS for 48 hours and you will have unlimited attempts. The attempt with the highest grade will be kept in the gradebook. ***There are no make-ups or extensions for LMS quizzes.*** The instructor will drop the two lowest quiz grades for each student at the end of the semester, so the LMS quiz portion of the course grade will be based on five quizzes (%6 each).

In-class short quizzes (10%): There will be recurring in-class short quizzes consisting of three to five questions. There will be a total of around 10 in-class short quizzes and taking 5 of these will be sufficient to earn the full assigned credit of 10% of the course grade.

Two Exams (60%; 30% each): There will be two exams during the semester consisting of multiple-choice questions. Questions will be on materials (e.g. lectures, readings from text, exercises) covered in class up to the exam date.

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own.
