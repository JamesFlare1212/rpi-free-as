## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will explore research findings on canine cognition and the application of these findings to dog training, behavior modification, and creating a positive dog-human bond. Topics include research paradigms, domestication, early development, socialization, communication, olfaction, emotions, training philosophies, learning theory, play, fear, stress, dopamine, neuroscience, temperament, breed differences, and more.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students will learn about the methods, challenges, and limitations of studying canine cognition and behavior. They will understand models of canine cognition deriving from the application of research methods. They will understand how scientific findings can be applied to improving the human-dog bond, especially through science-based training.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Topics include, non-exhaustively: research paradigms, domestication, early development, socialization, communication, olfaction, emotions, training philosophies, learning theory, play, fear, stress, dopamine, neuroscience, temperament, and breed differences.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students who successfully complete this course will have demonstrated that they understand: methods, challenges, and limitations of studying canine cognition and behavior; models of canine cognition deriving from the application of research methods; how scientific findings can be applied to improving the human-dog bond, especially through science-based training.
- Students will understand linguistic and psychological principles of effective communication, including associated scientific concepts and terminology.
- Students will be able to analyze specific communication situations, such as how authors of primary literature write for their peers, how professional writings can be simplified for non-professional audiences, and how professionals can teach non-professionals using videos, texts, and visual models.
- Students will be able to communicate effectively in more than one genre, including answering questions on exams and writing analytical pieces with iterations.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- All exams, quizzes, and writing assignments will be in-class, closed-book, using a pencil or pen. Illegible responses will not receive credit, so please work on your handwriting if you need to.
- Your grade will be based on the outcome of three grading blocks. Each block is comprised of an exam, which is worth most of the grade, and, optionally, various kinds of classwork. The grading blocks approach allows me to decide what we do based on what works best for, and is most interesting to, your particular group of students.
  - In-class assignments might include announced quizzes, pop quizzes, writing assignments, group assignments, small group discussions, and so on.
  - The in-class work will total between 0% and 30% of the grading block, with the exam accounting for the rest.
  - Each grading block will be worth 1/3 of the course grade.
  - Below is an example of how grading blocks might play out for a particular student in a particular run of the course.

- **Making up missed work**: You will have the opportunity to make up all missed work — both exams and class assignments — during the Final Exam slot for this course. This slot is available because there is no final exam for this course: all three exams will be taken during regular class periods. The credit for missed classwork can be earned by taking a quiz on additional readings, with the quiz score playing the same role as the original assignment’s score. Handling make-up work during the Final Exam period has the advantage of giving you ample time to deal with whatever special situation caused you to miss class and to prepare adequately. If you plan to make up missed classwork assignments (it is not obligatory — you can just accept the grading penalty), you need to let me know no later than **November 19.** Requests submitted after this date will not be accommodated.
- Exams will be cumulative. This incentivizes real learning over cramming then forgetting. It also means that there is no disadvantage to having all make-up exams at the end of the semester since all learned material must be retained throughout the semester.
- Students who leave the exam room will not be permitted to return, except under accommodations arranged in advance through disability services.
- At the end of the semester, final grades will be assigned as follows: A *= 92 or higher; A- = 91-89; B+ = 88-86;B = 85-83; B- = 82-79; C+ = 78-76; C = 75-73; C- = 72-69; D+ = 68-66; D = 65-61; F = 60 or lower.*
- The instructor reserves the right to boost the course grade for outstanding work that cannot be neatly folded into a grading policy, such as outstanding contributions to class discussions. This is solely at the instructor’s discretion and is not subject to appeal.
- Exams can contain questions of any type: T/F, multiple choice, fill in the blanks, writing responses of any length (from clause to essay), drawing diagrams, and so on. Recall will be tested.
- Examinations may include more questions than are required to be answered: for example, answer 9 of the following 10 questions. Students are responsible for their selections. No appeals regarding question clarity, wording, or interpretation will be considered for questions a student has elected to answer when alternative questions were available.
- Some questions will be graded by holistic, comparative evaluation. This means that it will be possible for a response to not receive full credit because other students’ responses were markedly better: more precise, more detailed, more insightful, better written, featuring better examples, and so on. According to RPI’s grading policy (https://registrar.rpi.edu/services/academic-records/grades), A/A- are “excellent”, B+/B/B- are “good”, C+/C/C- are “average”, D+/D are “passed” and F is “failed”. This means that if you give an average response to a question, you should be getting some version of a “C” for it. I don’t intend to be quite that strict, but I also don’t intend to engage in excessive grade inflation. This is not being punitive, it is being fair to students who learn more and perform better.
- There will be no grade restitution opportunities.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance will be taken. You are expected to attend all class sessions in full. There will be no direct penalties for missing classes but the lack of a penalty should not be interpreted as “I don’t need to attend class.” See Other Course Policies for additional information relevant to attendance.

### Other Course Policies:

- The classroom will be a technology-free zone unless otherwise instructed. Your handwritten notes will be your record of what happens in class.
- You should always bring paper, scratch paper, a pen, a pencil with an eraser, and your computer to class.
- You should keep a log of *what* you studied, for *how long*, and *how*. You will need to show this if you appeal a grade or request guidance in studying.
- You may drink from non-spill containers. No eating, please.
- If I am late to class, please wait for 30 min., then you can leave. You can use technology during that time. If you leave before 30 min., you will be counted absent.
- I will post an evolving class schedule on the LMS, which you should check regularly for updates.
- Class meetings will not be recorded and online attendance will not be available unless the entire class meeting is shifted online, as during a snowstorm. This encourages students to freely ask questions and participate in class discussions.
- If you need to miss class, you’ll need to get notes from a classmate and read/watch the materials listed as references for the class meeting.
- Class meetings will include lectures as well as assignments intended to help you to learn the lecture material and develop writing and other communication skills.
- You are welcome, and even encouraged, to use earplugs or silent headphones for the non-lecture portion of class.
- To make class run smoothly, everyone needs to work collaboratively. Please:
  - come to class on time (an occasional inconspicuous lateness is OK)
  - keep all technology stored away unless instructed to use it and, when using it, use it only for the intended purpose
  - stay until the end of class and do not start packing up your things while I am still talking
  - participate in class activities as instructed
  - calmly address me with any concerns
  - refrain from engaging in activities that will distract me or your fellow students
  - address everyone in the classroom in a polite tone.

Infringements of these standards will incur a grading penalty: a 10% deduction off the next exam score for the first instance, a 20%  
deduction for the second one, a 30% deduction for the third one, and so on.

- You are responsible for everything that happens in every class, whether or not you attend and whether or not the material is noted in any distributed class materials. Classes are dynamic learning environments, and we are not going to follow a script. For this reason, I strongly encourage you to exchange contact information with multiple classmates to share notes if anyone misses class.
- Office hours are for fielding questions and discussing issues that cannot be addressed in class. Material from missed class sessions will not be reconstructed.
- If you need to miss an exam, please contact me as soon as possible. There is no need to contact me about missing a class on non-exam days.
- For every class meeting, I will post a list of readings and videos that I used as source material. There are often a lot of materials and, in lecture, I will focus your attention on a subset of the content they cover. If you miss class, you need to read and/or watch these materials. If you attend class, you are still encouraged to read/watch the materials as review and to gain a broader and deeper understanding of the material.
- Whereas many course materials are freely available, some must be purchased or accessed through interlibrary loan. Please stay on top of this and don’t expect to be able to find everything you need at the last minute before a test.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

*All work submitted for a grade must be done exclusively by you, without the help of other humans or AI tools, unless otherwise instructed. If it is group work, then all collaborators and their roles must be listed.* Any of the following will result in a course grade of F and a report of academic dishonesty: cheating on a test or assignment, giving information about a test to someone who needs to take a make-up, or receiving information for a make-up from someone who has already taken the test.

As regards the use of AI tools specifically:

- AI may not be used for exams — nor may anything else other than one’s brain and a manual writing tool.
- AI may not be used for in-class writing assignments unless I give you a specific task and specific instructions about how to use it.
- If you choose to use AI to learn about course material outside of class, understand that it can make mistakes and that you are responsible if you learn incorrect material.

If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask me for clarification.
