## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a co-listed 4000/6000 course. The field of digital games is rapidly evolving, and game development is a complex task. A game's success depends on the level design and artificial intelligence (AI) that adapts to players' strategies and abilities. This course focuses on using machine learning techniques to automate the game design process. It covers fundamental and advanced concepts in Artificial Intelligence and Learning, including Decision Trees, Neural Networks, Genetic Algorithms, and Reinforcement Learning. Students will gain practical experience in applying these techniques to computer games. Additionally, the course will explore psychological theories and research related to decision-making and emotions, and how they impact players' experiences. The course format will include lectures, student presentations, class discussions, and independent study. Students will develop critical thinking and analytical skills through constructive critiques of their own and others' work.

Students may not receive credit for both the 4000 level and 6000 level versions of this course.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Gain knowledge about the core AI algorithms used in games, including genetic algorithm, reinforcement learning and Monte Carlo tree search
- Gain skills in implementing and using the above algorithms
- Gain knowledge about player modeling and HCI for games
- Gain skills in reading and presenting research papers.
- Practice constructive criticism on others and own work.
- [6430] Know the state of the art in game AI research
- [6430] Practice in self-directed learning

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The course grade will be based on assignments, presentations, exams and class participation

**Assignments:**

Each assignment may contain multiple parts. Unless specified otherwise, all assignments should be completed in a group.

For programming assignments, you need to make your project ready to compile/run, clearly document your codes, and supply a readme file. The readme file should describe the project, list and describe the included files and the main functions, and explain why you think your algorithm will work for the problem.

Grades for homework will be available on the LMS, and if you wish to dispute your scores, please reach out to me within two weeks of the posting.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Class Participation: 20 points (10 points for attending classes/discussions;10 points for participating in exercises in classes)  
Homework (4-5 projects which will be broken down into smaller parts): 40 points  
Paper reading and Presentations (including surveys): 15 points  
Final Project 30 points  
Total: 105 points The letter grade will be assigned based on a total of 100 points, which means you all have 5 extra points by default.  
COGS-6430 Students: Students in the 6000 level need to finish the same assignments as in the 4000 level. The total grades for all assignments and attendance will be scaled down to count for 75% of the final grade. In addition to the shared coursework, 6000-level students must also submit an extra survey paper on AI competitions. This paper should provide an overview of the latest techniques in the field and will contribute 25% to the final grade.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
