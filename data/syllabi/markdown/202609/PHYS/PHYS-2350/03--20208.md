## Course Goals

### Goals:

The goals of this course are designed to apply techniques in experimental physics to acquisition and manipulation of large data sets and to perform sophisticated data analysis. These goals include:

1. Observation and collection of experimental data;
2. Practical experience in working with and manipulating common laboratory equipment;
3. Fundamentals of error and uncertainty in scientific measurements;
4. Computational skills for basic data analysis
5. Written and oral presentation of results

## Course Content

### Content Details:

This course consists of lectures, hands-on experience of physics experiments, and preparation and presentation of written and oral reports. This course is taught differently than most other courses at RPI in that it stresses independent work and self-motivation. The written instruction covers the minimum necessary instruction to conduct the experiments, and student-initiated gathering of further information is expected.

A group of two students will complete six experiments (Electronics Lab + 5 Experimental Labs) during the course. Lab partners are chosen before or during the initial class meeting and complete all labs together in subsequent class meetings.

For preparation of the laboratory work, students are asked to read all pre-lab materials, including the lab instructions and theory prior to the scheduled lab. There will be a pre-lab quiz before each experiment with 4 questions testing students’ preparation There will be a pre-lab quiz before each experiment with 4 questions, which will count for 10% of your final grade.

The instructions on the lab notes are a minimum requirement of the labs. Exploring further steps of each experiment beyond the instructions in the notes is expected. **During the experiments, we will instruct more about “what” to do (e.g. principles), but much less about “how” to do it (e.g. which button to push and why).** It is an important problem solving training to find a way to take the desired data based on the information you have and through the logical thinking process when you are in the situation where you have never been before. A separate document that will accompany the Instructions Manual will describe the theory behind the experiment. This is a concise write up but has sufficient information to guide you to an understanding of the experiment.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Communicate clearly with your lab partners, prior to, during, and after each experiment. You cannot do these assignments on your own.
- Communicate your results to the outside world in three different ways: lab notebooks, formal lab reports and a final oral presentation
- Present effectively on scientific measurements

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The **grades** you will receive on the lab notebooks, formal lab reports, and final presentation **are the** **metrics for all three CLOs listed above**. You will receive continuous feedback on what you present that will teach you how to effectively present scientific measurements to peers.

This course is communication intensive. It addresses important aspects of interpersonal scientific communication, i.e. how to effectively present scientific measurements to peers. Firstly, students learn to communicate clearly with labpartners, prior to, during, and after each experiment. Secondly, students learn to communicate experimental observationsto peers using different approaches. The qualities of the written lab notebook, the Jupyter Notebooks, the final Report, and final oral presentation represent the metric for assessing the communication intensive designation of the course.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

#### This course follows the definitions and procedures for academic integrity violation (academic dishonesty) as outlined in the Handbook of Students’ Rights and Responsibilities.

Working together on conducting the experiment, collecting data, and analyzing date is permitted and expected among lab partners. Working together means preparing properly, performing an equal share of the laboratory work, and discussing ideas openly. Students are permitted to discuss any aspect of the experiments with anyone.

Students are required to engage actively in the experiment within the limits of their abilities. ***Substitution***, i.e. utilizing a proxy or acting as a proxy when conducting the experiment ***is considered an academic integrity violation*** and will result in zero points for the assignment.

All assignments (homeworks, Jupyter notebooks, final report, oral report) are handed in individually. Lab partners musthand in separate and different write-ups. ***Copying*** your partner’s laboratory notes, data analysis and writing, or copying from the internet or an existing reference ***is considered an academic integrity violation*** and will result in zero points for the assignment.

***Fabrication*** of data and ***falsification*** of lab records are ***considered an academic integrity violation*** and will result in zero points for the assignment. However, in the unfortunate case where data was compromised as a result of honest errorsand the analysis proves fruitless, students are allowed to use data from another source with instructor permission provided this is cited appropriately in the text. You will need to include in the Appendix portion of the report, the original erroneous data and the accompanying analysis to provide to the grader a convincing “paper trail” of the original experiment.

***Collaboration*** on prelab quizzes, or copying prelab quiz results from another student, the internet or an existing reference are ***considered an academic integrity violation*** and will result in zero points for the quiz.

***Taking digital images of the quizzes*** with smartphones/notepads/cameras and supplying the digital images to other students or ***using the digital images of prelab quizzes taken by other students*** are considered as copying, ***are treated as an academic integrity violation***, and will result in zero points for the quiz.

**Use of Artificial Intelligence in PHYS2350**

Students ***are permitted*** to use Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools to learn how to convert Jupyter Notebooks into pdf files, as well as generate schematics, images, and Python code. The AI tools used for these purposes has to be properly documented and credited in the Jupyter Notebook, final report, and oral presentation. Students use AI tools at their own risk.

Students ***are not permitted*** to use AI to generate text for Jupyter Notebooks, the final report, and the oral presentation. Also, students are not permitted to use AI to generate solutions to homework problem sets Violations will be treated as academic dishonesty.
