## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course is an introduction to theoretical and experimental solid state physics. Symmetry in molecules and crystals. Wave mechanics in the perfect crystal and nanostructured materials.  
Diffraction, electrons, phonons and electrical properties of metals and semiconductors.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

## Course Content

### Content Details:

*List of Topics to be Covered in the Course:*

1.- Symmetry in crystals and molecules (Atkins and de Paula, Physical Chemistry, Chapter 11).

2.- Structure of Crystals (Kittel, Chapter 1).

3.- Diffraction and Reciprocal Space (Kittel, Chapter 2).

4.- Crystal Binding (Kittel, Chapter 3 not including elastic constants part).

5.- Free Electron Fermi Gas (Kittel, Chapter 6).

6.- Energy bands (Kittel, Chapter 7).

7.- Phonons I: Crystal Vibrations (Kittel, Chapter 4).

8.- Phonons II: Thermal Properties of Crystals (Kittel, Chapter 5).

9.- Semiconductor Crystals (Kittel, Chapter 8).

10.- Nanostructures (Personal Notes).

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Student will be able to use the symmetry operations of crystals to elucidate their structure and some properties.
- Students will be able to apply the concepts of diffraction to calculate diffraction patterns of crystals and to compare experimental diffraction patterns.
- Students will be able to apply wave mechanics concepts to calculate the electronic band structure and phonon dispersions of simple crystals; this information will be very useful to interpret experimental results in crystals.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

In this course there will be 2 exams, homework, activities, and a group project with presentation.

Exams will take place in person in the classroom.

**First exam includes topics: 1,2,3, 4,5. Exam on October 20th, 2026**

**Second exam includes topics: 6,7, 8,9,10. Exam on December 4th, 2026**

*Homework assignments*:

Homework assignments are due on the date indicated, ***at the start of class at 12:20pm***. Late homework will not be accepted without prior approval from the instructor and if accepted may have a grade penalty.

*Projects:*

Projects will be assigned and students will work in groups: The topics of the projects will be: X-Rays characterization, scanning and transmission electron microscopy, Raman spectroscopy, Computer simulation of 2-D materials with curvature and 3-D printing, layered materials and nanostructures to fight the virus which cause COVID-19 (See below).

**Project Due Date the: December 8th 2024****.**

**Presentations on December 8th (15 minutes and 5 min. questions).**

**Projects will be carried out in groups of 3 students and the topics will be:**

1.- X-Rays. Results from an actual sample will be given to carry out the characterization and a report (article like) and a presentation must be made.

2.- Scanning Electron Microscopy and Transmission Electron Microscopy (SEM-TEM). Results of an actual sample will be given to carry out the characterization and a report (article like) and a presentation must be made.

3.- Raman Spectroscopy. Results from an actual sample will be given to carry out the characterization and a report (article like) and a presentation must be made.

4.- 2-D carbon materials with curvature: Coordinates of different structures will be provided to carry out theoretical analysis such as symmetry, X-Rays, Genus, etc. A report (article like) and a presentation must be made. 3-D printing of two of the structures should be generated and will be checked online since the course is online and there is no access to 3-D printing facilities.

5.- Bulk and Monolayers of transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDS). Electronic, magnetic and optical properties. Coordinates need to be found in the databases, X-Rays need to be calculated and discussion on the symmetry, electronic, magnetic and optical properties should be given.

6.- Single Photon Emission

In an atmosphere of academic integrity, students and teachers are on the same team trying to achieve the same learning objectives.

Collaboration and discussion **are allowed** **and encouraged** in the homework, the project and in-class activities. However, you must write-up your own solution, as the results of synthesizing discussions with others, and not merely copying others’ solutions. On exams, you are on your own and not allowed to discuss anything with your classmates. Thus, don’t copy someone else’s homework, in-class activities, and quizzes and don’t cheat on exams. If I suspect you of either, I’ll ask for an explanation. If your explanation is unsatisfactory, you’ll be given a grade of **0** (zero) and reported to the Dean of Students.
