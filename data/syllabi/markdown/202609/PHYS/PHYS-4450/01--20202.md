## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

In physics we encounter group theory in many areas. May it be in the

symmetries of space-time, crystalline structures, the quantum mechanics of spin, particle physics,

the Standard Model, or Grand Unifying Theories. This course will introduce group theory to the

student with application to the aforementioned topics. This course is not focused on be mathemati-

cal rigor, but will rely more on physical intuition and will be aimed at practicality and concreteness.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

“Group Theory in a Nutshell for Physicists”, A. Zee, ISBN: 9780691162690

## Course Goals

### Goals:

By the end of the course, and upon satisfactory completion of all HW assignments, class activi-

ties and exams, the students will have an elementary understanding of Group Theory and, more

importantly, its applications in physics.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Examine given sets and operations, if they exhibit group structure.
- Test representations for irreducibility.
- Calculate character tables.
- Demonstrate the use of special orthogonal groups in physics.
- Demonstrate the use of special unitary groups in physics.
- Relate SO(N ) and SU (N ).
- Demonstrate the connection between Lie groups and their algebras to continuous transformations in physics.
- Use gauge groups to understand the fundamental interactions and particle physics.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Learning will be assessed using a combination of homework assignments and exams, as described elsewhere in this

syllabus.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Reading Assignments:

There will be a reading assignment in advance of every class period. In order to receive credit

for the reading assignment, the student has to submit a question on the topic to the instructor

through LMS no later than 6:00 pm the day before the scheduled class time. The question has to

be substantial, e.g. ”Can you explain equation XYZ?” will not receive credit. The lowest reading

assignment score will be dropped to compute your final grade in the course. Therefore, if you can

miss one reading assignment without a penalty. The reading assignment will account for 20% of

the final grade.

Homework:

There will be a homework assignment every week. Homework assignment are due at the beginning

of class time. Homework solutions are to be submitted in whatever hard copy format you prefer as

long as it is legible. Late homework will be marked zero. The homework will account for 20% of

the final grade.

Use LLMs responsibly! It is assumed that you have a clear and full understanding of any submitted

work. If there is evidence to the contrary, you will be asked to defend your work, and your score

might be adjusted depending on the outcome of the defense.

Exams:

There will be 2 oral exams: the first one on or around October 16, the second one on or around the

last day of class, December 11. The better exam score will count double. So your exam average

will be calculated from effectively 3 exam scores. The exams will account for 60% of the final grade.

Grades:

The composition of your final grade will be 20% homework, 20% reading assignments, and 60%

exams. Letter grades will be determined as follows

• 90%-100%: A

• 80%-90%: B

• 70%-80%: C

• 60%-70%: D

2• 0%-60%: F

Grade Modifiers (i.e., ’+’ or ’-’) may be used when entering final course grades, if appropriate.
