## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

An introduction to quantum physics principles and the Schrodinger equation. Solutions to the time independent Schrodinger equation in one, two and three dimensions, including one electron atoms.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Quantum Physics: A Fundamental Approach to Modern Physics J. Townsend, (University Science Books, 2010) ISBN 978-1-891389-62-7

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The successful student will be able to apply concepts in quantum physics to understand and solve standard problems in quantum physics including one-dimensional and spherical potentials.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- By the end of this course, students will be able to solve introductory quantum physics problems and be able to apply core quantum concepts required for advanced coursework and research.
- Through the completion of this course, students will be able to model basic quantum systems algebraically and graphically, and interpret these systems through both mathematical and intuitive lenses.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The student's understanding will gauged through homework, projects, and exams. There are 9 homework sets which each have a common foundational theme for introductory quantum physics. The projects will involve using graphically technology (particularly Desmos) to illustrate traditional quantum systems in an intuitive graphical way. The exams will test whether the student understands the concepts and can be able to solve problems similar to in-class problems and homework problems.

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

**In-Class Examinations**: For exams, you will have the whole class period to take the exams and you are allowed to bring a double-sided equation sheet. Exams will consist of 5 questions short questions (8 pts each) and 4 longer questions (15 pts each) which are related to example and homework problems. You have the opportunity to show your work for potential partial credit. The topics which will be covered on the exam will be covered in the Exam Reviews.  
**Projects:** There will be three projects, which involve using computation to solve.  
**Homework:** Nine homework assignments with problems selected from the textbook will be due at the beginning of the class at 10 am on the specified dates. There will be two HW assignments for Chs. 1, 2, & 4, but Chs. 3, 5, & 6 have one HW each. (-10% per day late, applies to projects too). Unexcused absences deduct minimum 3% and up to 15% from the HW grade based on frequency. If a student misses class twice in a row or beyond four total times without notifying the instructor, an email request for reasons will be sent to potentially prevent this deduction.  
**Take-Home Final:** The take-home final is a mix of an exam & project and will be due on will be focused mainly on Chapter 6 and have a focus mainly on the hydrogen atom, deriving spherical harmonics, and applications of Pauli matrices. **This final is due by 10 am on 12/21/2026.**
