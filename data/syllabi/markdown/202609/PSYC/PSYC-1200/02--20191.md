## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

An introduction to the general field of psychology for undergraduate students of any major. Topics include the history of psychology, research methods, biological aspects of behavior, sensation and perception, learning and memory, cognition and language, consciousness and cognitive abilities, motivation and emotion, human development, personality, psychological disorders and their treatment, and the social aspects of behavior.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Define and discuss basic psychological terminology, concepts, and theoretical paradigms
- Apply psychological principles to everyday life experiences and to other disciplines
- Understand how psychological evidence is gathered and use scientific reasoning to critically analyze psychological knowledge
- Express psychological concepts and arguments effectively in both oral and written communication
- Reflect on how socio-cultural and contextual issues shape human behavior and how psychology can contribute to promoting positive societal outcomes
- Demonstrate effective teamwork skills through collaborating with classmates
- Create policies to bring forth applications of the principles learned, at the societal level, that account for a true understanding of human psychological needs and expressions.
- Create a product prototype that serves the US public’s psychological interests.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Quizzes 50% -** Four multiple choice questions covering several chapters in the text and any supplemental material posted on the slides. To be taken in class on the day indicated in the syllabus. The quiz will be taken in class via the LMS system. Each quiz is worth 12.5% of the student’s grade.

**State Project Assignment 40% -** Students will be put into teams of 5 people where they will create policies for implementation of psychological research, teaching, and practice of psychology in for one of the US States. Across a series of assignments, students will address issues such as addiction treatment policies, improving psychological development in children and old persons, and with their group will design and create a real-world solution, to be presented in a showcase at the end, for educating or helping others in their state with a specific psychological issue. Assignments 1 – 3 are worth 8% of students’ course grade. Assignment 4, a presentation of each group’s real-world solution, is worth 16% of students’ course grade. The rubric of requirements for each assignment will be posted to the LMS. The real-world solutions are presented as a Poster and Prototype demonstration which will take place during finals period at a pre-determined time and place as determined by the registrar’s office. An independent panel of judges will evaluate each team’s poster and prototype on the rubric of criteria to be posted on LMS.

**Research Participation 10% -** The student is required to sign up for and participate in four different research studies, of their choosing, offered by the faculty in the cognitive science department. Individual studies must total one hour in participation time and be in person. Each research study is worth 2.5% of the student’s grade.

**Instructor’s AI policy:** As an instructor, I believe that AI should not be used without first understanding the fundamentals of a concept or topic. Once you know “how” to do something, assistance with AI is a fair use of the resources available to you. However, if you would like to use AI as a starting point for an assignment, such as outlining, brainstorming, and planning, you will need to disclose which AI software you used and how it was used for the assignment.

Please cite the AI-generated material in-text (i.e., APA style in-text citations). You will also need to demonstrate that you have created sufficient content above and beyond (i.e., at least 65%) what the AI generated. Please note that AI doesn’t know any better and hallucinates, or fabricates, information, such as citations for research articles that don’t exist.

It is your responsibility to verify that the content generated by AI is true. You can do this by providing links to the research articles generated by AI in your references section. AI-generated text and references must be credited appropriately. Authors and/or creators, and not the AI tool(s), are accountable for their work, its originality, accuracy, and integrity.
