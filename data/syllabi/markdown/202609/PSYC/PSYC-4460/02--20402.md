## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course examines in detail theories and studies about the acquisition, storage, and retrieval processes of knowledge. Major topics are working memory, sensory and short-term memory, information coding and retrieval processes, implicit and explicit memory, long-term memory, knowledge representation, memory for space and time, reconstructing processes in memory, eye-witness memory, memory deficits, neural bases and computational models of memory.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Analyze human memory processes at an advanced level
- • Recognize how different studies in the literature of empirical and theoretical research increased our scientific knowledge of memory processes
- • Express knowledge and informed opinions on the field of human memory in both written and verbal form
- • Apply this knowledge in a class project

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Course Assessment Measures:  
Assignments  
AI Writing tools are not permitted for any stage or phase of work in this class. If you use these tools, your actions would be considered academically dishonest and a violation of the Academic Integrity Policy.  
Reading Responses:  
Reading responses will consist of your thoughts, reactions, and/or questions to readings and class discussions, which you will submit on a Discussion Board on LMS by 1:00 pm on class day (or before). These responses must be your original thoughts or questions! You are smarter than AI :)  
Your reading responses may be as long (or short) as you wish (a few sentences or a question, a few paragraphs or a whole page), but it must show evidence that you read and thought about at least a part of the assigned reading. To get credit for reading responses, you must submit at least 7 reading responses throughout the semester.  
In-class Group Meetings:  
You will form groups of two or three to work together on an article presentation and an experimental project (details of which are below). At the end of each class, the instructor will work with each group to narrow down topics for the article presentation and the experimental project. To get credit for the group meetings, all members of a group must attend at least 5 meetings throughout the semester.  
Group Experimental Project:  
Students groups will carry out a research project. For the project, with the instructor’s help and approval, the groups will design and conduct a pilot experiment on a certain memory process. The instructor will help each group choose a topic and design their experiment, possibly creating novel versions of tasks from classic experiments. In the end of the semester, you will report and interpret your results in an 8-minute conference-style presentation.  
Group Article Presentation:  
Before the pilot experiment is conducted, each project group will present and lead class discussion on a research article, which will be related to the experimental project.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
