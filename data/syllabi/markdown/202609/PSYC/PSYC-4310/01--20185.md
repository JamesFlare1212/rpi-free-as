## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is the second in a two-semester sequence on research methods and statistics in the behavioral sciences. It covers approaches to statistical modeling and inference that lie within the null hypothesis significance testing (NHST) framework as well as alternative frameworks. Students will learn how to analyze various kinds of data using linear regression and analysis of variance and gain proficiency with the R programming language and software environment for statistical computing. Each student will carry out a research project and prepare a formal report that comports with the guidelines from the American Psychological Association.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to select appropriate statistical methods and properly apply statistical methods to evaluate data using the R programming language and software environment.
- Students will be able to apply best practices for conducting reproducible research.
- Students will be able to effectively communicate the results of statistical analyses in APA format.
- (PSYC 4310) Students working collaboratively will be able to contribute to the literature review, design, execution, analysis, and interpretation of a research project by taking responsibility for specific components of the research process and integrating their work into a group study that tests hypotheses related to behavior or mental processes.
- (COGS 6310) Students will be able to independently conduct a research study that integrates literature review, design, data collection or use of existing data set, statistical analysis, and interpretation to test hypotheses related to behavior or mental processes.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Collaboration**  
In this class, you will be given a number of assignments. You are expected to work individually on all assignments unless directed otherwise by the instructor. All analyses and write-ups should be your independent work.

Problems will be sufficiently open-ended that identical solutions or write-up language (especially if the results are incorrect!) will be viewed as possible plagiarism. To be formal about it, plagiarism means passing off the statistical designs, R code, or written results of others as one's own work. Any student who engages in plagiarism or any other form of academic dishonesty will receive zero credit for the assignment or exam, and will be reported to the Dean of Students for further disciplinary action. The Rensselaer Handbook defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All of these forms are violations of trust between students and teachers. Please familiarize yourself with this portion of the handbook. If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

**AI usage policy**

You are allowed to use internet resources and AI tools (such as ChatGPT and Claude) to assist in completing homework assignments and analyzing data for your project. However, AI tools should be used to assist not replace your statistical reasoning. An important goal of this course is for you to develop statistical reasoning skills and basic literacy in R. Quizzes and exams may assess these skills without access to AI or other outside resources. You may, for example, be asked to interpret a short piece of R code, identify an error or inappropriate analysis, select appropriate code for a particular analysis, or make a simple modification to existing code.

You may use AI to help you implement analyses in R when completing homework assignments and for the research project. Appropriate uses include asking AI to:

- help you remember or find R syntax
- explain an R function or piece of code
- help diagnose an error message
- suggest ways to modify code to accomplish a particular task
- generate code to implement an analysis that you have identified as appropriate
- explain unfamiliar aspects of R output

You remain responsible for all work that you submit. You should understand the code you submit, be able to explain what it does, and determine whether the analysis and interpretation are appropriate.

**Sharing course materials**

You may not upload or share course materials in a way that makes them accessible to other current or future students. This includes assignment, quiz, and exam questions, solutions, answer keys, or other restricted course materials. You may not post such content to online repositories, discussion forums, Discord servers, social media, or similar services. When using an AI tool, do not provide restricted course materials when doing so would cause those materials to be stored, published, shared, or made accessible to others. When possible, describe the statistical or R problem in your own words rather than copying course materials into an external service.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

**COURSE FORMAT**

The course format will be a combination of lecture and in-class assignment. For some topics, lectures will be short and will only serve to introduce a topic, with the rest of the class period being used to start the assignment. For other topics, I will lecture for most of the class period. After class, you should read the corresponding chapter of the textbook. A quiz assessing your understanding of the material covered in the last 2-4 classes will be given at the beginning of certain class period (see course schedule). There will also be some time set aside for working on the research project.

**EXAMS AND QUIZZES**

There will be two exams in this course. Exams will include a mix of multiple choice questions and questions that require written responses. Exam #2 will not be cumulative -- it will focus on material since Exam #1. Quizzes will comprise multiple choice questions and will assess your understanding of the material covered in the previous 2-4 classes. Quizzes will be given at the very beginning of six of the classes, so it is important that you arrive on time. If you do not complete a quiz before the window closes, you will receive a zero for that quiz. Quizzes may not be rescheduled (no exceptions) but your lowest quiz grade will be dropped. During exams and quizzes, you may refer to one sheet of hand-written notes that you prepared. You may not refer to other notes, the textbook, or the internet.

**ASSIGNMENTS**

Learning how to analyze and interpret data requires extensive practice working with a wide variety of data sets. As such, you will need to spend significant time both in class and outside of class practicing the statistical techniques that you read about and using R to explore, analyze, and interpret data. In general, students who come to class prepared to work should be able to complete a significant portion of each assignment before the end of class. Due dates for each assignment will be given at the time that the assignment is made available. Extensions beyond the due date will be given only under exceptional circumstances and with prior permission. Assignments that are submitted after the due date will incur a 10% penalty per day.

**RESEARCH PROJECT**

Students will work in small groups of 3-5 (PSYC 4310) or individually (COGS 6310) to complete a research project on some topic of their interest related to psychological science. Some class time will be set aside for students to work on their projects, but most of the work will be done outside of class. Each group (PSYC 4310) or individual (COGS 6310) will submit an idea paper, a proposal, a pre-registration plan, and a pre-recorded presentation on their project. More details about the research project, including a project milestones and timeline, will be provided in mid-September.
