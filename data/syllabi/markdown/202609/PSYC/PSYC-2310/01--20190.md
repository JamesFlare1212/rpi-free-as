## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Description:** Research Methods and Statistics I is the first part of a three-course series required for psychological science majors and Part I of a two-part requirement for cognitive science majors. It is the job of the instructor to provide students with a firm foundation in designing psychological research methodology and an understanding of the statistics needed to analyze and interpret quantitative research data.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

The Process of Research in Psychology, Dawn McBride

The Process of Statistical Analysis in Psychology, Dawn McBride

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Goals/Learning Outcomes:** Students will gain an understanding of introductory psychological research concepts and when to apply certain tests and measures to sets of quantitative data. Students will also learn how to conduct and interpret these measures using R programming.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- How to design research methodology to investigate various psychological research phenomena.
- To determine which statistical tests should be applied given the type of research data being analyzed.
- The importance of ethics of psychological research, and how to report the outcomes of psychological research studies.
- To implement a variety of statistical tests using R.
- To recognize the value of calculating the value of p, determining confidence intervals, and identifying suspected statistical errors.
- To communicate scientific content effectively in written and oral formats.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Quizzes 50%** – Each quiz is worth 12.5% of your total grade. Quizzes will take place in class and will consist of 15-20 multiple choice questions.

**Research project 30%** – Students are expected to design and implement their own research study (software will be provided) and then write up and report the results in a class presentation to be made during the last week of class. You will work in groups of 3 or 4 and you and your group members may do a survey, a true experiment, a correlational study, whatever you and your group members prefer. More details will be provided as the course proceeds. Generally speaking, the project will be graded based on the following components, each of which constitutes a separate assignment:

- **Research Proposal (8%)** – **Project part 1** – Student groups will propose a research study to be completed for the course including a research question, literature review, experimental design, and methodology. The topic must be approved by the instructor in advance prior to beginning work on the assignment. Students will be graded on the strength and novelty of their research hypothesis, and the appropriateness and robustness of their research methodology. A rubric will be posted on Blackboard.
- **Statistical analysis and interpretation (8%**) – **Project part 2** – students will analyze the data they are using for their project and interpret the results. Students should submit all supporting datasets and outputs and will be graded based on the appropriateness and thoroughness of their analysis, and the accuracy of their interpretation. Please see the rubric to be posted.
- **Presentation of results (8%)** – **Project part 3** – students will present their full mini-project during a 10–15-minute oral presentation given in the last week of class. Students will be graded based on the clarity thoroughness, and level of engagement in their presentations. Please see the rubric to be posted.
- **Final paper (6%)** – Students will submit their final paper with all feedback and instructed changes incorporated on the last day of class.

**Class participation 10%** - In-class laboratory-based assignments will be due some weeks (see the course schedule). Please note that these are to be brief, but complete, laboratory reports, with an abstract, summary of analysis, graphs/tables, and discussion/conclusion of approximately 1-3 pages. (Templates will be posted for guidance)

**Attendance 10%** – Class performance is directly correlated with academic success, especially in a class such as this with nuanced concepts and layers of information that must be learned. There are only about 24 instruction classes in total, attending daily and arriving promptly is an easy way to guarantee a full 10% and will also improve the likelihood of receiving a good grade. We will use iClicker to record attendance during class (link above).

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance policy:** Attendance is a required portion of the class which, as outlined previously, is worth 10% of your grade. Attendance is taken during each lecture session via iClicker at some point during the session as announced by the instructor. Attendance is not taken on quiz days because of the number of students taking the test in the testing center. If you are going to be sick, or have some other unavoidable emergency, you may email the professor and/or TA before the class session begins to inform them you will be absent. Your absence will be excused and you do not need a doctor's note. However, emails must be sent before class starts for your absence to be excused. If you are a member of a sports team and will need to miss all or part of some class sessions, please submit your documentation along with the schedule of days and times you will be absent. The attendance grade is calculated by dividing the number of days attended by the total number of attendance days. The number of days attended also includes days in which the student emailed the professor or TA in advance notifying them of an absence and only does not include unexcused absences. Quiz days are not included at all in the calculation. Please email the professor or TA if you have further questions.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
