## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Throughout the day we tend to spend much of our time thinking about the past and the future. Mindfulness involves giving particular attention to our moment-to-moment experiences in a way that emphasizes observing, rather than judging or evaluating. Everyday activities such as driving a car or eating a meal provide us the opportunity to practice this focused, moment-to-moment attention. In addition, we can practice mindfulness through formal meditation. There is empirical evidence of the many health benefits of mindfulness meditation, including the potential to produce fundamental changes in brain structure. In this class, we will discuss the psychological foundations of mindfulness and empirical findings supporting its positive effects. Students will participate in structured exercises. This course is a very hands-on approach to mindfulness…you will be required to practice mindfulness and meditation and develop your mindful skills. It is also more of a discussion course, so be prepared to talk in class.

***Note about Communication Intensive Course***: This is a communication intensive course. Therefore, you need to be prepared to communicate in class frequently.

We will be employing the Koru Mindfulness program. This is a program specifically designed for college students. I am merging this program into our Psychology of Mindfulness course.

Listening to the bamboo is a way to clear our minds of the thing we are worried about so we can think of a way to move forward.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to define and describe mindfulness and distinguish it from relaxation
- Students will be able to explain the impact of mindfulness on psychological variable such as anxiety and depression.
- Students will be able to demonstrate and reflect on basic mindfulness practices.
- Students will be able to apply mindfulness to everyday psychological experiences (e.g., stress, challenging emotions, interpersonal relationships, and academic challenges).

Well-Being Improvement Project used to assess knowledge of what mindfulness is (and isn't) along with extent to which they have improved on various well-being assessment measures

**Academic Integrity and Plagiarism**

Academic integrity is a fundamental expectation in this psychology course and in the scientific study of behavior. All written work submitted in this class must reflect **your own original thinking**, while appropriately acknowledging the ideas and research of others using **APA citation format**.

**Plagiarism is defined as presenting another person’s words, ideas, data, or theoretical arguments as your own.** In a writing-intensive psychology course, this includes—but is not limited to—the following:

- Copying or closely paraphrasing text from journal articles, textbooks, websites, or other sources without proper APA citation
- Failing to use quotation marks for direct quotes, even when a citation is provided
- Submitting summaries or paraphrases that too closely mirror the original wording or structure of the source
- Using research papers, literature reviews, or assignments written by others or obtained online
- Reusing your own work from another course (self-plagiarism) without prior written permission from the instructor
- Submitting AI-generated text (e.g., ChatGPT or similar tools) as original writing unless explicitly authorized for a specific assignment

Because psychological writing relies heavily on research literature, students must take particular care to **distinguish their own analysis and synthesis from the findings and interpretations of others**.

**Citation Expectations:** All sources in your Draft 1 and Draft 2 papers must be cited in **APA style**, including empirical journal articles, theoretical papers, textbooks, lecture material, and online sources. Students are responsible for understanding proper paraphrasing, quotation, and reference list formatting.

**Consequences of Academic Misconduct:** Violations of this policy may result in:

- A zero on the assignment or the possibility of a required revision with grade penalty
- A second offense will be referred to the university’s Academic Integrity or Conduct Office, in accordance with institutional policy

Students are encouraged to use university resources such as the **Writing Center, library research support, and APA style guides**. If you are ever unsure whether your use of a source is appropriate, **ask before submitting the assignment**.
