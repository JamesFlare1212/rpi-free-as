## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

## This course examines the psychology and neuroscience of learning. Traditional theories of classical and operant conditioning are studied. A wide range of perspectives on this topic will be considered, including human development, clinical disorders, computational theories, and the role of memory, emotion, and social influences on learning.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students who successfully complete this course will be able to: • Describe historical and modern theoretical frameworks for understanding learning. • Understand techniques and methods used to study learning experimentally. • Apply research on learning theory to real-world scenarios. • Utilize critical thinking in order to understand the strengths and weaknesses of • current research in learning.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Course Assessment Measures:**

***Assignments***

Experimental design presentation (20%):

Each student will work in a group of two or three students and design an experiment that would study a certain aspect of learning. The group will present in class all details of the experiment including operational definitions, stimuli, procedures, and theoretical relevance.

***Quizzes & Exams***

LMS Quizzes (20%): There will be seven LMS quizzes consisting of multiple-choice or true/false questions from assigned readings and the lecture materials that were covered in the previous weeks (not cumulative). They will be available on LMS for 48 hours and you will have unlimited attempts. The attempt with the highest grade will be kept in the gradebook. ***There are no make-ups or extensions for LMS quizzes.*** The instructor will drop the two lowest quiz grades for each student at the end of the semester, so the LMS quiz portion of the course grade will be based on five quizzes (%4 each).

In-class short quizzes (10%): There will be recurring in-class short quizzes consisting of three to five questions. There will be a total of around 10 in-class short quizzes and taking 5 of these will be sufficient to earn the full assigned credit of 10% of the course grade.

Two Exams (50%; 25% each): There will be two exams during the semester consisting of multiple-choice questions. Questions will be on materials (e.g. lectures, readings from text, exercises) covered in class up to the exam date. While taking the exam you will be allowed to use *one US letter size cribsheet* on the front and back of which you may write anything from the textbook and/or lecture notes.

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own.
