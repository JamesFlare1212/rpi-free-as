## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Managing Information Technology Resources introduces undergraduate Information Technology (IT) students to the principles of management and their application to information technology resources. The course examines how organizations plan, develop, implement, and manage information systems to support business processes and achieve organizational objectives. Topics include information systems, project management, systems analysis and design, business process improvement, organizational learning, and the strategic use of IT.  
The course is organized around three complementary components. The first is a textbook that provides the conceptual foundation of information systems and IT resource management. The second consists of business case studies that examine the use of information systems and information technology in a variety of organizational settings. The third is a semester-long project in which student teams work with a real client organization to develop an information system solution.  
The term project includes client engagement, requirements analysis, feasibility and cost-benefit analysis, project planning, systems design, risk assessment, implementation planning, change management, and a professional presentation. Together, these three components provide both the conceptual foundation and practical experience needed to manage information technology resources effectively.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

To successfully complete this course, the student must submit all assignments and project deliverables, attend and participate in class (as applicable), and perform satisfactorily on the tests.  
All assignment submissions must be in a Microsoft Office format, unless otherwise specifically noted. Submissions that do not follow this protocol will not be graded. These MS Office formats are the standard, supported formats of the Institute as all students have access to the tools necessary to adhere. Please contact the RPI Help Desk for assistance if needed – the instructor and TA are not able to provide technical assistance with these tools.

Assignments from the cases, readings and project milestones are stated in the Course Calendar. Specific questions will be posted on LMS. Each student must post their answers to the questions prior to the assignment deadline posted on LMS. Each assignment posted on LMS will be given a grade. The grades for the accumulated class participation, weekly assignments, and project milestones will be posted on LMS. Late submissions will result in a grade point deduction. Case assignments have a hard deadline of prior to the start of the class in which the case will be discus.

The objective of the Term Project is to apply the concepts studied in the course to a real situation concerning information systems in a management (or business) context. Projects will be completed in teams of 4 to 5 students assigned by the instructor.

The tests will cover material in the text, readings, lectures, class discussions, cases, and projects. There will be two tests in the course – each is designed to assess the students’ knowledge gained for the material relevant to each test. Quizzes will be given at the discretion of the instructor, in various formats, both announced and unannounced. Quiz material is open to anything covered in class (text, readings, lectures, cases, projects, etc.) up to and including the class in which the quiz is given. Quizzes are much shorter than tests and have less weighting as a result.

The mid-term assessment process will provide each student with feedback on their performance in the class based on graded homework, project assignments, participation, and test 1. The Electronic Warning System (EWS) function in the Student Information System will be used to further notify students who are experiencing academic difficulty after the mid-term exam.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance and Participation (20 points)**

A significant portion of your grade will be received through classroom participation (20%) and the Term Project (25%). Therefore, it is expected that you attend every class and participate in team meetings associated with the Term Project. A participation grade will be given for each student for each class and the participation grades will be returned to students on a quarterly basis. A student may request an excused absence for health or career (job interviews) reasons by emailing the instructor prior to the beginning of that class.

Participation Grading on a 5-Point Scale (each class): Quality is more important than quantity. Being on time for class, team, and client meetings is also important. If on time, each student begins class with a ‘3’. Up to 15 minutes late, each student begins class with a ‘2’. 15 – 30 minutes late, each student begins class with a ‘1’ and cannot score higher than a ‘3’. Greater than 30 minutes late, each student begins class with a ‘0’ and cannot score higher than a ‘2’. From each respective starting point, the number is then adjusted based on participation during the remainder of class.

| **Point** | **Description** |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Absence without excuse. |
| 1-2 | In attendance, but not paying attention, e.g. sleeping, or doing email or Internet unrelated to class, with little to no participation, and not apparently having done pre-class reading. |
| 3 | In attendance, paying attention, appear to have done the pre-class reading, but contributing very little to the class. |
| 4-5 | In attendance, paying attention, having done the pre-class reading, and contributing in a very significant manner with especially insightful comments and questions that inter-connect readings, case, and personal experience to enrich the class. |
