## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course introduces the fundamentals of robotics, providing students with a broad understanding of how robots sense, think, and act, and how their mechanical structures support these functions. Topics include the history and applications of robotics, robot subsystems (sensing, computation, actuation), statics and structural analysis of robotic systems, basic kinematics, control principles, perception, and autonomous decision-making.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Implement and integrate simple control and perception methods to achieve desired robot behaviors.
- Analyze and model robot motion, structure, and actuation using basic kinematics and statics.
- Analyze basic electrical components and circuits in a robotic system.
- Identify and articulate robot types, functions, and applications, including societal, ethical, and safety considerations.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

#### Statement of Academic Integrity

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty. Familiarize yourself with these definitions. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work.

Relevant academic integrity violations to emphasize include:

- **Unallowed Collaboration**: Collaboration is defined as deliberately facilitating an act of academic dishonesty in any way or form; for example, allowing another student to observe an exam paper or allowing another student to ”recycle” one’s old term paper or using one another’s work in a paper or lab report without citing it as another’s work.
- **Copying**: Copying is defined as obtaining information pertaining to a graded exercise by deliberately observing the paper of another student; for example, noting which alternative a neighboring student has circled on a multiple-choice exam.
- **Plagiarism**: Plagiarism is defined as representing the work or words of another as one’s own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference. Examples include using sentences verbatim from a published source in a term paper without appropriate referencing, or presenting as one’s own the detailed argument of a published source, or presenting as one’s own electronically or digitally enhanced graphic representations from any form of media. Homework, quizzes, and exam submissions must represent the student’s own work. **In cases where help was received or given, a notation should be included to indicate the collaboration**.
- Usage of materials (books, internet sites, etc.) outside the listed resources should be cited.
- Evidence of academic dishonesty will result in a zero for the associate assignment and a report to the Dean of Students for possible additional actions.

#### AI Tool Usage

AI tools can be useful for helping to understand concepts or re-word questions for possible clarification. Usage of AI tools to complete labs, assignments, or projects must be acknowledged. Direct copying of the outputs is not allowed. Submissions must be rewritten in your own words.

AI tools may not be used to complete quizzes or exams. Use of AI tools to complete quizzes or exams will result in a zero for that exam.

Repeated violation will result in a report of academic dishonesty to the Dean of Students for possible additional actions.
