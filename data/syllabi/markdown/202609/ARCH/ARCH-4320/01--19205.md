## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides critical technical knowledge and skills for students to critically address environmental building regulations and the practice of health safety and welfare in the design of the built environment. It prepares students with the necessary foundational knowledge to work collaboratively with adjacent expert professionals to foster an integrative design process and places emphasis on developing critical approaches to both active and passive building systems and building enclosures. The course will deliver material through lectures, software tutorials and skill building workshops and requires the students to have a baseline of established knowledge on the subjects. As such, this class cannot be taken without first passing the pre-requisite courses, Construction Systems (CS) and Environmental and Ecological Systems (EES).

The software instruction provided by this course will take the form of site-specific “shoebox” models that will be identical for each individual student. Assignments, tutorials and workshops will prepare students for the simulations required for the deliverables of Building Integration 2, taken the following semester.

Students may not receive credit for both the 4000-level and 5000-level versions of this course.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Outcome 1_Ressiliency Strategies: Students will demonstrate an ability to interpret the necessary data and identify basic resiliency strategies appropriate at the scales of site and building.
- Outcome 2_Daylighting and Passive Solar Strategies: Students will demonstrate an ability to apply basic daylighting design principles and quantify their effects by simulating daylighting conditions and producing iterative improvements to the results. Additional CLOs for Graduate Students: In addition to the course outcomes listed for Undergraduates, Graduate students will demonstrate a deeper understanding of applying daylighting principles by enhancing interior daylighting performance through material selection.
- Outcome 3_Natural Ventilation and Buoyancy-Driven Ventilation: Students will demonstrate an ability to apply basic natural ventilation design principles by designing a ventilation scheme for their studio projects and analyzing that scheme using pressure and buoyancy-driven ventilation principles and rules of thumb. Additional CLOs for Graduate Students: In addition to the course outcomes listed for Undergraduates, Graduate students will demonstrate a deeper understanding of applying passive ventilation principles by analyzing site environmental factors.
- Outcome 4_Materials and Enclosures: Students will demonstrate the ability to identify critical characteristics of materials and building enclosure systems and use those characteristics to assess them against a project's design and performance objectives.
- Outcome 5_Active Building Systems Integration: Students will demonstrate an ability to identify critical components and functions of an active building systems (heating, cooling, ventilation, electrical, plumbing), their relationship to occupant comfort, safety and well-being, and their impact on building form and function in the schematic design phase of a project.

### Attendance Policy:

## Attendance

Attendance for lectures and pinups will be taken for each class and accounts for your total grade. Please see the Assessment Measures section. If a course cannot be attended live or in person, all lecture slides will be posted to the course website; however, you must notify the instructor to account for attendance. There is no need to provide a medical excuse for absences of up to a week. Students are responsible for completing any work they might miss – including assignments, quizzes, labs, exams, etc. – due to illness, quarantine/isolation, or other circumstances. Note: three unexcused absences will cause a failing grade. Be sure to reach out to faculty members if you are missing more than one class. Attendance is worth ten points toward your final grade. Each unexcused absence deducts one-third of those points (approximately 3.3 points), so three unexcused absences bring your attendance score to zero and result in a failing grade. Even one or two unexcused absences can cost you several points, so it is essential that you contact your instructor in advance to have any qualifying absence excused.
