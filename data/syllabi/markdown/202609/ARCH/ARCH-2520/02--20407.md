## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Architects use drawing in two primary ways: 1) as an instrument of invention, 2) as a demonstration of that invention. In other words, architectural drawings must be able to accurately describe configurations of the physical world, and they must be able to help architects imagine, or speculate upon, how the physical world could be configured in different ways. This dual function of drawing will be introduced over the course of the semester in a series of exercises that introduce fundamental drawing skills for architects today. Today architects must think of buildings as assemblies of objects (walls, floors, stairs, windows, etc.), but also of other aspects of the physical world such as flows of materials and energy. Architects must learn to skillfully represent these related aspects of the physical world, and how they come together in buildings. In this course students will be introduced to these different modes of representation by studying a household appliance that embodies similar assemblies of objects, materials, and energy without all the complexities of a building.  
As contemporary architectural drawing is done primarily with digital tools, this course will focus on gaining proficiency in drawing in two and three-dimensional digital environments. Students will acquire expertise in Rhinoceros, Adobe Illustrator, and Adobe Photoshop.  
This is the first semester of the four-semester Digital Constructs sequence. Drawing and software skills acquired in this course will be built on in subsequent semesters of the sequence. At the conclusion of the Digital Constructs sequence, students will be able to generate and communicate sophisticated architectural concepts through drawing and they will be able to use drawing creatively and effectively in design and technical courses across the curriculum.  
Class meetings will incorporate critique, tutorials and working sessions. The semester’s work is organized into eight exercises, which will be introduced weekly with breaks for midterms and the Thanksgiving holiday. Exercises 1-5 will be presented to the entire class in the midterm review. Exercises 2-9 will come together cumulatively in a final presentation board that will be presented to the entire class in the final review.  
The final presentation board will incorporate a series of beautiful, complex, and rigorously produced drawings that demonstrate the student’s mastery of all drawing tools and techniques introduced during the semester. These drawings must stand alone as a creatively authored work.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Learning Outcome #1: Descriptive drawing – Ability to use to use two-dimensional projected drawings, two-dimensional diagrams, and three-dimensional models to accurately describe different aspects of the physical world including the geometry of objects and flows of material and energy.
- Learning Outcome #2: Speculative drawing – Ability to use to use two-dimensional projected drawings, two-dimensional diagrams, and three-dimensional models to imagine and visualize different configurations of the physical world including the geometry of objects and flows of material and energy.
- Learning Outcome #3: Two-dimensional projection drawing – Ability to develop and relate orthographic elevations and sections related through a projection system. Ability to at scale draw in a precise, measurable manner.
- OUTCOME #4: Three-dimensional modeling – Ability to build a three-dimensional digital model at scale in a precise, measurable manner.
- OUTCOME #5: Graphic layout – Ability to compose a presentation board that combines multiple types of drawings, images, diagrammatic information, and text in a beautiful and clear manner.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Assessment Measure #1: Midterm review drawings – 30 points  
Students must do the assigned drawings in accordance with instructions received in class handouts and from their section leaders. Students must incorporate feedback from pin-ups and class discussions into drawings submitted for the midterm review. These drawings will be collected and be given a grade out of thirty points according to how well the work responds to the relevant learning outcomes below. Late midterm submittals (those not presented at the beginning of class on the date of the review) will receive at least one-half letter grade deduction, and potentially more at the instructor’s discretion.  
Assessment Measure #2: Final review drawings – 40 points  
Students must do the assigned drawings in accordance with instructions received in class handouts and from their section leaders. Students must incorporate feedback from pin-ups and class discussions into the board submitted for the final review. This board will be collected and be given a grade out of forty points according to how well the work responds to the relevant learning outcomes below. Late final submittals (those not presented at the beginning of class on the date of the review) will receive at least one-half letter grade deduction, and potentially more at the instructor’s discretion.  
Assessment Measure #3: Attendance and participation – 30 points  
Students must pin-up printouts of drawings, diagrams, and/or images created for each exercise on the submittal date indicated in the course schedule. Students must attend and participate in critical discussions of their own work and the work of their colleagues. Full credit will be given for exercises pinned-up and handed in on time. Half credit will be given for exercises handed in up to one week late. No credit will be given for exercises handed in more than one week late.  
Section instructors will evaluate each assessment measure individually. For example, ‘excellent’ midterm drawings may receive 28 points, ‘very good’ ones 26 points, and ‘average’ ones 23 points. See grading rubric below for specific descriptions of excellent, very good, average, etc. Total points available for the semester is 100.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grades will be based upon the quality of drawings and models produced as well as attendance and participation in class.

- \- Mid Review: 30%
- \- Final Project: 40%
- \- Attendance and Participation: 30% (This course will be taught entirely online; attendance will be recorded by attending scheduled WebEx meetings AND by handing in weekly assignments on time).

Mid-semester evaluations for the course will be provided to each student.

Grades will be established according to the following policy:

- \- A (EXCELLENT) Exceptional performance strongly exceeding the requirements of the course, showing strong academic initiative and independent resourcefulness.
- \- B (GOOD) Performance above the norm; accurate and complete; beyond the minimum requirements of the course; work demonstrates marked progress and initiative.
- \- C (AVERAGE) Satisfactory work that adequately meets minimum requirements and demonstrates satisfactory comprehension, communication skills, and effort; demonstrates little initiative to investigate the problem without substantial input from the instructor; work shows little improvement.
- \- D (INFERIOR) Unsatisfactorily meets minimum requirements; demonstrates minimum comprehension, communication skills, and effort at an inferior level; initiative lacking; improvement not noticeable.
  - \- F (FAIL) Does not meet minimum requirements; fails to adequately demonstrate comprehension, communication skills, and effort.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:**

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermine the educational process. Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violates academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust will merit a failing grade in this course and the violator(s) will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.

**Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism:**

Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of this course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student. Any miss-representation of work shall be evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student will result in a failing grade for the course.
