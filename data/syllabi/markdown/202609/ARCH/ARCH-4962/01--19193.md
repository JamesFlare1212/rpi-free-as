## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

LATIN AMERICAN STUDIES  
Sailing back from one of his voyages Christopher Columbus (Cristobal Colon, in Spanish) wrote in his diary that he was returning from Paradise, such was the impact the newfound lands have made on him. From its “discovery” until today, such a dreamlike image, more desire than reality, has been a constant driven force through the history of Latin America. El Dorado myth, an ideal golden city somewhere deep inland, took many shapes (and names: Trapalanda, La Ciudad Dorada, the City of the Caesars) in the mind of the early Spanish conquistadores that madly searched for it –as so well it is portrayed in Herzog’s “Aguirre, the Wrath of God”--. The persistence of this utopian, and in many ways violent vision could be traced today in many contemporary practices, as in the cultural appropriationism proposed by the avant-garde Antropofagia movement -- later continued by the Tropicalistas--.This idea continue to be foundational on the 1960’s “Hombre Nuevo” (The New Man) carry out by the leftist revolutionaries, and even in the bold nationalism that drove the building of Brasilia.

Colon’s mention is not casual. It is a starting point for a definition to this part of The Americas, the foundational act that will link it to the European cultural traditions that name it in the first place. A Greco / Roman / Catholic / Mediterrean (first Spanish/Portuguese and later Italian) lineage that will conflict and integrate with the (still very much alive) indigenous cultures, the forced African “migration”, and the successive waves of newcomers. A mestizo identity often defined in opposition to the Anglo-Saxon / Protestant / English-speaking part above the Rio Grande, a narrow oppositional definition used at both sides of the border that migration fluxes have placed under question.

History has shaped Latin America not as a single cultural territory but one where many temporalities are simultaneously operating with many different threats connecting the symbolic practices. Any attempt to frame this constant shifting field under one fixed perspective would result partial. It demands an open conceptual approach, an analytical grid as flexible (and arbitrary) as its reality is fundamental, in order to grasp the issues at play.

What are the conditions and practices emerging in this scenario? This course aims to identify and research recent architectural and urban practices in Latin America. We will study the production under an analytical framework driven by the notions such as hybridity, coloniality, and mestizaje.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Course Requirements: In this seminar, students will discuss issues emerging from lectures and presentations, as well as weekly readings and viewings, and present reports and updates of their work. Students are required to complete the assigned readings/viewings in advance, submit their assignments, and come to class prepared to participate actively. Students must provide weekly brief reports/questions of the assigned material and may be asked to lead class discussions at least once per semester. As a final assignment, students will work individually on the development of a section of shared travel guide.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- The student will have been introduced to cultural and theoretical issues that are currently shaping the intellectual discussion in Latin America.
- The student will have an introductory knowledge of topics, issues in discussion, and recent developments in contemporary Latin American architecture and urbanism.
- The student will be familiarized with Latin American canonical work, as well to identify relevant and emerging practices in the region.
- The student will be able to understand the role these practices operate within the Latin American architectural tradition/practice and global contemporary architectural discourses.

Artificial intelligence used in a limited capacity: AI language models, such as ChatGPT, may be used in a limited capacity with appropriate citation. If students are in doubt as to whether they are using AI language models appropriately in this course, they are encouraged to discuss the issue with the faculty. Students are responsible for fact-checking statements composed by AI language models.

Students shall give credit to AI tools whenever used, even if only to generate ideas. Students shall not use AI tools in assignments unless explicitly permitted and instructed. Overall, AI tools should be used wisely and reflectively with an aim to deepen understanding of the subject matter. When using AI tools on assignments, add an appendix showing:

- the entire exchange, highlighting the most relevant sections
- a description of precisely which AI tools were used (e.g. ChatGPT private subscription version or DALL-E free version)
- a brief explanation of how the AI tools were used (e.g. to generate ideas, turns of phrase, elements of text, lines of argument, pieces of evidence, illustrations of key concepts, etc.)
- an account of why AI tools were used (e.g. to save time, to surmount writer’s block, to stimulate thinking, tohandle mounting stress, to clarify prose, to translate text, to experiment for fun, etc.)
