## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Goals**

The Studio investigates the following architectural problems:  
\- Typology, modality, and organization: the idea that type and typology involve a reductive, essentialist approach to architecture, and that the notion of modality by-passes both characteristics without relinquishing the will to organization  
\- Classical order and singularity: an approach to classicism in architecture where the rules of classical orders and the genius, beauty, and inscrutability of architectural singularity are extremes of a single form of thought and production  
\- Models (generality through variability): the operativity of variables, variations, variabilities, ranges, thresholds, singularities in the understanding of models as regimes of differentiation, transcending the mechanistic modern paradigm  
\- Architecture as medium of urbanity: the role, extent, and capabilities of architecture in the thinking and transformation of a project for the contemporary city, and the critique of the understanding of architecture as building design  
\- Multiplicity and masterplan: the critique, through the idea of multiplicity, of the modern idea of masterplan as top-down hierarchical organizational system, and the revalidation of its capacity as an affirmative medium for a project for the city  
\- Construction and representation: the approach to the architectural project as a medium for the abstract construction of building organizations (in a general sense), and the corresponding reevaluation of the idea of representation  
\- Individual and collective authorship: the ethos of multiple ideas of authorship, by learning from the lessons of classicism and by developing systems of variable collaboration without eluding the self-reliance and responsibility of authorship  
\- Consistency as systematic difference: the overcoming of the false dichotomy between coherence and freedom as conditions of architectural project, and the integration of both in regimes for the consistent management of difference

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**The Pantheon Circus** Design Studio

**Course Overview**

**00 The Joyful Imperium of systematic idiosyncrasies**  
Neither neutral generic systems, qualitatively poor and devoid of sensibility, nor mystified contingencies that lack order and reject complex forms of thought, the Studio proposes a serious but light-hearted design investigation characterized by attention, agile development of concepts, rigorous procedures, and formal overflowing, driven by a systemic thinking ethics but overcoming its stiffness through the play with difference. The Studio aims to unfold a vitalist materialist ethics by constructing a joyful imperium of systematic idiosyncrasies, projects built up through the differentiation of culturally wealthy canonical architectural pieces, which it regards as living forms of organization.  
**01 The life of canonical architectural materials**  
Architecture is the discipline that envisions material and spatial organizations through the differentiation of previous ones. Canonical architectural organizations are constructs that evolve in time as the medium of constitution of a culture along its transformation. Both historically charged and memorable, their constituency is built upon and troubled by layers of knowledge that accrue in the form of a language, staying perpetually unstable, constituting ever new meanings, inspiring new usages, and expanding the spectrum of sense of the discipline. Canonical architectural organizations resonate with their times and vibrate across times, as if alive. This is why they cry, weep, roar, or complain when they are transformed, as if they were delicate beings. They have both an internal propensity to change and a susceptibility to external changes, a drive to transform the world and an inertia that protects their consistency. Their cultural life bestows them both of eternal value and surplus.  
**02 Canonical lineages: gradations and shifts**  
High-brow material organizations, saturated of layers of knowledge, interpretation, and influence, the normative of the canon permeates across the architecture that we know and value. Master strokes become habits, then givens, and then conventions that pile up as naturalized rules, that build up or decay along history, guiding the unfolding of culture with alternating constructive and destructive impulses comparable to wind blows, atmospheric silences, air stagnancies, histrionic storms, dramatic rainfalls, slow-motion avalanches, catastrophic landslides, or infernal earthquakes. Canonical lineages unfold, in often-contradictory frenzy, not simply indicating what is good or evil, but illuminating, across time, through their poignancy, what is embedded as potential in the present, and annunciating, through their overflow, what is inexorably coming.  
**03 Hypercanon: integration, differentiation, singularity, desecration**  
“The (ancient) Romans were never particularly disposed to invention and creativity, but they were masters of co-option and assimilation.”  
Mike Duncan, The History of Rome (podcast), 038-The Catiline Conspiracy (https://thehistoryofrome.typepad.com)  
What if, in this context, powerful forms of classical architecture are embraced, varied, and integrated with one another, and if their integration was taken to extremes? What happens with the canon when its norm, which defines the normal as we know it, is absorbed in a pliant model and stretched beyond its limits, superseding its dogma? By configuring complex artifacts and through the deviation of their norms, the creativity of the normative arises out of the conventionality of the normal. Profane forms stem from the sacred, unfurling ever more sophisticated standards of intelligence. Hypercanon engenders new forms of canon through the saturation of existing ones and through the configuration of new kinds of synthesis. Its profanity disputes the canon from within, through the management of excess and the coordination of redundancy.  
**04 The Renaissance-Baroque trans-historical continuum**  
Renaissance architecture, according to Heinrich Wölfflin, aims at the perfection of the architectural body through the congruous and balanced relationship between parts, while Baroque architecture is driven to engender a phenomenon, an event, through the determined movement toward which the architectural body is directed. While the force of the object is gracefully distributed in a harmonic whole in the former, in the latter this same force is concentrated in one point, sustained by a passionate effort from which it bursts and becomes incommensurable. However precise and articulated with the historical processes in which they are commonly inscribed, oppositions between these discreet sensibilities overlook their dynamic participation in the transhistorical continuum that fluidly yet enigmatically communicates the beautiful and the sublime.  
**05 Avant-garde-classicism**  
Classicist architecture, the architecture of the Renaissance and the Baroque that embraced and revitalized the articulation of the architecture of the Greeks and the Romans with their primeval sources by projecting them as new eternal models onto the modern world, is charged by the same Dionysian-Apollonian mask that characterized its predecessors, yet constructing this mask intentionally, as a conscious project, and thus becoming the intrinsic precondition to the secular understanding and conception of architecture of all times after, including our times. Newly alive, their former impetus carries, since then, a deliberate, sensual, bold, tempestuous drive, while its previously open virtue appears wilfully and methodically harmonic and newly stable.  
**06 Self-conscious aesthetic plane**  
The candour of the source is reinscribed in an aesthetically self-conscious plane of technical rigor and formal prosperity. This is why, although resting as a ground from which meaningful references constantly emanate, classical architecture assertively advances into the future as the sharpest weapon into the unknown. No believable forward-looking avant-garde and no real search for authenticity can escape from this dual magnetic spell of classicism without falling in the trap of shallow innovation or dull essentialism. Any avant-garde is grounded on that same illusion of eternality, as much as any idea of permanence reveals its inherent structural instability.  
**07 Double vividness**  
The decay, rottenness, breakdown, and decadence of classical architecture, its turning into ground, into earth, into debris, into atmosphere, or into quasi-nature, on the one hand, and the splendour, artificiality, affectedness, and overconfidence of classicist architecture, on the other, intensify the double-life, or rather, the double-vividness of classicism, its being all the more alive when presenting itself explicitly as dead and reborn. Archetypes become paradoxical, they become archetypes at a new level, archetypes with self-consciousness, not because expressions of an alleged original truthfulness, but rather because they are the incarnations of a newly discovered (or invented) origin that their self-contradictory presence invokes and surpasses.  
**08 Oxymoronic mixtures**  
The wonders of the high Renaissance and the early Baroque, as much as the inventions and surveys of Rome by Giovanni Battista Piranesi are such oxymoronic constructs. While the former recall, ordain, and celebrate, the latter discover, survey, and reveal. Yet, more structurally, they both invent, falsify, and construct. Their mix of dogmatism and inventiveness turns the appearance of science of architecture into a rigorous form of art, the creativity of which is not indetermined but overtly grounded on profound cultural roots, which they fundamentally surpass. Bursting from within the sadness of an inexorable loss, these oxymoronic apparitions mix the true and the false, the real and the fake, their presence lying at once between both and beyond both. Melancholy thus turns into force, and latency takes the form of absolute objects, phantom archetypes.  
**09 Typologies and essences**  
Architectural typologies are disciplinary settlements that stabilize forms of order in the otherwise unstable series of organizational variations and relationships that unfold in time. They crystallize order in immutable immaterial essences, distinguishable from one another, naming, classifying, and making them allegedly atemporal. In turn, as its counterpart, the organizational variability of types is commonly regarded as contingent, accidental, inconsequential, or ornamental nuances that inhabit the margins of tolerance of these typological concepts without altering their recognizability, challenging their reproducibility, or putting in crisis their subsistence along time.  
**10 Models and modalities**  
And yet, that variation precisely constitutes the very vehicle through which types can be defined, and, on the other hand, the very means by which they can mutate and become other than themselves, eventually surpassing their own history by the elevation of those nuances into new forms. The congregation of these variations in softly constrained clusters, that is, in modalities, that are not simply elastic, constitute new forms of recognizability beyond the limitations of typological essences, thus communicating two mutually exclusive realms, the typological variety of organizations and the gradual variation of their internal relationships, in a medium where one turns into the other, thus constituting forms before they decline into either essences or nuances.  
**11 Oppositions as gradients**  
On one end, as Heinrich Wölfflin says, peaceful and liberating beauty, steady expansion of the vital force, free manifestation of form, full, effortless, methodical vivaciousness, perfect roundness and ample, soft, pleasant proportions with the calm, ascetic satisfaction that is intrinsic to the slowness of an enduring effect: a world of the fullness of being that must never be abandoned. On the other end, as he also remarks, the captivating and overwhelming power of affect, of shock, of ecstasy, and the intoxication of a strong action that suddenly abandons us in perplexity, anguish, and unease: a world of perpetual becoming, of an instability and the dissatisfaction by which we are inevitably carried away. What is between those extremes. What lies beyond them?  
**12 Variability in models**  
A model is a consistent set of modalities that transition between those extremes and potentially reaches out beyond their opposition. A model is the fluent yet consistent medium by which modalities overwhelm distinguishable concepts and typologies from inside. Constituted as systems of variables that embody cause-effect relationships between the internal attributes of an architectural organization and with external ones, the variability of models opens the field by which relationships can be systematized as the inherent constituents of self-differentiating processes in organizations, rendering them malleable and systematic, ductile yet sturdy, rather than simply flexible or rigid. Powerful, rather than dogmatic, robust, rather than rigid, and outreaching, rather than detached, models open the very determination of stability to its self-transformation.  
**13 Models**  
A model is thus a system for the fluent yet rigorous control of consistency. It is not the severe control demanded by a doctrine, but the seriously playful one that is demanded by the artful, musical thinking of architecture. It is the blossoming seed that grows through the canon and beyond, into the realm of the hypercanon, the relativism that lies at the threshold at which freedom encounters beauty, just before the settlement of an absolute essence. In other words, a model is a truth that embraces its fundamental error, engulfing its structural lie within belief, and turning rigor into the instrument for a joyous science. The variability of models neither pursues flexibility nor crystallize ideals. Neither spotless clarity nor passive adaptability, models constitute the medium of a balanced self-differentiating consistency: white noise, both sound and noise.  
**14 The Hypercanon Model**  
The Hypercanon Model is a systematic forward-looking unprecedented archetype by which a multiplicity of canonical architectural modalities participates in a consistent medium without losing their recognizability, rather strengthening their presence through the mix, diversifying their influence while remaining unassuming, and intensifying their effects onto the whole while staying perceivable. The Hypercanon Model operates as a bold but complex architectural organization made of a series of modalities of various scales the variables of which vary in degrees according to their position, intertwining with each other, and differentiating themselves. The Hypercanon Model is a battlefield of interacting individuals, a heterogenous melange of a variety of forms that exert power, change one another, and fight for the dominance of a whole that is never subsumed.  
**15 Singular yet multiplicitous objectuality**  
The Hypercanon Model is thus a complex form of architecture that, by absorbing a multitude of components and relationships within its objectuality, breeds and enriches its singularity without compromising its boldness. Rather than instituting itself as a figural proposition, the edges of which protect its coherence and disallow conceptual seepages, knowledge deviations, technical spills, and performative ambiguities, its singularity is constructed as an object that contains within its body an open disciplinary investigation that, through systematization and self-challenge, engenders a creation. The Hypercanon Model is both passionate and rigorous, both caring and destructive. Its singular yet multiplicitous objectuality is the manifestation of self-authorship.  
**16 Indexicality and integration**  
The singular yet multiplicitous objectuality of the Hypercanon Model is set as a deeply idiosyncratic continuous system capable to absorb and digest a wide multiplicity and variation of elements within its increasingly poignant and robust form. All foreign elements are turned into variables of its self-enriching modalities, in a growing three-dimensional diagram that systematically configures its complex identity. Its elements negotiate with the physical presence of others of the same or of a different kind, with which they get merged in the same fluid and yet highly heterogeneous continuum. Organizational varieties, and their variation, friction, collaboration, competition, or synergy, are subsumed in a general intertwinement of systemic relationships.  
**17 Artificial idiosyncrasy**  
The complexity of the Hypercanon Model artificially engenders idiosyncrasy. The irreducibility of this idiosyncrasy is grounded on its culturally embedded systemic consistency, and it is nurtured by an endless process of indexing of elements. The Hypercanon Model results of this constant process of absorption, integration, and synthesis by which a canonical multiplicity becomes a singularity. Alternately divergent in the proliferation of its directions and convergent in the consistency of its order, this artificial idiosyncrasy is increasingly sharp and defined but remains open and diffuse. Its multivalence is, in this sense, the very source of its self-surmounting identity: the Hypercanon Model is the turning of a becoming process into an idiosyncratic being.  
**18 A black hole masterplan**  
The Hypercanon Model is a singularity-multiplicity that cannot simply be reduced to a top-down hierarchically structured organization that deterministically coordinates systems, nor a dispersed indeterminate form of urbanism that evades decisions by hiding behind the contingencies of everyday life, the intricacy of power relations, or the unpredictability of situations through bottom-up, scenario-driven formats. Instead, the singularity-multiplicity masterplan that the Hypercanon Model involves is articulated by its very idiosyncratic, the complex internal relationships of which are as contingent as systematic. The Hypercanon Model works as a self-generating black hole, the dense critical mass of which absorbs the city in a quasi-silent, white-noise-like void.  
**19 White noise**  
White noise is the new kind of generic organization that, instead of rooted in homogeneity, indifference, and neutrality, results from the excessive accumulation of multiple sources in a singular idiosyncrasy, an internally diversified, apparently chaotic noise that has the curious appearance of calm, silence, sooth, and perpetuity. As the fourth generation of globalization (the post-politically correct era) looms on the horizon, we find ourselves facing a completely new form of generality, dispersed and headless, obnoxiously rarefied, alienated, that paradoxically appears as a tempered synthetic project, the factuality of which is dangerously uncertain, but the emergent ethics of which appears to overcome the dichotomy between systems and contingencies. White noise is the self-fulfilled prophecy of noisy multiplicities rendered silent singularities.  
**20 What does this weirdest of silences sound like?**  
This ubiquitous form of generality of these silent singularities is paradoxically composed of a multiplicity of bellicose and intransigent stereotypes, often insignificant, made of the microscopic hyper-individualistic politics of millions that are obsessed with broadcasting their profile in a world now devoid of any form of authenticity. In anxious search of both equality of status and freedom of expression, these structurally fake profiles corrupt them both, but the resulting proliferation of their nuances paradoxically gives rise to a polyphonic noise, the passive-aggressiveness tone of which, is both locally impatient and globally serene. Its voice is exasperating but peaceful, its effects are as aggressive as innocent. What does this weirdest of silences actually sound like?  
**21 The Forumdome**  
The Forumdome is the singular yet multiplicitous Hypercanon Model that instals its powerful presence in the city, organizing, through the architectural richness of its white noise organization, its immediate and mediate surroundings, thus capable to launch the city as a new form of global node. The Forumdome is a bold, archaic architectural form, whose artificial idiosyncratic figure, charged with manifold cultural resonances, strategically indexed, superficially stamped, and systemically embedded, surpasses any naturalistic spontaneity. The Forumdome works simultaneously as an absolute form and as an open forum, integrating the organizational richness of the latter within the iconic synthesis of the former in an amalgamation that absorbs the city within its anti-urban ethics and aesthetics. The Forumdome is a multifarious organization that works as a blank enclave that projects itself as an over-touristic archetype.  
**22 Roma Intempestus Urbis**  
The eternity of Rome: its predetermined destiny of atemporality reaches a new level, where the ultimate (matter of fact, straightforward) form of the grandiose encounters a renovated version of the picturesque, paradoxically unfolding under the unemotional ethics and humour of radical systematicity. Roma Intempestus Urbis turns the tempestuous machine of over-tourism inside out, and that crazy dynamic that has slowly but consistently pushed away its cosmopolitan people out of its primordial territory, paradoxically precipitates in a peaceful, relentless, timeless architectural object, where the primordial and the cosmopolitan find a one-of-a-kind synthesis. The sacred city is once again profaned and consecrated, taking a new form, such being its trans-historical destiny. Paradigm of atemporality, Rome is the ultimate medium for the extemporaneity of the contemporary world: history folds onto itself and generates a new form of architectural being.  
**23 Over-tourism**  
Just like other European cities, Rome has become a global touristic node. But given its singularity as a source of western culture, its touristic wealth, its cultural diversity, the variegation of its ruins, its mythological food, its wide-ranging panoramas, its appetite for design and for fashion, its tackling of well-being through the dolce vita, and its memorable water infrastructures, the over-burdened tourism of the contemporary masses (disciplines, practices, cultures, races, ages, geographies) visiting the ancient centre has become extreme. Irreducible to a theme park, the local population of the over-touristic city is being expelled out of the historical centre to the surroundings.  
**24 The over-tourism abstract machine**  
Is it structural enough, in this context, and, above all, is it daring enough to put at work tactics of survival of the local population through mitigation policies that forcefully confront over-tourism by confining it, with no economic return involved, like most European urban policies are currently attempting? Are we not, instead, in front of the unprecedented opportunity of embracing a new form of city and, with it, envisioning a new role for architecture, by radically intensifying tourism and channelling over-tourism at its very core, turning it over, and repositioning the very concept of Rome as the new model of the global city? Is this sombre panorama, perhaps, part of its always yearned-for destiny? What is the abstract machine that can ground, through architecture, rather than through policy, such process, using its force as a disciplinary opportunity?  
**25 The Uber-soul of the lost souls**  
In the over-touristic city, people behave as zombies, secular phantasmagorical deities curiously alike the wandering shadows that scarcely populate Piranesi’s inventions. The contemporary hysterical masses frantically drift through the mysterious fragments of Rome, following the invisible mediatic forces that carry them across the same places, to do the same things, in the same ways, and at the same times. Routines and gestures are prescribed, and when interruptions or information gaps arise within the abstractly managed domestic multitudes, individuals get lost, stay motionless, get stuck, start turning in circles, or simply shut down, until new instructions appear.  
**26 Secular gods of indifference**  
In the over-touristic city, the multitude is broken down in small homogeneous clusters (between 02 and 40) that go through one another, ignorant of anything that is not their prescribed aims: tours inside or around attractions, never detours, that only abandon the stimulating spots if they have been efficiently consumed according to the provided protocols. The over-touristic city is populated by blank, lost souls that paradoxically share the mutual indifference that is key to their common ritual, the path of the eternal return to the same. The uber-soul of the lost souls is the new form of the metropolitan, the secular gods of indifference that traverse their bodies without any transcendence.  
**27 The collective anti-urbanism of a city with no exteriority**  
The behavioural pattern is clear: navigate as if you are shopping, move straight to the given aims, slow down as you get tired, turn around several times, look around, move around, gather and join groups of equals, remain structurally indifferent, do not pay attention to anything other than expected, do not get bored, and shut down. Tourists participate in a multidirectional congregation with no exteriority: pure interior without time, a mall, an arcade, a multiplex cinema, an Apple Store. Entertained concentration, rather than attention, is the precondition of its effectiveness, its warranty of success, the appearance of inevitability of the inescapable interior in which they are subsumed.  
**28 The City of the Global Populus**  
Tourists are held inside the timeless enclosure of Rome, from which they cannot escape, and do not want to. The critical mass of the cultural paraphernalia of the city creates the feeling that there is nowhere else to go, that everything outside is trickery, that the world was turned inside out, that the illusion actually lies beyond the Aurelian Walls, and that the city is the atemporal evidence that reveals the only real, the unique and perpetual here-and-now, looping over and over. Rome assumes the inevitable destiny for which it seems to have been created, and for which it has persistently endured, self-overwhelmed, over millennia. The challenge is now to reincarnate its fortune and turn it into the ultimate form of global city: the City of the Global Populus.  
**29 Intempestus Urbis, umbilicus of a multi-centric world**  
The City of the Global Populus is the new state of things of the generic city: the global city that achieves ubiquity through uniqueness, because everybody wants to be there, not because it is everywhere, the non-place dystopia, but because it has become the inescapable order of reality, its logos. Contrasting with its generic predecessor of the millennium, that looks and works like an airport (blank, clean, white, insipid, homogeneous, neutral), the City of the Global Populus looks and works like a quasi-religious singularity-multiplicity, a complex ever returning of classical buildings, supreme cultural artifacts, and newborn residues, remains, and reminders. It is the forever-land, a city of ruins-in-advance that behaves as the ultimate artificial nature: anti-urbanism becomes Intempestus Urbis, umbilicus of a multi-centric world.  
**30 Anti-urban entertainment infrastructure**  
The City of the Global Populus is a culturally wealthy, endlessly prosperous anti-urban entertainment infrastructure made of auratic public interiors, formally sophisticated, atmospherically evocative, poignant and programmatically diffuse, a secularly sacred venue. Its all-embracing anti-urbanism works as an erratic set of indiscriminate but systematic accumulations of cultural matter, formally crisscrossed and tense with one another. Its multitude of singular architectural forms take over exterior public space: idiosyncratic over-touristic pieces, the oversaturated singularity of which is populated by amenities, public spaces, urban equipment, and entertainment venues that become indetermined through over-determinacy.  
**31 Roma Intempestus Urbis 01: the Matrix-Multiplicity (2024)**  
Unhinged central piazzas, lethargic cloisters and courtyards, warmhearted atriums, embracing facades, shock-and-awe landmarks: a series of case studies from the High Renaissance and the Early Baroque in Rome (piazzas, courtyards, atria, naves, facades, and landmarks from Donato Bramante, Antonio da Sangallo, Michelangelo Buonarroti, Carlo Maderno, Francesco Borromini, Gian Lorenzo Bernini, Giacomo della Porta, Pietro da Cortona) were the material from which a matrix-multiplicity masterplan of variable models was constructed. Each model was grounded on a generic typology, integrating the organization of three canonical projects, opening the generality of their types into consistently variable organizations that intermingled across the field of Campo Marzio.  
**32 Roma Intempestus Urbis 02: the Forumdome (2025)**  
Storytelling columns, radiant pyramids, and thick walls; rotten aqueducts, broken arcades, and semi-collapsed vaults; outlandish block tombs, rotonda tombs, and tower tombs; bridges on bridges, underground cisterns, and sunken porticoes; hybrid mausoleums, rotondas, and temples; grandiose baths, fountainheads, and reservoirs: a series of case studies adopted from the inventions of Giovanni Battista Piranesi’s surveys of Ancient Rome were the material to construct Forumdome models. Each model was built as a bold architectural form, integrating the organization of three canonical projects, opening the generality of their types into an organizationally profuse model, an art, education, fashion, gastronomy, landscape, music, religion, waterscape, or wellness-driven archetype that projects its poignancy on the field of Campo Marzio.  
**33 Roma Intempestus Urbis 03: the Pantheon Circus (2026)**  
The tiny ideal prototype of the Tempietto di San Pietro in Montorio, by Donato Bramante, the blossoming staircase overwhelming the vestibule of the Biblioteca Laurenziana, by Michelangelo Buonarroti; the ambiguous interior-exterior of the Teatro Olimpico di Vicenza, by Andrea Palladio, the voluptuous yet austere atrium of San Carlo alle Quattro Fontane, by Francesco Borromini, the multicentric directional Piazza San Pietro, by Gian Lorenzo Bernini, five canonical milestones of the High Renaissance and Early Baroque are the material from which Pantheon Circus models will be constructed, grounded on the organizational modalities of their sources, opening the sensibility of their authors through a consistently variable archetype that will launch its white-noise, quasi-religious, silent, musical black-hole effect onto Campo Marzio.  
**34 Canon, differentiation, hypercanon, white noise, critical mass, black hole**  
00 Bramante, Michelangelo, Palladio, Borromini, Bernini, will be the background sensibilities of the Pantheon Circus models; 01 The Tempietto, the Biblioteca Laurenziana, the Teatro Olimpico, San Carlino, and San Pietro, will be the canonical ideals that will work as raw materials; 02 A generic model will be constructed; 03 A generic model variation will unfold from within the model; 04 A formal organizaitonal strategy for Piazza Navona will follow; 05 Generic models will index palettes of modalities from other works by Bramante, Michelangelo, Palladio, Borromini, Bernini; 06 The generic models will be overloaded, saturated, differentiated, constituting an internally consistent hypercanon; 07 The critical mass resulting of the white noise of the hypercanon model will aim to project a black hole effect onto Campo Marzio; 08 The spatial musicality of the hypercanon will be evaluated, calibrated, distilling a silent sound, which will be expected to work as a concrete from of aura. The architectural procedure, apparently linear, will run from sensing, to intuiting, to understanding, to abstracting, to varying, to differentiating, to indexing, to changing in kind, to creating a phenomenon, to recognizing, enjoying, and calibrating it, to naming and manifesting it.  
**35 Hypercanonical sound**  
What is the apparently silent sound that will result of this hypercanonical white noise? What peaceful effect will it have on the over-touristic frenzy of contemporary Rome? How can the classical architectural canon, at its most artificial and sophisticated peak, the High Renaissance and the Early Baroque continuum, right after the consolidation of classicism as a set of dogmatic principles, but much before its self-indulgent process of decadence, that is, right at its most joyful scientific peak, be brought to a critical-mass, another peak among the potential manifold of hypercanonical future peaks, in which the canon changes in kind, self-superseding and abandoning altogether its principles, and thus becoming both the most of itself and the most of other-than-itself, not out of critiquing them, not out of challenging them, not out of obsolescence, but as the result of a spontaneous process of self-differentiation, distilling a new, out of control, and yet overdetermined version, the hypercanon? What is the peculiar sound of its white noise? How does the black hole feel like when within its ecstatic interior? What does a Pantheon Circus, the hypercanonical overtouristic fun-palace that feels like the ultimate silent retreat, right in the middle of Rome, centroid of the West, feels like? What is the sound of its secular architectural religiosity, the sound of its aura?  
**36 The architectural calligraphy of an eternally undecipherable hieroglyph**  
The architecture of the City of the Global Populus results, therefore, 01, of the infinite tangential relationships and minute rubbings that constitute the irreducible uniqueness of its Matrix-Multiplicity masterplan, 02, of the infinite resonances of its Forumdome archetype models radiating into one another, and 03, of the oversaturated magnetism and the peculiar sound of its ultimate-silence retreat, the Pantheon Circus. The architecture of the City of the Global Populus is a white noise of architectural models, built through the variation, integration, agglomeration, and differentiation of classical architectural master strokes that together configure a singularity-multiplicity. Its figure-figure without-a-canvas white-noise manifold takes either the self-expanding form of a ubiquitous system, or the self-imposing form of a series of enclaves, or the self-retreating form of a singular black hole, inspiring (or rather, perspiring) many undecipherable messages without projecting a coherent meaning. The architecture of the City of the Global Populus is the calligraphy of a trans-historical hieroglyph, the writing system, the idiosyncratic signatures, and the evocative silence that refuse to release the final clues to their enigma, both beautiful and sublime.  
**Site**  
The site, understood as a central void of transformation of the Campo Marzio area, is Piazza Navona, one of Rome’s most intensely sedimented architectural-urban artifacts, where successive interventions have accumulated and condensed through time into a singular form of public space. Occupying the footprint of the Stadium of Domitian and transformed through the successive accretion of layers of cultural appropriation, the piazza constitutes a paradigmatic object-field in which canonical architectural forms, public space, events, and spectacles, touristic facilities, and everyday life converge. Rather than treating Piazza Navona as an isolated object, the Studio approaches it as a concentrated fragment of the City of the Global Populus: a singular yet multiplicitous interior whose historical layers provide the preconditions for a new hypercanonical over-touristic architectural organization. Following the precedence of the two major survey-like radically inventive mid XVIII century projects by Giambattista Nolli and Giovanni Battista Piranesi, the projects will operate within Piazza Navona through the abstraction, variation, and differentiation of Renaissance and Baroque canonical projects, understood as the raw materials with which to constitute hypercanonical models capable of reorganizing and intensifying public space by absorbing it within architecture. The Pantheon Circus will be not simply an intervention in the piazza, understood as a container, but an architectural transformation of its cultural and spatial conditions into an over-touristic enclave that projects its critical mass as a black-hole through the sound of the silence of its white-noise spatiality.  
**Program**  
The Pantheon Circus is constituted as a consistently variable architectural archetype that seeks to produce a condition that is neither urban nor anti-urban, but their radical other. Its hypercanonical critical mass operates as a black hole: a dense and self-contained field of canonical architectural resonances that absorbs the frenzy and indifference of the over-touristic city and transforms them into a particular form with a sound of simultaneous silence and intensity. Urban programs familiar to Rome—markets, gastronomy, churches, shopping, hotels, landmarks, and other forms of public and collective life that work as its touristic assets—may persist within it, but at a level of intensity that will overwrite the specificity of their functionality and take it over, reinscribing them as nuanced frequencies within the peculiar sound of the larger silence of the hypercanonical architectural artifact. The overwhelming presence of Piazza Navona, understood as an urban form sedimented through centuries of architectural transformation, will ground the singularity of this hypercanonical multiplicity that works as an almost silent sound. Conversely, its silence will not be simply absence of noise, but rather an absence that stands as a poignant presence imbued with a white noise that projects a subtle form of sound, an aura. The resulting space will be conceived as a quasi-religious musical interior, an environment in which the integrated multiplicity of canonical architectural organizations at various scales configures an auratic resonance box, where noise becomes nuance, and within which the over-touristic city is transformed into an ecstatic retreat.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 01 Research 01a_Ability to use/develop specialized knowledge Students live in Rome throughout the semester. Through the direct interaction with canonical architectural pieces, the involvement with urban conditions and phenomena, and the fluid access to museums, artwork, and bibliographical resources in the city, students augment their disciplinary baggage and construct an ability to attentively observe and document multiple forms and scales of knowledge about buildings, cultural artifacts and sites that ground the architectural procedure of the project, nurture its design techniques, and substantiate the decision-making process. 01b_Ability to use / develop broad and integrative knowledge Students live in Rome throughout the semester, engaging in a culturally immersive process that, aside primarily consisting of regular visits to Renaissance and Baroque buildings in the city centre and of participating in real time in the cultural life of the city in the context of its omnipresent history, is fostered by a series of field trips to the outskirts of the city, Tuscany, and the Veneto, providing a rich and multilayered context of historical and cultural significance to the architectural projects, which broadens the understanding and thinking both of their causes and of their consequences.
- 02 Intellectual design skills 02a_Ability to design Students are conceptually and technically nurtured to develop an ability to ground design decisions on a hands-on operative approach to architecture as material. This approach is articulated by studying and literally using the organizational knowledge embedded in existing buildings as sources of design intelligence. 02b_Ability to design using information resources Students construct architectural models from the integration of visual resources, formal descriptions, and organizational data, programming operative models that are built upon existing case-study buildings in Rome, thus developing a process of architectural abstraction that turns information resources into form, and vice versa. 02c_Ability to design engaging diverse perspectives Students are trained to produce projects as a synthesis of various viewpoints that are progressively integrated in a single artifact by means of an iterative design process that goes from individual work to teamwork, to all-Studio work. These viewpoints incarnate multiple perspectives: cultural, commercial, civic, and urban. 02d_Ability to design with ethical reasoning Students are confronted with the contemporary urban problem of overtourism (at work, particularly, in major European cities like Rome), and are challenged to think ingenious solutions for the overloading of historic centers with touristic infrastructure and the overwhelming ephemeral population that brings radical changes into the social fabric. 02e_Ability to design with quantitative fluency Students are taught to construct, program, control, and manage complex abstract architectural models: systems of architectural organization that determine form and space through the control of quantifiable variations of variables and relationships between them, learning to go from quantifiable data into form, and vice versa. 02f_Ability to design with communicative fluency Students are challenged to fluidly communicate with (and be influenced by) different interlocutors at different stages of the course, from local professors and consultants of various backgrounds to student colleagues of other universities in the Rome Center, to guest professors that participate in reviews and the lecture series program.
- 03 Collaboration 03a_Ability to collaborate with team Students develop the ability to work in three-student teams, learning and using collaborative methods based on design integration through digital modelling, and supported by interpersonal skills. After an initial phase (framed by architectural typologies associated to student teams), an intermediate phase involves the development of teamwork strategies grounded on the sharing of acquired knowledge, the synergetic intertwinement of design techniques, the project ideation through conceptual and organizational integration, and a management and workflow ethics that fluctuates between the differentiation and the integration of tasks. A final stage integrates the teamwork development into a proposition for central Rome. The change of roles along the process provides of evidence of different strengths of the individual students, who document the process in a portfolio that allows its clear understanding. 03b_Ability to collaborate with a community partner Students develop the ability to feed the work with the influence of potential community partners and users that are surveyed from the local community and from disparate global sources and cultures by registering their behavioural patterns. These patterns are embedded in the project through the recognition of habits and rituals, and the abstraction of forms, typologies, and material traditions. The community, understood in a non-paternalistic materialistic sense, contributes, with these patterns, to the strategic thinking, the programming, and the organizational enrichment of the projects.
- 04 Civic and global engagement 04a_Ability to engage the civic realm Students demonstrate the ability to engage with an urgent issue of public interest: the overpopulation of the historic centre of the city of Rome due to the rapidly growing influx of global tourists, from which they are being expelled due to the increase in the living cost and the changes in the real estate market, while becoming economically dependent from it, and the transformation of the historic centre into a new form of generic city: the singular city. Students develop models to accommodate local conditions within global ones, rather than disappointingly resisting them. 04b_Ability to engage the global realm Students demonstrate the ability to engage with the positive aspects present in the rapid growth and transformation that global overtourism is bringing into the historic centre of the city of Rome, rethinking and expanding the capabilities of typologies (piazzas, courtyards, atriums, naves, facades, and landmarks) to absorb public space within architecture and integrate communities that appear to be, in principle, in conflict. Students develop models to accommodate local conditions within global ones and use their synergy to think about the current state of the global city.
- 05 Design execution 05a_Midterm Review Students demonstrate the ability to implement the learning outcomes 01/02/03/04 in a clear, complete, sophisticated, and compelling way through a public presentation to guest jurors and an intermediate submission of the production, including: a portfolio presentation and submission containing organizational descriptions, photographic surveys, source material, digital models, and conventional orthographic drawings (plans, elevations, axonometries, perspectives). 05b_Final Review Students demonstrate the ability to implement the learning outcomes 01/02/03/04 in a clear, complete, sophisticated, and compelling way through a public presentation to guest jurors and a final submission of the production, including: a book-like portfolio presentation and submission containing front and back matter, reading reports, organizational descriptions, photographic surveys, source material, digital models, diagrams, evaluation drawings, conventional orthographic drawings (plans, elevations, axonometries, perspectives).

**Studio Policy**

**Pin-ups**

Students are periodically required to present their work in a formal review process. Presentations are a critical part of the architectural education. They are opportunities to judge the project and critically evaluate the design process. It may be appropriate at points in the semester to evaluate only selected projects to convey information relevant to the entire class. In this situation, it is important for each student to witness and participate in the review process. Even if his/her project is not reviewed, the criticism and discussions inform the evaluation of his/her progress and design. Failure to attend one or more pin-ups for the full class period may result in a grade deduction.

**Desk Critiques**

The Studio environment involves two-way communication. The role of the Studio professor is to evolve and strengthen the student’s design process. Every time the student meets with the faculty, he/she must have new iterations of work based on previous conversations. If the student has not produced new work, the faculty reserves the right to skip the critique and continue with other students who have produced new material.

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance**

Attendance is required at all class meetings during the semester. Two (2) unexcused absences from class will result in the lowering of the grade by one (1) letter. Four (4) unexcused absences from class during the term result in an automatic F. Whether an absence (health, immediate family death, officially approved field / sport trip, etc.) is excused or unexcused is determined by the Students Experience Office (4th Floor Academy Hall, x8022, se@rpi.edu), upon presentation of required documentation, according to established policies and procedures (see http://se.rpi.edu/policies/ea/).

Students are required to work in the Studio space during Studio hours and have the required materials and basic tools to develop the assignments. Work includes participation in site visits, design and development, pinups, presentations, discussions and field trips. Failure to work in class with undivided attention and disruptive behavior (tardiness, leaving early, lack of participation, general socializing, etc.), as well working on assignments from other classes during class, will count as absences.

**Timely Completion of Work**

All work must be submitted for reviews, pinups, critiques and/or discussions on the dates noted under each assessment measure. However, final assessment of work does not occur until after the final review and the final submission. Students must respond to feedback received from the Studio professor and the guest jurors to refine and develop work. Students are encouraged to attend faculty office hours and tutoring sessions to help plan the completion of assignments at different Studio phases.

**Excused Absence policy**

There is no need for a medical excuse to be provided for absences of up to a week.

Students missing more than a week of classes due to illness will be required to provide a doctor’s note or other supporting documentation to the Student Success Office. The Class Dean will communicate the receipt of the note (with expected end date) to the relevant faculty.

Students are responsible for completing any work that they might miss – including assignments, quizzes, labs, exams, etc. – due to illness, quarantine/isolation, or other circumstances.

Students who adhere to these processes should not be penalized per the attendance policy for the course and as detailed in the course syllabus. Instructors should make every effort to work with and provide reasonable modifications for students who cannot attend class due to illness to complete assignments and exams. It is recognized that virtual learning may not be possible in some in-person laboratory and performing arts courses.

Attendance is mandatory at all Rome Center Talk Series. Unless otherwise noted, lectures take place on Thursdays at 12:30pm or at 06:00pm at the main hall of the Rome Center. Unexcused absences from lectures result in grade reduction.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process.

**Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism**

Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust merits a failing grade in this course and the violator will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.

Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of the course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student. Any misrepresentation of work is evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student results in a failing grade for the course.
