## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Digital Constructs III will focus on advanced nurbs modeling, drawing, and rendering. These objectives will be addressed through a semester-long design project that is divided into three sections. First, we will look at advanced nurbs modeling by creating a detailed digital model of several sculptural artworks. This modeling will incorporate techniques that students may not have been introduced to during AD1 & AD2. We will put particular emphasis upon modeling clean curvature, modeling with precision, and creating closed solid models.  
During the seminar’s second module, our focus will be on developing drawing technique. Students will produce experimental drawings that analyze their detailed models from module one. Through a combination of axonometric, isometric, and/or orthogonal drawing projections, students will generate and develop design ideas as to how the original objects might operate as a building through the introduction of scale. Here, we will focus on building skill and technique, and students will be given the latitude to begin developing a personal aesthetic. Importantly, this method of drawing will not merely serve as a means of representation, but rather as an opportunity for the students to actively engage in the process of architectural design, promoting creative exploration and critical thinking through the deployment of lines.  
The final section of Digital Constructs will cover rendering using VRay for Rhino. In three progressive sessions we will cover: (1.)object-focused rendering, (2.)scene-based rendering / photo matching, and (3.)Post processing / photoshop techniques. The Object focused rendering session will establish fundamentals of controlling the lighting environment, surface materiality/ texture modification, camera set up, et cetera. The object the students render in this session will be site-less and without context, focusing on the qualities for the object and the lighting environment. The scene-based rendering session will expand the skills from the first session by developing a basic context for the object to be placed. This will include view-matching a context photo, constructing a camera set up that matches the view and developing a scene. The final session will focus on post processing strategies and Photoshoping techniques.  
An important element of Digital Constructs is the idea that skills and techniques taught in this course are directly translatable to AD3 and future studios. We encourage and expect you to find ways to take your knowledge in this course and use it in your studio projects. Faculty members are always available to discuss ways you might use this class to improve your design and representation in studio and other courses.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Outcome 1 Advanced Nurbs Modeling: (B.Arch+M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to produce advanced nurbs models that utilize clean modeling technique and appropriate approaches to modeling single and double curvature. Students will also demonstrate the ability to produce closed (watertight) nurbs models.
- Outcome 2 Line Drawing: (B.Arch+M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to produce orthographic line drawings. (M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to produce perspectival line drawings.
- Outcome 3 Line Drawing Legibility: (B.Arch+M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to embed visual hierarchy that adds information and clarity to line drawings.
- Outcome 4 Rendering: (B.Arch+M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to produce both object and scene renderings. (M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to object and scene renderings with mutiple camera locations and focal depths.
- Outcome 5 Image Post-Processing: (B.Arch+M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to increase the understanding of a drawing and its context through post-processing. (M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to develop and execute a unique personal agenda through the post-processing process.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is required at all class meetings during the semester. Two (2) unexcused absences  
from class will result in the lowering of the grade by one (1) letter. Four (4) unexcused absences  
from class during the term result in an automatic F. Whether an absence (health, immediate  
family death, officially approved field / sport trip, etc.) is excused or unexcused is determined by  
the Students Experience Office (4th Floor Academy Hall, x8022, se@rpi.edu), upon presentation  
of required documentation, according to established policies and procedures (see http://  
http://se.rpi.edu/policies/ea/).  
Students are required to work in class during class hours, and have the required materials and basic tools to develop the assignments, including a working computer running the required software. Mac users MUST have Bootcamp, Windows, and the PC version of Rhino installed. Failure to work in class with undivided attention and disruptive behavior (tardiness, leaving early, lack of participation, general socializing, etc.), as well working on assignments from other classes during class, will count as absences.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).
