## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

“Design Research Studio” is a graduate studio focused on Material, Energy, and Information as frameworks for design research in the era of socio-ecological concern. Students identify and quantify a problem or opportunity in the built environment, map it as a system of inputs/outputs across scales, and propose a preferable alternative. The semester culminates in a pilot design research project that integrates analysis, precedent, and graphic/oral communication into a persuasive proposal.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

During this semester the goal of the studio is twofold:

1.You will identify and quantify a problem or opportunity that permeates the built environment and understand it as a holistic system of inputs and outputs, products and by products at all scales and all levels. Furthermore, you will propose a preferable alternative as an intervention to the current condition (system) and identify the steps required to enable it. 2.You will begin a PILOT design project that synthesizes the prior analytical work to a comprehensive research project. You will develop a design research project whose program, systems, and performance is an intervention on an existing site, condition or system of your choosing. Using Material, Energy, and Information as guiding frameworks, your goal is to critically develop ecological alternatives to conventional practices for the built environment.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Mini Lecture Series

Mini lecture series for the research studio featuring CASE faculty (and potentially PhD students). Lectures are structured around three core themes: Material, Energy, and Information. Following the lectures, students will frame their interests using these themes, focusing on the “WHY” behind their projects and describing a plausible scale and output (physical prototype, software, experiment, framework developments).

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Students will develop a design proposal for a specific built environment problem or opportunity, incorporating a complex interrelationship of social economic and technological factors at a scale of their choosing.
- 2\. Students will integrate technical and scientific research into the conceptualization and design of the built environment.
- 3\. Communicate interdisciplinary knowledge effectively through coherent visual and oral presentations.
- 4\. Analyze and elaborate how one scale of the built environment informs and impacts other scales through diagramming, oral presentations, and drawings.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

10% Participation: Active participation in class discussions, pin-ups/reviews, workshops, and short reading responses/quizzes will constitute the participation grade.

40% Midterm: The midterm will be a cumulative review of the work performed to that point, including the research question and thesis statement, facts/evidence, system diagrams of Material/Energy/Information, state-of-the-art precedents, methodology, and Gantt/timeline, plus an initial research project direction. Students will be assessed according to the clarity of argument, rigor of evidence, and level of integration across themes.

50% Final project: The final project will review the full and final integrated development of the research and design proposal, including refined thesis, comprehensive system analysis, documented prototype(s)/experiment(s) or simulations, evaluation of performance/impact, and a final presentation package and archive.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own.
