## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Overview**

This studio takes a capacity that does not yet exist at full scale — a new material, a new structural behaviour, a new way of growing or joining or repairing, a change in what a surface can do or how long a thing lasts — accepts it as a new reality, and designs everything that follows. Every premise must produce a new tectonic: a new way something is made, joined, held up, closed, opened or maintained.

Science fiction has often worked this way: some works proceed by suspending disbelief about one or more inventions or extrapolations, and the story is within an ecosystem enabled by these new realities. We will work the same way in architecture, where a single new possibility reaches the detail, the plan, the program and the city in turn — and where what becomes ordinary matters as much as what becomes obsolete.

What if walls were grown rather than built? Mycelium has already raised a tower in a museum courtyard; living roots already carry bridges in Meghalaya. What if concrete healed its own cracks, as it now does in the laboratory? What if a façade were made of algae and fed the building, as one in Hamburg does? What if a structure could be taken apart and reassembled without fixings — or a building could tell you where it hurts?

The invention is the beginning, not the project. Teams take a building or typology that already exists — a school, a market, a library, a warehouse — and imagine what it becomes. The new capacity has its own idiosyncrasies: its tectonic, its assembly time, its duration, its upkeep, its errors. A relative value. It runs itself against the world as is: existing weather and bodies, cities as they already stand. We follow the one thing that changes, to see where it goes about meeting reality as we know it — how it changes reality, and is changed by it.

Drawings, models, 1:1 images, and a two-minute film in the CRAIVE Lab — the world made large enough to stand inside.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Course Goals / Objectives**

— Develop a new tectonic from a granted premise, and resolve it as buildable detail.

— Carry that tectonic upward into room, building and situation, and outward into cultural consequence.

— Work on an existing typology whose paradigm is already under pressure, using its resistance as the measure of what has changed.

— Develop drawing, model, image and film as instruments of architectural argument, and practice collective authorship.

**Learning Outcomes**

— Students will be able to derive a new tectonic from a speculative premise and resolve it in drawing and model at full scale.

— Students will be able to specify the conditions a proposed capacity brings with it, across material, temporal, labour and institutional dimensions, and translate them into tectonic decisions.

— Students will be able to analyse an existing typology and its operative paradigm, and develop a project coherently across joint, room and situation.

— Students will be able to produce a time-based architectural argument and present work in an immersive environment.

**Outline of Course Work**

Classes engage lecture, seminar discussion and design studio formats. All assignments are mandatory. Students are required to maintain a consistent working pace by which weekly tests of concepts and strategies are realized, evaluated and calibrated. See PHASES for Assignments.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Statement of Academic Integrity

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process. Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust will merit a failing grade in this course and the violator(s) will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.  
Students engaging in plagiarism will be asked to redo their projects and failing to do so, will automatically be a F in the course.

"The School of Architecture uses 3 main principles - Transparency, Traceability, Accountability - to guide academic integrity best practices for the use of Generative AI tools for its course work. These 3 principles are adopted - and adapted to the School of Architecture context - from ACM Technology Policy Office's *"PRINCIPLES FOR THE DEVELOPMENT, DEPLOYMENT, AND USE OF GENERATIVE AI TECHNOLOGIES"*, June 2023

[https://www.acm.org/binaries/content/assets/public-policy/ustpc-approved-generative-ai-principles](https://www.acm.org/binaries/content/assets/public-policy/ustpc-approved-generative-ai-principles)

Transparency: Faculty and students shall be forthcoming of generative AI use, in general and particular instances, when applicable, and cite the circumstances and methods of use.

Guidelines/Recommendation:

- Course Content: where generative AI has been used to produce part of or the whole work, disclosure must happen at some point before the end of content delivery.
- Seminar Presentation or Discussion: where generative AI has been used to produce part of or the whole work, disclosure must happen at some point before the end of content delivery/discussion.
- Desk Critics between Faculty and Student: where generative AI has been used to produce part of the work, disclosure must happen at some point before the end of discussion of the respective project.
- Printed/Fabricated/Projected Work: Faculty and Student should be forthcoming of any and all, total or partial, work produced by generative AI software.
- Review Context: where generative AI has been used to produce part of the work, disclosure must happen by the student at some point before the end of discussion of respective project.
- Other non-specified contexts: where generative AI has been used to produce part of or the whole work, disclosure must happen at some point before the end of content delivery/discussion.

Traceability: when citing materials presented in the classroom, in a review or included in the FP Book, Faculty and students shall provide references and citations, when applicable, that enable tracing back originals of both sources and processes by using the Chicago Manual of Style for citation for Generative AI Tools, for both Text and Image. Citations for generative AI will follow the Chicago Manual of Style: [https://camosun.libguides.com/Chicago-17thEd/ai](https://camosun.libguides.com/Chicago-17thEd/ai)

Accountability and responsibility: Faculty and Students will be held accountable for decisions made by algorithms they use, even if it is not feasible to explain in detail how those algorithms produced their results. Responsibility will be held for entire work, not just for the individual parts that make up the whole.
