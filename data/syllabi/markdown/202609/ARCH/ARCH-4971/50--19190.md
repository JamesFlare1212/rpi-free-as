## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Spolia-Spolia** **The Formful Formless** Design Seminar

**Course Overview**

**Spolia**  
Spolia, perpetually unfolding.  
Spolia (from the latin spolium: spoils) are recognizable architectural elements (or groups of elements, or parts of elements, or groups of parts of elements), often made of stone, that are encountered and/or looted from old structures and used anew for the purpose of introducing allegorical ornamentation in an altogether different architectural artifact, celebrating, establishing dominion or appropriation of, resonating with, or overshadowing their provenance, and fundamentally carrying within them multiple elusive meanings that are projected as enigmas. Spolia is applied onto a building that in principle operates as a passive background to its figural presence. And yet, figure and background compete in a struggle of equals, often making it difficult to recognize which piece works as a figure onto which one working as a background. More boldly, spolia constitutes material organizations that, through a profanation process that involves the evaluation, recognition, decontextualization, segregation, categorization, organization, and repositioning of elements, migrate from one building into another, triggering and putting at work a perpetually incomplete process of architectural transformation. Spolia carry (or rather, miscarry), through this migration process, an active form of knowledge (that is, a form of knowledge that transforms matter), turning the latent capabilities of an architectural material (deliberately distorted by the migration process) into an actual architectural force and a source of a new form of sense that transfers vital and cultural impulses and messages from object to object. Spolia is live material, whose inertness is overtly challenged, turned into a force, and effectively made a vector of change. Spolia, thus, does not simply decorate a piece of architecture that persists as it would be without, but rather transforms the architecture itself of the building into which it is embedded. In its subtle versions, it incorporates disturbances that, because of their isolation from their context, create sparks that trigger an entire revolution in the concept of the piece of architecture. And, in its wilder versions, it redefines its very organization.  
**Spolia-spolia**  
Spolia over spolia, the perpetually unfolding of the unfolding.  
The incorporation of spolia into an already spoiled building structure introduces, in this context, a second level of ornamentation (the ornamentation of an ornamentation) and/or of transformation (the transformation of a transformation). This involves that the simple (and often ambiguous) figure-background relation of the single spolia is turned into a self-contradictory manifold of figure-background and figure-figure relations that alternate or overlap, thus disrupting the status of both figure and background, which then turn into one another in an ambiguous, inscrutable manner. A spolia-spolia operation blurs altogether the distinction between background and figure. It is not a matter of relative dominance, but a matter of mutual consecutiveness. What comes before and after, if one becomes the other in a process? What is fundamentally lost here, because of this manifold of ambiguities, is the background itself, the plane of support, the canvas, which happens to set back in direct proportion to the degree in which the uncertainty of the compound grows. This loss creates a point of no return to the source, by which, when iterated, the transformation process is turned into a loop of lineages (and not only multiple segments) of differentiation, flattening the historical process lying behind the linear idea of architectural evolution, and turning it into an instantaneous action where history happens all at once and keeps looping, as if in a limbo, thus squeezing the temporal unfolding of architecture as a discipline into the sudden objectual condition of a single building artifact. The spolia-spolia complex can be thus seen as a meta-authorial trans-historical act, the consistency of which is artificial and deliberate, and the form of which can be intensified, turned upside down, looped back, skipped over itself, and become redundant at wish. The content of this perpetual act frees allegories from any remnant of a linear historical determinism. Its structural artificiality opens the process of differentiation into the procedural real and flips the dependence of architecture both on the mandates of linear historical narratives and on the dependence of figure-ground compositional methods.  
**Pre-Spolia**  
Pre-spolia, the enduring folded latency of the perpetual unfolding of the unfolding.  
The seminar explores the construction of the material preconditions or perpetual latencies toward the potential unfolding of a spolia-spolia method. It distinguishes incipient, barely recognizable, and yet intensely determined forms within what, to an untrained, inattentive, and/or careless eye, appears to be formless and indetermined. The explicit aim of the exploration is to construct a repertoire of proto-formal modalities that constitute a progressively recognizable lineage, a formful vocabulary, present but on hold within the formless. And its implicit aim is to construct a material palette that can inspire the creation of pre-architectural organizations that can be eventually activated, through the iterative indexicality of the spolia-spolia method, by other architectural organizations, through montage, recognition of frictions, assemblage, topological integration, and differentiation. A series of more-or-less complex but discrete organizational materials, which we generally call debris, coming from different ancient Roman ruins (the Rome that is no more, but the Rome that will be), guided, both in focus and in spirit, by the apparent surveys, and rather inventions, depicted and presented as visionary architectural concepts and impossible material-spatial configurations by Giovanni Battista Piranesi in his unprecedented archaeological reconstructions. These literal recognitions of preexistences right in front of your eyes, these radical imaginary settings that predict still unforeseeable futures will work as seeds to construct lineages of formful modalities within the formless. These retroactive and proactive configurations, pre-spolia, will result of the identification of specific formal operations in ancient Roman ruins, a definition of their constituency, an intuitive analysis of alternative variations of their form, and the construction of a taxonomy of the variety of debris figures, which will be collected and organized as modalities.  
Note: implicit in pre-spolia, is the potential systematic running of figures to populate a medium that works as a primitive organization to be differentiated, thus transforming it. Iterations of spolia accumulate and generate organization through saturation, layering traces of formfulness across and within a formless primitive, thus complexifying their spectrum. The indexical operations intertwine the iterative linearity of the process.  
**Open-ended ruins**  
Open-ended ruins, snowballing modal consistency and meaningfulness of form.  
Ruins are not simply the remnants of a culture that has disappeared a long time ago and is largely forgotten. They are neither reminders nor just residues of a former presence. Nor are they traces that work as hints or as indexes that both reveal and conceal former long-gone pasts, nor are they incomplete incongruous fragments of things that are no longer among us, after having been eroded, destroyed or perished. Ruins are not simply the inert material leftover that refers to a dead culture, but rather the most palpable evidence of a latency, here and now, alive, open-ended, and meaningful. Furthermore, they are the here and now of a culture, its allegedly original state and meaning having become a sudden flagrant presence, characterized by what can be described as a form of meaningfulness beyond meaning. Ruins are enriched forms of matter that have not lost their meaning but that have rather shifted and opened it up to a manifold in latency. Ruins are culturally rich and open-ended working materials, wealthy mediums sharpened by time, open-ended containers of ideas yet-to-come, weapons loaded with manifold potential inventions, vehicles charged with both inertia and drive, embedded forms of architecture that carry forces and project propensities, the very presence of which shakes and steers the here and now. Ruins are the very present of architecture toward the many futures of architecture. And thus, they must not be thought of as the result of a process of loss or degradation of form back into matter. Its chaoid condition is not the decaying evidence of an irremediable entropic process of form becoming formless, indetermined, or undefined, but rather the opposite, the open-ended state of a continuous loop of construction and destruction, accretion and erosion, thought and mimesis, soulfulness and forgetfulness, contained in a tendentious compound, made of multiple convergent and divergent vectors, the negentropic condition of which, both chaotic and ordered, is formful, overdetermined, and sophisticatedly delineated. Cumulatively meaningful, ruins do not recall an original past as much as they call for many (yet not any) futures. The latent forms embedded in a ruin configure the blueprint of a prospective arché.  
The seminar explores, in this context, the uber-classical and trans-barbaric formfulness of ancient Roman ruins. Investigations takes the form of expeditions across the city, photographic surveys, systematic collections, and formal conceptualizations of lineages of formful modalities that anticipate upcoming forms of pre-architecture.  
**Lineages of formful modalities within the formless**  
A deverbal is a word (usually a noun or an adjective) formed or derived from a verb. The Formful Formless  
“On the other hand, affirming that the universe resembles nothing and is only formless amounts to saying that the universe is something like a spider or a spit.” George Bataille, L’Informe, Critical Dictionary, Documents, Paris, 1929.  
And yet, a spit is formful, both concealing and revealing a peculiar formal consistency.  
The formful formless overturns the assumption that there is such a state of things that can be (dis)regarded as indeterminate, and with this, it dismantles the myth of infinite openendessness, paradoxically rooted both in the modern idea of neutrality and in the postmodern idea of autonomy of form, reconfiguring them within the idea that the singular openendedness of tendentious pre-formal forms can be attentively treated, assumed, varied, distilled, diverted, exaggerated, differentiated, and/or synthesized.  
The formful formless involves, on the one hand, the daring embracement of the formal overdetermination and the multiplicitous cultural inertia that is present across all matter, the caring attention to the embedment of becoming processes within the present being of all things, and, inversely, as a consequence, the thorough and playful liberating engagement with these inertial conditions to turn existing beings into new becoming processes, highly determined and actually liberated via overdeterminacy.  
The formful formless is made of manifold nuances, embedded traits, turns, flips, folds, echoes, striations, seams, cracks, rims, niches, steps, seepages, efflorescences, markers, imprints, rubbles, mounds, propagations, protuberances, tiles, and mélanges, noticeable but barely recognizable hints of difference that, contained in a medium, appear, to an inattentive, indifferent, puerile, uncaring view, as accidents, anomalies, or curiosities, thus remaining consciously disregarded and/or unconsciously ignored.  
The formful formless results of revealing the singularities that are inevitably sedimented (and fortunately present) within the allegedly normal state of matter, of regarding them as the incipient conditions of a new normativity that systematizes difference, of turning a still diffuse formal potential into a highly differentiated form of order, and of following them to transform the normality of the normal. Debris as traces of upcoming becoming processes and new forms of order through the attentive systematization of difference.  
The formful formless turns the dominant presupposition of decay that is commonly taken for granted when both disregarding and celebrating architectural ruins, together with their enslaving referentiality to an original past, into new forms of architectural life, culturally loaded but ideologically unburdened. Loving contemplation, attentive recognition, caring treatment, counterintuitive description, uninhibited systematization, rigorous playfulness, and serious irresponsibility are its uncommon allies and friends.  
Excursions  
The working material of the investigation consists of sixteen pools of ancient Roman ruins, most of them located within the confines of the Aurelian Walls, a few beyond those limits, and some of them at the core of the Campo Marzio area. Each pool, made of one or more ancient enclaves, will be visited each week, in two-hour-walk excursions, during which students will survey the formal modalities that are commonly blurred by their formlessness and concealed by their status as ruins. Through these excursions, students will construct a taxonomy of variants of those modalities, making evident, though the focus, position, and framing of their mobile-phone cameras, the elusive characteristics of the operations hidden behind and/or blurred by formlessness.  
Operativity  
The Formful Formless is cumulative research made of subsequent photographic surveys in which consistent architectural forms hidden by the alleged formlessness of Roman ruins, are revealed in the form of images, regarded as registers of operations, and are edited and organized in a systematic taxonomy, crystalized by a portfolio. Etchings by Giovanni Battista Piranesi work as guidelines for the recognition and definition of these modalities and operations. The research is conducted first through photographic surveys in the context of excursions to ancient Roman ruins, and then through the editing of registered operations consistently varying across them.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 01 Research 01a_Ability to use/develop specialized knowledge Students live in Rome throughout the semester. Through the direct interaction with canonical architectural pieces, the involvement with urban conditions and phenomena, and the access to museums, artwork, and ancient ruins, in the city, students augment their disciplinary baggage and construct an ability to attentively observe and document multiple forms and scales of knowledge about buildings, cultural artifacts and sites that nurture their imagination of the architectural project with specific observations. 01b_Ability to use / develop broad and integrative knowledge Students live in Rome throughout the semester, engaging in a culturally immersive process consisting of regular visits to ancient Roman ruins and museums in the city, and of participating in real time in the cultural life and omnipresent history of the city. This engagement is fostered by a series of field trips to the outskirts of the city, Tuscany and the Veneto, providing a multilayered context of historical and cultural significance to the architectural projects, which deepens the referentiality of their projects.
- 02 Intellectual-visual design skills 02a_Ability to foresee design with imagination Students are conceptually and visually nurtured to develop an ability to ground design decisions on a hands-on visual approach to architecture as material. This approach is articulated by visually studying, surveying, and organizing the organizational knowledge embedded in existing buildings as sources of design intelligence. 02b_Ability to design with communicative fluency Students are challenged to fluidly communicate with (and be influenced by) different interlocutors at different stages of the course, from local professors of various backgrounds to student colleagues of other universities in the Rome Center, to guest professors that participate in reviews and the lecture series program.
- 05 Design execution 05a_Weekly Submissions Students demonstrate the ability to organize the result of an iterative process of the image-based design research in a sophisticated way through regular submissions of the material collected in photographic surveys, including a cumulative portfolio containing a photographic survey organized in an archive of source material. 05b_Final Review Students demonstrate the ability to implement the learning outcomes of the process of image-based design research in a systematic way through a final presentation and a final submission of the production, including a final portfolio containing the cumulative photographic surveys developed, organized in a systematic archive of source material.

They are opportunities to judge the project and critically evaluate the design process. It may be appropriate at points in the semester to evaluate only selected projects to convey information relevant to the entire class. In this situation, it is important for each student to witness and participate in the review process. Even if his/her project is not reviewed, the criticism and discussions inform the evaluation of his/her progress and design. Failure to attend one or more pin-ups for the full class period may result in a grade deduction.

**Desk Critiques**

The Seminar environment involves two-way communication. The role of the Seminar professor is to evolve and strengthen the student’s design process. Every time the student meets with the faculty, he/she must have new iterations of work based on previous conversations. If the student has not produced new work, the faculty reserves the right to skip the critique and continue with other students who have produced new material.

### Attendance Policy:

**Rome Center Talk Series**

Attendance is mandatory at all Rome Center Talk Series. Unless otherwise noted, lectures take place on Thursdays at 12:30pm or at 06:00pm at the main hall of the Rome Center. Unexcused absences from lectures result in grade reduction.

**Attendance**

Attendance is required at all class excursions during the semester. Two (2) unexcused absences from class will result in the lowering of the grade by one (1) letter. Four (4) unexcused absences from class during the term result in an automatic F. Whether an absence (health, immediate family death, officially approved field trip) is excused or unexcused is determined by the Students Experience Office (4th Floor Academy Hall, x8022, se@rpi.edu), upon presentation of required documentation, according to established policies and procedures (see http://se.rpi.edu/policies/ea/).

Students are required to work attentively during excursions across the city, and complementarily in the Seminar space during office hours or working weekends and have the required materials and tools to develop the assignments. Work includes active participation during site visits, including photographic surveys and conceptual discussions. Failure to work during class with undivided attention and disruptive behavior (tardiness, leaving early, lack of participation, general socializing), as well working on assignments from other classes during class, will count as absences.

**Timely Completion of Work**

All work must be submitted for interim reviews, submissions, and/or discussions on the dates noted under each assessment measure. However, final assessment of work does not occur until after the final submission. Students must respond to feedback received from the Seminar professor during classes and preliminary submissions to adjust and/or refine the work developed. Students are encouraged to attend office hours and tutoring sessions to help plan the completion of assignments at the different phases.

**Excused Absence policy**

There is no need for a medical excuse to be provided for absences of up to a week.

Students missing more than a week of classes due to illness will be required to provide a doctor’s note or other supporting documentation to the Student Success Office. The Class Dean will communicate the receipt of the note (with expected end date) to the relevant faculty.

Students are responsible for completing any work that they might miss, including the submission and/or presentation of assignments, due to illness, quarantine/isolation, or other circumstances.

Students who adhere to these processes should not be penalized per the attendance policy for the course and as detailed in the course syllabus. Instructors should make every effort to work with and provide reasonable modifications for students who cannot attend class due to illness to complete assignments and exams. It is recognized that virtual learning may not be possible in some in-person courses.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process.

**Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism**

Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust merits a failing grade in this course and the violator will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.

Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of the course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student. Any misrepresentation of work is evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student results in a failing grade for the course.
