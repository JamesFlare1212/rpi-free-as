## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

INST 4930V Social Innovation Abroad studies selected global challenges through engagement with the individuals, communities and agencies working to address them in local contexts.

This course is part of the Arkansas Global Changemakers project.

Changemakers aims to empower local communities to address global challenges by 1) raising awareness among change agencies and change ecosystems about the global context of local issues; 2) bringing Arkansas communities into dialogue with communities around the world facing similar challenges; 3) using intercultural competence and social innovation skills to adapt innovation, sustainable, and scalable solutions across national or cultural boundaries.

The AGC Rome sails with the 17 UN Sustainable Development Goals, their 169 targets and 232 indicators as the guiding lighthouse. Furthermore, it embraces the philosophy underlying the UN 2030 Agenda, with Opportunities for All and Leave No One Behind.

The Rome AGA has established connections with partners (communities, NGOs, grassroots, municipalities, scholars, academic institutions, social enterprises, private entities, cooperatives) making transformational change in the fields of: Food Security, Urban Resilience, Juvenile Justice.

Food security – the entire food chain, from farm to fork, from seed to plate – means sustainable agriculture, hunger and nutrition, health, environmental waste.

Urban resilience means public transport, social interaction, affordable housing.

Juvenile justice means job skills development, education, security and safety – a healed society.

All of these matters are threaded into the five pillars of transformational change: people, planet, prosperity, peace and partnership. And they intertwine with the four pillars of sustainability itself: human, social, economic, environmental. For this reason, not only are they relevant in Rome, Europe and the Mediterranean, but they are also interconnected with issues crucial in Northwest Arkansas – and, indeed, globally.

Combining academic and applied learning, the course teaches students to understand social problems as they appear both at home and abroad and leads them to develop innovative and scalable solutions to challenges faced everywhere.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

***Rubric of evaluation criteria:***

# ASSIGNMENTS:

\- **ATTENDANCE AND READING PARTICIPATION – 15%**

Also please refer to paragraph above re Attendance policy at UA Rome.

Students are required to attend all meetings (in-class and field visits) and **participate actively.**

Students are expected to be engaged in all scheduled excursions and field visits.

Participation includes joining in class discussions on assigned readings, responding to the discussion boards posted on specific readings, interacting with host agencies, guest lecturers and communities during excursions.

In a course that happens mostly outside of the classroom, students are expected to prepare all the materials autonomously and then come to the classroom or the field visit ready to use that knowledge to discuss and address research questions.

For this reason a portion of the grade is derived from the reading participation, which includes commenting in class, responding to content questions in the discussion board on BB, and stimulate each other’s critical viewpoints.

\- **TRIGGERING CHANGE – 30%**

In collaboration with John Cabot University in Rome, Oxford Brookes University, Turin Polytechnic and Richmond University, AGC students will partake the Triggering Change competition. The assignment consists in submitting a two-minute pitch that uses creativity to inspire action on sustainability. In each edition, the pitches address two critical UN Sustainable Development

Goals (SDGs). Participants engage in a comprehensive experience, including expert-led workshops, sustainability literacy assessments, and the creation of compelling, action-driven pitch. In addition to the grade for AGC, all Students who complete the program will be awarded a Certificate in Sustainability.

Submissions will be made via e-mail to cl060@uark.edu **and also** on JCU portal.

- \- **ISSUE PAPER – 20%**

Students will write one reflection paper in Editorial /Op-Ed style to submit via email at cl060@uark.edu (approximately 800-1000 words) on the themes identified and explored in the course (Food Sustainability, Juvenile Justice, Mafia, Sustainability, Urban Resilience), assessing their sense of the impact of the challenges faced, analyzing the link to the academic components, connecting the impact of this course and experience to their personal and academic goals.

Papers are **argumentative and personal viewpoints**, with based research from the reading material on BB and information acquired through lectures and guest lectures.

- \- **FINAL PROJECT – 35%**

The class will be divided into teams to apply the learning of the academic and cultural experience throughout the course and assess a challenge within the three themes which formed the focus of the course. The project will involve a full research analysis of the challenge and project chosen, a SWOT analysis, suggest viable solutions and recommendations for improvement, and especially link the project/challenge to an agency/challenge in Northwest Arkansas.

The project will be presented in class and submitted in the form of a power point (with bibliography and endnotes).

All written assignments must follow the Harvard manual of style. A copy is available on BB.

Written assignments must be clearly readable. A component of the grade will take into account ‘clarity’, which includes grammar, spelling, broken sentences, punctuation and general stylistic rules of the English language.

For the correct and transparent evaluation of all assignments, Professor Camilla Lai applies a grading rubric available on Blackboard for course attendees.

Late submissions will penalized 5 points per each day (24 hours) of late submission.

All written assignments are required to be submitted with:

- \- Cover page with Title and name of student (+ e-mail)
- \- Endnotes
- \- Bibliography

Use of AI is not allowed.

All assignments (papers, oral and written component of the presentations) will be graded according to the following components, in accordance with the learning objective identified in the syllabus and corresponding to the direct assessment tool.

# A. Content 40 %

1. **Research 30%**
2. **Originality 15%**
3. **Clarity 15%**

Each of the four above mentioned components is further explained below in full detail:

A: Content: The paper provides innovative argument on human right issue; the details focus on the most important information. Argument helps the reader see things in a new way representing a breaking theory in the issue at hand. Through this assessment tool, the student has demonstrated to have fully developed the ability to identify and distinguish specific human rights as well as to relate them to an expanded contemporary political debate. The student has also developed sufficient analytical skills so as to understand similarities and differences among different legislative contexts and frameworks.

B: Research: Argument in paper is fully and adequately supported by thorough research, with different legal frameworks and international legal instruments as well as recent reports, academic articles, documentation. The student demonstrated to is well acquainted with several international and regional human rights’ legislations and to be able to use them, as deemed useful and fit, to support own argument and opinion.

C: Originality: The written assignment, submitted via SafeAssessment where expressly indicated, is an original product by the student, and each citation is adequately identified, as well as indirect other source are acknowledged.

D: Clarity: Legibility and clarity include Grammar, Mechanics, Spelling, and Sentence Structure. Written assignments are written in English, without grammatical errors, spelling mistakes, broken sentences and non sequitur.

Taking into account the four above mentioned components, the following scale is applied (table C), where a paper will be regarded as level 5 if it has full clarity, maximum score on the Plagiarism software, innovative argument supported not only by research presented during classroom assignments, lectures, or as suggested in the syllabus or by the Professor, but also by autonomously conducted research on theme explored.

At the opposite side of the spectrum, a paper will score 0-2 if the clarity is hindered by grammatical and spelling errors, if it scores red on SafeAssessment, evidencing lack of acknowledgement of sources/plagiarism, inadequate argument not properly supported by research.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process.

**Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism**

Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust merits a failing grade in this course and the violator will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.

Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of the course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student. Any misrepresentation of work is evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student results in a failing grade for the course.
