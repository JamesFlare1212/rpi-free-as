## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

ELEMENTARY ITALIAN I is the introductory course to learning Italian language and culture. This class will give you the opportunity to cultivate interpersonal, interpretative, and presentational communicative skills. During the semester, you will access eye-opening and thought-provoking web resources. You will learn communication strategies to understand a variety of simple texts, express yourself in formal and informal settings, and acquire cultural awareness. The course utilizes various methodologies but emphasizes the communicative method. You will be expected to speak during class and engage with your classmates meaningfully.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- **Class Participation.** Regular attendance and active participation in class activities are essential to be a successful language learner. Therefore, you must prepare carefully for class by completing all the assignments in advance. The class participation and homework grade can be further affected by the student’s effort made to answer questions in Italian and take part in activities while remaining on target. You will lose 5 points on your participation grade with every unjustified absence.

  **Quizzes.** There will be several weekly quizzes during the semester. The lowest quiz score will be automatically dropped. Quizzes usually cover topics from the previous week(s).

  **Written Exams.** There will be two written exams throughout the semester: a midterm and a final. Each exam will consist of the following components: vocabulary, reading, grammar, culture, writing, and listening. Activities will be both receptive (multiple-choice, true-false, etc.) and productive/communicative (e.g., question-answer, open questions). Please, inform your instructor of any conflict as soon as possible.

  **Homework**. This course requires you to complete homework assignments (on Blink Learning platform) and written compositions by using the structures and vocabulary learned in class. Presence of grammatical structures not yet studied (verbs, pronouns, etc.) will negatively impact your grade for that assignment and is considered Academic Dishonesty since it is not an accurate reflection of your language skills

  During the semester, your instructor will assign you cultural activities where your attendance is mandatory.

  **Oral Exam.** Topics will reflect those discussed in class in the form of a brief conversation/dialogue between you and your instructor or another student. More information will be given during the semester.

  **Blog or Journal.** At the end of the semester, you will submit a personal ePortfolio created through WordPress.com (https://wordpress.com/) or any other software of which you may be aware (ex. Prezi, Vix,etc.). More information will be given during the semester. If you do not submit your ePortfolio, regardless of the grade that the other grade items generate, the final grade will be lowered by one letter.

Mandatory rubric of evaluation criteria:

**A-**The student **consistently exceeds the objectives of the course** and demonstrates excellent progress in developing basic Italian language skills. The student communicates confidently in familiar, everyday situations using appropriate vocabulary and basic grammatical structures. They demonstrate strong comprehension of simple spoken and written Italian and make effective use of the language learned in class. Work is **carefully prepared, complete, and submitted on time**. The student regularly practices Italian, actively participates in class activities, and demonstrates a willingness to communicate in Italian even when their language is still developing. Errors are generally minor and do not interfere with communication.

The student shows **excellent preparation, sustained engagement, and noticeable progress** throughout the course. They respond positively to feedback and increasingly use Italian independently.

**B-**The student generally **meets all course objectives** . They demonstrate a good command of the basic vocabulary and grammatical structures introduced in the course and can communicate effectively in familiar situations. The student understands the main ideas of simple spoken and written texts and can produce clear, appropriate language for their level.. Errors occur, but they generally do not prevent communication. Work is **generally complete, well prepared, and submitted on time**. The student participates regularly, makes a consistent effort to use Italian in class, and responds positively to feedback. They demonstrate good progress and increasing confidence in using Italian independently.

**C-**The student **meets the basic objectives of the course**. They demonstrate an appropriate developing command of vocabulary, grammar, and communicative functions. The student can understand and produce simple Italian in familiar contexts. Work is not always **complete and submitted on time**. The student does not always participate actively in class. Errors in grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation, and spelling are present. The student does not always apply feedback or demonstrate sufficient progress in learning.

**D-**The student **meets only some of the basic objectives of the course**. Their command of basic vocabulary and grammatical structures is inconsistent, making communication difficult in familiar situations. The student has difficulty understanding simple spoken or written Italian and/or producing basic sentences independently. Frequent errors in grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation, or spelling may interfere with communication. Work may be **incomplete, late, or insufficiently prepared**. Participation and regular practice are inconsistent. The student does not consistently apply feedback or demonstrate sufficient progress

**F-**The student **does not meet the minimum course objectives**. They demonstrate very limited ability to understand or produce basic Italian and have not developed sufficient control of the vocabulary, grammatical structures, and communicative functions required. Work is frequently **missing, incomplete, or submitted without adequate preparation**. The student demonstrates limited engagement with class activities and insufficient effort to practice and use Italian. There is **little or no evidence of satisfactory progress** toward the basic language skills required for successful completion of the course.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process.

**Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism**

Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust merits a failing grade in this course and the violator will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.

Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of the course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student. Any misrepresentation of work is evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student results in a failing grade for the course.
