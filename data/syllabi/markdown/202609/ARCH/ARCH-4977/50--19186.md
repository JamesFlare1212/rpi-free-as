## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course explores the connections between Fashion and Architecture. Fashion and architecture are two completely different and independent disciplines: architecture produces inhabitable art, while fashion gives us wearable art — and both shelter the human body. Even though the two industries rely on completely different sets of construction materials, they meet in terms of aesthetics, conceptualisation and process, and they play with the same elements and principles of design. Technological and scientific developments have shortened the distance between fashion and architecture, opening an interesting future in which the boundaries between them still remain to be explored.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand the complex interrelation between the two disciplines and demonstrate an enriched, cross-disciplinary point of view towards design.
- Develop divergent thinking — looking across disciplinary boundaries to compare and contrast concepts and generate new creative ideas.
- Demonstrate increased critical and problem-solving skills, together with strengthened and more inventive creative approaches.

### Attendance Policy:

Students must attend a minimum of 80% of the total contact hours to receive a passing grade. All absences, regardless of reason — including illness or personal matters — count toward the 20% limit; minor illnesses, even when supported by a medical certificate, do not qualify for exemption. Exceptions may be granted only in cases of serious and documented circumstances (e.g., hospitalization, family emergencies) and must be approved by the Academic Office in consultation with the instructor.

Students who exceed the 20% absence threshold will be formally notified by the instructor and issued a written warning acknowledging the potential impact on their final grade and course completion. Given the shorter academic week and the frequency of weekend travel within the study-abroad program, regular attendance is essential to academic success.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process.

**Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism**

Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust merits a failing grade in this course and the violator will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.

Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of the course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student. Any misrepresentation of work is evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student results in a failing grade for the course.
