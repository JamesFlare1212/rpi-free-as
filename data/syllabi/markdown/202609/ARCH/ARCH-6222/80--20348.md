## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

System Prototyping I introduces students to using Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) to study, construct, and represent complex systems in the built environment. The course treats AI not only as a set of tools, but as a way to frame systems: to define boundaries, reveal interdependencies, and test how local decisions produce global behaviors. Systems may include urban morphology and collective form, environmental networks, infrastructure, or any multi-variable, multi-agent system where feedback, constraints, and nonlinearity shape outcomes.

Students will work with available datasets to understand mechanisms inside a system, such as optimization problems in nonlinear dynamics or morphological logics embedded in urban form. Through hands-on exercises, students will build data-driven workflows, train and interpret ML models, and use AI-assisted tools to prototype their own system logics and system drivers. Alongside technical work, the course also engages theories of representation, information, and cybernetics to support critical discussion of AI in architecture and society.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. Frame complex built-environment questions as systems with boundaries, variables, constraints, and feedback loops.
2. Implement ML/AI workflows to infer system logic and workflow mechanisms from data, and interpret results with appropriate caution.
3. Formulate and test optimization problems (including nonlinear behaviors) to explore tradeoffs and performance improvements.
4. Translate computational insight into architectural representation and prototyping: drawings, diagrams, interfaces, and model environments.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Define a system of interest by establishing its boundary and scale and produce an environment representation with a basic data inventory (sources, variables, assumptions).
- Implement a baseline AI/ML workflow (data preprocessing → model training → evaluation) to infer relationships or behaviors in the system and interpret results in relation to system diagram.
- Prototype a visualization, dashboard, or interface that makes system behavior legible and supports scenario exploration and early design thinking.
- Communicate system insights through drawings, diagrams, and presentations, and reflect on the ethical and societal implications of AI-driven system design and modeling.
