## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Self-design plans offer RPI students the unique opportunity to innovate, explore, and expand their boundaries with immersive projects, job shadowing, and/or coursework and training. This personalized experience allows students to design a meaningful Discovery Semester that combines their educational/professional goals and personal interests, resulting in students who feel accomplished, responsible, and ready to make an impact in their field(s) of interest. Students will be challenged to develop in at least three (3) of the areas outlined by the National Association of Colleges & Employers (NACE) Career Readiness Competencies. These competencies are:

- Career & Self-Development
- Communication
- Critical Thinking
- Equity & Inclusion
- Leadership
- Professionalism
- Teamwork
- Technology

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Practice setting and achieving measurable, meaningful professional and personal development goals
- Possess a newly-created portfolio of work to showcase their experience (including but not limited to projects, course completion certificates/credits, an expanded professional network, a secured job offer, etc.)
- Understand the eight NACE Career Readiness Competencies (listed at the end of this document), how each connects to the world of work, and how they’ve personally developed them through their own self-set goals
- Be able to reflect on and effectively communicate the progress they’ve made

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

This course will require you to complete **three (3) written/survey assignments, three (3) assigned Percipio modules** on the NACE Career Readiness Competencies, as well as a series of **weekly** **critical reflection journals** throughout the semester. These assignments, modules, and reflections are to be directly connected to the three objectives you have set for yourself. At the end of the semester, you will receive an “S” or a “U” – complete all assignments thoroughly and thoughtfully and you will successfully meet the graduation requirement.

## Course Calendar

### Calendar Details:

**Assignment 1:** Objectives, Milestones, & Self-Evaluation | **DUE DATE:** 5pm Friday,September 11th

Using the three measurable outcomes you described when submitting your experience for approval, create a schedule of weekly milestones you will accomplish to keep yourself on track this semester. This document can be uploaded into the required self-evaluation survey, as well as a recent resume. **You should plan to actively work toward your goals for 15-20 hours per week during the semester in addition to completing required assignments.**

**Assignment 2:** Industry Exploration | **DUE DATE:** 5pm Friday, October 16th

Write a two-page (minimum), single-spaced report on the industry you are interested in pursuing. This will include identifying the industry, reporting on three potential job titles or roles that fall under that field, and determining/reflecting on which skills you already have *and* still need to acquire or strengthen to prepare for a career in said industry.

**Assignment 3:** Final Survey & Reflection | **DUE DATE:** 5pm Friday, December 4th

Submit a final self-evaluation survey and a reflection on your Discovery Semester. This will take the form of a two-to-three page, single-spaced essay. While the prompt is open-ended and offers you the opportunity to creatively tell your professional development story, the assignment should ultimately address the top three (3) NACE Career Readiness Competencies you have gained or enhanced during your Discovery Semester. This includes specific actions you took or projects you completed to develop those skills and why those competencies matter to your field of study.

**Percipio Modules | DUE DATE:** 5pm Friday, December 4th

As a part of your NACE Career Readiness Competency development, you will need to complete three (3) of the assigned Percipio modules. These modules will help you learn more about the competencies and how they relate to the world of work. **A link to the available modules will be added to the LMS course after the start of the semester.**

**Critical Reflections | DUE DATE:** Weekly on Sundays at 5pm throughout the summer

Each week, you will be required to submit critical reflection entries (at least 100 words) via LMS discussion boards to reflect on what you are learning during your experience. The prompts and corresponding weeks are listed below:

| **WEEK** | **PROMPT** |
| --- | --- |

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
