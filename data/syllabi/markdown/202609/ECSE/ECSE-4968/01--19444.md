## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course introduces the fundamental principles of RF and microwave engineering, emphasizing the physical understanding and design of high-frequency electronic systems. Topics include noise, linearity, transmission lines, impedance matching, and microwave circuit analysis using scattering parameters (S-parameters). Students develop practical design skills using Smith charts and microwave CAD tools. The course also introduces electromagnetic simulation techniques and RF measurements, including 2D/3D EM modeling and characterization of microwave circuits and components.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Student Learning Outcomes (for ECSE 4968):**

**Students Successfully Completing this Course will be able to:**

1. Understand the fundamental concepts of RF and microwave engineering, including transmission lines, impedance matching, noise, linearity, and stability in high-frequency circuits.
2. Analyze passive and active microwave components such as transmission lines, filters, and amplifiers using S-parameter techniques.
3. Design impedance matching networks and biasing circuits for RF and microwave devices.
4. Use Smith charts and microwave CAD tools to analyze and design basic RF circuits.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**For ECSE 4968:**

1. Homework: Homework assignments will include analytical problems on RF and microwave circuit concepts as well as simulation-based exercises using microwave CAD tools (e.g., Keysight ADS). These assignments reinforce theoretical understanding and introduce practical RF circuit analysis and design.
2. Midterm Exam: The midterm exam will assess students’ understanding of core topics such as transmission lines, impedance matching, S-parameters, and microwave circuit analysis.
3. Final Project: Students will work as a 2-3 people group to complete a final project involving the analysis and design of an RF or microwave circuit or subsystem. The project may include analytical modeling, circuit simulations, electromagnetic simulations, and/or experimental characterization.
