## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

ECSE 2610 is a design-oriented introduction to digital computer components and their operations. The course begins with number representation, Boolean logic, combinational and sequential circuit design, and hardware timing. These foundations are then used to construct and understand arithmetic units, registers, memories, buses, processor datapath, and control units.

A subset of the RISC-V instruction set connects digital hardware to assembly and machine-language programming. A short introduction to C-style memory, arrays, and pointers bridges high-level programming to assembly language. By the end of the course, students will be able to trace instruction execution through a basic processor and explain how memory, input/output devices, interrupts, and control mechanisms work together.

SystemVerilog hardware description language, simulation, and assembly-language tools reinforce the relationship between software-visible behavior and hardware implementation. SystemVerilog is introduced incrementally through short lecture segments coordinated with the laboratory sequence.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Represent information in standard binary codes and perform computer arithmetic, distinguishing carry from signed overflow and interpreting IEEE-754 floating point.
- Apply Boolean algebra, canonical forms, and Karnaugh-map minimization to design combinational circuits from gates and standard components, accounting for logic levels, propagation delay, and hazards.
- Design synchronous sequential circuits — latches, flip-flops, registers, counters, and finite-state machines — and handle asynchronous inputs and metastability safely.
- Design and explain datapath components: arithmetic logic units with status flags, register files, memories, and interconnect.
- Relate software to hardware: map C variables, arrays, and pointers to memory, write and trace RISC-V assembly including procedures and the stack, and translate instructions to machine code.
- Construct and trace a basic processor with its hardwired control unit, including memory-mapped input/output and interrupts, and model, simulate, and verify designs in SystemVerilog.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Exam-related assessments constitute 60% of the course grade: two in-class midterm examinations at 15% each and a cumulative final examination at 30%.

Short quizzes are given in class through the semester without advance notice. Each quiz covers the most recently solved homework and is given only after that homework's solutions have been posted, so reviewing the posted solutions is the best preparation. Approximately eight graded quizzes are planned; Quiz 0 on 9/4 was an ungraded example. The lowest graded quiz score is dropped, so a single absence carries no penalty; for that reason there are no make-up quizzes.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

# Laboratory Policies

- Laboratories that do not use the FPGA board are completed individually: Lab 1 (tool flow), Lab 5 (gate-level timing simulation), and Lab 9 (RISC-V assembly programming). The remaining board-based laboratories — 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, and 10 — are completed in teams of two, with one report submitted per team and each member identified with their contribution.
- A laboratory submission is valid only after the design has been checked off by a teaching assistant during the team's laboratory section or at a posted checkoff time.
- Checkoffs are granted per component, so a partially working design receives partial credit. Each team member must be able to explain the portion of the design they built.
- Reports document the specification, the design decisions and assumptions, the verification performed, and the results observed on hardware.
- All submitted HDL sources must be the team's own work. Instructor-supplied scaffolding may be used and must be identified as such in the report.

### Other Course Policies:

# Deadlines and Slip Days

- Homework is due at 11:59 pm Eastern. Homework 1 and Homework 2 are due on Thursday 9/10 and Thursday 9/17; from Homework 3 onward, each assignment is due on a Monday and its solutions are posted on the following Wednesday. Laboratory reports are due at 11:59 pm Eastern on the Tuesday of the week following the laboratory session; the report for the two-week finite-state-machine controller (Lab 7) is due one week after its second session. Every release date and due date appears in the course schedule.
- The integrating design project is due Friday December 11, 2026, at 11:59 pm Eastern. This is a hard deadline: slip days may not be applied to it, and no extension is available, because the demonstration and design review are held in the final laboratory session.
- Each student receives five slip days for the semester. A slip day extends a homework or laboratory-report deadline by twenty-four hours with no penalty and without requesting permission. Gradescope timestamps determine the number of days used, and any part of a day counts as a whole day. At most one slip day may be applied to any homework assignment, because homework solutions are posted two days after the due date so that students can review them before the following quiz. At most two slip days may be applied to any laboratory report.
- Slip days are spent in whole days and are drawn from each team member's own balance. A two-day extension on a team report costs each member two days, and a team may take an extension only if every member has enough days remaining.
- Slip days extend written submissions only. The in-laboratory checkoff must still be obtained during the team's laboratory section or at a posted checkoff time; a report is not graded until its design has been checked off.
- Slip days may not be applied to the design project, to either midterm examination, to the final examination, to in-class quizzes, or to in-laboratory checkoffs. A student who has no slip days remaining may still submit late within the same limits, with a penalty of 25% of the assignment's value for each day or part of a day late. Beyond the limits - more than one day late for homework, or more than two days late for a laboratory report - a submission is not accepted and receives no credit, because solutions have been posted by then. Extensions beyond these limits are granted only for documented illness, family emergency, or Institute-approved absence, and should be arranged with the instructor before the deadline whenever possible.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Intellectual integrity and credibility are the foundation of all academic work. A violation of the Academic Integrity policy is, by definition, a flagrant offense to the educational process. It is taken seriously by students, faculty, and Rensselaer, and it will be addressed. A student found responsible for academic dishonesty may be subject to an academic (grade) penalty administered by the instructor, to disciplinary action through the Rensselaer judicial process, or to both.

Four forms of violation are worth emphasizing in this course:

- Collaboration: deliberately facilitating an act of academic dishonesty in any way or form — for example, allowing another student to observe an examination paper, or using another's work in a report without citing it as another's work.
- Copying: obtaining information pertaining to a graded exercise by deliberately observing another student's paper or screen.
- Fabrication: the unauthorized falsification or invention of information in an academic exercise — for example, falsifying laboratory records, simulation results, or measured waveforms.
- Plagiarism: representing the work or words of another as one's own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference, in prose, figures, or source code.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines the full list of forms of academic dishonesty, and you should make yourself familiar with them. In this course every submission turned in for a grade must represent the submitter's own work. Where help was received, or where teamwork was permitted, a note on the submission must identify the collaboration. If you have any questions about this policy before submitting, ask for clarification.

These rules apply in full to hardware-description-language source, testbenches, simulation data, and assembly programs produced by a student or a team. Tools exist to detect similarity between submitted files, and the teaching staff reserves the right to use them. Instructor-supplied scaffolding may be used and must be identified as such in the report.
