## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

A broad introduction to modern communication systems, with applications to emerging wireless communication technologies. Fundamental principles of wireless channel modeling; performance limits of communication systems; analog and digital modulation techniques; multi-antenna communication systems; multi-carrier modulation; multi-user communication systems. Emerging wireless technologies such as MIMO and OFDM, with applications to cellular and wireless LAN communication standards. Emerging wireless sensing applications such as localization, tracking, imaging, using OFDM and FMCW Radar waveforms; Understanding time, frequency, and space as fundamental dimension for estimating distance, angle, velocity of target objects and developing intuition for their range and resolution; Complemented with PlutoSDR-based hardware labs and MATLAB/Python computational exercises.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Analyze different analog and digital modulation schemes in terms of their performance advantages and disadvantages.
- Construct different source and channel coding schemes used in wireless communication.
- Describe how OFDM and MIMO operate and are used in cellular communication standards.
- Contrast the relative advantages and disadvantages of different multi-access schemes for multi-user wireless systems.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Assessment for ECSE 4560/6560**

- **Homeworks + Hardware/Programming Labs**

A total of 8 assignments, assigned weekly, with regular homeworks and hardware (PlutoSDR)/ programming (MATLAB/Python) assignments. Instructions for labs are only provided in Matlab format. The homeworks will include some qualitative and quantitative questions, and the MATLAB/Python assignments would require some programing using these tools. Some labs involve working with PlutoSDR hardware.

*In the homeworks, ECSE 6560 students will be assigned one or more problems/subproblems that are more advanced than those assigned to ECSE 4650 students, requiring deeper thinking and focusing on applying mathematical tools to analyze performance of communication systems.*

- **Midterm Exams: 2 exams**

Midterm exams will be conducted during regular class hours. The midterm exams are not comprehensive; Midterm Exam 1 will cover channel modeling, modulation, OFDM. Midterm 2 will cover MIMO and wireless sensing applications. Exams will comprise of a mix of qualitative and quantitative questions – similar to the homeworks but without the MATLAB/Python assignments.

*In the exams, ECSE 6560 students will be assigned one or more problems/subproblems that are more advanced than those assigned to ECSE 4650 students, requiring deeper thinking and focusing on applying mathematical tools to analyze performance of communication systems.*

- **Final Project**

Student-defined project proposal or choice from list provided by instructor at the beginning of the semester. Students can build upon the MATLAB/Python assignments assigned in the homeworks into a more comprehensive project. Can be done individually or in groups of two. Students will be expected to make a presentation on the project at the end of the semester, submit a report and perform peer-evaluation.

*ECSE 4560 students can choose to replace programming component of the project with paper reading. They should read and summarize two papers in the final presentation and report.*

*ECSE 6560 students will assess the limitations of the current state-of-the-art for analyzing emerging communication systems and implement these techniques on a new system (e.g., Matlab toolbox) or dataset (e.g. NIST, ASU, Colosseum dataset) or hardware (e.g., PlutoSDR, FMCW Radar).*

### Other Course Policies:

**Course Policies:**

- **Quiz/Homework:** Per-class Quizzes will be assigned on LMS before every lecture (Lecture 2 onwards) and will be constitute of simple questions that will require you to pay attention to the material discussed in class. They will be due the end of day (11:59pm on the same day). Homework/Lab assignments will be posted on Piazza. Students need to submit their solutions on Gradescope by the deadline. These assignments will be posted roughly on a weekly basis.
- **Integrity:** All assignments are to be completed on your own. You are allowed to consult with other students in the current class or the instructor (e.g. through Piazza) regarding the conceptualization of the problem and possible methods of solution, but you may not share details or your final answer with other students. You may not copy solutions from anyone or any source. Failure to follow the rules will be considered violation of academic integrity.
- **Lateness:** Late submission of assignments are generally not allowed, and late submission without prior instructor permission will not be graded. Exception-1: Late submissions submitted by the late submission deadline on GradeScope (2-3 days after main deadline) will incur a 20% penalty. Exception-2: You are allowed a total of 2 late submissions of your choice until the late submission deadline on GradeScope free of penalty.
- **Class attendance/participation**: Attendance is taken for every class, and class attendance and participation is graded. Students are highly encouraged to attend lectures and ask questions during and after class. The lectures will be video-recorded, and handwritten notes made during the lectures will be posted on Piazza after each class.
- **Missed exams or assignments:** An approved absence is one that has been approved following Rensselaer’s rules and procedures, and should be documented, typically via a letter from the dean’s office, and emailed beforehand to the instructor. A missed exam due to an unexpected emergency should be communicated in writing as soon as possible.
- **Use of Wireless Devices:** Use of cell phones during the lectures or exams is not allowed. Use of other computing devices such as laptops, iPads, etc. is generally discouraged during class hours, unless used for taking notes only. Exams are open book, open notes, but no electronics, no internet, and no direct or indirect communication with any person other than the instructor is allowed. Any illicit information exchange during an exam will be considered a violation of academic integrity and will be dealt accordingly.
