## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Review of steady-state, dynamics and simulation, and small-signal power system analysis methods. Advanced modeling of power system components including loads, turbine-governor systems, HVDC systems, FACTS controllers, and other advanced grid-interfaced technologies. Analysis and control techniques cover reactive power control, damping control design, and subsynchronous resonance (SSR). Suitable for upper-level undergraduates and graduate students specializing in power systems.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Reflect on computations methods for steady-state, transient and small-signal stability analysis.
- Calculate HVDC operating conditions and define appropriate control modes.
- Develop and simulate power system loads, turbine-governor systems and voltage and power flow controls using FACTS devices (SVC, TCSC, STATCOM).
- Develop and simulate models of grid-enhancing technologies and alternative energy sources for transient stability analysis and small-signal analysis.
- Analyze, identify and potentially solve stability challenges in grids with new advanced transmission technologies.
- (ECSE 6180 Only): Synthesize advanced component models into a complex, research-level computational project. This includes a mandatory literature review and critical discussion of current research related to the chosen modeling task, demonstrating independent mastery.
