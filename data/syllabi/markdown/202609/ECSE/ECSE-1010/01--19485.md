## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides a broad overview of Electrical, Computer, and Systems Engineering (ECSE) through exploring five major content areas relevant to the ECSE department: electric circuits and power, control systems, image processing and machine learning, microelectronics and computer hardware design, and communication systems. In each unit, develop practical skills using essential tools, such as multimeters, breadboards, LTspice, Matlab/Simulink and microcontrollers, then apply those skills to complete hands-on projects in teams. ECSE faculty members give guest lectures in their areas of expertise to introduce the main concepts of each unit and give students an idea of what it is like to work as an engineer in their discipline. This course emphasizes developing practical skills through hands-on work, team-based collaborative learning and an understanding of the diverse areas of ECSE on a conceptual level.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Explain the basic concepts underlying the following areas of ECSE: (i) electric circuits and power, (ii) control systems, (iii) data processing and machine learning, (iv) microelectronics and computer hardware design, and (v) communication systems.
- 2\. Apply basic tools and analysis methods (resistive circuit analysis, LTspice, programming, Matlab & Simulink) to solve hands-on problems in ECSE topic areas
- 3\. Discuss current and future topics of interest in ECSE fields and describe what electrical and/or computer systems engineers do professionally in those areas
- 4\. Apply best practices for formatting technical data to prepare it for inclusion in a technical report

**Assignment Descriptions**

*5 Pre-Lab Assignments* – submission through Gradescope

Each unit contains a pre-lab assignment, which introduces the basic tools (LTspice, MATLAB, Arduino, etc.) needed to complete the unit’s lab assignments. While collaboration and discussion are encouraged, all pre-labs (except pre-lab 1) are individual assignments, and each student must submit their own work.

*5 Lab Assignments* – submission through Gradescope

The lab assignments form the core of the course. In each unit, groups of two students complete two hands-on projects to learn and apply foundational concepts. Students document their results and answer conceptual questions in an accompanying Gradescope assignment.

*5 Guest Lecturer Assignments* – submission through Gradescope

Each unit will include a guest lecture by an ECSE faculty member, highlighting the main concepts of the unit, topics of current and future interest, relevant career options, and key skills needed for work in the field. During or after the presentation, students will individually complete a short assignment based on the content of the presentation.

*5 Concepts Quizzes* – in-class handouts

At the end of each unit, students will be individually assessed on their high-level understanding of the material via a concepts quiz. Questions on the quiz will cover material from the lectures, pre-labs and lab assignments. These quizzes are closed-book and individual. Concepts quizzes are usually held at the beginning of class and students have 20 minutes from the quiz start time to complete them. Students who arrive after the start of a quiz are not granted additional time to complete it. Students who arrive after a quiz has ended receive a 0 for the quiz.

*5 In-Class Activities* – submission through Gradescope

Short activities completed in class will cover a range of topics including demonstrations of how to use essential tools, planning for completing a degree in ECSE and conversations on ethics.

*Class Participation*

The class participation grade will be based on answering concepts questions asked during most lectures.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are expected to attend class, although a few absences during the semester are understandable. The majority of the time in each class period will be dedicated to working on pre-labs, lab experiments and in-class assignments in groups. Class time is a time in which students are guaranteed to be able to work with their group members and get in-person help from course staff.

Students are required to communicate with the instructor prior to missing a scheduled assignment or quiz. In the case of a regular assignment, this means prior to the late due date; in the case of a quiz, this means prior to the start of the class in which the quiz is held. Official requests for alternate quiz times and/or additional time to complete quizzes will be accommodated if received two weeks before the scheduled quiz time. In cases in which arrangements for a makeup quiz cannot be made within a reasonable timeframe, the instructor and student will discuss alternatives.

### Other Course Policies:

**Due Dates and Late Due Dates**

Each assignment (excluding quizzes) will have two due dates: a due date and a late due date, both of which will be listed on Gradescope. The due date is generally set to be 11:59PM on the date indicated on the course calendar, while the late due date will be the next day at 11:59PM. While submitting on the due date ensures that you’re keeping pace with the class, you will not be penalized if you submit your assignment on Gradescope before the late due date. The late due date gives you some flexibility in the case that unexpected events prevent you from submitting by the first due date. Submissions after the late due date will not be accepted unless an official excuse is provided or special prior arrangements have been made with the instructor. If serious circumstances prevent you from completing the course’s assignments in a timely manner, please contact the instructor via email.

Intellectual integrity and credibility are the foundation of all academic work. A violation of Academic Integrity policy is, by definition, considered a flagrant offense to the educational process. It is taken seriously by students, faculty, and Rensselaer and will be addressed in an effective manner. If found responsible for committing academic dishonesty, a student may be subject to one or both types of penalties: an academic (grade) penalty administered by the professor and/or disciplinary action through the Rensselaer judicial process described in this handbook. Three relevant academic integrity violations to emphasize include:

Collaboration: Collaboration is defined as deliberately facilitating an act of academic dishonesty in any way or form; for example, allowing another student to observe an exam paper or allowing another student to “recycle” one’s old term paper or using one another’s work in a paper or lab report without citing it as another’s work.

Copying: Copying is defined as obtaining information pertaining to a graded exercise by deliberately observing the paper of another student; for example, noting which alternative a neighboring student has circled on a multiple-choice exam.

Plagiarism: Plagiarism is defined as representing the work or words of another as one’s own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference. Examples include using sentences verbatim from a published source in a term paper without appropriate referencing, or presenting as one’s own the detailed argument of a published source, or presenting as one’s own electronically or digitally enhanced graphic representations from any form of media.

Students in this course should be aware that the items emphasized above also apply to the experimental/simulation data, Matlab code generated by each student or student team towards the completion of the experiment report. **Policy on Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) Tools**

Artificial intelligence (AI) tools can be useful in helping you gain different perspectives on the course’s content by providing information that supplements what is already provided as part of the course materials. The general policy for the use of AI tools in this course is that AI tools *may* be used for gathering information and locating new sources of information (you must directly cite the sources that were used), but *may not* be used to generate any content that is submitted by a student as part of an assignment. Every element of all assignments you submit for this course must be fully prepared by you, and consequently, the use of generative AI tools for any part of a work that you submit will be treated as plagiarism. If you have questions, please contact me.
