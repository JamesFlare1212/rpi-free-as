## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a required first course for management majors and minors. It emphasizes broad, basic principles of managerial functions and processes using an interdisciplinary approach to goal-oriented situations of private and public organizations.

The course is designed to introduce students to the key managerial functions and processes used in businesses. We will cover finance, accounting, marketing, operations, entrepreneurship, business analytics, and other general management concepts. For those of you that are business majors, the class will serve as an introduction to these topics, such that you can be familiar with them as you work through your curriculum at Lally. For those of you that are not business majors, the course will give you familiarity with these concepts so that you can use them, or at least have a basic understanding of them, as you go to work in organizations as engineers, scientists, architects, etc.

We will use readings and lectures to provide content but then will use cases and/or exercises extensively in order to help you *apply* the ideas. In some deliverables you’ll apply concepts to specific questions but in others you’ll be tasked with making decisions and recommendations that would impact an entire business. That is one of the main objectives of this class: to get you experience in the complex, multi-faceted decision making that is done by organizational leaders.

You will also build team, interpersonal, and communication skills.

**Homework Expectations**

Homework is a required and important part of this course. All homework assignments are **open-book** and designed to be **straightforward** applications of course material. Their purpose is to help you practice concepts, prepare for exams, and **improve your overall course grade**.

Because homework is intentionally accessible, it represents an opportunity you should not skip. **Completed homework can significantly raise your grade**, while **missing homework will negatively affect it**. There is no way to “make up” the learning—or the points—by relying on exams alone.

In short: homework is one of the easiest ways to succeed in this course, but choosing not to do it will make success much harder.

**LATE SUBMISSIONS OF HOMEWORK ASSIGNMENTS.** Understandably, there are circumstances (e.g., job interviews, illness, family matters, etc.) that may cause you to miss class. ## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

**WHAT TO EXPECT**

This course will be using a flipped classroom approach *at times*. Students will be introduced to content at home through readings and some video lectures and will practice working through these principles during our live class time. This is the reverse of the more common practice of introducing new content at school, then assigning homework and projects to completed by the students independently at home. In this blended learning approach, face-to-face interaction is mixed with independent study, utilizing a variety of technologies. In a common flipped classroom scenario, students might watch pre-recorded videos or class lectures at home, then come to class to work on applying these skills and concepts in an in-class exercise, simulation, or group task, but you will be armed with at least some background knowledge.

As a student, you will interact with fellow students from different backgrounds, majors, and situations, providing opportunities to experience many perspectives and concepts throughout the semester. In addition to our in-person class time, we will also be using multiple communication methods such as video conferencing (WebEx Teams), chats, polls, online breakout rooms, email, social media, collaborative document sharing and more, you'll stay connected with fellow students, myself, and alumni guest speakers, building personal networks along the way.

Using different forms of technology, you’ll have numerous methods to interact with your peers, as well as myself. Teams will be formed early in the semester; you will be expected to work in collaboration with your classmates on group homework projects and assignments.

Some lectures will be pre-recorded and posted to LMS. You will be asked to watch the recorded lectures and answer any associated questions **prior to the start of our regularly scheduled class time.** The (live) class sessions will then be used to *apply* concepts that you read about or watched in the pre-recorded videos, and we will incorporate different activities into these live sessions.

All of your course assignments, discussions, quizzes, and homework, however, must be completed by their assigned due dates. Remember that there will be some group work that you need to take into consideration when allocating your time.

I am open to suggestions as the semester progresses. As we learn what works in our live format, and what can be enhanced, please feel free to email me suggestions for improvement. If you are aware of technology that can be utilized to make the class more efficient, more enjoyable, or simply more streamlined, please don’t hesitate to bring it to my attention.
