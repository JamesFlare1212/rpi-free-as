## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Overview:** The course is the first in a two-part series of Professional Development class sessions that teach practical skills to enhance business students’ readiness to enter the professional world and achieve workplace success and a high degree of professionalism. The course emphasizes the following themes: personal values and self-awareness, leadership and followership, ethics, basic oral presentation skills, as well as introductory employment search and application process skills.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Demonstrate and apply oral and written communication skills through class discussions, case analyses, formal and informal presentations, and written assignments.
- • Apply knowledge of leadership theories and skills.
- • Recognize and demonstrate professionalism.
- • Identify, demonstrate and evaluate their core leadership competencies through self-awareness of personal values and MBTI.
- • Demonstrate knowledge of ethical frameworks and techniques to make ethical decisions.
- • Apply knowledge of career planning resources and tools to effectively create a plan for identifying, applying to, and obtaining full time, internship, or co-op opportunities, including career searches through various job search platforms, application materials and online presence, professional networking, and effective interviewing techniques

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is mandatory and worth 10% of the final grade. Students who attend every class, or miss one class, earn ten points. Those who miss two unexcused classes earn five points. Students who miss three or more unexcused classes earn no credit for attendance. Should a student have five or more unexcused absences, they will **automatically fail** the course.

### Other Course Policies:

**Class Contribution:**

Students are encouraged to contribute thoughts and personal experiences whenever relevant. When students actively contribute to the discussion, everyone benefits from a more positive learning environment and the class becomes more interesting and fun. There will be different classroom activities that require interaction and then large group contributions. Some features might include classroom discussion, presentations etc.

**Academic Integrity:** Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and the Graduate Student Supplement (for 6000 level and above courses) define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in (1) an academic (grade) penalty and (2) reporting to Lally’s Associate Dean of Academic Affairs and either the Dean of Students (for Undergraduates) or the Dean of Graduate Education (for Graduate students).

In this course, the academic penalty for a first offense is a zero for the quiz, assignment, or test in question. A second offense will result in failure of the course as noted in Lally’s Three Strikes Policy.

If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

**Individual Instructor-Student Engagement Meeting (Optional):**

Students are encouraged to attend an individual meeting with the instructor during the semester. The meeting is designed to increase the instructor-student connection beyond the classroom setting. The purpose of this meeting includes discussion around: class progress and content, questions, feedback, professional development goals, etc. Students who attend a meeting will earn a maximum of 3 points added to their final grade point total. Individual meetings must occur between September 8, 2026-November 20, 2026, and while students may attend more than one meeting, the number of total maximum points added will be 3. Students can request a meeting via email (bondc@rpi.edu) and a date/time will be confirmed with the instructor.
