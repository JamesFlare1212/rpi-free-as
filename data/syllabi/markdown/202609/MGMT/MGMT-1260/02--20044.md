## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This undergraduate course is an introduction to ethical frameworks and business law. Ethical approaches such as utilitarianism, rights, justice and virtue ethics will be discussed. Legal frameworks currently being applied in the business environment in the United States will be discussed.

The course looks to engage with moral and legal challenges that businesses currently face and may face in the future, such as maintaining a safe workplace, respecting employee rights and responsibilities, addressing threats to data privacy and security, issues pertaining to climate justice and environmental sustainability, and issues arising due to employee surveillance mediated through digitization among others.

Readings include Harvard business cases and media articles that highlight the societal benefits and societal costs that businesses may perpetuate. Students use case studies and readings as tools to understand and critique current business practices in the realm of globalization, marketing, employee well-being, automation and the adoption of AI, and the impact of businesses on the environment.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. Become familiar with different ethical and moral frameworks for decision making
2. Understand the evolution of business ethics and the development of business laws
3. Develop an understanding of the effects of law, government, society, technology and ethics on business creation and operation.
4. Practice how to identify and resolve ethical tradeoffs in professional, and business investment decision making contexts.
5. Develop critical understanding of current business ethics issues: AI ethics, Intellectual Property Protection etc. by reading and analyzing challenging cases
6. Developing oral, written and presentation communication skills via written assignments and group projects.

# COURSE POLICIES

The structure of this course will be a mixture of lectures, in-class group work, and case discussions. I expect you to arrive in class having carefully read that day’s readings and prepared to engage critically with them.

You are expected to treat your classmates and their opinions with courtesy. Failure to do this will result in a penalty in your participation grade and may even result in removal from class if it is deemed disruptive to the class.

**Smartphone and laptop use is not permitted in class**. **This rule will be strictly enforced**. **I will deduct attendance and participation points for electronic use and distracting behavior in class.** This is to create an environment conducive to learning. You may use tablets for notetaking with prior approval. I urge you to use a notebook for notetaking. There is plenty of scientific evidence to suggest that student outcomes are much better without laptops in the classroom. https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/students-are-better-off-without-a-laptop-in-the-classroom/

# ACADEMIC INTEGRITY

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities define various forms of Academic Dishonesty, and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work, including exams. **In this course, the academic penalty for a first offense will result in a penalty of a failing grade for that exam or paper (subject to review of circumstances) and will be reported to the Associate Dean for Academic Affairs. A second offense will result in failure of the course as noted in Lally’s Three Strikes Policy.** Be aware that it is your responsibility for the security of your own work.

If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

**AI POLICY**

Use of AI tools, including ChatGPT and other LLMs, is permitted in this course for specific purposes. You may use these tools to conduct research (although you must verify the information these tools provide you) or to proofread and improve the quality of your writing.

Use of AI tools is prohibited when attempting exams/quizzes. My recommendation is to use AI to extract information and generate ideas. To adhere to our scholarly values, students must cite any AI-generated material that informed their work (this includes in-text citations and/or use of quotations, and in your reference list; using different color font to highlight text generated by AI). Where applicable, you must also verify the content generated by AI is true. Do not assume that AI tools are providing factually correct information. Finally, using an AI tool to generate content without proper attribution qualifies as academic dishonesty.
