## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Main course objective is to introduce students to basic economics principles and establish economics as a managerial decision-making framework. The course will draw on economic analysis to discuss topics that include supply and demand, consumer behavior, firm production, the effect of market structure on firm behavior and market prices, optimal pricing strategies, game theory, and the role of information. The course also introduces some behavioral economics concepts in its approach. The material in this course is relevant to managers of for-profit institutions and non-profit organizations, and to policymakers. After taking the course, the students will be empowered with some of the most important tools necessary to tackle operational business decisions.

Success in this 3-credit course is based on the expectation that students will spend a minimum of 6 hours of study time per week in preparation for class (readings, assignments, group projects, preparation for exams, etc.)

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Apply theoretical economic frameworks and mathematical approaches to management decisions while knowing the difference between rational economic models and behavioral economics.
- Understand the concept of supply and demand and elasticity and be able to estimate and forecast demand, production, and costs.
- Understand pricing and output decisions under perfect competition, monopolistic competition, and monopoly.
- Evaluate potential investment opportunities in various sectors and understand the concept of real options in capital budgeting.
- Understand the role of government, regulation in the market economy, and international trade.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**1. Attendance, Preparation, and Participation**: Students are expected to attend all lectures, come prepared by having done the assigned readings, and participate during lectures. The participation grade is based on consistent, thoughtful contributions or questions. To facilitate the class discussion, students are required to bring **name-cards (with large and legible lettering)** to each class. Throughout the semester, the instructor will assign specific readings to a selected group of students.

**Selected Reading Group:**

Throughout the semester, the instructor will assign specific readings to a selected group of students. The assigned group is responsible for leading the discussion on the reading during lecture.

**One-minute memos:**

As part of the class participation grade, students will be asked to write a one-minute memo at the end of some of the lectures. The purpose of the one-minute memo is to establish a direct line of communication between the students and the instructor. The memo will help the instructor assess the class’ understanding of the main concepts discussed during the lectures, identify unclear points, and address any other concerns.  
Please note the main objective of memos is to enhance students’ understanding of the material so honesty is important. Hence, the grade is not affected by the content of these memos. The instructor will respond to the major questions raised in the memos during the following lecture. Missing a class or failing to submit a memo will result in an absence being recorded.

**2.** Assignments**:** There will be 6-10 assignments. Only a randomly selected number of the problem(s) from each assignment will be graded, and the rest of the problems will be checked for completion. Students are responsible for turning in completed assignments by the due date. Unless the student has made prior arrangements with the instructor, no late assignments will be accepted. Solutions will be provided immediately after the due dates.  
3\. Exams**:** There will be two closed-book exams. Each exam will cover material discussed in class up to the exam date. The second exam will focus mainly on material covered after the first exam.  
**4. Final Group Project & Presentation****:** Students will be divided into groups of 4-5 to work on a research project. The groups will be the same as the groups assigned for the selected reading. More information regarding the research project will be given after the first exam. The written research report will include the following:

o 6-8 pages max of text and a max of 15 pages total of text, figures, tables, and references.

o Sections: an abstract, introduction, a short literature review, methodology, results and implications, conclusion, and list of reference.  
o Clean and well-documented data files (codes and data)
