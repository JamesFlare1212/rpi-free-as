## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course examines how to plan, execute, and evaluate integrated marketing communications (IMC) across channels. Topics include IMC fundamentals; consumer behavior and persuasion; source, message, and channel effects; creative strategy; media planning and evaluation; television; digital media; sales promotion; public relations; ethics; and measurement of communication effectiveness, among others. Students build foundational knowledge and analytical thinking through lectures, develop practical ability through applied assignments, and strengthen comprehensive capability in researching, reasoning, and communicating through presentations.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Master core IMC knowledge.
- Connect insights from external readings to core IMC knowledge to strengthen your understanding of the subject.
- Find, evaluate, and synthesize information through research.
- Communicate clearly, logically, and insightfully in presentations.
- Analyze real advertising and promotional examples.
- Create simple advertising/campaign outputs.
- Practice critical thinking by asking high-quality questions and reasoning from first principles.
- Develop strategic IMC thinking skills.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).
