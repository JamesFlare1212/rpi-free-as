## Course Goals

### Goals:

The increased professionalization of the workforce and high value placed on technology and knowledge work in the global economy emphasize the importance of good workforce management skills for managers and professionals. To be effective, we need to act as "applied" behavioral scientists, that is, we must carefully study our organizations, units, and direct reports and develop an ability to recognize what makes each one tick. In this course more than most others, we need to know not only the conceptual material presented but also how it applies to our work lives and ourselves. People who understand the work environment, know themselves, and can assess their effects on others are more likely to be effective than those who are blind to these dimensions.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

This undergraduate course provides an overview of basic processes in human behavior that influence the effectiveness of individuals, groups, and organizations. Its focus is on understanding what happens during interpersonal interactions in work situations, and what can be done to make employees more effective. Topics covered include organizational socialization, motivation, decision-making, team dynamics, virtual teams, influence, and conflict management. Numerous exercises and case analyses are used in class to help provide students with insights into these processes.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Exams (45%)**

Each exam will consist of 30 multiple choice questions and only cover the material leading up to it. Exams are meant to assess your ability to identify and understand the class content. Information from lectures and assigned reading will be included in exams.

**In-Class Work (20%)**

Each class will consist of an assignment that aims to test your understanding of the lecture material. These assignments may consist of mini-case studies, group debates, and other interactive experiences. In-class work will occur during the last 20 minutes of the class, with validation of participation or submission accepted only at the very end. The two lowest in-class assignment grades will be dropped to allow for dire circumstances.

**Quizzes 15%**

17 quizzes will be given throughout the semester, without prior notice. Quizzes **cannot** be made up. Quizzes will be given at the very beginning of class and will only be passed out once, meaning that late arrivals will not be able to take the quiz. The two lowest quiz grades will be dropped to allow for dire circumstances.

**Group Presentations (20%)**

Students will be put into randomly assigned groups responsible for presenting on an assigned topic. Full details about presentation requirements can be found in a separate document on the LMS. Groups will have two separate instances in which they must present. The first presentation will account for 5% of the full 20% grade. The main purpose of this presentation is for students to gain feedback. The second presentation will account for the other 15% of the grade, with the expectation that students will integrate the feedback from the first presentation.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute is committed to the principles of intellectual honesty and integrity. All members of the RPI community are expected to maintain complete honesty in all academic work, presenting only that which is their own work in tests and assignments.

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and the Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a grade of zero on the first assignment where a violation is detected, and the infraction will be reported to the Associate Dean for Academic Affairs. The Lally School has a Three Strikes Policy. A second instance of cheating by the same student in any course will result in the student receiving an F for that course. A third instance of cheating will result in expulsion from the program.

Plagiarism is always a violation. For any assignment, I will tell you in advance what the expectations are for individual versus group work.

If you have any questions regarding what is acceptable related to any aspect of course work, please contact me before the situation becomes problematic.

In this course, the academic penalty for a first offense is a zero for the quiz, assignment, or test in question. A second offense will result in failure of the course as noted in Lally’s Three Strikes Policy.

RPI is committed to the promotion of an environment for work and study which upholds the dignity and respect of the individual, and which supports every individual’s right to study and/or work in an environment which is free of any form of harassment, intimidation or bullying.
