## COURSE DESCRIPTION

This is an introductory course in data analytics that covers topics that you will use in future management courses. You will learn how data are generated in organizations and how data move through the various departments (Module 1), and how data are stored (Module 2), and how data can be converted to information and used for strategic, tactical, and operational decision making. You will also become proficient in relational databases (Module 2) and spreadsheets (Module 3), and practice using specialized software to conduct business intelligence and visualize data (Module 4). This course is highly interactive and you will need to come to class prepared to do the hands-on class activities individually and with a group.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

## ACADEMIC INTEGRITY

Student-teacher relationships are based on trust. Students need to trust that the teacher has made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the course, and the teacher must trust that the assignments that you turn in are your own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty, and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent your own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in (1) an academic (i.e., grade) penalty, and (2) reporting to Lally’s Associate Dean of Academic Affairs and the Dean of Students.

In this course, the academic penalty for a first offense is an automatic forfeiture of the grading component where the offense occurred. A second offense will result in failure of the course as noted in *Lally’s Three Strikes Policy*.

If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

***A special note on plagiarism.***

Please see the definition of plagiarism given below, as outlined in The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights. To be clear, any time material that is not the students’ own is used, it should be in quotes and contain a citation. If a student paraphrases work from another, it also requires a citation. Furthermore, even when citations are used, others’ work cannot be used as a student response on an assignment. In the case of software, we will address the topic of “copying” which is considered a common practice in software development in relation to ethics and conduct of software development.

***“Plagiarism.*** Plagiarism is defined as representing the work or words of another as one's own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference. Examples include using sentences verbatim from a published source in a term paper without appropriate referencing, or presenting as one's own the detailed argument of a published source, or presenting as one's own electronically or digitally enhanced graphic representations from any form of media.*”* - ACADEMIC INTEGRITY (Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights & Responsibilities, November 2017 rev.)
