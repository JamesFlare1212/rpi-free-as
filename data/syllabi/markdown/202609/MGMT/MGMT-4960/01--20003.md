## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The primary objective of this course is to provide the first or second semester financial engineering student with basic quantitative and analytical tools necessary for sound financial decision making, particularly in quantitative finance and risk analytics contexts. The course emphasizes financial computation techniques, whereby students learn standard methodologies to obtain numerical solutions to financial problems. Topics include solving linear and nonlinear systems of equations, optimization, lattices, Monte Carlo simulation, and finite difference methods as applied to common financial problems. R is the primary computing language used in this course.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1\. Develop quantitative and computational skills applicable to financial engineering and risk analytics.  
2\. Able to successfully apply computational tools such as numerical analysis, Monte Carlo simulation, finite difference methods, lattices, linear, and optimization to the analysis of financial instruments and markets.  
3\. Able to apply basic computational skills to successfully value and replicate a variety of types of financial instruments under varying market conditions and settings for hedging and regulatory purposes.  
4\. Able to apply standard hedging and relative valuation and hedging techniques for portfolio analysis.  
5\. Apply basics of simulation – random variable generation, statistical analysis of simulation output, and variance reduction methods.  
6\. Ability to simulate to value securities, estimate the Greeks, and assess risk management strategies.  
7\. Ability to use simulation to make investment decisions and measure investment performance.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The grade for the course will be based on performance in four activities: exams, projects, homework assignments, and class participation.  
Project (LO4):  
One project will be done in groups of 2-3 students for MGMT 6510 students. The project will be assigned about halfway through the semester and will have several milestones as outlined in the project description. Each group will make a final presentation based on the project.  
The project is worth 30% of the final grade. The grades will be based on both group and individual performance. Details will be clearly outlined when projects are assigned. The final deliverables (written report) will be due during finals week.  
Exams (LO1, LO2, LO3, LO4):  
Exam structure: Exams will involve two parts – 1) a take home portion due at the start of the class when the in-class written exam will be administered (75%) and 2) an in-class, handwritten exam (25%). MGMT 6510 students will take two mid-term exams and MGMT 4960 students will take three exams. The third exam will be administered during finals week in virtual mode.  
MGMT 6510: There are two exams that will be given roughly 1/3 and 2/3’s of the way through the semester. The exams will be worth 40% of your grade. The second exams will be worth 2/3’s of the 40% and the first one is 1/3 of the 40%.  
MGMT 4960: There are three exams that will be given roughly 1/3 and 2/3’s of the way through the semester and one during finals week. The exams will be worth 50% of your grade. The weighting of the exam scores will be 40%, 30%, and 30% of your exam grade, with the second one holding 40% allocation.
