## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course aims to introduce a variety of concepts in both corporate and investment finance and accounting, providing a solid foundation in financial decision-making principles and practices. It assumes minimal prerequisites, such as basic statistics, algebra, financial accounting, and a general interest in business, making it suitable for beginners. However, even students with prior finance knowledge will find value in the course through deeper insights and a chance to reinforce their understanding.

The course starts with asset valuation principles, applying them to various real and financial assets, including personal finance, corporate projects, debts, equities, and firms. It explores the relationship between risk and the required return for different investments. The course also covers corporate capital structure, tax effects, and their implications for valuing corporate projects, culminating in firm valuation, which integrates nearly all course topics.

As a broad survey, the course covers many topics, emphasizing both the “how” and “why” of financial decision-making. Due to time constraints, not all topics can be covered in depth. Active participation and idea-sharing are crucial, and regular attendance and preparation are expected to enhance learning and performance.

The course primarily follows a lecture/discussion format, supplemented by seven in-class case studies involving discussions.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

ASSESSMENT

There are 4 required homework sets, 9 required quizzes (9 best out of 10), 6 in-class case studies, five in class pop-up quizzes, a midterm and a final exam. Assignments and quizzes must be done on-time. Homework assignments will be due exactly ten days from the date and time assigned, while online quizzes will be due 48 hours from the date and time assigned.

Your grade for the course will be determined by homeworks, short quizzes, in-class case studies, class participation, mid-term and a final exam:

MyFinanceLab Quizzes Nine @ 2% each = 18%

MyFinanceLab Homework Assignments Four @ 4% each = 16%

In-class Case Studies Six @ 2% each = 12%

Exams 15% (midterm), 29% (comprehensive final)

Class Participation 5%

In-class Pop-up quizzes Five @ 1% each = 5%

Bonus Points Up to 8% (2 Games @ 2% each)

Please note that the midterm counts for significantly fewer points than the final. This design allows the midterm to act as a “red flag” or warning for (some) students, while still allowing those students a reasonable chance to “catch up” by the semester’s end. Both exams will be “closed book/notes, open calculator,” some formulae will be on the cover page.

In addition to the required problem sets, there are optional problems (with solutions) included (will be included at a future date) on MyFinanceLab. I recommend these problems to you. These are written to help extend your understanding of material presented in class, and to help prepare you for the required problem sets and examinations. If you would like even more problems, beyond these, then MyFinanceLab offers an “infinite” supply of problems.

MyFinanceLab Homeworks and Quizzes (must be done)

These must be done individually and online at MyFinanceLab. These must be done on-time as once the due date passes, your access to the assignments will end. Observe that for any given homework problem (but not for any quizzes), you can take hints in exchange for a partial deduction in your feasible score for that problem.

Assignments are made for students’ benefit. Two attempts per question will be permitted on homework assignments. All assignments close at midnight on the due date. Assignments will be automatically submitted “as is” on the due date. They are all individual-basis homework. No late assignments will be accepted and no extensions (for both assignments and quizzes) will be granted.
