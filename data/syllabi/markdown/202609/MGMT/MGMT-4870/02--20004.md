## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

In this course you will integrate learning from various functional fields of management to develop your skills in the following areas:

- analyzing business situations and formulating strategy
- implementing strategy and making decisions that enhance a firm’s competitive advantage
- communicating strategic concepts and ideas persuasively to internal and external audiences

At the end of this course you will develop an appreciation of how various elements of an organization fit together to result in superior performance and sustained competitive advantage. In addition, you will also be able to identify effective business and corporate strategies, and differentiate them from those that are weak and uncompetitive.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Some student learning outcomes:**

- Learn how to conduct an in depth industry analysis
- Apply frameworks to understand firm strengths, weaknesses, sources of competitive advantage
- Learn the costs and benefits of different business level strategies
- Analyze the costs and benefits of various firm scope decisions- vertical integration, product diversification, geographic expansion
- Identify the appropriate entry mode for expansion- e.g. alliances, acquisitions
- Analyze different organizational structures and their benefits and disadvantages

**Class Participation (Individual)**

Class contribution is an essential and significant component of evaluation and will make a significant difference to your grade. In order to facilitate a constructive discussion, please go through all the material assigned for a particular session (see the class schedule below). You should be able to offer insights into the central themes being discussed, and outline the main ideas/concepts in each reading and case.

Depending upon how the class progresses, I will assign additional material and homework from time to time. You are expected to go through these additional readings and complete the additional homework assignments by the due date. The additional homework assignments will be graded as Satisfactory/Unsatisfactory. Not completing these required assignments will result in a reduction of class participation points.

Your class participation in the semester will be graded based not just on how often you participate, but on consistent evidence and demonstration of the knowledge of assigned content. If you participate but do not connect the discussions to assigned material, then that will be reflected in your grade.

An attendance sheet will be circulated at the beginning of every class. It is your responsibility to make sure that you record your attendance. You are allowed a maximum of *two absences* (unexcused without documentation). Any additional absences will require clear and supporting documentation (e.g. a doctor’s note) giving reasons for why you should be excused from class. In general, unless there is an emergency (e.g. a well documented health emergency), I would expect your total absences to be minimal. Unexcused absences and excessive excused absences will reduce your class participation grade. For instance, three unexcused absences will reduce your attendance grade by one full letter (ex. B to a C or 6 percentage points), after which three percentage points will be deducted for every additional absence.

Note: Attendance alone will not count toward your class participation grade, and the two will be graded separately.

Student athletes- you will be excused from class only with supporting documentation, and for travel etc. for games. Please arrange practice and other schedules so they do not interfere with class attendance.

**Quiz I and II (Individual)**

Dates for the two quizzes are indicated in the schedule (February 26th ; April 16th). These quizzes will cover all topics discussed in class up to that point in time, including cases. An important objective of this course is to help you develop an ability to critically evaluate the material covered. Hence straightforward questions requiring a commitment to memory will not be on the quizzes.

Please make note of the dates of the quizzes and ensure you are in class on those dates. Make ups will not be provided, barring very exceptional circumstances, such as health related reasons (supporting documentation will be needed).

**Case Analysis- Report and Presentation (Group)**

The group case report and presentation account for 12.5% of the final grade. Students will be earmarked into groups of about 5 members. Each team will be responsible for preparing a written report and making a formal presentation of their analysis and recommendations. The enclosed schedule indicates the case and the date it is to be presented. Broadly speaking, your presentation will cover the background of the case, the issues identified (see the discussion questions for each case below), recommendations for the future, and any other points that you think are relevant.

Each team will prepare a report that is no longer than 12 pages, excluding the cover page (please see the guidelines for written reports below on page 3). In addition, each team will make a presentation in class for a maximum of 20-25 minutes (guidelines are also provided below for presentations on page 3). Note that the report is due on the day the case is scheduled to be presented. Late submissions will not be accepted. Please email your report and slides on the day the case is to be presented, ahead of class time.

About Cases and AI- The cases discussed in class are designed to illustrate specific concepts and decision scenarios. Please therefore do not look up any online material or ‘solutions’ for the case, including using any ChatGPT/LLMs for analyzing the case which is **strictly prohibited**. Any attempts to do so will be treated as a serious ethical violation, and in the minimum, will be treated as the equivalent to a first offense (see below for Academic Integrity policy).

**Workshop Assignment (Group)**

The workshop assignment requires you to develop an insightful presentation on an assigned topic (e.g. the role of culture, a real life example of a successful or failed acquisition). You will be given brief guidelines ahead of the assignment, and your task is to develop a presentation of about 20 mins as a group. The purpose is to not only get you thinking about the topic, but also to inform the rest of the class and enhance their understanding of the subject. It is essential that you develop an in depth analysis and come well prepared to present on the day that the workshop assignment is scheduled, since you will be playing the lead role in these discussions. Please email your workshop slides on the day your workshop is scheduled, ahead of class time

Note- For the workshop, you may use LLMs to provide a basic framework/ starting point; However be prepared to identify and discuss what insights you added, what are your unique thoughts, how you expanded on the LLM output and tied it to class discussions etc. A simple ‘cut and paste’ of LLM output will not work, and you need to be ready to defend yourself.

Students must follow all institute safety and health protocols at all times throughout the semester. There will be no exception to these requirements at any time.

In addition, please abide by the following rules:

1. Avoid coming late.
2. Turn off your cell phones

Any behavior that interferes with the learning environment in class will be viewed seriously. **Guidelines for Written Reports and Presentations**

Written reports should be prepared in a professional manner, with attention to both content as well as stylistic aspects. Please submit reports formatted with 1-inch margins all around. The report should be double spaced, and the font size should be 11. Your report should use appropriate headings/sub headings to reflect what you are discussing in each section, and to guide the reader throughout. Please proofread your report adequately to ensure there are no grammatical and spelling errors.

Please include page numbers while adhering strictly to the specified page limits. E-mail me your report no later than 10 mins. ahead of class time the day it is due.

The objective of your presentations should be to communicate your ideas clearly, and to generate audience enthusiasm about the topic. The presentations, like the report, should be prepared in professional manner with minimal typos etc. For the presentations, please email a copy of the slides you intend to use no later than 10 mins. ahead of class time the day it is due.

**Academic Integrity**

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in (1) an academic (grade) penalty and (2) reporting to Lally’s Associate Dean of Academic Affairs and either the Dean of Students (for Undergraduates) or the Dean of Graduate Education (for Graduate students).

In this course, the academic penalty for a first offense is 30% of the total course points, irrespective of the assignment involved. A second offense will result in failure of the course as noted in Lally’s Three Strikes Policy.

If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

**Group Assignments and Peer Review**

40% of your grade is determined by group case assignments. At the end of the semester, your group members will be asked for input on whether your contribution to group activities was satisfactory or unsatisfactory. This system is designed to identify any ‘free riders’ in the group. If two or more group members rate your contribution as unsatisfactory, you will be awarded a substantially lower grade (e.g 0%) on that assignment. Additionally, at my discretion, you may be awarded a lower grade on other assignments as well. It is therefore essential that you do your fair share of group work.

Note- If any group member does not contribute satisfactorily to group activities, please let me know asap. Additionally I may award a particular group member a lower grade if I have reason to believe that group member has not contributed adequately to a project (for instance if the group member is unable to answer questions on a group project satisfactorily).

**LLM Policy**

LLMs can facilitate student learning, but also harm it significantly. In this course you are not allowed to use LLMs for analyzing cases. While LLM use for other assignments is permitted, as noted above, simply cutting and pasting the output is not acceptable, and will result in a failing grade on the assignment. Students are expected to use AI primarily as a collaborative tool where the generated output is evaluated critically, and is modified/ enhanced using individual thinking and analysis.

If there is concern that a student has cut and paste AI/LLM output without adequate individual effort, then the student may be called upon for a one on one Q&A session at my discretion. Not performing satisfactorily in the Q&A session will result in a failing grade on the assignment, and will be treated as a first violation as per the Academic Integrity policy outlined above.
