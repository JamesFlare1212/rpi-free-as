## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The increased professionalization of the workforce and high value placed on technology and knowledge work in the global economy emphasize the importance of good workforce management skills for managers and professionals. To be effective, we need to act as "applied" behavioral scientists, that is, we must carefully study our organizations, units, and direct reports and develop an ability to recognize what makes each one tick. The techniques in behavioral science, no less than the techniques in marketing, finance, and accounting, are essential for managers and professionals to understand and apply. In this course more than most others, we need to know not only the conceptual material presented but also how it applies to our work lives and ourselves. People who understand the work environment, know themselves, and can assess their effects on others are more likely to be effective than are those who are blind to these dimensions.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Framework for the Course**

This course will move from the "micro" side of O.B. to the "macro." We will examine increasingly complex levels of social behavior, moving from the individual to the interpersonal to the group and finally to the organizational level.

**The Classroom as a Learning Laboratory**

We will bring a practice-oriented approach to the material in this course. In addition to analyzing behavior in cases and texts, we will use the classroom situation itself as a source of data to examine organizational behavior. Consequently, we will use class time for simulations, role-playing, and group and intergroup interaction. Considerable work will be done in your learning groups, which will be used for several class sessions and out-of-class work. You should be able to develop your analytic skills in this context, as well as gain insights into your own interpersonal styles. Also, if your group is effective, you will be able to create an open, low-risk, non-threatening environment where you can feel free to explore your behavioral tendencies and gain valuable feedback from your colleagues.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\) Apply course concepts with increased behavioral skills in relation to self-awareness, interpersonal and group interactions, and organizational effectiveness
- 2\) Demonstrate enhanced skills in analysis, critical decision making, and problem solving, including related to business ethics
- 3\) Communicate more effectively in written and oral presentations
- 4\) Work in a more informed manner with differences based on diversity and culture
- 5\) Analyze and evaluate different organizational practices, especially related to that of leadership.
- 6\) Generate discussion and insight on topics involving organizational behavior and leadership.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Midterm (10%)**

A multiple-choice exam meant to assess your broad understanding of class concepts, allowing you and I to update our strategies in facilitating your learning. 20 questions in total.

**Comprehensive Final (25%)**

A multiple-choice exam meant to assess your overall understanding of the class material. 50 questions in total.

**Quizzes (15%)**

17 quizzes will be given throughout the semester, assessing knowledge of recent material. Quizzes **cannot** be made up. Quizzes will be given at the very beginning of class and will only be passed out once, meaning that **late arrivals will not be able to take the quiz**. The two lowest quiz grades will be dropped to allow for dire circumstances.

**In-Class Problem Sets (25%)**

Five times throughout the semester, class will be dedicated to analyzing and solving an organizational behavioral problem based on recent lectures and reading. The first 40 minutes of class will allow for preparation and the last 40 minutes will be for 5-minute presentations, which will be graded and given feedback. This will be a group-based grade. Groups will be different for each problem set. More information can be found on the LMS.

**Group Presentations (25%)**

Students will be put into randomly assigned groups responsible for presenting on an assigned topic. Full details about presentation expectations can be found in a separate document on the LMS.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
