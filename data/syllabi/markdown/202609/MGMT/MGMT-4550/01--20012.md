## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This zero-credit seminar connects students in the Lally Graduate Pathways Program with the resources, experiences, and opportunities available through the Lally School of Management. Students will explore graduate degree and career pathways while gaining early access to professional development activities, industry speakers, company visits, networking events, student organizations, and professional tools and software such as Bloomberg and FactSet.

The seminar also provides ongoing academic and program advising to help students strategically plan for graduate study, including graduate course selection, credit-sharing opportunities, transfer credits, and other ways to maximize the time and financial advantages of the Graduate Pathways Program. The seminar is designed to complement students’ undergraduate studies while helping them build professional skills, industry connections, and a clearer vision for their future.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Evaluate the fit and expectations of Lally graduate programs, including the MBA and specialized M.S. options, in relation to their undergraduate studies, career goals, and professional interests.
- • Demonstrate increased professional readiness through the development of materials such as a professional portfolio, resume, and/or LinkedIn profile appropriate for internship, early-career, and graduate-level recruiting
- \<span>\<b>·\</b>\<b>Apply effective networking strategies when interacting with alumni, recruiters, industry professionals, faculty, and peers in both on-campus and off-campus settings.\</b>\</span>
- •\&nbsp;\<b>Engage professionally in site visits, employer interactions, guest speaker events, and other experiential opportunities, including appropriate preparation, conduct, and follow-up communication\</b>
- •\&nbsp;\&nbsp;\<b>Identify academic, advising, financial, and professional resources that support successful progression from undergraduate study into a Lally MBA or specialized M.S. program, including opportunities for early planning and credit sharing.\</b>
- •\&nbsp;\<b>Explain how business, management, analytics, and specialized graduate-level competencies can complement and extend knowledge developed in any undergraduate major and support a range of career paths.\</b>

## Course Calendar

### Calendar Details:

This seminar offers a flexible and dynamic curriculum that varies each semester based on Lally School of Management programming, resources, and opportunities available to Pathways students. Sessions and activities may include guest speakers and alumni visits, professional development workshops, business software training, information about Lally student clubs and resources, networking events, company site visits, academic and program advising, and other relevant opportunities. The course calendar will be determined by Lally programming and the availability of speakers, alumni, company hosts, and other resources throughout the semester. An up-to-date schedule of seminar sessions and activities will be posted in the course Learning Management System (LMS), and students are responsible for monitoring the LMS Announcements for updates.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**PARTICIPATION AND ATTENDANCE POLICY:** The Lally Graduate Pathways Seminar does not meet on a regular schedule; instead, sessions and activities are offered selectively throughout the semester based on relevant academic, professional, and co-curricular opportunities. These may include professional development workshops, business software training, information sessions on Lally student clubs and resources, guest speakers and alumni visits, networking events, company site visits, academic advising sessions, and other activities designed to support students’ academic planning, career exploration, and professional growth .Participation in individual events is optional, and students with academic, research, work, or other scheduling constraints are exempt from attending specific events. However, all students are strongly encouraged to make a good-faith effort to participate whenever possible, as seminar activities are intentionally designed to strengthen readiness for Lally graduate study, expand students' professional networks, and support career development.

When possible, seminar notes, presentation slides, or other relevant materials will be made available through the LMS platform for students who are unable to attend a scheduled session.

Students who choose to participate in seminar events are expected to engage **professionally and responsibly**, including arriving on time, being prepared, and adhering to professional standards of conduct. Because many activities involve alumni, recruiters, or off-campus site visits, advanced registration and commitment are expected. Students who register for an event but are unable to attend should notify the program coordinator in advance whenever possible. Failure to communicate regarding attendance or repeated last-minute cancellations may be considered unprofessional and may affect the student’s seminar standing.

*Professional behavior in participation and attendance is an essential component of the seminar and reflects expectations students will encounter in Lally graduate programs and in professional settings.*

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
