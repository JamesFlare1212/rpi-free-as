## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course introduces Ph.D. management students to theory development, exposes them to a broad array of research techniques, and helps students design research programs and write about them. The underpinnings of scientific theory are reviewed along with a range of quantitative and qualitative research methods in various management disciplines (i.e., entrepreneurship, finance, information systems, marketing, and strategy). Drawing on their own interests, students will demonstrate skill acquisition through the application of different research methodologies, multiple quizzes, and a full-scale research proposal. Throughout the course, students will learn about the role of theory, how meaningful theories are developed, how research is designed to test theories rigorously, and what are the limitations and ethical issues involved in conducting academic research.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- To be be able to describe the main research methods available to management scholars (including each method’s strengths and weaknesses).
- To be able to choose appropriate research methods given the study’s objectives and other constraints.
- To be able to interpret empirical results of common statistical methods (e.g., T-tests, ANOVA, ANCOVA, Regression, SEM).
- To be able to design experiments (e.g., lab, online) and questionnaires.
- To be able to select an appropriate sampling frame.
- To be able to assess validity and reliability (both of the research method as a whole and of its individual components).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Plagiarism, including the uncredited use of others' ideas, text, or data, is a serious violation of academic integrity and will not be tolerated in this Ph.D. course. This includes material generated by generative AI tools (such as ChatGPT or other large language models). While these tools can support your learning, using AI-generated content without proper attribution or as a substitute for your own critical thinking and original work is considered plagiarism. **Students are expected to produce and submit work that reflects their own understanding, analysis, and voice**. Any instance of academic dishonesty—whether intentional or not—may result in disciplinary action, including failure in the course or further consequences in accordance with university policy. **If in doubt, ask before submitting**.
