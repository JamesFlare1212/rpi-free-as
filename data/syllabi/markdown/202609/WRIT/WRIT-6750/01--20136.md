## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

“Expert” writers and speakers must convey complex technical information to non-specialist audiences with different knowledges, skills, assumptions, and goals. Therefore, this course is meant to (1) introduce students to major approaches to technical communication and (2) help students in technical professions or professional fields prepare for the types of research, writing, and information presentation that they may do in their careers after graduation. Moreover, students will learn about the basic social implications of technical communication.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will identify key questions and concerns for technical communication, including social, cultural, and technological concerns through journal responses.
- Students will recognize and apply genre conventions for different audiences and technical communication situations through externally-facing documents like résumé and cover letter, flyer, and proposal and internally-facing documents, like memos. This is also achieved through peer review.
- Students will differentiate between audiences and examine relevant context features for different types of documents through composing external and internal-facing documents.
- Students will create documents that communicate to specific audiences given using appropriate genre conventions, and responding to specific contexts, including how the technological, cultural, and social contexts shape specific media products through analyzing examples of technical documents and composing the aforementioned documents.
- Students will propose solutions to local problems, and effectively communication those proposed solutions to a professional audience using appropriate genre conventions, and considering specific contexts, including how the technological, cultural, and social contexts through a group proposal.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Assignment Descriptions**

***Reading Responses* (15 total X 7 points each = 105 points /10.5%).**

These are short (150-word minimum) journal assignments where you will respond to questions about the readings (posted online and below). These questions are meant to (a) help direct your reading, and (b) “scaffold” you into major assignments. As 6000-level students, you will be expected to complete more difficult readings and questions ***in addition*** to the readings and questions that 4000-level students complete. These readings and questions complicate and add to the 4000-level readings and questions. You can (and should!) use the writing that you do for the reading response assignments on your major assignments. These cannot be informal journals to meet the communication-intensive requirement. Therefore, please use complete sentences and follow punctuation guidelines to meet the communication-intensive requirement.

***Résumé and Cover Letter*** **(120 points/14%). (200 words)**

As 6000-level students, you may be looking at academic jobs, and therefore you may choose between a CV or a résumé. Therefore, you will turn in four files for this assignment: (1) a PDF of a relevant job ad, (2) a résumé *or* a CV (3) a cover letter (of at least 200 words), and (4) a one-page reflection about You will be graded on how well you tailor the materials to the ad and follow standard genre conventions that respond to the specific audience of professionals in your field. (PDF for job ad).

***Instructions and Memo*** **(120 points/14%). (1000 words)**

You will write an external-facing set task-oriented of instructions for a task with which you are familiar. The audience for these instructions are for lay undergraduate students at RPI. They will be from at least 600 words and include some sort of figure. The instructions will be accompanied by a 400-word internal-facing memo that you would write for an internal audience that explains how the instructions respond to the undergraduate audience at RPI. The memo should follow a standard memo format. You will be graded on the content and format of the instructions and the memo.

***Document Design with Graph and Memo*** **(120 points/14%). (750 words total).**

The purpose of this assignment is to create a ***one-page handout*** *or **online infographic*** that uses a graph to address an issue here at RPI or in Troy, NY more generally (e.g. join a club, expand food options, etc.). This assignment is meant to build on the skills you developed on your previous two assignments as the audience for a handout will be more broad. It will be accompanied by a 750-word internal-facing memo that you would write for others in your organization to explain why your document design is effective and should be adopted. The memo should follow a standard memo format. You will be graded on the content and format of the handout and the memo. (Handout may be PDF).

***Individual Proposals and Memo*** **(210 points/21%). (2,500 words)**

Many technical writers work in teams to write proposals for Requests for Proposals (RFPs). In this assignment, you will write a proposal. As 6000-level students, you may choose between a research proposal or a Goods and Services proposal that responds to an imaginary RFP to propose a change or solution to some problem at RPI or Troy generally. The proposal will be between 2,000-3,000 words and include (1) a cover page, (2) an executive summary, (3) an introduction and background (4) a problem statement, (5) a project description or a research method, (6) a list of your credentials (7) a timeline, (8) a budget, (9) a conclusion, and (10) references. Your proposal will also incorporate a well-design and labelled graph to support your argument. The audience for this assignment is an imagined team of administrators and a grant office at RPI. The proposal will be accompanied by a 500-word internal-facing memo that you would write reflecting on your processes. The memo should follow a standard memo format.

***Final Presentation*** **(100 points/10%).**

Sometimes you will also need to present about your proposals. Thus, you will also practice presenting your proposal. The audience for this assignment is the imagined granting agency from the proposal assignment. The presentation will be between 10-15 minutes long. It will include a visual aid. You will be graded on the content and format of the visual aid and your presentation skills (individually). (PPT file for presentation).

***Peer Reviews*** **(4 total x 30 points each=120 points/12%. Graded on complete/incomplete).**

For peer reviews, you will bring in nearly complete drafts of your upcoming assignments (excluding memos). In class, peers will respond to your drafts using a template of questions. They will return the document to you with the questions answered. You will then write a revision plan. You will turn in your draft, peer responses, and revision plan at the end of class. These are graded on complete/incomplete, but ***you must be in-class to complete peer reviews.***

***Poster Presentation*** **(105 points/10.5%).**

6000-level students will give a lecture on a Technical Communication article and explain how the article is relevant to your research or work. The lecture and explanation of its relevance should last **10-15 minutes with about 5 minutes for questions.** You will also use an academic research poster. Research posters are used in many fields, so they are valuable for you to learn to create.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

I take attendance each class period. You are graded on participation, including in-class activities. As we do a lot of in-class work to prepare for upcoming assignments, it is imperative that you attend class. This will ensure you keep up with the materials and that you receive participation points. You cannot receive participation points if you are absent, unless there is a documented health-related issue. If you show up for class more than **5 minutes** late, or if you leave more than **5 minutes** early, you will only receive half credit. **You can miss up to two classes this semester, no questions asked, with no penalty to your grade.** After two absences, you will lose points. **On the days that you are absent, you will still be responsible for what we do in class, so I highly suggest that you** use these days judiciously in case there is an emergency (family illness, car accident, etc.). **If you foresee any reasons that you will miss class, please let me know as soon as possible so that we can make other arrangements to get you the course materials. If you delay communicating to me that you will miss class, that makes it more difficult for me to make alternative arrangements to get you the course materials for which you are responsible.** If you are absent for COVID, you **MUST** report your positive test result to student health services, stay home for five days, and wear a mask for five days.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity Statement**

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs.

RPI’s Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities states:

“If found responsible for committing academic dishonesty, a student may be subject to one or both types of penalties: **an academic (grade) penalty administered by the professor and/or disciplinary action through the Rensselaer judicial process described in this handbook**.

Academic dishonesty is a violation of the Grounds for Disciplinary Action as described in this handbook. A student may be subject to any of the following types of disciplinary action should disciplinary action be pursued by the instructor: disciplinary warning; disciplinary probation; disciplinary suspension, expulsion and/or alternative actions as agreed on by the student and hearing officer. It should be noted that no student who allegedly commits academic dishonesty will be able to drop or change the grade option for the course in question and is not eligible to request an F examination for the course. The definitions and examples presented below are a sampling of types of academic dishonesty and are not to be construed as an exhaustive or exclusive list. The academic integrity policy applies to all students, undergraduate and graduate, and to scholarly pursuits and research. Additionally, attempts to commit academic dishonesty or to assist in the commission or attempt of such an act are also violations of this policy.”

You can read more about the judicial process here: https://www.rpi.edu/dept/doso/resources/policies/Fillable2015-2016Version2AcademicIntegrityBrochure.pdf

For this class, I differentiate between misuse of sources and intentional plagiarism. The extent of the infraction will determine my response. For example, minor misuse of sources may result in you having to rewrite the paper and take a late penalty. Papers purchased online may result in an F in the class and disciplinary action through the RPI judicial process.

For clarity, all your papers should have (1) a works cited page at the end of a paper ***and*** (2) in-text citations. Materials that you **must cite in-text** include: (1) Quotations, paraphrases, and summaries of words, ideas, and opinions of others, (2) Visual and statistical material and computer programming code derived from others (3) Facts not widely known and arguable claims (4) Help from others.

As an upper-division class, you are expected to know how to cite sources. For this class, you may choose between MLA or APA. You can read more about how to use both through the OWL Purdue. If you have any other questions about citations, I suggest you take your paper to COMM+D (see below).

**Artificial Intelligence Statement**

Artificial intelligence (AI) is often used in the writing process, and may have a place in your other classes. However, for this class, you may not use **copy and paste language from Artificial Intelligence, such as ChatGPT, DALL-E, etc. into your assignments, nor may you use fictious sources. Both will result in an automatic 0 for the assignment, and you may *not* make up these assignments**. All assignments must accurately reflect the readings and answer the questions. Research shows that using AI uncritically can undermine your ability to develop critical thinking, writing, or research skills that are essential for this course and your academic success.

Since ChatGPT frequently makes up information, **if statements in your paper are false, there will be a penalty, up to and including a 0 for the assignment**. Since AI hallucinates, if statements are not backed up by sources, I will assume they are AI.

To avoid situations where there is concern about whether you have used ChatGPT, ***I highly recommend keeping time-stamped drafts of your assignments to demonstrate that you have drafted the paper***. An easy way to do to this would be to use Google docs or another cloud storage service that can restore previous versions of your document.

Failure to follow these guidelines may result in academic integrity violations, up to and including failing the assignment and failing the course. If you use AI, I will report the incident to the Dean of Students’ Office, so that there is a record of the violation. If you are in doubt as to whether you are using AI language models appropriately in this course, I encourage you to discuss your situation with me.
