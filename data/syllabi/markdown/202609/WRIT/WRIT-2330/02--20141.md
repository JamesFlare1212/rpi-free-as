## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will demonstrate knowledge of fiction writing in general and the short story in particular by utilizing appropriate terminology to analyze and discuss published fiction.
- Students will demonstrate craft-based reading strategies through annotations, short analytical responses, and discussions.
- Students will establish a consistent creative practice by regularly producing short creative writing exercises.
- Students will demonstrate engagement in the writing process through observing, drafting, peer critique, and revision.
- Students demonstrate participation in a creative community of peers through workshop and discussion.

## Course Requirements & Assignments

**Reading.** In this course, you will learn to read like a writer, which is somewhat different than the reading you may be used to in English classes. The expectation is that as you engage with these texts you are “looking behind the curtain” to determine how the artistic effects were achieved and preparing to discuss your theories with others. Reading should be an active endeavor, and your preparedness will be evaluated as part of your class participation and through CRAFT demos.

**In-Class Writing.** We will regularly engage creative writing prompts in class. These are designed to give you practice in a particular skill, and some of these exercises will become the seeds of your stories. You will select a number of these to be graded formally as part of each story portfolio, to demonstrate experimentation and effort.

**CRAFT Demos**: (Close Reading and Analysis of Fictional Tools)

Twice each unit, you will annotate a short passage of fiction, choosing just one element of craft) on which to focus. You will write a short, focused paragraph on how that element is working in the passage and the story overall. The lowest grade will be dropped.

**Drafting Portfolios (2):** This is where you will demonstrate your primary engagement with the writing process. Evaluations are based on a) how your story attempts to integrate course concepts b) key developmental exercises from your in-class drafting portfolio: one of these will be your story “seed” and c) a conversation with me in which you discuss your craft choices.

**Participation:**

*Class Discussions & Activities:* I expect that each of you contribute to discussions, in large and/or small groups, every class. I will routinely evaluate your preparation for class in the following ways: observing small groups, posing questions, requesting questions, informal presentations of group or individual work, in-class writing, discussion, and peer feedback.

*Workshops***:** Instruction in this course follows the guidelines established by the Association of Writers and Writing Programs for the teaching of creative writing, which include a “challenging writers’ workshop” as a hallmark. They define this as*…a seminar in which students critique one another’s work under the mentorship of an accomplished writer-teacher. The workshop is writing intensive, offering each student multiple opportunities for submission and revision of creative work.* This method of instruction is considered the gold standard for developing writers at all levels of expertise, and workshopping is a key learning tool. I will weight your contributions heavily in your class participation grade.

**Revision Portfolio:** At the end of the term, you will create a plan to revise your flash fiction and your short story based on a thorough reading of peer and instructor comments and provide evidence of drafting.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

This is a small seminar class, the success of which is entirely dependent on active student presence and participation. I do not routinely distinguish between excused and unexcused absences. Everyone has two absences to use for illness, self-care, or other obligations (e.g. interviews, medical appointments). Otherwise, I expect that you will come to class every class (school athletes see me at the beginning of the term). No documentation is needed. Each absence beyond the second one will drop your grade by ten points (1%). If you miss more than five classes, you will receive no credit for the course even if you are otherwise passing. Note that three tardies/early departures of half hour or more accrue to one absence. Students with documented attendance accommodations should meet with me to discuss.

### Other Course Policies:

Due to the nature of workshop, late workshop submissions cannot be accepted. **Do not miss workshop submission deadlines.** In-class writing and CRAFT demos must be completed in class. There are no make-ups. However, the lowest grade will be dropped. Per institutional policy, late work cannot be submitted after the last day of class for a better grade.

Students are expected to monitor their grades throughout the semester via Blackboard’s grading feature, and I am happy to help you navigate that. If you have any questions about your grade at any time, please do not hesitate to respectfully inquire. All inquiries should be made as soon as possible and no later than 24 hours after final grades are posted on LMS. All grades on SIS are final.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).
