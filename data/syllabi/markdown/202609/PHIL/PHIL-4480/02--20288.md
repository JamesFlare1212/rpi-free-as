## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Daydreams about some tropical paradise... The smell of freshly baked bread... The flash of anger experienced when one person cuts another off... The vision of an albino squirrel on the campus green... Humans take all of these to involve activities or states of consciousness. But what is this consciousness with which we claim to be so intimately familiar? What are its metaphysical implications, and can people reconcile those implications with a scientific understanding of the world? We will begin with more general metaphysical issues and then move into topics more directly focused on philosophy of mind and consciousness. This is an upper-level communication intensive course.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Students will be able to explain Metaphysics as a subfield of Philosophy, including being able to identify and articulate several of the major issues within Metaphysics.
- 2\. Students will be able to identify and discuss major varieties of Dualism, including interactive substance dualism, emergent entity dualism, property dualism, and epiphenomenalism, as well as being able to identify Idealism.
- 3\. Students will be able to identify and discuss the major varieties of Physicalism, including Type-Type Identity Theory, Token-Token Identity Theory, Functionalism, and Eliminativism.
- 4\. Students will be able to discuss the main challenges to Physicalism, such as the qualia argument and the knowledge argument.
- 5\. Students will be able to explain the difference between the so-called "easy problems of consciousness" and the "hard problem of consciousness."
- 6\. Students will gain experience writing philosophical prose.
- 7\. Students will practice and demonstrate the skill of carrying on meaningful philosophical discussion.

## Course Calendar

### Calendar Details:

Week 1: Intro to course; basics of arguments.  
Week 2: The nature, scope, and history of metaphysics; The continued relevance of metaphysics; relationship of metaphysics and science.  
Week 3: The continued relevance of metaphysics; Relationship of metaphysics and science.  
Week 4: Matter, energy, and reality: an historical overview. The concept of matter in the history of philosophy and science. Reconceptualizing reality in terms of mathematics?  
Week 5: Reconceptualizing reality in terms of information?; Thinking about the history of the universe in terms of information processing revolutions.  
Week 6: Metaphysical status of unobservable entities postulated by science. Experimental philosophers investigate people's intuitions about consciousness and its functions. **Paper 1 due on 09/29/26**.  
Week 7: Spectrum of metaphysical positions on mind-body relationship. **Review for Exam 1**.  
Week 8: **Exam 1**.  
Week 9: Recent version of interactive dualism that invokes aspects of quantum physics. Emergent entity dualism; Property dualism.  
Week 10: Contemporary neuroscience, physicalism, and the importance of emotions in real life decision-making. Type-Identity Theory and reductive physicalism.  
Week 11: Watch and discuss NOVA "Secrets of the Mind" video. Functionalism and Token-Identity Physicalism.  
Week 12: Functionalism, computers, and A.I.; Dreaming as a form of consciousness; **PAPER 2 due on 11/10/26**.  
Week 13: Chalmers' distinction between "easy problems of consciousness" and the "hard problem of consciousness." Qualia-based arguments for the inadequacy of physicalism. Vagueness of the term "physical" (or "body") as used by philosophers and physicists.  
Week 14: NO CLASSES. Thanksgiving Break!  
Week of 15: Vagueness of the term "physical" (or "body") as used by philosophers and physicists. Personal identity and re-creation of the physical person; Clark and Chalmers' concept of the "extended mind."  
Week of 16: **Review for Exam 2. Exam 2.**

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

In this class, all exam answers and papers that are turned in for a grade must represent the student's own work. Also, please note that you cannot submit the same term paper for a grade in two different courses without the permission of both instructors. This holds true regardless of whether the courses were taken in the same semester or in different semesters. Although you may have another person or an AI/LLM read a draft of your paper and offer suggestions, the paper must be your original work. You must cite all sources that you use in researching and writing your papers, including sources that we have read and discussed in class. You will receive an instruction sheet before each paper, explaining how properly to cite sources.  
Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty. If found to be in violation of the Academic Integrity Policy, students may be subject to two types of penalty: The Instructor may administer a grade penalty and the student may be reported to the Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education, as appropriate. The first violation will result in a grade of 0 for that exam or paper. A second violation would result in failure of the course.  
If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
