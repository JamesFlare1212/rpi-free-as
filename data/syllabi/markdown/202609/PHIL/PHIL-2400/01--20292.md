## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course examines a number of issues related to biology through the lens of philosophical analysis, such as the impact of preconceived notions on research and reception of discoveries, the pace and mode of evolution, the relationship between microevolution and macroevolution, nature and nurture, implications of the view that important aspects of our psychology are explainable in terms of our evolutionary background, whether the propensity to think in terms of ethics may be an evolutionary adaptation, and prospects for the future of human evolution.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Students will be able to identify and briefly explain several of the most important general issues and themes in philosophy of science, and, where appropriate, to express how these issues arise within philosophy of biology specifically.
- 2\. Students will be able to articulate the main concepts of the theory of Punctuated Equilibria, and will be able to distinguish this theory from the older, rival view of Phyletic Gradualism.
- 3\. Students will be able to explain the traditional differences between the study of Microevolution and the study of Macroevolution, and they will be able to articulate points on both sides of the debate as to whether these two are talking about the same things.
- 4\. Students will be able to explain the contributions that paleobiology has made to our understanding of life on Earth, as well as of the theoretical/philosophical issues and challenges that arise from using paleobiology in this way.
- 5\. Students will be able to articulate well-informed views on human evolution, including the debate over how much of human behavior was determined by forms of selection during the prehistory of our species.
- 6\. Students will be able to identify and distinguish the concepts of core genome, epigenome, and microbiome, especially as these apply to human biology.
- 7\. Students will develop the skill of being able to engage in philosophical discussions.

## Course Calendar

### Calendar Details:

Week of 08/27: Intro to course.  
Week of 08/31-09/03: History of science and philosophy of science from Ancient Greece to today. Survey of philosophy of science from Mid-20th Century until today: Popper and falsificationism, Kuhn and paradigms, normal science, anomalies, and scientific revolutions; begin overview of evolutionary biology, its history, and related philosophical issues.  
Week of 09/07-09/10: Does evolutionary science meet the criteria for good science?  
Week of 09/14-09/17: Intro to issues of paleobiology; organismic vs. evolutionary paleontology. Punctuated Equilibria vs. Phyletic Gradualism as interpretations of the fossil record. Punctuated Equilibria and the relationship between Macroevolution and Microevolution.  
Week of 09/21-0: Species and macroevolution. **Review for Exam 1**.  
Week of 09/28-10/01**: EXAM 1**. How contingent is evolution?  
Week of 10/05-10/08: Diversity, disparity, and the Burgess Shale; cladistics. Fossils, including molecular fossils. Is there progress in evolution?  
Week of 10/12-10/16: Video: What Darwin Never Knew. Human genomes.  
Week of 10/19-10/22: Intro to human evolution. Video: Ape to Man.  
Week of 10/26-10/29: **Review for Exam 2. Exam 2.** Week of 11/02-11/05: Evolution and ethics. Biological altruism vs. true altruism; Video: The Kindness of Strangers.  
Week of 11/09-11/12: Big data, genomics, and continuing human evolution. Sex, sexual selection, and reproductive technologies.  
Week of 11/16-11/19: Sex, orientation, and race. Video: Skin Deep.  
Week of 11/23-11/26: No Classes. Thanksgiving Break!  
Week of 11/30-12/03: Our evolved relationship with our microbiomes. Thinking about where future evolution may take our species.  
Week of 12/07-12/10: **Review for Exam 3; Exam 3.**
