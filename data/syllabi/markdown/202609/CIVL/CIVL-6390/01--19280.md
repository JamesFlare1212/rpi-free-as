## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will develop understanding and integrate skills across the fields of fluid mechanics, meteorology, climatology, bluff-body aerodynamics, structural dynamics, code provisions for design, wind tunnel testing and damage documentation.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Studnents will be able to define and use terminology in Wind Engineering appropriately,
- 2\. Students will be abel to explain basic fluid mechanics,
- 3\. Students will be abel to explain the mechanisms driving the wind,
- 4\. Students will be abel to explain and model the atmospheric boundary layer, its structure and climatology,
- 5\. Students will be abel to explain/describe bluff body aerodynamics,
- 6\. Students will identify problem areas for wind environment,
- 7\. Students will be able to determine wind induced forces on structures,
- 8\. Students will be able to determine structural response to wind loads,
- 9\. Students will have an expectation of wind tunnel model studies.
- 10\. Student will know how to document wind damage.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

There will be 10 homework assignments during the semester, worth 70% of the total grade. Students should consider the appropriate submission format for the type of assignment; in some cases, hand-written math working is appropriate, however, often, a brief report is necessary to explain theory, assumptions, working and interpretation of results. HW assignments will be due ~14 days after they are issued.

The final project is a short report and presentation of the report and will be scheduled for each student in the last weeks of the semester. Students will base their report on a topic of their interest (appropriate support of peer reviewed journal papers required) with the instructor’s approval.
