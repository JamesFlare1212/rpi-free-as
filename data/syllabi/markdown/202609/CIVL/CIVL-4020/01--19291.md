## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course is an introduction to lightweight structures design and engineering.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Suggested Reading:  
"The Tower and the Bridge: The New Art of Structural Engineering", by David P. Billington  
"Architects and Engineers", by Andrew Saint

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course will examine form finding techniques, historic engineering approaches to surface structures, and explore the basics of lightweight structures.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Form finding through physical and digital modeling, Lightweight structures, Branching structures

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Collaborative Design: The student will develop the ability to work in a multidisciplinary team on an architectural/engineering project. BArch and MArch students are expected to collaborate with the rest of the team (including architects and structural engineering students), and show understanding of the discussed subject matter relevant to their respective programs.
- Structural Design: The student will be capable of understanding and discussing fundamental structural systems and their capacity to resist gravitational, seismic, and lateral forces, along with selecting and employing the suitable structural system. BArch and MArch students are understood to have differing levels of knowledge of structural systems and are evaluated accordingly
- Design Considerations: The student will gain a deeper understanding of fabrication, lightweight, and constructability considerations in the design of physical objects in the built environment
- Detail Design: The student will improve their skills in detailing of architectural/structural objects, and how detailing relates to the other major design considerations
- Advanced Analysis Tools: The student will become more familiar with advanced architectural modeling and structural analysis tools, and how architects and engineers can interface and communicate using these tools

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.
