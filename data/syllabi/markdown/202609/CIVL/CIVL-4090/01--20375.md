## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course introduces the principles and practices of high-performance concrete in modern civil engineering. Topics include the fundamentals of high-performance concrete, specialty molding and release agents, aggregate types and properties, mix design methodologies, and experimental testing. The course also covers the role of supplementary cementitious materials, chemical admixtures, and fiber reinforcement in enhancing concrete performance. Emphasis is placed on designing with specialty concrete mixes, optimization-based approaches, and sustainability considerations. Students will work on a real-world project focusing on the design of an ultra-lightweight concrete structure from conception through construction. Teamwork, project planning, and management are emphasized, providing students with valuable hands-on experience in structural engineering and professional collaboration.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Explain the effects of constituent materials on concrete performance.
- Design specialty concrete mixes to meet specific performance objectives.
- Conduct experimental testing to determine the mechanical properties of high-performance concrete.
- Apply optimization-based strategies to design high-performance concrete for multi-objective problems.
- Develop a design and manufacturing plan for a concrete structure that meets target objectives subject to constraints.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are expected to attend all classes. If a student does not attend class, it is the responsibility of the student to notify the instructor in advance and to provide justification. Every student is allowed a maximum of two automatically-excused absences for medical reasons, as long as the instructor was notified in advance and justification was provided. Students missing more than two classes due to illness are required to provide a doctor’s note or other supporting documentation to the Student Success Office. The Dean of Students will communicate the receipt of the note (with expected end date) to the instructor. For all absences that are not due to medical reasons, students can request an excused absence from the instructor by providing a valid reason and supporting documentation to the instructor. In such cases, and based on the provided information, the instructor will determine whether the student will be excused from class. Each unexcused absence will result in a deduction from the attendance portion of the course grade.

### Other Course Policies:

**Late Assignment Submission:**  
All students are expected to submit all assignments by the specified due date. Excusal from class does not automatically excuse students from submitting homework/lab reports/pre-labs. If a student cannot submit a particular assignment by the associated due date, it is the responsibility of the student to notify the instructor in advance and to request an extension. Based on the provided information, the instructor will determine if such an extension will be allowed.

For each type of assignment, students are allowed one late submission without a grade penalty. For any subsequent late submissions of assignments of the same type, the maximum points that can be earned in each late assignment are reduced by 10% of its originally possible points for every day past the due date that the assignment has not been submitted. As an example, if an assignment was submitted 8 days past its due date, the maximum points that can be earned in that assignment are now 20% of its originally possible points, even if all parts of the late assignment are correct.
