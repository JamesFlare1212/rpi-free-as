## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Analysis of elastic and inelastic single and multiple degree-of-freedom structural systems under time-dependent loads including harmonic, impulse, earthquake, and other general dynamic loads. Development of equations of motion. Analytical and numerical evaluation of free and forced vibration response. Identification of dynamic system properties. Modal analysis. Vibration isolation and force transmissibility. Dynamic measurement sensors. Shock loading spectrum. Frequency-domain analysis.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Dynamics of Structures: Theory and Applications to Earthquake Engineering, A.K. Chopra, Sixth Edition, Pearson, 2023.

### Content Details:

Undamped and damped free vibration

Response to harmonic loading  
Displacement, velocity and acceleration response spectrums  
Resonance testing for system identification  
Response to periodic loading  
Response to general dynamic loads  
Pulse loading and shock spectrums  
Numerical dynamic analysis  
IDOF SYSTEMS MODELED AS SDOF SYSTEMS:  
Equation of motion  
Natural frequency via Rayleigh's method  
MDOF SYSTEMS:  
Equation of motion and solution methods  
Undamped free vibration (natural frequencies and mode shapes)  
Orthogonality conditions  
Modal analysis for undamped free vibration response  
Damping distribution in structures  
Damped free vibration (natural frequencies and mode shapes)  
Modal analysis for damped free vibration response  
Modal analysis for general dynamic loads  
IDOF SYSTEMS  
Equation of motion  
Undamped free vibration (natural frequencies and mode shapes)  
Response to general dynamic loads

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Describe the distinguishing characteristics of static and dynamic structural analyses.
- Construct mathematical models of structural systems subjected to dynamic loading and derive the associated equation of motion.
- Solve the equation of motion using analytical and numerical methods.
- Solve the equation of motion using modern software tools.

Homework Policy:  
1\) Homework assignments will be posted on LMS. The primary purpose of the homework is to help students learn the course material and thus to be well prepared to participate in class, to present and discuss homework, and to take exams.  
2\) To maximize the learning experience for each homework assignment, students should initially attempt to solve problems on their own. Having done that, students should discuss their work with other classmates to help each other arrive at the correct solution or to check solutions to verify that the correct result was obtained. However, this must be done without becoming overly reliant on other students. In addition, students should consult with the instructor if they have questions about the homework.  
3\) On or after the homework due date, one or more students will be selected at random to present their solution to the class. All students should come to class sufficiently prepared to present their solution (even if a student's solution is not completely correct, it should at least be well thought out and organized so the student can clearly explain what they did and can present it in an efficient manner). Furthermore, to maximize the portion of the course grade that relates to homework presentations, students should exhibit good oral and written communication skills when presenting homework solutions.  
4\) Some homework solutions will be submitted to the instructor for evaluation (check on correctness of solution) while other homework solutions will not be submitted. However, students are expected to solve all homework problems and eventually obtain the correct solution. Students should attempt to obtain the correct solution on their own or by working with others as needed; if this is not successful, students will have access to the correct solution and final answers via homework solutions that are presented and discussed in class.  
5\) Each student is responsible for creating a complete set of correct homework solutions, which will be useful for understanding the course material and thus will be useful for preparing for exams. Each student will have the opportunity to check the correctness of their solution when the homework is presented and discussed in class.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

In this course, **all homework solutions that are developed by a student must represent the student’s own work**, although, after attempting to solve problems on their own, students are encouraged to discuss their work with other classmates to help each other arrive at the correct solution or to check their solution (see Item #2 under *Homework Policy*). Development of a solution that is in violation of this policy (e.g., one student copying work done by another student, or a student copying from a previously developed solution, including a solution from a previous semester or a solution that is contained within a solution manual, or a solution that is obtained from the internet via the use of AI tools) will result in a course grade reduction of up to one full letter grade (e.g., from B to C). In addition, subsequent work done by the student will be scrutinized to evaluate potential cheating. Finally, the Dean of Students, who maintains records of academic dishonesty, will be notified. Additional disciplinary actions may be taken by the Dean of Students.

In this course, **all exams must represent the student’s own work**. If cheating occurs during an exam, the student will receive a grade of zero for that exam. In addition, and in accordance with the *Handbook*, the Dean of Students Office will be notified of the incident of academic dishonesty. Additional disciplinary actions may be taken by the Dean of Students. In addition, all **mobile/electronic devices (cell phones, computers, etc.) must be stored securely away during an exam** and are not to be used unless specifically directed otherwise by the instructor. Any use of, or interaction with, a mobile/electronic device during an exam without explicit permission of the instructor will be interpreted as the illicit transfer of exam data and thus will be considered an act of cheating.
