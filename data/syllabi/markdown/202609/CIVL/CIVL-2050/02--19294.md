## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

CIVL 2050 is the introductory course in fluid and flow phenomena applicable to civil and environmental engineering problems. It is also the laboratory course in fluids and flow phenomena. The course emphasizes real-fluid behavior, engineering analysis, experimental measurement, and interpretation of observations in the context of theory.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Required text: Fluid Mechanics for Engineers, David A. Chin, Pearson, 2017/2022, 1st edition or any compatible edition, ISBN 9780137848317.  
Additional resources: Course notes, lecture slides, laboratory handouts, report templates, homework statements, solution notes, and announcements will be posted through LMS and/or Gradescope. Some assignments may require tools such as Microsoft Excel, MATLAB, or water-distribution modeling software. Submit only requested results, tables, figures, and written work unless otherwise instructed.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. 1\. Analyze pressures exerted by fluids at rest and in uniform flow.  
   2\. Apply conservation of mass, energy, and momentum to a control volume.  
   3\. Analyze energy losses in pipe flows.  
   4\. Use dimensionless numbers to evaluate pipe friction, pump performance, and model/prototype relationships.  
   5\. Acquire skills in analyzing and solving engineering problems.  
   6\. Make measurements of flow phenomena.  
   7\. Apply hydrostatics, conservation of mass, energy, and momentum to control-volume problems.  
   8\. Write succinct reports on experiments and discuss observations in the context of theory.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Introduction and overview; units; fluid properties; pressure and manometry; hydrostatic forces on plane and curved surfaces; buoyancy; flow classifications; systems and control volumes; fluid kinematics; Reynolds transport theorem; conservation of mass, energy, and momentum; Bernoulli equation; flow measurement; uncertainty and laboratory reporting; dimensional analysis and similitude; laminar and turbulent pipe flow; steady-flow energy equation; minor losses; single- and multiple-pipe systems; pump performance; boundary layers and drag; flow over cylinders and spheres; hydraulic turbines and hydropower.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is mandatory. Consistent presence is essential for learning and participation, and attendance counts for 5% of the course grade.  
Attendance will be recorded using the Blackboard Attendance tool. You may miss up to three classes without penalty during the semester. These absences are intended to cover minor illnesses, travel, or other personal circumstances. No documentation is required for the first three absences.  
For any additional absence, notify the instructor in advance when possible, such as for university travel or religious observances, or provide an explanation during office hours for consideration.  
Exam 1 and Exam 2 are exempt from the attendance calculation. All other scheduled class meetings count unless the instructor exempts a meeting. In Blackboard, an Excused status applies to an individual student and does not count as an absence. An exempt meeting is excluded from the attendance calculation for the entire class. A Late status receives 50% attendance credit for that meeting. After the first three absences without penalty, each additional unexcused absence reduces the attendance grade proportionally across the remaining counted meetings.

The course includes two 110-minute lecture meetings per week, three laboratory experiments conducted in small teams, homework, attendance, and examinations.

Ten homework assignments will be submitted as a single PDF through Gradescope, accessed from Blackboard. Use a dedicated scanning app; individual photos are not accepted. Students are responsible for scan quality and legibility. The lowest homework grade is dropped, excluding HW 10. Homework is normally due Wednesday at 11:59 PM. The homework immediately before an exam is due Friday night before a Monday exam or Monday night before a Thursday exam. Posted calendar dates govern.  
Homework presentation: Include name, homework number, date, and A/B page numbering. Use ruled engineering paper, physical or digital. Start each problem on a new page. Present given data and diagrams, required results, a separator line, calculations and diagrams, and a boxed final answer. Show sufficient steps for another engineer to follow. One fully worked sample plus a table is acceptable for repetitive calculations. Include correct units on intermediate and final results.
