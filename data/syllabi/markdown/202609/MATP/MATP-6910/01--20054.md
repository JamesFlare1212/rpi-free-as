## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course/internship focuses on data analytics research applied to real-world problems using open data, inspired by our challenge to create a better world through data analytics. In this course/internship, highly motivated students work in multidisciplinary teams on course-long team based, hands-on data analytics projects. Student teams are coached to solve open problems. Datasets and projects will be selected at the start of the semester from a collection of challenges supplied by on- and off-campus clients. Each client provides project and dataset background and assists in setting team objectives. Students actively engage in the process of transforming knowledge to data, gaining critical practical experience applying data analytic methods learned in prior coursework. Students will learn the process of setting project objectives, planning, and management, including the use of best-in-class collaboration tools and methods. Teams will conduct their project based on research goals and workflows they develop to attain these goals.

Students will be guided via classroom demonstrations and individualized team coaching by the instructors and teaching assistants in the employment of visualization, analytics and modeling methods on health care data. The demonstrations will be done in the R data analytics environment and will involve the use of R routines, external packages, script building and the use of pre-built scripts provided by the instructors. An R ‘boot camp’ will be held at the beginning of the semester to get those unfamiliar with the environment started.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The instructional focus will be on problem formulation, data management, preparation, exploration and use of visualization, analysis and modeling methods and their application on real world problems. Interpretation of results, formulation and evolution of project objectives and work plans will be an important part of the hands-on process. Project execution and the development of management skills, and written and oral presentation skills will also be emphasized.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Students will do applied data analytics research on an open problems for a real-world client. They will work in teams and be coached by the instructors. They will interact regularly with and present their work to clients. Their final results will be formed into a paper and presentation presented at the class mini-conference during the final exam period. They will gain experiences with the steps necessary for successful applied data analytics research such as

- Data analytics problem formulation based on project objectives and data
- Problems in real-world applications and their potential contribution
- Data set evaluation and selection
- Data preparation and visualization
- Data analytics and AI application and results interpretation
- Data modeling and results interpretation
- Project planning and management
- Presentation and report preparation and delivery
- Researching approaches and methods

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will learn to pose data analytics questions needed to find insights into data to meet project objectives and translate these questions to data analytics workflows.
- Students will learn to evaluate data sets for completeness, utility for a given purpose, and presence of errors, ambiguities and other inconsistent content.
- Students will learn to correct data set flaws and deficiencies, and otherwise prepare data sets for use in various analytical processes (workflows).
- Students will apply quantitative analytic methods and learn to interpret the results and translate these into further analysis, modeling and project planning.
- Students will learn about health analytics and its potential contributions
- Students will practice presenting their results in oral and written form.
- Students will demonstrate use of collaboration tools for remote team work.
- Engage in the relevant literature and produce a review of the relevant research methods to their project including a presentation of their findings suitable for a diverse audience.
- Elucidate the knowledge acquired in the research review, assess how the research methods can be applied to their problem., and illustrate how to use one or more method in their project.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1. Individual notebooks: Students will individually document their research contributions and projects using an online, R-based project notebook submitted through githubs.
2. Two group project presentations
3. Group project paper documenting group work.
4. Final group research code or app with documentation
5. Attendance at standup and breakout for your team
6. Prepare and present relevant research review

## Project Notebook, Webex and Other Tools

In this course, we use "real world" collaboration, coding, and project management tools essential for highly effective group work.

R Notebooks: Students will be required to do R Notebook-based "project notebooks" at the individual and group levels. The intent of these notebooks is to encourage and help students manage their project work, including notes from client meetings, project team meeting notes, questions, references, code, intermediate and final results, and individual contributions. R Notebooks can also be used as the basis for frequent project presentations, by knitting (rendering) slides from RStudio in `slidy` or other slide format.

As with true laboratory notebooks, students should add content to their R notebooks documenting their project work while they are performing that work and while it is fresh in their minds. Students should not plan to wait and "tidy up" their notebooks before each evaluation; this just wastes time.

One or more model R Notebooks will be shared with the class at the beginning of the course.

WEBEX TEAMS: Online communication both chat and video (as necessary) for this course will be done primarily on WEBEX TEAMS. Our TEAM is called . Our main channel is called General DATA ANALYTICS RESEARCH [MATP 4910]. We recommend you install the WEBEX app and follow all class announcements, etc as well as Webex chat activity specific to each project. We have found through experience with projects, classes and internships that online chats are an extremely effective way to get answers quickly, address problems, and to coordinate with team members across location and time.

RPI IDEA RStudio Server: All course-related computation and data sharing will be done on IDEA's Web-based RStudio Server, hosted on the IDEA Cluster; this will include sharing R code with fellow project team members and sharing Project Notebooks with the instructors.
