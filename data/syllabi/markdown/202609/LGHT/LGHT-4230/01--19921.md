## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Lighting Design is a design studio teaching fundamentals to both undergraduate and graduate students, exploring the role and application of light in the built environment through design. Students observe, conceive, evaluate, and synthesize approaches that contribute to successful lighting and architectural design.

The Lighting Design class emphasizes lectures, class discussions, field trips, individual and group research, design, writing, and reading assignments, supplemented with studio work. Detailed assignment statements will be distributed through the LMS.  
In addition to the regular class times, students shall plan to be available for required field trips, reviews, and guest lectures that might be scheduled outside of class time. During scheduled studio sessions, students are expected to be present in person, unless specifically announced otherwise. During studio desk crits, students will work on their projects or sketch assignments and/or observe and participate in desk crits of the other students. Observation of lighting might require darkness outdoors, so several classes may be held outside of these core hours, off-site.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

As part of this course, the students shall:

- “Learn to see,” becoming acquainted with the process and importance of visual observation and creative visualization.
- Understand the process and importance of client interviews, understanding client needs, and be able to effectively conduct a client interview and needs assessment.
- Establish project goals, objectives, and design intent based upon client needs.
- Understand the information needed to convey the concept of a project.
- Plan and prepare the appropriate verbal, written, and visual presentations required to convey the design concept for conceptual and comprehensive lighting design projects.
- Understand and make use of design philosophies, processes, and approaches to lighting design and develop their own personal philosophy of design.
- Create and develop their own unique lighting designs.
- Understand the role of daylight in lighting designs and be able to analyze daylight contribution to lighting in interior building spaces.
- Experience light as a visual medium.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to develop their own unique lighting designs

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

The class is structured in three general parts: 1) Experiencing and Analyzing Light; 2) Designing with Light; 3) Communicating Lighting Design.  
The final course grade will be determined based on the following deliverables throughout the semester:  
A. Homeworks (sketches, writings, observations, analyses): 20%  
B. Workshops (lighting experiences, effects, simulations): 10%  
C. Quizzes: 5%  
D. Individual Lighting Designs (Concept, Detail, Presentation): 30%  
E. Group Lighting Designs (Concept, Detail, Presentation): 30%  
F. Class participation: 5%  
Mid-semester students will receive a pre-assessment indicating their standing in the class.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are expected to attend all classes, however, there is no need for a medical excuse to be provided for absence of up to a week (five days). Students missing more than a week of classes due to illness will be required to provide a doctor’s note or other supporting documentation to the Student Success Office. The Class Dean will communicate the receipt of the note (with expected end date) to the relevant faculty. Students are responsible for assignments, quizzes, labs, exams, etc. – due to illness, quarantine/isolation, or other circumstances. When possible, students should notify the instructor in advance of an absence. The instructor will make every effort to work with and provide reasonable modifications for students who cannot attend class due to illness to complete assignments and exams. Students should keep in mind that virtual learning or class recording may not be possible. Students who adhere to these processes will not be penalized per the attendance policy (class participation) for the course. Students are expected to maintain punctuality and to participate actively in all class discussions, studio and laboratory sessions, and project reviews. All students are expected to come to class with all required reading and assignments completed. On time attendance at all scheduled meetings is mandatory. Late assignments will only be accepted for valid reasons as described above. An unsatisfactory record of attendance may result in a failing grade, and/or dismissal from the program. Students are expected to maintain punctuality, and late arrivals may be recorded as absences.
