### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**1.) Attendance is required.**

Three unexcused absences and you fail. (Of course, allowances are made if there is grave illness or something of this nature.) Please notify Professor and TA of excused absences via email.

Attendance must be logged in for each class via the app supervised by TA Ed Morris.

**2) Contribution to class discussions based on required readings, assignments, and lectures/presentations.** **(8% of total grade)**

Discussions will be founded on students weekly reading/listening/viewing of news media sources, additional required readings and screenings, and the course lectures and presentations.

Students will prepare oral reports based on their analysis of news media reading/listening/viewing and required readings and screenings. Readings will be available online or at Rensselaer's Learning Management System (LMS) site: https://lms.rpi.edu.

**Class participation, notes:** It is expected that students will participate in class and take notes during class, and your attention to this matter may affect your class participation grade. If you have a learning issue where note-taking is a distraction, or, for some reason taking notes is a hindrance to your learning, please contact Professor Staniszewski about this.

**Laptops/Electronic Devices:** The use of laptops, phones, and electronic devices for non-class related activity will result in a lower final grade for the course.

**Summary of class participation guidelines and grading/evaluation**: If you do not contribute to class discussions or regularly use electronic devices for non-class activity, or never take notes: this is an D or F grade for the class participation portion of your grade; very occasional participation or some non-class online engagement with electronic devices or very occasional note taking is a C-, C, or C+; some participation with contributions to discussion, with some notes, and no online mis-use, is a B-, B, or B+; and full participation with regular contributions to discussions and notes, and no on-line mis-use, an A-, A, or A+.

**3) News Media Analysis of Course New Media Sources Assignments:**

**Part 1: Introductory “Round Robin”: Oral “informal” 5-minute presentation (no PowerPoints or podium) where students present their initial assessments of the 6 course media sources:** **(8% of total grade)**

**Part: 2: Take-home essay questions dealing with the 6 course news media sources and introductory readings: Minimum 4 pages of analysis with 2 pages of notes and bibliography. This will be a take-home essay based on your weekly research of news media sources and course content** **(28% of total grade)**

Your essay should be at a minimum 6 double-spaced pages with additional pages for Chicago Manual of Style “notes-and-bibliography-style” endnotes (not footnotes) and bibliography. You will be graded on your endnote and bibliography formatting. The endnotes and bibliography can be singled-spaced.

**4.Two presentations and essays (28% each).**

Students will give 2 approximately 5-minute presentations analyzing: a contemporary political/social issue and the news media, or a contemporary political/social issue and a work of art dealing with these concerns. All topics must be approved by Professor Staniszewski. The presentations will be an opportunity to integrate students’ particular interests into the course.

An essay is required for each presentation. Each essay should be at least 4 double-spaced pages with 2 pages of Chicago Manual of Style “notes-and-bibliography-style” endnotes (not footnotes) and bibliography. You will be graded on your endnote and bibliography formatting. The endnotes and bibliography can be singled-spaced.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grading/Evaluation of presentations and essays:  
A: Excellent presentations and essays, distinguished by originality of topic, and depth of research. Essays have good grammar, are spell-checked, and formatting requirements are met.  
B: Good presentations and essays, with solid research. Essays have good grammar, are spell-checked, and formatting requirements met.  
C: Minimal work on presentations and essays. Essays have satisfactory grammar, may have some issues with bibliography, citations, spelling, and formatting requirements.  
D: Poor quality work on presentations and essays with the latter having incorrect content, insufficient length, sloppy bibliographies and citations, and incorrect formatting, spelling, and grammar.  
F: Extremely inadequate work on presentations and essays, or incomplete assignments. Essays with incorrect content, insufficient length, incorrect bibliographies, no citations when necessary, incorrect formatting, spelling, and grammar.

Students are not permitted to re-do or re-submit papers or assessments. However, if improvements are made during the semester, there will be a strong emphasis on this improved work when determining the final grade.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**ACADEMIC INTEGRITY**

“Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy.”\[1][2]

“Intellectual integrity and credibility are the foundation of all academic work. A violation of Academic Integrity policy is, by definition, considered a flagrant offense to the educational process. It is taken seriously by students, faculty, and Rensselaer and will be addressed in an effective manner. If found responsible for committing academic dishonesty, a student may be subject to one or both types of penalties: an academic (grade) penalty administered by the professor and/or disciplinary action through the Rensselaer judicial process described in” The Rensselaer Student Handbook.

“Academic dishonesty is a violation of the Grounds for Disciplinary Action as described in this handbook. A student may be subject to any of the following types of disciplinary action should disciplinary action be pursued by the instructor: disciplinary warning; disciplinary probation; disciplinary suspension, expulsion and/or alternative actions as agreed on by the student and hearing officer. It should be noted that no student who allegedly commits academic dishonesty will be able to drop or change the grade option for the course in question and is not eligible to request an F examination for the course.”[iii]

See the “RPI Student Handbook” for types of academic dishonesty, such as plagiarism, and for more information.[iv]Plagiarism, for example, is quoting or very closely paraphrasing a text without citing the source. All quotes, citations of data, and close paraphrases should be footnoted with a citation to the original source. If you are uncertain about how to properly source material in your assignments for this course, check with the Professor or TA about the proper procedures. Guidelines for proper sources will be reviewed in class and are listed at the end of the syllabus. If a student commits academic dishonesty, such as plagiarism, they will receive a zero for the assignment. If this occurs a second time, the student will fail the course. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
