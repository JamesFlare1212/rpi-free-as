## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Explain foundational AI concepts, including machine learning, neural networks, large language models, recommendation systems, and generative AI, in accessible language.
- Describe how data, model design, objectives, and evaluation choices shape the behavior of AI systems.
- Evaluate AI systems using evidence about accuracy, reliability, bias, fairness, transparency, and failure modes.
- Analyze how AI systems interact with social, economic, institutional, and cultural contexts.
- Identify key stakeholders in an AI system and assess how benefits, risks, and responsibilities are distributed among them.
- Conduct small-scale experiments and investigations with contemporary AI systems and interpret the results critically.
- Compare technical, organizational, and policy approaches for addressing AI-related risks and harms.
- Develop and communicate an evidence-based recommendation for the design, deployment, or governance of an AI system.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students will demonstrate mastery of learning outcomes through regular assignments, applied briefs, reading checks, in-class participation, and a staged final project:

- **Reading Preparation Checks:** Brief pre-class submissions (identifying one important idea, one specific claim/example, and one question/connection).
- **Hands-on Assignments & Audits:** Small-scale experiments and evidence-based argument briefs evaluating data classifiers, fairness/explanations, generative AI, recommender systems, and high-stakes impacts.
- **Final Project & Presentation:** Staged team milestones leading to a comprehensive Final System Dossier + Write-up and a final oral presentation.
- **In-Class Activities:** Collaborative discussions, case critiques, experiments, and peer review.

For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Rensselaer's Academic Integrity Policy applies to all work in this course.

**Use of Generative AI:**

- Because this course studies AI through direct use, generative AI tools will be permitted or required for some activities, with permission being assignment-specific.
- When AI use is permitted, disclose the tool and how it was used. For substantive use, retain relevant prompts/outputs or provide them when requested.
- You remain responsible for the accuracy of facts, citations, calculations, and claims in anything you submit.
- Do not present AI-generated analysis or prose as your own thinking when an assignment asks for your individual reasoning, reflection, or evaluation.
- Do not fabricate experiments, outputs, sources, or user data. If an AI tool generates a source, verify that the source exists and supports the claim.
- When an assignment restricts AI use, those restrictions are part of the assignment requirements and violations fall under the Rensselaer Academic Integrity policy.
