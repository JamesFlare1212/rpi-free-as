## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Welcome to *Well-being: Cultivating Curiosity!*

From the course catalog: How do we maintain a sense of well-being in our lives? Each person’s path to well-being in body and mind is unique—arising from an awareness of our needs, goals and what we find fulfilling. We will use the theme of curiosity to explore what makes us tick, what makes us feel balanced, stressed, or calm. This interdisciplinary course uses practice-based learning, in-class writing, lectures, creative play, and reading. The class is Communication Intensive.

Additional info: *Attendance and curious participation* in class are required. Your attendance and engagement is crucial because much of the class is centered on experiential learning right in the classroom. Experiential learning can be a rather slow process. It can be frustrating, because often we can *mentally* understand something quickly, but the body and mind may need time to embody this knowledge. We acknowledge that each person in class arrives with different backgrounds, skill-sets, curiosities, and bodies/brains/minds. Please be patient with yourself, your classmates, and your professor.

Our class is geared toward heightening your awareness of experiences and to develop your writing about these experiences, building your own well-being toolbox, as well as nourishing intellectual insights about the material. You will have time during each class meeting to encounter a wide variety of experiences in order to generate ideas and to write on the spot. This process of generating text is expected to be exploratory and even messy at times. Know that generative writing stages can provide new insights and you will have opportunities to re-write, edit and compose as necessary. As a Communication Intensive course, students will receive detailed feedback on at least one draft before the final submission of papers. Expect to write often in class and for graded assignments.

We will be reading one novel this semester and several other chapters. Although they will be on different topics, they are similar in that we will be considering different lived experiences and relating these topics to well-being from different viewpoints. Thinking about our own identities in relation to the world around us as well as the authors and characters in our readings is a key component of our class.

In addition to daily writing in class, students should expect to regularly work in pairs or small groups and engage fully in class (discussions, dialogues, in-class generative and creative projects, etc). Please expect that you will be asked to share your thoughts, questions, and work each class period. Expect to contribute to our regular conversations about the material covered in our class as well as the readings.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\) Students will be able to communicate their ideas clearly, effectively, and creatively.
- 2\) Students will engage curiosity and consider well-being through different forms of expression.
- 3\) Students will be able to demonstrate their understanding of stress and well-being from their own and different perspectives.
- 4\) Students will review and compare a range of well-being practices and technologies.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**RPI’s Academic Integrity Policy**

Intellectual integrity is critical to the foundation of all academic work. Academic dishonesty, therefore, is considered a serious matter and will be addressed as such. As defined in the current Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities, examples of academic dishonesty include, but are not limited to: plagiarism, academic fraud, collaboration, copying, cribbing, fabrication, plagiarism, sabotage, and substitution. Additionally, attempts to commit academic dishonesty, or to assist in the commission or attempt of such an act, are also violations of the academic integration policy. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities define***s*** various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. You should make yourself familiar with these definitions of academic dishonesty and policies. All forms of academic dishonesty are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own performance. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work for this class. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. If found in violation of this policy, students may be subject to two types of penalty. I will administer a grade penalty and the student is reported to the Dean of Students (or Graduate Education). The first violation results in 0 for that assignment. The second violation results in failure of the course. Please ask for clarification if you have any questions.

**Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism**  
Although there will be collaboration in activities and some assignments in class, all assignments (except when noted, e.g. the group project) that are turned in for a grade must be the student’s own original work for this class only. In cases where help was received, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration (e.g. if someone else helped with editing or provided feedback). Work composed in whole or in part by artificial intelligence (e.g. ChatGPT) will be considered cheating. Plagiarizing, as defined by Webster’s, is “to steal and pass off (the ideas or words of another) as one’s own” or use of another’s material without crediting the source. In this class you will be expected to attribute due credit to the originator of any ideas, words, sounds, or work, which you incorporate substantially into your own work. This applies particularly to citation of sources for quotes and ideas included in your compositions that you did not write yourself. Submission of any assignment will result in penalties as described above. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
