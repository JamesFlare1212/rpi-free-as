## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

## Economics examines how people make choices in the face of limited resources. This course introduces students to the fundamental question of allocation: how societies decide what to produce, how to produce it, and who receives the resulting goods and services. Building on this foundation, students will learn to reason through decisions using the concept of trade-offs and opportunity cost, and will study how prices coordinate economic activity through the interaction of buyers and sellers in demand and supply theory. The course examines a range of market structures and evaluates the circumstances under which government involvement in markets may be warranted.

## The second half of the course turns to the economy as a whole. Students will learn how economists measure national output and income, track price changes over time, assess labor market conditions, and examine the role that money and the banking system play in the broader economy.

## By the end of the course, students will be equipped to apply economic reasoning to individual decisions and to interpret major macroeconomic indicators and policy debates.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Apply the concept of opportunity cost to explain how individuals, firms, and society make choices under scarcity.
- Analyze market outcomes using demand and supply theory, including how changes in prices, incomes, and other determinants shift equilibrium price and quantity.
- Assess the rationale for and consequences of government intervention in markets, including taxes, subsidies, price controls, and regulation.
- Interpret key macroeconomic indicators, including GDP, inflation, and unemployment, and explain what they reveal about the health of an economy.
- Explain the role of money and the banking system in the macroeconomy, and describe how monetary and fiscal policy tools are used to influence economic outcomes.
- Communicate economic reasoning clearly, using appropriate terminology and data to support arguments about individual decisions and broader policy questions.
