## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**COURSE DESCRIPTION**

First Year Studio is an introductory seminar where students engage in Collaboratory, interdisciplinary, practice-based projects. This work helps to define the connection between research, discussion, ideation, and making in the world of visual arts.

This course exposes first-year arts students to the purposes and methodologies of a studio practice, and to the social, cultural, environmental, and economic issues being addressed throughout the historical trajectory of artmaking.

A series of studio projects are undertaken. These are based on individual lectures by contemporary artists, scientists, engineers, and other professionals; whose concepts and methods are t re-interpreted as artworks through research, group making sessions, and ultimately finished individual works for review.

Critical abilities are developed through group discussions and critiques, which reveal a diversity of perspectives and the need for ethical responsibility in the artists’ voice.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Assignments**

You must complete all major assignments. **If you do not submit all assigned projects, you will not pass the class**. Projects presented in an unfinished state on the due date **will not** be critiqued or graded.

**Late Work**

You should submit your work on time. Due dates and times will be announced in class (another reason for regular attendance). Late work will only be accepted after consultation with the professor prior to the day in which the work is due, or an official excuse is presented.

**EVALUATION AND GRADING AS A PERCENTAGE OF SEMESTER TOTAL**

All grades are based on the following:

- **10%** / **Attendance** at all lectures and demonstrations. (**2** points deducted for any missed lecture.)
- **10%** / Interest and **regular** **participation** in discussions and critiques (Final number represented by a sliding scale 10,8,6,4,2,0)
- **10%** / Motivated problem solving, serious and lively approach to **presentation** of projects, and timely completion of **sketchbook** assignments. (Final number represented by a sliding scale 10,8,6,4,2,0)
- **60%** / **(10%** for each of 6 projects.) Quality of completed **projects** represented by understanding of concepts discussed CLEARLY demonstrated in work.
- Each finished project is graded using the following scale: **√+ = 10** points, **√+/√ = 8** points, **√ = 6** points, **√/√- = 4** points, **√- = 2** points. There are also ½ scale steps of 9, 7, 5, and 3 points which are given for smaller moments of positive or negative attributes in the finished work. These steps are indicated with a circle around the √+ or √-.
- √+: Finished work exemplifies a **complete understanding** of the principles relevant to that project. The work is made with an attention to the individual **student’s** **best** **craft practices** and presents a **thorough****exploration** and application of the concepts discussed in group critiques and demonstrations.
- √+/√: Finished work demonstrates an understanding of **most** of the principles relevant to that project. The work is made with an attention to the individual student’s best craft practices and presents a **concerted effort**towards exploration and application of the concepts discussed in group critiques and demonstrations.
- √: Finished work shows **some** understanding of the principles relevant to that project. The work is made with the student’s **individual** craft practices and presents some exploration and application of the concepts discussed in group critiques and demonstrations.
- √/√-: Finished work exemplifies **very little** understanding of the principles relevant to that project. The work is made **without concern** for the individual student’s best craft practices and presents only **a few moments** of exploration and application of the concepts discussed in group critiques and demonstrations.

- √-: Work is **unfinished** or exemplifies **no understanding** of the principles relevant to that project, and clearly reveals **no exploration or application** of the concepts discussed in group critiques and demonstrations.

- **10%** / Presentation of final written statement and completed **portfolio**.

**Grading example for good but not excellent work over the semester:**

Missed 1 lecture by visiting professional without official excuse or agreement - **2** pts. = **8** pts.

Participation regular but not exemplary = **8** pts.

Presentation of projects and sketchbook work of very good standard = **8** pts.

Work in both individual and finished projects averaging very good but not outstanding = **8** pts. each + extra **2** pts. for excellence in midterm project. = **50** pts.

Final portfolio finished and presented on time = **10** pts.

Total of **84** pts. = a letter grade of **B**.

**Conditions for evaluation of student performance**

- Is the student mentally present, ready to work, and committed to contributing to the day’s conversation?
- Is the student turning in finished works by the due date?
- Is the student working productively and to the communal benefit when working as part of a team?
- Is the student making well crafted, complex, and conceptually layered works that fulfill their stated goals?
- Does the student search for higher ground, and push to expand their knowledge and skill set to produce work of increasing quality and significance?
- When critiquing the work of others, does the student show interest in the artist’s sources and interpretations, and make comments related to their presentation, which help to amplify their meanings and inform the artist of possible alternative choices for realizing their intent?
- Does the student present their work using intelligent and applicable references to their stated intent and to their awareness of the relationship of their work to related examples from the greater history of art making?
- After receiving constructive criticism, does the student respond in a positively considered manner to improve their work?

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance, participation in critiques, and presentation of final portfolio are required.** Regular attendance is mandatory unless special circumstances have been presented, discussed, and agreed at the beginning of the term. **Regular absences which affect the quality of the learning experience and the work produced will negatively affect the final grade.**

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Dishonesty**

*The Rensselaer Handbook* defines various forms of academic dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Students are responsible for familiarizing themselves with these policies. Students should note that the penalty for plagiarism and other forms of cheating is a grade of “F” for the course.

In this class “cheating” consists of plagiarism or representing the work of others as your own through deliberate omission of acknowledgments or references. Cheating also consists of copying graphics, created by others and submitting that work as your own.

It is always acceptable (and encouraged) to discuss your assignments with others in the class and solicit feedback to improve your work.
