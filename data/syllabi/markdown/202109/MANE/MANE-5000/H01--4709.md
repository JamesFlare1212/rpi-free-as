## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Description:**

An introductory graduate course in advanced engineering mathematics. This course provides the knowledge necessary to study engineering applications consistent with our physical laws. Topics include: differential equations, linear systems, vector calculus, series solutions, and partial differential equations.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Kreyszig, Advanced Engineering Mathematics, Wiley and Sons, 10th Ed. (ISBN 978-0-470-45836-5)

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Course Objectives:**

- *To impart an understanding of ordinary differential equations methods for application to several engineering disciplines.*
- *To familiarize students with linear and Eigen-systems, vector space, and vector calculus as mathematical models for engineering applications**.*
- *To provide an understanding of concepts and applications of partial differential equations.*

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Learning Outcomes: By the end of the course, students will be able to:  Understand and solve relevant engineering application problems through use of ordinary differential equations, and linear systems.  Be able to select series solutions methodologies to address and evaluate various sets of engineering problems.  Be able to apply partial differential equation models to solve problems in several engineering disciplines

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity and Rules of Behavior**

Any form of cheating will result in an F for the test. Both types of violations will be reported immediately to the Dean of Students Office.

I do not expect that we will incur any issues with academic integrity. With that being said, if such issues do arise, the proper measures, laid out in the student handbook and discussed in Section I, will be taken.

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust, and this trust is especially critical for online classes. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments which students turn in are their own effort. Acts which violate this trust, undermine the educational process. An extensive and general discussion of this subject is contained in the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and Graduate Student supplement to the handbook.

Evidence of copying another person's work is considered a serious violation of professional and academic ethics. Violations will be reported immediately to the Dean of Students Office.

**A special note regarding the use of solution manuals:** The use of solution manuals to solve quiz, HW, or test problems is prohibited. Using solutions manuals is considered plagiarism and/or copying and is in direct violation of the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities, as well as the Graduate Student supplement to the handbook. All quizzes and assignments showing evidence of the use of solution manuals will receive a zero and, under my discretion, may also be reported to the Dean of Students Office.

**Buckley Amendment**

The Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (Buckley Amendment) guarantees privacy to students, particularly in the areas of grades and performance.
