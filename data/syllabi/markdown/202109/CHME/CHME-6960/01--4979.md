## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is designed to deliver introductory concepts and methods to understand the physical chemistry aspects of linear polymers and associated systems. Emphasis is placed on building theoretical and analytical models of linear chain polymers to understand their thermodynamic and dynamic properties translated to the thermal and mechanical properties of polymers. Those course objectives for students will be achieved by promoting students’ understanding of basic and fundamental physical phenomena and logical reasoning. Courses at RPI are generally expected to require 2-3 hours of outside work for each credit hour for average outcomes. *You should expect, therefore, to commit at least 6 hours/week outside the classroom for this course.*

### Goals:

This course aims to establish students’ understanding of the physical properties of polymeric materials through logical reasoning for processing polymeric materials and using polymeric materials for engineering applications.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand basic structures of linear polymers and their derivatives.
- Understand thermodynamics of polymer blends.
- Understand principles of scattering characterizations of polymers.
- Understand viscoelastic properties of polymers.
- Understand elasticity of polymeric materials.
- Understand glass states of polymers.
- Understand semi-crystalline states of polymers.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The learning outcomes of this course will be measured by homework, exams, and attendance/participation. Students showing active reasoning on the course subjects will receive favorable considerations for the final grades.

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

**Regrading Policy**: If you feel your work/exam was not accurately graded, you must submit a review request in a written form within one week of receiving your graded work/exam. You must cite the posted solutions in your written argument why you believe it was not accurately graded.

**Collaboration**: Students are encouraged to discuss the underlying concepts and approaches with classmates. However, the work turned in should reflect one’s own efforts and development for the final answer. Any sign of non-self-work in the homework will result in the serious penalty to the final grade even failure of this course.

**Note about Showing Work:** In this class, the process of getting to an answer is as important (if not more) than the actual answer. Therefore, you *must show and justify* all of your work to receive credit. If you do not show your work, you will receive zero credit (even if the answer is correct).
