## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course introduces common computational techniques that are used in Chemical Engineering. Topics include solving algebraic equations, data analysis, numerical differentiation and integration, and numerical solutions of differential equations applied to chemical engineering problems. Multiple numerical approaches will be used, including an introduction to computer programming.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

“Numerical Methods with Chemical Engineering Applications” by Dorfman and Daoutidis, Cambridge University Press, 2017.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Develop algorithms to solve linear/non-linear systems of equations
- Write short computer programs for applying numerical methods
- Apply numerical curve fitting and interpolation techniques

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

**Note about Showing Work:** In this class, the process of getting to an answer is as important (if not more) than the actual answer. Therefore, you *must show and justify* all of your work to receive credit. If you do not show your work, you will not receive credit (even if the answer is correct). **For example, a series of equations without explanation of what manipulations are being done will not receive credit even if all equations are correct.** If you are algebraically manipulating equations to simplify, you should state that in words. We will be using computer programs to perform some calculations. Unless specifically requested in the problem statement, it is not acceptable to use packages such as MATLAB, Maple, Mathematica, etc. to perform manipulations, integrals, etc.

**Extra Credit:** It is possible that there are some typographical errors in the textbook. If you find them, you will have an opportunity to earn extra points towards your homework score. To earn points, you should submit *in writing or by email* a description of the error (including the location in the text), what the correct version of the text should be, and why. For each correct “error”, you will receive 2 points towards your homework score. You will not receive points if the error was already submitted by another student. Submissions should be made either in person or by email. If a group of students submits an error, the points will be distributed equally among the students in the group.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
