## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is designed to introduce students to the basic data analysis and statistical methods necessary to conduct and understand environmental research. Topics and methods covered in this course include experimental design, graphing and data presentation, descriptive statistics, comparison tests, correlation and regression, and other parametric and non-parametric statistical methods. There is a strong emphasis on the application of these methods to datasets that describe current environmental problems and issues. No prior experience with specialized mathematics, coding, or any particular software is required.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Samples, populations, summary statistics, experimental design, data exploration, graphing, assumptions, t-tests, F-tests, analysis of variance (ANOVA), correlation, regression, repeated measures, Chi-square tests, non-parametric tests, principle component analysis (PCA), cluster analysis

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Design environmental science experiments that can be statistically tested and avoid bias
- Identify which graphing and statistical methods are most appropriate for different types of data
- Perform exploratory analyses and apply statistics to draw conclusions from environmental datasets
- Assess the validity and interpretation of data presented in scientific and mainstream publications
- Exhibit proficiency in using Excel for data exploration, graphing, and statistical analysis
- Demonstrate how data presentation and statistics is used as an effective means of communication

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Class participation** – Students will complete Gradescope assignments during each lecture. The assignments are designed to reinforce key points covered in the lecture and assigned textbook readings. If the assignment grade is ≥ 70%, then the student will receive full credit. If the assignment grade is < 70%, then the student will receive the actual grade earned. Understanding why answers are wrong is part of the learning process and this grading approach is meant to encourage critical thinking efforts while you explore a new topic, without penalizing a few missteps that may happen along the way.

**Data labs** – Students will work in small groups to complete and submit a lab assignment that asks them to apply graphing and/or statistical methods learned in class to analyze and draw conclusions from an environmental dataset or assigned readings. Working groups will be assigned by the instructor. Students in each working group are expected to coordinate with one another to discuss, complete, and submit each lab assignment on time. Groups will turn in a single lab assignment. As part of their submission, group members will be asked to describe their individual contributions to the assignment. The lowest lab assignment grade per student will be dropped.

**Semester project** – There will be a final project in which students will work to analyze environmental datasets. Students will work individually and self-select their dataset based on their research interests (e.g., climate change, agricultural development, urbanization, energy policy). The final project is composed of several sub-assignments that will be graded throughout the semester.
