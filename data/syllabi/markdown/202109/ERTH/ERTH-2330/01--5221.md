## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

In this class we will explore the chemical and physical properties of the minerals that comprise the solid earth. Major topics include crystal chemistry and structure, optical properties of minerals, chemical reactions involving minerals, and classification of minerals. Labs will include a mixture of physical hand specimen classification, petrographic microscope examination of minerals, and use of RPI lab facilities to characterize mineral properties.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

***Text:***

***Minerals: Their Constitution and Origin*** by H.-R. Wenk and A. Bulakh

Class lectures will be separate from textbook readings. Both class notes and textbook chapters will be referenced for class assignments and exams.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Learning outcomes**

The student will, at the successful completion of the course be able to:

(1) Identify and characterize 30-40 minerals of geologic significance by examining hand samples and petrographic thin sections.

(2) Students will become familiar with the chemical formula and different mineral crystal structures of key minerals that comprise the majority of the solid earth.

(3) Become proficient at using a petrographic microscope to identify minerals, categorize mineral assemblages, and describe common mineral textures.

(4) Be able to relate chemical data to mineral composition and classification.

(5) Students will have a basic understanding of Phase diagrams

Please note: several learning outcomes depend on students spending enough time in lab to become familiar with physical hand specimens and thin sections. Due to potential social distancing protocols, additional times will be arranged for students to be in the classroom to complete their labs.

Science is fundamentally collaborative, and students are encouraged to engage with their peers to work on problem sets, lab exercises, and studying for exams.
