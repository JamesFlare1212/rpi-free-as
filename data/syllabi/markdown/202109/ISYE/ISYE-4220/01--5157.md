## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Design, analysis, and implementation of algorithms for combinatorial optimization problems. Introduction to theoretical analysis of algorithms and applications that can be formulated as combinatorial optimization problems. Specific topics include complexity analysis, network flow problems, traveling salesperson problems, matching problems, knapsack problems, and greedy algorithms. Implementation of combinatorial algorithms in a commercial software language. An introduction to this software language will be given at the beginning of the course.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

- *Combinatorial Optimization* by William J. Cook, William H. Cunningham, William R. Pulleyblank, and Alexander Schrijver.
- *Introduction to Algorithms*, Second Edition by Cormen, Leiserson, Rivest, and Stein.
- *Combinatorial Optimization: Algorithms and Complexity* by Papadimitriou and Steiglitz.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course aims to provide students with:

- An ability to apply concepts in computer programming and theoretical computer science to the field of Operations Research.
- An ability to take real-world problems from its problem statement to implementable solution approaches to solve the problem. Students will learn to analyze problems for hidden structure and properties and to use these properties to their advantage in developing algorithms to solve the problem.
- An ability to develop and compare (both theoretically and practically) algorithms that solve combinatorial optimization problems.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

- Complexity analysis, including asymptotic analysis and NP-Completeness.
- Types of algorithms including greedy, divide and conquer, dynamic programming, branch and bound, approximation algorithms, and local search.
- Combinatorial optimization problems, such as the knapsack problem, minimum spanning trees, shortest path problems, maximum flow problems, facility location problems, and the traveling salesperson problem.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**In-Class Assignments:** There will be in-class problems that will be completed every class. You will be responsible for handing these for credit. To receive full credit, you must watch the appropriate videos before class. I will track your video views through LMS. We will use gradescope to submit the in-class assignments. At the end of the semester, I will drop five of your lowest assignment grades. These may include any assignments that you did not submit because you missed class.

**Homework:** There will be four homework assignments that should be done individually. These will be progressively released through LMS. The student may discuss their work with other students, yet each one should upload their own solutions through LMS.

**Project:** Students must develop two projects throughout the course. The instructions will be available in LMS. These may be done in groups of 1-3 people. Students should select their project team within the first three weeks of class and should send an email to the instructor with the names of their groups.

**Final Exam:** The final exam is a final project that should be done individually at the end of the course. The student will have a few days to develop the project. The student may not seek help from any person (other than the instructor) during this time or use any external help other than the resources available in the course. More information about the format of the exam and rules are given prior to the exam. The exam will be released in LMS and will be available for a specific time frame. The final exam deliverables should be submitted through LMS prior to the posted deadline.

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

***Class attendance:*** Class attendance is not mandatory, but highly encouraged. Your participating grade depends on it. At the end of each class you will be required to turn in your on the in-class assignment. Towards the end of the semester, you will be able to drop your lowest in-class assignment grade (up to five) in case you miss any classes. To achieve the course objectives, there will be times when we, as a class, examine problems and algorithms together, interactively. Active class participation will be helpful in your learning the material and may positively affect your grade.

Being prepared for class is also essential. That means, watching the videos prior to class and bringing your laptop. If for some reason you were unable to watch the videos, do not think you cannot attend class. Please do come to class. You need to bring your laptop every time to class so that you are prepared to code an algorithm or use an optimization program as part of the in-class assignment. We will use Gradescope to submit in-class assigments, and we will use Python as our programming language. I am also using GAMS to solve some models analytically (although this is not the focus of the class). At the beginning of the course, I will provide some videos for those who need an introduction to Python and GAMS. You will need to have Python installed before the second lecture.

Please silence your cell phones during class time.

**Grade Appeals:** It is possible for you to appeal your grade on certain assignment. All appeals should be done within one week of when the assignment is handed back in class and must be in writing with some exceptions.

**Grade Resurrection:** There is no way to improve your grade on the projects and final exam. However, I may do a curve up of the grades by the end of the semester. You will also be allowed to drop up to five in-class assignment grades in case you missed class.

**Missing Tests or Homework:** Missing tests are only allowed if there is an emergency verified by the Dean of Student’s office. There are *no* makeups for tests. Missing assessments without official excuses will receive a zero.

**Excusal from Course Assignments and Examinations:** If you know ahead of time that you will miss an exam/assignment deadline, then you must make arrangements before the assessment is due. Failure to do so will result in 25% off for each 24-hour period the assessment is late.

If an emergency arises and a student cannot submit assigned work on or before the scheduled due date or cannot take an exam on the scheduled date, the student (or the Student Experience Office) MUST give notification to the instructor NO MORE THAN 48 HOURS AFTER the scheduled date. If you have an emergency, the campus is here to help, please coordinate with the Student Experience Office – 4th floor of Academy Hall, x8022, se@rpi.edu

End-of-semester grades are not given. Instead, end-of-semester grades are calculated according to a student’s performance on the individual assessment requirements and the weights in this syllabus. Final grades are, final. Therefore, final grades will not be changed unless a numerical error is made in computing your final grade. After final grades have been calculated, I will not respond to emails received requesting a grade change unless it is in response to a numerical calculation error. Please do not ask for extra credit or extra work after final exams have been administered.
