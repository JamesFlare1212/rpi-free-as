### Text Details:

REFERENCE MATERIALS / READINGS / DISCUSSIONS

The studio, like the design development phase in practice, requires research. You are expected to initiate investigations about systems, materials and precedents to determine how a particular effect or performance can be achieved. The thirst for knowledge, curiosity, and invention within the restraints of material characteristics, given economies and construction techniques is arguably what keeps the profession alive for so many.

Readings that provide specific and varied insights into the approaches and attitudes taken by select architects will be assigned throughout the semester. It is expected that you read and prepare yourself to discuss their relevance to the efforts of the studio and your project as they are distributed. They will serve as an aid in helping us understand the importance of material selection and detailing as a design act that relates to larger intentions. Other references (the tech library (studio), the Architecture library reserve, and the web) are provided for your use and continuing development. Your initiative in finding and using these and other materials is expected.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

LEARNING OBJECTIVES AND OUTCOMES

NAAB 2020 STUDENT CRITERIA STUDENT CRITERIA (SC): STUDENT LEARNING OBJECTIVES AND OUTCOMES

SC.5 Design Synthesis—How the program ensures that students develop the ability to make design decisions within architectural projects while demonstrating synthesis of user requirements, regulatory requirements, site conditions, and accessible design, and consideration of the measurable environmental impacts of their design decisions.

SC.6 Building Integration—How the program ensures that students develop the ability to make design decisions within architectural projects while demonstrating integration of building envelope systems and assemblies, structural systems, environmental control systems, life safety systems, and the measurable outcomes of building performance.

OBJECTIVES (GOALS)

1\. To develop the capacity to creatively integrate materials, systems, and geometry in order to provoke a tangible and persuasive architecture that:

1. Advances the project brief,
2. Furthers sustainable approaches to building, and
3. Produces architectural knowledge and expands possibility.

2\. To develop the capacity to design at various scales of material realization and assembly in support and development of conceptual intentions/ideas while integrating building systems and addressing regulatory and technical requirements.

3\. To develop the capacity to integrate building systems and technological concerns in design. Students will develop their capacity to pay critical attention to those joining - both tangible and poetic, which constitute the concreteness of Architecture – a concreteness that is perceived, tactile, and produces effect.

4\. To develop the capacity to integrate those aspects of the building enterprise that lead to material, physical construction according to codes and other performance requirements.

5\. To learn the value of addressing multiple design considerations iteratively, and in doing so, develop and awareness and understanding regarding how:

1. One scale informs/impacts another
2. One consideration informs/affects another, and
3. Disciplines affecting architectural design (structural, mechanical, acoustic, lighting, etc...) cannot be regarded as exclusive, isolated, or compartmentalized practices, and to develop the capacity to integrate that understanding early into the design process.

6\. To develop an awareness of information technology’s potential to radically impact building design and construction, including an ability to directly incorporate analysis and performance criteria, represent and communicate regarding the ultimate building design, how to construct it, and how digital techniques have the potential to further transform building processes and products.

At the end of the semester, each student team should have developed a more compelling design project with supporting documentation describing it in relation to all of its major systems and material considerations.

Assessment: Architectural Design Process and the degree to which the various concerns and information – introduced weekly, provoke questions, iteration and development in relationship to the ideas framed in the project statement and the relevant criteria and standards such as code, programmatic distribution, and systems integration, etc.

Assessment: Final Architectural Design Project: An integrated building project design that includes a well defined intent and basic resolution of structural, mechanical, material, enclosure, code, and programmatic considerations.

Assessment: Code analysis assignment and building design project integration including

full integration of accessibility / universal access and life-safety concerns in the final project.

Assessment: Design Development Project Set, process and final models [physical and digital] and Material Outline Specification

Assessment: Design and integration of structural systems to resist gravity and lateral forces in the final project.

Assessment: Integrated design of a building envelope system in the focus area as represented in renderings, in the focus area model and wall section.

Assessment: Project design and in particular through the focus area, wall section and detailing of the project in relation to aesthetic, programmatic and environmental considerations

Assessment: Design integration of vertical transportation and mechanical systems including plant space, location of major components and integrated distribution networks.

Assessment: Design integration of code analysis, structural and environmental and building systems consideration as demonstrated and documented in charrette topics (process), assignments and finally realized (integrated) into the final project proposal.

Assessment: Final Architectural Design Project: An integrated building project design that includes a well defined intent and basic resolution of structural, mechanical, material, enclosure, code, and programmatic considerations.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

ATTENDANCE

The studio meets on Monday, Wednesday, and Thursday afternoons from 2:00 to 5:50pm. When not participating in a review, lecture or critique you are expected to work on your project in the studio space and are strongly encouraged to do all studio work (including after hours) in the studio. At 2:00pm of each scheduled studio session there will be a meeting to discuss relevant issues, general progress, and the day’s objectives. This provides an opportunity to address questions that may be common to all students and/or pertinent to the progress of the projects. Topical seminars to address issues contemporaneous to studio concerns will be scheduled during studio hours.

Digital Workshops focusing on BIM and workflow will be held during regular class hours front-loaded to the first half of the semester. Attendance is required.

Attendance at select semester all school lectures and seminars will be required.

Participation in the studio, studio seminars, computing seminars, and field trips is required. Attendance is mandatory; 3 unexcused absences may adversely affect your grade by up to 25%, and 6 unexcused absences will result in automatic failure.

### Other Course Policies:

The studio will be organized in a manner that is conducive to collaboration and learning using both digital and analog tools. We will be encouraging you to use the studio as effectively as possible. A personal computer is studio is required. Laptop users must have a second monitor that you base (and secure) in the studio. If you have a desktop computer (recommended over a laptop), we strongly urge you to locate it in the studio with two monitors. We strongly recommend that all personal technology is locked to the desk.

The studio should become (both for the studio participants and those visiting the studio) a gallery of work in progress. Original models, site photos and drawings (images of the published work you are developing) should be prominently posted for continual reference. Digital images of your models, diagrams, your research projects, new drawings and prototype models should also be posted, as should precedents, projects, articles and images that you find during the semester. Whatever is not of interest should not be present or evident in the studio. We will make regular efforts at keeping the studio in an inspirational and professional working condition.
