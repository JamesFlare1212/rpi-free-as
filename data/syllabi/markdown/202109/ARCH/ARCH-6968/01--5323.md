## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Lighting is an integral part of architecture, highlighting the beauty, color, and texture of the built environment. The lighting fixtures selected for a space affect the overall light level, color rendering, and light distribution, which influence the occupants’ impression of the space. Lighting is more than just design. Learning about the; the science of lighting, the available technologies, and how to quantify the different parameters of light are very important when it comes to the selection of architectural lighting.

This course provides a broad, practical foundation in the science and mathematics of light. It teaches the skills and provides the analytic tools needed for quantitative lighting calculations; describes measurement techniques and instruments. In this class, students will also learn about the propagation of light through different transparent media, and its interaction with different interfaces such as reflective and refractive surfaces. Students will learn about different optical phenomena and optical components observed in lighting and for tailoring the beam to serve an application.

This course will teach important scientific concepts in lighting, lighting technologies, and the key components of a lighting system and their functions. Hands-on exercise will allow students to build a sample lighting product for a given task. This course has several laboratory sessions that helps to reiterate the concepts learned during lectures by observing demonstrations to see the possibilities of what lighting can offer with the different technologies. In addition, students will learn how to use laboratory instruments and hand-held-meters to quantify the amount of light and other associated lighting characteristics.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The course goal is for students to have a clear understanding of the terminologies used in lighting technologies and applications, to be able to measure and quantify light, able to calculate lighting quantities used in different applications, able to identify the required technologies and products for use in different lighting applications to achieve the desired illumination in a built environment.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand the basic principles of photometry and be able to take accurate and meaningful lighting measurements
- Understand the basic principles of lighting systems components, such as the source (inorganic and organic Light Emitting Diode(LED)), optics and light guides, electronic driver, and be able to apply them in the selection of luminaires and components for use in architectural lighting designs
- Be able to accurately calculate - light levels for an architectural lighting design or application of light. - direct and indirect luminous flux from light sources and reflector surfaces in different angular zones - light coupling efficiency from a source into a light guide
- In addition to having a good understanding of the Physics of light mentioned above, including, photometric quantities, optical phenomena when light encounters interfaces like reflective and refractive surfaces, and travels through a dispersive medium. Must have a good understanding of various technologies used in lighting and be able to select products to solve an illumination task or build a lighting system to solve a lighting task. The student should be able to synthesize the knowledge and be able to apply the it when conducting advanced lighting research.
