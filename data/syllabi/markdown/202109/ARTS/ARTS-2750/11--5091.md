**Studio Objectives:**

The goals of primary and secondary percussion studies in the RPI percussion studio are as follows:  
1\) Maximize the growth of students’ musicianship  
2\) Explore percussion techniques for application to any musical situation  
3\) Provide students with an opportunity to explore the variety of instruments that percussion offers

Specific semester-by-semester goals will vary from student to student. The skills we work on in lessons are meant to be applied to all the playing you do both in school and out and are geared toward a lifelong pursuit of improving your musicality.

**New for Fall 2020: All lesson will be held remotely, via Zoom. The same link will be used each week. If students have access to campus, they should use West Hall 321 so they have access to the percussion instruments there, and Dr. Francis will teach from his home studio.**

**Primary Lessons**: The final grade for this course is a balance between the studio grade (preparation for lessons), performances, if applicable, and jury grade.

**Secondary Lessons**: The final grade for this course will depend on the successful completion of goals established by Dr. Francis and the student over the course of the semester. Secondary percussion students are not required to play a jury at the end of the semester.

Occasionally, Dr. Francis will be travelling for clinics or performances on lesson days. In this event, lessons will be made up either on prior lesson days or subsequent lesson days. These double lesson days will be infrequent, and will be scheduled well in advance with the students affected.

**All percussion students are required to have the following:**

- \- A key for West Hall Room 321 – request this from the Arts Office on the first floor of West Hall. 321 is where we will meet for all lessons, and where you can practice as well.
- \- Pencils for every lesson and rehearsal
- \- a metronome or metronome app
- \- a drum key
- \- a practice pad (Vic Firth 12” or equivalent gum-rubber style pad)
- \- a supply of cymbal felts and sleeves (five of each)
- \- Stick and mallet requirements will vary depending on the repertoire being studied, but you must bring the necessary equipment to lessons.

The following list includes the basic materials we will be using during lessons, not including solo repertory or additional exercises. You should purchase these on your own, rather than using copies; building your library of repertory and teaching materials is essential for your own teaching and performing in the future. You need not purchase all of these right away – I’ll let you know what’s needed as we progress through the semester.

**Books:**

Stick Control – George Lawrence Stone

Instruction Course for Xylophone – George Hamilton Green

Intermediate Snare Drum Studies – Mitchell Peters

The All-American Drummer – 150 Rudimental Solos – Charley Wilcoxon

Vibraphone Technique: Dampening and Pedaling – David Friedman

Progressive Steps to Syncopation for the Modern Drummer – Ted Reed

Survival Guide for the Modern Drummer – Jim Riley

The Art of Bop Drumming – John Riley

**Suggested Sticks/Mallets (to be purchased according to lesson needs):**

2 pair Vic Firth drum set sticks (5A, AJ1, Erskine “Big Band” or comparable)

1 pair concert snare sticks (Vic Firth Tim Genis Generals, or comparable)

2 pair Vic Firth Van Sice (new model) M123’s, M124’s or similar medium-weight yarn marimba mallets (Vic Firth Giff Howarth M162, for instance)

1 pair Vic Firth M141 Xylophone Mallets

1 pair Vic Firth M142 Glockenspiel Mallets

2 pair Vic Firth M31 Vibraphone Mallets

1 pair Vic Firth HB Heritage Brushes

Vic Firth GEN 5, 6, or 7 (Tonal, Hard Tonal, Articulate) Timpani Mallets or similar medium and articulate timpani mallets

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
