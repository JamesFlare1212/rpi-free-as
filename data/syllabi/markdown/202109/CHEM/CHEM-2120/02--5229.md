## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Students will be able to search the chemical literature for necessary information 2. Students will be able to recognize the components of and be able to write a complete laboratory report and incorporate reference citations (where appropriate) into their written documents 3. Students will be able to use fundamental chemistry laboratory instrumentation, including analytical balances and pH meters, and analytical glassware, including volumetric flasks and pipettes. 4. Students will gain an understanding of and be able to effectively perform experimental techniques, such as titrations, the method of standard addition and the method of internal standards. 5. Students will gain a basic understanding of the use, function, and applications of experimental instrumentation such as Fourier Transform Infrared Spectrometers (FTIR), Gas Chromatographs (GC), and Scanning UV-Vis Spectrometers.

### Other Course Policies:

**Laboratory Safety**

Safe laboratory practices are an important part of any laboratory experience. These rules are in place for the safety of everyone in the lab and will be strictly enforced. Below are some rules regarding appropriate lab attire. A complete list of safety guidelines are posted online.

1. Goggles: Goggles can be purchased from the bookstore or Walker stockroom. You must wear full eye goggles – safety glasses are not permitted. Goggles must be worn at all times while you are in the lab. There are no exceptions to the “Goggles on at all times” rule.

2. Shoes: You must wear closed toe shoes in the lab. No sandal or open toed shoes allowed. You will not be allowed to enter the lab under any circumstance with improper footwear.

3. Hair: If you have long hair, please make sure that you tie it back while you are in the lab.

4. Clothing: Protective clothing offering coverage from foot to neck including arms is required. You must wear a long-sleeved shirt and pants or slacks that cover your legs while in the lab – no shorts or short skirts. You must wear a lab coat while in the laboratory. Make sure to purchase a lab coat that fits properly -- loose fitting clothing that can get in your way or knock over reaction vessels is dangerous in the lab.

If any of the laboratory personnel must ask you more than once during a laboratory period to put on your goggles or adhere to any of the safety procedures, you will be asked to leave the lab for the day. You will have no opportunity to make up the lab time lost due to your failure to follow the safety rules.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

The Rensselaer Handbook defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and the procedures for handling such acts. I ask that each of you familiarize yourself with these policies.

In this class, some examples of academic dishonesty are: 1) Copying from another student’s lab report,

2\) Using another student’s data as your own, and 3) Doctoring your data to fit what you believe is the “right” answer.

The penalty in this class for academic dishonesty is: First offense: a zero on the lab report and a drop in the course letter grade one level (an “A” would become a “B”) Second offense: a failing grade in the class, with no opportunity to drop the course. If you have any questions about what constitutes plagiarism or any form of academic dishonesty, please consult the Student Handbook located on the Dean of Students web site https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs/#StudentRightsandResponsibilities
