## Course Goals

### Goals:

**CHEM1100 (Chemistry I):**  
Principles of chemistry, with particular focus on atomic and molecular structure and bonding, periodicity, basic thermodynamic principles, introduction to acid-base chemistry and elementary chemical equilibrium, and introduction to organic chemistry. Students cannot get credit for both this course and CHEM 1110.  
**CHEM1110 (Chemistry I with Advanced Labs):**  
Covers the same lecture material as CHEM-1100, but laboratory experiments will be more technique-oriented to provide better preparation for students who plan to take future laboratory courses in chemistry. Students cannot get credit for both this course and CHEM-1100.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

\- Quantum Mechanical Model of an Atom  
\- Periodicity and Trends of the Periodic Table; Ionic Bonding  
\- Chemical Bonding I: Lewis Model and VSEPR Theory  
\- Chemical Bonding II: Valence Bond Theory, Molecular Orbital Theory, and Band Theory  
\- Organic Chemistry with Polymers and Biomolecules  
\- Aqueous Solution Reactions  
\- Gas Laws  
\- Chemical Equilibrium and Acid/Base Equilibria  
\- Thermochemistry

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate a proficient knowledge in the core areas of chemistry (i.e., analytical, inorganic, organic, and physical) in order to prepare themselves for advanced courses in the various subject areas of science and engineering.
- Solve quantitative problems in chemistry and chemical systems using fundamental concepts of chemistry and mathematics.
- Effectively communicate ideas and inquiries related to topics in chemistry to professional chemists in academia and in the industry.
- Demonstrate safe and proper chemistry laboratory techniques relating to mass measurements, volume measurements, solution preparation, dilution, titration, elementary separation, and synthesis methods.
- Utilize resources to identify various properties of chemicals and use proper precaution in handling these chemicals during experimentation.
- Collect, analyze experimental data, and determine experimental errors using various scientific methods.
- Identify and use scientific literature and other reputable sources as references for experimentation and research.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Class Participation:**  
Class participation is an essential part of this course and is measured primarily by attendance. The participation portion of your grade will be assessed by your class attendance, participation in discussions and laboratories, participation in class room i>clicker questions, and mentoring session attendance. Classes are conducted in-person. They will also be recorded for your review at a later time. The links to the recorded lectures will be posted on the LMS. You must attend and participate during lecture in order to receive credit for participation.  
**Pre-Lecture and Homework Assignments:**  
There will be pre-lecture and homework assignments associated with each chapter is covered in lecture. The assignments will be completed on Achieve (an online homework system) electronically by the posted due dates. Late homework or homework submitted on paper will not be accepted.  
**Lectures: (Attendance, in-person or online, is Mandatory)** There is an 80-minute lecture delivered by your course professor on Tuesdays and Fridays. The material presented in the lecture will cover and expand on the material in the required reading assignments. The pace of the lectures will be very fast. Please must attend lectures “in-person” by answering clicker questions in order to receive credits for participation.  
**Discussion/Recitation Participation:**  
During these sessions, your discussion faculty member will go through several problems related to the current lecture topics. At the beginning of each discussion session, you will need to participate in discussion clicker questions in order to receive credits for discussion. Make sure to logon to i>clicker REEF with your laptop or a smart device to answer these questions. Discussions will last approximately one hour and will be held “lin-person” during the first hour of each lab period. They will be recorded so you can review them later. A short quiz will be given during each discussion session. If you missed the questions, you must contact the discussion faculty member to schedule a makeup.  
**Laboratory:**  
Chemistry is an experimental science. Due to the current circumstances where we are not able to meet in person, all labs will be conducted in a hybrid mode (online and in-person) using a simulated platform as prelabs and a recorded lab performed by a TA. Each lab will be performed either in-person and lab partners will share their data. These lab simulations will focus primarily on applying your lecture gained knowledge to a hands-on but virtual laboratory experience. All prelabs and post labs MUST be submitted via the Hayden McNeil online course. After completing the experiment, you will analyze the data, perform any necessary calculations, and answer all post-lab questions. The experimental data you record for the experiment is the data that you will be using for your laboratory report. You will need a lab notebook, one able to make carbon copies, to record the data and write down any observations, and perform your calculations. All excused laboratory absences must be cleared and rescheduled through Dr. Morrissey (NOT your lab instructor).  
Three-Strike Policy for UNEXCUSED Absences:  
1\. One absence with no makeup will result in a grade of “zero” for the corresponding lab session.  
2\. Two absences with no makeup will result in a grade of “zero” for the entire lab portion of the final grade (20%).  
3\. Three absences with no makeup will result in an “F” for the entire course.  
You are expected to collaborate with your lab partner(s) to perform the experiment and collect the data. However, each lab partner is expected to analyze the results and work on the post lab questions independently. Make sure your post lab assignments are in your own words. Anyone who is caught copying from someone else’s work or work from past years will be subjected to academic disciplinary actions as follows:  
Three-Strike Policy for PLAGARISM:  
1\. The first infraction will result in a grade of “zero” for the corresponding prelab and/or post lab with a verbal and a written warning.  
2\. The second infraction will result in a grade of “zero” for the entire lab portion of the final grade (20%). A second written warning will also be issued.  
3\. The third infraction will result in an “F” for the entire course.  
If you missed or need to miss an experiment, please contact Dr. Morrissey to reschedule either to another lab section and/or a separate time. All experiment must be made up even ones with legitimate excuses.

There will be three 60-minute exams given on selected Wednesdays. The syllabus provides an outline of the materials that will be covered for each exam. Exams may include material covered in lecture, discussion, reading, homework, or laboratory. No makeup exams will be given. (e.g., Using someone else’s REEF code is considered cheating.)

Cheating can include sharing answers, as well as stealing answers and looking up answers online during exams. Plagiarism means copying words from someone’s work, even if you “change the sentence a bit.” If you share your laboratory report, you are as guilty as the person copying it. The Rensselaer Student Handbook and the Dean of Students Office Webpage (http://doso.rpi.edu/update.do?artcenterkey=676) defines various forms of academic dishonesty. Please familiarize yourself with these and ask us if you have questions about what constitutes academic dishonesty.
