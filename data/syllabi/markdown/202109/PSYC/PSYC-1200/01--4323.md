## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This asynchronous, online course provides an introduction to the field of psychological science. Topics covered include: a brief history of the field; psychological research methods; biology of behavior; consciousness; human development (through the life span); sex, gender, and sexuality; sensation and perception; learning; memory; thinking, language and intelligence; motivation and emotion; stress, health and human flourishing; social psychology; personality; and psychological disorders and therapy. Students will demonstrate their mastery of the course material via four timed, online exams administered via Rensselaer’s LMS**.** Additional required online activities are included to facilitate students’ understanding of the course material. Students will also have an opportunity to gain first-hand experience with psychological research, and earn extra credit, by participating in one or more IRB-approved research projects being conducted by researchers in Rensselaer’s Cognitive Science Department.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will demonstrate their knowledge of basic psychological concepts, principles, and procedures.
- Students will demonstrate their understanding of the strengths, limitations and appropriate applications of commonly employed behavioral/social science research designs and methodologies.
- Students can gain first-hand experience with psychological research by serving as participants in one or more IRB-approved research projects being conducted by the faculty and graduate students affiliated with Rensselaer’s Cognitive Science Department.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Learning outcome S5824 and S5825 will be assessed four online multiple-choice exams and by completion of homework assignments (i.e., Video Activities and LearningCurve activities) for each of the 46 separate modules comprising the course content.

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
