## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is the second in a two-semester sequence on research methods and statistics in the behavioral sciences. It covers approaches to statistical modeling and inference that lie within the null hypothesis significance testing (NHST) framework as well as alternative frameworks. Students will learn how to analyze various kinds of data using linear regression and analysis of variance and gain proficiency with the R programming language and software environment for statistical computing. Each student will carry out a research project and prepare a formal report that comports with the guidelines from the American Psychological Association.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students who complete this course will be able to design and conduct experiments to test hypotheses concerning behavior and mental processes.
- Students who complete this course will be able to select an appropriate statistical method and properly apply statistical methods to evaluate data using the R programming language and software environment.
- Students who complete this course will be able to communicate the results of statistical analyses in APA format.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

In this class, you will be given a number of assignments. You may consult with other class members about general statistical issues and procedures, use of statistical software, etc., but you are expected to work individually on all assignments unless directed otherwise by the instructor. All analyses and write-ups should be your independent work.

Problems will be sufficiently open-ended that identical solutions or write-up language (especially if the results are incorrect!) will be viewed as possible plagiarism. To be formal about it, plagiarism means passing off the statistical designs, software commands, or written results of others as one's own work. Any student who engages in plagiarism or any other form of academic dishonesty will receive zero credit for the assignment or exam, and will be reported to the Dean of Students for further disciplinary action. The Rensselaer Handbook defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All of these forms are violations of trust between students and teachers. Please familiarize yourself with this portion of the handbook. If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

**COURSE FORMAT**

The course format will be a combination of lecture and in-class assignment. For some topics, lectures will be short and will only serve to introduce a topic, with the rest of the class period being used to start the assignment. For other topics, I will lecture for most of the class period. After class, you may wish to read the corresponding chapter of the textbook. A short quiz assessing your understanding of the material covered in the previous class will be given at the beginning of the next class period. There will also be some time set aside for working on the research project.

**EXAMS AND QUIZZES**

There are no major exams in this course. Instead, quizzes will be given via LMS at the very beginning of about half of the classes. These quizzes will assess your understanding of the material covered in the previous lecture. Therefore, it is important that you arrive on time and attend class, pay attention, and take good notes. You may refer to your notes and the textbook (if you decide to purchase it) during quizzes but due to the time limit, you will not have much time to search for answers. If you do not complete a quiz before the window closes, you will receive a zero for that quiz. No exceptions. Quizzes may not be rescheduled but your two lowest quiz grades will be dropped.

**ASSIGNMENTS**

Learning how to analyze and interpret data requires extensive practice working with a wide variety of data sets. As such, you will need to spend significant time both in class and outside of class practicing the statistical techniques that you read about and using R to explore, analyze, and interpret data. In general, students who come to class prepared to work should be able to complete a significant portion of each assignment before the end of class. Due dates for each assignment will be given at the time that the assignment is made available. Extensions beyond the due date will be given only under exceptional circumstances and with prior permission. Assignments that are submitted after the due date will incur a 10% penalty per day.

**RESEARCH PROJECT**

Students will work in small groups of 3-4 students to complete a research project on some topic of their interest related to psychological science. Some class time will be set aside for students to work on their projects, but most of the work will be done outside of class. Each group will submit an idea paper, a proposal, and a pre-registration plan, and will give a presentation on their project at the end of the semester. More details about the research project, including a project milestones and timeline, will be provided at a later point in time.
