## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Daydreams about some tropical paradise... The smell of freshly baked bread... The flash of anger experienced when one person cuts another off... The vision of an albino squirrel on the campus green... Humans take all of these to involve activities or states of consciousness. But what is this consciousness with which they claim to be so intimately familiar? What are its metaphysical implications, and can people reconcile those implications with a scientific understanding of the world? We will begin with more general metaphysical issues and then move into topics more directly focused on philosophy of mind and consciousness. This is an upper-level communication intensive course.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Students will be able to explain Metaphysics as a subfield of Philosophy, including being able to identify and articulate several of the major issues within Metaphysics.
- 2\. Students will be able to identify and discuss major varieties of Dualism, including interactive substance dualism, emergent entity dualism, property dualism, and epiphenomenalism, as well as being able to explain and discuss Idealism.
- 3\. Students will be able to identify and discuss the major varieties of Physicalism, including Type-Type Identity Theory, Token-Token Identity Theory, Functionalism, and Eliminativism.
- 4\. Students will be able to discuss the main challenges to Physicalism, such as the qualia argument and the knowledge argument.
- 5\. Students will be able to explain the difference between the so-called "easy problems of consciousness" and the "hard problem of consciousness."
- 6\. Students will gain experience writing philosophical prose.

## Course Calendar

### Calendar Details:

A detailed course calendar was provided to you in a separate document, but the following is a condensed version:  
Week of 8/31: Intro to course; basics of arguments; survey of the nature, scope, and history of metaphysics.  
Week of 9/7: The continued relevance of metaphysics; relationship of metaphysics and science.  
Week of 9/14: Relationship of metaphysics and science, continued; Matter, energy, and reality: an historical overview; the concept of matter in the history of philosophy and physics.  
Week of 9/21: Vagueness of terms "physical" and "body" as used by philosophers and physicists; reconceptualizing reality in terms of mathematics?  
Week of 9/28: reconceptualizing reality in terms of information?; Thinking about the history of the universe in terms of information processing revolutions.  
Week of 10/5: Metaphysical status of unobservable entities postulated by science; Spectrum of metaphysical positions on mind-body relationship; PAPER 1 due on 10/8.  
Week of 10/12: Descartes' *Meditations* and mind-body interactive dualism; Review for Midterm Exam.  
Week of 10/19: MIDTERM EXAM; Recent version of interactive dualism that utilizes principles of quantum physics.  
Week of 10/26: Emergent entity dualism; Contemporary version of Idealism.  
Week of 11/2: Contemporary neuroscience, physicalism, and the importance of emotions in real life decision-making; Type-Identity Theory and reductive physicalism.  
Week of 11/9: Watch and discuss NOVA "Secrets of the Mind" video; How useful are neuroimaging and other brain study techniques for answering philosophical questions?  
Week of 11/16: Functionalism and Token-Identity Physicalism; Functionalism, computers, and A.I.; PAPER 2 due on 11/19.  
Week of 11/23: Personal identity; Concept of the "extended mind;" Thanksgiving Break.  
Week of 11/30: Qualia and Nagel's "What is it like" argument; Chalmers' distinction between "easy problems" and "the hard problem of consciousness."  
Week of 12/7: Review for Final Exam.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
