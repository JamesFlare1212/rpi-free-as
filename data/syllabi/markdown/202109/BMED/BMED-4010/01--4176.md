## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a hands-on laboratory course for Biomedical Engineering juniors and seniors. It gives the student practical experience with a wide variety of different experimental aspects within the field. Students should have previously taken a course in human physiology, an engineering laboratory course, and a course in the modeling of dynamic systems. This course provides part of the background for the Biomedical Engineering Design course, which follows it in the curriculum for undergraduate students. Although some students are attending the course remotely, BME lab team will work hard to ensure consistent course experience among both platforms with course standards and student outcomes being withheld.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The course meets on Tuesdays and Fridays for one-hour interactive lectures, and on Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday for a three-hour lab period. Initial lectures will be used to introduce the laboratory experiments. There will be one exam following the introductory material. Later lectures will introduce concepts of practical experimental design.

At the beginning of the semester, teams will be formed with 2-3 students to conduct experiments in the laboratory setting. The first two laboratory experiments will be basic biomedical engineering based experiments common to all students namely common lab 1 and 2. The next two experiments will be introductory to one of the tracks: Biomaterials (Track 1) or Biomechanics (Track 2) allocated to the team at the beginning of the term. The fifth laboratory experiment, specific to a track allocated to each team, will build on the previous experiments allowing the students to accomplish a more comprehensive experiment with an expectation of written technical manuscript as a report. The course will then move to two weeks of experimental design conducted remotely. The students must recreate, remotely, two experiments showcased so far: one from the common labs and one from their track specific lab with the aid of a care package provided by BME lab team. The semester will conclude with a review paper written by each team in the area of their track.

Prior to the lab period, each student must individually prepare for each laboratory by reading the Laboratory Procedure and supplemental notes. Each Laboratory Procedure will be provided to the students via RPILMS. A 5-minute quiz will be administered at the start of the lab through LMS that will cover materials contained in the Laboratory Procedure and Supplementary Information. Students reporting to lab more than 15 minutes late will be counted as absent.

Students will have one week each to complete reports for one week labs and two weeks to complete a more comprehensive 2-week lab. Students will have two weeks to design, conduct, and complete two labs related to common lab and within the sequence.

Laboratory experiments will be conducted as groups both in lab and via online resources and laboratory turn-ins/reports will be completed and submitted as a group. Every student in a lab group must participate in the laboratory procedure and contribute to report preparation. Two times during the semester, students will have the opportunity to evaluate the performance of their fellow teammates. The instructor will provide additional performance evaluation based on individual participation.

If a student misses a lab for a legitimate reason, such as documented illness [documented by the Dean of Students], the student may make up that experiment at a later time. The student will be responsible for conducting the experiment and preparing a laboratory turn-in/report independently. No more than one laboratory exercise may be made up by a single student. If a student misses a laboratory exercise without a legitimate reason, the laboratory exercise may not be made up.

A hard-copy submission for all prelabs, the common lab reports, and one-week track lab reports, and electronic submissions of the two week lab report, DOE reports, and the final report are due at the *start* of the lab meeting times for each section. For example, labs performed on Wednesday afternoon are due at the start of the Wednesday afternoon lab session. Microsoft Word versions should be emailed to the instructor **at the start of the lab** to: **bmed.4010@gmail.com.**

**Items handed in late will be given zero credit.** Verify if your email has been sent to ensure submission.

In the case of requesting regrades, the student(s) have a one-week period from the time an item is handed back to request a regrade. Attached to the original item, the student(s) must attach a sheet with which item they would like regraded, and why they feel additional credit is due. Note that the entire assignment will be re-checked: where *we* owe *you* points we will give them, but if *you* owe *us* points, we will take them back.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students who successfully complete the course will be able to demonstrate the operating principles and describe the characteristics of a variety of diagnostic and therapeutic instruments. Students will be able to identify, and subsequently avoid (or minimize) causes for inaccuracy in these instruments. Students will be able to properly display, analyze, and communicate experimental methods, results, and measurement errors through brief, thorough, and well-organized laboratory reports. Students will be able to recognize and avoid conditions that may injure or damage an instrument, the system to which the instrument is applied, or the operator. Students will be able to comprehensively document their activities in a legible manner. Students will be able to identify and utilize resources from outside the laboratory where they can obtain further information regarding laboratory safety, regulations, and standard operating procedures. Identify resources in your own vicinity and use experimental procedures to derive/analyze scientific data from it
