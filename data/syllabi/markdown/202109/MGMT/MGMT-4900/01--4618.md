## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a cross listed course for grad and undergrad students and is a project-based course. MGMT6840 students will work directly in a consultant role for a company and for others enrolled they can work on their own startup idea as an alternative to working as a consultant. We will begin the semester with lectures to teach some basic business skills that you will apply to your team project throughout the semester. Project proposals with stated milestones and deliverables with be developed in the first two weeks of class and must be approved by the instructor. Projects from interested clients will be discussed in class on the first day and teams will be formed quickly to begin the work. The written proposal describing the milestones and deliverables is key and will be used throughout the class. Understand that plans change and this is a living document. This is very typical of the type of project plan you will be exposed to in industry.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Effectively diagnose and solve organization problems and obstacles
- Develop effective strategic options and plans for a sponsoring business
- Learn how to effectively work with a diverse team
- Learn how to develop a project plan and to manage the work flow
- Communicate effectively both written and verbal

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students will be asked to work individually and in teams. They will be asked to work individually to hit certain milestones given in the project description. Project teams will be formed early in the semester based on the submission of project plans. This grading is outlined in the next section but is a combination of participation, report writing, presentations, and project management.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
