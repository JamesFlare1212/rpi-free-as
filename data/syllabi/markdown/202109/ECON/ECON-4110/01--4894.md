## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Technological innovation is responsible for most of economic growth, and is crucial to deal with major societal concerns. This course examines the economics of innovation and new technologies. It shows how technologies are created and put to use, from basic science through invention and development to diffusion and practical use. Additional topics include R&D determinants, sources of innovation, product improvement, cost reduction, incentivizing and stimulating innovation, patents, evolution of innovation and technology, innovative competition in industry, and the national innovation environment.

**Writing for ECON-4110:**  
Writing is required at least weekly. The writing is a way of using the content from the course and reflecting on your ideas about it. Writing may be prose or poetry, science fiction or science. It may be highly mathematical or not at all. You might write in blog form, or as a summary of the scientific literature and your reactions to it. Choose a form (or multiple forms in a collection), and choose a clear target audience such as policy makers, students, economists, or the general public. The form and target audience may change for different parts of the course. The writing must satisfy these requirements:

- It must reflect the content of each part of the course. (Two class days’ content may be excluded to allow for necessary absences.)
- It must include some of your own ideas or reflections about these topics, including some ideas on policies.
- Sources must be cited, with a proper list of references. Cite in the normal style you see in economics papers, or in some other way such as footnotes or endnotes if it would better fit a writing style such as poetry or fiction. Include specific page numbers if the content you use comes from a particular part of a book or paper.
- Text quoted from a source must be put in quotation marks (or clearly indicated as quoted text by using an indented block), with a citation with specific page numbers. Use ellipses … for omitted text, and square brackets [] for your own clarifications inside quotations.
- If you copy a table or figure, cite where *you* obtained the table or figure, with specific page numbers. (Students often want to cite where the table or figure originated, and it is fine to indicate the original source, but you must cite where *you* got it.)
- For reference lists, follow the style of any good economics journal. You are encouraged to use bibliographic software such as Zotero, Mendeley, or EndNote, though it will take an hour or more to get used to the software.
- Keep *all of your writings in one file*. The one file contains all writings so far in the whole semester.
- On a cover page, include your name. At the beginning of each writing, include a title and briefly indicate your choice of writing form and target audience. You may have a separate title and form for each class day, or you may combine all the class days between writing due dates as long as it is clear what topics you are combining this way.
- When writing, also go back and improve prior weeks’ work. Improvements may be in reaction to comments of the instructor or TA, other students’ thoughts, and your own insights and editing. Part of the writing grade is based on how well you revise your prior work.
- Use Track Changes in Word to document improvements to prior weeks’ work. Please turn *off* Word’s Track Changes feature just before writing a new entry, and please turn *on* Word’s Track Changes feature when making changes to an entry you wrote previously. That way, I can see what you changed.[1] If you cannot use Word for some reason, please discuss with me to find a workable alternative.
- Over the semester, your writing must total at least 4,000 words at absolute minimum. (This requirement comes from rules for communication-intensive courses at Rensselaer.) However, please don’t make your writing wordy just to fill space, nor leave in content that deserves to be cut! Other than this, there is no specific length requirement.
- During the semester, vary your genre, and perhaps your target audience. (E.g., federal policy report, blog to the general public, advice to a company’s top management, fiction, poetry, cartoon guide for beginners, etc.) Use at least two or three different genres.
- Indicate the target audience for each writing. You may simply indicate a default target audience on the cover page, and then indicate for specific assignments when you change the target audience.

Submit and revise your writing weekly. Upload the Word file weekly to our LMS website, by each Friday of term, excepting 11/27 (during the Thanksgiving break).  
The Comm+D Center at Rensselaer provides feedback to improve your writing. It is a requirement to:

- Submit, at least once by Sept. 22, some of your weekly writing to Comm+D to receive feedback on your writing. Use the Comm+D feedback to make improvements to your previous and future writing. At the bottom of the cover page of your cumulative writing document, list the date(s) when you submitted writings to Comm+D for feedback.

Using Comm+D multiple times will generally improve your writing grade, both because I will check the dates and because the feedback will help your writing.  
For 11/15 you will choose a paper and present about it (see details in the list of readings). Your writing grade for that day may be changed to reflect your presentation.

**Written Reports for ECON-6110:**  
For graduate course credit, you must do a data analytic project report. The project report must be appropriate for your professional purposes (career goals) and your field. It should be focused on a particular topic or purpose related to our course, and must involve data analysis in a way that exercises the data analysis skills you have built up for your discipline. I don’t want you to spend a lot of time looking for data, so if you need data and don’t know what to analyze, ask and I’ll give you some data (probably patent data). You could also download the data used for a journal publication, if the journal has archived the data along with the publication. It is important to pick a topic and get started quickly near the start of term, reading ahead as needed to suit your topic.  
Please use the following due dates:

- 9/13 - tell me your choice of topic, and your thoughts on where to get data
- 9/30 - preliminary rough draft due, with some literature review and basic data analysis (perhaps just descriptive data analysis)
- 10/18 - improved rough draft due, more complete and with a start on further data analysis
- 11/1 - semi-complete rough draft due
- 11/15 - complete first draft due
- 12/2 - complete report revision due
- 12/10 - final project report due

Submit a document (Word or PDF) for each of these dates in LMS. If you need extra time, get your work in as soon as you can, let me know, and usually I can be flexible.  
If you want to do the ECON-4110 writing too, you may. I can credit this in your grade by—only if it improves your grade—counting it for 12 of the 36 percentage points attributed to your project report grade.
