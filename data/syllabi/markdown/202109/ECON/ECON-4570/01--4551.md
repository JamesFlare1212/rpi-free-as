## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is an introduction to econometric data analysis. The statistical methods covered enable analysis of relationships between variables in data, with special attention to the identification of true causal effects. Topics covered include linear and simple nonlinear regression models, internal and external validity, methods for panel data and binary dependent variables, instrumental variables methods, and use of experimental and quasi-experimental data. The course includes hands-on experience with data analysis using R, as well as write-up of empirical results.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

(Required) James H. Stock and Mark W. Watson, *Introduction to Econometrics*, 4th edition.

It is okay to buy an earlier edition to save money, but this does not include the publisher’s web site access, and if using any earlier edition then check exercise problems using the problem sets posted online.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The topics below correspond to chapters 1-13 in the text, with roughly one week per chapter.

1. Introduction (ch. 1)
2. Review of (or introduction to) probability (ch. 2)
3. Review of (or introduction to) statistics (ch. 3)
4. Linear regression with one regressor (ch. 4)
5. Linear regression with a single regressor: statistical inference (ch. 5)
6. Linear regression with multiple regressors (ch. 6)
7. Statistical inference in multiple regressions (ch. 7)
8. Nonlinear regression functions (ch. 8)
9. Assessing studies based on multiple regression (ch. 9)
10. Regression with panel data (ch. 10)
11. Regression with a binary dependent variable (ch. 11)
12. Instrumental variables regression (ch. 12)
13. Experiments and quasi-experiments (ch. 13)

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to show proficiency in interpreting and performing linear regression analysis.
- Students will be able to show proficiency in conducting hypothesis testing for simple and multiple regressions and interpreting the inference results.
- Students will be able to show proficiency in demonstrating understanding of regression analysis with linear probability model.
- Students will be able to show proficiency in applying instrumental variables models to estimate causal relationships.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

I will randomly check attendance for 6 times. First absence will be waived. After that, any absence will lose 20% of the credits for this category. If you have a legitimate reason to miss the class, please let me know ***in advance***.

### Other Course Policies:

- **Quizzes:** There will be 6 quizzes in total. All quizzes are closed-book and -notes. I will ***announce quizzes in advance***. Quiz will be graded on a 0-100 scale. The lowest score will be dropped when final grades are calculated.
- **Labs** about once per week give experience with state-of-the-art econometric software as you analyze actual data, as well as practice reading published literature. You need write up your lab work according to the format specified at the beginning of each lab section, and submit your work at the end of each lab section.
- **Problem sets** are required. (1) Please use the scanned questions for the problem sets. Make sure you are using the SAME version of text for the problem set!!! If you are not sure, please use the scanned questions uploaded in LMS. (2) Problem sets are due two classes after the relevant lecture. Submit your answers in LMS; please do NOT email any assignments. Problem sets (but not empirical exercises) may be handwritten. To receive full credit, they need to be reasonably neat. (3) The purpose of the problem sets is for you to learn and understand key mathematics used in econometrics. You are responsible for learning these skills well, and questions on the mathematics (often less involved given time constraints) will appear on the exams. (4) Problem sets will usually be checked for the percentage completed, although parts may be graded if deemed necessary to benefit student learning. Please check your answers against the solutions given out, and ask for help if needed. (5) You may work with other people when doing the problem sets. However, it is crucial also to exercise your skills on your own so that you can solve similar problems on exams and in future. You must write out your own set of answers to each assignment. ***Do not just find answers online and copy them – the TAs and I tend to notice this and then you get in big trouble (see Academic Integrity Policies below).*** Instead, just make your own response to each question as best you can, ask if you have questions, and remember problem sets are not graded harshly and are mainly a way to learn (which will help your quiz and exam scores).
- A **midterm exam** will take place on Thursday ***October 21*** during class. A **final exam** will take place during the usual final exam period (date TBA). The final will emphasize material after the mid-term but will cover some material predating the mid-term. Exam questions must be answered by yourself. All exams are open-book and -notes.
- **More about grades:** Problem sets may simply be checked for the percentage completed, or may be graded based on content as teaching assistant resources permit. Late assignments will have their grades multiplied by 0.8 if handed in no more than one week late, or by 0.5 if handed in up to two weeks late, but cannot be credited if more than two weeks late or if after 4:00 p.m. on the last day of classes in the term. If answers to assignments are provided, please check them to identify any problems you are having and learn how to deal with these problems.
