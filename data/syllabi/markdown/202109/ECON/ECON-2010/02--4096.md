## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

This class is an in-person class. All students are expected to attend class and are responsible for any assignments given in class or any material discussed in class. I will not provide one-on-one tutoring for students who miss classes without an official excuse. Any student seeking to be excused from class should obtain an excuse from the Office of Student Success. Opting out of the iclicker portion of your grade does not give you permission to miss class.

### Other Course Policies:

**NO CELL PHONES IN CLASS**  
No cell phones out in the classroom once class starts. I will not remind you to put your cell phone away at the beginning of class. It has a clock on it. You can see the time and you know when class starts. Put it away so that you cannot see it (as opposed to hiding it behind the chair in front of you or in your lap). I will lower your grade by one increment (e.g., from A to A- or from B+ to B) each time that I see you with a phone once class starts. If you feel you are unable to spend 100 minutes without looking at your telephone, you should not take this class.  
If you have an emergency situation where you need to be reachable by telephone, let me know before class starts and we will find a way to accommodate your situation.  
**NO LAPTOP COMPUTERS IN CLASS**  
Computers are useful for doing work, but experience shows that they are more of a distraction than an aid to learning in this course. If you have a tablet for note taking, you may use it if you are sitting in the first row of seats in the classroom so that I can see the screen. If you are using the tablet for anything other than note taking, you will not be allowed to use it in class.  
For note taking, I recommend printing out the course slides that are available on LMS and bringing them to class. They are not exactly the same as my lecture slides, but they are probably 85% identical. This will require you to take some notes during class (and therefore pay attention), but you will not have to race to write down every word on every slide.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
