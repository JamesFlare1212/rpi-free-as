## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course provides an opportunity for the development of theorem-proving skills in the field of mathematical analysis. Expansion of a knowledge base comes as a by-product of energy expended in theorem proving and subsequent exposition. Analysis topics included are: sets, functions, the real numbers, cardinality, induction, decimal representations of real numbers, Euclidean spaces, abstract vector spaces, and metric spaces. This is a communication-intensive course.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

*Analysis With and Introduction to Proof*, Fifth ed., by Steven R. Lay, Pearson, 2014.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

We will study sets, functions, cardinality, properties of real numbers, convergence of sequences, limits of functions and metric spaces.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to read mathematical proofs and verbally communicate how each sentence contributes to deducing the assertion.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

The course structure is a flipped class setting where lectures are published 2 days prior to class. Class time is devoted to completing worksheets consisting of definitions, short answer problems and proofs. Worksheets are uploaded to Gradescope and attendance and participation points are awarded for each lecture and required as part of the overall course grade.

### Other Course Policies:

Make-up exams will only be given to students with an [excused absence documented by the Student Success office](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/student-success/excused-absence). **Missing an exam should not be taken lightly.** Thus, students missing an exam due to an excused absence should notify the instructor to make arrangements for a make-up exam before the exam begins. If a student misses an exam due to an unexcused absence (e.g., did not realize due date of exam, etc.), then the student must inform the instructor within 24 hours of the exam. The student may elect to take a make-up exam at a time arranged by the instructor and a 30% penalty will be applied to the score. Excused absences where students either fail to notify the instructor or make arrangements to make up the missed exam in a timely manner may also receive a penalty.

No make-up quizzes will be given for any reason. To compensate for this, one quiz score for each student will be dropped at the end of the semester. Only students who miss a quiz due to an [excused absence documented by the Student Success office](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/student-success/excused-absence) may be excused from a quiz.

Changes to exam, quiz or homework grades will be made only if something has been overlooked. All appeals must be made within 1 week of the time of the exam, quiz or homework grade is made available on LMS. Please check LMS and comments on graded assignments in Gradescope in a timely manner.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

All work submitted on exams and quizzes must be the student’s own. A student may not change an answer on an exam or quiz before submitting it for an appeal. Additionally you will be asked to “sign” an honor code that indicates your work is your own and you have not given or received any help on exams and quizzes. The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

Learning LaTeX is part of the course. Students must use LaTeX in writing homework.

LaTeX is free to download. While the download and installation is time consuming, the advantage of downloading LaTeX is that you can edit files locally on your own machine without connection to the Internet and you can easily control your own source files. Most downloads come with a LaTeX editor, but LaTeX may be edited with any text editor. There is a choice of downloads with two major ones being TexLive and MikTex. Either will suffice for this class.

Alternatively, students may use an online LaTeX editor such as “Overleaf”. While this makes it a little easier to get started, it requires Internet connectivity and it controls your files. Note that the homework rules above explicitly forbid sharing LaTeX files in any fashion and thus prohibit using any of the sharing facilities of online programs such as “Overleaf”.
