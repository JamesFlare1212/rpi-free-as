## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is an in-depth study of current papers within the broad field of Circadian Biology. Students will read and critique primary papers, present current research articles, and lead critical discussions.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

none needed

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The goal of this course is to familiarize students with the current work in the field of Circadian Biology. Students will learn to analyze and present a primary research paper as well as critically evaluate the material within that paper.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

6000-level Students:

- \- Deliver a lecture, with an eye toward critical review, on a primary scientific research paper whose topic focuses on circadian research.
- \- Analyze primary research papers and defend their review to an audience of their peers.

4000-level Students:

- \- Analyze primary research papers and defend their analysis in a written review.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

6000-level students will be assessed by participation in class discussion and their presentation. A rubric for participation which will be given to the students on the first day of class. For their presentation, students will choose papers in consultation with their advisor. After each presentation students will meet with the instructor and receive feedback on their performance. Presentations will be graded by the instructor.

6000-level Student Grade Allotment:

Class Participation – 60%

Presentation – 40%

Total – 100%

Note: D and D+ grades are not available in BIOL/BCBP 6961

4000-level students will be assessed by participation in class discussion and on their written reviews of 2 of the chosen papers. For their reviews, students will read the chosen papers and write a page about the finding and conclusions of the work. After each review, students receive feedback on their assessment of the work. Reviews will be graded by the instructor.

4000-level Student Grade Allotment:

Class Participation – 60%

Paper Reviews – 40% (20% each)

Total – 100%

**Grading Criteria for Presentation/Paper reviews**

**A:** The presentation/paper reviews provided background on the scientific question that is raised by the paper. Figures are assessed (e.g. reviewing individual panels important to the findings and removing panels that are not central to the paper). The presenter/reviewer has an in-depth understanding of the motivation, methods, interpretation for each experiment, and how they relate to the over-arching scientific question.

**A-:** The presentation/paper reviews fell just short in one of the elements described above.

**B+:** There are significant flaws in one of the described areas, but others are achieved well.

**B:** There are multiple problems with the presentation/paper reviews.

**B-:** The presentation/paper reviews had major flaws in many of the above described categories.

**C:** The presentation/paper reviews had major flaws in all of the above described categories.

**C-:** The first paper review met standards but the second had the above described issues and needed grammar improvements.

**D+:** The paper reviews both had the above described issues and needed grammar improvements.

**D:** One of the paper reviews was not completed and the other fell short in the above described criterion.

**F:** The student did not present/review papers.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments which students turn in are their own. Acts, which violate this trust, undermine the educational process.**

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Students should familiarize themselves with this portion of the Rensselaer Handbook and Graduate Student Supplement and should note that the penalties for plagiarism and other forms of cheating can be quite harsh. Submission of any assignment that is in violation with these policies will result in a penalty that is deemed by the instructor to be appropriate to the infraction ranging from a zero on the assignment to a F in the course. The student will also be reported to the Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education as appropriate. Collaboration is encouraged in the preparation of the presentation and paper reviews when getting feedback from your peers but is not allowed when giving the presentation or writing the reviews.
