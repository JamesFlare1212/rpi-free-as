## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

A detailed survey of important topics in the neurosciences. Some of the topics to be covered in this class are: basic mechanisms of neural signaling (neurophysiology, synaptic transmission, and molecular signaling); understanding of sensation/movement and in particular how it relates to neuroanatomy; neurodevelopment and how the mature brain can change (plasticity); complex brain functions and neurological disease. May be used to fulfill the Culminating Experience requirement in Biology. BIOL 4100 is an undergraduate course taught together with the BIOL 6100 graduate section.  
From Neuron to Behavior is a lecture course and assignments are comprised of major exams and article critique writing assignments. Students enrolled in the 6100 graduate section have an additional paper and presentation assignment.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 4100/6100 - Demonstrate a proficiency in the basic electrophysiological and biochemical properties of brain cells by the conclusion of the course.
- 4100/6100 - Discuss the development of the brain by the conclusion of the course.
- 4100/6100 - Discuss how cells of the central nervous system communicate with other cells in the central nervous system and with the peripheral nervous system.
- 4100/6100 - Demonstrate a proficiency in how the brain interprets its surroundings through the senses (sight, hearing, taste, touch, and smell).
- 4100/6100 - Demonstrate a proficiency in the understanding of the role of the brain in higher, complex behaviors (i.e., cognition).

- 4100/6100 - Apply basic concepts in neurobiology to challenging questions in the field of neuroscience by the conclusion of the course.
- 6100 - Apply basic concepts in neurobiology and be able to analyze and interpret neuroscience data in research papers by the conclusion of the course.

CRITICAL REACTION PAPERS – The course will be roughly divided into 5 sections, one for each of the 5 major exams (see below), and students will be required to find and read a peer reviewed empirical journal article relevant to some aspect of the material covered in each of the 5 sections of the course. Students will then be required to write a critical reaction of the article they selected using MS-Word. Students may select an article relevant to any of the material that was covered in that particular section of the course.

A peer reviewed primary empirical journal article is a publication in a peer reviewed journal that represents the first reporting of results from one or more experiments. Such articles traditionally have an introduction, methods (including a clear description of the procedures utilized), results (normally involving statistical analyses and data presentations in tables and/or graphs), and a discussion or conclusion section. Students are encouraged to contact Dr. Flint with questions regarding the appropriateness of an article if they are at all in doubt.

Each Critical Reaction will be comprised of a 700-750 word analysis of the article, excluding any title or reference pages) and students must provide a reference citation of the article being critiqued at the end. Students should also be prepared to provide a copy of the article to Dr. Flint if requested. Since this assignment is restricted to a single research article, and because students must provide a reference citation, there will be no need to provide any in-text references/citations in the paper. However, students are prohibited from using direct quotes. Any information from the article should be paraphrased in the student’s own words.

Critical Reaction Papers must be submitted online in LMS/Blackboard by 11:59 pm the day before the scheduled exam. Since these assignments are meant to reinforce and expand each student’s knowledge of the covered material, these assignments will not be accepted after the due date.

Critical Reaction Papers will be worth 20 points each. These papers are NOT summaries of the articles that were read, but instead should be critiques of the articles. In composing each critical reaction, students might consider one or more of the following:

1. How does the information in this article enhance or expand your knowledge of a topic covered in the class?
2. Is the hypothesis for each experiment clearly stated and do you think that it is an important issue to study? Why or why not?
3. What are the strengths of the study and why do you consider them strengths?
4. What are the weaknesses of the study and why do you consider them weaknesses?
5. Do the authors under or over generalize the impact of their results? Please explain.
6. Do the authors use appropriate statistical analyses of their data and/or appropriately present their data in tables and/or graphs?
7. Are the author’s findings correctly interpreted? Please explain.
8. Are the techniques, methods, and/or procedures utilized in the experiment(s) appropriate for the purpose of the study.

Critical Reach Papers will be graded using the following rubric:

- Writing = 5 points
  - o 5 pts – Paper is well-written and organized in a meaningful manner with appropriate paragraphs. Paper is devoid of spelling, grammar, and punctuation errors.
  - o 4 pts – Paper is moderately well-written and/or moderately well-organized and/or has 2-4 spelling, grammar, and/or punctuation errors.
  - o 3 pts – Writing and/or organization is average and/or there are 5-7 spelling, grammar, and/or punctuation errors.
  - o 2 pts – Paper is poorly written and/or poorly organized and/or the paper has 8-10 spelling, grammar, and/or punctuation errors.
  - o 1 pt – Paper is poorly written and/or poorly organized and/or had more than 10 spelling, grammar, and/or punctuation errors.
  - o 0 pts – assignment was not submitted
- Content (paper clearly focuses on providing a critical reaction to the article and does not summarize the article beyond what is necessary to place the critical reaction into context) = 15 points
  - o 14-15 pts – Paper provides an excellent critique, examining the article thoroughly for at least 4 key issues. There is no unnecessary summary of material from the article.
  - o 12-13 pts – Paper provides a good critique, thoroughly examining the article for 3 key issues and/or there is one instance where there was unnecessary summary of material from the article.
  - o 8-11 pts – Paper provides an average critique, presenting shallow examination of 3 or more issues or a thorough examination of only 2 key issues and/or there are two instances where unnecessary summaries of material from the article were provided.
  - o 1-7 pts – Paper provides a below average or poor critique, presenting a shallow examination of fewer than 3 issues or a thorough examination of only 1 key issue and/or the paper contains significant unnecessary summaries of portions of the article.
  - o 0 pts – assignment was not submitted.

Note: Critiques that do not fall within the word limit for the assignment or that use quotations from the article will receive additional point deductions.
