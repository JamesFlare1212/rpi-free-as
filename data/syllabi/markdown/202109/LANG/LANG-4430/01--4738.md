## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a continuation of Chinese III, a course for standard modern Chinese language (Mandarin). Students learn additional Chinese characters and words, reach a total of about 800 characters, 1350 words, and complicated grammatical structures, e.g., expression of approximation, comparison of structural and aspect particles, etc. In sum, students learn more in all four aspects—listening, speaking, reading, and writing—presented in Chinese III.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

In the course of the semester, students are engaged in communicative tasks inside and outside of classroom. We will learn how to talk about work studies, internships, festivities, etiquettes, and social media communication. While continue to strengthen oral communication skills, more emphasis will be given to reading and writing than in lower level courses. Students will be exposed to innovative learning environments based on new technologies and language acquisition theories. Broad spectrum of Chinese culture will also be brought to students throughout the semester.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students who successfully complete this course will acquire the following competences and be able to perform tasks pertinent to Intermedia Low level of proficiency and world-readiness described by the American Council on the Teaching of Foreign Languages: 1. Interpersonal Communication: Students will be able to meet some limited practical writing needs (e.g., reply to an ad) and handle successfully a limited number of uncomplicated communi¬cative tasks by creating with the language in straightforward social situations. Example topics are: part-time job, internship, festivals, etiquettes, and social media. 2. Interpretive Communication: Students will be able to understand some information in basic personal and social contexts and understand some information from the simplest connected texts dealing with a limited number of personal and social needs. Be able to derive meaning from connected texts. 3. Presentational Communication: Students will be able to (1) present information on very familiar topics (e.g., job and internship, festivals, communication etiquette, and social media) using a variety of words, phrases, and sentences that have been practiced and memorized; (2) produce short passages in Chinese describing job, social life, festival, and travel. 4. Cultural Awareness: Students will be able to (1) recognize and uses some culturally appropriate vocabulary, expressions, and gestures when participating in everyday interactions; (2) recognize that differences exist in cultural behaviors and perspectives and can conform in familiar situations.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

*Participation & Performance*: Language learning involves using the vocabulary and grammar you learned to interact with others for all purposes of life in a target language society. **You are required to participate in various language learning tasks/activities in and outside classrooms. Some will require recording your voice and role-plays**. You receive full credit of participation for excused class absence and can schedule a make-up work for the absence. No make-ups for any unexcused absences and lateness.

*Homework*: Daily practice and homework are important integrals for successful Chinese learning. **For the best effect of proficiency-based language learning, assignments of this course are spread out over the week** so that you practice 20-30 minutes daily. Each new week’s homework is posted on the Monday of the previous week on LMS and there are *THREE (3) due times for the routine weekly homework*: (1) Tuesday before class; (2) Friday before class, and (3) Sunday Midnight.

It is **your responsibility** to check the weekly homework and due time carefully when posted on LMS. If homework is submitted late, 10% of the possible points will be deducted per 24-hour period after the due time. Log on to the LMS from this link with your RPI credentials: https://lms.rpi.edu/

*Unit Dictation*: Dictation is a form to test your listening and handwriting for the unit vocabulary and sentences. During the dictation, you write down what you hear. Unit Dictations usually take place after we finish each Unit (two chapters). You are not allowed to make up dictations for unexcused tardiness and/or absence.

*Quizlet*: Quizlet is an online flashcards website allowing you to study each chapter’s vocabulary with different modes of study. Much of the vocabulary learning and assessment will be through this Web 2.0 platform. An invite to join will be sent to you via RPI emails.

*Project*: There will be a couple of projects in this course. Check out a folder called “Projects & Instructions” under “Course Documents” on LMS Blackboard.

*Exam*: There will be two exams in this course. Each exam consists of two parts: written and oral. The two exams test your knowledge of vocabulary, grammar, cultural knowledge as well as your competence in listening, speaking, reading, and writing of the Chinese language designated by the course level.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

It is VERY EASY to spot cheating in a foreign language course. Students are not permitted to turn in work done by someone else. Getting assistance from a native speaker on assignments is OK, but your final product must be your own work as it is easy to identify the excessive structures beyond your level of proficiency. In addition, use of a WEB TRANSLATE (e.g., Google Translate) for translation other than vocabulary or phrase is not permitted.

The first time I suspect cheating I warn the student and tell why I suspect (e.g. use of non-course grammar or vocabulary) and I take off 50% points of the grade. If it's very obvious I do not give a warning. I will just give a zero. If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of F for the course or be reported to the Student's Office.
