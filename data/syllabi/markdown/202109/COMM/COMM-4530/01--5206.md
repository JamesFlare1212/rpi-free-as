## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Undergraduate.  
This course considers the sociopolitical and ethical dimensions of reality television and post-factual media content. An analysis of a variety of “reality” and news formats allows students to understand the evolution, economics, and, above all, the cultural and political significance of modern media. Topics include: simulation; social representation; commercialization; social media; surveillance; nationalism; and globalization.

Students in this course will study a popular media phenomenon through the analysis of video material and reading of selected critical essays. As assessed by class discussion and written papers, they will demonstrate their ability to read closely, analyze, and make critical arguments based on academic literature.

**Guideline for Assessing Essays**

In general, you will be evaluated according to how well you engage with class material and how well you articulate your ideas in well-written papers. Such papers will demonstrate three key elements—**relevance, coherence,** and **writing proficiency**.

Papers should have:

- a clear thesis
- coherent organization and development
- focused paragraphs
- sentence-level competency (punctuation, grammar)

We will devote some time in class sessions to writing expectations and guidelines.

### Other Course Policies:

**Class Participation**

1. **Class Report** (5-10 minutes). Each student will be asked to give one brief report highlighting a major point or points in the assigned reading. This report should be based on prepared notes (*not handed in*) and include a brief summary of the article and a topic to discuss further.

2. **Highlighted notes**: to prepare for each class, everyone will be asked to write a **few sentences** about the **assigned reading** (identifying its main or most striking idea). This is *not handed in* but you may be called upon to share these ideas during class discussion.

3. **Class debates:** periodically, there will be a short debate that will require no prep outside class.

4. **Bonus**: There will also be a bonus **elimination quiz** **or quizzes** based on recent readings and lectures. The winner will have 1% added to their final course grade (no repeat winners).

The remainder of the participation grade will be based on your general contribution to class discussion. To give you some sense of how you are doing, you will receive an estimate of your participation grade halfway through the course. Remember that quality counts for more than quantity in discussion contributions.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**If you plagiarize once,** you will receive an **F on the assignment.** If you plagiarize again, you will **fail the entire course** and may be reported to the *Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education* as appropriate. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask me for clarification. The basics: when using information from another source **(including anonymous online sources**) always cite the original text or URL and distinguish it from your own commentary. If you directly cut and paste another person’s language, you need to use it as a **quotation**.
