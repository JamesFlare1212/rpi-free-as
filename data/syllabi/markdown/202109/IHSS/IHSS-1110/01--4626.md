## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

What will we cover in this class? : Course Description

Nature/Society is the core seminar for the Vasudha (Sustainability) Living & Learning Community. In this course we will explore how major environmental and climate challenges can be addressed, focusing on re-thinking human/environment relationships. What are the different relationships between “nature” and “society”, how do they materialize, and how can we rethink human/environment relationships in generative ways?Throughout the semester we will consider these questions and use them as a foundation to build new questions. We will explore these relationships “in the field” and meet people who take deliberate and real-world steps to bring about meaningful changes and solutions to major problems across scales, from local communities to the global.

How will we do this?

The course has three segments: First (September) we will explore the explore different perspectives on relationships between humans and their environments — via guided tours, nature explorations and critical engagement with resources (readings, documentaries, podcasts, etc.). Place-based experiential learning will be central to our work this semester. Second (October), we will meet with change-makers to learn about how to realize projects, facilitate change, and engage with communities.

Throughout the semester, we will work on developing our own real world projects and/or campaigns. In the first two months we explore different approaches to a problem you identify as particularly interesting. In the third section of this course (November), you will take what you learned and develop your own concrete project ideas while further exploring critical concepts and approaches through literature and other resources.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

What will I learn in this class? : Learning Goals

After the successful completion of this course you will have the tools to:

- Identify, understand and analyze complex, dynamically unfolding global challenges and their impacts on and implications for concrete local communities.
- Develop and articulate a wide range of approaches, methods and tools to address global challenges on the local level and beyond.
- Understand the complex relationships informing concepts of nature and society, analyze the social relations intertwined with environmental sustainability issues, and ground your solution and change-making approaches in these concepts.

FAQ: Course Policies Guide

How am I expected to participate in class?

WITH RESPECT:

Classroom expectations are all about being respectful to everyone involved in the learning process. This includes your classmates, the professor, Teaching and Learning Assistants, any guest speakers, and yourselves. You are investing valuable time and financial resources to attend this class. If you participate in a meaningful and respectful manner, and apply what you learn in discussions, readings, and research, you will leave this course with widely applicable knowledge and skills.

In this class we are an intellectual community and we will respectfully engage in scholarly discussions. We will cover material in this course that may challenge your beliefs and opinions. You will be expected to participate in class discussions and engage with these topics, issues, and perspectives in a manner that is respectful, open, and intellectual.

You can find more details about respectful discussion participation in our class assignment guide. We will create respectful discussion guidelines together as a class.

WITH DEDICATION & ACTIVE ENGAGEMENT:

This course requires quite a bit of preparatory reading, research and in-class discussion. It is essential to stay up to date on research fieldnotes and complete the assigned readings prior to the class for which they are assigned in order to engage in meaningful discussions about the material. Learning in this space is a collective process in which we build knowledge as a group.

We are all in different situations now, in the midst of ongoing pandemic disaster conditions. With this in mind we will do everything we can to create a learning community that is focused on care and wellbeing, as well as cultivating intellectual curiosity and rigorous academic skills.

WITH COURTESY:

Please communicate with me about any needs that may arise throughout the semester regarding scheduling, technology, or any other challenges you experience. We are building a learning community that requires dedication and contributions from each member. The learning community is also flexible to accommodate challenging and uncertain circumstances, so just be sure to communicate your situation to me.

As a general rule for email communications, in this class and future professional scenarios- provide an informative subject header, include a greeting (feel free to address me as Lee, Professor Nelson, or Dr. Nelson), provide the context of your email (what class are you in), and don’t forget to include a sign-off (Sincerely, Best, Thanks, etc.)
