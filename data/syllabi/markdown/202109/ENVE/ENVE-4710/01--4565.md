## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course will prepare you to understand the role of groundwater in the hydrologic balance of lakes and rivers; systems for water supply, irrigation, drainage, dewatering, and remediation; assessing environmental impacts. Darcy’s Law is developed as a framework for understanding how soil properties influence transmission of water by aquifers. Darcy’s law is employed as the basis for describing flow in porous media, and direct application to flow, infiltration, flow nets, and aquifer pumping is illustrated. The incorporation of Darcy’s Law within other, more sophisticated groundwater flow models is developed, including non-steady radial flow to wells. Students will become proficient in predicting the effects of pumping on the piezometric surface and analysis of aquifer test data under ideal and non-ideal boundary conditions. The geological processes that lead to the formation of aquifers will be introduced.

No Course Learning Outcomes are available for this course.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students who successfully complete this course will be able to:

1. Quantify components of the hydrologic cycle to assess groundwater/surface water interactions, perform hydrologic balances, and estimate groundwater contributions to surface water bodies.
2. Identify and characterize aquifer properties, geologic boundaries (type of confinement), and mechanisms of water storage and release.
3. Prepare groundwater maps to identify features of the potentiometric surface and quantify the distribution of head and flow in cross-section.
4. Employ material balances to develop and calibrate groundwater flow models and use them to estimate flow quantities and predict potentiometric surface profiles, including aquifer response to pumping or recharge.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

***Academic Integrity***

Student-teacher relationships are **built on trust**. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own.

Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities (www.rpi.edu/dept/doso/handbook.html) defines various forms of academic dishonesty and procedures for responding to them.

Students should familiarize themselves with this portion of the Handbook and should note that the penalties for academic dishonesty can be quite severe. This now also applies to students who use platforms such as Chegg.com to hire someone to do their homework/quizzes.

In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work, although students are encouraged to work with each other by collaborating on assignments (see item 4 under Homework Policy above).

Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy (e.g., one student copying work done by another student or a student copying from a previously developed solution will result in a grade of zero for that particular assignment. Also, cheating on an exam, which includes using services such as Chegg.com, will result in a grade of zero for that exam and, in accordance with the Handbook, notification to the Dean of Students Office of the incident of academic dishonesty.
