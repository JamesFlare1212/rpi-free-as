## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Physical processes governing occurrence and distribution of precipitation, infiltration, evaporation, and surface water runoff. Hydrology, mechanics of flow, and well hydraulics. Statistical hydrology, unit hydrograph theory, and watershed modeling. Floodplain hydrology and open channel hydraulics. Urban hydrology, hydraulics, design of storm sewers, and design of detention structures for flood control. Design project using ACE HEC-HMS flood hydrograph package.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The objective of this course is to develop a working knowledge of the basic principles of hydraulics and hydrology. Students will do this by building on the knowledge gained through fluids (ENGR 2250). Upon successful completion of the course, students will have a basic understanding of open channel flow, precipitation and runoff, and flow and flood routing through natural and manmade catchments. Having successfully completed this course, students will have the necessary skills to take senior-level design courses (ENVE 4180/CIVL 4920).

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Model physical systems (channels, rivers, streams, catchments, watersheds) by employing principles of conservation of mass, energy, and momentum to develop equations describing their behavior,
- Solve these equations via concepts of specific energy to determine characteristics (flow, runoff) and compute a variety of flow and water surface elevation scenarios and observe these in laboratory experiments,
- Develop skills in computer modeling of these systems using commercial software (HEC-HMS)

Grade will be attributted using the following criteria:

\- Master (10 points): Demonstraded understanding of the topic, calculations are correct (within error and assumptions). In real life, if the problem was a project the answer would satisfy the requirements.

\- OK (6 points): Solution was not completely correct due to a small calculation or assumption mistake during the process. In real life, if the problem was a project, it would be easily fixable by another engineer visiting your calculations.

\- Failed Attempted (3 points): Solution lacked demonstration of understanding of the topic. In real life, if it was a project, it would be far from correct, requiring another engineer to redo the project.

\- Blank (0 points): Not attempted or inconclusive solution (e.g. scribbles of formuals taking nowhere)

The course will also have 4 laboratories (up to 10 points total) and a Reservoir Project (up to 10 points).

Each student will have custom-made quizzes containing not only questions about current examination topics, but also the opportunity to achieve mastery in topics not yet mastered by the student.

Each topic will have at least 2 retake opportunities. Be mindful that topics discussed in the beginning of the semester will have more opportunities to retake than others, as they can be retaken in all subsequent quizzes.

Important: if the student chooses to retake a topic, the grade for that topic will be replaced by the newer grade. e.g. if a topic was “Ok”, but your retake was a “Failed Attempt”, the grade for the topic will be a “Failed Attempt” because you did not show correct understanding about the topic. The only exception is if you leave your retake questions blank. In this case, your previous grade will be conserved as you may have chosen to use your time to work in other problems.

By mastering a topic, the load of topics to study through the semester will be easened and the you may even not need to sit for a Final Exam.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own.  
Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities (www.rpi.edu/dept/doso/handbook.html) defines various forms of academic dishonesty and procedures for responding to them.  
Students should familiarize themselves with this portion of the Handbook and should note that the penalties for academic dishonesty can be quite severe. This now also applies to students who use platforms such as Chegg.com to hire someone to do their homework/quizzes.  
In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work, although students are encouraged to work with each other by collaborating on assignments (see item 4 under Homework Policy above).  
Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy (e.g., one student copying work done by another student or a student copying from a previously developed solution will result in a grade of zero for that particular assignment. Also, cheating on an exam, which includes using services such as Chegg.com, will result in a grade of zero for that exam and, in accordance with the Handbook, notification to the Dean of Students Office of the incident of academic dishonesty.
