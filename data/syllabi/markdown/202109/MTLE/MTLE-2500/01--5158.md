## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Introduction to some experimental methods used to fabricate and measure the structure and properties of materials. Thermal and mechanical processing and properties are emphasized. Specimen preparation, examination by light optical microscopy, report writing, and measurement systems analysis are covered.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

\## Grading  
All assignments will be graded against the following rubric (4 total points per item):  
\* Narrative Exposition - Organization and Mechanics of Writing.  
\* The narrative is logically organized and where appropriate the narrative is used to support the technical aspects of the presentation.  
\* Prose and equations are clear, readable and makes good use of transitions between sentences, paragraphs and sections.  
\* Appropriate use of captions for figures and tables, numbering is appropriate, headings are used, citation style follows the ACS guidelines.  
\* Narrative Exposition - Relevance and Thoroughness  
\* The images, figures, and narrative text and equations completely address the problem set by the instructor  
\* Any additional assumptions that limit the scope of the problem are clearly stated rather than left for the reader to assume.  
\* Ideas are placed in the appropriate materials engineering, processing, or other necessary context.  
\* Application of Physics, Chemistry, Thermodynamics, and Materials Science to the Solution  
\* Written documentation and technical solution addresses all aspects of the problem  
\* Visualizations, figures, and derivations are clear and thoughtfully explained.  
\* Calculations are organized and the logic is presented in an easy to follow manner  
Your final grade will be determined by your performance on lab activities and the final lab report.  
\* Each listed module requires training and demonstration of one key lab activity. Instructors will train you and observe your technique. This training and technique will be documented in the lab notebook.  
\* The final lab report will be submitted at the end of class and be graded using the rubric above.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

It is likely that you will wish to or, in some cases, need to work with other members of the class to complete assignments. All submitted work must be your original work and must clearly indicate with whom you have collaborated. Failure to indicate with whom you have collaborated will result in a zero for the assignment and you will be reported for plagiarism to the undergraduate Dean.
