### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

# Course Assessment

Homework is assigned on a semi-weekly basis. Each individual is expected to submit her or his own work for individual grading, although collaboration between students is allowed. The homework problems are *identical to past year’s exam questions* and are the best way to prepare for the exams.

Team in-class problems contribute to the final grade. In your solutions, the method of calculation must be shown clearly. When physical properties are involved, all units and their cancellations must be shown clearly. If assignment contains creation of plots, then all plots must be generated on a computer using an appropriate computer program of the students’ choice. The class TA will be responsible for classwork collection and grading.

There will be one pseudo-lab assignment. There are no actual laboratory experiments, rather we will use the data provided from an experiment in Prof. Palermo’s lab to calculate certain quantities. The deliverables for each assignment will be defined in the assignment. All laboratory assignments must be typed and handed in for every individual student. No hand written assignments will be accepted. The class TA will be responsible for grading these assignments.

Examinations: There will be two 80-minute examinations given during regular lecture periods, and final exam at the end of the course.

- Exam 1: material from chapters 2, 7, 8, and 9.
- Exam 2: material from chapters 10, 11, and 12.
- Final Exam: all material covered.

A final examination covering all the material in the course will be given at the end of the course during final exam period. The graded lecture exams will be returned to students, while the instructor will keep the final exam. Students can review the grading of the final exam in the instructor’s office.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

# Academic Integrity

There is zero tolerance for breach of academic integrity such as cheating or plagiarizing. ***Anyone caught cheating or plagiarizing will receive an F in this course***. Cheating can include sharing answers, as well as stealing answers. Plagiarism means copying words from someone’s work, even if you “change” the sentence a bit. Students work in teams for the classwork problems and help each other in understanding particular difficulties. ***Examination work is strictly individual.***
