## Course Goals

### Goals:

The intent of this course is to introduce the principles and physical applications of classical thermodynamics and statistical mechanics so that students can develop an intuitive understanding on how macroscopic observations can be explained from microscopic effects. In addition, basic concepts in classical and quantum statistical mechanics will be introduced and their relations to thermodynamics will be developed. By the end of the course, students should get a new appreciation of thermodynamics concepts and the power of statistical mechanics in explaining those concepts.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Topics include:**  
Heat, Probability, Temperature, Boltzmann factor, Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution/Pressure/Collision, First Law, Heat engines, Entropy, Information theory, Thermodynamics potentials, Rods, bubbles, and magnets, third law, Equipartition theorem, Partition function, Ideal gas, Chemical Potential, Photons, Phonons, Relativistic gas, Real gas, Cooling real gases, Phase Transition, Bose-Einstein + Fermi-Dirac, Ising model; Metropolis algorithm, Quantum gas, Thermodynamics of Earth.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to provide definition and explanation of concepts such as: temperature, entropy, phase transitions, partition function, Gibbs energy, Helmholtz function.
- Students will learn all four laws of thermodynamics and explain their origin and applications
- Students will be able to calculate the partition functions of simple quantum mechanical systems (e.g., harmonic oscillator, hydrogen atom) and obtain thermodynamic potentials from them.
- Students will be able to explain basic statistical mechanics of quantum gas. Those concepts include Fermi-Dirac and Bose-Einstein distributions.
- Students will be able to apply their knowledge of thermodynamics to complex systems such as the Earth atmosphere and provide a critical description of the effect of global temperature increase.
- Students will demonstrate the elementary use of Monte Carlo computational techniques to study phase transitions or similar problem

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**HW:** There will be 10 HW assigned (including 2 computational-based ones).  
**Quizzes**: One quiz will be organized for each meeting.  
**Exams:** There will be three exams. The first two exams will consist in two parts: conceptual quiz and problem solving. There will be an additional exam at the end of the course during final week. That exam will be an oral exam (administered in-person) covering the entire material covered in the course. A list of questions will be provided ahead of time for both the oral exams.
