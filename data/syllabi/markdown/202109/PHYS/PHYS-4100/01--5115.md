## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is an undergraduate level course. I will introduce the fundamental concepts of quantum mechanics, including the state of a quantum mechanical system, operators, superposition principle, and measurement postu- late. The notion of an operator and the algebra of operators is introduced with the help of elementary concepts of mathematical analysis. Topics covered in this course include the general formalism of quantum mechanics, Symmetries and conservation laws, quantum mechanics in three-dimensions, angular momentum, spin, perturbation theory, and scattering; to name a few topics.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course will introduce the fundamental concepts of quantum mechanics, including the state of a quantum mechanical system, operators, superposition principle, and measurement postu- late. The notion of an operator and the algebra of operators is introduced with the help of elementary concepts of mathematical analysis. Topics covered in this course include the general formalism of quantum mechanics, Symmetries and conservation laws, quantum mechanics in three-dimensions, angular momentum, spin, perturbation theory, and scattering; to name a few topics.

The homework is an essential part of the course. You should attempt all the problems yourself, but you may argue with your colleagues about the basic ideas underlying the problems. Simply copying each other’s solutions or downloading solutions is, however, counterproductive for all parties, and contrary to the honor code. Your solutions should go beyond the usual “just writing

down” solution, in that special care should be given to the presentation and discussion of the logic of the solution. In science, the communication of arguments and results is nearly as im- portant as the results themselves, so this should be good practice. Homework solutions will be posted on the course LMS web site.

While some of the problems in each set will be new, many others will be from the textbook. Its solution manual is floating around, but it is required for you to work out your own solution both according to the honor code, and because (of course!) that is the only way you will learn the material for yourself.

There will be a problem set every week. The main purpose of the problem sets is to help learn quantum mechanics, not to grade performance. They are included in the final grade as a safety net in the event exams do not consistently reflect a student’s understanding.  
Sitting down by yourself and reasoning your way through a problem will help you learn the material deeply, identify concepts that are not clear, and develop the analytical skills needed for a successful career in science. If you can solve the problems by yourself, you can expect to do well on the exams.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Student will learn the fundamentals of solving quantum mechanical problems with the wave function approach. S342
- Student will be able to solve the 3D time-dependent and time-independent Schrödinger equation for simple potentials like for instance the harmonic oscillator and hydrogenlike atoms, as well as the interaction of an electron with the electromagnetic field
- Student will be familiar with spin, angular momentum states, angular momentum addition rules, and identical particles
- Students will learn approximate methods for solving the Schrödinger equation ( the variational method, perturbation theory, Born approximations)
- Student will be made aware of the modern application of quantum mechanics: entanglement application, laser cooling, atom trapping
- Student will be introduced to the EPR discussion and quantum spookiness

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Cumulative mastery of homework is intended to be the primary learning tool in preparation for exams. There will be 1 mid-term exam during the normal lecture time. There will be a three-hour final exam on the last of class.
