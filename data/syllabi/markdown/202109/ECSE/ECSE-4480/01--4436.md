## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

A survey of the fundamental issues necessary for the design, analysis, control, and implementation of robotic systems. The mathematical description of robot manipulators in terms of kinematics and dynamics. Hardware components of a typical robot arm. Path following, control, and sensing. Examples of several currently available manipulators.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students who complete this course satisfactorily will be able to: (1) De- scribe rigid body configuration and motion. (2) Describe intrinsic and extrinsic camera parameters and perform camera calibration (3) Characterize the kinematics of an articulate robot and plan its motion in the joint space or task space. (4) Evaluate the singularities of an articulated robot and understand its impact in motion planning and control. (5) Describe the kinematics of a wheeled mobile robot and explain the difference between holonomic and nonholonomic constraints.

For 6xxx sections, students will in addition be able to: (1) Analyze the kinematics and dynamics of constrained articulated robots and parallel robots. (2) Design compliance force controller for constrained robots.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

This course covers the basic tools and methods to analyze the kinematics, dynamics, and control for articulated robots. The focus will be on using coordinate-free approach to analyze open chain articulated manipulators. Other types of mechanisms will also be briefly covered, including constrained robots, parallel robots, and wheeled mobile robots. The emphasis of the course will be on developing a systematic, analytical understanding of the basic principles in the operation and analysis of robotic systems. 6xxx sections will cover dynamics and control for articulated chain, and kinematics and control for constrained robots and mobile robots, in greater depth.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Intellectual integrity and credibility are the foundation of all academic work. A violation of Academic Integrity policy is, by definition, considered a flagrant offense to the educational process. It is taken seriously by students, faculty, and Rensselaer and will be addressed in an effective manner. If found responsible for committing academic dishonesty, a student may be subject to one or both types of penalties: an academic (grade) penalty administered by the professor and/or disciplinary action through the Rensselaer judicial process described in the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities. Several relevant academic integrity violations to emphasize include:

• Unallowed Collaboration: Deliberately facilitating an act of academic dishonesty in any way or form is forbidden. Examples include, allowing another student to “borrow” one’s software code or report, or observe an exam paper, or using one another’s work in a report without citing it as another’s work.

• Plagiarism: Plagiarism is defined as representing the work or words of another as one’s own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference. Examples include using sentences verbatim from a published source in a project report without appropriate referencing, or presenting as one’s own the detailed argument of a published source, or presenting as one’s own electronically or digitally enhanced graphic representations from any form of media. The key here is to always cite your source material and be very clear what published material you are using.

• Copying: Copying is defined as obtaining information pertaining to an assigned work by observing the work of another student; for example, copying the derivation from another student and use in a report, copying the code from another student and use it for an assignment.
