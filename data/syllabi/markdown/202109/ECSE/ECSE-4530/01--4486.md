## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides a comprehensive treatment of the theory, design, and implementation of digital signal processing algorithms. This course emphasizes frequency-domain and Z-transform analysis. It also investigates advanced topics in signal processing, including FFT algorithms, multi-rate signal processing, filter design, and adaptive filtering. The course provides a strong theoretical foundation for future study in communications, control, or image processing.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

J.G. Proakis and D.G. Manolakis, Digital Signal Processing: Principles, Algorithms, and Applications, 4th Edition, Prentice-Hall, 2006. ISBN: 978-0131873742.

Supplemental Reference

A. Oppenheim and R. Schafer, Discrete-Time Signal Processing, 3rd edition, Prentice-Hall, 2009. ISBN: 978-0131988422. This is a classic DSP reference but doesn't cover some of the more advanced material.  
Hayes, Schaum's Outline of Digital Signal Processing, 2nd Edition, McGraw Hill, 2011. ISBN: 978-0071635097 . This outline contains hundreds of fully solved problems and is a good reference for learning a concept better and studying for exams.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The main objective of this course is to provide a comprehensive treatment of the theory, design, and implementation of digital signal processing algorithms. This course will mainly focus on frequency-domain and Z-transform analysis. This course will also discuss advanced topics in signal processing, including multirate signal processing, filter design, and adaptive filtering. The course is intended to be fairly application-independent, to provide a strong theoretical foundation for future study in communications, control, or image processing.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Analysis of discrete time, linear time invariant systems

Discrete time sampling theory and amplitude quantization

Design and implementation of digital filters

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- An understanding of the use and application of Z-transform and Discrete-Time Fourier Transform techniques for the analysis of discrete time, linear time invariant systems.
- An understanding of discrete time sampling theory and amplitude quantization.
- An understanding of the design and implementation of FIR and IIR digital filters.
- An understanding of adaptive digital filtering, signal estimation and prediction.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

The grade will be based on the average homework grade (25%) and three in-class exams (25% each).

Homework 25% (For each of you, the lowest one will be dropped, and others have an equal percentage).

Homework will be assigned every 2-4 classes (about 8 homeworks total) and posted on LMS. You are allowed to discuss problems with other students, but you must prepare your solution independently. No copying!

Homework is due on Gradescope. Late homework will NOT be accepted.  
In the rare scenario that Exam 1 or Exam 2 has to be missed (at the consent of instructor), a missed exam will have the grade proportion added to Exam 3 (for example: if you miss Exam 1, then your Exam 3 will count 50%. If you miss both exams, Exam 3 will count 75%). Exam 3 MUST be taken. Any conflicts with the exam dates must be brought to the attention of the instructor as soon as they arise.
