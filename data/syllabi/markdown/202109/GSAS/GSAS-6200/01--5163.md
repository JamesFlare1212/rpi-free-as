### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

There are two primary ways to earn points in the course: Root Quests and Branch Quests (detailed below).

Additionally, each student has a “Goodwill” pool of 50 points to give out to other students in increments of 10 or 20 to mark particularly high quality work and/or particularly notable contributions to the class community. No student can receive more than 100 goodwill points.

Root Quests

There are 10 root quests.

Each of them comes in three sizes, worth 40, 50 or 60 points.

They are assigned points for completion only.

The 10 core quests are

- 1\. Semester Plan (develop your plan for earning the points you want for your desired grade)
- 2\. Syllabus Project Reflection (explain what work you did on the syllabus project and reflect on it)
- 3\. End of Semester Reflection (look back on the semester, assess yourself and the class as a whole)
- 4-10. Weeklies. Weekly core quests will be done until End Game starts on Nov 1. The parameters for the next weekly quest will be developed collaboratively each Thursday in class.

Branch Quests

- You cannot earn more than 60% of an A on branch quests. (i.e., 600 for undergrads, 750 for grads).
- They are assigned points using the medal system. For any quests with public presentation components, medals will be assigned through the anonymous feedback of the entire class.

| Award | Percentage | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diamond | 100% + 20% bonus | Considerably unique in all the best ways. An exemplar for the class. |
| Platinum | 100% + 10% bonus | Insightful; Does more than required (qualitatively). |
| Gold | 100% | Addresses all quest requirements. Solid effort. |
| Silver | 90% | Not a strong response. Some key components missing |
| Bronze | 70% | Significant components missing and/or generally shoddy work. |
| Honorable Mention | 50% | Credit for completion, but far less effort than expected. |

The initial set of branch quests and their base values are indicated below, but additionally quests may be developed by consensus. Detailed explanations of branch quests are available through the course site.

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

All communities are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in grade penalties. Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the Dean of Students.

Plagiarism is understood to include not only passing off someone else’s creative or scholarly work as your own, but also failing to properly cite sources, whether intentionally or unintentionally.
