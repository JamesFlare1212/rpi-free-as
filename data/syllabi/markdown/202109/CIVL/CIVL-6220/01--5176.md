## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Graduate level course intended to provide the students with a broad understanding of current methodologies in transportation modeling and economics not thoroughly covered in other courses and emerging issues pertaining to transportation research and practice.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The specific course objectives and outcomes include:

- Students will have the ability to analyze complex transportation problems considering economic, social, environmental and political factors.

- Students will be able to understand and use commonly available data sources to identify and propose solutions to transportation problems

- Students will have the ability to incorporate worldwide best practices in transportation in analyzing transportation issues.

- Students will have the ability to clearly communicate to a broad audience complex technical concepts concerning transportation issues.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Students will have the ability to analyze complex transportation problems considering economic, social, environmental and political factors.
- • Students will be able to understand and use commonly available data sources to identify and propose solutions to transportation problems
- • Students will have the ability to incorporate worldwide best practices in transportation in analyzing transportation issues.
- • Students will have the ability to clearly communicate to a broad audience complex technical concepts concerning transportation issues.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Class Discussion Leadership**

Each student will be required to lead a class discussion for 5-10 minutes about 5 times during the semester. Students should review articles in the popular press, distribute a link to them to all students a few days in advance and lead a brief discussion on their assigned dates.

**Course Papers**

There will be two course papers and presentations.

**Paper 1- Case Study** -The first paper and presentation is a paper describing how a community (city, state, country) addressed a particularly complex transportation issue. The paper should include a clear articulation of the problem, alternatives considered, methods of assessing alternatives and the results. The paper does not have to be a “success story” but rather it should focus on the practical problems associated with analyzing transportation issues. Paper length about 4-5 pages. This paper is due on the last day of class.

**Paper 2 – Foreign Practices** - The second is a paper and presentation on some innovative concept in transportation **outside of the United States**. The paper will discuss the problem that the solution addresses, how well it achieved its objectives and an honest assessment of likelihood of adoption in the United States. The paper itself is due on the date in the syllabus. Paper length about 4-5 pages.

**Grading Criteria:**

The grading will consist of the following.

Class discussion leadership 30%

Homework 10%

Final exam (take home) 20%

Case Study paper 20%

Foreign practices paper 20%

**Student Feedback:**

Throughout the semester, students are especially encouraged to bring attention of the professor any difficulties/issues encountered during the lectures. The primary purpose of this is to provide the instructor with continuous feedback on how to improve the classroom learning environment.

**Academic Integrity:**

Students are reminded: "RPI expects the highest standards of honesty and integrity in the academic performance of its students. Any act of scholastic dishonesty is regarded as a serious offense, which may result in expulsion. RPI defines scholastic dishonesty as submission of false records of academic achievement; cheating on assignments or examinations; plagiarizing; altering, forging, or misusing an academic record; taking, acquiring, or using test materials without faculty permission; acting alone or in cooperation with another to obtain dishonestly grades, honors, awards, or professional endorsement. Aiding and abetting an act of scholastic dishonesty is also considered a serious offense."

All classwork, including tests and homework, will be assigned to students individually. Where collaboration among students in groups is permitted, such as in the main course project, this will be made clear in the assignment.

**Attendance and Absences:**

You are expected to attend class promptly and regularly. If you are unable to attend class, it is your responsibility to obtain class notes from a classmate. If you arrive late to class, or must leave early, please be considerate with your classmates, and do so as quietly and unobtrusively as possible. If you are unable to submit a homework assignment or take and exam or quiz due to an absence, you will be granted a waiver of the late homework policy and be given the opportunity to make up for the missed exam or quiz only under special circumstances. These include 1) illness or personal injury and 2) university-related extracurricular activities. Illnesses and personal injuries include you or your children or spouse, and extracurricular activities include athletics. A written statement from a responsible party is required to be granted these special considerations. In the case of illness or personal injury, a letter from a physician must indicate that you were incapacitated from attending class. In the case of athletics, a letter from your coach or trainer must explain why you had to miss class

*Note: The above guidelines are subject to modification as deemed appropriate by the instructor.*
