## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Description:**

This HASS graduate seminar examines major theories and approaches to studying media from a cultural studies perspective. Readings will include both foundational and contemporary works examining television, film, and digital media including mobile devices and social media platforms. Analysis of readings will address topics such as the politics of representation across categories of gender, race, class, and ability; political economy of media; surveillance; datafication; globalization and (de)coloniality; and democratic vs. authoritarian media cultures. In addition, this seminar will discuss ways to conduct and publish research, submit to and attend conferences, build a professional network, and be an effective media studies educator.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will understand both foundational and contemporary works in media studies and be able to apply these understandings to their own arguments
- Students will examine how topics in media and communication relate to gender, race, class, ability, coloniality, and democratic vs. authoritarian media cultures
- Students will develop papers drawing on course material, focused on an area related to their research interests, and present these projects to the class
- Students will gain an understanding of both professionalization processes and pedagogical best practices, and deliver a relevant lesson to the class

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Assignments:**

- Participation, including pedagogy & professionalization assignments when relevant (20%)
- Weekly Response Papers (15% – 10 at 1.5% each, about 500 words, starting Week 2. You can skip two weeks of your choice)
- Mid-term Paper + Presentation (30%, 8-10 pages, 5-7 minutes)
- Final Paper (35%, 15-20 pages on a new topic, or 20–25-page revision and expansion of mid-term topic)

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
