## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

*Advanced Structure and Bonding in Materials* is a graduate-level Materials Science and Engineering core course aimed at developing an advanced understanding on various aspects of structure and cohesion in solids. Scope includes fundamental concepts to describe and experimentally measure structure and relate structure to bonding in solids. After a brief review of undergraduate-level basics, we will develop key advanced concepts in symmetry, Fourier space, and diffraction theory to describe and determine structure in crystalline solids. We will then describe classical and quantum models that relate structure and cohesion in crystals with different types of bonding.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

*Advanced Structure and Bonding in Materials* is a graduate-level Materials Science and Engineering core course aimed at developing an advanced understanding on various aspects of structure and cohesion in solids. Scope includes fundamental concepts to describe and experimentally measure structure and relate structure to bonding in solids. After a brief review of undergraduate-level basics, we will develop key advanced concepts in symmetry, Fourier space, and diffraction theory to describe and determine structure in crystalline solids. We will then describe classical and quantum models that relate structure and cohesion in crystals with different types of bonding.

The student will learn the fundamentals and applications of empirical and quantitative descriptions of crystal symmetry and structure, theories of primary and secondary bonding in crystals, and the theory and application of diffraction. The student will also learn to appreciate the intimate connections between structure, bonding, electronic structure, and properties of solids.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- The students will learn the fundamentals, advanced concepts and example applications, of phenomenological and quantitative descriptions of crystal symmetry and structure.
- The students will learn the fundamentals and example applications of diffraction theory.
- The students will learn theories of primary and secondary bonding in crystals.
- The students will learn to appreciate the intimate connections between structure, bonding, electronic structure, and properties of solids.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Home/class work – ~25%.  
Three exams during the semester (~25% each).  
This scheme subject to change depending on the overall performance of the class in the first two tests.

Participating in class discussions is key to staying on top of the material covered in the textbook and class lectures. Pre-/post-lecture readings will serve as strong bases for asking questions, clarifications, and sharing thoughts. Disruptive behaviors such as texting and internet browsing in class are not allowed.

Homework is perhaps the best way to prepare to strengthen and test your understanding of the topics covered in class, and prepare yourself for solving exam questions. I therefore urge you to take these assignments seriously. Your homeworks must be neatly presented with clear descriptions of procedures and reasoning behind the steps followed to arrive at the answer. You may consult and work together with others to solve the problems, but you must acknowledge them appropriately. Copying answers from *any* source will be considered plagiarism and will fetch an F grade.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

For homework and classwork, you may consult and work together with others to solve the problems. In fact, you are encouraged to do so. However, you must acknowledge appropriately. Copying answers from *any* source will be considered plagiarism and will fetch an F grade.  
All exams will be open book exams. Any exchange of information or ANY interaction with any other sources (including, but not limited to, other students, web sources, mobile devices, other's solutions) during an exam/quiz without the instructor’s permission will be interpreted as illicit transfer of exam data and considered an act of cheating.

Anyone caught cheating or plagiarizing will be subject to disciplinary actions, such as a failing grade for the course and referral to the Dean of Students. Cheating includes sharing answers, stealing answers, and placing text strings or equations on your calculator, cell phone, or other electronic device. Plagiarism means copying words from someone else's work, or information obtained from an unauthorized source even if you "change the sentence a bit".
