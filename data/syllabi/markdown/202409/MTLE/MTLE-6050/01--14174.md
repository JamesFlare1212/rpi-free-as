## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Introduction to Polymers is a first course on polymer science and structure-property relationships. Topics include chemical and molecular structure; morphology (structure) of amorphous and crystalline polymers; crystallization phenomena; polymer solutions and blends; physical properties of polymers in relation to structure, including rubber elasticity, viscoelasticity, and glass transition; and mechanical and rheological properties and testing.  
MTLE-6050 is the graduate level version of MTLE-4050. All of the information for MTLE-4050 applies for the graduate level course, but there is an additional assignment required. In order to get credit for 6050, students are responsible for doing the additional assignments whether they are enrolled as graduate or undergraduate students. Students can only earn credit for MTLE 4050 or MTLE 6050, not both!

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The objective of this course is to familiarize the student with polymer terminology, structure of polymeric materials, their properties, means of examining these properties and finally, give the student the necessary background for further studies on polymers.  
To achieve these objectives, we will resort to lecture style instructions comprising of the introduction of new concepts, derivation of formulas, and solutions of sample problems. Lectures will, for the most part, follow the order of the text, although not always on a page-by-page basis.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Describe the chemical structure of polymers
- Distinguish and describe the crystalline and amorphous structure of polymers,
- Distinguish and derive the different forms of molecular weight averages,
- Evaluate the chemical structure and morphology via experimental techniques,
- Relate the structure of polymers to their mechanical and rheological properties.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Exams, homeworks, and other assingments are clearly communicated in class.  
**For all students:**  
Homework is assigned on a semi-weekly basis. Each individual is expected to submit her or his own work for individual grading, although collaboration between students is allowed. The homework problems are identical to past year’s exam questions and are the best way to prepare for the exams.  
Team in-class problems contribute to the final grade. In your solutions, the method of calculation must be shown clearly. When physical properties are involved, all units and their cancellations must be shown clearly. If assignment contains creation of plots, then all plots must be generated on a computer using an appropriate computer program of the students’ choice. The class TA will be responsible for classwork collection and grading.  
There will be one pseudo-lab assignment. There are no actual laboratory experiments, rather we will use the data provided from an experiment in Prof. Palermo’s lab to calculate certain properties. The deliverables for each assignment will be defined in the assignment. All laboratory assignments must be typed and handed in for every individual student. No hand written assignments will be accepted. The class TA will be responsible for grading these assignments.  
Examinations: There will be two 80-minute examinations given during regular lecture periods, and final exam at the end of the course.

- Exam 1: material from chapters 2, 7, 8.
- Exam 2: material from chapters 11-15
- Final Exam: all material covered, including special topics

A final examination covering all the material in the course will be given at the end of the course during final exam period. The graded lecture exams will be returned to students, while the instructor will keep the final exam. Students can review the grading of the final exam in the instructor’s office.  
**[For 6000 level students]**  
**Additional Assignment**  
Students taking MTLE-6050 will be required to do a literature review of a special topic in polymer science, assigned by the instructor or chosen by the student. Each student will give a ≤ 30-minute oral presentation in class.  
The presentation is a semester-long effort, not something that can be done last minute.  
Throughout the semester, there are milestone due dates (topic selection, outline, 1st draft) and feedback will be provided by the instructor.

| Topic | Common Examples |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Conducting Polymers | PCHT, others |
| 2. Piezoelectric Polymers | PVDF, others |
| 3. Antibacterial Polymers | various |
| 4. Biodegradable Polymers | PLA, PGA, PGLA, many others |
| 5. Natural Polymer: Spider Silk | Spider Silk |

The reports should clearly demonstrate the link between **polymer structure** and/or **processing conditio**ns with the relevant **physical pro**perties of the material.  
At least 10 references are required, from the primary academic literature (not web resources). You may use SCOPUS or other academic search engine to find the relevant articles in your special topics area. The oral presentation will be not more than 20 minutes long.
