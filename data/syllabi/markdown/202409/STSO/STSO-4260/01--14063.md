## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is an advanced undergraduate course focusing on social science research in the field of agri-food studies. Students enrolled in this course should be prepared to do a great deal of reading, writing, and speaking. Completing this course will prepare students to do graduate level work and professional research.

## Course Text(s)

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- When students have successfully completed this course, they will be able to: • Recognize and apply key social science concepts for analysis of food and agricultural issues, such as social class, gender, commodity chain, agri-food system, food justice, food system, vertical and horizontal integration, commodity chain, globalization, and nutritionalization.
- When students have successfully completed this course, they will be able to: • Use social theories and concepts to interpret, explain, and critique the socio-cultural, political, economic, and philosophical factors that influence contemporary food production, distribution, and consumption.
- When students have successfully completed this course, they will be able to: • Critically analyze and assess social science research on the agri-food system.
- When students have successfully completed this course, they will be able to: • Generate original hypotheses about the agri-food system—including possible solutions to problems such as hunger, environmental degradation, and rural poverty.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Grades will be based on student performance in the following assignments. Note that there is a semester-long research project, with grades for key milestones. Assignments will be turned in and graded on Blackboard. If there are any discrepancies between this document and the assignment/grading descriptions on Blackboard, Blackboard will be considered the authoritative version.

Attendance and participation (15%)

- Class attendance is required, and when you are in class you are expected to actively participate in discussions and activities.
- I take attendance at the start of class, and I take note of class participation at the conclusion of class each day. You can earn one point for attendance and one point for participation.

Reading response journal (25%)

- You will maintain a weekly reading response journal and turn it in for review at designated intervals. For this assignment, you should not worry about achieving a “proper” or “elegant” writing style. Use your own voice. The goals are analysis, communication, and making connections among issues, theories, data, and experiences.
- You should aim to write at about 300 words each week that respond directly to the assigned readings and to the “Consider” questions posed in Blackboard.
- Each entry should have a title and a date.
- Each time you hand in the journal, include the previous journal entries, including my comments, so that there is an ongoing conversation in the document. I recommend that you use Google Docs for this assignment so that you can maintain one continuous document and allow me to comment on it. Turn it in by providing the link to the document.
- You are permitted to update previous entries, if you have further thoughts on the topic. Please note any changes by highlighting or using different color text.
- Each entry will earn up to 5 points.
- Due dates are **September 13, October 17, and December 5**.

Discussion leadership (10%)

- Pairs of students will be responsible for guiding and structuring class discussion of the assigned readings for 45 minutes once during the semester. Your main goal in this assignment is to orchestrate a rich, fruitful discussion. Therefore, you will have to read the chosen selection and come up with a list of provocative questions to help guide, nurture, and stimulate the conversation of your classmates.
- Some questions should be factual, asking fellow students to summarize main point(s) of the reading or to ponder the usage of key terms/concepts. The others should be open-ended, asking students to share their interpretations and opinions of the reading. More guidance for this assignment will be provided in class.
- Your grade for this assignment will be based half on the questions and definitions that you prepare and half on your facilitation of the discussion. You will receive a percentage grade.
- **By 12 noon on the day prior to the class meeting**, the BOTH discussion leaders should submit (on Blackboard) **6-10 proposed discussion questions and a list of terms/concepts (with definitions)** that are important for understanding the text. I will provide feedback and suggestions in time for you to make changes before the class meets.

Major assignment: research paper

This semester, you will choose a topic, find and read sources on that topic, develop a focused research question, and use evidence from credible sources to substantiate an answer to that question. You will not do this all on your own; we will have weekly research workshops where you can learn new research skills, get peer review of your draft writing, and spend time on writing. If you engage with this process actively, this incremental and supported approach to research will result in a high-quality paper at the conclusion of the semester, which you could use as a writing sample for applications for employment or graduate school.

The broad theme of your research paper is “fixes for food system problems.” My goal is to encourage you to question how things get framed as problems and to think about why techno-fixes, while tempting, don’t often succeed. You should choose a topic for your research paper where you can examine a proposed “solution” for a “problem” in the food system. For example, you could examine a technological fix such as genetic engineering to extend the shelf life of produce, artificial sweeteners to reduce the calorie content of processed foods, or farming efficiency improvements to avert famine. Your research should unpack how the issue came to be framed as a problem and consider alternative framings based on research into the social and ecological contexts.

Even if you are an experienced writer, I strongly urge that you take advantage of the research and writing assistance resources available of this campus. Visit the Center for Global Communication and Design and familiarize yourself with the services it offers (info at https://info.rpi.edu/comm-d ). The library also has excellent reference and research resources and you can take a self-guided tutorial here: https://guides.lib.rpi.edu/InfoLit/ .

You will be graded on three increments of your research process as well as on the final paper. Please note: the percentages listed indicate what each assignment is worth toward your *final course grade*.

Memo on research questions and preliminary research bibliography (10%)

- Decide on your term paper topic and use the library to find at least 25 relevant books, research articles, and magazine/newspaper articles. Download digital sources and save them in a reference manager like Zotero or Mendeley. Borrow physical copies of books and organize them in your workspace. If you are not able to access physical copies of books, use interlibrary loan to request scans of selected chapters. Create a bibliography listing these sources using Chicago author-date style.
- Once you have done a preliminary review of your sources, write a 1-page memo in which you state your research question and explain why this is a good question to try to answer in your final paper.
- Turn in your memo and bibliography in one document, due **September 30**.

Annotated bibliography (10%)

- Turn your preliminary research bibliography into an annotated bibliography that summarizes the value of each source for answering your research question. Eliminate sources that turned out not to be useful. Add sources that you discovered while taking notes on your initial list of sources (e.g. books cited by a source you read).
- Recommended: organize your annotated bibliography into themes and alphabetize within those themed sections.
- Due **October 21.**

Presentation and response to feedback (10%)

- Prepare a short (5-10 minutes) talk in which you explain your research question, your preliminary thesis, and the evidence that led you to this conclusion.
- Your presentation should state the argument that you can make with the evidence you have considered so far. Outline the key ideas that support your thesis and cite the sources that provide supporting evidence of each of those points.
- When you give your presentation in class, listen attentively to questions and feedback and write up a plan for responding to the critique.
- You will be graded both on your presentation and on your revision plan.
- Turn in your revision plan no later than **November 21**.

Final paper (20%)

- Word count should be between 4,500 and 5,000 words (20 double-spaced pages), excluding bibliography.
- Cite all sources using Chicago author-date style (https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/tools_citationguide/citation-guide-2.html ).
- The paper is due on **December 2**.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty. If found in violation of the academic honesty policy, students may be subject to two types of penalty. The instructor administers an academic grade penalty and the student is reported to the Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education as appropriate. The first violation results in 0 grade for that assignment. The second violation results in failure of the course.

Plagiarism includes purchasing term papers; copying or handing in the writing of another student (current or former); using sentences verbatim from a published source without appropriate referencing (when in doubt, cite the source); presenting as one’s own the detailed argument of a published source; and presenting as one’s own the text produced by an AI tool such as ChatGPT (see below for more on this). “Recycling” papers written in other courses is also forbidden. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
