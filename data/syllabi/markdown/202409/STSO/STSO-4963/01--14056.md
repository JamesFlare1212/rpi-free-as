## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Today's global health challenges extend beyond biological and technical concerns, encompassing social, economic, and political dimensions as well. This course seeks to explore the intricate relationships among the economic, political, social, ethical, behavioral, and biomedical aspects of health through interdisciplinary lenses. It offers an introduction to key concepts and methodologies of Medical Anthropology/Sociology, Science and Technology Studies, and Critical Public & Global Health. Students will gain a comprehensive understanding of the diverse health challenges faced by populations both locally and internationally, alongside the economic, political, and cultural factors that influence these issues. By examining a diverse range of health issues, from addiction and eating disorders to cancer and reproductive health, students will explore how health is affected by structural inequalities, stigma, and discrimination. Throughout the course, students will engage in critical readings, discussions, and assignments designed to enhance their understanding of global and public health issues and to develop their ability to conduct independent research. The course will also delve into issues of deservingness in health care, exploring who is deemed worthy of protection and care within different systems.

The course aims to equip students with the skills necessary to critically analyze health policies and to advocate for equitable health practices. Students will engage with critical scholarship that will cover not only the foundational principles of global health but also the contemporary debates on public health ethics and policy. Students will develop critical analytical skills by engaging with a broad array of readings, case studies, and discussions, enabling them to understand and address pressing health challenges in a global context. In-class discussions will explore the ethical implications of prioritizing profit over saving lives, scrutinizing who gains from medical advancements and who holds power over access to treatments, while also examining the impact of influential donors like the Gates Foundation on global health initiatives.

*This course is reading-intensive and designed to encourage students engage with complex theories, methods, and ethical considerations central to global and public health.* Students will delve into a wide array of challenging texts that will deepen their understanding of health disparities and global health dynamics. Active participation in discussions is essential, as it will enhance comprehension and critical thinking skills. This course will prepare students for the rigors of graduate study by fostering analytical skills and intellectual curiosity. Completing the readings and engaging in class discussions are crucial components of this course, providing a solid foundation for advanced academic and professional pursuits in the field of public and global health.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

✦ Reflect on and discuss the ethical challenges and professional attributes essential in addressing global health problems.

✦ Critically assess the frameworks and paradigms within global health, including the influence of donors on health policies.

✦ Differentiate between public health and global health, understanding their interconnections and the concept of deservingness in health care.

✦ Analyze the tension between profit motives and the ethical imperative to save lives, evaluating the ownership and accessibility of medical treatments.

✦ Explore the political and social implications of medical conditions, focusing on the importance of understanding intersectionality in analyzing the distribution of health and illness.

✦ Examine cultural and societal perspectives on eating disorders and addiction, and their impact on public health responses.

✦ Investigate cancer as a global health issue, considering socio-economic factors and healthcare access disparities.

✦ Evaluate reproductive health issues, focusing on the cultural, political, and ethical dimensions of gynecological practices.

✦ Understand the impact of policing on public health and its broader implications for community well-being.

✦ Analyze global ethnographies of HIV, focusing on stigma, risk, and the social dynamics of health care.

✦ Explore the intersections of stigma, gender, race, and disability in health, and their impact on access to care and treatment.

✦ Assess the role of health activism and prevention strategies in promoting equitable health outcomes and policies.

✦ Enhance your written and verbal communication skills by engaging in written assignments, participating in class activities and discussions, and presenting your final research project.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Develop the skills necessary to read and evaluate social science research in an intelligent and critical manner.
- 2\. Demonstrate understanding by explaining the role of theory in the construction of sociological inquiry.
- 3\. Demonstrate understanding by describing and comparing and contrasting different theories about human culture, social identity, economic entities, political systems, and other forms of social organization.
- 4\. Summarize sociological concepts and theories as they apply to medicine and healthcare.
- 5\. Identify sociological research methods used to define and measure health outcomes.
- 6\. Apply epidemiological principles and methods to health-related outcomes.
- 7\. Describe how health and illness behavior are socially constructed.
- 8\. Describe the impact of social and economic influences on health.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

***1) Office Hour Meeting***

You are expected to come to my office hours at least one time during the first part of the semester to chat with me about your experience and/or your personal process and engagement in the class. If my office hours do not work with your schedule, we can work together to find a time that works for us both. This one-on-one meeting is a requirement of the course, because I believe that the best learning happens when we are all personally invested in the material and the class as a learning community. The better I can understand your personal process and experience in the class, the better I can help facilitate your learning and success. To earn credit, you must schedule this required meeting before December.

***2) Attendance and Active Participation***

In a discussion-based course, student attendance and active participation is required and crucial to creating a successful learning community. This means coming to class ready to discuss the assigned material with observations, questions, and comments, as well as participating fully in any small and large group activities. Our class time is specially designed to prepare you for successful completion of the other graded components of the course. Thus, your full engagement and participation during our class time will directly and positively influence your grade more broadly. I expect each of you to come to class having finished all the readings and assignments for the day. I acknowledge some of you may be uncomfortable in large group discussions, and so I recognize engaged listening and note taking as valid forms of participation. Still, consider preparing one specific point about the readings to address in class each day, or challenge yourself to speak up in class once a week in some way.

During class discussion, you should be intentionally aware of your participation, if you find you are speaking repeatedly, step back and allow others the chance to speak up; conversely, if you notice that you are not speaking during class discussions, find ways to contribute to the conversation e.g. draw upon your notes for questions or previously formed thoughts.

***3) Discussion Facilitation***

**Objective:** This assignment is designed to enhance students' ability to engage peers in critical and reflective discussions about complex global health topics. Students will develop skills in leading discussions, encouraging diverse perspectives, and fostering an interactive learning environment.

**Instructions**

1. **Assignment Overview:**
   - o Starting with Week 3, each student will be responsible for facilitating one class discussion during the semester. This role involves guiding the conversation, encouraging participation, and enriching the discussion with relevant materials.
2. **Preparation:**
   - o Thoroughly review the assigned readings for the week you are facilitating. Identify key themes, arguments, and areas of contention within the texts.
   - o Develop a list of 4-6 open-ended discussion questions that provoke critical thinking and debate. Avoid straightforward yes/no questions; instead, focus on questions that encourage ethical and theoretical exploration.
3. **Discussion Questions:**
   - o Ensure your questions invite diverse viewpoints and challenge classmates to think deeply about the readings' implications. Consider questions like:
     - How do the authors’ arguments challenge or reinforce existing global health paradigms?
     - What ethical dilemmas arise from the issues discussed in the readings?
     - How might the themes explored in the readings apply to current global health challenges?

4. **Enrichment Materials:**
   - o Supplement the discussion by bringing in relevant materials such as news articles, videos, case studies, or current events related to the readings. These should provide additional context or contrasting perspectives.
   - o Be prepared to explain how these materials connect to the readings and enhance the discussion.
5. **Facilitation Approach:**
   - o Begin the class with a brief introduction to the topic, highlighting its significance and relevance.
   - o Pose your prepared discussion questions to the class, encouraging participation and engagement.
   - o Facilitate the conversation by prompting quieter students, redirecting the discussion as needed, and ensuring respectful dialogue.
   - o Be open to exploring unexpected avenues of discussion that arise during the session.

***4) Weekly Reflections***

**Objective:** The weekly reflections (every Tuesday and Friday) are designed to encourage students to engage deeply with the course material, critically analyze the readings, and develop thoughtful questions that promote discussion and reflection on the topics covered. *Often the reflections will be randomly distributed among students for everyone to engage with others’ ideas and come up with discussion questions. So, please be prepared for sharing your reflections with other students.*

**Instructions**

**Weekly Submission:**

- From Week 2 onwards, each student must submit a written reflection on the readings and topics discussed in class each week. Reflections are due at the beginning of each class.

**Content Requirements:**

**Questions:**

- Formulate 2-3 insightful questions about the readings. These questions should demonstrate a critical engagement with the material, highlighting areas of interest, confusion, or contention.
- Questions should aim to provoke thought and discussion, rather than simply seeking factual answers.

**Reflection:**

Write a brief (anywhere between 100-300 words) reflection on the week’s readings. Consider the following prompts to guide your reflection:

- What are the key arguments or themes presented in the readings?
- How do these themes relate to current global health issues or personal experiences?
- What ethical, social, or political implications do the readings suggest?
- How have the readings challenged or reinforced your understanding of global and public health?

***5) Futures of Health***

**Objective:** This assignment aims to encourage students to think critically and creatively about the future of global health by considering current trends, potential challenges, and innovative solutions. This is a creative writing assignment and students should think out of box: do you think the future of health is going to be utopic or dystopic? Or, do you believe it is not going to be black and white? Whom do you think will be more vulnerable to diseases in the future? Or, are we going to solve all health problems based on developing technologies? Are there going to be new pathogens in the future with no cure? Or is it more likely for the existing pathogens to mutate?

**Instructions**

1. **Research and Analysis:**
   - o Begin by researching current global health trends, focusing on social, economic, technological, and environmental factors.
   - o Identify at least three key trends or issues that are likely to influence global health in the next 50 years. Consider factors such as climate change, technological advancements, demographic shifts, or changes in healthcare policy.
2. **Writing the Essay:**
   - o **Introduction:**
     - Introduce the key trends or issues you have identified. Provide a brief overview of how these factors currently impact global health.
   - o **Body:**
     - For each trend or issue, discuss its potential impact on global health over the next 50 years.
     - Consider how these factors might affect health outcomes, healthcare delivery, and health inequalities.
     - Discuss possible challenges and opportunities associated with each trend.
     - Incorporate evidence from academic sources, expert opinions, and relevant data to support your analysis.
     - Propose innovative solutions or strategies to address the challenges identified. Consider technological, policy-based, and community-driven approaches.
   - o **Conclusion:**
     - Summarize your predictions and reflections on the future of global health.
     - Highlight the importance of proactive measures to address emerging health challenges.

3. **Formatting and Submission:**
   - o The essay should be 1,500-2,000 words in length.
   - o Use a clear and coherent structure with appropriate headings and subheadings.
   - o Submit the essay via the course's online portal by the specified deadline.
4. **Evaluation Criteria:**

**Research and Analysis (30%):** Depth of research, understanding of trends, and quality of analysis.

**Creativity and Innovation (30%):** Originality of ideas and proposed solutions.

**Writing Quality (20%):** Clarity, coherence, grammar, and adherence to formatting guidelines.

**Critical Thinking (20%):** Ability to critically evaluate trends and predict their impact on global health.

***6)*** ***Public Health Campaign***

**Objective:** This assignment challenges students to design and develop a comprehensive public health campaign that addresses a specific health issue. Students will work collaboratively to create a campaign strategy that effectively communicates health messages to a target audience and promotes positive health outcomes.

**Instructions:**

**Group Formation:**

- Form groups of 3-5 students. Each group will select or be assigned a specific public health issue to address through their campaign.

**Topic Selection:**

- Choose a public health issue relevant to your community or a global context, such as vaccination, mental health awareness, substance abuse prevention, or nutrition.

**Research and Analysis:**

- Conduct research on the chosen health issue, including its prevalence, causes, and impact on different populations.
- Analyze the demographics and characteristics of your target audience, understanding their needs, preferences, and barriers to behavior change.

**Campaign Strategy:**

**Objective Setting:**

- Define clear and measurable objectives for your campaign. What do you want to achieve (e.g., increase awareness, change behavior, reduce stigma)?

**Message Development:**

- Create compelling and culturally sensitive messages tailored to your target audience.
- Use evidence-based information and persuasive techniques to convey your message effectively.

**Media and Outreach:**

- Decide on the media channels and platforms you will use to reach your audience (e.g., social media, print materials, community events).
- Develop a timeline and plan for disseminating your messages across these channels.

**Creative Materials:**

- Design and produce creative materials for your campaign, such as posters, flyers, videos, or social media content.
- Ensure all materials are visually appealing, informative, and aligned with your campaign objectives

6. **Evaluation Criteria:**

- **Research and Analysis (15%):** Depth of research and understanding of the health issue and target audience.
- **Campaign Strategy (15%):** Clarity and feasibility of objectives, messaging, and outreach plan.
- **Creative Materials (50%):** Creativity, design quality, and effectiveness of campaign materials.
- **Presentation (20%):** Clarity, organization, and engagement in the presentation.

This assignment encourages students to apply theoretical knowledge to practical situations, enhancing their skills in communication, collaboration, and public health advocacy.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
