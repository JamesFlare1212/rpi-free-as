## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Course Description  
Strong community engagement supports climate resilience. This course supports students in working with civic, community, and government organizations in the context of climate change. The course is a project-based experience that connects students to ongoing initiatives in Troy and the Capital region. Students will explore a variety of “theories of change,” connecting these theories to approaches used by a wide variety of organizations, developing their own stances on how to effectively contribute to goals of community resilience, environmental justice, and social cohesion. Students will develop skills in project management, communication, community building, and civic engagement.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Self Assessment  
You will assess your work and your growth over the course of the semester through a 3-part assessment. You will use The Learning Record (see below) to help you consider what is important to you, what sources you might use, and how you might assess yourself during the semester.  
1\. Baseline  
This will describe what you want to assess, and why, It sets expectations for yourself for the course. This will be described in detail within the first few class sessions.  
2\. Mid-point  
You will use your baseline assignment to assess yourself mid-semester. This allows you to consider what you have accomplished already, what your challenges are, and where you’d like to be by the end of the semester. You may add to, or otherwise adapt your baseline goals based upon what you have already learned.  
3\. Final  
You will use your baseline & midpoint assignments to assess yourself. Have you accomplished what you set out to do at the beginning? How did your midpoint assessment  
help you to make changes as you completed the rest of the semester? You will be guided in how to tie assessments to grading, and you will use this final assessments to explore why you have earned the grade you believe you have earned.

Journal  
You will write 5 journal entries using a combination of the readings, class discussions, and your work with your organization. The first 3 are loosely-themed to give you something to organize around. The last 2 are your choice of theme. The expectation is for you to engage deeply with the ideas or themes in at least one of the readings. You should make connections to class discussions and the work you are doing with  
your organization. You should describe the organizational context and work that you are doing and show the relationship to the key themes in both readings and discussions. Journal entries should be 5 pages (1500-2200 words).

1\. Decision-making  
2\. Practice  
3\. Ongoing relationship with org

Radio/Podcast Interview  
This will be a 10 minute audio interview with people from your organization. You may interview one, or many people, depending on what you are interested in accomplishing.  
1\. Key themes  
Using your other assignments, class readings and discussions in conjunction with the work you are doing with your organization, you will choose themes that are pertinent to explore with people from your organization.  
2\. Interview logistics  
You will create a timeline-schedule to organize the who, what, where, and whens of the interview(s) you are doing, and include time for some light editing within that schedule.  
3\. Proposal with rough questions  
This proposal will describe the whys & hows of your interview(s). It will include 10-15 broad, but well-considered questions for you to use as a foundation for your interview.  
4\. High quality final recording with written transcripts.  
You will turn in a 10 minute recording with written transcripts. Class time will be provided for learning technical knowledge and working to ensure high quality recording.  
Final Report  
1\. In-class formal presentation  
Presentations will be approximately 10 minutes and will incorporate what you have learned this semester, the potential future of your project work, and what knowledges you will take with you at the end.  
2\. Letter to future students  
This letter will be to future students who take this course. You will describe what you’ve done, what you’ve learned, and potential projects and work that might be considered.  
3\. Letter to organization  
This letter will be to people in your organization describing your own relationship to their organization, their challenges and successes, and after some TBD project closure with & for the organization, incorporate all of these ideas, and if applicable, potential ways forward on their projects.  
Active Engagement  
1\. Fulfill practical hours  
You will complete 45-60 hours over the course of the semester. Additionally you will have some required meetings and planning over the course of the semester to complete.  
2\. In-class active participation  
(Includes things like group work, studio work, class discussions, feedback, etc) In addition to assigned readings and screenings, learning will come through discussion with each other, as well as collaborative work. Your active participation is both expected and valued. The topics we will be discussing are content-rich and thought provoking, from many different knowledge frameworks. Many will be new to you, making the learning experience that much fuller. Please be sensitive to, and respectful of each other.  
Being actively engaged with the course material and being a constructive collaborator is crucial to your learning experience. Because this is both a practice- and discussion-driven class, the quality of your experience is in large part dependent on the quality of preparation and visible engagement of each participant. Your preparation directly affects your classmates’ ability to participate. Please be generous and come to class ready to actively engage.  
Class Discussions  
Reading, watching, and playing the course materials prior to the class they are due also means note taking and preparing for discussions and other class activities. Participation means engaging in active listening, asking questions when you have them, making thoughtful comments when you think of them, and putting the work in so you will be able to respond.  
It does not mean dominating discussion or talking for the sake of talking. If you are shy, it means stepping up and challenging yourself to speak when you have an idea or a question. If you are outgoing, it means stepping back when you have had your say and letting the conversation open up to others. It means giving the person who is speaking your full attention and treating everyone in the room with respect even when you disagree with them.

It is expected that you will attend all classes, be on time, and fully present for the duration of each class. To be present in class means to be actively engaged in the discussions, activities, and workshops, to be generous with your skills, as well recognizing your classmates’ skills and

Course policies for attendance & engagement are listed here according to usual in-person requirements and expectations. I do expect you to attend class – and to be on-time and ready to work. ### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

the Rensselaer Title IX page.
