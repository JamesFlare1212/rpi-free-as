## Course Goals

### Goals:

The objectives of the course are to provide the student with the ability to recognize and analyze problems related to the safe operation of chemical processes, the transient behavior of chemical manufacturing operations, and the automatic control of dynamic processes. The course will introduce the student to the basic principles and concepts of chemical process design and the use of chemical process simulators for the control of chemical processes. MATLAB and SIMULINK are used for dynamic process simulation and control system development. Many of the homework will require computer-based simulations.

The course is taught in a studio format, combining lectures, discussions and computer-based simulations in a single laptop-based classroom. To install MATLAB on your laptop, please follow the instructions posted at:

https://afsws.rpi.edu/AFS/dept/arc/software/matlab/2011a/public_html/install.html

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Ability to design and conduct an experiment (computer experiment) and analyze the data.
- 2\. Ability to design a system component (controller) to meet a set of specifications
- 3\. Ability to communicate effectively based on an oral presentation that will be given on a project the students will work on during the last month of the semester.
- • From step response data, students should be able to develop empirical transfer function models to fit the data.
- • From a low-order process transfer function, students should be able to perform a Routh stability analysis to find controller tuning parameter values that cause the closed-loop system to lose stability. For a proportional-only controller, students should be able to confirm that Routh stability analysis results are consistent with a Zieger-Nichols closed-loop stability test.
- • For a given transfer function model, be able to design an internal model controller (IMC). The steps include: factorization of right-half-plane zeros and time-delays to form invertible (‘good stuff’) and non-invertible (“bad stuff”) elements. Choose an appropriate order filter to assure that the resulting controller is physically realizable. Be able to implement the IMC design in a closed-loop block diagram and use SIMULINK to simulate the control system behavior. Be able to adjust the tuning parameter (IMC filter time constant) to satisfy the desired closed-loop performance, including the desired closed-loop time constant and manipulated input (controller output) constraints.
- • For a given transfer function, students should be able to design a PID controller using the IMC-based PID design procedure. Students should be able to relate the IMC filter factor (closed-loop time constant) and the PID controller parameters (controller gain, integral time and derivative time).
- • For a multivariable steady-state process gain matrix, students should be able to perform a relative gain array (RGA) calculation and recommend input-output pairings for control system design. Students should perform a failure sensitivity analysis to predict if the closed-loop system will remain stable of one of the control loops is “opened” or if one of the manipulated inputs (controller outputs) saturates.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

In the tradition of the academic enterprise, the primary objective of an academic program is focused on the discovery, discussion, understanding, clarification, and dissemination of knowledge. The development of intelligence and strengthening of moral responsibility are two of the most important aims of education. The intent of this course is to provide students with an ability to analyze problems related to the dynamic operation and control of a chemical process. The environment of instruction is intended to be open, challenging, respectful, and fair.

Breaches of academic honesty include cheating, plagiarism, and the unauthorized possession of exams, papers, or other materials. The following actions constitute major forms, but not exclusively all forms, of academic dishonesty:

- submission: submitting academically required material that has been previously submitted in whole or in substantial part in another course, without prior and expressed consent of the instructor.
- plagiarism: copying or receiving material from a source or sources and submitting this material as one's own without authorization, consent, or appropriate acknowledgement to the source (quotations, paraphrases, basic ideas), or otherwise representing the work of another as one's own.
- cheating: receiving information, or soliciting information, from another student or other unauthorized source, or giving information to another student, with the intent to deceive while completing an examination, quiz, class test, individual assignment, or other academic exercise. Cheating includes copying the work of another individual or the unauthorized collaboration between two or more students during an academic exercise such as a homework assignment, quiz, class test, or examination.
- falsification of academic materials: fabricating laboratory materials, notes, reports, or any forms of computer data; forging an instructor's name or initials; or submitting a report, paper, materials, computer data, or examination (or any considerable part thereof) prepared by any person other than the student responsible for the assignment. Falsification also includes unauthorized modification of returned homework assignments, class tests, reports, or examinations for reconsideration or regrading by the instructor or teaching assistant.
- procurement: acquisition, distribution or acceptance of examinations, laboratory results, or confidential academic materials without prior and expressed consent of the instructor.
- improper or illegal computer usage.

Breaches of academic integrity will result in disciplinary measures that may include:

- A failing grade or grade adjustment for a particular assignment, quiz or examination.
- A failing grade or grade adjustment for the course.

If there is a reason to believe that a student may have been involved in academic dishonesty the following procedure will be followed:

- The student will be contacted within 10 Institute business days of having discovered the possible dishonesty and a meeting will be scheduled to discuss the allegations.
- During the meeting, a review of the circumstances and evidence related to the suspicion of academic dishonesty will take place. The review will give the student the opportunity to provide his/her perspective of the situation.
- After reviewing the situation, the Instructor will make a determination as to the guilt or innocence of the accused student.
- If it is concluded that a student has violated the Institute academic dishonesty policy, it will be up to the Instructor to determine the academic (grade) portion of the penalty and to communicate the decision in writing to the student. This communication will occur within five Institute business days of having met with the student.
- This written decision will also be communicated to the Senior Judicial Administrator in the Dean of Students Office, along with a brief summary of the case facts and a copy of any supporting documentation.
