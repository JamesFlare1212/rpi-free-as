## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

A two-term laboratory course on experimental analysis of the operations and processes of chemical engineering. Emphasis is placed on planning of experiments, data evaluation, and report writing.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

There is no required text for this course beyond your recommended texts in prior and related concurrent chemical engineering courses. Laboratory manual(s) (electronically posted on LMS), instructional videos, and software, are required for this course. Supplementary reading may be recommended within the experiment manuals and during lecture hours in the first few weeks.

For physical experiments in Ricketts 008A or Ricketts 101, basic personal protective equipment (safety gear) is required, consisting of a face mask, goggles, lab coat, and nitrile rubber gloves at a minimum. ## Course Goals

### Goals:

The purpose of this course is to provide senior chemical engineering students with hands-on experience operating and analyzing common chemical engineering unit operations. The unit operations covered in this laboratory course build on the theoretical material covered in previous and concurrent chemical engineering courses. Emphasis is placed on planning and executing the experiments, analyzing data, and dissemination of results via written reports. As a primary goal of this course, you should develop an aptitude for performing unit operations by applying fundamental chemical engineering knowledge to their operations. You will also develop communication skills necessary to work in a team or group. Courses at RPI are generally expected to require 2-3 hours of outside work for each credit hour. You should therefore expect to put in at least 8-10 hours/week outside the lab or classroom.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

### Other Course Policies:

Each experiment will generally consist of a three or four week cycle.  
**Week 1: Conducting the Experiment**  
In-person laboratory experiments will be supervised by a teaching assistant and/or Dr. Hedden.

Week 2: Individual Assignments Due

Following the experiment, all students have seven days to prepare calculations and write sections of the report on their own. Submission of individual contributions through LMS for grading is required by the seventh day, and no individual extensions will be granted. *Please plan ahead!*  
à Individual assignments ensure that everyone brings something useful to the first team report writing session and the interim meeting, helping your team stay on schedule.  
Specific Individual Assignments for each experiment will be posted by the third week of class. Generally speaking, Individual Assignments consist of the following items.

1. a) Writing of a specific report section
2. b) Preparation of at least one properly formatted Figure or Table
3. c) Completion of at least one full page of hand-written calculations, which may include

uncertainty analysis  
**Policy Regarding Individual Assignments.** Your individual work submitted through LMS serves as a record of what you brought to your first team writing session. *No make-up assignments can be given because we cannot turn back the clock and repair harm done to your teammates due to lack of effort or poor time management.* Individual contribution grades reflect your effort level and the quality of work done to support your team. Failure to attend your afternoon laboratory session and/or poor preparation for the laboratory session may also result in a reduction of your Individual Contribution grade.  
**Week 2/3: Interim Meeting**  
An interim meeting with the instructor will take place usually during the second or third week. *Students should bring completed calculations, tables of data, and figures to this meeting.* Each group member is to be familiar with the calculations and data analysis by this time. Be prepared to ask any questions about the data or analysis and the instructor’s expectations for the final report and/or presentation, which is due the following week. Generally, these meetings will take place from 5:00 to 6:20 PM in Ricketts 206 (if possible) or via WebEx, and several laboratory teams will be present for each meeting. At least two students from each group should attend the meeting; the meeting will be recorded for those who cannot attend.

## Week 3/4: Final Report & Foreman Report

Each experiment concludes with a final report tentatively due one week after the interim meeting. In cases where the tentative due date falls on a day that inconveniences one or more team members, extensions may be granted. However, please inform your instructor of the problem in advance. *Detailed guidelines for preparing reports will be posted on LMS and covered during lecture time.*  
The final grade on each report is based on the overall quality of report preparation, technical writing aspects, scientific content, correctness, and uncertainty analysis. Please pay attention to any special requests within the laboratory manuals of specific experiments.  
**Policy Regarding Report Preparation.** Laboratory reports are team-based writing assignments. You will find that your grades on the reports depend to a large extent on whether your teammates communicate well and attend the meetings. *If any teammates fail to attend the group writing sessions, the remaining team members are expected to complete the work on their own.* There will be an opportunity to report poor meeting attendance and/or lack of effort on the Foreman Reports. *Teammates' ratings of effort, quality of work, and meeting attendance may be used at the instructor's discretion in determining an individual's course grade.*  
Failing to contribute to writing of a given laboratory is justification for receiving a grade of zero on the report, even if you were present when the experiment was being performed. Students who contribute less to the writing of their reports than expected by their teammates may receive substantial deductions on the final report grade; there is no stipulation that all team members will receive the same grade on a given report.  
**Foreman Report Submission.** On the day your report is submitted for grading, each team member must submit a 1-page Foreman Report on LMS evaluating the contributions of your teammates to that report. Foreman reports are confidential to some extent in that your teammates will not be able to see your comments. Missing Foreman Reports at the end of the semester could result in a deduction from your course grade at the instructor’s discretion.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

All work in this course is to be done independently by each lab group. No collaboration between different groups is permitted. Questions should be addressed to the Instructor or TAs. Evidence of collaboration or falsification of laboratory data will be dealt with according to the Student Handbook. Additionally, a signed ‘Foreman Report’ must be completed and submitted individually through LMS to document the contribution of each individual member in the group.

**Academic Integrity:** Consulting “back work”, meaning graded or ungraded laboratory reports from past years’ students, is considered cheating and is strictly forbidden. Laboratory reports showing evidence of copying, paraphrasing, or adapting writing or calculations from previous years’ students, no matter how much or little work was borrowed, will be grounds for a grade of zero on the report in question for the entire group. Fair warning!

### Additional Course Information:

#### LABORATORY GROUPS

The experiments in this course require multiple participants for successful completion. Lab groups will consist of four students, though a few groups of three may be required. Students are free to choose their own groups, provided their schedules are compatible enough to permit team-based completion of laboratory reports. *CHME 4150 is a writing-intensive course, and it is essential that everyone contributes to the writing of all three laboratory reports.*
