## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Principles of kinetics, reactor design, and analysis for both homogeneous and heterogeneous (catalytic) systems. Topics include design for multiple reaction networks (optimum selectivity), analysis of simple reactor combinations, and design of isothermal, adiabatic, and optimum temperature profile reactor

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

ed., Nob Hill Publishing (2014).

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The purpose of this course is to introduce undergraduate students to the theory and practice of chemical reactor analysis and design. The MATLAB and ASPEN Plus simulation packages will be used, and many of the homework assignments will require computer-based simulations. The course is taught in a studio format, combining lectures, discussions and computer-based simulations in a single laptop-based classroom. Thus, students should plan on bringing their laptop computers to each class. An up-to-date version of MATLAB should be installed. Students are expected to read the course material before each class and to watch the assigned screencasts, often from learncheme.com: https://learncheme.com/screencasts/kinetics-reactordesign/ before class. Also, there will often be on-line quizzes related to the screencasts, videos and reading material, to be taken before class.  
Courses at Rensselaer are generally expected to require 2-3 hours of outside work for each credit hour. You should expect, therefore, to put in roughly 6-9 hours/week outside the classroom.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Demonstrate an understanding of the modeling, design and operation of batch stirred tankreactors, continuous stirred tank reactors (CSTR), and plug-flow reactors (PFR), and compare the performance characteristics of the different reactor types
- • Analyze rate data using integral and differential methods to find rate constants and reaction order.
- • Demonstrate an understanding of multiple steady-states, reactor stability, and runaways.
- • Demonstrate an understanding of the safety implications of reactor design
- • Use computer packages (such as MATLAB or Aspen Plus) to solve reactor models.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**On-line Quizzes**  
Before most classes there will be a brief on-line quiz that must be completed before class begins. These will often be multiple-choice, true-false, or short-answer questions related to the reading assignment (from the textbook and other sources), screencasts, or videos.  
**Homework Assignments**  
Homework is due at the beginning of class. Late homework, including homework turned in during or after class will be accepted, with a penalty of 20% for each day late. Although an assignment may have several problems, often one will be selected at random for grading. Students may discuss the problems with other students but are not allowed to share solutions (MATLAB m-files, etc.). Violations will be handled in accordance with the student handbook.  
**Projects** There may be one or more projects during the semester, requiring more detailed solutions than a typical homework assignment**. Exams**  
Exams are during class periods. A single crib sheet, in your own handwriting and not photoreduced, will be allowed. The crib sheet must be turned-in with the exam solution. There will be no make-up quizzes without a written excuse from the health center or the dean of students. Homeworks, projects and exams will be returned in class, or in the Coonley Lounge (Ri 120).
