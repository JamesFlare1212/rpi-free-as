## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This 2-credit reading class is for senior undergrad. and grad. students with a background in separations (at least one class). We will focus on reading and reviewing peer-reviewed publications and patents of the major trends of liquid separations using synthetic membranes, monoliths and chromatography. Systems, designs and applications with be discussed. We will study patents, peer-reviewed papers and industrial pamphlets and I will invite a few external speakers. The focus and procedures will be similar to those we used last Fall, where the students present reviews of patents, peer-reviewed papers and industrial pamphlets. Students will review and present their findings for discussion and analysis and will learn the ins and outs of patents. Taking this course may be useful in securing a position the biotechnology and separation industries. There may also be homework periodically.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Summarize how to (i) how different vaccines interact with our immune systems (ii) how to purify nucleic acid vaccines using membranes, monoliths and beads, and (iii) how to understand and read a professional agreement, from a series of visiting (remote and in-person) expert guest lecturers’ presentations
- 2\. Identify and explain the background, results and methods of each topic listed above
- 3\. Summarize key results and conclusions of speaker's presentation via graded homework
- 4\. Discuss possible future directions of the research presented
- 5\. Ability to ask a good question and to present a cogent rational talk with excellent slides
- 6\. Ability to critique a presentation on major limitations, presentation, clarity of each topic.
- 7\. Final class: Summarize what you learned from each visiting speaker’s presentation and explain the reasons for ranking each speaker

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work.
