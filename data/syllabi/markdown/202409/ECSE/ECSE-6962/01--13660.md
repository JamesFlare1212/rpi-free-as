## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Integrated photonics fundamentals, materials and processing, guided wave principles, planar waveguide, coupled mode theory and devices, electro-optic and nonlinear optical switches and complex integrated photonic systems for applications in optical communication, photonic computing, imaging and so on.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Understand the concepts of wave propagation in guided medium, modal solutions, coupled mode theory and phase matching
- 2\. Analyze guided wave propagation properties, loss and dispersion characteristics Design simple passive planar lightwave components such as power splitters
- 3\. Understand the working principles of advanced planar waveguide devices such as ring resonators, MMIs, optical switches.
- 4\. Understand the basics of waveguide fabrication techniques and challenges in optical packaging for an integrated photonic system
- 5\. Use integrated circuits technology (22nm technology node) for the design and characterization of basic building blocks in a transceiver chain
- 6\. Learn to combine electric circuit level and electromagnetic simulations to perform electrical and physical characterization of RF/microwave blocks
- 7\. Review and analyze research papers to study the effect of different technology nodes on the performance of active and passive microwave components

Week 1 Introduction and review of wave equations

- Homework assignments serve as practice problems and will be collected for grading. Evaluation will be based on effort and punctual submission. While group discussion is encouraged, each student must individually submit their own practice problems.
- There are no midterm quizzes or final exams. Instead, there will be two mini-projects during the semester. Students are required to submit individual reports for each mini-project; no presentations are required for mini-projects.
- A final term project will be announced approximately four weeks before the end of the semester. Each student must work on their final term project independently and submit a written report. Presentations are required for the final term project.
- The grading structure for ECSE 4962 and ECSE 6962 is identical. Students from either class will complete the same set of mini-projects. However, the difficulty level of final term projects will be adjusted according to whether the course is at the 4000 or 6000 level.
