## Course Goals

### Goals:

The purpose of the ECSE Enrichment Seminar is to have students consider some important engineering challenges that may shape their future work, with students asking questions like: What type of critical societal issues is engineering involved in? What role might I want to play and how can I contribute? With what kind of long-term role might I play as an industrial or academic engineer? The goal is to have students consider what they might feel when they are older and look back at their careers. Did I enjoy my work? Do I feel I contributed to something of value? Something the world needed? Did I make a difference?

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Be familiar with grand technology and societal challenges facing today’s engineers.
- Conduct research and synthesize a cohesive story based on reliable information.
- Present summaries of technology and societal topics to an audience.
- Be aware of broader issues beyond technology, including ethics, equity, societal impact, policy, etc.
- Develop a broad view of the ECSE domain and potential career paths.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Outcome 1: Gradescope surveys (will be graded on the completeness of responses, answers to quizzes)

Outcome 2: Class presentation slides and report (will be graded on thoroughness, clarity and conciseness, grammar and spelling)

The slides and report should include the following content:

- Problem statement and importance
- Current state of development
- Challenges and approaches
- Future prospect and potential impacts
- References

Outcome 3: Class presentation (will be graded on presentation clarity, time management, Q/A)

Outcome 4: Reflection surveys on guest lectures (will be graded based on demonstrated understanding of the lecture)

Outcome 5: Reflection surveys on guest lectures (completeness)

### Other Course Policies:

AI Policy:  
Publicly available applications driven by generative artificial intelligence (GenAI), such as chatbots (ChatGPT, Google Bard, Microsoft Bing) or image generators (DALL-E 2, Midjourney) have increasingly impressive capabilities, and students have become aware of their utility in the classroom in answering questions, producing example text or images, or other applications. For this class, students will not be penalized for using such tools. However, it is expected that each student will use them responsibly and not employ them as a substitute for learning class concepts or completing one's own class assignments.

Each student is expected to do the following:

- When AI tools are employed to complete an assignment, the student should disclose this with a brief statement at the end of the assignment stating which tool( s) were used and how they were used.
- Responsible AI use should be understood to mean following the same rules for "collaboration" with AI as in collaboration with a human student. This means that the work completed by AI should be disclosed (as discussed above), and also that each student is responsible for developing their own synthesis of the information gained from GenAI rather than simply copying the answers produced.
- Failure to follow these guidelines may result in academic dishonesty sanctions as discussed in the Academic Integrity Policy.
- Many GenAI tools are prone to "hallucinations" - incorrect answers or outdated information - and so a student employing GenAI tools is strongly encouraged to check their responses against a secondary source.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
