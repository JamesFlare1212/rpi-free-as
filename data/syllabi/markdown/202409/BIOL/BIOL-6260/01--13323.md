## Course Goals

### Goals:

This Advanced Cell Biology course is designed to enable students to understand how an organism functions at the cellular level, how the cell functions at the molecular level, and how this functioning is regulated so that cells can adapt to changes in their environment. Students will learn about the fundamental components of the cell (from DNA to protein to organelle), their essential biochemical and organizational characteristics, and how these components operate in both normal and diseased cells. Students will also learn biochemical, structural and mechanical aspects of cell functioning and regulation in normal and diseased cells. In addition, students will learn to critically read current scientific literature. By reading current literature, students will gain knowledge of the practice and presentation of science, as well as learn about cutting-edge techniques and findings.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students who successfully complete this course will: Demonstrate knowledge of the fundamental components of the cell (from DNA to RNA to protein to organelle), their characteristics, and integrate this knowledge into an understanding of the role these components play in both normal and diseased cells. Demonstrate knowledge of biochemical signaling within the cell and apply this knowledge to an understanding of the biochemical aspect of cell function and regulation. Demonstrate knowledge of the structural and mechanical elements of cell functioning (including adhesion and motility) in tissue and integrate this knowledge into an understanding of the structural and mechanical constraints in normal and diseased cells. Demonstrate knowledge of experimental procedures used to derive cell biological scientific knowledge, the advantages and limitations of experimental techniques, and develop ability to interpret data derived from experiments. Demonstrate the ability to read, understand, and summarize primary scientific literature in the field of cell biology and integrate knowledge gained in the course in critical evaluation of this literature. Develop ability to work in groups with colleagues to research a topic in cell biology (both broadly and in depth) and coordinate public scientific presentations. Develop ability to identify current state-of-the-art research results, interpret the presented data, and explain and communicate this scientific literature to a group via a short presentation. Demonstrate the ability to identify novel scientific techniques and methods used by active researchers, explain how the technology was developed and is currently being employed, and present this summary to a group of colleagues.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Learning Outcomes with be assessed by several measures, namely: (1) Exams (50% of total grade, 3 total exams during the semester plus one comprehensive final take home exam, with the top 3 counted towards the final grade), (2) Homework assignments (top 5 graded assignments out of a possible 12 will be counted towards 15% of the total grade, (3) Scientific Paper Presentation (15% of total grade), (4) Technique/methodology presentation (15% of total grade), and (5) Class participation (5% of total grade).
