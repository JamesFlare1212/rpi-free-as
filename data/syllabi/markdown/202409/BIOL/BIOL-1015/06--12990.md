## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

## Course Description

This is an introductory course in Biology. It is intended for undergraduates, both non-majors and majors. It serves as the first course for life science majors and for students interested in the health professions.

Laboratory activities: Students generate hypotheses, and share their ideas on how to test their hypotheses, within interactive in-person mini-groups. Hands-on wet and dry labs include basic cell and molecular biological skills. Project-based activities may rely on the use of Artificial Intelligence for data collection. Activities include a basic introduction to scientific computing in interactive virtual environments using Python and R.

Laboratory Goals: Students will gain biological literacy by applying the scientific method. Students will collect various types of biological data, such as whole organismal or cellular or molecular data. Students use quantitative tools, and simulations, as well as artificial intelligence, and applications on their devices. Students develop data dexterity, and analytical thinking skills while learning basic fundamental laboratory skills. This is a D1 Data Intensive Course.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

## Course Content:

The course content is broadly divided into three modules:

1. Biostatistics and Quantitative Biology - Ecology
2. Bioinformatics and Cladistics - Evolution
3. Cell Biology/Molecular Biology - Genetics and Medicine.

However, holistic learning is emphasized throughout the semester.

The Scientific Method is applied to understand fundamental concepts, including the following introductory topics:

- Cell Biology - Mitosis, Meiosis, and the Cell Cycle.
- Molecular Biology - DNA replication, Protein Synthesis, and Regulation; DNA extraction, PCR, Gel Electrophoresis.
- Microbiology - Prokaryotic (versus Eukaryotic) cell size, shape & morphology.
- Biochemistry - Structures of amino acids, nucleotides, atoms and bonds, polymers. For example: Virology - SarsCov2 genome & spike protein data visualization using PDB.
- Genetics - Mendelian Patterns, probabilities, ratios, Chi Square Analysis using corn purple/yellow plump/wrinkled mutant kernels or Fruit Flies.
- Ecology, Biodiversity and Conservation - Aquatic Ecosystems by computing EPT, H, J indices; Student's TTEST. Epidemiology, for example, R0, vaccine critical threshold, Hardy Weinberg Allele frequency and other calculations/topics.
- Evolution, Phylogeny - Cladistics, Bioinformatics, Using Public Databases and Bioinformatics Software.
- Medicine - Drug/vaccine design and genetic engineering using Simbio’s simulations.
- Computational Biology: Excel, Introduction to Jupyter Notebooks with R Statistical Language and Python using free Supercomputer access.

Students will study data *in silico*, *in vitro* or *in vivo*, *in situ* or *ex situ*. Students will use Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools, iNaturalist, LLAMA, Merlin, and others. Some labs may be announced as optional during semesters impacted by supply chains/resource limitations/TA support or for other reasons announced in labs.
