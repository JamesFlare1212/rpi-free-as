## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Research in biomolecular conformation and interactions. options include in vitro biophysical studies or in vivo fluorescence microscopy studies

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

None

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Provide students with laboratory skills and independence in research.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Pressure-based mapping of protein conformational landscapes  
The molecular mechanisms of transcriptional adaptation to pressure  
Size homeostasis in budding yeast

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Basic biochemical or microbiological laboratory skills
- Capacity to analyze and interpret experimental results
- Understanding of the methods and instruments used in the research project
- Ability to organize experiments and results
- Ability to clearly present written or oral summaries of the project questions, goals and results.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Presentations of results at group meetings.  
Undergraduate research fair

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Assiduity in the work  
Understanding of the project  
Presentation quality

## Course Calendar

### Calendar Details:

As a research experience the calendar is to come to the lab for the number of weekly hours of the credits taken and to participate in the ongoing research on the chosen project.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Obviously attendance is required. If virtual learning is a necessity then participation will be limited to data analysis that can be carried out remotely.

### Other Course Policies:

Students must complete safety training prior to carrying out experiments

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work.
