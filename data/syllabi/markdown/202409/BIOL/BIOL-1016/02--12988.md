## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The goal of this laboratory course is to learn about biology through computational data analysis. Students will be introduced to a wide variety of real-world biological data sets. Students will learn how to manage, visualize, analyze, and interpret biological data. This class culminates in a collaborative research project. Students enrolled in a major other than biology and students with an interest in computational biology are particularly encouraged to register for this course. Prerequisites/Corequisites: Corequisites: BIOL 1010.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will demonstrate the ability to develop and execute a plan to perform exploratory data analysis for a wide variety of biological data formats.
- Students will demonstrate the ability to create clear and professional data visualizations and provide an accurate interpretation of figures (and associated statistics).
- Students will apply their ability to effectively summarize biological data by working collaboratively with their peers to clearly define a research question, analyze data appropriately, and create and deliver a clear and professional team presentation that engages the audience in discussion.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Data Labs: The first nine labs teach fundamental data skills. Students will be evaluated on their ability to 1) reproduce and 2) modify code to generate summary statistics, create data visualizations, and interpret results. Students will also learn how to work collaboratively and convey scientific results via presentations and / or posters. Each week students will hand in a word document with their results form the lab including figures, captions, statistics, and interpretations See detailed schedule below for a list of topics. Students will be assessed on their ability to 1) manipulate data and prepare for analysis, 2) create professional data visualizations, 3) interpret data, and 4) communicate their findings via figure captions and key messages.  
Culminating Project: The last portion of the course gives students the opportunity to dive deeper into a data set and perform their own analysis while working as part of a small team. Students will 1) generate research questions, 2) analyze data and create figures to answer these questions, and 3) convey this information in either an oral presentation or poster. Students will be assessed on 1) their ability to work cohesively as a team (via instructor observation and peer feedback), 2) Their ability to manipulate and summarize data (by handing in their work to the instructor at key check points), and 3) Their ability to present this information via poster or oral presentation (graded by instructor and feedback provided by peers).  
Class participation will be graded by attendance for weeks 1-9. During the final project, group members will evaluate the contributions of each group member.  
Homework will be limited since it is a laboratory course. Exceptions include making up labs due to absence or needing additional time to complete laboratory assignments.

Statement on Ai-content generation tools  
Scientists commonly use AI-content generation tools in their work, especially when programing in R. In this course, using AI-content generation tools is permitted when it is used according to the below criteria. In this course, neglecting to follow these requirements may be considered academic dishonesty. Any such use must be appropriately acknowledged and cited. It is each student’s responsibility to assess the validity and applicability of any AI output that is submitted; you bear the final responsibility. Violations of this policy will be considered academic misconduct.

I envision AI-content generation tools will be most helpful when completing the second part of each lab where you need to apply the skills you were just taught on a modified data set. As a general rule, if you find yourself seeking help online, you should first revisit your class notes. It is very likely a similar problem was discussed and solved in class. Next, you should discuss the problem with a peer, mentor, UGM, TA, or Instructor. For each assignment, you are required to include a paragraph that explains what AI content- generation tool you used, the prompts you used to generate the content, and a proper APA style citation.

Example citation:

OpenAI. (2023). ChatGPT (Mar 14 version) [Large language model]. https://chat.openai.com/chat

We draw your attention to the fact that different classes could implement different AI policies, and it is the student’s responsibility to conform to expectations for each course.
