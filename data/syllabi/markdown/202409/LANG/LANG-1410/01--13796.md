## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is designed for students who do not have prior knowledge or learning experience in the Mandarin Chinese language. It will provide students with fundamental skills in listening, speaking, reading, and writing in Chinese. Students will have opportunity to participate in interactive communication during class as well as through the Chinese Mentor Program meetings and activities. Together with the instructor, students will create and enjoy a friendly, supportive, and collaborative Chinese learning community in which everyone can study at ease, support each other, and grow to reach their own maximum potential.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

In addition to developing language skills, this course will provide students with the opportunity to deepen their comprehension of Chinese society, people, history, tradition, and culture through a variety of interactive mediums by going beyond the limitations of the textbook. These include,

\* Exploration of topics related to Chinese people’s everyday lives and ways of thinking

\* Exposure to selected Chinese classical poems, folk songs, tongue twisters, or cross-talk art

\* Presentation on Chinese culture

\* Discussion of Chinese sayings or idioms

\* Discovery of the origin and evolution of Chinese characters

\* Watching relevant video clips

\* Enjoying 1-2 Chinese movies

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Understand and use Mandarin Pinyin (including initials, finals, and tones)
- 2\. Recognize and write around 150 Chinese characters
- 3\. Read and use about 300 words (with their collocations)
- 4\. Communicate on a range of topics: a. Exchange greetings (including how Chinese address one another with their proper titles and how they call a person by his/her full name) b. Express and reply to appreciation and apology c. Say goodbye to people d. Introduce oneself or others (including recognizing Chinese names, and foreign names in Chinese) e. Ask about someone’s nationality f. Talk about family members and pets, numbers, and proper measure words for different nouns in Chinese, and ask and answer questions pertaining to one’s age, profession, and language ability g. Express time, dates, days of the week, and months (including understanding Chinese Lunar and Solar calendars) h. Talk about daily life schedules (do what at what time) i. Make an appointment; talk about some sports and hobbies (like or dislike) j. Wish someone “Happy Birthday” k. Select and give a gift that is culturally appropriate in a particular context l. Inquire and express preferences for food and drink m. Discuss various dishes and their flavors n. Order at a Chinese restaurant, offer to pay for a meal

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The assessment will cover: Attendance, Participation, and Class Activities (including the required meetings with Chinese mentors); Textbook, Workbook, and Other Assignments; Reading Fluency Checks and Dialogue Performances; Language and Culture Projects; Quizzes (Vocabulary, Pinyin, Sentence, and Grammar); Final Exam OR Final Writing Assignment. Each of them has specific and detailed instruction, requirements, and grading rubrics which will be explained and handed out to students when it is assigned.

- (1) Important exercises in both the textbook and workbook will be assigned with some additional work related (if necessary).
- (2) Listening exercises, words used in sentences, interviewing native Chinese speakers, news report in Chinese, authentic reading, and/or composition writing may be assigned.
- ***(3)*** ***Workbook exercises need to be finished in Characters, not only in Pinyin, except for Chinese I, Unit 1 Lesson 1. (Please follow the detailed instructions in the Box folder). The speaking part also needs to be finished (just write down your answers in characters, no need to submit an additional audio recording).***
- (4) Listening to the recordings and repeating sentence by sentence until you can recognize all characters and read the dialogues in each lesson fluently and accurately.
- (5) Dialogue Performance

Students are also expected to apply what they have learned from each unit to create and perform/act out their own dialogues without reading from scripts. The dialogue performance for each unit has a specific due date that is clearly listed in the syllabus.

The first two units are intended for practice purposes. Any student who performs the dialogue without reading from scripts can receive full credit of 5 points (Scripts are not required). If a student does not complete one dialogue, their final grade for the Dialogue Performance assignment will be reduced by 5 points (2 dialogues, 10 points).

**The last two units are intended for students to choose for grading purposes. They can select either unit, thoroughly revise the scripts, ACT OUT the dialogue (IN CLASS) to the best of their ability (which will be recorded in class), and then submit the scripts to our Box folder before or during the class (not after).** If they are satisfied with their performance for the third unit, they will not need to complete another one for the fourth unit. If they are not satisfied, they will have one more chance.

- (6) **Presentations on Chinese language and culture need to be well prepared and well revised. All students are expected to use what they have learned in the class (vocabulary, grammar, expression, and cultural information) and are very much encouraged to search for and try out some new things**. All scripts should be well polished and sound like natural and authentic Chinese if you strive for a high grade. And you will need to PRESENT, not READ OFF the scripts (the same for the dialogue or skit performances) Students should prepare and revise their scripts, rehearse several times to gain fluency, and perform or present naturally and professionally.
- (7) Assignment submission

It is the instructor’s belief that students are responsible adults and are to be treated as such.

All students must do their best to submit their assignments in advance or on time if they expect to receive full credit.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

- (1) Attendance is mandatory. Learning a language is very different from learning in other domains, which may be done by reading the materials yourself. Every language practice class directly builds upon the previous ones. Any absence means the loss of an invaluable opportunity to participate in group exercises and practice communicating with others, and this loss will continue to negatively affect future classes. In other words, if you miss one class, you actually lose much more than the two-hour period itself.

- (2) Due to the unpredictable nature of life, everyone is granted TWO absences that will not be counted against the final grade ("free pass"). The instructor will not attempt to distinguish between "excused" and "unexcused" absences, as it is challenging to consistently evaluate individual circumstances. Despite my best efforts to be considerate, students may always find reasons to request exceptions.

Every student may use these two “free” absences for doctor’s appointments, illnesses, sports waivers, job interviews, religious reasons, car trouble, family issues, funerals, alarm clock issues, and so on. Because the situations cannot be predicted, it is wise to save the two “free” absences for when you have to use them.

***Although the first two absences do not count negatively toward a student’s grade, the third absence becomes costly. Upon the third absence, the final grade will be dropped from A to B, B to C, C to D, or D to F. In other words, students having SIX absences (both excused and unexcused) for the entire semester will result in an automatic grade of F for the course.***

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
