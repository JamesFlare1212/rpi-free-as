## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a continuation of Chinese I, a course for the standard modern Chinese language (Mandarin). Students learn more Chinese characters and words, reach a total of near 500 characters and 650 words, and use more complicated grammatical structures, including some complement phrases and topic-comment sentences. In sum, students will learn more in all four aspects—listening, speaking, reading, and writing—presented in Chinese I.

**Note**: In some cases, students who have had some Chinese courses, study abroad experience, or heritage background may take this course with the instructor's diagnosis. Based on the pre-course assessment, students may be required to do extra or substitute assignments.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

During the semester, students will engage in a variety of communicative tasks, both inside and outside the classroom. Topics will include describing daily life, shopping and making payments, as well as discussing travel plans and academics. The main focus will be on enhancing listening and speaking skills, with some attention given to writing. Furthermore, students will have the chance to experience cutting-edge learning environments that integrate new technologies and language learning theories. Throughout the course, there will be a strong emphasis on exploring different aspects of Chinese culture.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students who successfully complete this course will acquire the following competencies and be able to perform tasks pertinent to Novice Mid level of proficiency described by the American Council on the Teaching of Foreign Languages: 1. Interpersonal Communication: Students will be able to communicate on very familiar topics using a variety of words and phrases that they have practiced and memorized. For example, negotiate prices; ask and answer questions about vacation plans. 2. Presentational Communication: Students will be able to (1) present information about themselves and some other very familiar topics using various words, phrases, and memorized expressions, and (2) write lists and memorized phrases on familiar topics. For example, describe relative locations; express interest in something or academic life. 3. Interpretive Communication: Students will be able to (1) recognize a few memorized words and phrases when he/she hears them spoken, and (2) recognize and identify a few memorized words and phrases when he/she reads. For example, receive directions; understand and use different denominations and amounts of money. 4. Cultural Awareness: Students will be to (1) use culturally appropriate gestures, and formulaic expression in highly practice applications (e.g., phone greetings, bargaining), (2) show awareness of the most obvious cultural differences (e.g., traditional architecture), (3) list Western chains in China and associate the chains in Western countries.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

***Participation & Performance***: Language learning involves using the vocabulary and grammar you learned each week to interact with others for all purposes of life in a target language society. Active engagement is a must. Some assignments require recording your voice and role-plays. You receive full credit for excused class absences and can schedule make-up work for the lack—no make-up for ***unexcused absences and lateness***.

***Routine Tasks***: Daily practice are necessary and integral for successful Chinese learning. *For the best effect of proficiency-based language learning**, assignments of this course are spread out over the week*** so that you practice 20-30 minutes daily. Check out each new week's tasks/dues on LMS. There are ***THREE (3) due times*** *for the weekly routine tasks*: (1) Before the classes on Tuesday and Friday, and before Sunday Midnight.

***Projects***: There will be several projects in this course that *require self-regulation to plan and monitor the completion throughout the semester*. Check out the details in the "Project Instruction and Rubric" folder in the LMS Course Documents.

***Quizzes & Exams***: This course has two exams and three unit review tests. Each exam consists of two parts: written and oral. Unit tests are comprehensive practices for the course contents. Follow the exam study guides to prepare for the exams.**\*\*\* Note**: If you need **extended time on exams** or other forms of accommodation, please send me **a memo issued by Disability Services for Students at the beginning of the semester**.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**P****olicy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism in this course**

It is VERY EASY to spot cheating in a foreign language course. Students are not permitted to turn in work done by someone else. Getting assistance from a native speaker on assignments is OK, but your final product must be your own work as it is easy to identify the excessive structures beyond your level of proficiency. Besides, **using a WEB TRANSLATE (e.g., Google Translate) for translation other than vocabulary or phrases is not permitted**.

The first time I suspect cheating, I warn the student and tell them why I suspect (e.g., use of non-course grammar or vocabulary or structures beyond your proficiency level), and I take off 50% of the grade. If it's very obvious, I do not give a warning. I will just give a zero. If there is a subsequent infraction, the student will receive a grade of "F" for the course or be reported to the Dean's Office.
