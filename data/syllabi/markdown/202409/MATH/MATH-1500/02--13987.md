## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- •basic and advanced symbol manipulation skills,
- •the ability to convert between calculus concepts and their graphical, numerical, and symbolic representations,
- •the ability to apply calculus to selected problems in business, economics, and sciences
- •the ability to apply certain fundamental theorems, basic concepts, and rules from calculus to solve symbolic and graphical problems.
- •the ability to present problems related to the theory of calculus and its applications to business, economics, and sciences disciplines

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

All learning outcomes will be measured using Pearson homework, Gradescope Problems, recitation quizzes, and exams.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Your final grade will be computed as follows:

25% Pearson Homework assigned each class (lowest assignment grade dropped at the end of the semester)

5% Gradescope Problems assigned each class (lowest score dropped at the end of the semester)

10% Quizzes given during each recitation except the weeks following an exam (lowest quiz grade dropped at the end of the semester)

60% Exams (3 exams will ALL count at 20% each)

Letter grades will be assigned according to the following scheme (there is NO rounding or curving of grades at the end of the semester): A 93-100; A- 90-92.99999; B+ 87-89.99999; B 83-86.99999; B- 80-82.99999; C+ 76-79.99999; C 72-75.99999; C- 67-71.99999; D+ 62-66.99999; D 57-61.99999; F 0-56.99999

Exams: Exam problems will consist of problems for which the students must show all their work, as well as multiple-choice, multiple-select, true/false, and fill-in-the-blank questions. Exam problems for which the students must show all their work are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples. Missed exams may only be made up if a student has notified the instructor prior to the exam (in the rare case of an emergency, students may have until midnight of the exam day to notify the instructor). Exams must be made up within a week of the date the exam was given unless a medical excuse can be provided. The 3rd and last exam will be given on the last day of class, therefore its makeup exam will be given during the scheduled final exam slot. There is no final exam for this course. Exams will be uploaded to Gradescope by the instructor, where students will be able to check their grades and feedback. It is the responsibility of the students to review their exams for any discrepancies between their work and points awarded as described by the rubric.

Recitations: you will have recitation on Mondays during which you will take a short quiz and practice more examples with Jean. There are no quiz makeups for any reason. Instead, the lowest quiz grade will be dropped at the end of the semester. Quizzes will be on the material covered the week before recitation and will be graded using Gradescope to provide some feedback before the exam.

Gradescope Problems: Each class, I will assign some short problem(s) resembling short answer exam questions for practice. Students can work on these in groups or individually, then submit them using Gradescope. Students can submit their work from the time we start working on them in class until midnight the following day. After that, submissions will not be accepted. Submissions will be graded as they would be on exams to give some feedback on how you present your work.

Homework: Pearson homework assignments will be due twice weekly on Tuesdays and Fridays at 11:59 PM and will be graded automatically by the system. Students may submit these assignments late up to three days after the original deadline, but a 50% penalty will be applied to the score of the questions submitted after the due date. No other due date extensions will be granted for any reason. To further compensate for this, the lowest homework assignment score will be dropped at the end of the semester.

As stated under Exams, exam problems are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples. This means that you must be able to correctly solve and show all your work as well as fully understand your answer to each homework problem and lecture example to consider yourself well-prepared.

Change to the grading scheme may be made at the discretion of the instructor.

### Other Course Policies:

Calculator Policy

Calculators will not be allowed on exams. Problems will be designed so that this does not create an issue. Occasionally, a calculator may be required to complete a homework problem, but students should not rely on calculators for simple arithmetic or trigonometric calculations.

Classroom Decorum

Phones may not be used during class and should be silent. Computers and tablets may not be used for anything other than taking notes. Students who are unable to courteously comply with these policies may be asked to leave the room. If you are on your phone or laptop during the lecture, then you are probably missing most of the lecture, including practice questions, common errors or hints that may be given about graded assignments, quizzes, or exams.

Health and Safety Protocols

Students are required to follow the health and safety protocols of RPI, which are subject to change throughout the semester. Please be courteous to others when feeling sick. Consider wearing a mask, covering your face when sneezing and/or coughing, and washing your hands more frequently.

For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams must be the student’s own. A student may not change an answer on an exam before submitting it for an appeal. In addition, no calculators, computers, tablets, open books, open notes, phones, headphones, or smartwatches may be used during exams. Talking or communicating with other students in any way during exams is also prohibited. The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the exam. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
