## Course Content

### Content Details:

Topics

- – Formulation, analysis and implementation of Runge-Kutta methods, linear multi-step methods, and some related methods, for non-stiff and stiff initial-value problems; symplectic methods for Hamiltonian systems
- – Other possible topics, led by students via team-projects: spectral deferred correction (SDC), parareal algorithm, time integrators with mixed precision, time integrators for stochastic differential equations etc

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- The students will ⋄ be able to perform numerical analysis for consistency, stability, convergence and other prop- erties of the methods;
- be able to implement numerical methods efficiently and robustly, and more importantly, to be able to interpret /analyze the observations and results, and to extract useful information and draw meaningful conclusions;
- be able to evaluate/compare various methods, such as regarding their accuracy, efficiency, reliability, either numerically or mathematically;
- be able to choose or to design numerical methods to solve a given problem
- be able to communicate effectively, work responsibly, and make meaningful contributions in a collaborative setting.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Homework: Homework will be assigned regularly and graded. The work you hand in is expected to be clearly written and well organized.

Team-project (report & presentation): To expand the learning experience, there will be a team-project. Topics and references will be suggested around weeks #5-#7. You are welcome to suggest/propose topics as long as they are not part of your past and ongoing research projects. Your proposed topics, along with the suggested references, will be reviewed by the instructor, and, once approved, they will be added to the topic collection for the entire class to consider. Teams will be formed based on your preferences for the topics and potential teammates, and will be coordinated and finalized by the instructor. Once your team is formed and a project is picked, your presentation will be scheduled. Your team will have 3-4 weeks to work on the project, and the report will be due within 10 days after the presentation. The contribution of each team member needs to be stated explicitly in both your presentation and the report.
