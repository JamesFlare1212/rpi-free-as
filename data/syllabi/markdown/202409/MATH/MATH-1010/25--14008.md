## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate basic and advanced symbol manipulation skills.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to convert between calculus concepts and their graphical, numerical, and symbolic representations.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to apply calculus concepts to selected problems in science and engineering.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to apply certain fundamental theorems, basic concepts, and rules from calculus to solve symbolic and graphical problems.
- Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate the ability to present problems related to the theory of calculus and its applications to science and engineering disciplines.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Your final grade will be computed as follows:

15% Achieve Homework assigned each class (at least 2 lowest assignment grades will get dropped at the end of the semester)

5% Gradescope Problems assigned each class (at least 2 lowest scores will get dropped at the end of the semester)

80% Exams (4 exams will ALL count at 20% each)

Letter grades will be assigned according to the following scheme (there is NO rounding or curving of grades at the end of the semester): A 93-100; A- 90-92.99999; B+ 87-89.99999; B 83-86.99999; B- 80-82.99999; C+ 76-79.99999; C 72-75.99999; C- 67-71.99999; D+ 62-66.99999; D 57-61.99999; F 0-56.99999

Exams: Exam problems will consist of problems for which the students must show all their work, as well as multiple-choice, multiple-select, true/false, and fill-in-the-blank questions. Exam problems for which the students must show all their work are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples. Answers to odd-numbered textbook problems can be found on Achieve at the end of the E-book. Missed exams may only be made up if a student has notified the instructor prior to the exam (in the rare case of an emergency, students may have until midnight of the exam day to notify the instructor). The exam may only be made up during the Wednesday test block on the week that follows that exam or receive a grade of 0. The 4th and last exam will be given on the last day of class, therefore its makeup exam will be given during the scheduled final exam slot. There is NO final exam for this course. Exams will be uploaded to Gradescope by the instructor, where students will be able to check their grades and feedback. It is the responsibility of the students to review their exams for any discrepancies between their work and points awarded as described by the rubric.

Gradescope Problems: Each class, Gradescope Problem(s) will be assigned for practice and feedback. Students can work on these in groups or individually, then submit them using Gradescope. Students can submit their work from the time we start working on them in class until midnight the following day. After that, submissions will not be accepted. Since late submissions are not accepted, a few Gradescope Problem grades will be dropped at the end of the semester. Submissions will be graded as they would be on exams to give some feedback on how you present your work. These are also great problems to review before the exam. Solutions are posted on LMS after the due date.

Homework: Achieve homework assignments will be due twice weekly on Tuesdays and Fridays at 11:59 PM and will be graded automatically by the system. Students may submit these assignments late up to two days after the original deadline, but a penalty will be applied to the score. A 10% penalty will apply to assignments submitted the day after the deadline and a 20% penalty will apply to assignments submitted two days after the deadline. No other due date extensions will be granted for any reason. To further compensate for this, several homework assignment scores will be dropped at the end of the semester.

Additional problems will be assigned from the textbook and will be posted on LMS under each week. They will not be collected nor graded, however, success in the course depends on successful completion of all homework, both Achieve homework and textbook homework. As stated under Exams, exam problems are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples. This means that you must be able to correctly solve and show all your work, as well as fully understand your answer to each homework problem and lecture example to consider yourself well-prepared.

Change to the grading scheme may be made at the discretion of the instructor.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are responsible for all material covered in class, so attendance of every lecture is strongly recommended and encouraged. If a student is absent, they should get the class notes posted each week on LMS and keep up with the homework assignments as assigned on Achieve and LMS, as well as make sure to submit their Gradescope problem(s). The best way to stay on top of the course is to come to class and pay attention.

### Other Course Policies:

Calculator Policy

Calculators will not be allowed on exams. Problems will be designed so that this does not create an issue. Occasionally, a calculator may be required to complete a homework problem, but students should not rely on calculators for simple arithmetic or trigonometric calculations.

Classroom Decorum

Phones may not be used during class and should be silent. Computers and tablets may not be used for anything other than taking notes. Students who are unable to courteously comply with these policies may be asked to leave the room. If you are on your phone or laptop during the lecture, then you are probably missing most of the lecture, including practice questions, common errors or hints that may be given about graded assignments, quizzes, or exams.

Health and Safety Protocols

Students are required to follow the health and safety protocols of RPI, which are subject to change throughout the semester. Please be courteous to others when feeling sick. Consider wearing a mask, covering your face when sneezing and/or coughing, and washing your hands more frequently.

Visitors

Visitors are prohibited during all class meetings. Only students who are registered for the class should be present in the classroom. Students may only attend class meetings at the time and place listed on their schedule on SIS unless cleared by the instructor.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities (pp. 16–18). In addition, no calculators, computers, tablets, open books, open notes, phones, headphones, or smartwatches may be used during exams. The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the exam. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.

### Additional Course Information:
