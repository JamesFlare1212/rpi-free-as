## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- (1) Students will learn the importance of proper experiment set-up, including pre-laboratory activities, note taking, data analysis and reporting of results.
- (2) Students will show competence with chemical laboratory techniques including mass measurements, volume measurements, solution preparation, dilution, titration, instrumental methods and synthetic techniques. Spectroscopic methods will be used; these include Infrared and Nuclear Magnetic Resonance spectroscopies. Separation methods of thin layer/column chromatography and gas chromatography may be used.
- (3) Students will show competence in handling and processing laboratory (experimental) data.
- (4) Students will be able to search for relevant information in the chemical literature.

**Re-grading Policy**

If at any time you feel that any of your work was graded incorrectly, please don't hesitate to bring it to our attention. If you dispute a particular grade, please first discuss it with the TA or instructor who assigned the grade. If questions still remain, please contact your course instructor (Dr. Kirova-Snover) using the following protocol:

- (1) On a separate piece of paper, write a short note explaining what writing or work you would like to be reassessed, and the reason why you think the original grade was incorrectly assigned.
- (2) Attach the note to the original assignment and give both to your instructor (Dr. Kirova-Snover) either in person or by putting it in her mailbox.
- (3) You will be contacted by e-mail within one week with the regrade decision. You may also be emailed if it is necessary to clarify something in your note.

Regrades will never be decided while the student involved is in the room. That creates unfair pressure on instructors to make a quick decision. Also remember to not make any changes to the assignment itself before resubmission. Any change will be considered a violation of academic integrity and will be penalized accordingly. **Any time you ask for a re-grade on completed work, we reserve the right to regrade any portion of the work, not just the part requested.**

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance and Make-up Opportunities**

Attendance is mandatory at all recitations and laboratory meetings. If you are absent without a valid excuse, you will receive a grade of zero for that week's experiment. If your absence is due to a foreseeable event (such as an athletic event), you must submit an excuse through the Student Experience Office before the date of your absence. If your absence is due to an unforeseeable event (such as illness or family emergency) on the day of the recitation or lab meeting, you must provide proof of the event through your Class Dean within one week.

If you miss a recitation and do not have a valid excuse, you may not complete the associated experiment, and will receive a zero. If you miss a recitation and do have a valid excuse, you are still expected to complete the experiment. Schedule a meeting with the instructor in order to have an informal “make-up” recitation before your assigned lab section.

You will generally not be allowed to make-up missed laboratories this semester. The lab sections are all full. Thus, even with a valid excuse, there is not enough room in the labs to permit make-up labs. Any departures from this policy can only be approved by a course instructor. An absence coupled with a valid excuse will result in that week's experiment not counting toward your final grade.

All excuses will be handled by your Class Dean. Take documentation of the reason of your absence to the dean's office, 4600 Academy Hall. The dean's office will then contact the course instructor.

### Other Course Policies:

**Safety**

Safety is the most important goal of every laboratory exercise! Never complete an experimental step if you do not know what you are doing, why you are doing it, and how to minimize any risks involved. When in doubt, ask!

Safety rules must be observed in this laboratory course at all times. Any of the laboratory staff can immediately dismiss a student who poses a danger to themselves or others. This includes proper attire. A student who is not “prepared” to do the experiment definitely poses a safety hazard. Ignoring the safety rules also will be grounds for dismissal.

Remember that all injuries must be reported immediately to a TA or lab supervisor, and that cleanliness/ tidiness and proper disposal of leftover chemicals are part of good safety practices. No student should leave the Walker Lab facilities during the lab periods, without getting permission from a lab supervisor. Student checkout entails a TA-approved laboratory bench and hood cleanup followed by a TA locking his/her equipment drawer.

For more about laboratory safety, see pages 13-30 in the Lehman text.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

Each submitted report must be your own work and not copied from, or shared with, other students except when specifically authorized by your instructor.

In this course, academic dishonesty includes “fudging” laboratory data such as weights, melting points, etc. The fabrication of scientific data and information is an egregious violation of the underpinnings of scientific inquiry and the scientific method. Students are also expected to carry out all experimentation and data analysis independently, i.e., without referencing information collected by or conclusions deduced by others.

A violation of this policy will be treated as outlined below.  
**Penalties for Academic Integrity Violations**

A first instance of academic dishonesty will result in a grade of 0% on the experiment during which the dishonesty took place and the incident will be reported to the Dean of Students Office. A second instance of academic dishonesty will result in automatic failure of the course without option to withdraw, and again the incident will be reported to the Dean of Students Office. Procedures for appealing an academic integrity decision can be found at http://doso.rpi.edu/update.do? artcenterkey=676.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

**Succeeding in This Course**

The purpose of the organic chemistry laboratory is to demonstrate the concepts learned in lecture, to expand your chemical intuition, to give you opportunities to practice scientific communication, and to help you develop practical skills used that will be useful in either industrial or academic careers.

Some hints and tips for succeeding in the Organic Laboratory:

- Read the experiment in your textbook before it is introduced in recitation. This way you will already know which chemical concepts or experimental protocols you do not fully understand, and you can pay extra attention to those points during the recitation. If they are not covered, ask!

- Watch the videos illustrating new laboratory operations as recommended by your instructor during recitation each week. These videos will be posted on the LMS. Also, read about each new operation in the Lehman text (Part V). This will help you visualize the operation before you perform it in the lab.

- Complete your pre-lab write-up several days before your scheduled laboratory section so that you have time to ask questions of TAs or instructor. However, it is important that the goals and procedures of the experiment are fresh in your mind when you enter the lab. So whenever you write your pre-lab, take time to carefully review what you will be doing less than 24 hours before your laboratory section. This will save you both time and stress in the laboratory.

- Don’t hesitate to ask questions! Losing a technique point or two for incessant question-asking is far preferable to being unsafe or completing procedural steps incorrectly.

- When studying for the quiz, make sure you understand the experimental purpose, the procedure you followed, and the chemical concepts, reactions, and mechanisms involved. (If you already finished your post-lab report, then you have already forced yourself to review these things. Re-read your report as part of your review!). To assess your preparation, practice answering the study questions introduced during recitation.
