## Course Goals

### Goals:

Course Objectives:

- To expose students to diverse current trends in chemical research through weekly seminars given by experts in the field.

- Provide important professional development content through several presentations by faculty or guest speaker.

- Provides a forum for current graduate students in the Dept. of Chemistry & Chemical Biology to present their own research findings as part of the requirements for the Ph.D. degree.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Through being exposed to a diversity of seminar speakers and/or by presenting a seminar on their own work, graduate students are expected to communicate scientifically at the level of a professional chemist, consistent with one of the outcomes of our Ph.D. program. Attendance to seminars, engagement in the Q&A session, and presenting their own graduate work will serve as evidence students have achieved this learning outcome.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Course Assessment:

Chemistry Ph.D. candidates in their first and second year and other students registered in the course must attend all seminars in-person. Attendance will be taken at each meeting. Students who miss more than two (2) seminars will not receive a passing grade.

Additionally, Ph.D. candidates in the Dept. of Chemistry and Chemical Biology must present a seminar on their thesis material during their third or fourth year in the graduate program. If you are in your 4th year of graduate studies and have not given a departmental seminar, contact the instructor to schedule a date for your seminar.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grading Criteria and Breakdown:

This course is graded as Satisfactory or Unsatisfactory based on attendance at weekly seminars. Missing more than two seminars will result in an “Unsatisfactory” grade for the semester.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Class Attendance:

Attendance at the scheduled weekly seminars is required. The seminar class will only be held when there is a seminar scheduled. The schedule for the class will be distributed to all members of the department and registered students at the beginning of the semester and whenever there are updates or changes. Potential conflicts with the seminar dates must be resolved with the class instructor at least one week in advance of the date. Except for verified illness or family emergencies, it will not be possible to make up the class.

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Policy regarding academic dishonesty:

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. The following is considered academic dishonesty:

- Signing the attendance sheet for another student.

- Multiple cases of signing the attendance sheet and leaving early missing most of the seminar.

- Multiple cases of arriving towards the end of the seminar and signing the attendance sheet.

- A student giving a seminar presents the work of others as their own work.
