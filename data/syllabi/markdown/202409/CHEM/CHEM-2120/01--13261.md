## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Students will be able to search the chemical literature for necessary information 2. Students will be able to recognize the components of and be able to write a laboratory report and incorporate reference citations (where appropriate) into their written documents 3. Students will be able to use fundamental chemistry laboratory instrumentation, including analytical balances and pH meters, and analytical glassware, including volumetric flasks, burets and pipettes. 4. Students will gain an understanding of and be able to effectively perform experimental techniques, such as titrations, the method of standard addition and the method of internal standards. 5. Students will gain a basic understanding of the use, function, and applications of experimental instrumentation such as Fourier Transform Infrared Spectrometers (FTIR), Gas Chromatographs (GC), and Scanning UltraViolet-Visible Spectrometers.

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

**Laboratory Report Submission Policy**

Laboratory reports must be handed in one week upon the conclusion of the experiment. You must turn in a type written report via experiment submission folder on the LMS course site. Any report that is handed in late will be penalized 1 point per day (including weekend days.) Any lab report that is submitted more than one week after the due date will not be accepted and you will receive a zero for the experiment.

**Absences**

You are expected to attend all laboratory sessions as scheduled. If your absence is not excused, you will not be given an opportunity to make up any laboratory time you missed. Excused absences (with written excuse from the Student Experience Office) will be handled on an individual basis.

**Laboratory Safety**

Safe laboratory practices are an important part of any laboratory experience. These rules are in place for the safety of everyone in the lab and will be strictly enforced. Below are some rules regarding appropriate lab attire. A complete list of safety guidelines are posted online.

1. Goggles: Goggles can be purchased from the bookstore or Walker stockroom. You must wear full eye goggles – safety glasses are not permitted. Goggles must be worn at all times while you are in the lab. There are no exceptions to the “Goggles on at all times” rule.

2. Shoes: You must wear closed toe shoes in the lab. No sandal or open toed shoes allowed. You will not be allowed to enter the lab under any circumstance with improper footwear.

3. Hair: If you have long hair, please make sure that you tie it back while you are in the lab.

4. Clothing: Protective clothing offering coverage from foot to neck including arms is required. You must wear a long-sleeved shirt and pants or slacks that cover your legs while in the lab – no shorts or short skirts. You must wear a lab coat while in the laboratory. Make sure to purchase a lab coat that fits properly -- loose fitting clothing that can get in your way or knock over reaction vessels is dangerous in the lab.

If any of the laboratory personnel must ask you more than once during a laboratory period to put on your goggles or adhere to any of the safety procedures, you will be asked to leave the lab for the day. You will have no opportunity to make up the lab time lost due to your failure to follow the safety rules.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

The relationship between students and faculty is based upon trust and the continued maintenance of this trust is necessary for education to be successful. Students need to trust faculty to make appropriate judgments about the content and structure of the course. Faculty members need to trust that the work turned in by students represents their own effort. Violation of this trust undermines the educational process. In addition, as professionals, you may be designing products upon which lives depend, as a result, there is no tolerance for breach of academic integrity such as cheating, plagiarizing, or inappropriate sharing of laboratories, data or related materials (past or present).

Chat-GPT or similar AI software is prohibited. Any use of this AI software will be an automatic failure. Detection software will be employed if work submitted is questionable.

**The penalties of a breach in academic integrity for this class are the following:** First offense: a zero for the work in question, (i.e., report, data sheets, etc...) and a drop in the course letter grade one level (for example, an A would become a B). Second offense: a failing grade in the class, with no opportunity to drop the course.

The Rensselaer Student Handbook and the Dean of Students Office Webpage https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-conduct link defines various forms of academic dishonesty. Please familiarize yourself with these and ask us if you have questions about what constitutes academic dishonesty.
