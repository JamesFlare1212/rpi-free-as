## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**CHEM1100 (Chemistry I):**  
Principles of chemistry, with particular focus on atomic and molecular structure and bonding, periodicity, basic thermodynamic principles, introduction to acid-base chemistry and elementary chemical equilibrium, and introduction to organic chemistry. Students cannot get credit for both this course and CHEM 1110.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Course Goal: To provide the introductory chemistry student with basic chemical literacy and problem-solving skills. Expected Student Outcomes:**

Upon the completion of the course, students will be able to

1. Demonstrate a proficient knowledge in the core areas of general chemistry (atomic structure, fundamental quantum mechanics, chemical bonding, stoichiometry, thermochemistry, and the periodic table) to prepare themselves for advanced courses in the various subject areas of science and engineering.
2. Based on the reactivity of atoms, ions, and molecules, apply knowledge to describe, predict and solve quantitative and qualitative problems in chemistry and chemical systems using fundamental concepts of chemistry and mathematics.
3. Develop a curiosity for chemistry that fosters continued learning and exploration beyond the course and into life.
4. Demonstrate safe and proper chemistry laboratory techniques relating to mass measurements, volume measurements, solution preparation, dilution, titration, elementary separation, and synthesis methods.
5. Utilize resources to identify various properties of chemicals and use proper precaution in handling these chemicals during experimentation.
6. Collect, analyze, interpret experimental data, and determine experimental errors using various scientific methods.
7. Identify and use scientific literature and other reputable sources as references for experimentation and research.
8. Communicate scientific ideas clearly and effectively in written format.
9. Present experimental results, conclusions, and interpretations in a coherent manner.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

\- Scientific Method, Dimensional Analysis, Significant Figures, Basics of Atomic and Molecular Theory, Nomenclature  
\- Quantum Mechanical Model of an Atom  
\- Periodicity and Trends of the Periodic Table; Ionic Bonding  
\- Chemical Bonding I: Lewis Model and VSEPR Theory  
\- Chemical Bonding II: Valence Bond Theory  
\- Organic Chemistry with Polymers and Biomolecules  
\- Aqueous Solution Reactions  
\- Gas Laws  
\- Chemical Equilibrium and Acid/Base Equilibria  
\- Thermochemistry

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Class Participation:**  
Class participation is optional. Attendance is not required. Each student can make his or her own decision. Decisions based on time-managment and task prioritization. This is covered on the first day of lecture.  
**Pre-Lecture Assignments:**  
There will be pre-lecture assignments associated with each chapter is covered in lecture. The assignments will be completed on Mastering Chemistry (an online homework system) electronically by the posted due dates. Late homework or homework submitted on paper will not be accepted.  
**Lectures:** There is an 80-minute lecture delivered by your course professor on Tuesdays and Fridays. The material presented in the lecture will cover and expand on the material in the required reading assignments. The pace of the lectures will be very fast. Although attendance is optional to each lecture, “in-person” participation clicker questions are included to rapidly assess student learning. Participation is not part of the grading criteria.  
**Discussion/Recitation Participation:**  
During these sessions, your discussion faculty member will go through several problems related to the current lecture topics. At the beginning of each discussion session, you will need to participate in discussion clicker questions in order to receive credits for discussion. Make sure to logon to i>clicker REEF with your laptop or a smart device to answer these questions. Discussions will last approximately one hour and will be held “lin-person” during the first hour of each lab period. They will be recorded so you can review them later. A short quiz will be given during each discussion session. If you missed the questions, you must contact the discussion faculty member to schedule a makeup.  
**Laboratory:**  
Chemistry is an experimental science. Due to the current circumstances where we are not able to meet in person, all labs will be conducted in a hybrid mode (online and in-person) using a simulated platform as prelabs and a recorded lab performed by a TA. Each lab will be performed either in-person and lab partners will share their data. These lab simulations will focus primarily on applying your lecture gained knowledge to a hands-on but virtual laboratory experience. All prelabs and post labs MUST be submitted via the Hayden McNeil online course. After completing the experiment, you will analyze the data, perform any necessary calculations, and answer all post-lab questions. The experimental data you record for the experiment is the data that you will be using for your laboratory report. You will need a lab notebook, one able to make carbon copies, to record the data and write down any observations, and perform your calculations. All excused laboratory absences must be cleared and rescheduled through Dr. Morrissey (NOT your lab instructor).  
Three-Strike Policy for UNEXCUSED Absences:  
1\. One absence with no makeup will result in a grade of “zero” for the corresponding lab session.  
2\. Two absences with no makeup will result in a grade of “zero” for the entire lab portion of the final grade (20%).  
3\. Three absences with no makeup will result in an “F” for the entire course.  
You are expected to collaborate with your lab partner(s) to perform the experiment and collect the data. However, each lab partner is expected to analyze the results and work on the post lab questions independently. Make sure your post lab assignments are in your own words. Anyone who is caught copying from someone else’s work or work from past years will be subjected to academic disciplinary actions as follows:  
Three-Strike Policy for PLAGARISM:  
1\. The first infraction will result in a grade of “zero” for the corresponding prelab and/or post lab with a verbal and a written warning.  
2\. The second infraction will result in a grade of “zero” for the entire lab portion of the final grade (20%). A second written warning will also be issued.  
3\. The third infraction will result in an “F” for the entire course.  
If you missed or need to miss an experiment, please contact Dr. Morrissey to reschedule either to another lab section and/or a separate time. All experiment must be made up even ones with legitimate excuses.  
**Course Material Exams:**  
There will be three 60-minute exams given on selected Wednesdays. The syllabus provides an outline of the materials that will be covered for each exam. Exams may include material covered in lecture, discussion, reading, homework, or laboratory. No makeup exams will be given. If a student misses an exam for an unavoidable and compelling reason, the student may be excused from the exam at the instructors’ discretion provided that the student presents a reasonable excuse. A written excuse is required from the Student Office, a doctor, or a coach. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:** https://rpi.app.box.com/s/bfzzwdsrqxzrm3jikr2uv6gn7zu6dhlt

The relationship between students and faculty is based upon trust and the continued maintenance of this trust is necessary for education to be successful. Students need to trust faculty to make appropriate judgments about the content and structure of the course. Faculty members need to trust that the work turned in by students represents their own effort. Violation of this trust undermines the educational process. For example, as professionals, you may be responsible in designing products (aircraft, the computers that run them, or the runways on which they land) where lives are at stake. As a result, there is no tolerance for any breach of academic integrity such as cheating, plagiarizing, or inappropriate sharing of laboratories or quizzes. (e.g., Using someone else’s REEF code is considered cheating.)

Cheating can include sharing answers, as well as stealing answers and looking up answers online during exams. Plagiarism means copying words from someone’s work, even if you “change the sentence a bit.” If you share your laboratory report, you are as guilty as the person copying it. This includes copying results and answers from previous years’ lab reports. If you do use material from an appropriate source, make sure you reference it properly in your reports. The Rensselaer Student Handbook and the Dean of Students Office Webpage (http://doso.rpi.edu/update.do?artcenterkey=676) defines various forms of academic dishonesty. Please familiarize yourself with these and ask us if you have questions about what constitutes academic dishonesty.

You will immediately receive a grade of an “F” if you are caught cheating during an exam. One strike and you are OUT!
