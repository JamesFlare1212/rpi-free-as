## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course explores the fundamental principles that define the relationship between chemical structure and the biological action of drug molecules. A major focus of the course is the application of these chemical principles to molecular-targeted drug discovery, molecular mechanisms of pharmacological activity, and the biopharmaceutical properties of drugs. The relationships between drug structure, therapeutic properties, and physicochemical characteristics will be discussed. Structure activity relationships (SAR), structure-property relationships (SPR) and ADME (absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion) will be explored and presented through case studies. The roles of computational chemistry, molecular modeling, and biophysical methods in the understanding of the relationship between structure and biological activity will also be studied. Although an emphasis will be placed on orally bioavailable small molecules, alternative drug modalities will also be discussed.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Identify ligand-molecular target binding interactions.
- Define and explain the concept of structure-activity relationships and how the chemical structure of a drug influences affinity, potency, and efficacy (therapeutic properties).
- Define and explain structure-property relationships and physiochemical properties that affect drug absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion.
- Identify the various modalities of drugs and compare their different mechanisms of action and their implication for their uses.
- Consider how drugs are discovered, designed, and developed.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance and Make-up Opportunities

If you are absent for a quiz or semester exam:

- If your absence is not excused, you will receive a grade of zero.

- The first time you miss a quiz and provide a valid excuse, no make-up will be given, and your other quizzes will hold more weight toward your final grade. In other words, the missed quiz will not be used toward the quiz portion of your final grade. For second and subsequent missed quizzes with valid excuses, a make-up must be scheduled not more than one week after your absence. In such a case, those students should bring an official document that proves one's legitimate excuse of absence to the Dean of Students who requests a recommendation to the instructor. After discussing with the Dean of Students, the instructors will decide whether a make-up quiz should be given or not. In case a make-up is given, it must be scheduled not more than one week after your absence.

- Activities cannot be made up. If a valid excuse is provided, the corresponding percentage of your final grade will be transferred to quizzes.

- Exams missed with a valid excuse for the date of the exam must be made up not more than one week after your absence.

- If your absence is due to a foreseeable circumstance (such as an athletic event) on the day of the quiz/activity/exam, you must receive permission to be absent at least 48 hours in advance.

- If your absence is due to an unforeseeable event (such as illness or family emergency) on the day of quiz/exam, you must provide proof of the event within one week of returning to classes.

- All excuses will be handled by the Dean of Students. Take documentation of the reason of your absence to the Dean’s office, 4600 Academy Hall. The Dean’s Office will then contact me.

Exam periods:

The exams will be administered during a regularly scheduled class and students will have a maximum of 50 minutes to complete a course exam.

Quizzes will also be administered during a regularly scheduled class and students will have a maximum of 20 minutes to complete a course quiz.
