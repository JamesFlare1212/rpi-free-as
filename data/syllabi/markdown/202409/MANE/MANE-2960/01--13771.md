## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course covers deformable bodies in static equilibrium. It introduces the fundamentals of the mechanics of deformable bodies, including basic concepts such as traction, stress and strain, and leading to the balance equations needed when analyzing any problem in continuum mechanics. A very simple finite element based computational tool is used to solve these elastic problems. When dealing with examples that can be modeled by the classical beam models, i.e., bending, torsion, and shear, the course delivers these classical technics to show, when possible, how back of envelope calculations are synched with computational analysis in engineering practice. The course includes many real-life examples and walks the students through the engineering process of defining a problem, creating a model, solving the model, and interpreting the results.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Understand stress and strain in tensorial form, deformation, displacement, equilibrium and constitutive equations.
- 2\. Understand how to define boundary conditions in solid mechanics and perform stress and deformation analysis using a finite element-based computational tool.
- 3\. Perform stress analysis for beams and structures of beams subjected to various loads using analytic and numerical methods.
- 4\. Use classical Euler beam theory to calculate deflection of structures modeled by beam model and perform stability analysis.
- 5\. Evaluate what problems can be modeled by classical beam models and compare the results of these classical analysis with the computational solution.
