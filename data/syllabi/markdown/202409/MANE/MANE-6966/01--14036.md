## Course Goals

### Goals:

The course provides a comprehensive exploration of optimization principles applied to the design of complex engineering systems. It underscores the importance of multidisciplinary design optimization as an approach to integrate analysis and design across multiple disciplines for designing complex coupled systems. The course includes a brief overview of optimization methods, including important gradient-based methods and non-traditional/heuristic methods for optimization such as genetic algorithms and particle swarm optimization. The course also examines multidisciplinary systems analysis, emphasizing modeling techniques and sensitivity analysis to understand system behavior. MDO frameworks and algorithms are reviewed, showcasing hierarchical, collaborative, and concurrent approaches. Design space exploration strategies are discussed, emphasizing Pareto optimization and decision-making under uncertainty. The role of machine learning and artificial intelligence in multidisciplinary design optimization is also examined.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Formulate engineering design optimization problems in a standard format – be able to define appropriate objective criteria, design variables, design parameters, and constraints
- • Subdivide a complex system into smaller subsystems, manage their interfaces, and reintegrate them into a coupled system model
- • Define mathematical optimality
- • Become familiar with commonly used optimization algorithms - gradient, gradient-free and heuristics-based algorithms
- • Select appropriate optimization algorithm for the design problem under consideration, understand it’s limitations, and ways to counter those shortcomings
- • Critically evaluate results in results of optimization – understand tradeoffs between computational costs and accuracy
- • Understand concept of multicriteria optimization – what the Pareto frontier represents?
- • Develop an understanding of the role of emergent computational tools in the MDO solution space – AI and Machine Learning
- • Be able to formulate design problems, propose and implement solutions, and present their findings to a critical audience
- • Experience the challenge of working collaboratively in small teams

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Homework Assignments**

Homework problems will be assigned periodically and will account for 50% of the grade. All homework will be due by the close of business on a designated day. Homework assignments should be presented in a professional format, i.e., legible work that is presented in a logical order such that it can be readily followed. Homework submitted late but before the next lecture date will be penalized 20% of the grade. Any homework that is submitted after that time will not be graded. Homework will not be accepted after the last day of class. Medical exceptions will be accommodated as necessary.

#### Project Work

Students will formulate and work on a multidisciplinary design optimization problem throughout the course of the semester. There will be project teams (typically 2-3 students in each team). The project will account for the remaining 50% of the course grade. There will be no mid-term or final exams in the course.

Following the selection of a project that you wish to work on, you will submit (team submission) a two-page description of the proposed work. This will account for 15% of your project grade. A progress report will be due four weeks before the end of the semester (5 pages max). This should describe what has been accomplished, what challenges or hurdles you have encountered, and what are the next steps that you will pursue – this submission will account for 25% of your project grade. A final report detailing all that was accomplished during the project – results, their interpretation, major conclusions drawn from the study will be due on the final day of classes for the semester. This report should be prepared like an AIAA Conference paper using the template shown here. This submission and oral presentation will account for the remaining 60% of the project grade. In the week preceding the final week, each team will present a brief oral project report to the class.
