## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Composite materials are important because of their inherent advantages compared to traditional engineering materials (metals alloys, polymers, ceramics) including, but not limited to high specific stiffness and strength, durability, long service life, dimensional stability, and tailorable properties and ease of part consolidation that expands design options for engineers. However, manufacturing of composite materials is generally more complex and expensive than for other engineering materials. This graduate-level course covers the technology, science, theory, and process/tooling design details for a wide range of advanced and engineering composites manufacturing methods. The first parts of the course provides students with general knowledge about properties and processing of composite material constituents (reinforcement, matrix, core), and different part formats (laminate, sandwich construction, engineered composite). The remainder of the course builds upon this fundamental knowledge of the constituent materials and focuses on the technology, science/theory, and design practice for most common composite manufacturing processes through a series of lectures, homework activities, and in-class labs.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The main objective of this course is to educate graduate-level engineers, primarily mechanical and aerospace, through lectures, homework activities, and hands-on activities about the current technology, science, and practical implementation of composites manufacturing methods to a variety of product applications.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Topics/Activities:

- Course introduction, overview of composite materials with emphasis on polymer matrix composites
- Properties, forms, and manufacturing of synthetic (including polymeric and mineral-based) and natural fiber reinforcements
- Properties and manufacturing of synthetic and bio-based thermoset (with emphasis on unsaturated polyesters and epoxies) and thermoplastic matrices, additives, and a brief treatment of metal and ceramic matrices
- Fiber/matrix interface
- Properties, forms, and manufacturing of core materials
- Laminates vs. sandwich structures and engineering composites vs. advanced composites
- Advanced composite design, analysis, and material characterization with Dr. Ronald Bucinell, PE (Professor at Union College) as guest lecturer

*Advanced composites background exam (in-class)*

- Advanced composite manufacturing requirements
- Manufacturing process technology & hardware, theory, and design practice
  - o Open molding methods
  - o Closed molding methods
  - o Sheet molding methods
  - o Resin infusion methods
  - o Fiber/filament placement methods (incl. 3D printing)
  - o 2-D shaping methods
  - o Composites bonding/fastening and co-molding
  - o Composites machining and trimming

*Composites Manufacturing Processes Exam (finals week)*

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Understand polymer matrix composite (PMC) materials, structures, and their constituents including reinforcement fibers and textiles, thermoset and thermoplastic matrices, and cores
- • Identify characteristics of PMC constituents and understand their effect on composite mechanical properties
- • Understand/Identify/Discuss how manufacturing requirements are formulated
- • Learn how to choose the best manufacturing approach for a particular part shape, material system, and annual production quantity
- • Apply analytical theory to design and/or specify tooling, production equipment, process details, and manufacturing system requirements.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Assignments/Labs: There are 9-10 assignments during the semester. Assignments 1-3 pertain to background material on advanced composites and their constituents (15% of total grade). The remaining homework assignments, many of which include an in-class lab component, pertain to composites manufacturing processes (45% of total grade).

Exams: A one-hour exam covering background material on advanced composites (10% of total grade) is given during class time. Another 1-1/2 hour exam (3 hours available time) covering material on composites manufacturing processes (25% of total grade) is given during finals week.
