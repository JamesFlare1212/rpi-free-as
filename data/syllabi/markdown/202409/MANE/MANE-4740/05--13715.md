## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Course Objectives**

The topics to be covered will be a combination of new material specific to this lab course and material covered in previous courses.

The specific objectives of the course are:

1. To demonstrate thermodynamic, heat transfer, and fluid flow principles;
2. To integrate theory and practice through the operation of experiments and through the acquisition and analysis of experimental data;
3. To familiarize students with complex, realistic systems and the interactions among thermodynamics, heat transfer, and fluid flow;
4. To introduce some new concepts and techniques in the three topic areas;
5. To develop abilities to understand, experiment with, and analyze thermal and fluids systems, including analysis and decision making in the face of experimental uncertainty;
6. To exercise technical communication skills in a variety of modes.

**\*\* Quizzes**

Throughout the semester, there will be a series of quizzes administered on topics covered during lectures and in the laboratory. Quizzes may be online or during a lecture and will be announced in lecture at least one week in advance. The intent of the quiz is to draw attention to details in both the lab manual and the laboratory procedures manual that may be important during the lab, and to be sure students understood the underlying physical principles of the experiments. You may use any materials you want during the quiz, but you must take it alone, without any assistance from anyone else. The quizzes will be timed.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance**

Attendance is expected for the weekly lecture. Attendance is mandatory for the lab sessions. Attendance in the studio discussions is expected for all group members. You must attend the lab scheduled for **your** section and **your** group. Alternative arrangements should be extremely rare and only with the approval of your section instructor. Absence from the lab session without an instructor-approved excuse will result in a grade of zero for the report. Please communicate with your section instructor if you need an accommodation.

In the event of an illness that prevents a student from participating in a lab session or discussion session, the student should inform the section instructor and TA right away. If the student cannot participate in a lab session, the student’s group is encouraged to use Webex so that the student can experience the lab remotely.

Students are expected to pay attention and take notes in the lectures. Use of laptops and cellphones is not permitted in the lectures. Photography is not permitted in the lectures. Students should not rely solely on the instructor’s lecture notes as lecture notes may not be posted to LMS.

**Team Participation**

All students are expected to participate fully in each laboratory experience and in the preparation of all group assignments.

### Other Course Policies:

**Lab Material Reading**

Much more can be gained from an experiment if, before attempting to perform the experiment, the student has the background knowledge about the objectives and concepts (theory) governing the processes to be studied. It is also critical to understand the objectives and the plan of work for each lab before coming to the lab. This information is provided in the extended handouts for each experiment. **Please carefully read all of the handouts.** Some supplemental materials may also be provided on LMS for those wishing a deeper understanding of the theory, instrumentation, and/or standards related to the experiments.

**Final Exam:** There is no final exam. (A timeslot for this course might appear on the finals schedule, but that is just a placeholder.)

**Late Policy:** All assignments are to be submitted on time. No credit will be given for late submittals. Students are expected to be on time for their labs. At the discretion of the instructor and TA, a penalty of 2 points (out of 100) per minute late may be applied to the lab report grades of students reporting late to lab sessions.

**Grade Curving:** The difficulty of the material may require curving the class grade, but this is seldom needed given that students have a high degree of control of their level of performance on assignments. Equalization of the sections may be performed to account for differences in report grading between instructors.

**Grade Modifiers:** Grade modifiers (+, −) will be used in assigning the grade in this class.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity and Rules of Behavior**

Rensselaer’s policy on academic dishonesty is explained in the *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities*. You are expected to review and understand that information. In addition, the following policies apply specifically to this course.

- Committing, or attempting to commit an act of academic dishonesty, or assisting in the commission or attempt of such an act, is a flagrant offense to the process and culture of education. The default penalty for cheating is automatic failure of the course, i.e., a grade of ‘F’.
- You are expected to refrain from any academically dishonest act, and to be vigilant in protecting your work from others who might attempt to commit an act of academic dishonesty.

In this course, academic dishonesty – *cheating* – can take the following forms. This is not a complete list.

1. Submission of a lab report that, except for short phrases or properly referenced material (e.g., quotes and figures from the lab manual, etc.), consists of any content not created by and original to the report authors listed.
2. Taking a quiz with any assistance from another person, regardless of who they are, or the medium of communication for assistance (talk, phone, texting, etc.).
3. Using any materials (such as a cell phone, camera) that are not explicitly permitted during a quiz. (Quizzes are open book, open notes, open lab reports, open laptop, open internet searches, but closed communication.)
4. Copying the work of another (current or former) student. This includes sharing of calculation spreadsheets for homework assignments. Students are responsible for keeping their individual calculations out of shared data sheets.
5. Use of so-called artificial intelligence (AI), e.g., ChatGPT, to assist with report writing or homework.
6. Use of Chegg.com or any other online/internet assignment sharing system for any graded assignment.
7. Use of templates and/or solutions obtained through any website other than the current semester’s LMS.

Mobile Devices: During a quiz all mobile devices that are not your laptop (e.g., cell/smart phones, pagers, cameras, etc.) must be stored securely away and are not to be used unless specifically directed otherwise by the instructor. The use of, or any interaction with, a mobile device during a quiz without explicit permission of the instructor will be interpreted as the illicit transfer of quiz data, which will be considered an act of cheating and treated as such.

The penalty for violating these policies is at the discretion of the course coordinator. In general, the penalty for any violation is failure of the course and referral to the Dean of Students. See Appendix 1.

**Collaboration**

You are expected to collaborate with the members of your laboratory group with respect to the conduct of your experiments and the preparation of your group reports. However, as stated above, your report, including the experimental data and observations described therein, must be that of your own group. In rare cases (e.g., lab equipment failure) your instructor may provide you with experimental data to use as if you had obtained it yourselves; this will be considered “your data”, regardless of the actual source.

For homework assignments, you **must** complete and submit your own work individually. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask your instructor for clarification.
