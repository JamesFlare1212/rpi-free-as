## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This two-semester sequence for seniors majoring in nuclear engineering is intended to be a “capstone” design project where students have the opportunity to utilize the broad range of their undergraduate experience to solve a multidisciplinary design problem. Projects are selected to provide interaction between nuclear engineering majors to provide exposure to cross-fertilization of ideas and team interaction, which simulates anticipated future professional experience. The product of each design project is a comprehensive report or design proposal having both global and detailed completeness. Under some circumstances, the project may involve development of cost information necessary to effect construction and may involve construction and commissioning of the designed apparatus. This is a communication-intensive course.

While administratively this course is broken into two semesters, this is effectively a single course that extends equally over the entire academic year. Thus, while the credit load appears on a transcript as 1 credit in fall and 2 credits in spring, you should be spending ~1.5 credits and thus 4.5 hours per week on this course, on average, every week in the fall and spring semesters. That is an average of 126 hours of class time and work per member of each team, and thus approximately 500 hours across a four-person team. Even with three people that’s approximately 375 hours, from which you should be able to produce some excellent design solutions.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Experience the design process introduced in the Engineering Design Elective (ENGR-2050 Introduction to Engineering Design or MANE-2220 Inventor’s Studio I) in the context of a complex nuclear engineering design challenge that relies on applying analytical skills gained in previous courses in the nuclear engineering curriculum.
- Extend technical communication skills to nuclear engineering specific content, including written technical reports and oral presentations.
- Apply team effort and accommodate team diversity and group responsibility.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
