## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

In this course, we will cover the basics of quantum computing with a hands-on, application-oriented approach. In addition to covering the fundamentals, this course will introduce students to contemporary methods in quantum computing such as variational quantum algorithms and error mitigation techniques. Example applications will be drawn from simulations, machine learning, system optimization, and adjacent technologies.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Required: None. Handouts and readings will be provided.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course aims to provide students with a hands-on survey of quantum computing as it is currently practiced in the research community. The course will emphasize experimental techniques for working with quantum hardware, demonstrate the current capabilities, limitations, and emerging applications, and familiarize students with future development directions of the technology.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Upon completing the course, students will:  
● Understand the fundamentals of working with quantum computing hardware:  
○ Be able to perform calibration, characterization, and benchmarking experiments.  
○ Determine and mitigate sources of noise in quantum computations.  
○ Develop proof-of-concept applications for quantum computing application research.  
● Be familiar with the essential features, properties, and behaviors of quantum hardware.  
● Be familiar with a broad spectrum of quantum technologies, such as quantum networks and sensors.  
● Understand the current state of the quantum technology industry, and recognize the available career opportunities.  
● Compare and contrast different quantum computing modalities and their respective engineering tradeoffs.  
● Understand sources of noise, how noise affects the performance and scalability of quantum computers, and how to manage noise in the development of useful  
algorithms.  
● Recognize the structure of problems for which quantum computing may offer advantages.  
● Implement basic quantum algorithms using Python and Qiskit, execute them on superconducting quantum hardware, and interpret the output.  
● Analyze contemporary quantum technology research literature, and translate research findings into practical algorithm demonstrations.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Collaboration Policy  
● Some assignments must be submitted individually, while others may be submitted as a the product of a group effort. For assignments allowing collaboration, you are encouraged to work together in groups of up to 3 (exceptions may be granted with instructor permission). Each group submission must clearly state all members’ names and contributions to the assignment.  
● You are permitted to consult with classmates and outside experts (e.g. other faculty, IBM staff) on all assignments, provided such consultation is properly cited and acknowledged.  
● You may not use external consultants to complete any part of an assignment as a collaborator or on your behalf, regardless of affiliation.  
Plagiarism Policy  
The Rensselaer Student Handbook defines plagiarism as “representing the work or words of another as one's own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference." It is your responsibility to ensure that all material is cited and attributed properly. The Rensselaer Library has resources available on proper citation and attribution at https://library.rpi.edu/citation-and-copyright/plagiarism. Minor or unintentional errors in citation or attribution for assignments submitted in good faith will receive a warning, and students will have the opportunity to correct the errors without penalty. Assignments found to intentionally have included plagiarized material (including plagiarized material produced by generative AI tools) will result in a zero for the assignment. Subsequent offenses will result in an F for the course.  
Generative AI Policy  
Use of generative AI tools (e.g. ChatGPT or other large language models) is discouraged. The use of such tools must be properly acknowledged in all submissions, and you are solely responsible for the correctness and quality of all material submitted. Any incorrect, missing, or low quality information will be will be scored accordingly. Failure to disclose the use of generative AI tools will be treated as intentional plagiarism, resulting in a zero for the first offense, and an F for the course in subsequent offenses.
