## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course examines the use of modern simulation tools for modeling nuclear reactors.  
Both Monte Carlo and deterministic transport solvers will be used to model a variety  
of systems ranging from a single pin cell to full reactor cores. Problems of interest will  
include power distributions, calculation of reaction rates, fuel depletion, and nuclear data  
processing. Basic concepts of nuclear reactor core design will be considered, such as fuel  
assembly loading patterns, burnable poisons, chemical shims, and reactivity control.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The objective of this course is to build upon the student’s previous course work on the  
theoretical function of nuclear reactors, and expose them to types of real world problems  
and applications that they are likely to encounter in industry. The nuclear engineering  
design process is heavily based on the use of specialized computational tools to perform  
simulations of systems of interest. This course will instruct students on the basic use of  
such tools, teaching them how to address real world problems from the area of nuclear  
reactor design. The topics and problems considered have been chosen so as to reinforce  
the material students learned in Physics of Nuclear Reactors (MANE 4480), elucidating  
their practical application.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will understand the differences between stochastic (Monte Carlo) and deterministic methods for performing reactor analysis simulations.
- Students will know how to define the geometric and material properties for a system using OpenMC.
- Students will know how to obtain quantities of interest from a Monte Carlo simulation, such as the flux and reaction rates.
- Students will know how to process ENDF files using NJOY to produce Monte Carlo ready ACE files.
- Students will understand the basic principles of energy self-shielding to produce multi-group cross sections at different dilutions.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Homework**  
All homework assignments are graded out of 100 points, and are due 1 week after they  
are assigned by 6:00PM Eastern Time. Homework assignments must be typed and submitted  
in electronic format. Homework and plots may not be done by hand or with Excel !  
All students are granted two, no-questions-asked, 48 hour homework extensions. To use  
this extension, you must notify the instructor before the nominal homework due date.  
**Tests**  
Two tests will be administered over the course of the semester. Both will take place  
in class, and will require your computer. You may use any and all freely accessible  
resources during the tests, with the exception of AI tools (such as ChatGPT, Copilot,  
etc.) or interpersonal communication tools (be it for communicating with students in  
the course, or others outside the course). All tests will be graded out of 100 points.  
**Course Projects**  
The project will be on a topic of the student’ choice, though  
must be approved by the instructor. It is expected that the project will leverage the  
tools/skills/knowledge acquired in the course. It is likely the student will chose a topic  
related to their research, and as such, the project does not necessarily need to be reactor physics  
based. Students will give oral presentations on their projects on the last two days  
of class, and must also submit a final report, due on by 6:00PM on the last day of class.  
The project will be graded out of 100 points.
