## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course examines representations of science and technology in fictional narratives.

Some questions we might consider:

- ¨ What happens to scientific ideas when they appear in fiction?

- ¨ Can fictional texts anticipate and even lead technological developments?

- ¨ What are the special difficulties and challenges of representing science or technology in fictional works?

*Final grades will be submitted by Dec 9th* **Science and Fiction Analysis Guidelines**

Questions to consider when reading these texts:

- How are ideas presented to the reader? (**Exposition**).
- What kind of **viewpoint or narrator** is employed (e.g., first person, omniscient)? What are the advantages or limits of the position the author has chosen?
- How important and competent are **plot and characterization?**
- Does the author use make a good use of **fiction**?
- Are references to science or technology **accurate**? Does this matter? Did the author **predict** any scientific developments?
- How does the author make us **feel** about science or technology as a form of knowledge or as a profession? Are any particular **fears or desires** expressed?
- Are there identifiable **philosophical or ethical principles** in the text?

Guidelines for Essays

In general, you will be evaluated according to how well you engage with class material and how well you articulate your ideas in well-written papers. Such papers will demonstrate three key elements—**relevance, coherence,** and **writing proficiency**.

Papers should have:

- a clear thesis
- coherent organization and development
- focused paragraphs
- sentence-level competency (punctuation, grammar)

We will devote some time in class sessions to writing expectations and guidelines.

**Class Participation**

- **Class Presentation** (**5-10 minutes**). Each person will be asked to give one report in the form of a **PowerPoint** presentation or equivalent. **Format:** include a topic or question for the class to discuss further. No more than **4 sentences per slide**. Send slides to deeryjune@gmail.com

*Tip: A good presentation will avoid mere plot summary and will focus on the assigned topic. It will demonstrate that you are familiar with the text, that you have given the topic some thought, and that you can articulate your ideas clearly.*

- **Class debates:** periodically, there will be a short debate that will require no prep outside class.

*The remainder of the participation grade will be based on your general contribution to **class discussion**. To give you some sense of how you are doing, you will receive an estimate of your participation grade halfway through the course. Remember that quality counts for more than quantity*
