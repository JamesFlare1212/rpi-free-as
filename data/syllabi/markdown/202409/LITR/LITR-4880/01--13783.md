## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

How can the lens of multiculturalism broaden our knowledge of American literature and culture in unexpected ways? This course introduces students to contemporary U.S. literatures with an eye toward understanding the various cultural, historical, political, and aesthetic contexts that shape the nation and its influences beyond national borders. Students will learn to analyze a variety of ethnic American literatures with respect to their diverse cultural contexts and understand the objectives and challenges of situating a unified American literary canon (and perhaps the nation itself) in the twenty-first century. Assignments help students refine analysis and writing skills and practice creative, open-ended responses. This course emphasizes critical thinking, experimentation, creativity, collaboration, and student-centered learning.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

- To foster enjoyment of literature.
- To introduce a range of works in American literature representing diverse cultures and identities.

- To help students recognize that literature does not occur in isolation but is part of a complex dialogue within cultural and historical contexts.
- To disclose and discuss which books have been historically taught and why.
- To facilitate team building, small group-dynamics, and interpersonal communication skills by fostering a learning community.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- ● Produce strong analyses of literary texts supported by scholarly evidence
- ● Utilize theoretical knowledge from literary and cultural studies to situate discussions of historically underrepresented American cultures and contexts.
- ● Demonstrate clear and cohesive oral communication skills through class participation and presentations.
- ● Articulate, formally and informally, how works of literature from discrete cultural traditions speak both within and against broader American and Transamerican contexts.
- ● Practice extra-textual methods of critical thinking and response, including but not limited to creative writing, graphic arts, and digital storytelling.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Reading:** Your primary assignment in this course is to read course texts actively and critically.  
**Participation:** This is not a course in which you will read for information and pass an exam. Instead, the primary mode of learning will be consistent, active preparation (reading) and discussion amongst peers. To do well in this category, **at a minimum, you should keep up with all readings and be prepared to ask one specific** **question and analyze one specific passage in the assigned reading for every class.**  
**Critical Response Quizzes.** These are your thoughtful, organized responses to essay question prompts about a specific text within its cultural contexts.  
**Short Essay:** You will revise and expand two critical responses into one short, comparative essay.  
**Summary-Evaluation & Discussion Facilitation**: After reading the article several times to understand it fully in all its nuance, you will present a brief overview of the article in which you identify the critic’s thesis and the supporting evidence for that thesis using your own words and choice quotes from the article. Then you will offer an evaluation of the scholarship  
**The Alt-essay:** You will transform another set of critical responses into a comparative alternative essay. This assignment has the same rudimentary functions as a typical essay in that you must 1) express textual analysis and 2) provide evidence to support your conclusions. However, the form of the alt-essay is entirely variable, asking you to consider media and form deliberately and thoughtfully.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

This is a small seminar class, the success of which is entirely dependent on active student presence and participation. Everyone has two “freebie” absences to use for illness, self-care, or other obligations without point penalty. Therefore, I do not routinely distinguish between excused and unexcused absences (school athletes see me at the beginning of the term). Each absence beyond the second one will drop your grade by ten points (1%). If you miss more than five classes without administrative excusal, you will receive no credit for the course even if you are otherwise passing. Note that three tardies of half hour or more accrue to one absence. Attendance is calculated into your unit participation grade. Students with disabilities who need attendance accommodations should meet with me to discuss a personalized plan.

- ### Academic Integrity
