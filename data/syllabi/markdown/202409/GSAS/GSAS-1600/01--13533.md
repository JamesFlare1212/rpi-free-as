## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

History and Culture of Games is a course that examines the role of games in culture and society throughout history. We will explore discourses on games, analyze their cultural impact, and develop critical analysis techniques. This class introduces theories that view games as socio-cultural activities and forms of expression. Throughout the semester, students will scrutinize a wide range of digital and non-digital games, posing questions such as: What defines a game? Who plays games and why?

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

## Course Goals

### Goals:

\- Critically analyze games through cultural and historical perspectives.  
\- Develop a deeper understanding of the relationship between culture and games beyond typical video game consumerism.  
\- Engage in theoretical discussions about games.  
\- Apply theoretical concepts to game design practices.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Participation  
Students will be graded on their contributions to class discussions and in-class exercises. Failing to complete the assigned readings and arriving unprepared to discuss them will negatively impact the participation grade. While the frequency of participation is important, the quality of contributions is equally critical.  
Short Essays  
Before every Tuesday class (except week 2 is on Friday), students will complete the assigned readings and play the designated game. Following this, students will write a brief essay (approximately 300-400 words) connecting the week’s reading to the game. The essay can explore the relationship between the game and the reading from various perspectives, such as culture, history, design, art, or science. The game can either support or disprove the arguments in the reading, and students are encouraged to reference other games to reinforce their points. Only the best 10 short essays will count towards the final grade. Late essays submitted after the due date will not be graded!  
Game Analysis Presentation  
Students will select presentation days and games starting from September 27th Friday. After choosing a game, students need to play it extensively in order to comprehensively understand its context, story, design, function, and aesthetics. The presentation should take 8-10 minutes to introduce the game, analyze the game’s features, and discuss notable experiences or insights gained from playing it. After the presentation, there will be a 2-5 minutes Q&A session. Pick a slot in this spreadsheet.  
Final Essay + Draft (Draft: > 500 words, Final: 1000-1200 words)  
In the final essay, students will either analyze a specific game or compare two games. Students should identify a particular aspect of the game they want to examine and apply the theories that support their argument. Alternatively, students can choose to address a question about games that they believe has not been answered or explored. Students can draw on theories from other disciplines or disciplines that intersect with game studies. A draft of the essay is due at midterm and the final essay is due by the end of the semester.
