## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will teach the fundamentals of cinematography including color theory, contrast, and composition using 3D computer lighting, rendering, and camera authoring. This course will focus on producing high quality renders for animation, games, and digital art.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance, Participation and Lateness Policy** This class runs much like a real game studio team, similarly to what one would find post graduation. In professional game art team standups, there are frequent check-ins for logging production effort and for smoothing out potential issues. This supports accountability and responsibility for each party. In this classroom setting, attendance is ***crucial*** to the success of the student so that the instructor can give proper notes, help, and course instruction to the class. The instructor will do their best to check in with each student during studio time which requires the students to be present in class with their current work. Working remotely during class time is *not allowed* unless agreed upon with the instructor prior. Working remotely without this agreement will result in unexcused absence(s) and will reflect on the student’s project grade and to overall grade. Individual student participation in this class is noted for an end of the semester participation grade. This relies on the student’s factors such as being ready to class on time, on time with an assignment and materials, participating in class/critiques, as well as general professionalism. The instructor will not dock points for any student for their general shy or quiet outward demeanor but *will* dock points for not showing professionalism in the instances above. This class allows up to **three** excused absences without grade penalty at the instructor’s discretion. Excused absences include job interviews, events, transportation issues, personal reasons, mental health days etc. Please contact the instructor prior to missing class if available for instruction and possible assignment modification. The student never has to tell the instructor details of why they are missing class if they are uncomfortable disclosing it, much like to an employer (IE, "*I am having transportation issues.*” and not “*I have transportation issues because of my specific medical issue etc*”). If the student *does not* tell the instructor of their absence it will count as an unexcused absence. **More than eight unexcused absences is an automatic failure.** If there is a larger unforeseen absence for the student such as an incredible loss or ailment where the student is unable to attend class for any reason, the Office for Student Success will contact the instructor and will work together for a solution for said student. There is an allowance of seven minutes post class start time for proper punctuality in respect for the instructor and your classmates’ time. If there is an unforeseen event causing the student to miss a portion of the class, please contact the instructor or inform them when appropriate during or after class. Lateness counts as a *full excused absence* at the halfway mark of allotted class time. Frequent lateness will impact negatively on the student’s grade at the instructor’s discretion.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Trust**: Student-Teacher relationships are built on trust. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. And, teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts, which violate this trust, undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

**Plagiarism**: All work produced in this course must be original and created by the student. Incidents of academic dishonesty or attempted academic dishonesty will result in grade penalties and may be referred to the Dean of Students. While the creation of digital artwork may utilize prior works for inspiration, reference, and source material, assignments in this class are expected to represent the student’s originality and technical ability through the creation of substantially new works. When in doubt, ask your instructor.

Generally, digital artwork frequently involves using materials from many sources including your own original work. In some cases an assignment may clearly specify starting files to begin with, and in others it may be allowable to use third party textures, model parts, rigs, scripts, or other assets. A good general guideline is that anything related to the learning goal of the particular project should be your own original work. ***If in doubt, just ask**.* When used, any third party materials must be used in accordance with applicable license / IP restrictions and must be clearly indicated when submitting assignments. This class has set expectations for the student’s learning outcome, and does not accept third party assets such as textures, model parts, rigs, scripts, or others as final deliverables.

**Additional Notes on Academic Integrity (PLEASE READ CAREFULLY)**

While deciding who the "author" is in artistic production can be a thorny process, in this class, all work must be made WHOLLY by you. If there are any exceptions to this rule, I will inform you in advance.

This means:

\* If a model is required for a project, you and you alone will be doing that modeling.

\* If a rig needs to be constructed, you and you alone will be the one constructing it.

\* If a script needs to be written, you and you alone will write that script.

The first infraction against this policy will result in failure of the project.

The second infraction will result in failure of the course and a report to the Office of the Dean.
