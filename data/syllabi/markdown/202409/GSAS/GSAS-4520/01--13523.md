## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

In this intensive studio production course, students work in interdisciplinary teams to create short digital games using multiple game engine technologies and a variety of creative design prompts. Developing video games takes a myriad of talents: art, programming, design, and most importantly, the ability to work in a team and communicate with others.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

**Software**

The course is structured primarily around 2 team projects, each using a different game engine. There are many game engines in use throughout the industry, some publicly available, others proprietary. These are selected to demonstrate a variety of approaches to game development. The engines for this semester will be:

- Unity
- Unreal Engine

We will use online tools and platforms for collaboration, version control, and project management, including:

- Helix Perforce (P4V)
- Basecamp
- Discord
- WebEx

Additionally, you will use other software as appropriate for creating game assets - images, 3D models, animations, audio files, etc. We will cover some of these tools in class, but you may use other ones. Software we will cover includes:

- Maya
- Substance Painter
- Adobe Creative Cloud
  - ○ Illustrator
  - ○ Photoshop
  - ○ Animate
  - ○ Audition
  - ○ After Effects

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Your grade will be based on individual assessments by your instructors and teams, the two team-based projects, and assessment of overall participation and effort. Team-based projects will have team members preassigned beforehand. On project presentation days, Each team will give a short presentation of their game to the class during each phase. Afterwards, each team will get feedback regarding their work. Each team will hand in a short printed document indicating what work was performed by each member of the team, during each phase with the document signed by every member of the team. This record of work will be balanced against the quality of the project to determine each student's individual grade. each student will also submit a one page summary of their personal assessment of their work and their teams performance (aka, 360 evaluation)

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is mandatory. You are a member of a team, and constant communication with your team is necessary. This class features an intense workload, as complete games are very difficult to make, and you'll be working within a highly compressed timespan. You will spend more time with your team outside of the classroom than within. If you cannot attend class for some reason, you must email both instructors with a written excuse or your grade for the associated project will be affected. More than three unexcused absences may result in failing the course.

### Other Course Policies:

**Other course work**

If you would like to use Game Dev 1 work as a part of another course’s project or assignment, or another course’s project or assignment as part of your Game Dev 1 game, you must first get the OK of the professors in both courses. Failure to do so could result in failure in this course.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Trust: Student-Teacher relationships are built on trust. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. And, teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts, which violate this trust, undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

Plagiarism: All work produced in this course must be original and created by the student. Incidents of academic dishonesty or attempted academic dishonesty will result in grade penalties and may be referred to the Dean of Students. While the creation of digital artwork may utilize prior works for inspiration, reference, and source material, assignments in this class are expected to represent the student’s originality and technical ability through the creation of substantially new works. When in doubt, ask your instructor.

AI: As Artificial Intelligence Creation methods become more prevalent, students will be able to utilize them in industry congruent workflows. As AI still can not be copyrighted and is not prevalently used in final products, AI will not be allowed to demonstrate final art capabilities.

AI can be used for proof of concept, art direction, and conceptualization. And all usages should be cited in the design document. Prompts, software, and appearances in pitches, documents, mood boards, etc.

Premade Assets: Existing asset libraries such as cubebrush, epic games store, artstation, etc. should be used for quick ideation, greyboxing, proof of concepts, prototypes, etc. Generally these assets should be replaced over time, and especially if recognizable. Keep a record of these assets in your design document as they are implemented.

Purposeful Ambiguity: This course is treated as a production course. And often the guidelines will be vague, abstract, or not specific.

- This is done to mimic an industry level setting where developers must be comfortable working with loose direction from their Creative and Art directors.
- It may be frustrating at first, but it is necessary to become a more unique and professional developer.
  - ○ The instructors will always be available to help when necessary.

Discussion/production of sensitive topics: As this is a creative field it is possible that there will be production of subject matter that some may deem offensive, inappropriate, etc. The instructors will make every effort to keep the discussion and production of such material safe, productive, rich, and uncensored.

- Art often challenges our operating assumptions, and a university setting makes sure to do that as often as possible.
