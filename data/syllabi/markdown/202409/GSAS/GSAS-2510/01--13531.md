## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Intro to Game Design is a course that explores the basic theory and practical aspects of game design. This exploration extends to various forms of games and their cultural significance. The core methodology in this class involves crafting playable games through a rapid prototyping and iterative design process. While the course focuses on the creation of non-digital games, discussions about digital games will also take place. The coursework comprises several short assignments along with a final project. Each assignment requires students to write a design statement, explaining the game’s concept, gameplay mechanics, and overall design approach. For the final project, students will select and further develop a project that was previously completed in the class by refining its design and presentation.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Collaborate as a class to develop a heuristic model for game evaluation
- Utilize an agile workflow
- Build a portfolio by reflecting on hands-on practice of designing analog games (Group dynamics, iterative design, game mechanics)
- Read and reflect upon different approaches to design
- Define terms contextually (historically situated, disciplinary context)
- Explain the concepts of copyright and fair use
- Judge the reliability/relevance/and usability of articles and other media

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Grading standards for this class are high. Game development is one of the most competitive fields in the job market, with design being even competitive inside game development itself.

1. A’s will denote work that is currently representative of industry level practices and quality.
2. B’s mean the student is rendering work just on the cusp of transitioning into professional quality, but still has visible denotations of student work. All required assignment fields submitted, on time, and to denoted level of quality.
3. C’s translate to student work that is clearly rendered by a student hand and eye. The effort and time is present, but the quality level is not on the cusp of being valuable to the game industry.
4. D’s & F’s when students are not only delivering work that is not industry level, but also lacks the effort of a committed student, and has failed to include the required amount of assets, techniques, and expectations of each milestone.

###### Late Work

Submissions are due on their date, but there is flexibility in the submissions pending illness or other proclivities. Again make sure to communicate to the professor if you feel unable to submit the assignment on time.

###### Academic Integrity

**AI:** As Artificial Intelligence Creation methods become more prevalent, students will be able to utilize them in industry congruent workflows. As AI still can not be copyrighted and is not prevalently used in final products, AI will not be allowed to demonstrate final art capabilities.

AI can be used for proof of concept, art direction, and conceptualization. And all usages should be cited in the design document. Prompts, software, and appearances in pitches, documents, mood boards, etc.

**Premade Assets:** Existing asset libraries such as cubebrush, epic games store, artstation, etc. should be used for quick ideation, greyboxing, proof of concepts, prototypes, etc. Generally these assets should be replaced over time, and especially if recognizable. Keep a record of these assets in your design document as they are implemented.

**Purposeful Ambiguity:** This course is treated as a production course. And often the guidelines will be vague, abstract, or not specific.

- This is done to mimic an industry level setting where developers must be comfortable working with loose direction from their Creative and Art directors.
- It may be frustrating at first, but it is necessary to become a more unique and professional developer.
  - The instructors will always be available to help when necessary.

**Discussion/production of sensitive topics:** As this is a creative field it is possible that there will be production of subject matter that some may deem offensive, inappropriate, etc. The instructors will make every effort to keep the discussion and production of such material safe, productive, rich, and uncensored.

- Art often challenges our operating assumptions, and a university setting makes sure to do that as often as possible.
