## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course reviews the influence of lighting conditions on people's visual capabilities, perceptions, moods, and alertness, with understanding derived from the structure and operating characteristics of the human visual system. A review of the human factors issues related to applications such as office lighting, retail lighting, emergency lighting, outdoor lighting, security lighting.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Textbook: Boyce, P.R., 2014. *Human Factors in Lighting*, 3rd Edition, London: CRC Press.

Additional readings will be maintained on reserve in the Lighting Research Center's Resource Collection Library, or distributed to students in class.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course will help the students further their understanding of how lighting affect peoples’ visual performance, comfort, and behavior and how this understanding can be applied to lighting applications. At the conclusion of the course, students will be able to:

- Understand how the visual system works
- Analyze the visual requirements of various visual tasks; make and critique lighting recommendations
- Specify the stimulus presented to the visual system under various conditions
- Recognize and predict lighting conditions likely to cause discomfort, generate specific impressions, and/or modify behavior
- Conduct assessments in terms of the lighting effects on people

## Course Content

### Content Details:

- Introduction to the course
- Basics of the human visual system
- Sources of light
- Spatial vision
- Visual comfort
- Light and color
- Flicker and stroboscopic effects
- Perception
- Brightness
- Non-visual effects of light
- Lighting in different applications (e.g., work, commercial, outdoor)
- Mesopic vision
- Lighting and health
- Lighting and energy use
