## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

*This laboratory course reiterates the important concepts learned in the Physics of Light class and provides hands-on experience to instruct students in the use of different types of light meters and laboratory instruments.*

*This course continues instruction in the various theories and laws of the physics of light Learning the following two topics is important for students enrolled in the MS in Architectural Science degree with a concentration in lighting.*

1. *Radiometry is the science of the measurement of radiant energy and photometry is the science of the measurement of light as perceived by the human eye. This laboratory class concentrates on the measurement aspects and teaches the different instruments and laboratory measurement equipment that are necessary to quantify electromagnetic radiation and light. Furthermore, it teaches industry recommended and best laboratory practices.*
2. *Optics is an important topic that studies the behavior and properties of light. Learning the science of how light moves from one place to another via different media and its behavior when it interacts with objects is fundamental for the application of light for general illumination. An important aspect of this is the understanding the optical elements needed to redirect light. As with most lighting concepts, successfully learning these concepts is mostly achieved though observation and experimentation in a laboratory or realistic setting.*

*This is the only course within the School of Architecture that teaches these above concepts for architectural students who need to understand and measure lighting in the built environment.*

*Even though optics and radiation may be topics taught in the Physics department, the application of the theory and quantification of light required in architecture are not taught. There is no other course at RPI that teaches these topics in the context of built environment and its occupants.*

*The required qualified faculty, staff, facilities, and instruments needed for this laboratory course are available at the School of Architecture’s Lighting Research Center.*

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The primary objective of this course is to provide the students with basic understanding and hands-on measurements involved in the Physics of light, to introduce some of the important concepts in lighting, and addresses the science of measuring visible light.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- integrate theory and practice through the operation of experiments and through the acquisition and use of experimental data
- take accurate and meaningful lighting measurements and perform experiments and report results in a professional manner
- conduct uncertainty analysis for quantified experiment data and information. apply quantified experiment data and information in the design of luminaires and architectural lighting systems
- apply their knowledge of photometric quantities and measurement in the performance of lighting research
- apply their knowledge of colorimetry to lighting research, design, and evaluation
- independently design and conduct experimentation, data collection and analysis to synthesize the concepts and outcomes learned in the theory and laboratory classes
