## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course introduces you various econometric methods for analyzing econometric data. It is designed specially for senior undergraduate or students in master program. This course emphasizes both the theoretical and the practical aspects of econometric analysis, focusing on techniques for estimating econometric models of various kinds and for conducting tests of hypotheses of interest to economists. Topics covered this semester include linear and nonlinear regression model and its various extensions with classical assumptions violated. qualitative and limited dependent variables, panel data and time series econometrics, forecasting, robust estimation and applied econometrics.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

James H. Stock and Mark W.Watson, “Introduction to Econometrics”, 4th edition, Pearson, 2018

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The main goal of this course is to provide students an intermediate level introduction to standard econometric theory.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Topics (subject to change):

- Review of Probability and statistics
- Simple linear regression model
- linear regression with multiple regressors
- Nonlinear regression
- Assessing Studies based on multiple regression
- Regression with panel data
- Regression with a Binary Dependent Variables
- Instrumental variables regressions
- Experiments and Quasi-Experiments
- Prediction with Many Regressors and Big Data
- Time series regression and forecasting
- Estimation of Dynamic causal effects
- Additional topics in time series regression
- The Theory of Linear Regression with One Regressor
- The Theory of Multiple Regression

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to demonstrate understanding of quantitative, data and modeling skills to identify causal effects such as developing testable hypothesis, building basic models for data analysis, drawing inferences, and discussing conclusions. Students will be able to show proficiency in: (a) Interpreting and performing linear regression analysis; (b) Conducting hypothesis testing for simple and multiple regressions and interpreting the inference results; (c) Demonstrating understanding of regression analysis with linear probability model; (d) Applying instrumental variables models to estimate causal relationships. (e) demonstrating an ability to critique empirical studies in economics.
