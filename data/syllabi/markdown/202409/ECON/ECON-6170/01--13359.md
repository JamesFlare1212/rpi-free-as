## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Examine various facets of the healthcare industry. Application of microeconomic theory and tools will be used to: analyze healthcare demand and supply; examine the impact of public policies; study market segments such as health insurance and pharmaceuticals; understand the role played by technological changes; explore economic choices of healthcare professionals; identify potential socioeconomic and demographic determinants of health status and healthcare; and gain insights into issues related to the ongoing healthcare policy reform.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

*Health Economics* by J. Bhattacharya, T. Hyde, and P. Tu (2013)

I will also assign additional readings/articles from academic, policy, and industry sources. I will announce these in advance and make them available online via Learning Management System (LMS). Tests will cover these supplementary materials.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Analyze the healthcare industry using microeconomic theory and tools.
- • Examine market segments such as health insurance and pharmaceuticals through the lens of economics.
- • Gain insights into the role of technological advancements in healthcare.
- • Identify economic implications behind the current healthcare policy reforms.
- [For students taking ECON 6170]: • Determine appropriate method of collecting and analyzing economic data, formulating hypotheses, and drawing inferences.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

There will be two assignments and I will announce the due dates at least two weeks in advance. Assignments will be due at the beginning of class. Although students may work on these assignments with other students, each student must submit independently written work. **No late assignments will be accepted.**

Midterms:

There will be two in-class midterms. These midterms will be closed note and closed book, and they will be all cumulative. **There will be no makeup midterms. The only exceptions include: 1. Illness (must provide a doctor’s note) 2. RPI-approved official activities (must provide documentation).**

Midterm #1 approximately on October 15

Midterm #2 approximately on November 26

I may change midterm dates marginally. I will let you know in advance if there are any changes.

Research Paper:

Each student will write a research paper and make class presentations on it. Note that there is no final exam for this course and instead students will submit the final version of the paper. I will provide more information on research paper/presentation later in the semester. Importantly, I will communicate directly with each student during the semester and make sure that everyone receives the proper guidance on this.

- 1\) **ECON 4170:** Students may work on research papers individually, or as a group with no more than two students per group. Students can do meta-analyses or surveys of existing studies to answer their research questions.

**ECON 6170**: Students must work on research papers individually. Writing a research paper will require defining a research question, doing a literature review, collecting and analyzing data, and presenting results and implications. Research papers must show evidence of rigorous and direct applications of microeconomic and statistical tools.

I will schedule meetings with each student/group to provide guidelines throughout the research process and address any related issues.

- 2\) This class is designed to meet RPI’s Communication Intensive (CI) Requirement: a minimum of 15 pages (double-space, 12-point font size, and one-inch margins) is required per student. A paper submitted by a group of two students needs to be at least 25 pages in length (ECON 4170 students only).
- 3\) Students will be asked to submit intermediate drafts of the paper and I will provide feedback on them during the semester. Students are expected to address any major/minor comments in their next drafts. This is a good way to ensure that everyone stays on track. I will announce the due dates as we make progress.
- 4\) Along with the submission of intermediate drafts of the paper, students will make two class presentations (10-20 minutes each) to show progress and interactively share research ideas and findings with other students. Presentations may be on research question, literature review, data analysis, and results and implications, etc. I will announce the dates as we make progress.
- 5\) For students working as a group, each group member will take turns and present different parts of the paper (ECON 4170 students only). **It is very important that each member contributes equally to the paper, and so there must be a clear sense of each member’s contributions.**
- 6\) **Class attendance is required for all students on presentation days.** There will be a 10 points deduction (out of 100) from your research paper for each class you miss when other students are presenting their work. Excused absences require formal documentation (same as makeup midterms).
- 7\) The final version of the paper will be due by 5 PM on Friday, December 13. **This deadline is firm and will not be extended for any reason.**

Class Participation:

Although class attendance is not mandatory (except on presentation days), students are strongly encouraged to come to class and participate in discussions.

Also, I will give in-class exercises to evaluate students’ understanding of lecture materials. These will be graded on a 0-1 scale and students are allowed drop the lowest score before grades are calculated. **There will be no makeups for these in-class exercises.**

**Assessment for ECON 4170 vs. ECON 6170 students:**

There will be different expectations for the research projects between students enrolled in 4000-level and 6000-level courses. At the 4000 level, students can do meta-analyses or surveys of existing studies to answer their research questions. At the 6000 level, projects must involve more rigorous and direct applications of microeconomic and statistical tools. This should include collecting publicly available data and analyzing them to provide results and implications based on microeconomic theory.

## Course Calendar

### Calendar Details:

I will follow the textbook chapters closely for many topics. I will focus on the key aspects from the chapters during class, and so students are expected to read and study the remaining materials on their own. I will also assign readings/articles from academic, policy, and industry sources to supplement the textbook. All lecture materials, assigned textbook chapters, and supplementary readings will be relevant for midterms. Some of these topics will be particularly relevant for research papers.

Chapter 1: Why health economics? (5-page introduction)

Chapter 2: Demand for health care

Chapter 3: Demand for health: the Grossman model

Chapter 4: Socioeconomic disparities in health

Chapter 5: The labor market for physicians

Chapter 12: Pharmaceuticals and the economics of innovation

Chapter 13: Technology and the price of health care

Chapter 14: Health technology assessment

Chapter 15: The health policy conundrum

Note that I will emphasize some of these chapters and spend more time covering specific topics of interest while limiting the time allocated to other chapters.
