## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This first course in a two-course sequence analyzes micro- and meso-economics of innovation. Innovation is the direct cause of most of economic growth, and is essential to address societal problems such as climate change. At the micro level, we will examine where innovations come from and what incentivizes innovation, company R&D and roles of small and large businesses, diffusion of innovations into use, and science. At the meso level, we will examine industries, and the dynamics of science and industry. We will consider theoretical models and empirical analysis, and their interrelationships. We will consider data sources on R&D, innovation, and technology. We will seek appropriate policy. We will ask questions and look at how to answer those questions through our own research.  
(Macroeconomic growth and international trade are mainly left to the second course.)

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Analyze determinants of the amounts and types of innovation, through science, R&D, competing companies, and diffusion into use.
- Explain and apply economic models and econometrics to understand facts and data about innovation processes.
- Propose and evaluate research on innovation.

### Criteria Details:

At minimum the paper should be a specific research plan with research question and motivation, key context citing relevant literature, methods, and potential interpretation. Cite literature in all sections as appropriate. Data or modeling proposed should be feasible. You are strongly encouraged, to the extent data can be obtained during the semester, to complete initial data analyses and reporting. Your paper is due to me by Sat. 12/7 at 9:00 pm.
