## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course addresses classical and contemporary issues in the theory of money and banking. Topics covered include: financial markets and interest rates, commercial banking, the Federal Reserve System, theories of money demand and money supply, and the theoretical and practical aspects of monetary policy and economic stabilization. Subject matter classifies as macroeconomics, but also draws from microeconomic fundamentals.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- to gain a factual knowledge of money, banking, and the financial system
- to practice interpreting data in the monetary context
- to understand monetary policy and its implications for national and world economies.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Tests: There will be three tests administered online via MyLab. The basis for tests will be textbook readings, MyLab assignments, and lecture material. Each test will be made available for a period of seven days; see MyLab for availability dates. Test format is take-at-home, open book/note, multiple choice/numerical answer. Each student will receive a unique test. Tests must be completed in one session and will be timed. There is no late window for tests. Practice work for tests is available in MyLab and will be reviewed in class.

**MAKEUP TEST POLICY:** *Makeup tests will only be allowed at the discretion of professor. If allowed, the makeup test is scheduled during the week after the test.*

MyLab Assignments: Assignments using the Pearson MyLab online system will be due on three dates. These assignments include two types: 1) three sets of Homeworks and Quizzes which pertain to lecture/readings, and 2) one set of monetary data analysis problems, called the Fed Project. All MyLab Homeworks, Quizzes, and the Fed Project are available from the start of the semester through their due date/time. Due dates are cut-off dates for the submission of work. Due dates are not meant to be dates for you to begin or even finish work. It is expected that all students will complete work in a time efficient manner according to the outline described below. This means that work should be completed on a weekly basis and in advance of the due date. Any student who saves work to complete on the due date must expect that technical difficulties will prevent the finishing of said work by the cut-off time and credit will be lost. All assignments will be available for late completion until the end of the semester; a 10% late penalty will apply for assignments completed after the due date. The final “late” due date for all assignments is 4/25.

FED Essay: Students will submit a written analysis of the most recent meeting of the Federal Open Market Committee, to be submitted in LMS by the due date specified; specific instructions for content are posted to LMS and will be explained in class. The policy for academic integrity with regards to plagiarism from any source including from former students is in full effect for this and all work completed. Any student who is not confident of their writing ability is urged to contact the *Center for Global Communication+Design* called “Comm+D Center”, located in the lower level Folsom Library; *If you would like help with writing assignments, visual design projects, or oral presentations, please visit the Center online at* *https://info.rpi.edu/comm-d* *to find helpful resources or to schedule an appointment.* *Comm+D is a FREE resource for all members of the Rensselaer community.* Late Policy: essays are accepted late at the discretion of professor.

Optional Final Exam: An optional final exam will be offered as a way for students to attempt to improve their grade. The final exam will cover all course material. If you take the final exam, the score you obtain on the final exam will be compared with the average of your mid-semester tests. You will be awarded the higher of these scores as your grade for the test portion of the course. *If you miss any of the three mid-semester* test(s)*, then plan on taking the final exam to improve your grade.*
