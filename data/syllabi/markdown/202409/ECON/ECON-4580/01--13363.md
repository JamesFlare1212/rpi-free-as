## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides skills to apply econometrics to diverse economic topics. Econometric methods are developed for tools such as instrumental variables, regression discontinuity, difference-in-differences method, quantile regressions, and spatial regressions. Methods to identify causal effects are emphasized. Students do hands-on data work that puts to use these econometric tools.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate modern econometric methods and explain key mathematical concepts behind these methods.
- Implement statistical inference and draw meaningful interpretation from the analysis.
- Apply appropriate econometric techniques to data analysis and report the research findings.
- Gain competence in the statistical software R.
- Evaluate the validity of econometric analysis in empirical studies.
- Conduct independent (individual) research to develop expertise in selected topics of interests in applied econometrics.

**Weekly Assignments:**

Unless otherwise noted, one assignment will be given at the end of each lecture on Monday. You can work in groups on homework assignments, but each student must turn in his or her own version of the assignment. **Please submit your homework in LMS before 8 PM of the due date. Late submission will not be accepted.**

All assignments will be graded on a 0-100 scale. The lowest score will be dropped when final grades are calculated. Problem sets will usually be checked for the percentage completed, although parts may be graded if deemed necessary to benefit student learning. Please check your answers against the solutions given out, and ask for help if needed.

Some problem sets will ask you to estimate econometric models and interpret the results. Your grade will depend on both whether or not you apply appropriate econometric methods, and on how well you explain your models and discuss your results.

This course will also focus on the importance of computational methods and *reproducibility* in econometric research. Thus, **you should also submit your electronic code files in LMS before the due date of the assignments (if computational analysis is required).** If you have any questions about your submission, feel free to contact me. The recommended software for the computational aspects of the course is R. R is an open source project. You can download it for free from: *http://cran.r-project.org/*.

In R, Wooldridge’s package can be installed by the following R commands:

*install.packages("wooldridge")*

*library(wooldridge)*

**Midterm:**

The first midterm will take place after Chapter 8. This midterm will cover all materials from Chapter 2 to Chapter 8. The midterm-II will be a take-home exam. All exams must be taken completely alone. **Late submission will not be accepted. The only exceptions include: 1. Illness (must provide a doctor’s note) 2. RPI-approved official activities (must provide documentation in advance).**

**Project:**

Students will work in groups on projects that are often chosen to fit their interests. The goal of this group work is to enable students to build experience in research using the econometrics tools taught in this class. Depending on the availability of the data, topics can be flexible. You may need to collect the economic data related to your topic, apply the appropriate econometric methods taught in this course, articulate your results and construct tables and figures. A complete project report must consist of (1) introduction, including literature review, (2) econometric models, (3) data description and statistical analysis, (4) conclusion, and (5) reference. Your project should be no more than 10 pages (excluding reference, tables and figures).

More instructions about the final project will be given after the first midterm exam. This final project should be submitted **before 8 PM, 12/9/2024**.
