## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course examines how the US labor market works, addressing topics of labor supply and labor demand, theories of wage determination, employment benefits and regulations, how wage differentials develop and evolve, and the growing role of technology, automation, and artificial intelligence. We will pay special attention to the effects of discrimination, using economic theory and empirical methods to help us understand how gender, racial, and sexual minority discrimination affect earnings and employment. We will also examine the effects of education and skills training on wages and employment prospects, focusing on the effects of training in Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics (STEM).

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- By the end of the semester students will be able to: Explain the central features of the US labor market to others, including how they impact employment conditions, wages, and benefits.
- Explain to others what drives differences in earnings among workers, including the effects of education, experience, and effort.
- Define and explain discrimination to others, including how discrimination differs from prejudice and the differences between statistical and taste-based discrimination.
- Describe the extent of disparities in outcomes in the United States to others, including disparities by gender, race, sexual orientation, national origin, age, health behaviors, and disability status.
- Explain the key research methods used to identify differences in labor market outcomes between groups, including uncovering evidence of discrimination in market/economic settings.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

1. **Problem Sets (25 points)**

There will be at least five problem sets that will require you to work on various questions relating to economic theory, econometric/statistical issues, or to provide thoughtful written answers to prompts. I will count your best five “problem set” scores towards your grade (5 points each).

2. **Exams (75 points = 3 exams at 25 points each)**

The first exam will focus on economic theory. The second will focus on applied economics, including statistics/econometrics. The third exam will be focused on “evaluating evidence” and will involve several questions based on an academic article that you have not seen before. See course schedule at the end of this document for anticipated exam dates.

3. **Quizzes (10 bonus points)**

We will have at least five short quizzes (2 pts each) based on assigned readings. These will be graded as check (2 pts) or check minus (1 pt). I will count your best five scores on these quizzes. You score zero if you do not take the quiz.
