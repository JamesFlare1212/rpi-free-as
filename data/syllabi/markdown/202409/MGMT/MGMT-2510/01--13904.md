## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Office Hours (1 point)**

To earn this point, you’ll need to drop by during office hours **no later** than September 9.

**Participation (5 points)**

It is not sufficient to make simple comments in class; you are expected to help the class to develop a shared understanding of issues related to the focal topic/problem.

Note: You may lose participation points if you are using your laptop (and other electronic devices) for a non-class related activity (e.g., watching videos, social media, games).

**Attendance (4 points)**

Attendance is mandatory. You begin the semester with 4 points and are allowed up to two unexcused absences. After that, you will lose 1 point for each unexcused absence.

If by the end of the semester you had missed more than ⅓ of the classes you will not be permitted to take Test 4 and would likely fail the course.

Note: To be counted as present, you must **arrive on time and stay for the duration of the class**.

**Tests (80 points)**

There are 4 tests (20 points, each). **All 4 tests will count toward your final grade**. The tests will afford you an opportunity to demonstrate your mastery of the skills you learned in the preceding course component. Active class participation will help improve your chances of doing better on the tests and quizzes. **There is no end-of-term test**.

**Pop Quizzes (10 points)**

There will also be ~12 in-class pop quizzes. Each quiz will focus on the topic from the previous class. **I will drop your lowest two (2) quiz scores**. If you miss a quiz, there is no “make up” pop quiz unless you can convince the Student Experience Office and me of the *extreme circumstances*.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. **Respect the comments of your peers:** One aspect of learning is the exchange of ideas. Respect the opinions of your peers (even if you disagree with them) by listening actively. Present your arguments objectively by sticking with the relevant facts, using reason, and keeping a cool head. Focus on the topic, not the individual (i.e., depersonalize the issue). The purpose of the discussion is to attain a higher level of learning by examining diverse justified opinions, not to achieve consensus. Try to contribute to the conversation by raising questions or comments that will encourage learning, rather than engendering defensiveness or conflict.

Student-teacher relationships are based on trust. Students need to trust that the teacher has made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the course, and the teacher must trust that the assignments that you turn in are your own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty, and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent your own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in (1) an academic (i.e., grade) penalty, and (2) reporting to Lally’s Associate Dean of Academic Affairs and the Dean of Students.

In this course, the academic penalty for a first offense is an automatic forfeiture of the grading component where the offense occurred. A second offense will result in failure of the course as noted in *Lally’s Three Strikes Policy*.
