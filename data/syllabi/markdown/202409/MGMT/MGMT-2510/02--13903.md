## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

An introduction to the data driven decision making using Excel. Through hands-on exercises students will develop competencies in working with data through spreadsheets. Topics include foundational concepts in business process modeling, dashboard creation, and programming Excel.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Create Business Process Models
- Understanding basic Excel functionality
- Understanding advanced Excel functionality
- Understanding how to program Excel using Visual Basic for Applications

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Test 1: Business Process Model Fundamentals**

Percent of course grade: 20%

Students will be given a business process and will be required to build a Business Process diagram and an Entity-Relationship diagram.

**Test 2: Excel Basics**

Percent of course grade: 20%

Student will solve business problems using Excel functions.

**Test 3: Advanced Excel**

Percent of course grade: 20%

Students will use Access to write queries against a pre-built Data Warehouse.

**Test 4: Programming Excel**

Percent of course grade: 20%

Students will be tested on their mastery of Excel fundamentals through a series of problems.

**Final: TBD**

Percent of course grade: 20%

**If you have an average of 90.0 or better after the Programming Excel test, you do not have to take the final exam!**

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work.
