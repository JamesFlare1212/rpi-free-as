## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Introduction to financial markets, financial instruments, and basic investment principles. The course provides students with an understanding of how to value securities, how to assess risk and return tradeoffs, how to make investment decisions, and how to measure investment performance. Topics include market microstructure and impact of technology on securities markets, principles of investment banking, valuation of stocks and bonds and hybrid instruments, portfolio theory, asset pricing models, bond portfolio management, and derivative securities.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

As a result of this course, a student will be able to accomplish the following:

- The student will be able to describe the general structure of various financial markets.
- The student will be able to determine relative risk among investment cases and between investments consistent within an investment sector.
- The student will learn to study the future environment as compared to retrospective score keeping.
- The student will value financial instruments including common stock, preferred stock and fixed income securities.
- The student will create investment portfolio based on various competing objectives and competing constraints and assess portfolio performance.
- The student will obtain a basic understanding of financial derivatives.
- The student will demonstrate verbal and written skills in presenting investment decisions and in defending them as to clients, bosses, or other decision markers.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- The course focuses on investment analysis, which allows students to learn practical applications of investment theory and to convey insights of practical value. A systematic collection of Microsoft Excel spreadsheets gives students tools to explore concepts more deeply and improve their overall Excel skillset. These spreadsheets are available both from the text and the instructor and provide a taste of the sophisticated analytical tools available to professional investors. Investments I will introduce students to the major issues of concern to all investors. It gives students the skills to conduct sophisticated assessment of current issues and debates covered by both the popular media and more specialized finance journals. Whether a student plants to become an investment professional/Chartered Financial Analyst managing institutional portfolios or simply a sophisticated investor aiming to produce an ample retirement, students will find the skills in this course essential. In summary, Investments I allow students to discover what type of investor they are (passive, active, some combination, etc.) that aligns risk tolerance with investor behavior and provides frameworks and knowledge to implement the appropriate strategies based on these individual preferences.
