## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is an introductory course in business statistics. This course will provide participants the quantitative foundations for statistical methods, which will be used extensively in future management courses. In this course, students will learn the basic concepts and tools in statistics, and then apply the techniques on phenomena of interest. In particular, students learn to present and describe data, analyze probability distributions, make statistical inferences based on data samples, and develop models for prediction and forecasting.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate and understand the basic concepts and tools in business statistics.
- Distinguish between different statistical tests and identify the usage of different tests
- Conduct statistical tests of hypothesis and infer conclusions.
- Use software for statistical analysis and apply statistical methods in a real business environment.
- Discuss the ethical considerations of data analysis.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- This course is designed with the expectation that students will dedicate two hours per every lecture-hour studying and preparing for the class. In other words, 8 hours per week (excluding lectures).
- Attendance, Preparation, and Participation: Students are expected to attend all lectures, come prepared by having done the assigned readings, and participate during lectures.

One-minute memos:

As part of the class participation grade and to track attendance, students will be asked to write a one-minute memo to be submitted online at the end of some of the lectures. The purpose of the one-minute memo is to establish a direct line of communication between the students and the instructor. The memo will help the instructor assess the class’ understanding of the main concepts discussed during the lectures, identify unclear points, and address any other concerns.

- I want students to feel that they can be open and honest in these memos, so your grade is not affected by the content of these memos. I will respond to the major questions raised in the memos during the following lecture. Missing a class or failing to submit a memo will result in an absence being recorded.
- In-Class Group MiniLabs: During certain lectures, there will be in-class group minilabs. These will take place either at the beginning or the end of the lecture and must be completed via the LMS platform, so students should bring their laptops or tablets to every class. Minilabs will be unannounced and will cover material from the previous two lectures, focusing on key concepts discussed. These minilabs are intended to foster a collaborative group dynamic, enhance class participation, and reinforce the application of course concepts. To accommodate unforeseen circumstances, the lowest two minilab grades will be dropped. Please note, however, that there will be NO Make-Up Minilabs. If you miss a minilab, it will count as one of your two dropped grades.
- Assignments: There will be a number of self-assessment assignments (10-12 assignments) posted online to help you with the material. Each assignment will be graded based on general effort, and completion. Students are responsible for turning in completed assignments by the due date. Unless the student has made prior arrangements with the instructor, no late assignments will be accepted. Solutions will be provided immediately after the due dates.
- Exams: There will be three exams. Each exam will cover material discussed in class up to the exam date. Although the material covered build upon each other, the subsequent exams will focus mainly on material covered after the last exam. Tests are individual work; students cannot collaborate in any way. Copying, communicating or using any materials during an exam is cheating unless it is an open-book exam that will be decided by the instructor. THERE ARE NO MAKE-UP EXAMS (unless previously coordinated with the instructor).

The student should refrain from using electronic devices for email, texting, chatting, games, social media, or any other activities not related to class since they can be distracting to the student and others.

You can use any calculator you would like when solving problems on the problem sets, including scientific, programmable, graphing, financial, or software (such as Excel or R programming). However, on the exams, you may not be allowed to use a computer, phone, or any other wireless or broadband device unless stated otherwise by the instructor prior to the exam. In order to prepare for this, I strongly advise everyone to make sure that you have a calculator available that you can use to solve problems at exam time. Financial calculators are permitted on the exams.
