## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate and understand the basic concepts and tools in business statistics.
- Distinguish between different statistical tests and identify the usage of different tests.
- Conduct statistical tests of hypothesis and infer conclusions.
- Use software for statistical analysis and apply statistical methods in a real business environment.
- Discuss the ethical considerations of data analysis.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**(a)** **Class Attendance.** This course is a face-to-face course. Attendance is required. Students are expected to attend all lectures, come prepared by having done the assigned readings, and participate during lectures.  
**(b)** **In-Class Exercise**. There will be in-class exercises for some of the lectures. These tasks are unannounced. The exercises will be based on the concepts taught in the class and serve the purpose of taking attendance. The submission deadline will be determined in class based on class progress. Make-up exercises will NOT be offered.  
**(c)** **Homework Assignments.** There are 7 homework assignments in the semester. Each student is expected to finish all homework questions individually. All homework is due 11:59pm on the specified dates. **Late submission is subject to 50% penalty**, unless the student has made prior arrangements with the instructor. The lowest homework score among the seven homework scores will be dropped. Solutions will be posted after the deadline.  
**(d)** **Mid-Term Exams.** There are 3 mid-term exams. See the course schedule (the last part) for the exam coverage. Tests are individual work; students cannot collaborate in any way. Copying, communicating or using any materials during an exam is cheating unless it is an open-book exam that will be decided by the instructor.  
In accordance with the updated university policy (https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/current-students/academic-and-personal-support/requesting-excused-absence), formal excuses are not required for medical absences lasting up to one week. To accommodate unforeseen events or preplanned opportunities that may conflict with scheduled exam times, I allow **the lowest mid-term score to be dropped**.  
However, make-up exams will only be granted under specific circumstances, such as documented illness, family emergencies, major religious holidays, or official university business. Students must consult with me before or on the exam day to arrange for a make-up exam. Requests for make-up exams after the exam day will NOT be accepted. Please note that make-up exams will NOT be provided for students seeking to benefit from the updated policy regarding medical absences.  
**(e)** **Final Exams.** There is one final exam. The final exam is comprehensive and cumulative. Tests are individual work; students cannot collaborate in any way. Copying, communicating or using any materials during an exam is cheating unless it is an open-book exam that will be decided by the instructor.  
Student Success does not issue excuses during **final exams**, contact your Class Dean immediately if you missed a final exam. In extreme circumstances (such as being hospitalized), your Class Dean may intercede on your behalf and issue a "No Examination Grade."  
In general, **no make-up exams will be given**. In case you have a legitimate reason for missing an exam (for example, documented illness or family emergency, major religious holidays, or official university business), consult with the instructor before or on the exam day. Students should make arrangements to avoid time conflicts. Request for make-up tests after the exam day is not accepted.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
