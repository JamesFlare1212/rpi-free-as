## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Digitization is rapidly transforming the nature of work and fundamentally reshaping organizations. Organizational decision-making is increasingly augmented by algorithmic approaches, while the rise of digital solutions, especially Internet-based applications, is redefining both intra- and inter-organizational linkages. These technologies are revolutionizing market exchanges, enhancing consumer access, and fostering collaboration. Additionally, they are reshaping the economics of transactions, enabling the formation of novel business relationships within organizational networks, such as supply chains. In this rapidly evolving landscape, understanding the challenges and opportunities associated with managing organizations in a digitized context is critical.

This doctoral seminar delves into the assumptions, theories, and methodologies that inform the study of the strategic and organizational implications of digitization. Through extensive reading and in-depth discussion of the research literature, the course aims to provide doctoral students with a comprehensive understanding of the diverse theoretical frameworks, methods, levels of analysis, and perspectives that underpin research on the impact of IT on organizations and markets.

To grasp the foundations of management in the digital age, it is essential to explore how researchers from various disciplines approach the topic of digital transformation. Throughout this semester, we will examine digital transformation from multiple perspectives, uncovering how it serves as a cornerstone for effective management in the digital age.

The course is designed for doctoral students across all disciplines. As digitization permeates research in marketing, management, operations management, strategy, finance, and accounting, the opportunities to explore research questions that explicitly address the effects of digitization are expanding across all fields. This seminar offers doctoral students a unique opportunity to engage with the theories and research concerning the use and impact of digital technologies on organizations and markets.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate knowledge of concepts, theories, and assumptions related to the strategic and organizational implications of digitization.
- Demonstrate skill in interpreting, evaluating, and extending current research streams on the impact of digital technologies on organizations and markets.

- Present research on the implications of digitization to an academic audience, effectively communicating complex ideas and findings.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Class Participation and Discussion Expectations**

In every class session, all students are expected to engage actively in the discussion of the assigned readings. While the designated discussion leader will guide the conversation, every student should thoroughly read each assigned article and come prepared to address the following critical questions:

1. Assessment of the Article’s Contribution:
   - o What do you believe is the article’s primary contribution to its field? Is the contribution theoretically significant or novel? Why or why not?
   - o How does this article advance our understanding of the topic it addresses?
2. Article Structure and Presentation:
   - o Analyze the construction of the article. How is it written and organized? Consider its positioning within the broader literature, the effectiveness of the introduction, and the overall narrative flow.
   - o Specifically, how do the authors frame the research problem? What theories do they reference, and how are the hypotheses or propositions developed? Evaluate the clarity and coherence of the arguments presented.
3. Evaluation of Theoretical and Empirical Arguments:
   - o For both theoretical and empirical papers, how effectively do the authors build their arguments to support the theory and hypotheses/propositions? Are these arguments convincing? Why or why not?
   - o Do you find the hypotheses or propositions to be testable? What recommendations can you provide for improvement? Are there additional research methods that could enhance the study?
4. Critique of Research Methods (For Empirical Papers):
   - o Critically evaluate the research methods used in the study. Consider the design, how well it aligns with the arguments and constructs, the measures employed, the construction of the sample, and the analysis techniques.
   - o Would you categorize this article as a strong or weak example of research? Identify potential threats to the validity of the study’s conclusions. What improvements could be made? Do you agree with the authors’ interpretations? Why or why not?
5. Opportunities for Extension and Improvement:
   - o How might we extend or build upon the ideas presented in this article? Where should the research go from here? Discuss the broader implications for the research stream.
6. Managerial Implications:
   - o What is the most significant takeaway from this article for a practicing manager? How could the findings be applied in a real-world managerial context?

**Course Assessment Measures:**

1. **Intermediate Paper Assignments (10%):**
   - o It is important to make early and steady progress through the semester. These assignments are primarily to obtain initial feedback. In each case you will receive full credit or be asked to resubmit the assignment, incorporating feedback.
   - o
2. **Quality of Contribution During Class Discussions (30%):**
   - o Participation is critical in this doctoral seminar. Students are expected to attend every class, be well-prepared, and actively engage in discussions. Your ability to contribute insightful perspectives on the research articles will be evaluated. Punctuality and preparedness are key. Each student will also serve as a discussion leader, presenting assigned articles and guiding the seminar in exploring key concepts and theories. This role is an opportunity to enhance your understanding of the material and develop your presentation skills in an academic setting.
3. **Presentation of Articles as Discussion Leader (20%):**
   - o As a discussion leader, you are responsible for presenting two assigned articles, refreshing the class on the key concepts, and providing deep insights into the research. Your presentation should include a conceptual model or diagram to illustrate relationships between variables or constructs. You should present an overview of the article and then lead a class discussion (totaling 30-45 minutes). The presentation should be structured to maximize the learning experience for your peers, with an emphasis on critical analysis and interpretation.
4. **Final Paper and Presentations (40%):**
   - o The final paper is a combination of a theory paper and a research proposal. It should develop a testable model, supported by well-grounded theoretical arguments. The paper must be original, of a quality suitable for submission to a top-tier academic journal, and it should follow the formatting and methodological guidelines of leading journals such as the *Academy of Management Journal*. Additionally, you will peer-review other students’ papers, providing constructive feedback similar to a formal journal review.
