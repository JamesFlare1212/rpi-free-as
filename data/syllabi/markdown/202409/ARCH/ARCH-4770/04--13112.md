## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Here Comes the Flood  
Infinitely Scalable Gestures, Aeternal Artifacts, or:  
the Design of Worlds for Minds  
Professor: Edward Keller FALL 2024 Vertical Studio  
To help us define what’s at stake in 2024- as AGI and the ‘Singularity’ seem increasingly close- this studio  
will explore the relationship between architectural design and generative AI. We will work with concepts  
from speculative architecture, the philosophy of technology, deep ecology, cinema, emerging media- and  
of course science fiction. William Gibson has called the coming singularity ‘the Jackpot’ in his novel The  
Peripheral- indicating that it may bring both the arrival of unprecedented wealth, but that it also will create  
unprecedented catastrophe. Our design brief in 2024 should be to put some form of scaffolding in place  
that shepherds life through this singularity.  
Architecture’s conception of itself as a discipline has depended for centuries on a model of the artificial in  
relation to the natural, and the primal human action in the wilderness: to build a hut or make a bonfire?  
Within this tension between codecs of preservation or rituals of conflagration, our contemporary models of  
the world might deploy an alternative to the dialectic of ‘conservation of energy or expenditure’: what we  
can call a next nature or a third nature, within which all disciplines can operate.  
At the core of this inquiry is the role of architecture and gesture in relation to nature and cognition.  
A multimodal approach is uniquely suited to important lineages in architecture. We can refer to Tschumi’s  
work in the 1970s, or Superstudio and Archizoom; the RSVP process of the Halprins; and in cinema an  
entire body of work investigating space, gestures, cities and culture.  
The concept of gestural ecology- scaling from the molecule to the hand to the body to the city to the  
planet to the cosmos, and back- provides a way to situate any project in a broader and universal  
framework. [ youtu.be/oX4HLjxNmEU ]  
Our studio projects will propose some form of ‘Gesture’ and/or ‘Artifact’- an object, a building, a device, a  
component or subroutine of a system, a connective network even- which might catalyze various forms of  
culture and cognition; or perhaps create an untranslatable yet indexically charged architecture, indicating  
that a gesture had taken place, operating and persisting for millions, perhaps billions of years.

Balancing design approaches to the physical world against the intangible, ineffable world- we might find  
ourselves like St. Jerome in their study, this semester, as we work…  
SCHEDULE and METHOD  
weeks 1-4: a mini seminar and design charrette  
• read theory, history, scifi: Rainbows End, Sand County Almanac, Lem, other texts  
• experiment with AI image/space generation toolkits [NightCafe, Dreamstudio, Wombo, Midjourney, etc]  
• precedent research in architecture; also films,incl.: Westworld, Her, London, Playtime, Ghost in the  
Shell2, Blade Runner 2049, etc  
WEEK 4: FIRST REVIEWS of concepts in progress  
weeks 4-7; experimentation with AI image/space generation continues  
each designer develops their individual project, choosing ‘gestures’ or ‘artifacts’  
MIDREVIEW  
weeks 8-14: project development  
FINAL REVIEW

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- RESEARCH \<p> 1a Ability to use / develop Specialized knowledge: Ability to manipulate concepts, technologies and materials involved in the production of virtual architecture experiences that relate architectural experience with interactive, immersive design. \<p> 1b Ability to use / develop broad and integrative knowledge: Ability to manipulate multidisciplinary knowledge and techniques, towards developing virtual architecture concepts. Ability to plan the integration of audiovisual and interactive components in an immersive virtual environment, and design their testing.
- INTELLECTUAL DESIGN SKILLS \<p> 2a. Ability to design: Ability to independently propose, refine and develop a masterplan for a project that explores a concept goal for user experience and connectivity, and can be tested for its experiential impact, focusing on formats of virtual or mixed reality. \<p> 2b. Ability to design using information resources: Ability to distinguish, compare and evaluate virtual environments - digital or other - as either part of architectural/urban design typologies, or models of architectural speculations. \<p> 2d. Ability to design with ethical reasoning: Ability to recognize, distinguish and apply techniques of spatial narrative in immersive environments that produce intentional relationships with cultural and societal paradigms and/or values. \<p> 2e. Ability to design with quantitative fluency: Ability to integrate data or context features in a design proposal, that characterize a spatio-temporal situation. \<p> 2f. Ability to design with communicative fluency: Ability to document and present work in a clear, effective and impactful way.
- COLLABORATION \<p> Ability to collaborate closely with a partner - as a team - for the whole duration of the semester.
- CIVIC AND GLOBAL ENGAGEMENT \<p> 4a. Ability to Engage the Civic Realm: Ability to engage and test the political, social, cultural and ethical problematics of virtual spaces and digital materials and their role in the creation of platforms for new collective and/or public spaces. \<p> 4b. Ability to Engage the Global Realm: Ability to engage and test the political, social, cultural and ethical problematics of virtual spaces, and digital materials, and their role as network spaces for communities, with local and global reach.
- DESIGN EXECUTION \<p> 5a. Ability to produce a Proof-of-Concept: Ability to select, manipulate and connect digital tools that interweave project components to achieve proof of concept of a playable, interactive virtual experience with visual and acoustic components. This can be achieved through game design software or by chaining together several other pieces of software or strategies. \<p> 5b. Ability to produce a Working Proposal: Creative ability to design, develop and produce an immersive, interactive/playable project of a virtual or mixed reality environment.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Statement of Academic Integrity

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process. Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust will merit a failing grade in this course and the violator(s) will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.  
Students engaging in plagiarism will be asked to redo their projects and failing to do so, will automatically be a F in the course.
