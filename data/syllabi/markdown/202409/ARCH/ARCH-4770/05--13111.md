## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**OVERVIEW**  
The project of the INCEPTION Studio is to fully design - and play - an immersive spatial experience.

The studio will explore the potential of designing, creating, and translating the paradoxical and impactful aspects of virtual experience we are accustomed to within physical reality.

The range of spaces studied/tested will encompass architectural memories from walking tours or through projection from and into architectural drawings, as well as personal reconstructions of past memories and dreams - including near-implausible or ‘suspension of disbelief’ experiences drawn from scientific theories or science fiction.

The studio will look into the potential of virtual architecture platforms and immersive conditions to allow inhabitation of spaces that are not easily rendered through 3D space and matter, but which have a clear space in the imagination, including: spatial paradoxes, 3 1/2D & 4D space, non-spatial dimensions, perspectives, projections, parallax, infinite spaces, juxtapositions, simultaneities, and network structures.

There is an opportunity in this studio to engage topics or structures related with the current events fostered by the pandemic, which have substantially changed the nature and frequency of use of physical spaces and increased the use of virtual spaces and protocols (gestures, rules).

We will focus on understanding and developing structural and ‘material’ tectonic potentials to experience and realize these qualities, while inquiring upon their influence on the body and behavior, and creating critical assessments of the standalone quality of the experiences, as architectural bodies themselves.

**Architectures of: Paradox, Non-Sense, Palimpsest**

In “The Architecture of Deconstruction: Derrida’s Haunt”, Mark Wigley declares Architecture to be the discipline where the dream of Deconstructive Discourse falls apart. His argument being that the intrinsic vocabulary of architecture - that of constructibility - is the basis upon which analytical thought in philosophy and cultural disciplines is founded upon. There has been nevertheless a continuous legacy of interest by architects in using architectural representation to inquire on how to bring forth aspects of characters which are present in a situation and that are important to dimensions of ‘constructibility’, but which cannot be rendered in the traditional dimensions and techniques of tridimensional space. Inevitably, concepts of analysis and narrative collude here, as translation devices put themselves between reality and its representation.

**Incommensurable Realities**

There is, contemporarily, a great demand for representing apparent contradictions of real life. As we have evolved ever better models of reality, we find them increasingly doubled or paired - models of complexity, matter, weather or the universe continue to challenge comprehension and are often dual: needing either two or more non-integrated theories to explain their behaviors or only working within a particular framework of measuring that evolves a complete new code, logic and measuring instrumentation.

As known entities grow increasingly smaller and larger - nanoscale and hyper objects - the tendency might continue to be that of accepting, temporarily, assemblies of explanations for divergences of behavior in what should be a coherent whole. The 90’s and early 2000’s digital architecture renaissance in architecture supposedly engaged this problem through evolving alternative geometries and architectures sourced from the same original models which were trying to solve these ambiguities. This period was specially influenced by the connection with new media theory.

The navigation of the incommensurable is also an important character now as Virtual Reality flees escapism - by reattaching itself through Mixed Reality Reality to unpacking dimensions of the real -; and news media focuses on entertainment - preying on boredom. Both models - modeling and mining - rely on the existence of a plastic game set with easily definable avatars and clear cut operations. Within this context, there is immense opportunity in rethinking and playing out the fuel and machinery that augments, distorts - is in charge - of perception and comprehension.

Gameplay and Virtual Space Characters

The objective of designing ‘gameplay’ is to create an a priori attention to how questions about sequence and narrative are intrinsically tied to scale and contiguity.

All impossible architectures above mentioned have a common resistance to thought, that results from the attempt of building them as one entity. But as we consider them more as models that live in another kind of ‘materiality’ we do not quite understand yet, the more their reality becomes not only possible: but compelling and inspiring.

The objective is to re-install a new sense of spatial volumetric and of time, a new spatial being, a specter, a new presence that can resume from the distance of its surface, attracting, repelling, spinning, plugging within minds and bodies in new architectural ways.

For this reason, we will be working less with external conditions of reward and/or traditional gameplay and instead attempt to focus on the intrinsic conditions of space play that can evolve other models for ‘level’ - including emotion, affect and drive - and in this way, generate representational dilemmas and constructs.  
Ultimately, the studio wants to reflect on how these constructs ambition to represent or navigate the physically impossible offer clues on how to re-install possibility and potentiality across social and cultural domains.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Course Goals/Objectives

Introduction to concepts, technologies and materials involved in the production of virtual architecture experiences. Introduction and discussion of concepts that relate architectural and cultural experience with interactive, immersive design.

Develop critical understanding of the role of virtual and immersive constructs and techniques to inquire and develop ways in which architectural experience can be produced and complex concepts of site be formulated.

Learn formats that integrate audiovisual and interactive components in an immersive environment, and test their impact on spatial experience.

Explore constructions that integrate spatial and time-based relationship to produce particular outcomes of experience (and/or) representation.

Develop, complete, test and evaluate a final design which is an interactive environment that embodies a thesis for the relationship between a concept goal and a final experienced outcome.

Develop potential insights and analysis from this inquiry and production to architectural design culture at large.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Statement of Academic Integrity

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process. Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust will merit a failing grade in this course and the violator(s) will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.  
Students engaging in plagiarism will be asked to redo their projects and failing to do so, will automatically be a F in the course.

The School of Architecture uses 3 main principles - Transparency, Traceability, Accountability - to guide academic integrity best practices for the use of Generative AI tools for its course work. These 3 principles are adopted - and adapted to the School of Architecture context - from ACM Technology Policy Office's *"PRINCIPLES FOR THE DEVELOPMENT, DEPLOYMENT, AND USE OF GENERATIVE AI TECHNOLOGIES"*, June 2023

[https://www.acm.org/binaries/content/assets/public-policy/ustpc-approved-generative-ai-principles](https://www.acm.org/binaries/content/assets/public-policy/ustpc-approved-generative-ai-principles)

Transparency: Faculty and students shall be forthcoming of generative AI use, in general and particular instances, when applicable, and cite the circumstances and methods of use.

Guidelines/Recommendation:

- Course Content: where generative AI has been used to produce part of or the whole work, disclosure must happen at some point before the end of content delivery.
- Seminar Presentation or Discussion: where generative AI has been used to produce part of or the whole work, disclosure must happen at some point before the end of content delivery/discussion.
- Desk Critics between Faculty and Student: where generative AI has been used to produce part of the work, disclosure must happen at some point before the end of discussion of the respective project.
- Printed/Fabricated/Projected Work: Faculty and Student should be forthcoming of any and all, total or partial, work produced by generative AI software.
- Review Context: where generative AI has been used to produce part of the work, disclosure must happen by the student at some point before the end of discussion of respective project.
- Other non-specified contexts: where generative AI has been used to produce part of or the whole work, disclosure must happen at some point before the end of content delivery/discussion.

Traceability: when citing materials presented in the classroom, in a review or included in the FP Book, Faculty and students shall provide references and citations, when applicable, that enable tracing back originals of both sources and processes by using the Chicago Manual of Style for citation for Generative AI Tools, for both Text and Image. Citations for generative AI will follow the Chicago Manual of Style: [https://camosun.libguides.com/Chicago-17thEd/ai](https://camosun.libguides.com/Chicago-17thEd/ai)

Accountability and responsibility: Faculty and Students will be held accountable for decisions made by algorithms they use, even if it is not feasible to explain in detail how those algorithms produced their results. Responsibility will be held for entire work, not just for the individual parts that make up the whole.
