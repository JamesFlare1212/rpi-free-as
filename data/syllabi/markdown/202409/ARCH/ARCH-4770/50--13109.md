## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Goals**

The Studio aims at investigating the following architectural problems:

\- Typology, modality, and organization: the idea that type and typology involve a reductive, essentialist approach to architecture, and that the notion of modality by-passes both characteristics without relinquishing the will to organization

\- Classical order and singularity: an approach to classicism in architecture where the rules of classical orders and the genius, beauty, and inscrutability of architectural singularity are extremes of a single form of thought and production

\- Models (generality through variability): the operativity of variables, variations, variabilities, ranges, thresholds, singularities in the understanding of models as regimes of differentiation, transcending the mechanistic modern paradigm

\- Architecture as medium of urbanity: the role, extent, and capabilities of architecture in the thinking and transformation of a project for the contemporary city, and the critique of the understanding of architecture as building design

\- Multiplicity and masterplan: the critique, through the idea of multiplicity, of the modern idea of masterplan as top-down hierarchical organizational system, and the revalidation of its capacity as an affirmative medium for a project for the city

\- Construction and representation: the approach to the architectural project as a medium for the abstract construction of building organizations (in a general sense), and the corresponding reevaluation of the idea of representation

\- Individual and collective authorship: the ethos of multiple ideas of authorship, by learning from the lessons of classicism and by developing systems of variable collaboration without eluding the self-reliance and responsibility of authorship

\- Consistency as systematic difference: the overcoming of the false dichotomy between coherence and freedom as conditions of architectural project, and the integration of both in regimes for the consistent management of difference

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**The City of the Global *Populus*** Design Studio

**Course Overview**

**00 The Joyful *Imperium* of Systematic Idiosyncrasies**

Neither blank and absolute generic systems, qualitatively poor and devoid of sensibility, nor anecdotal self-mystified relative contingencies, inherently lacking ever larger forms of order and ever more complex forms of thought: the vital ethics of a simultaneously serious and light-hearted design investigation (its acute attention, its revised categories, its rigorous procedures, its overflowing objects) is to carefully contain the virtues of systemic thinking while daringly overcoming its limitations. The Studio has the ambition of unfolding such ethics by constructing a joyful *imperium* (a new cultural ground) through the systematic differentiation of culturally wealthy architectural idiosyncrasies, which it will treat as forms of material organizational life.

**01 The life of architectural materials (material culture as surplus)**

Architecture is built upon material organizations. But material organizations are cultural constructs, rather than unaffected natural evidence. Culturally loaded, their internal componentry is cut across and troubled by manifold layers of knowledge and tradition that accrue in the form of language, constituting their meaning, influencing their usage, and expanding their spectrum of sense. This is why material organizations resonate and vibrate, as if they were alive, and why they cry, scream, weep, roar, or complain when they are transformed, as if they were delicate beings. Material organizations behave as if they had a propensity, a susceptibility, an inertia to protect. Their cultural life bestows them of preciousness and value, of surplus.

**02 Stylistic lineages: oppositions, graduations, and beyond**

High-brow material organizations, saturated of layers of knowledge, interpretation, and influence permeate in the configuration of architecture as we know it and value it. Master strokes pile up along history, guiding the becoming of culture with an impetus that is only comparable to delicate wind blows, elusive atmospheric silences, warm-air stagnancies, histrionic storms, dramatic rainfalls, catastrophic landslides, slow-motion avalanches, or infernal earthquakes. Stylistic lineages unfold, not to dogmatically indicate what is good or what is bad (what must be done), but to subtly insinuate, through their gradation, what lies embedded as potential in the present, annunciating, through their potential bursting into the future, what is inexorably coming.

**03 *Hypercanon*: integration, variation, differentiation, singularity, desecration**

“The (ancient) Romans were never particularly disposed to invention and creativity, but they were masters of co-option and assimilation.” Mike Duncan, The History of Rome (podcast), 038-The Catiline Conspiracy (https://thehistoryofrome.typepad.com)

What if, in this context, classical architectural projects are embraced, abstracted, varied, and integrated with one another, and if their integration is taken to extremes of differentiation? What happens with the canon when its norm, which defines the normal as we know it, is stretched beyond its limits, as if it was pliant? By configuring complex artifacts through differentiation, the creativity of the normative is forced to arise out of the conventionality of the normal, and the profane out of the sacred, constituting ever more sophisticated standards of intelligence. *Hypercanon* engenders new forms of canon through the saturation of existing ones. Its profanity disputes the canon from within, through the management of excess and redundancy.

**04 The Renaissance-Baroque trans-historical *continuum***

Renaissance architecture, according to Heinrich Wölfflin, aims at the perfection of the architectural body through the congruous and balanced relationship between parts, at the beauty of the plan, while Baroque architecture is driven to engender a phenomenon, a particular event, through the determined movement toward which the whole of the architectural body is directed. While the force of the object is gracefully distributed in total harmony in the former, in the latter it is concentrated in one point, sustained by a passionate effort from which it bursts, incommensurably. However precise and articulated with the historical processes in which they are inscribed, oppositions between discrete sensibilities overlook their role and participation in the transhistorical continuum that fluidly yet enigmatically communicates the beautiful and the sublime.

**05 Oppositions as gradients (between and beyond)**

On the one end, Wölfflin says, peaceful and liberating beauty, general well-being, steady expansion of the vital force, free manifestation of form, full, effortless, and methodical vivaciousness, perfect roundness and ample, and soft pleasant proportions with the calm and ascetic satisfaction intrinsic to the slowness of an enduring effect: a world of the fullness of being that must never be abandoned. On the other end, he remarks, the captivating and overwhelming power of affect, of shock, of ecstasy, and the intoxication of a strong action that suddenly abandons us in anguish and unease: a world of perpetual becoming, of instability and dissatisfaction, by which we are inexorably carried away. What is *between* those extremes? And what, *beyond* them?

**06 Typology and modality**

Architectural typologies are there to stabilize ever evolving forms of order, which they crystallize into unchanging immaterial essences, distinguishable from one another, which are given a name, classified, and made atemporal concepts. In this context, typological variability is regularly thought of as contingent, accidental, inconsequential, or ornamental. And yet, variation is itself the vehicle through which types mutate and get surpassed, precisely by the elevation of their nuances into consolidated forms. The congregation of variations in softly constrained clusters that acquire recognizability (modalities that are not simply elastic or flexible), communicates the two realms (typological variety and gradual variation) in a consistent medium where one turns into the other, constituting and destituting forms before they become either essences on one end, or nuances on the other.

**07 Variability in Models**

A model is a consistent set of modalities, that is, of variable cause-effect relationships between the internal formal attributes of an architectural organization (and between them and external ones). The variability of models enables those relationships to be systematized, so that they may become free from one another, or else mutually constrained in a variable manner, rendering the organization malleable or rigid to various degrees, and thus endowing the determination of their stability or openness to transformation (that is, the control of their consistency), before the establishment of a single and fixed absolute condition. The variability of models, therefore, does not simply pursue the crystallization of ideals (onto which they may be constructed), nor their infinite flexibility (their passive adaptability to anything): they constitute the consistent medium of a differentiable idealization process.

**08 The Matrix-Model**

A Matrix-Model is a systematic construct by which a multiplicity of variable architectural models participates in a consistent collective medium without losing their singularity and internal consistency, thus strengthening their presence, escalating their influence, and intensifying their effect onto the whole. The Matrix-Model operates as a series of parallel yet communicated fields, across which each variable model unfolds. The variables of each model vary in degrees according to their relative position in the field (which is constructed as a matrix), in relation to external conditions (within the matrix or along its edges), and in relation to one another (to other nodes in the same or other matrices). The Matrix-Model operates as a dynamic battlefield of interacting individuals, by which a high variety and variability of architectural forms systematically claims for power and fights for dominance over the others.

**09 Field of study**

Each form of architecture in a Matrix-Model unfolds across an in-principle-autonomous layer in the field of studies. Rather than stated and instituted as an allegedly objective thematic-driven disciplinary camp, the edges of which must be clearly defined to protect the coherence of the field and disallow technical spills, conceptual violations, and knowledge deviations, each field of studies is constructed as an operative platform of project-driven disciplinary exploration, investigation, systematization, differentiation, and, most importantly, creation: passionately rigorous, affective toward a particular cultural material, driven to architectural singularity, and profoundly authorial.

**10 Matrix-Model layers**

Each form of architecture in a Matrix-Model is set as a variable matrix in the nodes of which individual models are positioned. All models of a field of studies vary relative to their location in a map of multiple determinations, configuring a set of systematically different individuations embedded with rule-based responses to contingencies. Each field negotiates, in an equally systematic manner, with the physical presence of existing buildings, infrastructure, urban equipment, or with architectural models of the same or of other fields. A series of organizational frictions, collaborations, competitions, migrations, or synergies result of the local relationships.

**11 Multiplicity-Masterplan**

Grounded on a Matrix-Model, a masterplan is not reduced to a top-down series of hierarchically structured organizations that correspond to one another according to top-down dependences that coordinate the systems at play in a deterministic fashion. Nor is it an indeterminate or immaterial form of urbanism that struggles to resemble or evade the manifold contingencies of everyday life, the complexity of power relations, or the unpredictability of coordination through bottom-up, scenario-driven, or picturesque situational formats. A Multiplicity-Masterplan, instead, operates as a manifold of multiplicities of idiosyncratic architectural models, the complex relationships between which are as contingent as they are systematic.

**12 White Noise**

White Noise is the generic organization that results from the excessive accumulation of multiple idiosyncrasies, generating a diversified, but globally homogeneous noise, with the appearance of perpetuity. As the fourth generation of globalization looms on the horizon (the post-politically correct era), we find ourselves facing a completely new form of generality, dispersed and headless, obnoxiously metallic and rarefied, that appears as a synthetic collective project, the ideology of which is still unknown, but whose objective appears to overcome the false dichotomies between systems and singularities. This new ubiquitous form of generality is paradoxically composed of multiple, bellicose, and intransigent stereotypes, often insignificant, extremely delicate idiosyncrasies, made of the microscopic hyper-individualistic politics of millions obsessed with instituting their profile in a world that has been devoid of any form of subjectivity (and that, in search of equality and freedom, corrupts them both). The resulting proliferation of nuances gives rise to a polyphonic chaotic noise, the tone of which is as impatient as serene (passive-aggressive and provocative by default). White noise is its singular format, the self-fulfilled prophecy of the multiplicity-masterplan, its voice exasperated and exasperating, but peaceful, its effect as aggressive as innocent.

**13 *Roma Intempestus Urbis***

The eternity of Rome: its predetermined destiny of atemporality will now reach a new level, where the ultimate (matter of fact, cold-blooded, straightforward) form of the grandiose encounters a renovated version of the picturesque, paradoxically unfolding under the unemotional ethics and humour of radical systematicity. *Roma Intempestus Urbis* will turn the tempestuous machine of over-tourism inside out, and that crazy dynamic that has slowly but consistently pushed away its intrinsically cosmopolitan people out of its primordial territory, will precipitate into a peaceful, relentless, timeless state of things where the primordial and the cosmopolitan find a new synthesis, one of a kind. The sacred city will be once again profaned and consecrated, taking a new form, such being the precondition of its unique trans-historical destiny. Rome, the perfect paradigm of extemporaneity, is the ultimate medium for the extemporaneity of the contemporary world: history folds onto itself, once again, in the same place, and generates a new form of being.

**14 Over-tourism**

“If caught sitting on the Spanish Steps, tourists can face a €250 (£214) fine” (Source: https://www.bbc.com/travel/article/20240205-italy-battles-over-tourism-with-loudspeaker-bans-airbnb-crackdown)

Like other European cities in the globalized world, Rome has become a global touristic node. But given its comparatively small size, its sheer touristic wealth, its millennial cultural diversity, its mythological food scene, its wide-ranging landscape panoramas and sceneries, and the various material traditions around it, the over-burdened tourism generated by the wide assortment of peoples (disciplines, practices, cultures, races, ages, and geographies) coming back to the ancient and protomodern centre of the western world has swiftly become particularly extreme. While not simply reducible to a theme park city, overtourism is expelling the population out of the historical centre and threatens with a larger migration off its surroundings.

**15 The over-tourism abstract machine**

Is it enough (and, above all, daringly enough) to put at work a series of tactics of survival and mitigation policies that forcefully but vainly confront over-tourism through limitations and confinements, like other European urban policies are currently attempting? Or are we, rather, in front of the unprecedented opportunity radically of intensifying and accelerating tourism at its very core by promoting (by architecturally grounding and channelling) over-tourism, positioning strategically the very concept of Rome as the new model of the global city, its truest yearned-for destiny? Consequently, what is the abstract machine that, in such context, can ground, through architecture, such challenging process, using its force and its vibe as a disciplinary opportunity?

**16 The Uber-soul of the lost souls**

In the over-touristic city, people behave as zombies. They frantically wander, following invisible forces that carry them all away to the same places to do the same things, in the same ways, at the same time. All routines are prescribed, and when gaps arise, they get lost, start turning, stay stagnant, get stuck, or simply shut down, until new instructions appear. In the over-touristic city, people move in small homogeneous masses (between 02 and 40) masses that go through one another ignorant of anything that is not their informed aim (tours, never detours), which they abandon as soon as they have been consumed according to the written protocols. The over-touristic city is populated by blank, consumed souls. Indifference is what they share, their common, the key to their ritual, their eternal return. Their uber-soul, which traverses their bodies (and does not fly above them), is the newest (overwhelming) sign of god’s existence: *Tornos*.

**17 The collective anti-urbanism of pure interiority and no exteriority**

The patterns are clear: navigate (as if shopping), move straight to the aim (magnets), slow down in correspondence to getting tired or bored, turn around several times, look around, move around, gather and join groups of equals, remain stagnant, shut down. We all participate in a single collective multidirectional congregation where there is no otherness and no exteriority. *Intempestus Urbis* is pure interior without time, like a mall, like a video arcade, like a multiplex cinema, or like an urban church without explicit religion: distracted concentration is its precondition of its effectiveness, its appearance of inevitability, its warranty of success. All-encompassing diversified modalities of the classical architectural types will configure together this collective interior.

**18 Roman Taxonomy of (Auratic) Public Interiors**

Unhinged and unsteady central piazzas, sleepy-dreamy courtyards and lethargic-oneiric cloisters, warm caring atriums, embracing, squeezing, cuddling facades, shock-and-awe steering landmarks: a series of case studies from the architecture of the High Renaissance and Early Baroque in Rome (piazzas, courtyards, atria, naves, facades, and landmarks from Donato Bramante, Antonio da Sangallo, Michelangelo Buonarroti, Carlo Maderno, Francesco Borromini, Gian Lorenzo Bernini, Giacomo della Porta, Pietro da Cortona) will be the material from which to construct variable models. Each model will be grounded on a given architectural typology (understood in a generic sense) and integrate the organization of three case study projects in central Rome, which will introduce modalities in the overall compound, opening the rigidity and/or generality of the architectural type into a consistently variable and organizationally profuse model.

**19 The (Atemporal) City of the (Captive) Global *Populus***

*Tornos*, god of the eternal return, turns people around Rome and holds them captive inside a single timeless enclosure, from which they cannot (and do not want) to escape. Rome is the (atemporal) city of the (captive) global *populus*. The sheer critical mass of its cultural paraphernalia creates the verifiable feeling that there is nowhere else to go, that everything outside is trickery, that the world was turned inside out and the actual illusion lies beyond the Aurelian Walls, and that the city is the clear evidence that reveals the truly real, the only real, the non-beyond, the unique here-and-now, looping over and over in eternal return. Rome is both driven and compelled to assume this inevitable destiny for which it seems to have been created, and for which it has persistently endured, ever self-overwhelmed, over millennia. Its challenge is now to incarnate its fortune and turn it into the new form of global city: the singular city.

**20 *Millarium Aereum* / *Umbilicus Urbis* (“All roads lead to Rome”)**

The singular city is the new state of the generic city. The singular city is the global city that achieves ubiquity through its uniqueness, because everybody wants to be there (forever-land), not because it is everywhere (neverland). Unlike its generic predecessor of the break of the new millennium, which looks and works like an airport (blank, clean, white, insipid, qualityless, neutral, homogeneous), the singular city looks and works like a desecrated religious multiplicity, a complex of necessarily incomplete auratic building organizations, of newborn residues (both remains and reminders), of supreme cultural artifacts rendered everyday objects. It is a city of ruins-in-advance that takes over and is taken over by nature: the umbilical down-to-earth centre of a multi-centric world.

**21 The anti-urban collective as entertainment infrastructure**

The singular city is anti-urbanism collective made of rich, saturated, differentiated public interiors, formally sophisticated but programmatically banal, sacred and yet profane building-space couples. Its collective anti-urbanism works as an indiscriminate but systematic accumulation of architectural pieces formally crisscrossed and mutually tensed with one another, and promiscuously invading each other’s space: idiosyncratic atrium-hotel interventions, the singularity of which is constituted by pools of amenities and entertainment-related programs, urban equipment and public spaces, distributed across its surfaces, positioned within its kinks, its intersections, and its extremes, and contained or sheltered by their auratic envelopes.

**22 The architectural calligraphy of an eternally undecipherable hieroglyph**

The singularity of the City of the Global *Populus* results of the infinite tangential relationships and minute rubbings that constitute the irreducible uniqueness of its organization. The architecture of the singular city is a white noise manifold of variable architectural models (master strokes) that together configure a singular multiplicity. Its figure-figure without-a-canvas compound reads without projecting a coherent meaning, but many precise undecipherable messages. The City of the Global *Populus* is the beautiful-sublime architectural calligraphy of a trans-historical hieroglyph, a writing system that refuses to release the final clues to its enigma.

**Site**

The site, which coincides the material of study and transformation, is the historic city of Rome, the urban territory inside the Aurelian Walls, specifically the Campo Marzio area, both site and material of a multiplicity-masterplan. Following the precedence of the two major survey-like radically inventive mid XVIII century projects by Giambattista Nolli and Giovanni Battista Piranesi, City of the Global *Populus* aims at sensitively substituting the medieval fabric through the proliferation of an intrinsically architectural (anti-urban) strategy of hotel infrastructure and amenities that articulate the public realm and its services with the private domain and its benefits. The infrastructure is constructed by a series of models that result of the abstraction, idealization, integration, and variation of Renaissance and Baroque buildings, understood as modalities of differentiation of existing urban typologies capable to absorb public space within architecture.

**Program**

The multiplicity-masterplan is constituted by variable models of building organizations that ground the configuration of novel hotel typologies, constituted by various types of serviced rooms, vertical and horizontal circulation systems, common services and common areas, public and private amenities and commercial areas, and privately-owned public spaces. Framed within the phenomenon of over-tourism and its impact on European cities, the Studio aims to constitute a new, generic yet singular notion of the global city, irreducible to neutral and repetitive standards. New hotel forms are grounded on the organization of classical building types: piazzas, courtyards, atriums, naves, facades, and landmarks, the expansive, condensed, convoluted, peculiar, quasi-religious idiosyncrasy of which is regarded as having the potential to swallow the urban within the architectural object, turning it into the ultimate device of urbanity.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 01 Research. 01a_Ability to use/develop specialized knowledge. Students live in Rome throughout the semester. Through the direct interaction with canonical architectural pieces, the involvement with urban conditions and phenomena, and the fluid access to museums, artwork, and bibliographical resources in the city, students augment their disciplinary baggage and construct an ability to attentively observe and document multiple forms and scales of knowledge about buildings, cultural artifacts and sites that ground the architectural procedure of the project, nurture its design techniques, and substantiate the decision-making process. 01b_Ability to use / develop broad and integrative knowledge. Students live in Rome throughout the semester, engaging in a culturally immersive process that, aside primarily consisting of regular visits to Renaissance and Baroque buildings in the city center and of participating in real time in the cultural life of the city in the context of its omnipresent history, is fostered by a series of field trips to the outskirts of the city, to Tuscany, and to Sicily, providing a rich and multilayered context of historical and cultural significance to the architectural projects, which broadens the understanding and thinking both of their causes and of their consequences.
- 02 Intellectual design skills. 02a_Ability to design. Students are conceptually and technically nurtured to develop an ability to ground design decisions on a hands-on operative approach to architecture as material. This approach is articulated by studying and literally using the organizational knowledge embedded in existing buildings as sources of design intelligence. 02b_Ability to design using information resources. Students construct architectural models from the integration of visual resources, formal descriptions, and organizational data, programming operative models that are built upon existing case-study buildings in Rome, thus developing a process of architectural abstraction that turns information resources into form, and vice versa. 02c_Ability to design engaging diverse perspectives. Students are trained to produce projects as a synthesis of various viewpoints that are progressively integrated in a single artifact by means of an iterative design process that goes from individual work to teamwork, to all-Studio work. These viewpoints incarnate multiple perspectives: cultural, commercial, civic, and urban. 02d_Ability to design with ethical reasoning. Students are confronted with the contemporary urban problem of overtourism (at work, particularly, in major European cities like Rome), and are challenged to think ingenious solutions for the overloading of historic centers with touristic infrastructure and the overwhelming ephemeral population that brings radical changes into the social fabric. 02e_Ability to design with quantitative fluency. Students are taught to construct, program, control, and manage complex abstract architectural models: systems of architectural organization that determine form and space through the control of quantifiable variations of variables and relationships between them, learning to go from quantifiable data into form, and vice versa. 02f_Ability to design with communicative fluency. Students are challenged to fluidly communicate with (and be influenced by) different interlocutors at different stages of the course, from local professors and consultants of various backgrounds to student colleagues of other universities in the Rome Center, to guest professors that participate in reviews and the lecture series program.
- 03 Collaboration. 03a_Ability to collaborate with team. Students develop the ability to work in three-student teams, learning and using collaborative methods based on design integration through digital modelling, and supported by interpersonal skills. After an initial individual phase (framed by architectural typologies associated to student teams), an intermediate phase involves the development of teamwork strategies grounded on the sharing of acquired knowledge, the synergetic intertwinement of design techniques, the project ideation through conceptual and organizational integration, and a management and workflow ethics that fluctuates between the differentiation and the integration of tasks. A final stage integrates the lineages of teamwork development into a single all-Studio proposition for central Rome. The change of roles along the process provides of evidence of different strengths of the individual students, who document the process in a portfolio that allows its clear understanding. 03b_Ability to collaborate with a community partner. Students develop the ability to feed the work with the influence of potential community partners and users that are surveyed from the local community and from disparate global sources and cultures by registering their behavioral patterns. These patterns are embedded in the project through the recognition of habits and rituals, and the abstraction of forms, typologies, and material traditions. The community, understood in a non-paternalistic materialistic sense, contributes, with these patterns, to the strategic thinking, the programming, and the organizational enrichment of the projects.
- 04 Civic and global engagement. 04a_Ability to engage the civic realm. Students demonstrate the ability to engage with an urgent issue of public interest: the overpopulation of the historic center of the city of Rome due to the rapidly growing influx of global tourists, from which they are being expelled due to the increase in the living cost and the changes in the real estate market, while becoming economically dependent from it, and the transformation of the historic center into a new form of generic city: the singular city. Students develop models to accommodate local conditions within global ones, rather than disappointingly resisting them. 04b_Ability to engage the global realm. Students demonstrate the ability to engage with the positive aspects present in the rapid growth and transformation that global overtourism is bringing into the historic center of the city of Rome, rethinking and expanding the capabilities of typologies (piazzas, courtyards, atriums, naves, facades, and landmarks) to absorb public space within architecture and integrate communities that appear to be, in principle, in conflict. Students develop models to accommodate local conditions within global ones and use their synergy to think the current state of the global city.
- 05 Design execution. 05a_Midterm Review. **Peer Grading**

Each student is required to submit a weekly peer grade to the Studio professor. The grade is scored on a scale between 00 and 10, accompanied by an optional one-sentence written description summarizing the performance of his/her two teammates that week. Peer grades are due by 08:00pm on Thursday every week via email to the Studio professor. Faculty does not send reminders for submitting peer grades.

**Self-assessment**

As part of the assessment of the learning outcomes, students are required to complete a Student Self-Assessment Survey prior to receiving a grade for the semester. At the end of the semester, students are provided with a link to the survey.

**Pin-ups and Reviews**

Students are periodically required to present their work in a formal review process. Presentations are a critical part of the architectural education. They are opportunities to judge the project and critically evaluate the design process. It may be appropriate at points in the semester to evaluate only selected projects to convey information relevant to the entire class. In this situation, it is important for each student to witness and participate in the review process. Even if his/her project is not reviewed, the criticism and discussions inform the evaluation of his/her progress and design. Failure to attend one or more pin-ups for the full class period may result in a grade deduction.  
**Desk Critiques**

The Studio environment involves two-way communication. The role of the Studio professor is to evolve and strengthen the student’s design process. Every time the student meets with the faculty, he/she must have new iterations of work based on previous conversations. If the student has not produced new work, the faculty reserves the right to skip the critique and continue with other students who have produced new material.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process.

**Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism**

Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust merits a failing grade in this course and the violator will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.

Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of the course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student. Any misrepresentation of work is evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student results in a failing grade for the course.
