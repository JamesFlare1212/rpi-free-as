## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

CATALOG COURSE DESCRIPTION  
The focus of this studio is the design and programming of a civic building in an urban setting. In this course, “students develop  
the ability to make design decisions within an architectural project while demonstrating synthesis of user requirements,  
regulatory requirements, site conditions, and accessible design, and consideration of the measurable environmental impacts of  
their design decisions.” NAAB (SC.5 Design Synthesis).  
This course is required of all fourth-year architecture undergraduates in the B.Arch. Program.  
Any student receiving a second ‘D’ in any design studio must repeat the second design studio and receive a grade of ‘C’ or  
higher to proceed to the next design studio.

• A (EXCELLENT) The design work and technical development is exceptional with consistency throughout the  
semester. The student’s work has been undertaken with a high level of insight, consistency and discipline. The project  
must demonstrate a successful level of development from broad concepts through details, synthetic and technical  
resolution. The project evidences a strong ability to integrate architectural building systems while exceeding functional  
requirements. The resulting body of work, when taken as a whole, highlights the students excellence in architectural  
development and design synthesis well exceeding ‘ability’ as described in SC.5.  
• B (GOOD) Project is a creative and energetic response, delivered with consistency, to comprehensive architectural  
design and the criteria cited in SC.5. The work is inspiring, complete and well resolved. Overall performance is well  
above the norm and, in many aspects the project, rises to a level of excellence. Assignments are met in a timely  
manner, and generally considered comprehensive and complete, beyond the minimum requirements of the course.  
The resultant project, attendance, and in class involvement, is demonstrably strong.  
• C (AVERAGE) Work that meets minimum requirements and demonstrates a somewhat more than minimum level  
comprehension with both the design and technical requirements and ‘ability’ as described in SC.5. The project may  
not demonstrate initiative to develop without additional input from the instructor and may be summarized by an  
underwhelming level of improvement throughout the semester. Although the project as a whole may not inspire, the  
satisfactory completion of all required assignments and demonstration of above minimum level of resolution must be  
evidenced in the project as a whole.  
• D (INFERIOR) Project is complete, however to a generally low standard. Work must address the fundamental  
requirements, and demonstrate evidence of all Student Criteria described in SC.5 with the minimum requirement for  
the project to clearly demonstrate ‘ability’ for topics described in SC.5.

deliverables deadline at the end of the semester. The project provides insufficient evidence of the students ‘ability’  
to address the integrated design and technical concerns referred to in SC.5. Typically, the failing project is marked by

substantially incomplete. The evaluation of a projects completeness is considered in stages throughout the semester  
and culminates in the final review and end of semester deliverables. Informal appeals for ‘incomplete’ status will not  
be entertained as a means of avoiding a failing grade, students should review institutional guidelines for any process  
related to ‘incomplete’ status.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

ATTENDANCE Attendance is required at all class meetings during the semester. Students are expected to arrive prepared and to use the class period for development of their projects. Two (2) unexcused absences from class will result in the lowering of the grade by one (1) letter. Four (4) unexcused absences from class during the term result in an automatic F. Attendance is mandatory for milestone reviews, including the midterm review, penultimate review, and final review as well as other all-class pinups. Failure to present at milestone reviews will result in an automatic F. Legitimate excuses for your missing class, late arrival to class, and late hand-in of an assignment. The legitimate excuses are: illness (which must be accompanied by an authorized excuse), serious accident or injury, family emergency, religious obligation, participation in an off-campus intercollegiate athletic competition (this event must be preceded by a written excuse from your coach).

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and  
procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must  
trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must  
trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational  
process. Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of  
another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust will merit a failing grade in this  
course and the violator(s) will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.
