## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course surveys the architectural, urban, social and cultural changes that have been taken place since taken place since Rome became the Capital of Italy (1870) up to today. Population increased, due to major immigration, and a new political class came over and the city shape followed these events. New architectural typologies, neighborhoods and celebrative monuments were developed while old neighborhoods were demolished to widen streets, creating modern squares as well as to reveal the ancient city hidden under the medieval one that was mostly destroyed.

Rome started to expand behind the ancient Aurelian Walls and new areas once considered countryside have been fully integrated in the city becoming important urban landmarks (periferia storica) while others are still considered outskirts, and their residents live at the border of society. Likewise, after the Unification new upper classes’ areas were conceived as the historical hubs where old Roman aristocracy used to gather were not functional anymore in the new social and urban scenario.

The course will be articulated into both an historical and thematic perspective and will aim to broaden the study and comparison from the Roman context to American suburbs the students are acquainted with. Focus will be given on topics such as: public housing, genius loci and outskirts, spontaneous architecture, social issues, Roman life and traditions, gentrification, Street art and suburbs, reclamation of marginalized areas etc.

This course aims to give a real understanding of what is Rome today, its culture and people.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The intent of the course is to provide the students with a fundamental knowledge of the following topics:

· The history and development of Rome in the last century.

· The role and mission of contemporary architecture taking Roman peripheral areas as study case.

· The evaluation of the positive and negative effects of the gentrification and Street art in the reclamation of neglected areas.

· The different theories about social housing in use in modern and contemporary Rome.

· The new residential needs and typologies required by the modern and contemporary upper classes.

· The use literary and visual sources to study the issues of modern and contemporary Roman developments

· The social and anthropological aspect as well as Roman traditions

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The examinations will consist of:

1. Presentations: each student will do a 5 mins presentation on a neighborhood agreed with the instructor. Each week the instructor will ask one or two students to recap the class of the previous week.
2. The midterm exam will be a multi-choice answers test
3. The final exam will be the historical and morphological analysis of a Roman neighborhood based on historical research and sources.
4. Students will be also required to have a journal where they must post pictures, impressions and analytic information about the given day lecture and site visit. The journal must be kept updated from the beginning of the course.
5. Attendance (see related section in the syllabus)

### Attendance Policy:

Education at the UARK Rome Center requires active involvement in the learning process. Therefore, students have the responsibility to attend classes and to actively engage in all learning assignments or opportunities provided in their classes. There may be times, however, when illness, family crisis, or university-related activities make full attendance or participation impossible. In these situations, students are responsible for making timely arrangements with the instructor to make up for the work missed. Examples of **justified absences** include those resulting from the following: illness of the student, serious illness or death of a member of the student’s immediate family, or other family crisis. **The instructor has the right to request appropriate documentation from students for any absence for which they wish to be excused.**

As far as **unjustified absences** are concerned, students may miss up to a **maximum of 3 contact hours**. After the third contact hour is missed, students will be formally notified by the instructor. From the fourth contact hour missed, the final grade may be affected at the instructor’s discretion. Courses meeting once a week have 3 contact hours per class, therefore students may miss **only one class** without major consequences.
