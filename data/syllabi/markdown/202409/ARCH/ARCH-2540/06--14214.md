## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Descriptive drawing – Ability to use to use two-dimensional projected drawings, two-dimensional diagrams, and three-dimensional models to accurately describe different aspects of the physical world including the geometry of objects and flows of material and energy.
- Speculative drawing – Ability to use to use two-dimensional projected drawings, two-dimensional diagrams, and three-dimensional models to imagine and visualize different configurations of the physical world including the geometry of objects and flows of material and energy.
- Two-dimensional projection drawing – Ability to develop and relate orthographic elevations and sections related through a projection system. Ability to at scale draw in a precise, measurable manner.
- Three-dimensional modeling – Ability to build a three-dimensional digital model at scale in a precise, measurable manner.
- Graphic layout – Ability to compose a presentation board that combines multiple types of drawings, images, diagrammatic information, and text in a beautiful and clear manner.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students who successfully complete this course will be able to:

- \- Demonstrate the ability to use drawing as a communicative tool within architecture.
- \- Develop projective drawings in two and three dimensions.
- \- Differentiate between digital and real environments, leverage the advantages of both in order to communicate effectively, and work between both environments in an efficient manner.
- \- Become proficient and disciplined working in a digital modeling environment.
- \- Develop a sound methodological basis for the creation of drawings, digital models, and renderings of digital models.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grades will be based upon the quality of drawings and models produced as well as attendance and participation in class.

- \- Mid Review: 30%
- \- Final Project: 40%
- \- Attendance and Participation: 30% (This course will be taught entirely online; attendance will be recorded by attending scheduled WebEx meetings AND by handing in weekly assignments on time).

Mid-semester evaluations for the course will be provided to each student.

Grades will be established according to the following policy:

- \- A (EXCELLENT) Exceptional performance strongly exceeding the requirements of the course, showing strong academic initiative and independent resourcefulness.
- \- B (GOOD) Performance above the norm; accurate and complete; beyond the minimum requirements of the course; work demonstrates marked progress and initiative.
- \- C (AVERAGE) Satisfactory work that adequately meets minimum requirements and demonstrates satisfactory comprehension, communication skills, and effort; demonstrates little initiative to investigate the problem without substantial input from the instructor; work shows little improvement.
- \- D (INFERIOR) Unsatisfactorily meets minimum requirements; demonstrates minimum comprehension, communication skills, and effort at an inferior level; initiative lacking; improvement not noticeable.
  - \- F (FAIL) Does not meet minimum requirements; fails to adequately demonstrate comprehension, communication skills, and effort.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:**

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermine the educational process. Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violates academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust will merit a failing grade in this course and the violator(s) will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.

**Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism:**

Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of this course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student. Any miss-representation of work shall be evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student will result in a failing grade for the course.
