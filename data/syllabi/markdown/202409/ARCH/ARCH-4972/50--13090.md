## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course surveys the institutions and social networks in which European fine arts were created, consumed, critiqued, and eventually illegally traded.

Starting from the Antique Times and ranging to the post-II World War, the course will examine the variety of communities where public and private often intersected and which sponsored innovations in the arts. Artists, performers, patrons/matrons, politicians, journalists, and others collaborated and competed to give birth to outstanding works of art. This approach to the history of the arts in western culture puts the focus less on the individual creative genius of great composers, writers, painters, and sculptors, and more on the social exchanges and institutions that sponsored and received their work.

The course also examines the unique topic of art crime and its relationship with the cultural sector and the international art market, starting from real hot or cold cases.

Class meetings will be a mixture of lectures, group discussions, individual and group exercises, films, and guest speakers. The course includes a visit to “Comando Carabinieri Tutela Patrimonio Culturale”, an actual operating military unit to prevent and detect illegal art trade, and to an Auction House.

The course is divided into two parts. The first is a once-a-week class for the first nine weeks in a row. The second is a hands-on activity which includes a workshop/essay/study-case storyteller, talks, a visit to Comando Carabinieri Tutela Patrimonio Culturale, and a visit to an Auction House.

This course’s topic meets many gen-eds courses, as for the following codes: HRHS, FINN, HIST, HUMN, MRST, ECON, BLAW

This forum swirls around the concept of art/collecting/market/crime/legal and illegal trade/art history/museum studies.

Disciplinary perspectives include:

- Western conceptions of “collecting art.”

- Museum studies

- Ethics and market

- Pedagogical role of museums

- US vs. EU art market

- Art crime

- Art as tool of power

- Trafficking

- European Parliament

- Law

- Illicit

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Discussion Requirements:

Each discussion seminar will be graded @500 points.

Forum participants may miss one class without penalty; other absences will result in zero points for the day missed. In advance of each seminar, students will receive discussion guidelines posted on Blackboard.

Each class prepares discussion prompts.

Participants who exhibit consistent, analytical, and thoughtful comments on the readings will earn an “A” in discussion.

Objective:

Each reflection paper serves as a personal academic diary, capturing your thoughts, reactions, and learnings from the individual and in-class insights and research. The paper is your platform to express how you are engaged with the topic and if it is impacting your understanding and appreciation of the course.

Guidelines:

- Length: 700 (min) - 1500 (max) words per blog

- Format: Introduction, detailed body, and conclusion

- Submission: To be submitted after the completion of each activity uploading the PDF file into Bb

Content Directions:

Your reflection paper should address the following:

1. Description: Provide a brief overview of the activity. What was the nature of the event/class? Who conducted it, and what was the focus?
2. Experience: Detail your participation or observation. What did you do or witness during the onsite classes? Describe your roles, interactions, and any significant moments.
3. Learning: Reflect on what you learned. How has the activity contributed to your academic knowledge or cultural understanding? Relate this to your previous conceptions and course content where relevant.
4. Impact: Discuss the impact on you personally and academically. How has it influenced your perspective on the subject matter or culture?
5. Integration: Explain how you can integrate the insights gained from the activity into your ongoing studies or future aspirations. What lasting impressions or skills have you acquired?

Evaluation Criteria:

Content Quality and Relevance: Assess how effectively you describe the activity and its significance. This includes the ability to link the experience to both your personal development and academic learning, illustrating the impact on your worldview and scholarly pursuits.

Sources and relevant bibliography are a compulsory part of the content quality.

Originality, Creativity, and Organization: Evaluate the uniqueness of your perspective and the creative approach you bring to your reflection. Additionally, the organization of your paper will be reviewed for logical flow and the structured presentation of your ideas, from the introduction through to the conclusion.

Writing Quality and Adherence to Guidelines: Judge the clarity of your language, the correctness of grammar and spelling, and the overall readability of your paper. This criterion also considers your compliance with the specified guidelines, including the word count and the submission format.

Blog Post/Reflection Paper Evaluation Rubric:

1. Content Quality and Relevance

- High (100%): Deep connection with the topic of the course; profound insights and thoughtful analysis; demonstrates personal growth and clear understanding.

- Medium (75%): Moderate; some insights and personal reflections; general understanding with some signs of growth.

- Low (50%): Limited connection or understanding; superficial or minimal personal insights.

2. Originality, Creativity, and Organization

- High (100%): Highly original and creative; well-organized with a clear structure (introduction, body, conclusion).

- Medium (75%): Some originality and creative thinking; reasonably organized but may have minor structural flaws.

- Low (50%): Lacks originality and creativity; poorly organized or lacks clear structure.

3. Writing Quality and Adherence to Guidelines

- High (100%): Clear, concise, error-free writing; meets length requirements (700-1500 words).

- Medium (75%): Few grammatical or spelling errors; mostly adheres to length requirements.

- Low (50%): Multiple errors; does not meet length requirements.
