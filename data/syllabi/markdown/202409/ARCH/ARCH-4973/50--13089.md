## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

WOMEN IN THE ARTS AND ARCHITECTURE  
INTRODUCTION

As I sat watching Everyman at the Charterhouse, I said to myself, why not Everywoman?

George Bernard Show

Still in the present times women face obstacles and barriers to achieve their social and professional goals. This setting is an outcome of the disparity of the civil rights, determined by an aside position of women in the history. The feminist movement, especially in the ‘70s, has prompted the essential enhancement to the study of the history of women, and their role in the secularized society.

The feminist movement opened a path to study and investigate women in the arts giving birth to feminist art history and lately to gendered art history. As such, recent investigations are pointing to different ways women interacted with visual arts, trying to disrupt commonplace ideas surroundings medieval or modern women.

The aim of this course is that of exploring the history of architecture and the history of art through-and-by the history of women, analyzing crucial concept to better understand marginalization, emancipation and challenges women had to front and overcame to get equal opportunities and chances.

During the course we will study leading protagonists of women in the arts, and mythological, biblical or historical characters analyzed as study-case whose legacy in many regards is still affecting our language, habits, and cultural constraints.

The course will be held in class and onsite, taking advantage of the works of art present inside the major museums, churches, and historic buildings.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The intent of the course is to provide the students with a fundamental knowledge of the following topics:

- examine and understand gender diversity from a historical and contemporary perspective.

- examine crucial women protagonists in the history and their connections with present times.

- examine and analyze works of art done by and/or for women artists from a social, cultural, and historic perspective.

- identify key works of art produced in Europe and the United States by women artists throughout history.

- employ art historical methodology to collect evidence of human artistic production across time and across cultures.

- synthesize the relationship between specific works art and their social, cultural, and historic context.

- recognize and compose readable (and disciplinary specific) prose, as characterized by clear and careful organization, coherent paragraphs, and well-constructed sentences according to the style used in the history of art.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- familiarize yourself with gender studies.
- recognize and interpret works done by and/or for women.
- understand the importance of art works as a means of power, communication, and empowerment.
- built a critical gendered perspective on social, cultural, and economic issues.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

EVALUATION CRITERIA

All students are expected to be engaged in project related course work for the full duration of class. The following schedule establishes the structure of work, products required, and important events above and beyond the daily course content delivery.

- Class Participation. Regular attendance and active participation in class activities are essential to be a successful learner. Therefore, you must prepare carefully for class by completing all the assignments in advance. The class participation and homework grade can be further affected by the student’s active participation.

Being absent does not excuse you from completing the assigned work or the alternative assignments that your instructor will assign.

- Final exam. This exam will ask you to recognize 10 works of art (from those seen in classes and onsite visits) while placing them in a historical, social, cultural, and stylistic context. You will also be expected to use terminology and methodologies that are specific to the field of art history and to the topic that you are discussing. Each description should be of 500 words at least.

Each work of art will be evaluated as following:

⎯ 25 points: Identification

⎯ 25 points: description and exact contextualization in the proper time frame

⎯ 25 points: critical analysis of the subject matter and its meaning

⎯ 25 points: quotes from bibliography or citing from texts.

TOTAL: 100 POINTS

- Reflection paper. You will be asked to do a reflection paper every two weeks for a total of 4 papers where you are asked to analyze two works of art/topic/protagonist considered during the earlier weeks. Each reflection should be about 500 words and should follow a specific methodological approach (such as feminism, psychoanalysis, marxism or formalism).

You are asked to use a dedicated menu on BB’s page of the course.

- Oral Presentation. This course will teach the process of writing and rewriting – drafting editing and revision – to help students mature as disciplinary thinkers/ writers and to continually improve the relationship between content and clarity.

The presentation will focus on the work of a specific woman/topic/ artist/historic leader or protagonist, assigned by the instructor at the beginning of the course.

The presentation as well as the reflection paper will need to identify a specific art historical methodology as well as standard discipline-based terminology.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grading  
Reflection Paper - 25 points  
Midterm - 25 points  
Final Exam - 25 points  
Oral presentation - 25 points

The evaluation will be based on the following point scale. A: 90-100, B: 80-89, C: 70-79, D: 60- 69, R: 59 or below Promotion: A cumulative semester grade average of 60 or higher is required to pass the course for promotion. Descriptions of qualities and behavior represented in work evaluated at each level are:

A: Excellent work demonstrates that the student has excelled in satisfying all objectives and work methods. Characterized by exceptionally well-crafted work product that demonstrates a heightened level of understanding, intellect, and care in execution. Work is completed on time through a rigorous, continuously evolving, iterative/recursive process. Presentation strategies exhibit superior quality verbal, graphic and intellectual enthusiasm that carry the work beyond directives, expectations, and goals articulated in assignments. Clear willingness to engage in critical dialogue about design (architecture, interior design, and landscape architecture) with both faculty and peers must be evident. Habitual study is evidenced by production of new work each time the studio meets.

B: Above Average work demonstrates the student has satisfied all objectives and work methods outlined for the course, often exceeding those expectations. Strong, well-crafted work that demonstrates care and consideration of program, place, and use. Work product demonstrates initiative and represents effort in exceeding baseline expectation. The work is well developed intellectually, completed on time, and demonstrates continuous engagement with the design process. Effort demonstrates willingness to engage in the critical dialogue about design (architecture, interior design, and landscape architecture) with both faculty and peers, and exhibits strong evidence of iterative study. New work is developed for every studio meeting.

C: Average work demonstrates that the student has adequately satisfied all explicitly articulated course objectives and work methods. The work is characterized by competent craftsmanship, on time delivery and satisfactory effort. Presentation strategies demonstrate reasonable effort to communicate intellectual underpinnings and design process. Communication demonstrates an ability to listen and respond to the critical dialogue about design (architecture, interior design, and landscape architecture) with faculty and peers. Iterative development of work evident.

D: Below Average work demonstrates that the student has not satisfied all course objectives and work methods and that there is clear need for improvement. Work is incomplete and/or executed with inconsistent or flawed craftsmanship demonstrating a lack of effort, lethargy, or inability to engage the design process. Characterized by an unwillingness to listen and respond to critical dialogue about design (architecture, interior design, and landscape architecture) with either faculty or peers. Little to no evidence of iterative study.

F: Failing work demonstrates that the student has not satisfied many course objectives and work methods. Work is incomplete and executed with crude and rudimentary craftsmanship, lacking merit. Characterized by a demonstrated lack of engagement with the course content and/or objectives, an unwillingness to listen and respond to the critical dialogue of faculty, and an undeveloped command of the basic skills. Evidence of iterative study absent.

### Attendance Policy:

ATTENDANCE

Education at the UARK Rome Center requires active involvement in the learning process. Therefore, students have the responsibility to attend classes and to actively engage in all learning assignments or opportunities provided in their classes. There may be times, however, when illness, family crisis, or university-related activities make full attendance or participation impossible. In these situations, students are responsible for making timely arrangements with the instructor to make up for the work missed. Examples of justified absences include those resulting from the following: illness of the student, serious illness or death of a member of the student’s immediate family, or other family crisis. The instructor has the right to request appropriate documentation from students for any absence for which they wish to be excused.

As far as unjustified absences are concerned, students may miss up to a maximum of 3 contact hours. After the third contact hour missed, students will be formally notified by the instructor. From the fourth contact hour missed, the final grade may be affected at the instructor’s discretion. Courses meeting once a week have 3 contact hours per class, therefore students may miss only one class without major consequences.
