## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

As aesthetic and cultural enterprises art and architecture are closely related. But while art generally maintains purposeful uselessness to emphasize its aesthetic capacities, architecture is always at some level utilitarian. Relationships between the aesthetics of art and the utilitarianism of architecture are highlighted when designing museums, galleries, and other spaces for art. In his essay *Design and Crime*, the art critic Hal Foster implies this question in a general sense – how much is one willing to let the aesthetic and utilitarian concerns of architecture become conflated?

It could be argued that the most visible contemporary museum projects, Ateliers Jean Nouvel’s National Museum of Qatar for example, are quite extreme conflations of aesthetics and utility, and that in many high-profile contemporary museums architecture becomes at least as much of an aesthetic statement as the art it contains. In Architectural Design 3 students will design a new gallery building on the grounds of Art Omi in Ghent, NY, an arts center dedicated to exhibiting contemporary sculptural works, which are often large-scale outdoor pieces, as well as running artist residencies and educational programs. In designing this building students will investigate relationships between art and architecture. Over the course of the semester students must formulate a position regarding how and how much a building to house art should be aestheticized in relation to the art it contains.

Ideas of what a building dedicated to showing art should be, and how it should present art to visitors, are many and have shifted over time. Today different museums and galleries present art and engage culture in different ways. In the book Towards A New Museum, Victoria Newhouse describes several categories of the contemporary museum including: *cabinets of curiosities* that surprise, delight and ask visitors to make connections between objects; *sacred spaces* that invoke qualities of ecclesiastical architecture in order to raise the experience of viewing art to religious ritual; *monographic museums* that educate visitors about a particular artist’s development; *museums as entertainment* that attempt to engage touristic rather than academic culture; and *museums as environmental art* that become active rather than passive players in how art is viewed and experienced. In designing a new gallery building for Art Omi, students must consider how the existing institution and the new proposal falls into one or more of these categories or seeks to define a new category.

As becomes clear through reading Newhouse, a museum or gallery space is never neutral in terms of how it contextualizes the art exhibited. A museum always has an aesthetic, social and/or political agenda. Art also has aesthetic, social and/or political agendas. For example, art may have an explicitly didactic agenda, as in Bill McDowell’s re-presentation of rejected or ‘killed’ negatives from the Farm Securities Administration (FSA) sponsored depression-era photography, which shows not only living conditions on farms during the depression, but also how some of these photographs were deemed inappropriate for public consumption by punching a hole in the original negative. On the other hand, art may have a more commercial agenda, as in the collaboration between KAWS and Uniqlo, which leverages an aesthetic based on corrupted Sesame Street characters to sell t-shirts and tote bags. In designing an addition for Art Omi, students must consider the content of exhibitions and how architecture and exhibit design relates to the mission of the institution.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Learning Outcome #1: Analytical skills – Ability to read an existing building and distill an architectural idea from a case study project. ### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1. **Precedent Analysis**

To gain an understanding of how other architects on other sites have approached and resolved small museum and gallery projects, the semester will begin with an analysis of this type of building. Students will select one small (approx. 5-20,000 sf) built museum or gallery from a curated list, and analyze it in a series of diagrams that demonstrate comprehensive understanding of how the design was generated and how the building works. Particular attention must be paid to how programmatic and site requirements relate to the architect’s design agenda. Diagrams will be constructed as overlays on drawings collected from print publications and the internet. Existing floor plans, sections, and site plans do not need to be re-drafted.

*AD3 & GAD1 Assessment Measure #1 - Precedent Analysis:*

A series of notated analytical diagrams that describe the following:

- \- architectural concept
- \- orientation to north and solar exposure
- \- gross square footage
- \- net square footage
- \- program organization and distribution
- \- horizontal and vertical circulation
- \- site circulation
- \- lighting (approach to natural/artificial light in galleries)
- \- basic structural diagram (bearing wall, post and beam, etc).

Scale and format of diagrams to be determined with section critic’s guidance.

2. **Generative Drawings**

The precedent analysis will lead into a generative exercise that will become the basis of a formal and conceptual language for the museum project. Specifics of this generative exercise will be defined by each section critic individually.

*AD3 & GAD1 Assessment Measure #2 – Generative Drawings:*

A set of speculative drawings/models based on the precedent analysis that form the basis of a formal/conceptual design language for the museum design project. Scale and format of drawings to be determined with section critic’s guidance.

3. **Assessment of the Existing Institution**

Art Omi is an arts center located in Ghent, NY, the stated mission is:

*Art Omi believes that exposure to internationally diverse creative voices fosters acceptance and respect, raises awareness, inspires innovation, and ignites change. By forming community with creative expression as its common denominator, Art Omi creates a sanctuary for the artistic community and the public to affirm the transformative quality of art. Art Omi is a not-for-profit arts center with a 120-acre sculpture and architecture park and gallery, residency programs for international architects, artists, dancers, musicians, translators, and writers, and a thriving arts education program serving our local community.*  
Based on visiting the site, presentations from the institution’s leadership, learning about the institution from its website, researching its history, and becoming familiar with artists who have been exhibited at Omi, students must make their own assessment of how the existing buildings and site work and/or do not work in terms of the museum’s stated mission.

*AD3 Assessment Measure #3 – Assessment of the Existing Institution:*

200-word text and 5+ annotated sketches by hand, which describe and assess functioning of the existing museum and site in relation to its stated mission.

*GAD1 Assessment Measure #3 – Assessment of the Existing Institution:*

500-word text and 7+ annotated sketches by hand, which describe and assess functioning of the existing museum and site in relation to its stated mission. This text must make specific reference planning of the Art Omi campus, the history of landscape design, and readings assigned by the section critic.

4. **Site Plan**

Understanding and representing all aspects of an architectural project’s site through drawing is a crucial step in the design process. In this case, the site includes existing buildings and landscape. Draw the Art Omi site, show all relevant site information including:

- \- building footprints
- \- building entrances (service and public)
- \- parking
- \- roads
- \- sidewalks
- \- curbs
- \- curb cuts (if applicable)
- \- vegetation
- \- stairs
- \- contour lines of topography

This drawing must be presentation quality, it may be in color or black and white, it may include rendered elements and/or shadows. This drawing of existing elements will be considered a final presentation quality drawing and it will subsequently be updated to represent the design proposal.

Due to the size of the site, the plan must be output at two different scales. Show the entire site of the sculpture park at 1:2500. Show the area around the Benenson Center and Educational Pavilions at 1:500. Adjust line styles and detail for each scale accordingly.

*AD3 & GAD1 Assessment Measure #4 – Site Plan:*

Drawings of existing Art Omi site at 1:2500 and 1:500 scale. Clearly indicate all site elements listed above using carefully calibrated drawing graphics.

5. **Predesign – Building Massing**

The primary goal of this semester is to design a small freestanding gallery building on the Art Omi grounds with a precise tectonic language, highly considered and resolved interior spaces, and a clear daylighting strategy. Begin the predesign process with an exploration of program and how it informs building massing.

In architecture ‘program’ refers to activities occurring in a building and on its site. At its most fundamental level an architectural program specifies an activity and size for a building (i.e. museum), a zone within a building (i.e. exhibit spaces), and an individual space within a building (i.e. gallery). Decisions regarding design of a building are made in direct response to program. Program for this project is a 5500 square foot (sf) freestanding building containing two main programmatic zones: exhibit space, spaces where art is shown, and public space, non-exhibit spaces which all museum goers can access. Individual spaces that must be included are as follows:

- \- gallery space, 2000sf
- \- main entrance and lobby, 1000sf
- \- café, 1000sf
- \- vertical circulation, 500sf
- \- horizontal circulation, 500sf
- \- service space (restrooms, storage, etc), 500sf

Develop a physical massing model that explores and resolves distribution and organization of program. Note that a massing model works with simplified spatial volumes rather than detailed form, and that this massing model must represent the new building in relation to the existing building and site.

*AD3 & GAD1 Assessment Measure #5 – Predesign-Building Massing:*

Physical model showing how program of the new building is organized internally and placed in relation to the ground. 1/16” scale, model materials to be specified by section critic.

6. **Predesign – Response to Context**

In addition to developing massing models, the predesign phase of the project includes developing plan and section diagrams that demonstrate how program and massing relate to context of existing building and site. Consider the following:

- \- how the new building is placed in relation to existing buildings and other site features
- \- how alignments of grids, axes, nodes, or heights (datums) between the new and existing buildings and other site features are created or purposefully broken
- \- how new circulation elements join with and/or alter existing circulation elements
- \- how visitors approach and enter the building
- \- how staff, maintenance and deliveries approach and enter the building
- \- how views from site to building, building to site, and within the building are maintained, created and/or altered
- \- solar orientation of the new addition

*AD3 Assessment Measure #6 – Predesign-Response to Context:*

Annotated plan and section diagrams demonstrating how the new addition relates to specific grids, axes, nodes, datums and views in relation to existing buildings and landscape.

*GAD1 Assessment Measure #6 – Predesign-Response to Context:*

Annotated plan and section diagrams demonstrating how the new building relates to specific grids, axes, nodes, datums and views in the existing building and site. 300-word text describing how proposed contextual relationships between existing and new architecture relate to the current mission and architecture of Art Omi, as well as how proposed contextual relationships relate to a lineage of different approaches to the relationship between architecture and landscape.

7. **Design Synthesis / Parti**

While it is assumed that three-dimensional computer modeling will occur through all phases of project development, two-dimensional orthographically projected floor plans, sections, and elevations will also be used instrumentally in design. These drawings MUST:

- \- be built up from scratch – a three-dimensional model may be used as a reference underlay to help produce the drawing, however it must not be included in the drawing
- \- be used to explore and evaluate design decisions
- \- bring together aspects of the precedent analysis and generative drawings with design decisions made through explorations of massing, programming, and context
- \- be used to explore how natural light is brought into exhibit and public spaces
- \- describe all components of architecture at accurate scale
- \- be used to describe accurate floor, wall, and roof thicknesses
- \- be used to describe simple floor, wall, and roof assemblies
- \- show how all public spaces and restrooms are universally accessible
- \- be graphically clear through effective use of line weight and line type

Refer to Architectural Graphic Standards as well as the reference file provided to the studio for information regarding architectural drawing standards.

*AD3 & GAD1 Assessment Measure #7 – Design Synthesis / Parti:*

Two floor plans minimum (ground level, upper level), two sections minimum (longitudinal and cross), two elevations at 1/4” scale.

8. **Grading and Accessibility Plan**

The project must include 2-4 accessible parking spaces and an area for delivery vehicles to park and unload in proximity to the building. Designing these areas will include involve grading, which is the process of reshaping land, around the building. Develop a 1/16” scale plan showing the following:

- \- how accessible parking and delivery areas are incorporated into the landscape, and how their relationship to the main entrance of the building, and views to and from the building are carefully considered
- \- topography lines showing how land around the building has been reshaped to achieve the desired design, and provide appropriate slopes for the parking areas, roads, and walkways
- \- how vehicles circulate into and through the parking and delivery areas
- \- an accessible route from the accessible parking spaces to the building

*AD3 & GAD1 Assessment Measure #8 – Grading and Accessibility Plan:*

One plan showing parking and grading with 6” interval topography lines at 1/16” scale.

9. **Tectonic Model**

‘Tectonics’ refers to how construction is approached as an art rather than a prosaic technical endeavor. A tectonic approach to architecture implies that assembly, structure, space, and aesthetics are all intimately related. This model must represent at least half the volume of the building (it may be a section model) and how the building sits in the ground. It must explore and show the following:

- \- the structural system of the building
- \- detailed design of interior space development
- \- detailed exterior envelope development
- \- design of at least one daylighting element
- \- accurate floor, wall, and roof thicknesses
- \- simple floor, wall, and roof assemblies

Photograph the model in sunlight to test performance of the daylighting system.

*AD3 & GAD1 Assessment Measure #9 – Tectonic Model:*

Physical model of at least half the building and surrounding site, 1/4” scale. Model materials to be determined with section leader’s guidance. A series of photograph documenting the daylighting study of the new building.

10. **Three-Dimensional Imagery**

Three rendered views of the project are required: one aerial view, one eye level view of building exterior, and interior view within the building. Test and save multiple camera options for each view. Once views have been selected, add detail to the three-dimensional model in areas visible in the views. Images must be rendered as photo realistically as possible in Rhino/VRay. Develop a material and texture palette, a technique for handling context, and an overall aesthetic that complements and reinforces conceptual ideas of the project. Do not default to a conventional sunny day, blue sky, green vegetation aesthetic. Images must be started at least three weeks before the final review and be developed iteratively until the final review.

*AD3 & GAD1 Assessment Measure #10 – Three-Dimensional Imagery:*

Three rendered views of the new addition in relation to existing building and site. Size and format of images to be determined with section leader’s guidance.

11. **Project Manifesto**

A manifesto is a concise, incisive declaration of intent; it is an argument for a position taken. Write a manifesto for the project describing the following:

- \- an attitude toward aestheticization of the museum building in relation to art it contains
- \- how the new addition complements, enhances and/or alters functioning of the existing institution
- \- how program is organized
- \- how the new addition responds to context of the exiting building
- \- any other aspects of the project deemed most important
- \-

*AD3 Assessment Measure #11 – Project manifesto:*

300-word text graphically incorporated into final presentation boards.

*GAD1 Assessment Measure #11 – Project manifesto:*

300-word text graphically incorporated into final presentation boards. Critical summary of all other projects in the design studio section, one paragraph per project.

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is required at all class meetings during the semester. Two (2) unexcused absences from class will result in the lowering of the grade by one (1) letter. Four (4) unexcused absences from class during the term result in an automatic F. Whether an absence (health, immediate family death, officially approved field / sport trip, etc.) is excused or unexcused is determined by the Students Experience Office (4th Floor Academy Hall, x8022, se@rpi.edu), upon presentation of required documentation, according to established policies and procedures (see http:// http://se.rpi.edu/policies/ea/).

Students are required to work in studio during studio hours, and have the required materials and basic tools to develop the assignments. Work includes participation in site visits, design and development, pinups, presentations, discussions and field trips. Failure to work in class with undivided attention and disruptive behavior (tardiness, leaving early, lack of participation, general socializing, etc.), as well working on assignments from other classes during class, will count as absences.

### Other Course Policies:

**Digital Constructs 3**

AD3 will be accompanied by Digital Constructs 3 on Wednesdays. Half of each studio will attend the digital session 2-4pm, and the other half will attend 4-6pm. Digital sessions will introduce new, and reinforce known, tools and techniques that support development of studio projects.

**Pin-ups**

Periodically students will be required to present their work publicly in a formal review process. These presentations are a critical part of the architectural education. It is an opportunity for the student and the faculty to judge the project and critically evaluate the process of design. It may be appropriate at points in the semester to evaluate only selected projects in order to convey information relevant to the entire class. In this situation it is important for each student to witness and participate in the review process. Even if your project is not being reviewed, the criticism and discussion should inform the evaluation of your own progress and design.

**Desk Critiques**

The design studio environment involves two-way communication. The role of the studio critic is to evolve and strengthen the student’s design process. Every time the student meets with the faculty, he or she must have new iterations of work based on previous conversations. If the student has not produced new work, the faculty reserves the right to skip the critique and continue with other students who have produced new material.

**SoA Lectures**

Attendance is MANDATORY at all School of Architecture’s lectures regardless of whether lectures occur in-person or on-line. Unexcused absences from lectures will result in one letter grade dropped from the final studio grade.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.   Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism**

Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of this course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student. Any miss-representation of work shall be evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student will result in a failing grade for the course.
