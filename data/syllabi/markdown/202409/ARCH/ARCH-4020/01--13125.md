## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

In this seminar, design of building-scale objects is explored. The seminar focusses on fabrication, detailing, optimization, and sustainability, along with more traditional aspects of structural and architectural design. We will explore how to implement these considerations into a real-world design.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Suggested Reading:

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Aims

Students will increase their understanding of advanced materials, components, systems, and other critical design considerations. Students will be exposed to Architecture and Engineering vocabulary and concepts to allow them to communicate effectively on a technical level. Understanding of architecturally responsive, sustainable, and minimal structural systems will allow designers to effectively inform early-stage design decisions. The seminar will emphasize blurring the distinction between structure and architecture.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Become familiar with an array of available structural materials and how to use them in design of building-scale objects
- Gain a deeper understanding of non-traditional structural systems and how and when these can be implemented
- Gain a deeper understanding of sustainability, fabrication, lightweight, and constructability considerations in the design of physical objects
- Improve skills in detailing of architectural/structural objects, and how detailing relates to the other major design considerations
- Become more familiar with advanced structural analysis tools, and how architects and engineers can interface and communicate using these tools

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work.
