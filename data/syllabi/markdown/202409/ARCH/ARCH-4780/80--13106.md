## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Overview  
This studio will reimagine the Guild House at the Cathedral of All Saints in Albany, NY as a community benefit. Building in the early 20th century, the Guild House has served various purposes, but always maintained a core mission of providing a community benefit. The building as been used as a landing and housing location for immigrants and school. It has housed after-school programming and a range of supporting functions to the adjacent cathedral. During the 1970’s the building fell into disrepair, and has been abadoned for roughly 50 years. As it has continued to endure structural degredation, Guild House is at risk of being officially condemnged and demolished by the city of Albany. to prevent this outcome, members of the cathedral are actively raising funds and seeking partners in an effort to revitalize Guild House. This design studio is a part of that effort.  
The design project in this studio is adaptive reuse. We will be senstive and deliberate in the additions we propose to Guild House to ensure that our work complements and enhances the original architecture that will remain.

Program  
Programmatic aspirations have been outlined by our partners, and we will work to develop projects that fit the programmatic needs of the cathedral and local community. Guild House has always had a mission of serving the local community and in particular offering free services to those in need. The project also aspires to win state and federal grant money for taking on program that serve the public interest. Programmatic aspirations include:  
workforce training  
computer comptency training  
english language training  
public charter school  
after-school programming  
thrift store  
Guild House Structure and Interior  
Guild House is a well-built structure, however it has been uninhabited for 50 years, and lacks electricity and heat. It has suffered water damage due to the theft of its copper downspouts. Over the years the exterior structure of the building has been degraded, it is clearly in need of exterior repointing and on the top floor one layer of the exterior wall has collapsed. It is possible, however, that the overall exterior structure can be revitalized with specific, targeted intervention.  
The interior or Guild House has deteriorated further than the exterior structure. There are clear examples of water damage throughout the building, with numerous partially collapsed floors.  
The studio will rely upon advice of the team’s structural engineer to determine whether parts of the interior can be salvaged, or if it is better to gut the building.  
Materials  
The studio has an interest in understanding the original materials used in Guild House and proposing appropriate materials to be used in the new project. We will analyze existing materials to understand the embodied carbon value in the existing building, and we will do the same analysis on our design proposals. These analyses will serve as one design evaluation criteria for the success of each project.  
The studio also has an interest in proposing a reuse of some materials in the existing building. Upon recommendation from the structural engineer and preservation architect, groups may choose to propose a repurposing of material to be used elsewhere on site or off.  
Energy  
The project has an interest in proposing ways for the operational enegy use of Guild House to be as efficient as possible. Guild House and the adjacent Cathedral struggle to pay their energy bills. Energy costs was a primary reason Guild House fell into disrepair. The studio will look at effecient energy generation methods such as solar and geothermal to propose for Guild House. The studio will also look at proposing highly efficient building methods to ensure Guild House is able to operate using as little enegy as possible.  
Studio Sequence  
The studio will proceed in several phases. During each phase each group will be working on several tasks, the phases are meant to allos students to develop a comprenhenshive project by breaking it down into parts.  
Case Studies  
We will begin the studio looking at a series of case studies of adaptive reuse projects. Groups will analyze projects through modeling and drawing. It is critical to develop an understanding of the architectural materials and methods used to connect old and new parts of the project.  
Base Information  
Early in the smester we will develop a series of supporting documents and files to support our design project. This will include the development of detailed digital model of Guild House, a site model and site plan, regional study of similar programs, and template document in which we will compile our work.  
The template document will be expanded on throughout the semester to include all important pieces of work produced during the studio.  
Formal Exploration  
Prior to midreview groups will produce a series of formal explorations that will generate ideas for a design strategy for Guild House. These studies will interrogate ideas for the overall design concept for the project. During this phase of studio groups will also beging to suggest materials and program ideas, and will adjust these elements against one another.  
Chunk Development  
As the overall project begins to become conceptually resolved, groups will develop a key part (chunk) of their proposal in detail. The chunk will show in detail ideas of material, structure, connection to existing building and other qualities important to the proposal. The chunk development could be thought of as a roadmap to he larger project. Upon developing a successful chunk students will be able to deploy these ideas throughout the project.  
Landscape  
There is a small landscape on Guild House property just northeast of the building. Groups will consider uses and design strategies for this part of the site, and integrate it into their larger project.  
Final Production  
Each group will produce a considerable amount of final develiverables for their project. These deliverabls will include plan drawings of each level, 2 sections, exterior and interior renderings, as well as a final physical model. Exact specifications for final deliverables will be decided upon in consultation with the studio instructor.  
Additional Considerations  
Relationship to Concurrent Seminars  
The B.Arch cirriculum at CASE is designed to have tight integration with concurrent seminars. Studio work will overlap most notably with two seminars, Systems Prototyping and Materials Systems and Production. In these courses students will conduct analysis and detailed designwork on topics directly related to their studio work. Work produced in these seminars will be expected to augment and support studio work, and visa versa. Students should expect to incorporate work from these seminars into their studio presentations.  
Group Work  
We will work in groups during the research and design phases of this studio. Group work is essential to your development as a student and as a professional. If you have concerns about group work, please discuss with me directly. During the semester we will also work with project partners such as personnel from the Cathedral of All Saints, the project structural engineer, and historic preservation architect. It is essential that we use all of these parties as a resource and interact with them in a professional manner.  
Analysis and Visualization  
Students will be introduced to several softwares this semester that will enable advanced material anaylsis and data visualization. These tools will be are emblematic of the unique attributes of a semester spent studying at CASE.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Outcome 1: Research 1a abilty to use / develop Specialized knowledge: Demonstrate specialized knowledge of adaptive reuse projects through case study research. Demonstrate specialized knowledge of the way architectural systems work in adaptive reuse projects with particular emphasis on structural, material, and energy systems. 1b abilty to use / develop broad and integrative knowledge: Ability to make connections between disparate systems in a building. Ability to show connections and dependencies between existing and proposed buildings. Ability to show the dependencies of architectural systems on one another and the cascading impacts that can be incurred when one system is altered.
- Outcome 2: Intellectual Design Skills 2a ability to design: Ability to propose a design that is directly responsive to previously conducted research. Ability to propose a project that incorporates influence from multiple stakeholders and integrates existing and proposed buildings. 2b. ability to design using information resources: Ability to gather and represent data, and use data as a design driver. Ability to use data to benchmark existing and proposed building/site performance. 2c ability to design engaging diverse perspectives: Ability to propose a project that is sensitive to all relevant stakeholders. Ability to be cognizant of future impacts the site/project will have on its surrounding community and incorporate this knowledge into a design proposal. 2d. ability to design with ethical reasoning: Ability estimate a project’s impact on its surrounding community and incorporate community benifit as a design driver. Ability to propose materials and energy system that will redound to the benefit of immediate stakeholders and well as the surrounding community. 2e. ability to design with quantitative fluency: Ability to benchmark impacts the existing building has had on the site. Ability to evaluate design proposals through quantitative analysis of material and energy systems. 2f. ability to design with communicative fluency: Ability to communicate design intention through diagram, drawing, and verbal presentation. Ability to present a convincing case for a project through demonstration of extensive subject matter knowledge.
- Outcome 3: Collaboration Ability to work with partners and collaborate with the entire studio for the duration of the semester. Ability to incorporate input from and communicate with community stakeholders.
- Outcome 4: Civic and Global Engagement Demonstrate the connection of a project to the larger community and by showing the benefit of its proposed program. Ability to show quantitative analysis of the way in which a project’s architectural systems reponds to global pressures such as climate change.
- Outcome 5: Design Execution Ability to design a project that incorporates multiple stakeholders, research interests, quantitative analysis as well as personal preference. Ability to evaluate designwork by calculating expected performance outcomes. Ability to clearly articulate a project through drawing and verbal presentation.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
