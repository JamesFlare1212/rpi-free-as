## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Overview  
Urban Data I will examine the role of data representation and the use of web-based graphics and in contemporary design. Students will understand the power of combining the thought process and methods of an architect with increasingly sophisticated representational technique that broaden the function of a traditional drawing.  
Drawing from areas of their own interest, students will create unique data sets and design simple games that both educate and challenge the user. Urban Data I brings together a multitude of skills including research, design, and web creation. The ability to effectively combine the ideas and techniques taught in Urban Data I offers students uniquely relevant skills in an era where architects are constantly adapting to ever-changing technology.  
Topics of Interest  
Data Collection  
Students will learn the basics of data collection, primarily from open-source resources. Students will learn the advantages and disadvantages of different file types, and how to harness the power of geolocated data.  
Students will also learn to create their own data sets from the research they perform in Urban Data II. They will learn to format their work so it becomes usable in multiple data visualization softwares.  
Online Visualization and Interactivity  
A key to this seminar is that final work is visible online and has interactive elements. Students will learn to visualize their data online using javascript libraries specifically designed for this purpose. Student work will be visible and sharable on a publicly visible website, enabling it to be easily sharable.  
In addition to existing online, student work will include interactive elements that allow users to reveal further information about a project. Students will learn the importance of visual and informational hierarchy to ensure they are conveying their work in a clear manner. They will learn to leverage static and dynamic elements as it relates to interactivity, and how to draw in and keep a user’s attention.  
Investigative Research  
Students will use publicly available datasets to interrogate a pressing urban problem of their choosing. They will research, represent, and propose solutions using data they’ve collected to guide their decision-making process.  
Coding  
Urban Data I will require students to learn some amount of computer coding. Students will learn to write javascript code and a smaller amount of html. They will use the D3 javascript library to assist in their visualization work.  
The primary focus of this course is not to learn computer coding, but to understand how a small amount of coding knowledge can significantly expand the effectiveness of architectural representation.  
Seminar Sequence  
The seminar will largely contain two phases, a personal map and a larger research project, to be undertaken in teams.  
Personal Map  
The first phase of the seminar will teach students a basic introduction to data collection and representation. During this time students will develop a map of a location where they would like to travel. Students will develop a simple dashboard with the map, and several interactive elements. Inputs and mutable variables will be introdused. From a technical standpoint, this phase of the course will introduce the major techniques to be used during the course. Design and content critique will be less present during this phase, with an emphasis on technical understanding.  
Investigative Research  
The second phase of the seminar will include the development of a piece of investigative research. Students will develop an article akin to a typical data journalism piece to explain their topic and argue for their proposed solution.  
Additional Considerations  
Group Work  
We will work in groups during the second phase of this seminar. Group work is essential to your development as a student and as a professional. If you have concerns about group work, please discuss with me directly.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Outcome 1 Data Collection: (B.Arch+M. Arch) Demonstrate the ability to collect open- source data and manipulate data via Geospatial Information Systems software. (M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to represent relationships between different open-source data sets.
- Outcome 2 Data Representation: (B.Arch+M. Arch) Demonstrate the ability to represent data sets and communicate importance via web-based software.
- Outcome 3 Research: (B.Arch+M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to use data to quantifiably analyze, assess, and project outcomes of a site that is undergoing change over time. (M.Arch) Ability to predict how site outcomes might change given multiple future scenarios.
- Outcome 4 Data Creation: (B.Arch+M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to create data sets directly from course research, and represent these data sets alongside collected data. (M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to create multiple datasets that affect on another.
- Outcome 5 Communication: (B.Arch+M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to communicate existing and projected site conditions via web-based graphics that combine data sets and directly designed geometries. Demonstrate the ability to design and execute a data graphic that contains multiple layers and information to be revealed as a user investigates the work. (M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to create both spatial and non-spatial graphics

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is required at all class meetings during the semester. Two (2) unexcused absences  
from class will result in the lowering of the grade by one (1) letter. Four (4) unexcused absences  
from class during the term result in an automatic F. Whether an absence (health, immediate  
family death, officially approved field / sport trip, etc.) is excused or unexcused is determined by  
the Students Experience Office (4th Floor Academy Hall, x8022, se@rpi.edu), upon presentation  
of required documentation, according to established policies and procedures (see http://  
http://se.rpi.edu/policies/ea/).  
Students are required to work in class during class hours, and have the required materials and basic tools to develop the assignments, including a working computer running the required software. Mac users MUST have Bootcamp, Windows, and the PC version of Rhino installed. Failure to work in class with undivided attention and disruptive behavior (tardiness, leaving early, lack of participation, general socializing, etc.), as well working on assignments from other classes during class, will count as absences.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
