## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Architectural Design Studio One is the first of a ten-semester sequence of design studios that will be required over the course of your five years of study in the B.Arch. undergraduate program at Rensselaer. As the foundation studio of the program, we aim to introduce you to some of the elemental aspects of the creative process as it relates to the discipline of architecture. Of primary importance is the ability to formulate a clear and compelling architectural concept, utilizing the various mediums such as drawing, modeling, and writing. The semester will be structured as a three projects sequence which will build in complexity as the semester progresses.

In their recent book Nexus, scientist Julio Mario Ottino and designer Bruce Mau argue for a collaboration of ideas and knowledge between the disciplines of art, technology, and science. Given the inherent multi-disciplinary makeup of architecture, there may be a unique potential for architecture to bring awareness to some of the key challenges which will likely define our shared futures. As an educational primer to a collaborative future, AD1 will focus on the dialog between art and architecture.

“Knowing some of the history behind creation and discovery may increase creativity rather than diminish it.”

Julio Mario Ottino and Bruce Mau

1. Augmented Thinking for a Complex World, The Nexus, The New Convergence of Art, Technology and Science.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Ability to use architectural drawing, modeling, and visualization to creatively analyze two and three-dimensional spatial objects.
- Ability to generate and develop a design concept, utilizing multiples mediums such as sketching, drawing, modeling, and visualization.
- Ability to accurately represent the spatial, material, and dimensional qualities of an architectural concept in two-dimensions (drawn), and three-dimensions (physical models).
- Ability to use architectural drawing, modeling, and visualization in an exploratory manner to generate and test innovative architectural concepts. Ability to succinctly describe the intent of these architectural ideas in writing.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Assessment Measure Descriptions:

The below seven assessment measures will be applied to the Three Projects.

*Note: Assessment 4 will only apply to Project Three.

1. Object Analysis: 10 points
2. Floor Plans and Sections = 30 points
3. Exploded Axonometric Drawings = 10 points
4. Exploratory Drawings: Space / Time Relationships) (Project Three): 10 points
5. Tectonic Models = 30 points
6. Photographs of Tectonic Models = 10 points
7. Project Manifesto: 300 – 500 words = 5 points

Assessment Measure #1: Object Analysis

Studies and analysis of selected images of Josef Albers, Paul Klee, and GEGO

These studies should aim to uncover the primary qualities of space, form, and geometry inherent in the original artworks. Studies will include the use are multiple mediums and techniques such as:

1. Freehand sketching, hardline hand drawing
2. Digital studies utilizing programs such as rhino, and photoshop
3. Photographic studies
4. Exercises in writing

*(Each of the four components equal 2.5 points totaling 10 Points)

Assessment Measure #2: Descriptive Drawing: Plans and Sections

While it is assumed that three-dimensional computer modeling will occur through all phases of project development, two-dimensional orthographically projected floor plans, and sections will also be used instrumentally in design. These drawings MUST:

1. be built up from scratch – a three-dimensional model may be used as a reference underlay to help produce the drawing; however, it must not be included in the drawing.
2. explore and evaluate design decisions.
3. describe accurate floor, wall, and roof thicknesses.
4. describe primary horizontal and vertical spatial relationships
5. describe entry, circulation, rest, departure.

*(Each of the 5 components equal 6 points, totaling 30 Points)

Assessment Measure #3: Exploded Axonometric Drawings

Exploded axonometric drawings will be used to creatively analyze and study three-dimensional part to whole relationships in the projects.

Assessment Measure #4: Exploratory and Speculative Drawing: Space / Time Relationships *(Project Three Only)

Exploratory and speculative drawings should be used to study relationships such as time, movement, and spatial transformations.

Assessment Measure #5: Physical Models

Physical models must capture the primary three-dimensional spatial qualities of the project. Part to whole relationships must be evident in the constructive logic of the models.

Assessment #6: Photographs of Physical Models

The photographs of the models should aim to creatively analyze and capture

1. Light and shadow.
2. Discrete areas of focus.
3. Logic of Assembly & Disassembly of physical models.

Assessment #7: Project Manifesto: 300 – 500 words

The project text should serve to succinctly capture the overarching ideas of your design proposals.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grading:

1. You will receive one total grade at the end of the semester. You will receive a mid-semester evaluation from your professor, detailing your level of performance. Your final grade may be higher or lower than mid-semester projections, pending your subsequent level of performance. This Final grade will reflect your overall performance and progression over the span of the entire semester.

2. Weekly critical feedback and direction will be provided to the student through individual desk critiques as well as periodic group critiques and discussions. This weekly feedback is meant to keep each student aware of their overall level of development and achievement in the course. Students are encouraged to

request additional clarification of feedback if necessary.

3. The distribution, due dates, and deliverables of all projects and assignments may be

adjusted by your professor to reflect the overall or individual progress of work in the studio.

4. All deliverables represent the final basic minimum requirements for each project. Additional preliminary and / or subsequent drawings, models, photographs, and writings may be assigned by your professor.

5. At the end of the course, students will be asked to complete a self-assessment survey in which they will evaluate their own performance with respect to each assessment measure. Completing the Student Assessment at the end of the course is required, as it is essential for the school’s accreditation. Failing to complete this assessment will result in a 5-point deduction from the overall grade.

6. At the end of the course, select students will be required to submit all final digital and physical works to Publications. Publications will assign a liaison to the first-year studio; all selected students will be responsible for submitting work to the liaison. Publications will archive the digital and physical works and return physical works to the students. Failing to submit all work to Publications will result in a 5-point deduction from the overall grade. Additional submission requirements may be established by each instructor.

Grades will be established according to the following policy:

\- A (EXCELLENT: 100-94 points = A; 93-90 points = A-) Exceptional performance strongly exceeding the requirements of the course, showing strong academic initiative and independent resourcefulness.

\- B (GOOD: 89-87 points = B+; 86-84 points = B; 83-80 points = B-) Performance above the norm; accurate and complete; beyond the minimum requirements of the course; work demonstrates marked progress and initiative.

\- C (AVERAGE: 79-77 points = B+; 76-74 points = B; 73-70 points = B-) Satisfactory work that adequately meets minimum requirements and demonstrates satisfactory comprehension, communication skills, and effort; demonstrates little initiative to investigate the problem without substantial input from the instructor; work shows little improvement.

\- D (INFERIOR: 69-67 points = B+; 66-64 points = B; 63-60 points = B-) Unsatisfactorily meets minimum requirements; demonstrates minimum comprehension, communication skills, and effort at an inferior level; initiative lacking; improvement not noticeable. (D grades not available to graduate students).

\- F (FAIL: 59 points and below) Does not meet minimum requirements; fails to adequately demonstrate comprehension, communication skills, and effort.

Section critics will provide students with a mid-semester evaluation. Assessment measures will be evaluated at the midterm and/or final reviews.

### Other Course Policies:

STUDIO POLICY:

Digital Constructs 1

AD1 will be accompanied by Digital Constructs 1 on designated Wednesdays during studio time. On these days students will attend the digital session 2-4pm or attend 4-6pm. Digital sessions will introduce new, and reinforce known, tools and techniques that support development of studio projects.

Pin-ups

Periodically students will be required to present their work publicly in a formal review process. These presentations are a critical part of the architectural education. It is an opportunity for the student and the faculty to judge the project and critically evaluate the process of design. It may be appropriate at points in the semester to evaluate only selected projects to convey information relevant to the entire class. In this situation it is important for each student to witness and participate in the review process. Even if your project is not being reviewed, the criticism and discussion should inform the evaluation of your own progress and design.

Desk Critiques

The design studio environment involves two-way communication. The role of the studio critic is to evolve and strengthen the student’s design process. Every time the student meets with the faculty, he or she must have new iterations of work based on previous conversations. If the student has not produced new work, the faculty reserves the right to skip the critique and continue with other students who have produced new material.

SoA Lectures

Attendance is MANDATORY at all School of Architecture’s lectures regardless of whether lectures occur in-person or on-line. Unexcused absences from lectures will result in one letter grade dropped from the final studio grade.

Environmental Hazards

If you must use these materials, use the JEC high bay spray booth, or use these materials outside away from windows and doors and against a backdrop to protect lifeforms and building materials from overspray.

THESE MATERIALS CAN BE HARMFUL TO YOUR HEALTH, THE HEALTH OF OTHERS, AND THE ENVIRONMENT. FOLLOW ALL DIRECTIONS ON THE LABELS. This material must be left in the off-gassing chamber for the appropriate time as listed or taken from the Greene Building until the proper time has passed. If any student is caught violating this policy the following may occur:

1. The student may be charged for the cost of cleaning / repairing any damage to the building and its surroundings,
2. The material being worked on will not be included in the review of that project. It will be disqualified from consideration in the evaluation of the project.

WHEN USING ADHESIVES AND OTHER POTENTIALLY TOXIC MATERIALS, FOLLOW THE USER GUIDELINES. IF IT SMELLS BAD - IT IS BAD. IT IS UNACCEPTABLE TO SUBJECT OTHERS TO IT AS IT MAY POSE SERIOUS HEALTH RISKS. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic Integrity

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own performance. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism

Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of this course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student. Any miss-representation of work shall be evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student will result in a failing grade for the course.
