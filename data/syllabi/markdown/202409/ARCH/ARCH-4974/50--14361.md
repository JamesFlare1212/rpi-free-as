## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

INTRODUCTION

The aim of this course is to understand, analyze and represent the layering of Rome’s urban form. Historical, theoretical, and technical information will be introduced, both in class and on-site visits, to take full advantage of your experience as students at the Rome Center. Architecture of the City will foster the observational drawing skills of students with practical drawing exercises on proportion and perspective to prepare the student for further analytical diagramming of ideas. The opportunity to exercise critical thought and understand the urban system through sketches of architectural elements such as geometries, axis, thresholds, materials, volumes and/or voids, historic layering and placemaking is an important feature of the course. This course is a point of departure for further individual research and design, according to your specific goals and design interest.

The course will use the following five principles of perception (from K.Lynch, The Image of the city):

Edges as boundaries, both natural and artificial to understand the limits of the investigation.

Paths as the axial movement within a city space, pedestrian or vehicular, large, narrow, etc.

Nodes as strategic points, crossing streets and piazzas, sometimes gathering points.

Districts as typologies, height, volumes, details of buildings forming a neighborhood.

Landmarks as identity presences within the city, able to orient citizens, target points.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Course Aims:

Provide an overview of the city’s history and transformations.

Support a focus on urban form, historic layering and placemaking.

Promote the understanding architectural projects within the urban context.

Exercise critical thought by developing an argument through drawings and keynotes.

The acquisition of a deeper understanding of the city layout, its topography, and infrastructures.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand the morphology of the historic center of Rome.
- Locate and understand the relationships between different crucial parts of the city.
- Communicate effectively through sketching and on-site drawing.
- Critically assess urban space.
- Apply the vocabulary and theory associated with architecture and urban planning.
- Identify building materials & methods, elements of style & structure.
- Substantiate arguments about architecture and the history of the city.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

EVALUATION CRITERIA

Sketchbook:

The sketchbook will document your experience and learning in the course. It will include the following elements.

- Visual and written notes and from class prep material.

- Drawings and notes done on site, translating the three-dimensional experience of the space into a two-dimensional format. Aim for at least 5 analytical drawings for any site visit.

- Graphic reflections on the site visit in the form of diagrams and additional drawings based on the nature of the site. As a follow-up, students may be asked to visit a similar related location or return to a scheduled location on their own time for the purposes of practicing drawing exercises.

Boards:

- There are three boards due. Each student will, create a visual layout for each site visit, reflecting critical observation through synthetic juxtaposition.

- A rubric that evaluates time investment, craftsmanship, analytical is skills, understanding of the of the site to the city, and graphic organization will be used for evaluations.

- Final Collaborative class project and Individual Exhibition Board.

- Boards will be labeled clearly in Helvetica in the bottom right corner ARCH4653 Architecture of the City SPRING 2024 LASTNAME FIRSTNAME.pdf, (the Sheet remains in Horizontal Orientation).

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Education at the UARK Rome Center requires active involvement in the learning process. Therefore, students have the responsibility to attend classes and to actively engage in all learning assignments or opportunities provided in their classes. There may be times, however, when illness, family crisis, or university-related activities make full attendance or participation impossible. In these situations, students are responsible for making timely arrangements with the instructor to make up for the work missed. Examples of justified absences include those resulting from the following: illness of the student, serious illness, or death of a member of the student’s immediate family, or other family crisis. The instructor has the right to request appropriate documentation from students for any absence for which they wish to be excused.

As far as unjustified absences are concerned, students may miss up to a maximum of 3 contact hours. After the third contact hour missed, students will be formally notified by the instructor. From the fourth contact hour missed, the final grade may be affected at the instructor’s discretion. Courses meeting once a week have 3 contact hours per class, therefore students may miss only one class without major consequences.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
