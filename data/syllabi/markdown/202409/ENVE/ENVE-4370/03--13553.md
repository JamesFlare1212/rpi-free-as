## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Physical processes governing occurrence and distribution of precipitation, (from Catalog) infiltration, evaporation, and surface water runoff. Mechanics of flow and applied hydrology hydraulics. Statistical hydrology, unit hydrograph theory, and watershed modeling. Urban hydrology, hydraulics, and design of storm drainage/sewers, and design of detention structures for flood control. Design project using US EPA SWMM and ACE HEC-HMS flood hydrograph package.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The objective of this course is to develop a working knowledge of the basic principles of hydrology with applications to hydraulic design. Students will do this by building on the knowledge gained through hydraulics (CIVL 2060). Upon successful completion of the course, students will have a basic understanding of precipitation and runoff, hydrologic modeling, flow, and flood routing through natural and manmade catchments. Having successfully completed this course, students will have the necessary skills to take senior-level design courses (ENVE 4180/CIVL 4920).

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\) Model precipitation and its impact on physical systems (watersheds, catchments, channels, rivers, streams),
- 2\) Analyze climatological data for predicting design level precipitation/flood events,
- 3\) Employ principles of conservation of mass, energy, and momentum to describe the behavior of catchments under design-level events,
- 4\) Use concepts of specific energy to determine characteristics of hydraulic control structures and observe these in laboratory experiments.
- 5\) Develop skills in computer modeling of catchment systems using commercial software and various scales (SWMM, HEC-HMS).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* (found at [www.rpi.edu/dept/doso/handbook.html](http://www.rpi.edu/dept/doso/handbook.html)) defines various forms of academic dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Students should familiarize themselves with this portion of the *Handbook* and should note that the penalties for academic dishonesty can be quite severe.

In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work, although students are encouraged to work with each other by collaborating on assignments (see item 2 under *Homework Policy* above). Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy (e.g., one student copying work done by another student or a student copying from a previously developed solution) will result in a grade of zero for that particular assignment. Also, cheating on an exam will result in a grade of zero for that exam and, in accordance with the *Handbook*, notification to the Dean of Students Office of the incident of academic dishonesty.
