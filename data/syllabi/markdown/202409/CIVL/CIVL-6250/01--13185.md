## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The course discusses analytical techniques of transportation system planning, which includes generation, distribution, mode split, and traffic assignment; as well as their integration with the transportation planning process, and economic analyses.

The course will focus on the strategic planning of transportation systems (i.e. infrastructure and rolling stock). The main focus will be on urban transportation planning, though the models that will be covered can easily be adapted to other planning environments.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

**Required text:**

1. "Modelling Transport," 4th Edition, Juan de Dios Ortúzar and Luis Willumsen. John Wiley and Sons, 2011. (A great textbook written by two excellent researchers and practitioners.)

**Optional bibliography:**

2. "Integrated Land Use and Transport Modelling"; Tomas de la Barra. Cambridge Urban and Architectural Studies, 1989. (Students interested in land use-transportation should get this book. The author developed a land use-transportation model that has been applied in a number of different countries.)
3. "Discrete Choice Analysis" Moshe Ben-Akiva and Steven Lerman. The MIT Press, Cambridge Massachusetts, 2000. (This book is a key reference for professionals interested in random utility models. It may be available as a free download.)
4. “Travel Demand Forecasting Processes Used By Ten Large Metropolitan Planning Organizations"; Prepared by ITE Technical Committee 6Y-53; February 1994.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. To provide the students with a thorough understanding of the fundamentals of transportation demand modeling and its application to forecasting and economic analysis.

2. To provide the students with hands-on experience with quantitative techniques of transportation demand analysis.

3. To provide the students with real-life insights into the demand modeling process and the interpretation of modeling results.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Conduct basic trip generation, distribution, model split and traffic assignment as part of the transportation demand modeling process.
- 2\. Formulate transportation alternatives to solve urban congestion.
- 3\. Use statistical techniques to conduct various analyses, e.g., trip generation, generation.
- 4\. Produce basic reports and give presentations.
- 5\. Use computer software for transportation demand modeling.
- 6\. Master the fundamental principles and theories that underlie all aspects of transportation demand modeling and their applications.

## Course Calendar

### Calendar Details:

**I.-** **General background** (one lecture)

The objective of this section is to introduce the students to the different transportation planning environments found in practice. In addition, the limitations and main characteristics of different modal perspectives will be discussed. The current trend toward intermodal transportation systems planning will be analyzed.

1.1 The different environments of transportation planning and their main characteristics.

Urban transportation planning

Regional transportation planning

National transportation planning

1.2 The modal perspective of transportation planning and their main characteristics and limitations

Highway planning

Public transportation planning

Freight transportation planning

Maritime transportation, ports and railroads

Air transportation planning

Special projects: rural roads

**II.- Systems and models** (one lecture)

This section focuses on the concepts of systems and models, which will be used to define the characteristics of urban systems. These definitions will provide valuable information in understanding the benefits and limitations of different modeling approaches.

2.1 System, environment and black box

Definition of system, environment and black box

Classification of systems:

Natural and man-made systems

Probabilistic and deterministic systems

Open and closed systems

Adaptive and non-adaptive systems

Static and dynamic systems

2.2 Classification of models

Physical models: iconic and analog models

Conceptual models: verbal and symbolic models

Treatment of time: Static vs. Dynamic models

According to the modeler's objective:

Descriptive models

Predictive models

Exploratory models

Decision making models: single and multiple criteria

- 3 Model structure: structural equation, variables and parameters. Calibration vs. estimation.

**III.- Urban planning and urban transportation planning** (one lecture)

The objectives of this section are to familiarize the students with the basic concepts of planning and to put the transportation planning process, in the urban planning context, in perspective.

What is planning?

Transportation planning in the context of urban planning

Definition of objectives

Diagnosis of the system

Formulation of alternatives

Quantification of the impacts of different alternatives on the system

Selection of preferred alternatives

Implementation and follow up

Limitations of the traditional UTPS approach

The positive side of the traditional UTPS approach

**IV.- The four-stages model** (one lecture)

The objective of this section is to present the traditional transportation demand model, namely the four-stages model, or the aggregated sequential model. The main characteristics of this model and its limitations will be discussed.

4.1 Approaches to estimate transportation demand (aggregated, disaggregated and combined models). Main characteristics of the four stages model.

4.2 Definition of the network and the internal and external zones.

4.3 Structure of the four-stages model:

Trip generation

Trip distribution

Modal split

Traffic assignment

4.4 Variants of the four-stages model

Pre-distributional modal split

Post-distributional modal split

Elastic trip generation

Joint models vs. sequential random utility models

**V.- Trip generation** (three lectures)

The objective of this section is to familiarize the students with the basic trip generation models. The characteristics of each model will be discussed.

5.1 Objectives of trip generation. Basic definitions

5.2 Classification of trips. Variables affecting trip generation

5.3 Trip generation models:

Growth factor models

Regression models based on aggregated (zonal) and household data

Cross-classification / Category analysis

Special models (i.e. random utility models)

**VI.- Trip distribution** (four lectures)

The objective of this section is to put the students in contact with trip distribution models. A wide variety of models will be discussed, emphasizing the procedure for selecting trip distribution models (which is a function of project characteristics).

6.1 Objective of trip distribution analysis. Trip distribution in the context of the four stages model

6.2 Trip distribution models. General classification:

Growth factor models

Spatial interaction models (socio-physic approach)

Land Use - Transportation models

6.3 Growth factor models

6.3.1 Uniform factor

6.3.2 Fratar

6.3.3 Furness

6.3.4 Limitations and practical uses

6.4 Gravity models (socio-physic approach)

6.4.1 Simple gravity models

6.4.2 Singly constrained gravity model

Constrained on origin

Constrained on destination

6.4.3 Doubly constrained gravity model

Application of gravity models

Calibration of gravity models:

1. a) using the FHWA algorithm
2. b) using maximum-likelihood

6.5 Reinterpretation of the gravity model using entropy maximization

6.6 Additional comments about gravity models. Limitations and virtues. Conceptual-behavioral implications. Practicality.

6.7 Intervening opportunities model

**VII.- Modal split** (three lectures)

The objective of this section is to familiarize the students with post-distributional modal split models. The variables affecting modal split will be identified and the applicability of the different techniques will be discussed.

7.1 Objectives of modal split. Variables affecting modal split. Captive vs. choice. Pre-distributional vs. post-distributional modal split.

7.2 Traditional approaches to perform modal split. Limitations

7.3 Random utility models

**VIII.- Traffic assignment** (four lectures)

This section will provide an overview of traffic assignment models highlighting their range of applicability.

8.1 Objectives of traffic assignment. Traffic assignment methods:

8.1.1 Fixed route traffic assignment vs. route choice models

8.1.1 All or nothing vs. capacity constraint

8.1.2 Single route vs. multiple route assignment

8.1.3 Time dependent vs. static assignment

8.1.4 User optimality vs. system optimality

8.2 Link performance functions

8.3 Tree building techniques

8.4 All or nothing traffic assignment

8.5 Stochastic methods:

8.5.1 Simulation based methods

8.5.2 Proportional stochastic methods

8.6 Congested assignment. Wardrop's principles.

8.6.1 Hard and soft speed changes

8.6.2 Incremental assignment

8.6.3 Successive averages

8.6.4 Frank Wolfe

8.7 Ranges of applicability of the different techniques.

**IX.- Selection of preferred alternatives. Decision making models** (three lectures)

The objective of this section is to provide the students with a basic understanding of decision making models. The emphasis will be put on the models used in transportation decision making.

9. 1 Single criteria models. Based on performance measures (i.e., LOS). Based on economic indicators (e.g., C/B, IRR)

9.2 Economic analysis of transportation projects.

9.2.1 Demand curve. Direct (e.g. price and income) and cross elasticities. Classification of goods and services according to their elasticities.

9.2.2 Supply-Demand equilibrium. Consumer surplus.

9.2.3 Opportunity cost of the capital.

9.2.4 Shadow prices

9.2.5 Economic indicators of return: B/C, IRR, etc. Limitations

9.3 Multiple-criteria models:

Multicriteria matrix

Introduction to multiattribute value functions

**X.- Transportation surveys** (two lectures)

This section will provide an overview of the different types of transportation surveys. The main characteristics and limitations will be highlighted. The emphasis will be put on the statistical design of the survey, the logistical issues of conducting the survey and computational issues (coding, cleaning and processing the data). In addition, the survey's suitability for the calibration of discrete choice models will be discussed.

10.1 Exogenous surveys (passengers and freight):

Home interviews

Mail interviews

10.2 Endogenous surveys (passengers and freight):

On board interviews

Off road interviews

**XI.- Advanced models**

This section will provide a flavor of alternative modeling approaches (if time permits).

11.1 Integrated models of land use-transportation models:

Limitations and virtues

Calibration issues.

11.2 Advanced applications of random utility models:

Limitations and virtues

Calibration issues.
