## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is oriented towards engineering students interested in learning the concepts of intelligent transportation systems. These include highway and transit systems and commercial vehicle operation. Students will be exposed to both public, private and personal ITS systems.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Students will have an understanding of the various ITS applications and technologies in all surface transportation modes.
- 2\. Students will be able to determine the appropriate technology for various ITS applications including communications technology and sensors in vehicles and infrastructure
- 3\. Students will be able to perform an ITS requirements analysis and concept of operations
- 4\. Students will understand the role of the government and commercial sectors of the economy in ITS deployment
- 5\. Students will have an understanding of and appreciation for the environment in which ITS operates which includes concerns about privacy, the role of standards and ITS architecture, and ethical design
- 6\. Students will be able to effectively communicate technical concepts to a broad audience.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Course Papers and Project:**

There will be four brief and one major papers in the course.

Brief papers:

1. Data paper: description at the end of this syllabus.

2. The second will be a description of some sensor technology used in transportation. This may include vehicle presence detection (loop detectors), infrared sensors, GPS, weigh-in-motion sensors, weather (temperature and precipitation) sensors, facial recognition, speed acceleration and direction sensors). Students will make a brief 10 minute presentation on their paper. The paper should include a plain language description of the underlying science of the sensor and some application of the sensor. Presentations on a date to be determined.

3. The third will be a brief (3-4 page) paper describing in plain language a specific communications technology (Bluetooth, RFID, microwave, fiber optic, 802.11 wireless, GMRS, SMS, optical etc.). As in the previous sensor paper, some description of the underlying science and the attributes of the technology such as range, latency, security and throughput. Students will make a brief 10 minute presentation on their paper. Presentations on a date to be determined.

4. The fourth will be a brief paper (3-4 pages) on some non-technical ITS issue. This might include privacy issues, standards development, requiring users to install safety equipment etc. This will also include a presentation. Due date in November to be determined.

Final paper:

Student will also be required to prepare a proposed systems analysis design for an intelligent transportation technology of their choosing. The paper will include a concise definition of the problem to be solved, requirements analysis, concept of operations assessment of alternatives, and a conceptual plan including the specific technology for communications, human interface, possible problems and solutions. Students may work in teams of 2 or 3 people. Instructor has a few ideas on this.

**Grading Criteria:**

The grading will consist of the following.

Short papers 40%

Final paper 20%

Class engagement 20%

Final exam 20%

This course uses grade-modifiers (A, A-, B+, B, B-, C+, C, C- and D).

**Prerequisites**:

CIVL 2030 – Introduction to Transportation Engineering

**Exams:**

There will be a single terminal take home during the last week of class. Make-up exams will not be offered except under extraordinary circumstances.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
