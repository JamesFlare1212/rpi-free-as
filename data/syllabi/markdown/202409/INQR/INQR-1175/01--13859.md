## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Welcome to *Well-being: Cultivating Curiosity!*

From the course catalog: How do we maintain a sense of well-being in our lives? Each person’s path to well-being in body and mind is unique—arising from an awareness of our needs, goals and what we find fulfilling. We will use the theme of curiosity to explore what makes us tick, what makes us feel balanced, stressed, or calm. This interdisciplinary course uses practice-based learning, in-class writing, lectures, creative play, and reading. The class is Communication Intensive.

Additional info: *Attendance and curious participation* in class is required. *Come!* Your attendance is crucial because much of the class is centered on experiential learning right in the classroom. Experiential learning can be a rather slow process. It can be frustrating, because often we can *mentally* understand something quickly, but the body and mind may need time to embody this knowledge. We acknowledge that each person in class arrives with different backgrounds, skill-sets, curiosities, and bodies/brains/minds. Please be patient with yourself, your classmates, and your professor.

Our class is geared toward heightening your awareness of experiences and to develop your writing about these experiences, building your own well-being toolbox, as well as nourishing intellectual insights about the material. You will have time during each class meeting to encounter a wide variety of experiences in order to generate ideas and to write on the spot. This process of generating text is expected to be exploratory and even messy at times. Know that generative writing stages can provide new insights and you will have opportunities to re-write, edit and compose as necessary. As a Communication Intensive course, students will receive detailed feedback on at least one draft before the final submission of papers. Expect to write often in class and for graded assignments.

We will be reading one novel this semester and several other chapters. Although they will be on different topics, they are similar in that we will be considering different lived experiences and relating these topics to well-being from different viewpoints. Thinking about our own identities in relation to the world around us as well as the authors and characters in our readings is a key component of our class.

In addition to daily writing in class, students should expect to regularly work in pairs or small groups and engage fully in class (discussions, dialogues, in-class generative and creative projects, etc). Please expect that you will be asked to share your thoughts, questions, and work each class period. Be prepared to contribute to our regular conversations about the material covered in our class as well as the readings. In doing so, you will also gain firsthand experience in a social justice pedagogical approach called Intergroup Dialogue.

We will be using different analog and digital techniques for communication and honing awareness of your well-being. In addition, you will have an opportunity to opt in to an evaluation of your well-being using different methods to track the dynamics of your own well-being.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\) Students will be able to communicate their ideas clearly, effectively, and creatively.
- 2\) Students will engage curiosity and consider well-being through different forms of expression.
- 3\) Students will be able to demonstrate their understanding of stress and well-being from their own and different perspectives.
- 4\) Students will review and compare a range of well-being practices and technologies.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Attendance grading:** Attendance will be taken each day to determine your attendance grade (percentage of classes attended). I will not count two absences because I understand that health and other emergencies arise.

**Participation grading:** Participation grades will be determined by the percentage of assigned reading observations, reflections, and other activities during class-time that are completed. Work must be turned in on time and will be graded complete/incomplete. All readings and assignments are to be completed **by the date noted**. Always be prepared to discuss readings and activities we do in class…come with questions and comments and submit your reading observations before class starts!

Papers and presentations will be graded 0-100 points. All assignments are to be completed **by the date noted** and turned into LMS (papers) or presented in class (presentations). Late written assignments will only be accepted up to a week late (with a 3 point deduction for each day the assignment is late). Oral presentations will not be accepted late.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

**Professor Walf’s I.D.E.A. Statement** In this classroom, we will acknowledge and aim to respect **i**nclusivity, **d**iversity, **e**quity, and **a**wareness. In our classroom, all students are welcome and will be supported regardless of race/ethnicity, gender identities, gender expressions, sexual orientation, socio-economic status, age, disabilities, religion, regional background, neurodiversity, citizenship status, nationality and the many other diverse identities that we each bring here. Tthe diversity that the students bring to this classroom should be viewed as a strength, resource, and benefit.

We will be focusing on the material from a scholarly humanities and social sciences perspective. We must be aware that the social sciences, like other sciences, for example, are not always objective and unbiased and historically has not included representation of diverse individuals in the experiments or the researchers themselves. For example, individual differences between human subjects in experiments were often minimized or considered confounds because of the added complexity they may have contributed to the analysis of the data. However, in this classroom, we will strive to integrate a diverse set of experiences for a more comprehensive understanding of the material in this course, despite the potential for added complexity. An important part of a college education, and HASS Inquiry courses in particular, is learning about the complexity of various issues. We will also expand our understanding by hearing from diverse voices in the field through our readings and discussions of the material. Moreover, students in this class are encouraged to participate during class meetings and all efforts will be made for students to have an equitable opportunity to contribute and succeed in a supportive learning environment. Grading will also be equitable. Final course grades are non-negotiable and will be based upon the same metrics described in the syllabus for each and every student.

Our classroom will represent a diversity of individual beliefs, backgrounds, and experiences. We all must listen and show respect for every other member of this class even if we do not necessarily agree. Please honor the uniqueness of our classroom community and appreciate the opportunity we must learn from one another. If you have any questions or concerns, please discuss with me.
