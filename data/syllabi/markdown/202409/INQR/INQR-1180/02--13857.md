## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Listening to each other, ourselves, and our surroundings can be a transformative experience that enhances well-being. In this course, listening skills—as related to self-reflection, sensory awareness, interpersonal communication, and our environmental surroundings—will be developed through experiential exercises, creative projects, readings, lectures, and discussion. The course also introduces “Deep Listening”—a practice developed by pioneering composer Pauline Oliveros to enhance and expand listening abilities and to encourage creative work. All abilities and backgrounds are welcome! This course is part of the Well-being Pathway.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate listening skills through o detailed observations of their acoustic environment
- Demonstrate listening skills through o producing effective field recordings and interviews
- Demonstrate listening skills through o applying technical and listening skills to produce cohesive sonic works
- Explicate and discuss assigned texts
- Reflect on their own listening ability and creative work
- Offer meaningful feedback to peers

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

In this course, you will be asked to participate in activities such as listening meditations, communication exercises, and journaling. You will also submit short reading responses and essays, complete hands-on creative assignments, and produce a final creative project inspired by any listening-related topic covered in the course (such as sounding, listening, meditation, soundwalking, acoustics, instruction art, sonification, etc.).  
Complete a self-designed, creative,listening-themed project

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

**Late Submissions**

Assignments that are submitted late will be docked 5% per calendar day. Extensions for assignments may be given to accommodate special circumstances, *but you must arrange for an extension ahead of the assignment’s deadline.*

Remember Murphy’s Law: If anything can go wrong, it will. Be prepared for computer problems, power surges/outages, and corrupt or lost hard drives, etc. Email important files to yourself, or upload them to cloud storage, so that even if your computer and backup hard drive are compromised, you will be able to access copies of your most important projects.

**Statement on the use of AI**

In this class, you will be asked to produce written and sonic work that is exploratory and expressive in nature. Assignments are designed to help you discover and articulate your own concepts, ideas, questions, and challenges in relation to course materials. AI cannot help with this kind of work. Generative AI is also given to produce confidently-expressed “hallucinations” (or what can be more bluntly called “bullshit”). For pedagogical, as well as for social, ethical and ecological reasons, I strongly discourage you from using generative AI in this course. If you do choose to use AI to assist you, you must cite its usage**,** in order to avoid the penalties discussed below.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Collaboration between students in this course is strongly encouraged. Likewise, students are encouraged to exchange ideas, opinions, and information constantly. You are encouraged to help each other with performance, production, and presentation of projects.

With that said, plagiarism of any kind is in direct violation of the University Policy on Academic Dishonesty as defined in the *Rensselaer Handbook*, and penalties for plagiarism can be severe. In this class you will be expected to attribute due credit to the originator of any ideas, words, sounds, or work which you incorporate substantially into your own work.
