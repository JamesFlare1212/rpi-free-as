## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course critically and creatively engages with the concept of the “American dream” interrogating what the term “American” means and has meant to different peoples and cultures throughout history and into the present day. We will read from esteemed writers /creators of literature and other media who have diverse views of what Americanism means. The course focuses on the tension between American dreams and American realities as presented or resisted in the texts we analyze, overall providing students with a range of perspectives. Overall, the course will help you learn to ask meaningful questions, explore cultural conceptions, and analyze texts through meaningful inquiry and critical reading of texts.

Note: During our course you will encounter content that deals with racism, sexism, and other upsetting forms of injustice.

## Learning Outcomes

- Learning Outcomes: • Utilize rhetorical and narrative analysis as a means to investigate the broad, flexible, and interdisciplinary nature of studies in the humanities. • Analyze a variety of texts, visual and linguistic, through a particular historical and/or cultural lens. • Demonstrate the difference between summary, analysis, and synthesis. • Perform comparative close readings of literary and cultural texts, past and present. • Demonstrate oral presentation skills. • Use interpersonal communication skills through exploration and discussion of complex social, cultural, and intercultural issues.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Participation:** There is a participation grade for this course and to receive full credit for each class meeting, you must be prepared for class. Specifically, to get full participation points each class you will need to:

-have your course materials ready: Pen and paper are required, as well as a copy of that week’s assigned readings, whether in digital or hard copy.

-be prepared for class having read all assigned readings.

-share verbally if I ask for comments on in-class work from everyone.

-turn in any in-class written assignments that I ask to collect at the end of class.

-not spend class time chatting, working on anything not related to this class, or using technology such as laptop or phone for personal or recreational reasons.

**Essay in Miniature:** This will be a short critical writing assignment that follows a line of inquiry into the course theme. Major assignments (such as the essay in miniature, the course final project, or the oral presentation) will receive a **grade deduction of 10% per day when turned in after a due date.**

**Final Project:** The final project will ask of you to critically analyze a text from class. Details will be provided on LMS. Major assignments (such as the essay in miniature, the course research paper, or the oral presentation) will receive a **grade deduction of 10% per day when turned in after a due date.**

**Discussion Board/Close Reading Assignments:** Most Fridays a discussion board post will be due on LMS. The assignments need to explore a quote from the reading due that day in class and needs to include a cited quote from the reading. Citations should include an author’s name and a page number. Note that you will not be able to view your peers’ posts until you have posted. Respond with a close reading (zero in on a passage or quote, commenting on the significance of the terms you find in that portion of the text). You may also write about one or both of the texts (if more than one have been assigned for that day) more broadly, but you still need to include a quote to help support what you are saying. Places to start: *What* is the point of the text? *How* does the text do what it is doing? *What* are the results of a certain styles, formats, images within the text? *Why* might the author make the choices they do? Discussion board posts need not be polished like a formal essay, but they need to reflect a careful reading of the text, be legible, and meet the 200-word minimum. Do not attach a document. Post right into the text box. Discussion board posts need to be 200 words, typed, and turned in on LMS. **Late entries will receive 50% credit.**

**Oral Presentation:** The presentation will be based on your final project. You will present your findings to your peers. (Students must be prepared to present on the first day of presentations although not all will be able to present on the first day.) **Not being ready to present will be counted as lateness and will receive a 10% grade deduction for class days attended unprepared.**

**Midterm Exam:** This can be taken at home by the due date listed on the course calendar. The exam will not be timed. **Late exams will not be accepted.**

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance Policy: Attendance is mandatory** as well as an important factor for your success in this course. All students are granted **three absences** without penalty, no questions asked. After these three, each additional absence will result in a deduction of a letter grade (10 points) from your final average. This means that no student missing more than **seven classes** can pass this course and if that many classes are missed you will need to drop or withdraw from the course to avoid a failing grade on your transcript.

Some things to keep in mind:

\- While I ask that you communicate with me and let me know if you must miss class, you are not required to bring me medical documentation, photos, etc. pertaining to absences.

-Even excused absences count toward the three allowed.

-Missing a significant amount of a class meeting will also result in an absence. (This includes arriving late, leaving early, skipping group work, not completing an in-class assignment, etc.)

-It is your responsibility to ensure that you have been noted as present on my roster each day. If you come in after I have taken attendance or passed around an attendance sheet, you need to let me know at the end of class that you were present for the day.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:** If you are not yet aware at this point in your college career, academic dishonesty, including plagiarism, intentional and unintentional, is a serious offense at the college level and can result in your dismissal from the university. **All submitted work must be your own.** Words or ideas from any source (including but not limited to lecture, print, or digital mediums) must be properly cited. **Written work generated by AI and turned in as your own also counts as plagiarism.** This includes Chat GTP. DO not use ChatGTP or any other AI program to generate ideas, formulate or generate text, or reword your own work. **You are not permitted to use ChatGTP for this course any capacity.** Changing phrases from a source and putting them in your own words also needs to be cited. No one may complete in part or in entirety any of your class assignments besides you. Plagiarism will be reported to the Office of Judicial Affairs and will lead to a failing grade on the assignment in question or the course.
