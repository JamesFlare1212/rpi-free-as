The readings consist of excerpts from articles and books (from both the media and academic scholarship).

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- By the end of this course, students will be able to: - Better understand the relations between religion and society. - Appreciate the wide range of perspectives on the nature and function of religion. - Identify ways in which religion engages with society both in so-called traditional and modern cultures. - Relate class material to ethical or cultural issues encountered in daily life. - Gain a basic understanding of some key concepts and theories in social sciences and the ability to apply them to present-day debates.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1\. Reading Summaries

Students must write a brief summary of each assigned reading which must be sent to the instructor (email, Google drive) before the beginning of class.  
2\. Class presentation and Research Paper

Each student will conduct some original research or “fieldwork” on a theme of her/his choice.

3\. Exams: There will be three in-class exams scheduled during the class period

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grading is based on a total of 100 points (100%).

Each exam is worth 25 points (total: 75 points).

Presentation / Fieldwork Report: 25 points.

Reading summaries: 1 point off for a missing summary.

Good summaries and participation in class will earn bonus points.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

Students and teachers will act in honesty. The Rensselaer Handbook defines various forms of academic dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Students should familiarize themselves with this portion of the Rensselaer Handbook and should note that the penalties for plagiarism and other forms of cheating can be quite harsh.

Cheating on an exam. Anyone cheating on an exam will be given a zero for that exam. Two cheating episodes will result in an F in the course and may be reported for academic discipline. No notes, crib sheets or other written materials are allowed during exams. Looking at other student’s work is not allowed.

Do not write anything on an exam paper returned to you after grading by the Instructor. If an exam paper has new markings, this will disqualify you from any consideration of re-grading. Anyone submitting an altered exam for re-grading will be given an F in the corresponding exam. Two cheating episodes will result in an F in the course and may be reported for academic discipline.
