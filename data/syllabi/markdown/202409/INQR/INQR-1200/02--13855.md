## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Principles of Economics is a one semester survey course in micro- and macroeconomics which has two goals: (1) to introduce a wide variety of concepts and applications in the broad subject of economics, and (2) to function in its special Rensselaer designation as a HASS Inquiry and Data Intensive Course. Economics is the study of our choices. Traditionally, these choices have been framed as how best to employ scarce resources to produce goods and services and distribute them for consumption. To describe these choices we will introduce the concepts of opportunity cost, demand and supply theory, and market structures and consider the role of government in resource allocation choices. Also presented are macroeconomic topics such as gross domestic product, economic growth, inflation, unemployment, long-run trends and short-run fluctuations, money, and banking. As a HASS Inquiry course, a foremost objective will be to evaluate multiple diverse perspectives on contemporary and complex global issues and address their implications to social equity. Furthermore, the course is designated as a Data Intensive course and provides opportunity to practice analysis of economic data.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

(A) MyLab Assignments, 45%, (B) Average of four exam scores OR score on final exam, 45%, (C) HASS Inquiry Score, 10%

Final Grades: The letter grade breakdown is as follows, rounded: A 94 to 100; A- 90 to 93; B+ 87 to 89; B 84 to 86; B- 80 to 83; C+ 77 to 79; C 74 to 76; C- 70 to 73; D+ 67 to 69; D 60 to 66; F 59 and below. Grades are rounded to the nearest whole number.

MyLab Assignments: Assignments using the Pearson MyLab online system will be due quarterly. All Homeworks and Quizzes are available from the start of the semester through their due date/time. Due dates are cut-off dates for the submission of work; due dates are not meant to be dates for you to begin or even finish work. It is expected that all students will complete work in a time efficient manner according to Course Calendar on the last page of this syllabus. This means that work should be completed on a weekly basis and in advance of the due date. Any student who saves work to complete on the due date must expect that technical difficulties will arise and prevent the finishing of work, resulting in lost credit. All assignments will be available for late completion until the end of the semester; a 10% late penalty will apply for assignments completed after the due date. See Course Calendar on the last page of this syllabus for the final “late” due date for all assignments.

Tests: There will be four tests administered online via MyLab. The basis for tests will be all textbook readings, all lecture material, and all MyLab assignments. Each test will be made available for a period of seven days; refer to MyLab for specific availability dates. There is no late window for tests. Test format is take-at-home, open book/note, and multiple choice/numerical answer. Each student will receive a unique test. Tests must be completed in one session and will be timed at 110 minutes. **MAKEUP TEST POLICY:** *Makeup tests will only be allowed at the discretion of professor. If allowed, the makeup test is scheduled during the week after the test.*

Optional Final Exam: An optional final exam will be offered as a way for students to attempt to improve their grade. The final exam will cover all course material. If you take the final exam, the score you obtain on the final exam will be compared with the average of your mid-semester tests. You will be awarded the higher of these scores as your grade for the test portion of the course. It cannot hurt you to take the optional final exam.

HASS Inquiry: This course is identified as a HASS (Humanities, Arts, and Social Sciences) Inquiry course. The purpose of a HAAS Inquiry course is to provide opportunity for the collective discourse of diverse and divergent ways of thinking about complex global issues. In particular, we strive to be thorough in understanding many perspectives with regards to important issues and the outcomes they create for society, culture, the environment, the economy, and many other realms. Additionally, we must evaluate the ethical responsibilities of protecting the public’s health, safety, and welfare. These are the specific objectives charged to a HASS Inquiry course. Instructions for the HASS Inquiry assignments will be made available on LMS and discussed in class. **If you miss the HASS Inquiry assignment(s), you may contact the TA for permission to complete late for half credit.** Written work is strictly monitored among current and former students; any student who copies work from another student is violating the academic integrity policy of this course and will be penalized accordingly – see the Policy on Academic Integrity, below.

- ### Academic Integrity

For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

By enrolling in this course, all students are committed to absolute agreement to and understanding of the course policy on academic integrity. MyLab assignments allow teamwork.
