## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course investigates the relationship between warfare and technology in historical and present-day settings. We will also study how military technology might evolve in the future. In addition to considering the ways in which technology has changed the practices of warfare, we will examine the ethical, political, social and economic problems and developments that have arisen from the intersection of military and technological change.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Define warfare and technology in the terms scholars use to understand these concepts.
- Explain the relationship between warfare and technology in a variety of significant historical and present-day contexts.
- Identify the ethical questions raised by the role of new technology in warfare.
- Reflect upon the ways in which next-generation technology, such as Artificial Intelligence and the Internet of Things, might change the way societies experience warfare.

General guidelines for course assignments and requirements:

- Students who enroll in the course after the first day of classes are required to complete all work due within one week of enrolling.
- Late policy: Written assignments may be submitted after their due dates, with the loss of a full letter grade on the assignment for each day the assignment is late. In-class presentations cannot be made up.
- All written assignments should be uploaded to LMS. Due dates are listed in the weekly course schedule below.
- All references within papers should be properly cited according to the conventions of the *Chicago Manual of Style*. See http://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/tools_citationguide.html for more information.

**In-class reading presentations**

Over the course of the semester, each student will play the role of discussion leader for one day of class. On the assigned day, the discussion leader will begin discussion of the assigned reading by providing a five-minute summary of the main **arguments** presented in the reading or readings assigned for that day. The student will then pose at least three questions related to the reading, which we will answer during class discussion.

Presentation schedules will be coordinated during the first week of the semester. **Except in the case of official excused absences, students who fail to attend class or present on their assigned day will not be able to make up the assignment**.

**Identification quizzes**

On four occasions throughout the semester, you will complete an in-class identification quiz. In each quiz, you will be presented with 6 terms related to the course. You must identify each one by writing 1-2 sentences explaining what the term means and why it is relevant to the course. Quizzes will be graded on a pass/fail basis.

The quizzes cannot be made up, even for excused absences; however, you may miss one quiz over the course of the semester without penalty.

**Research paper**

You will write a research paper of at least eight pages in length that examines the relationship between a particular technology and warfare in historical, present-day and/or future contexts. The technology that you choose to write about is up to you. Following are some suggestions:

- Stirrup.
- Rifled gun bores.
- Radios.
- Airplanes.
- Mass production of goods.
- Financial institutions.
- Drones.
- Artificial intelligence.

You need not limit yourself to this list. You may write about a different technology (but in that case it would be a good idea to check with the professor to make sure your chosen technology is a feasible choice).

Your research paper should draw on a minimum of five scholarly sources to explain how the technology impacted/impacts/will impact, and/or was/is/will be impacted by, warfare. Pay attention not just to the technological specifications of the technology you are writing about, but also the broader social, political, economic and cultural factors that shaped its development and influenced its use.

In order to earn an A on your research paper, the paper should:

- Be well written and well organized. Writing quality counts!
- Not merely summarize the arguments made by other scholars, but also express your own, original, evidence-based analysis.
- Cite all sources according to the guidelines of the *Chicago Manual of Style*.

The paper is due on LMS by 11:59 p.m. on December 13
