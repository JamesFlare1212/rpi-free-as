## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

First Year Studio is an introductory seminar where students engage in Collaboratory, interdisciplinary, practice-based projects. This work helps to define the connection between research, discussion, ideation, and making in the world of visual arts.

This course exposes first-year arts students to the purposes and methodologies of a studio practice, and to the social, cultural, environmental, and economic issues being addressed throughout the historical trajectory of artmaking.

A series of studio projects are undertaken. These are based on individual lectures given in studio by contemporary artists, scientists, engineers, and other professionals; whose concepts and methods are then re-interpreted as artworks through research, group making sessions, and ultimately finished individual works for review.

Critical abilities are developed through group discussions and critiques, which reveal a diversity of perspectives and the need for ethical responsibility in the artists’ voice.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. To develop an understanding of the critical relationship between the history of art and art making, contemporary studio practice,and the place that art and the artist hold in contemporary society at large.
2. To develop an individualized methodology for correlating research and practice.
3. To build acohort of community-oriented students with a common desire towards learning, building, and understanding their place in the world of the visual arts.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to discuss the relationship of contemporary art-making practice and the history of art making in general with a broad range of influences.
- Students will explain interconnection of research and practice in art making.
- Students will describe the natural and logical inter-relation between disparate aesthetics, methodologies, and their resultant effectiveness for visual communication.
- Students will collaborate successfully in a studio setting.
- Students will effectively communicate ideas about aesthetic concepts in a group environment.
- Students will empower and encourage other makers to actualize their visions and designs.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students’ final grades are based upon the completion of the final portfolio at the end of the term. However, ongoing assessments for individual projects and current progress and standing can be discussed at any time upon request.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

All grades are based on the following:

- **10%** / **Attendance** at all lectures and demonstrations. (**2** points deducted for any missed lecture.)
- **10%** / Interest and **regular** **participation** in discussions and critiques (Final number represented by a sliding scale 10,8,6,4,2,0)
- **10%** / Motivated problem solving, serious and lively approach to **presentation** of projects, and timely completion of **sketchbook** assignments. (Final number represented by a sliding scale 10,8,6,4,2,0)
- **60%** / **(10%** for each of 6 projects.) Quality of completed **projects** represented by understanding of concepts discussed CLEARLY demonstrated in work.
- Each finished project is graded using the following scale: **√+ = 10** points, **√+/√ = 8** points, **√ = 6** points, **√/√- = 4** points, **√- = 2** points. There are also ½ scale steps of 9, 7, 5, and 3 points which are given for smaller moments of positive or negative attributes in the finished work. These steps are indicated with a circle around the √+ or √-.
- √+: Finished work exemplifies a **complete understanding** of the principles relevant to that project. The work is made with an attention to the individual **student’s** **best** **craft practices** and presents a **thorough****exploration** and application of the concepts discussed in group critiques and demonstrations.
- √+/√: Finished work demonstrates an understanding of **most** of the principles relevant to that project. The work is made with an attention to the individual student’s best craft practices and presents a **concerted effort**towards exploration and application of the concepts discussed in group critiques and demonstrations.
- √: Finished work shows **some** understanding of the principles relevant to that project. The work is made with the student’s **individual** craft practices and presents some exploration and application of the concepts discussed in group critiques and demonstrations.
- √/√-: Finished work exemplifies **very little** understanding of the principles relevant to that project. The work is made **without concern** for the individual student’s best craft practices and presents only **a few moments** of exploration and application of the concepts discussed in group critiques and demonstrations.

- √-: Work is **unfinished** or exemplifies **no understanding** of the principles relevant to that project, and clearly reveals **no exploration or application** of the concepts discussed in group critiques and demonstrations.

- **10%** / Presentation of final written statement and completed **portfolio**.

**Grading example for good but not excellent work over the semester:**

Missed 1 lecture by visiting professional without official excuse or agreement - **2** pts. = **8** pts.

Participation regular but not exemplary = **8** pts.

Presentation of projects and sketchbook work of very good standard = **8** pts.

Work in both individual and finished projects averaging very good but not outstanding = **8** pts. each + extra **2** pts. for excellence in midterm project. = **50** pts.

Final portfolio finished and presented on time = **10** pts.

Total of **84** pts. = a letter grade of **B**.

**Conditions for evaluation of student performance**

- Is the student mentally present, ready to work, and committed to contributing to the day’s conversation?
- Is the student turning in finished works by the due date?
- Is the student working productively and to the communal benefit when working as part of a team?
- Is the student making well crafted, complex, and conceptually layered works that fulfill their stated goals?
- Does the student search for higher ground, and push to expand their knowledge and skill set to produce work of increasing quality and significance?
- When critiquing the work of others, does the student show interest in the artist’s sources and interpretations, and make comments related to their presentation, which help to amplify their meanings and inform the artist of possible alternative choices for realizing their intent?
- Does the student present their work using intelligent and applicable references to their stated intent and to their awareness of the relationship of their work to related examples from the greater history of art making?
- After receiving constructive criticism, does the student respond in a positively considered manner to improve their work?

## Course Calendar

### Calendar Details:

**COURSE CALENDAR**

WEEK 1**:** Intro to class and concepts.

1. Discussion of student research interests and setting of goals.
2. Write a short statement of intent related to course of study and personal interest in the arts.

WEEKS 2-3: Project #1 M.

1st Visiting Artist/Professional lecture and demonstration.

**Kathy High, Professor – Video and New Media**

**B.A. Colgate University, M.A. SUNY Buffalo**

1. Individual statements of intent due for review and discussion.
2. Students research methods and practice basic skills represented in work of visiting lecturer.
3. Work individually to produce small works which represent or interpret the methods and skillsets of visiting lecturer.
4. Critique work as a group. This critique will include the visiting Artist/Professional and any additional visitors chosen by the group to assist with their understanding of the project. Discuss findings and their relationship to individual statements of intent.

WEEKS 4-5: Project #2

2nd Visiting Artist/Professional lecture and demonstration.

**Dr. Helen Zha, Assistant Professor - Chemical and Biological Engineering**

**B.Sc. MIT, Ph.D. Northwestern University in Materials Science and Engineering**

1. Students work in pairs, researching methods and practicing basic skills represented in work of visiting lecturer.
2. Work collectively to produce small works which represent or interpret the methods and skillsets of the visiting lecturer.
3. Critique work as a group. This critique may include the visiting Artist/Professional and any additional visitors chosen by the group to assist with their understanding of the project. Discuss findings and their relationship to individual statements of intent.

WEEKS 6-7: Project #3

3rd Visiting Artist/Professional lecture and demonstration.

**Dr. Moussa N’gom, Associate Professor – Physics, Applied Physics, and Astronomy**

**B.Sc. Lycee Lamine Gueye, Ph.D. University of Michigan**

1. Students work in groups of 3, researching methods and practicing basic skills represented in work of visiting lecturer.
2. Work collectively to produce small works which represent or interpret the methods and skillsets of visiting lecturer.
3. Critique work as a group. This critique will include the visiting Artist/Professional and any additional visitors chosen by the group to assist with their understanding of the project. Discuss findings and their relationship to individual statements of intent.

WEEKS 8-9: Mid-Term. Project #4

1. Work in groups of 3 or 4 to produce larger works which incorporate aspects of all three methodologies and skills represented by first 3 visiting artists.
2. Critique work as a group. This critique may include the visiting Artists/Professionals from the first three projects and any additional visitors chosen by the group to assist with their understanding of the project. Discuss findings and their relationship to individual statements of intent.

WEEKS 10-12: Project #5

4th Visiting Artist/Professional lecture and demonstration.

**Michael Oatman, Associate Professor – Artist in Residence RPI School of Architecture**

**B.F.A. Rhode Island School of Design, M.F.A. SUNY Albany**

1. Students research methods and practice basic skills represented in work of visiting lecturer.
2. Work individually to produce small works which represent or interpret the methods and skillsets of visiting lecturer.
3. Critique work as a group. This critique will include the visiting Artist/Professional and any additional visitors chosen by the group to assist with their understanding of the project. Discuss findings and their relationship to individual statements of intent.

2. Invitational critique of final projects including members of visiting lecturers’ group and guests.
3. Discuss findings and their relationship to individual statements of artistic practice.
4. Produce documentary portfolio of all works produced.
5. Revise statements of artistic practice for inclusion with portfolio for final presentation.

Visiting lecturers will change from semester to semester given particulars of availability and may be different from those listed on syllabus as warranted, but will always represent a variety of disciplines, media, interests, backgrounds, and methodologies.

The fundamental modes of work pursued by students may include, but is not limited to, analog still visual, digital still visual, sculpture, installation, video and other time-based work, sound, and performance.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

*The Rensselaer Handbook* defines various forms of academic dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Students are responsible for familiarizing themselves with these policies. Students should note that the penalty for plagiarism and other forms of cheating is a grade of “F” for the course.

In this class “cheating” consists of plagiarism or representing the work of others as your own through deliberate omission of acknowledgments or references. Cheating also consists of copying graphics, created by others and submitting that work as your own.

It is always acceptable (and encouraged) to discuss your assignments with others in the class and solicit feedback to improve your work.
