## Course Goals

### Goals:

Topics include domestication, early development, socialization, communication, olfaction, emotions, training philosophies, learning theory, play, problem solving, dopamine, MRI studies, fear and stress, punishment, breed differences, welfare, etc.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Learning Outcomes Related to Course Content: Students who successfully complete this course will have demonstrated that they understand: - methods, challenges, and limitations of studying canine cognition and behavior; - models of canine cognition deriving from the application of research methods; - how scientific findings can be applied to improving the human-dog bond, especially through science-based training.
- Learning Outcomes Related to Communication 1. Students will understand linguistic and psychological principles of effective communication; they will learn associated linguistic terminology; and they will be able to demonstrate their learning on exams. 2. Students will be able to analyze specific communication situations, such as: a. how authors of primary literature (specifically, about canine cognition and dog training) write for their peers; b. how professional writings can be simplified for non-professional audiences; c. how non-professionals can skim professional texts with utility; d. how professionals can teach non-professionals using videos. 3. Students will be able to communicate effectively in more than one course-relevant genre, including: a. asking and answering questions about the readings; b. writing short, analytical pieces; c. orally presenting analyses; d. collaborating with classmates on simplifying passages of the primary literature for a non-professional audience. 4. Students will be able to write clearly and succinctly. Most written assignments will be one paragraph, occasionally two. The focus will be on formulating a compelling thesis and supporting it in several well-crafted, logically organized sentences. Students will not be permitted to use AI-based writing tools on any assignments.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- There will be 3 exams, each worth 33.33% of the course grade.
- Class assignments (for the CI angle) will be graded but those grades will be folded into exam grades. For example, if we have two 10-point assignments that count toward a particular exam, then the test questions will be worth the remaining 80 points.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
