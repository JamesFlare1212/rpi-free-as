## Course Goals

### Goals:

The course is intended to make students informed consumers of scientific information and and research methods relevant to human motivation. While there is no one generally accepted comprehensive theory of motivation, there are many findings, concepts and models of behavior that can be very effective for understanding and predicting behavior when used appropriately. Research methods and strategies are available that can help develop useful information for the prediction and understanding of human behavior when reasonable answers are not available.

The course is focussed on providing practice using tools for developing an understanding of specific issues and phenomena. Developing ‘soft skills ‘ related to understanding human motivation is one key to long term career and life success

submit a report of any in class activities (this is a group presentation with individual contributions identified) 2-3 pages

**In order to obtain a C in the course**

**Lecture comment** Each individual must submit one paragraph **per lecture** topic that describes a component of the lecture, a theoretical point, a model of behavior, or an empirical finding, explains it and indicates in a preliminary way how this component can be applied in daily life. In the event that you miss class, submit either an additional paragraph based on a people watching paragraph. Each paragraph should describe what is interesting, why it is interesting, and how it can be useful or insight producing.

**Scavenger hunt**. Each week submit an interesting a youtube clip, a website, a reference to an article THAT ILLUSTRATES OR EXTENDS THE CURRENT TOPIC. These submissions will be evaluated as acceptable or, if unacceptable the student will have the opportunity to revise and resubmit

**Rubric for grading written submissions**

**Excellen**t well thought out, a good integration of course content and real-world applications.

In an ideal essay students will demonstrate:

1. that they understand the material b. that they understand how it relates to their life experiences c. develop a practical application for the material

**Good** makes extensive use of course content, now relate the material to real life issues

Ok **acceptable** but not sufficiently detailed. For future reference, in general what is expected is a paragraph of 3-6 sentences that demonstrates your understanding of the material, and your ability to relate it to real world issues

**Rubrics for grading longer essays and projects**

**A good plan has several elements**

1. A general goal statement ( general goals-intermediate goals-immediate goals)
2. A conceptually based plan.
3. Specific proposals for implementation that make sense but often need to be changed because they don't work, or the real world environment changes.
4. Uses course materials
5. theories. We all use theories and models to explain behavior; hopefully you are using some from the class and will cite them
6. An analysis of the problem
7. A self analysis
8. An assessment of the quality of proposed solution
9. An assessment of the feasibility of application
10. An evaluation of quality of writing

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
