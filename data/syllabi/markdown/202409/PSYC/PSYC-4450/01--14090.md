## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

## This course examines the psychology and neuroscience of learning. Traditional theories of classical and operant conditioning are studied. A wide range of perspectives on this topic will be considered, including human development, clinical disorders, computational theories, and the role of memory, emotion, and social influences on learning.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students who successfully complete this course will be able to: • Describe historical and modern theoretical frameworks for understanding learning. • Understand techniques and methods used to study learning experimentally. • Apply research on learning theory to real-world scenarios. • Utilize critical thinking in order to understand the strengths and weaknesses of • current research in learning.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Course Assessment Measures:**

***Assignments***

Experiment Presentation (10%): Each student will design an experiment that would study a certain aspect of learning and present in class all details of the experiment including operational definitions, stimuli, procedures, and theoretical relevance.

Short Project Paper (15%): Each student will submit on LMS by the last day of classes a written description of the experiment they designed.

***Quizzes & Exams***

Quizzes (15%): The LMS quizzes will be multiple-choice or true/false questions covering assigned readings for the class meeting and the lecture materials that were covered in the previous week. They will be on Blackboard for 24 hours and you will have two attempts and the attempt with the higher grade will be kept in the gradebook. No make-up quizzes will be permitted. The instructor will drop the lowest quiz grade for each student at the end of the semester.

Three Exams (60%; 20% each): There will be three exams during the semester consisting of multiple-choice and short answer questions. Questions will be on materials (e.g. lectures, readings from text and journal articles) covered in class up to the exam date.
