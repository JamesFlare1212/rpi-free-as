## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is the first of a two-course sequence focused on the use of computers as a creative tool in the generation of music and related sonic arts. This course provides a basic introduction to the use of digital audio in compositional contexts, electronic sound synthesis and design, graphical music programming languages, generative music, music interaction, and spatialization. No previous knowledge (other than the basic use of a computer) is required. While the course will focus on hands-on creative projects, additional topics will include relevant readings, facility with appropriate terminology and concepts, representational works and associated listening styles, and relevant local events including concerts and films. Although the course primarily uses the Max software, the techniques and processes covered are replicable in many different software environments. This course is a prerequisite for further creative work with Rensselaer’s computer music facilities, and for Music and Sound II (Arts 4160).

***Additional Course Materials***

**Laptop Computer** Students are required to bring their laptops to every class. Windows or Mac operating systems will work with all the software available and are recommended. A wide range of laptops is possible, but the machine must be powerful enough to do basic audio processing: most recent models will be fine. Linux users will need to use alternate software for the latter half of the course. Your computer must have a USB connection, or an adapter to be able to use USB Type-A connector.

**Headphones** Students are required to bring a set of headphones to class in order to be able to work on in-class audio projects without disturbing each other. It is highly recommended that you purchase a decent pair of headphones if you do not already have some. See the handout “Listening to Recorded Music” for a discussion on the topic and some recommendations.

Being able to hear the depth and detail in the sound is crucial to success in the class:

- Do NOT use the free earbuds that came with your computer/phone/etc.

- Computer speakers are notoriously poor at audio reproduction, so do not do compositional/audio processing work using only computer speakers.

**Software** The course utilizes the following two software environments. Students will be required to download and install all these at some point in the semester. You do NOT need to immediately download before the first class! You will only be required to purchase or subscribe to one software environment.

- **Reaper:** A full-feature DAW (similar to ProTools, Ableton Live, or Logic). It has a free 60-day “evaluation period” which allows full use. The cost of a license is $60, though there is no enforcement mechanism.

- Downloadable at https://www.reaper.fm/download.php

- **Max:** Formerly known as Max/MSP, Max is a popular and flexible visual programming language for interactive sound and media. There are a large number of highly developed externals, some created by institutions such as IRCAM and CNMAT, and can interface with other programming languages. Max has a free 30-day evaluation period, afterward it must be purchased. There are several purchase models available:

Academic pricing: https://cycling74.com/shop/academic/max

- $250 - full license
- $59 - annual license

Users who already own Ableton Live Suite or Ableton Live Standard with Max for Live: https://cycling74.com/shop/crossgrade

$199 - full license only

Monthly Subscription (can subscribe after your trial month): https://cycling74.com/shop/max

- $9.99/mo

At each of these links, you will also see a price/subscription for RNBO - YOU DO NOT NEED TO PURCHASE RNBO.

Linux Users: Max is not available for Linux operating systems, so students may use PureData with the Cyclone externals installed. Note that PureData does not have the same tutorials and packages available as Max, and students will need to be flexible and adapt as needed. (Ideally, Linux users are used to this – that’s why you use Linux!)

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will have a basic understanding and primary skills in fundamentals of acoustics and auditory perception as they relate to course material
- Students will have a basic understanding and primary skills infundamentals of digital sound as they relate to course material.
- Students will competently use a DAW as a compositional tool and creative environment.
- Students will demonstrate basic Max graphical programming skills.
- Students will demonstrate basic sound design and synthesis techniques.
- Students will successfully use Midi and OSC communication protocols.
- Students will use external controllers for digital instruments correctly.
- Students will demonstrate generative and algorithmic musical techniques.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Projects** There are 4 Projects to hand in over the semester, which form the core of the course. These are creative projects using the materials covered in the previous Unit. Each project is to be submitted in a way that shows the student’s work (such as including the relevant software patches), rather than submitting a single soundfile. These assignments may be cumulative - that is, incorporate earlier materials as well. All projects can be submitted through LMS - and some may be played in class, depending on available time. Due dates for projects will be posted along with the assignment. Projects are graded on a 10pt scale, and late projects will be downgraded .5pts for each day late.

**Quizzes** Quizzes are time-limited and taken online through LMS. They are designed to help solidify vocabulary and essential concepts. They should not difficult if you have been paying attention in class, and have done any assigned readings or software tutorials. You are given 12-15 minutes to complete the quiz, depending on the material..

Quiz availability for each unit may be different, and will be announced in class and/or through announcements on LMS.

**Events/Writing assignments** Students are required to attend one event (concerts, films, etc) which can be at EMPAC or other local venues, or through a online viewing. A list of events will be posted in LMS. Short writing assignments based on these experiences, or on class readings, will be posted through the semester.

**Final Project** There will be a creative final project incorporating material covered during the semester. Details of the Final Project will be posted in November. Final projects will be played in-class on the last day of the semester.

### Other Course Policies:

**Participation** This is a hands-on class. This includes working with the relevant software in class during lecture/demonstrations. There will be several in-class projects to be worked on individually or in groups. Participation also includes discussion and analysis of listening sessions and other projects.

Your computers will be open during the session: you are expected to pay full attention during the entire session. Multitasking impedes your ability to learn the material. Please resist the temptation to check email, Instagram, surf the web, do other homework, etc. Students who are consistently caught doing outside activities will be asked to leave the class session and will receive a grade reduction.
