## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Utopia**

"You never change things by fighting the existing reality. To change something, build a new model that makes the existing model obsolete.”

-Buckminster Fuller

Digital Imaging is an introductory studio visual arts class using raster and vector based digital imaging for creative expression. Inquiry and experimentation are encouraged as students develop basic skills through a series of projects.

Projects reflect equal attention to concept, expression, and technique. Screenings, related readings, and discussions of influential themes help give background to the history and theory of visual arts practice in contemporary culture. The themes and projects in the class lead to the development of conceptual and critical thinking skills.

This semester we will focus on the artistic, social, and cultural basis for utopianism, and how artists approach and visualize the concept of utopia. If you could make a better world, what would it be, and, more importantly, what would it look like?

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Course Structure**

This course will include online readings and online lecture viewing, augmented by studio art production, discussion, and quizzes.

The course will be structured around four main components. Each component will have its own readings, quiz, studio assignment, and supporting materials (images, videos, poetry, etc). All four components will work towards visualizing your fictional utopia. The four main components are:

**The People of Tomorrow**

-Photography basics

-Origins/Histories of Utopias

-Machine and Organic visual aesthetics

-Afro-Futurism

-Readings: Excerpts from Richard Nobel’s *Utopia, Utopia: Search for the Ideal Society in the*

*Western World, New York Times When the Shadows Took Shape Review*

-Studio Project: generate a photo essay of your utopian population

**Places of Tomorrow**

-Raster Graphics (Photoshop)

-Collage Basics

-Utopia and the natural and built environment

-Readings: *Art Forum*: Edi Rama feature, *Art Forum:* Thomas Hirschhorn’s Monumental

Endeavor, Guy Debord Excerpts, *Signal Magazine*: Adventure Playgrounds

-Studio Project: Generate a collage-based geography of your utopia

**Artifacts of Tomorrow**

-Vector Graphics (Adobe Illustrator)

-Diagrams/Maps

-Visual Culture of Utopias

-Readings: *Signal Magazine:* Yugoslav Partisan Memorials, *Selections on Agnes Denes*, *100 Diagrams, Signal Magazine: IWW Stickerettes*

-Studio Project: Create a Map, Diagram, object, or infographic of your utopia. Or create an element of printed ephemera from your utopia.

**A Moment of Tomorrow**

-Animation/4D experimentations

-Studio Project: create a literal moment in your utopia in video format. Use Photoshop, Adobe Animate, Adobe After Effects, and/or Adobe Premiere to create experimental video and/or animation

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Student will develop their creative ability and depth of expression using digital imaging tools.
- Students will develop technical skills in raster and vector based imaging, compositing, digital photographic retouching, collage, digital painting, and time-based media.
- Students will apply technical/aesthetic knowledge in the completion of a series of visual art projects.
- Students will gain experience in critique process by presenting their work in class.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students must demonstrate satisfactory achievement of course objectives through **fulfillment of course projects** and by **contributing to class discussions and critiques.**

Projects will be graded on the quality of ideas, the quality of execution and the ability to articulate and explain your thought processes.

Students will be evaluated on the amount of time, effort and thought given to the coursework and participation in course activities.

Consideration will be given to the willingness to explore and take genuine risks in your work as an artist.

After a critique, projects may be reworked and resubmitted. A resubmitted project may receive a high grade, but will never be assigned a lower grade than the original version.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

*The Rensselaer Handbook* defines various forms of academic dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Students are responsible for familiarizing themselves with these policies. Students should note that the penalty for plagiarism and other forms of cheating is a grade of “F” for the course.

In this class “cheating” consists of plagiarism or representing the work of others as your own. This can include the deliberate or unintentional omission of acknowledgments or references. Cheating also consists of copying graphics, created by others and submitting that work as your own.

The use of AI generated work is prohibited in this course, and will constitute as cheating, resulting in an “F” for the course.
