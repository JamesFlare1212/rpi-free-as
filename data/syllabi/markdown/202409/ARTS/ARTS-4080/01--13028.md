## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Engage creatively with the community!

We will begin by examining diverse case studies, with special focus on those located near Rensselaer’s campus in Troy, NY. We will focus this summer to examine case studies from the local to the global as community-based organizations implement innovative solutions for creativity, mutual aid, information exchange, network building and sustainability.

Each student will create a practicum which will represent their key work for the rest of the semester. ACT! provides a core creative forum for students to develop theoretical and practical research on the application of IT tools for art, creative community development, media/technology literacy and education. This course offers an opportunity for direct engagement in social practice and community.

Through direct experience in the community, this course explores the complex roles and relationships of art, education and technology. Students will develop a plan to work with a media arts center, community organization or school, etc. Final practicums will realize real-world arts and technology education projects that ultimately will be realized as significant additions to their professional portfolio. Projects can include a range of practices including but not limited to arts, creative writing, creative IT, community art and activism and practices across disciplines.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Students from a wide interdisciplinary range of studies are encouraged to enroll: a strong interest in how you can integrate creativity into your own knowledge base, and a desire to do field work in the community, are all that is required.

The course fosters the teaching and practice of creative interdisciplinary applications with technology, grounded by participatory theoretical models. Replacing passive learning models with praxis, the "learner" is an active agent within the educational process. There will be a special focus on developing skills of collaboration.

The ACT! class will also focus on building networks and tapping upon diverse resources within the School of Humanities, Arts and Sciences as well as connecting to the other schools of Rensselaer, with related interests ranging from arts to social sciences, urban design to biology, history to technological entrepreneurship, computer coding to sustainability studies.

A key goal of this course is to expand education beyond the walls of academe through participatory art and action, tapping on the multiple riches and planting a seed to network with organizations surrounding RPI in Troy, and thereby strengthening a bridge between town and gown. We look forward to the success of this semester’s creative collaborations.

We will examine the vital historical role of arts in community development from the early 1900’s until today. Looking toward the future, we see the opportunities offered in the “Art, Community Technology” course of the Arts Department as a tangible step in support of the Rensselaer Plan 2024 — "transformative in our students’ lives; transformative in our innovative pedagogy, nationally and internationally; transformative in the global impact of our research.”

- Students will use art and creative practice to support community animation and development.
- Students will Identify problems presented in a professional context and devise solutions.
- Students will communicate verbally, visually, and with written words as required in a professional environment.
- Students will apply creative skills and knowledge of technology to practical applications.
- Students will Integrate into a professional context to execute effective work, seeing the project from idea to production to realization.
- Students will develop and manage projects in a non-academic context.
- Students will develop a professional portfolio and resume with real-world applications, uniquely positioning for the job market upon graduation.
- Students will theorize and reflect through research and experience with host institution in relation to contemporary trends in art and social practice particularly community building using technology.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

This course provides students with experience in the practical application of arts practices in the community. It is good for professional development across disciplines, as well as strengthening relationships between RPI and the community. *On a graduate level, the course provides needed experience for those whose research involves social practice and community engagement.*

A plan of study will be developed that sets goals for the semester’s project (see next paragraphs for assignments and grading criteria).

We will create a foundation for your work by beginning with two projects:

1. Case Study

Each student will explore and present creative solutions for building community, demonstrating transformative approaches to social engagement in social practice.

1. Site Visits

Our site visits will offer a window into local organizations in Troy and an opportunity to brainstorm potential projects, from a base close to our campus in North Central Troy. For each site presentation, students will write reflections on the organization and potential opportunities for collaboration.

***Practicum:***

You will choose to accomplish one major practicum for the semester. Working independently is highly recommended; team projects are possible with approval. Each student will develop a written treatment, which will serve as a contract between student and professor.

Fall ‘24 Practicum Projects could include:

- Creative collaborations with recommended and student-researched projects
- Development and production of community media and technology projects
- Theoretical research to support production in the community
- Creation of alternative media messages
- Other related topics to be suggested by student or professor

**This course is originally structured for you to get out into the community to do your practicum; for those students who are virtual, projects are open to collaborations and research from the local to the global, from building grassroots networks to international strategies of engagement.*

Focus on process as well as product is key in your project.

Your collaboration will best be structured to build upon your strengths and areas where you hope to develop new expertise. Possibilities for engagement include: Producer/ Media maker/ Radio journalist/ Graphic designer/ Web designer/ Artist/ Educator/ Promotion and Outreach/ Social Media/ Theorist/ Engineer/ Computer Scientist/ etc. Socially distanced in person projects will be evaluated on an individual basis.
