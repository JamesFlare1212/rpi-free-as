## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

##### CLASS DESCRIPTION

##### Students who take this class learn to make videos using lightweight video cameras, tripods, portable lighting kits, and a variety of microphones. They will leave the class understanding basic rules of composition for individual shots, basic sound recording for film and video, as well as the language of cinema editing including styles ranging from continuity to montage. The students will understand and be able to use multi-track video editing software and will be able to create dynamic relationships between sound and image. The student will know pre-production skills including basic scripting and storyboarding. Students will develop their critical capacity relative to analyzing cinema, video art, and emerging time-based media, and will acquire a basic knowledge of film history dating from the 1890’s to the present. Students will also enhance their ability to work in teams on media projects and will learn about contemporary media practices and distribution.

### Goals:

**GOALS:**

Lab projects and the final project will be graded on three major criteria:

• Effectiveness: Overall strength of work, how well it conveys intent  
• Ambition: Students are rewarded for attempting to make videos that are interesting, sincere, or unique: in short for attempting to make something more ambitious than replicating what is already on television.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- By the end of the semester students will be able to: • Discuss and demonstrate an overview of cinematic principles and history.
- By the end of the semester students will be able to Use basic video production equipment - For example cameras, microphones and lights.
- By the end of the semester students will be able to • Produce and post-produce short films.
- By the end of the semester students will be able to • Create works that are communicative.
- By the end of the semester students will be able to • Plan and develop video projects using pre-production skills.
- By the end of the semester students will be able to • Analyze and critique moving images

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1\) Participation in class: Students are required to participate in class discussions, critiques, equipment demonstrations, and general discussions. Your enthusiasm and opinions are greatly welcomed. Due date: Ongoing.

2\) Exam: There will be one exam. The test may include any and all information from the assigned readings or all lecture sessions.

3\) Essay: (MOVIE REVIEW) Students will write a film review from a screening. Students will write brief essays (in the style of a film reviewer) about the films they see (Minimum 1000 words) AND RELATE THEM TO THE MATERIAL IN CLASS. All papers must be submitted digitally with your name on the file/doc; such as “Press_FilmReview1_Digital_Filmmaking.doc”

**Films will be assigned.**

### **4) Video Assignments:**

- VIDEO ASSIGNMENT #1: VIDEO PAINTING.
- VIDEO ASSIGNMENT #2: VIDEO DIARY/EXQUISITE CORPSE: Collaborative .
- FINAL PROJECT: Points: 25 points : 3-5-minute film: Individual or group project.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance, Evaluation and Time Commitment: Each class is important - we have a lot to cover in the sessions. As noted, much of each class will be devoted to in-class, hands-on work as well as lecture and discussion. Invariably, there will be things covered that cannot be made up outside of class. DO NOT MISS CLASS!

All of this reading, viewing, synthesizing, writing and producing will take time - plan on spending about six hours a week outside of class.

Attendance at classes and participation in discussions is mandatory, and your grade will reflect this. This means joining in class discussions and responding to questions asked by the professor. Please email me if you will not be attending class: presse2@rpi.edu. (In the case of a request for an excused absence due to extraordinary circumstance-- such as severe illness, family emergency-- the instructor must have been notified in advance of a possible need to miss class and must have approved the absence in advance, possibly pending later corroboration such as a letter from an authority.)

Students are required to attend class and will lose points toward their final grade if they are absent. After the first three absences, You will loose a ½ letter grade per additional absence.

Three late = one absence.

Missed exams or assignments: The due dates for all assignments and the mid-term exams are listed on the class syllabus/ updated in the LMS. If a student anticipates being late turning in assignments or has to miss the exam, they may discuss make–up due dates with the professor. Generally, projects will be due within two weeks of initial deadline unless otherwise agreed upon. If later, a half grade point will be deducted for each week delayed. We will try to be as understanding as possible about equipment problems, health problems, etc. So make sure and let the professor or TA know ahead of time if you will not be prepared by the due date. If you do not talk with us ahead of time, your lateness will affect your grade.

### Other Course Policies:

**Use of Wireless Devices:** Use of electronic devices in class is STRONGLY discouraged. This includes all laptops, iPads, PDAs, phones, and other devices. These devices all too often impede learning and concentration, and strain the pedagogical experience. The casual use of such devices is discouraged as it is considered to be detrimental to the social climate and the pedagogical goals of the classroom. Exceptions: accessing assigned readings, presentations and screenings, and note-taking. Violation of this policy will result in a lower grade.

Midterm Grades: If students want a midterm grade they may request it. If their grade is a “C” or lower they will be notified by the professor automatically by email.

**Grade Arbitration:** Your teacher has years of experience looking at time-based media art, and they will be able to give you a fair eye and ear. If the student requests that the professor arbitrate what they feel is an unfair grade, assignments may be redone and /or the professor will review their work a second time in discussion with the student.

**Extra credit policies**: Students may request extra credit work to increase their grade. To be discussed and agreed upon with the professor.

**Incomplete grades:** Students can withdraw from the course up to the registrar’s deadline to do so. Students should not assume that they will be granted an “I” grade unless the instructor has indicated this to them. Students can receive an incomplete if negotiated with the instructor before the end of the semester by filling out the registrar’s form that establishes a deadline for unfinished work. Requests for a grade of “I” will be considered on a case-by-case basis in keeping with registrar guidelines.
