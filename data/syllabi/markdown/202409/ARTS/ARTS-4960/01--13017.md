## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is designed for two Collaboratory cohorts of students studying Visual Arts in the HASS Department of the Arts.

Firstly, it is designed for those students working towards an EARTS Major and their culminating experience in Creative Seminar. By providing these students the space and opportunity to work on independent research in parallel with their established coursework, they will gain a greater understanding of the quality of research and the necessary skills for effective project management required by the Creative Seminar and the final Gallery Exhibition experience.

Secondly, it provides those students working towards an EARTS minor and with a Visual Arts Focus the opportunity to study in more depth media and approaches that are not available in any other courses offered in the program, and that help to amplify their own unique visual language and artistic goals.

In working together, these two cohorts can contribute all their disparate areas of knowledge and areas of research for the collective benefit.

This course offers a collaborative and extended experience for the development of a strong, individual, studio practice.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

After successfully completing this course, students will be able to:

- Develop a productive and efficient working studio process.
- Develop and articulate a personal approach to artmaking as it is reflected in their individually chosen media for the course.
- Interpret, compare, and critique a variety of forms of contemporary art and understand its impact and influence on their own work.
- Produce a predetermined number of works which effectively reflect their intended goals.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Produce and present a body of work that provides evidence of their understanding of, and facility with, their chosen media and approach.
- Produce a working production calendar which provides the necessary evidence of their process, progress, and project management skills.
- Write an artist statement which summarizes their approach and studio methodology as it relates to their working media and its place within the larger trajectory of historical and contemporary artmaking practices.
- Develop the conceptual and technical basis for their chosen media studies through research, studio practice, group discussion, and self-reflection.
- Develop and accomplish their own individualized studio practice.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Grading will be composed of three major components:

1. Did the student produce everything they set out to do, and do it with the quality and authority that it deserved?
2. Was the student consistently available and prepared for critique and review during all scheduled studio sessions?
3. Did the student consistently and productively contribute to group discussions?

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

In this class “cheating” consists of plagiarism or representing the work of others as your own. This can include the deliberate or unintentional omission of acknowledgments or references. Cheating also consists of copying images, audio, video, or any creative assets generated by others and submitting that work as your own. Deliberate plagiarism is grounds for failure of the course.
