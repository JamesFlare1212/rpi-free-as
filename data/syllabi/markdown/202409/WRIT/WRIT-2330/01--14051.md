## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Students will demonstrate knowledge of fiction writing in general and the short story in particular by utilizing appropriate terminology to analyze and discuss published fiction
- • Students will demonstrate craft-based reading strategies through annotations, short analytical responses, and discussions.
- • Students will establish a consistent creative practice by regularly producing short creative writings and craft analyses.
- • Students will demonstrate engagement in the writing process through observing, drafting, peer critique, and revision.
- • Students will actively engage in a creative community of peers through participation in workshop and discussion.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Reading.** In this course, you will learn to read like a writer, which is somewhat different than the reading you may be used to in English classes.  
**Short Exercises (SEs).** These are short creative writing prompts designed to give you practice in one particular skill.  
**Craft Analyses:** Once per unit (3 times over the semester), you will submit a two-page craft analysis in which you showcase your ability to read like a writer.  
**Drafts:** You will write at least three pieces of fiction this semester: a micro fiction (up to 300-500 words), a work of flash fiction (500-1000 words), and a short story (1200- 2000 words).  
**Participation:** *Class Discussions & Activities:* I expect that each of you contribute to discussions, in large and/or small groups, every class. *Workshops***:** I will weight your verbal contributions to workshops heavily in your class participation grade.  
**Peer End Comments.** You will write peer feedback letters for the flash fiction and short story assignments.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

This is a small seminar class, the success of which is entirely dependent on active student presence and participation. Everyone has two “freebie” absences to use for sleep, self-care, or other obligations without point penalty. Therefore, I do not routinely distinguish between excused and unexcused absences. I expect that you will come to class every class (school athletes see me at the beginning of the term), barring extended illness. Each absence beyond the second one will drop your grade by ten points (1%). If you miss more than five classes without administrative excusal, you will receive no credit for the course even if you are otherwise passing. Note that three tardies of half hour or more accrue to one absence. Attendance is calculated into your unit participation grade.

### Other Course Policies:

## Late Work Policy

Due to the nature of workshop, late workshop submissions cannot be accepted. **Do not miss workshop submission deadlines.** For all other assignments, late work is eligible for no higher than a “C” grade. However, I do allow a twenty-four-hour grace period ***only*** on the following assignments before they are considered late: short exercises, final reflection letter. Per institutional policy, late work cannot be submitted after the last day of class for a better grade.

## Final Grading Policy

Students are expected to monitor their grades throughout the semester via Blackboard’s grading feature, and I am happy to help you navigate that. If you have any questions about your grade at any time, please do not hesitate to respectfully inquire. All inquiries should be made as soon as possible and no later than 24 hours after final grades are posted on LMS. Please review the late work policy; students may not submit late work to better a grade at the end of the semester. All grades on SIS are final.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
