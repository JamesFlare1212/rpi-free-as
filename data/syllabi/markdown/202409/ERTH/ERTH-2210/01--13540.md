## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

•Catalog Description: Principles and methods of field work in Geological and Environmental Sciences, including geologic mapping of formations across spatial scales, basic orienteering, glacial geology, modern lake characterization, climate reconstruction using tree and sediment cores, drainage basin analysis, well-logging and water-well sampling and basic pedology. Students are trained in the use of instruments to address selected field problems. Several Numerous all-day field trips every Wednesday throughout the semester (usually occasionally on weekends) required. Course culminates with an independent research project. This is a communication-intensive course.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Course Content/Objectives**

- Represent three dimensional rocks in two dimensions (on paper)
- Identify rocks in the field
- Identify, measure, and describe geologic structures
- Determine relative timing relationships to produce a geologic event narrative
- Build a basic appreciation for the geology of NY state and the northeast
- Draft a polished, consistent, and interpretive geologic map and cross section with continuous coverage and structural measurements
- Recognize depositional environment and discuss changes in geologic setting during deposition of sediments/sedimentary rock
- Write a clear, concise technical report following a standard template, effectively separating observations from interpretations and correctly referencing any non-original material

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Student Learning Outcomes Upon completion of Field Methods, a student will be able to successfully measure and plot geologic data on a map. The student will compile and create polished geologic maps and cross sections based on data he/she compiled or collected. The student will apply field techniques using data collected on field trips to form and evaluate hypotheses about geologic events recorded in the rock record. The student will demonstrate an ability to communicate field observations and lead a group discussion in a field setting. The student will also independently create polished written technical reports, including figures and references. In a field setting, the student will take legible and useful field notes that can be followed by others. Applying these skills, the student will present scientific results in writing and in an informal setting. In a field setting, the student will take legible and useful field notes that can be followed by others.
