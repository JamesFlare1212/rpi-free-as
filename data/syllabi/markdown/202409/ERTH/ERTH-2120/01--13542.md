## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Scope of course:**

1. Introduction to the study of the structural geology
2. Introduction to and practice with the basic measurements of structural geology, including solution of simple structural problems using spreadsheets and computer programs.
3. Analysis and interpretation of geologic maps and geologic history
4. Analysis of stress, strain, and deformation - theory and practice
5. Recognition, description, and interpretation of structures from examination of photographs and virtual field trips (faults, folds, fabrics, etc.)
6. Introduction to the tectonic evolution of selected parts of the Earth, and especially the northeast US.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- The student will\&nbsp;learn the physical and mathematical analysis of stress and strain and be able to solve simple problems of mechanical failure using the Mohr circle, and to understand how geologic factors such as fluid pressure, confining stress, and temperature influence failure
- The student will learn to apply the fundamentals of structural measurements, how to record and display these data on in a variety of ways including on maps and stereonets, and how to solve numerous mathematical problems in structural analysis.
- The student will be able to read a geologic map, construct a structural cross section, and to interpret the structural history of the map area.
- The student will learn how structural features are related to regional tectonism in the context of the theory of plate tectonics.
- The student will also learn about the geologic history of the northeast US through virtual field trips and will develop an appreciation about how the local geology around Troy relates to broader plate motions.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Statement of Academic Integrity**

Rensselaer does not tolerate cheating. In my courses, collaboration in the form of discussion and sharing of ideas is not only permitted but is encouraged between students working on problem sets, laboratory exercises, field projects, and when studying for examinations. However, the final write-up of any problem set, field report, laboratory exercise or paper must be a student’s individual work. That is, under no circumstances shall a student turn in a problem set or a laboratory exercise that is a copy or paraphrase of another student's work. Collaboration of any sort (oral, written or otherwise) during an examination period is strictly prohibited.
