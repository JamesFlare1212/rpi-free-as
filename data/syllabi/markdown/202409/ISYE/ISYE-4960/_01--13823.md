## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides an introduction to game theory and common game theory models in supply chains. This course intends to enable students to develop an understanding of the interactions between multiple decision-makers and to provide a formal game-theoretical analysis. Topics tentatively include an introduction to game theory, static games of complete information, dynamic games of complete information, supply chain coordination, static games of incomplete information, and principal-agent models.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**(Tentative) Course Content**

- Introduction
  - o What is game theory? What is a game? Elements of a game.
  - o Motivation
- Static games of complete information
  - o Basic theory
  - o Applications
    - Cournot model
    - Bertrand model
    - Final-offer arbitration
    - The tragedy of the commons
  - o Mixed strategies
- Dynamic games of complete information
  - o Perfect information
    - Theory of backward induction
    - Stackelberg model
    - Sequential bargaining
  - o 2-stage games of complete but imperfect information
    - Bank runs
    - Tariffs and imperfect international competition
    - Tournaments
  - o Repeated games
  - o Dynamic games of complete but imperfect information
- Supply chain coordination
- Static games of incomplete information
- Principal-agent models

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Identify the type and elements of a game.
- 2\. Formulate different situations as a game
- 3\. Develop ability to solve for the outcome of a game
- 4\. Understand different supply chain coordination models and apply them
- 5\. 6000-level students: Develop ability to critique game theoretical models and suggest improvements

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Student learning outcomes will be assessed based on exams (3) and assignments (5-6).
