## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Description for ISYE 6600: Design of Manufacturing System Supply Chains, (***Credit Hours:* *3)*

An overview of the principals involved in the management of supply chains with an emphasis on demand forecasting, resource planning, supply chain operations and coordination, inventory management, and network design. The course will emphasize the theoretical aspects of these topics, with the development of quantitative approaches to aid in decision-making and managerial insights.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Create and manage a bill of materials and look for improvement opportunities.
- Develop and apply inventory models with focus on key levels of service
- Develop a working knowledge of Enterprise Resource Planning Systems.
- Understand the role and advantages of various modes of transportation.
- Design a supply chain network for regional and/or global requirements.
- Recognize the role of sourcing for either new suppliers or applying vendor management for existing suppliers.
- Document and present a representative Sales & Operations Planning process deliverable.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- 3.1. An ability to identify, formulate, and solve complex engineering problems by applying principles of engineering, science, and mathematics.
- 3.2. An ability to apply engineering design to produce solutions that meet specified needs with consideration of public health, safety, and welfare, as well as global, cultural, social, environmental, and economic factors.
- 3.7 An ability to acquire and apply new knowledge as needed, using appropriate learning strategies.
- An ability to develop and use mathematical models to guide management decisions in supply chain systems and processes.
- An ability to identify and quantify the impact of stochasticity and variability on managing supply chain systems.
- An ability to integrate variability and stochasticity into designing, managing, planning, and operating supply chain systems.
- An ability to apply time series forecasting methods, probability theory, and operations research concepts to supply chain problems and to use these methods to provide insights and managerial recommendations.
- An ability to discuss academic research in supply chain management.
- An ability to identify research contributions to understand why journal articles are published in supply chain management.

## **Homework Assignments:** Throughout the semester exploratory assessment activities will be assigned to reinforce or expand on material that is covered in the lecture. Four homework sets will be assigned and these are to be worked on outside of class. The homework assignments are due at the beginning of class. Homework is meant to be a learning tool. If you are having difficulty, find help right away – don’t wait until you fall even further behind!

## Students may collaborate on the assignments, meaning students can discuss the assignments, but *every student must complete and turn in his/her own written solutions in his/her own words (unless otherwise stated in the assignment instructions)*. Students must illustrate their own understanding of the material, which means that copying solutions from previous semesters or book solutions verbatim is not allowed and is considered a form of academic dishonesty. While students are allowed to use additional sources, but should not be copying directly from these sources. This also means you should not copy directly from resources, like ChaptGPT.

**Activity Assignments:** These assessments, which are meant to provide opportunities for students to test their knowledge throughout the course, will take multiple forms, including case studies, in-class activities, in-class quizzes and assignments, iClicker responses, online activities and online quizzes. Assignment weights will vary depending on the effort required of students.

**iClickers:** iclickers are required for this class; you can either purchase a physical remote iclicker or purchase a student subscription. Here’s information on how to create an iClicker Student Account: **https://macmillan.force.com/iclicker/s/article/How-to-Create-an-iClicker-Student-Account**

All students, even those using an iClicker remote, must create an iClicker student app account and enroll in your iClicker course via the app. Students using remotes must also register their remote in their student app profile.

**Journal Articles:** Students enrolled in the ISYE 6600 section of the course are required to read and analyze a number of journal articles in the field of supply chain. The purpose of these readings is to get students familiar with the type of research conducted in this area, as well as to understand why articles are published, and the variety of journals that publish supply chain research. Each student is responsible for turning in a written Reading Assignment. One type of reading assignment is a Level 3 Assignment, which is an approach to efficiently read journal articles and will be discussed further in class, along with other reading assignment formats.

**Course Project:** Each student will complete a course project that requires three main deliverables: a research bibliography that identifies 10 peer review papers on a future supply chain topic, a written research summary of these papers, and an in-class presentation. Additional guidance will be provided during the course.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

While explicit attendance won’t be taken, class is an opportunity to engage with the material and learn. Thus, students are strongly encouraged to attend all classes. Further, most classes have activities incorporated into them, whether in the form of clicker questions or in-class active learning. These activities constitute the activity assignment grade. If a student has a conflict with attending class, if this has been communicated to the instructor there may be opportunities to make up these activity points.

### Other Course Policies:

**Excusal from Course Homework Assignments and Examinations:** If you know ahead of time that you will miss an exam/homework assignment deadline, then you must make arrangements before the assessment is due.

**Opportunity to Shift Assignment Weights:** Each student can move the weight of a homework assignment to the weight of its associated exam. For example, if a student is not able to submit the homework assignment on time, then they can elect to have the homework weight transferred to the exam that the homework material is on. This option can be used multiple times throughout the semester AS LONG as the student documents in writing (via an email to the instructor) their intention to transfer homework weight to exam weight BEFORE the homework assignment is due. Late assignments (without arrangements before the assessment is due) will receive a zero unless an emergency arises.

**Emergency Situations:** If an emergency arises and a student cannot submit assigned homework on or before the scheduled due date or cannot take an exam on the scheduled date, the student (or the Student Experience Office) MUST give notification to the instructor NO MORE THAN 48 HOURS AFTER the scheduled date.

**Re-Grading Policy:** Document in writing which part you want re-graded and why you are advocating for a regrade. Turn in this document with your original assignment. You will be given one chance (per assignment) to request a re-grade. No re-grade will be accepted more than one week after an exam or an assignment has been returned to the student. Re-grades made after this period have elapsed will NOT be considered.

**End-of-semester grades:** End-of-semester grades are not given. Instead, end-of-semester grades are calculated according to a student’s performance on the individual assessment requirements and the weights in this syllabus. Final grades are, final. Therefore, final grades will not be changed unless a numerical error is made in computing your final grade. After final grades have been calculated, I will not respond to emails received requesting a grade change unless it is in response to a numerical calculation error. Please do not ask for extra credit or extra work after final exams have been administered.

**Inclusive Environment:** It is my intent that students from all diverse backgrounds and perspectives be well served by this course, that students’ learning needs be addressed both in and out of class, and that the diversity that students bring to this class be viewed as a resource, strength and benefit. It is my intent to present materials and activities that are respectful of diversity: gender, sexuality, disability, age, socioeconomic status, ethnicity, race, and culture.
