## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Modern (i.e. large-scale, or “big data”) machine learning and data science typically proceeds by formulating the desired outcome as the solution to an optimization problem, then using suitable algorithms to solve these problems efficiently.  
The first portion of this course introduces the probability and optimization background necessary to understand the randomized algorithms that dominate applications of ML and large-scale optimization, and surveys several popular randomized and deterministic optimization algorithms, placing the emphasis on those widely used in ML applications. The second portion of the course introduces architectures used in modern machine learning because of the proven effectiveness of their inductive biases, and presents common regularization techniques used to mitigate the issues that arise in solving the nonlinear optimization problems ubiquitous in modern machine learning.  
The homeworks involve hands-on applications and empirical characterizations of the behavior of these algorithms and model architectures. A project gives the students experience in critically reading the research literature and crafting articulate technical presentations.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Student will be able to formulate standard supervised and unsupervised machine learning tasks as deterministic and stochastic optimization problems.
- Student will be able to apply key concepts relating to convex functions, convex sets, and optimality conditions for convex optimization.
- Student will be able to implement first-order and stochastic first-order solvers for convex optimization problems.
- Student will be able to discuss the trade-offs inherent in using first- and second-order solvers for optimization problems arising in machine learning, including adaptive solvers.
- Student will be able to implement state-of-the-art classes of architectures used in deep learning in standard problem domains, including computer vision and NLP, and discuss the relevant inductive biases.
- Student will be able to apply machine learning techniques including deep learning methods to real-world problems and evaluate their performance.
- Student will be able to evaluate and discuss properties relevant to the selection and performance of optimization algorithms, including strong convexity, smoothness, and non-convexity.
- Student will demonstrate the ability to read and critically evaluate the contemporary literature on large-scale machine learning by discussing the theoretical guarantees and empirical performance of novel methods, and effectively sharing their conclusions in oral and written form.
- Student will demonstrate the ability to theoretically analyze the performance of stochastic optimization algorithms used in large-scale machine learning.
- Student will be able to implement second-order and quasi-Newton solvers for convex optimization problems.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The assessment mechanisms consist of six homeworks (equally weighted), a project, and weekly participation exercises (equally weighted). The homeworks for CSCI 6962 consist of the homeworks for CSCI 4140 (taught in a concurrent lecture) with additional questions intended to test the additional learning outcomes for CSCI 6962. The project will be conducted in groups of at most four graduate students, and will consist of students reading the ML research literature, implementing and conducting fresh empirical evaluations of a recent contribution to the ML research literature, implementing and evaluating a novel modification to the method, giving an in-class presentation explaining the method, their modification, and the results, and submitting a written report on their project formatted as an ICLR workshop submission.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Plagiarism or collaborative writing of code, program outputs, or written answers will be considered cheating on any assignments other than those relating to the group project. In cases of academic dishonesty, the minimum penalty is a course grade of F. Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).  
The use of artificial intelligence (AI) tools– including models using Transformer or diffusion architectures and foundation models of any type–to generate complete or partial solutions for assignments is prohibited unless instructor permission is obtained in advance. Submission of work generated by AI without (i) prior instructor permission, and (ii) citation of the tool used and how it was used to complete the assignment will be considered a violation of academic ethics.
