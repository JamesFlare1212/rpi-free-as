## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

A theory course on computing, starting with Turing Machines and Boolean circuits, and ending with Quantum Computing. Students must have a strong background in discrete mathematics, probability and linear  
algebra, but no background in quantum mechanics or physics is needed. The range of topics is from Turing  
Machines and solvability to the theory of Quantum Computing and new problems it can solve efficiently.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Learning Outcomes. Students entering this course should have a solid mathematical foundation in discrete mathematics, probability and linear algebra, in addition to a sound understanding of algorithms. This  
course will build from here to develop a a theoretical understanding of computing, quantum computing and  
the limits of computing, both theoretical and those implied by physics. The student will leave the course  
with an understanding of the basic paradigms of computing, the hard problems and the unsolveble problems.  
In addition the student will learn the potential that quantum computing has to offer and some of the hard  
problems it solves efficiently. The student should be able to identify problems which are classically solvable,  
and those not solvable. The student should be able to formulate quantum algorithms for basic problems and  
analyze their feasibility and performance. The student should gain familiarity with the basic mathematical  
techniques and be able to read and critique recent published literature in the field.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

1\. Theory of Computing.  
(i) Quick review of Languages, DFA, PDA.  
(ii) Turing Machines, P, NP.  
(iii) NP hardness and Boolean Circuits.  
(iv) Factoring and discrete logarithm.  
2\. Theory of Quantum Computing (tentative outline).  
(i) Quantum computing basics.  
(ii) Quantum circuits.  
(iii) Feasible Quantum Computing.  
(iv) Factoring and Discrete Logarithm.  
(v) Why don’t we have quantum computers yet?

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand the basic paradigms of computing and quantum computing.
- Identify which computing problems are impossible (undecidable), hard (NP) and easy (P).
- Understand he potential that quantum computing has to offer and some of the hard problems it solves.
- Formulate quantum algorithms for basic problems and analyze their feasibility and performance.
- Implement basic quantum circuits using some standard quantum programming language
- Understand the state of the art algorithms for general tasks such as search and counting.
- Formulate and analyze quantum algorithms for advanced problems using advanced techniques.
- Manipulate the advanced mathematical techniques necessary to read and critique recent published literature in the field.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Homework assignments and programming assignments

### Criteria Details:

Grade. The final grade is 100% based on (approximately 8) homeworks. 4xxx and 6xxx levels are separately curved in the standard way.

• 20% Homework Assignments focusing on basic material and quantum algorithms.  
• 10% Programming assignments focusing on implementation.  
• 70% Homework assignments focusing on intermediate and advanced quantum algorithms.  
• Must solve additional problems and demonstrate more advanced mastery of the techniques.  
• Solutions are graded with heavier weights on the advanced techniques.  
By RPI rules, the grade of ‘D+/D/D-’ is not available to graduate students or students at the 6xxx level.
