## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

In this course, we will concentrate on processes for information creation and consumption. We will investigate concepts related to correctness of information, making sense of information in the presence of uncertainty and psychology of information processing from an interdisciplinary perspective. We will study computational tools and methods to aim production and dissemination of information including text generation tools. We will also study computational tools to aid processing of information including fact-checking, credibility assessment and labelling of information and sources. The course will concentrate on critical analysis of these tools including user studies in their effectiveness, their potential misuse and the limits of capabilities.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

## Course Topics (tentative)

1. Sense-making as a process
2. Many shades of misinformation and disinformation
3. How to find and represent truth
4. Cognition and its biases, believing in truth and misinformation
5. Trust and reliability of information, value-based evaluation
6. Scientific misinformation, “trusting in science”
7. Effective communication, correction of misinformation
8. Fact-checking methods
9. Reliability prediction of sources and data
10. Automated content production and chatGPT
11. Algorithms for networked information, prominence and reliability in networks
12. Spread of rumors on networks
13. Fact-finding methods
14. Impact of ranking and personalization on decision making

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- understand different theories of information processing, trust and sense making from the perspective of psychology and cognition,
- learn abstractions of information processing used in computational methods and various analytic methods to assess their strengths and limitations,
- apply the computer science skills you have learnt towards deploying a complete project, propose a methodology for evaluation and carry it out.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Lectures, Discussion and Class Activities:** This is an experiential class, we will learn by doing. The main part of class will involve short lectures on class topics, discussion of readings and reports on other assigned activities. Every student expected to attend class, present on assignments and take part in discussions. The class will only be strong with your voice and a big part of the final grade is based on attendance and class participation.

**Assignments:** There will be individual assignments to do hands on exploration of topics relevant to this class. Each assignment will have clear guidelines of what the purpose of the assignment is. Assignments will also be discussed in class. Graduate students in this class will receive additional problems or assignments.

**Capstone Project:** There will be a group capstone project. The project groups are expected to be formed in the first three weeks of class and submit a project proposal. The project is expected to find existing sources of code or implement specific algorithms which are combined to solve a bigger problem. The grading of the project will depend not only on the running of the program but also the evaluation of its strengths and weaknesses through data collection and assessment efforts. Groups will be asked to report on their progress through written reports and presentations in class.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

I expect you to complete all the assigned work yourself for individual assignments or with your group for group assignments. If you need to seek assistance from an online tool or service, you need to assess whether:

1. the use of this tool undermines the learning objectives of the specific assignment,
2. the use of this tool is ethical for the assigned task given how it is constructed, and
3. the use of this tool is likely to impact the quality of your output, i.e. by providing biased or incorrect data.

In all cases, please explicitly discuss in your assignment how online tools are utilized and how you addressed any issues you may have encountered. The critical analysis of online tools is very much a part of this course and hence their use is encouraged if specific attention is given to these guidelines. However, I recommend that you do not use any single tool as the single source for consultation throughout an assignment or the course.

Cooperation on assignments is allowed to improve their quality, but any final text must be written solely by you or your group. Remember that learning to find, analyze and synthesize information is your learning objective and you can achieve this by making sure any work you turned in is written by you. I highly recommend that you include in your assignment a short paragraph on how you benefited from others in writing your solutions and cite any sources you consulted.

In both cases, feel free to discuss with me what is an acceptable level of consultation or cooperation for any assignment if you are unsure.

If you present other’s or any online tools output as your own without attribution, you will have violated the intellectual integrity rules. You will receive a significant penalty in your assignments depending on the severity of the offense.
