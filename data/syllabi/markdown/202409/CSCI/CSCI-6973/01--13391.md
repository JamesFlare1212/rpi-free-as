### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

This course provides a comprehensive exploration of quantum computing and its real-world applications. Students will gain a deep understanding of the basis of quantum computing from CS perspective, as well as hands-on experience with quantum programming. The curriculum is designed to bridge theoretical concepts with practical implementation, focusing on the utilization of quantum algorithms to solve real world problems in various domains such as chemistry, finance, power systems, drug discovery, etc. Students will review a lot of quantum computing literature and take advice from invited experts from quantum computing industrial companies.

Throughout the course, students will have the opportunity to program and run their algorithms on RPI's state-of-the-art quantum computer by requesting access via link1, enabling them to directly engage with cutting-edge technology. By the end of the course, groups of students will expect to present a team project related to the course topic.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Class Participation (10%)** Active engagement in class discussions and sharing insights from literature are crucial for a collaborative learning environment.

**Literature Review (15%)** You will conduct a thorough review of recent research papers and articles related to quantum computing. This task assesses your ability to critically analyze and synthesize information from academic sources.

**Literature Presentation (10%)** You will need to present a paper from the recommended paper list, public speaking is an important kill, and can evaluate how much you learned from the paper.

**Reflections (15%)** Regular reflections will be required to help you internalize and apply the concepts learned in class. And some of reflections will be based on students’ course presentation of literature review, you will expect to leave your comments to point out your fundings from your classmates’ presentation and your remaining questions.

**Project Proposal (20%)**

- Initial Presentation (5%): Present your project idea to the class, outlining the problem, proposed solution, and expected outcomes.
- Proposal Writing (15%): Submit a detailed written proposal that includes motivation, related work, methodology, and project plan.

**Group Project (30%)**

- Final Presentation (10%): Present your final project to the class, demonstrating your findings and the implications of your work.
- Final Project Report (20%): Submit a comprehensive report detailing the entire project process, results, and conclusions.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Statements on the Use of AI**

Students can use ChatGPT in this class, including reports and presentations! Students should state that they use ChatGPT if they do use it. Please avoid fake references/sources (or other misinformation) generated by ChatGPT. Within this class, you are encouraged to use foundation models (ChatGPT, GPT, DALL-E, Stable Diffusion, Midjourney, GitHub Copilot, and anything after) in a totally unrestricted fashion, for any purpose, at no penalty. However, you should note that all large language models still tend to make up incorrect facts and fake citations, code generation models have a tendency. You will be responsible for any inaccurate, biased, offensive, or otherwise unethical content you submit regardless of whether it originally comes from you or a foundation model. If you use a foundation model, its contribution must be acknowledged hand in; you will be penalized for using a foundation model without acknowledgement
