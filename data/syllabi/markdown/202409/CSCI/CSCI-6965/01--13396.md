## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will investigate how to use rapidly developing Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) technologies to help address humanity’s unprecedented challenges in environmental conservation. AI/ML offers powerful new techniques, mature code bases, and sophisticated models, but can they be effectively harnessed to solve the myriad challenges we face?

We will study this question in several contexts, thinking about everything from initial problem formulation, to data collection and curation, to model construction, training and evaluation, and to final deployment. Questions of ethics will pervade our conversations.  
4695 vs. 6965  
There are two versions of this course, meeting together, one at the 4000 level and one at the  
6000 level. Students in 6965 will need to complete more advanced work, in particular helping  
to investigate advanced application topics, and plan class discussions and assignments.  
Details will be clear (and will vary) for each assignment.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Define AI/ML problem(s) that can be solved to help address a problem in conservation, while also discussing the broader issues involved in formulating and solving the conservation problem
- Recognize the technical issues at the heart of the AI/ML problem(s)
- Gather, organize and curate representative data sets to train, validate and test solutions
- Precisely define and effectively solve technical AI/ML problems.
- Investigate and address ethical issues involved at all phases of the problem formulation, solution, and system deployment.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Homework assignments  
Lead class discussion  
Participate in class discussions  
Complete a final team project

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Class Attendance and Late Policy  
Students have four free “late” days they can use on homework throughout the semester,  
with at most two used for any one homework. A late day is defined as any whole or partial  
day after the submission deadline. These free late days are to be used for minor illnesses,  
balancing other course work, problems with your computer, etc. After all four late days  
have been used overall or after two days on an assignment a 15% penalty will be assessed  
per day. Students do not need to use late days for substantial personal emergencies: just  
talk to me.  
Class attendance and participation are expected. Similar to homework late days, students may miss up to two classes without excuse or explanation.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
