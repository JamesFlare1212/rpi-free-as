## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This intermediate-level undergraduate course introduces important mathematical and theoretical tools for computer science, including topics from set theory, combinatorics, and probability theory, and then proceeds to automata theory, the Turing Machine model of computation, and notions of computational complexity. The course will emphasize formal reasoning and proof techniques.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to rigorously reason about computation through the use of abstract, formal models. Specific skills learned will include:

- Formulating precise mathematical statements from plain-language descriptions and vice versa
- Using a variety of techniques (including direct proof, contradiction, and mathematical induction) to prove the truth of mathematical statements
- Solving discrete mathematics problems involving enumeration, permutations & combinations, and probability
- Understanding and building examples of several different models of computation such as finite automata, formal grammars, and Turing machines.
- Using the above models to reason about fundamental questions about the nature of computability.

## Specific Academic Integrity Policies

**Submitting answers copied from another student or any other source is never allowed.**

In that vein, students are **explicitly barred** from using Chegg, StackOverflow, or any similar sites for their work on this course. Students are also **explicitly barred** from using ChatGPT or any other large-language model or AI system.

**Exams:** Absolutely no collaboration is permitted on exams.

**Labs:** Students are specifically allowed to collaborate on lab assignments, in groups of up to five, submitting a single assignment for the group. All students in the group must contribute to the work, and all group members’ names must appear on the submission.

**Homework:** Each student must submit their own homework assignment, and in doing so, is affirming that the work is their own. However, students are encouraged to seek help from the instructor, TAs, undergraduate mentors, and each other while working on them. But remember – directly providing answers is not help, it’s cheating.

# A final word

**Take care of yourselves and each other.**

This is a challenging course at a challenging university. It is very likely to cause you stress at times during the semester, and if it doesn’t, some other course certainly will. Some stress is fine – it can lead us to perform better than we might have otherwise, sometimes better than we knew we could.

**But it doesn’t work unless you are in a position to handle it.** This means you need to eat, you need to sleep, you need to exercise, you need to shower, and you need to take time to be social and have fun. **No college course is worth sacrificing your health over.**

If you start to feel overwhelmed, there are many resources available to help you on campus: the Health Center, the Counseling Center, your friends and classmates, and your instructor. **Don’t suffer in silence.**

If you notice that another student appears to be struggling, **reach out!** Sometimes all it takes is someone noticing.

Finally, if you have some issue (on or off campus) that is affecting your class performance, please let me know – I can be very flexible as long as you’re communicating with me. I want you to be both successful and happy, and I will do what I can to facilitate that.
