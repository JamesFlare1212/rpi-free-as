## Course Goals

### Goals:

Acquaint the students with the research activities of COGS department members. This pursues two aims: a) broaden the students’ knowledge of a variety of topics in cognitive science; and b) prepare the students for their research and development activities in one of the departments’ laboratories.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students are acquainted with the research activities of COGS department members

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

A summary final exam consisting of several brief essays on the topics of the lectures and discussions during the weekly meetings where COGS department members described their research directions.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Each take home exam will count for 50% of the grade

## Course Calendar

### Calendar Details:

September 10 Dr. Nirenburg on how to carry out scientific research  
September 17 Dr. Si on the cognitive aspects of developing games  
September 24 Dr. Nirenburg on content-centric computational cognitive modeling  
October 1 Dr. McShane on linguistics for the age of AI and agents in the long game of AI  
October 8 Dr. Alicia Walf on neuroscientific issues in cognitive science  
October 15 Dr. Thomas Ferguson on logical underpinnings of cognitive science  
October 22 Dr. Bringsjord on philosophical underpinnings of cognitive science and cognitive robotics  
October 29 Dr. Strzalkowski on generative AI in the service of cognitive science  
November 5 Dr. Hendler on the future of AI and its impact on the future of cognitive science  
November 12 Dr. Fajen on modeling visual perception and locomotion of robots  
November 19 Dr. Sims on the uses and varieties of reinforcement learning and its uses in cognitive science research  
November 26 Dr. Radev on Bayesian reasoning and modeling complex processes on the basis of “big data”  
December 3 Dr. Ron Sun on cognitive architectures  
December 10. Dr. Nirenburg conducts a final summary class meeting and discussion plus administers the final exam.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
