## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This survey course is intended as an introduction to Cognitive Neuroscience. The topics covered will focus on exploring the neural underpinnings for cognitive processes, such as sensation, language, attention, motor control, executive functions, social communication, emotions, consciousness, and learning/memory. Basic aspects of nervous system function and neuroanatomy, brain development/evolution, structural and functional imaging techniques, and other research methods used in Cognitive Neuroscience will be discussed.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students who successfully complete this course will be able to: • compare and contrast the techniques (direct and indirect measures of brain activity) that are used in Cognitive Neuroscience (cognitive tasks, clinical-pathological correlations, to imaging). • identify brain regions that are involved in the basic aspects of cognition (sensation/perception, attention, memory, emotion, executive processing, social cognition). • communicate in scientific language about important questions in Cognitive Neuroscience. • describe findings and applications of research in Cognitive Neuroscience through articles published in professional journals as well as other media.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Course Assessment Measures:**

***Assignments***

Group presentation (“Journal Club”) (20%): Students will be required to participate in one group presentation this semester, which involves choosing the article to read, leading the discussion of the journal articles we are reading; the articles ***must*** be approved by the professor. Groups will be formed early in the semester based upon similar interest in topics and/or schedule availability (TBD).

ZAPS lab assignments (15%): Students will complete 10 online ZAPS labs related to the content in class throughout the semester. The work should be the student’s own and this is not to be done as a group project.

***Quizzes & Exams***

Quizzes (20%): The LMS quizzes will be multiple-choice or true/false questions covering assigned readings for the class meeting and the lecture materials that were covered in the previous week. They will be on Blackboard for 24 hours and you will have two attempts and the attempt with the higher grade will be kept in the gradebook. No make-up quizzes will be permitted. The instructor will drop the lowest quiz grade for each student at the end of the semester.

Three Exams (45%; 10% each): There will be three exams during the semester consisting of multiple-choice and short answer questions. Questions will be on materials (e.g. lectures, readings from text and journal articles, exercises) covered in class up to the exam date.
