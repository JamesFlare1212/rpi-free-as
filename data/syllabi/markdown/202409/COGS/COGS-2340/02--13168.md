## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course offers a survey of scientific and applied approaches to the study of human language, highlighting the endlessly surprising nature of this sophisticated mode of communication. Topics to be covered include:

- morphology
- phonology
- syntax
- semantics
- pragmatics
- lexicography
- psycholinguistics
- cross-linguistic analysis
- translation by people and machines
- computational linguistics
- pidgins, creoles, dialects
- historical linguistics
- writing systems
- animal communication

As of Fall 2024, this course is Communication Intensive (CI).

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

*The Study of Language*, 8th edition, by George Yule

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course will introduce students to a broad array of linguistic topics and prepare them to carry out more advanced study of descriptive, theoretical, and applied linguistics. It will also help them to develop effective communication skills.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will have sufficient knowledge of linguistics to pursue advanced linguistic topics as well as practical work in areas such as natural language processing.
- Students will understand that problem-solving skills are key to linguistic investigation.
- in fulfillment of the Communication Intensive requirements, students completing this course will: • be able to analyze specific communication situations, including audiences and purposes for effective communication; • be able to identify and describe effective and ineffective writing practices using linguistic concepts and terminology; • be able to generate and analyze paraphrases as well as understand the role of paraphrasing in editing one’s own and others’ writing; • know how to improve scientific writing to make it more understandable; • understand the utility and insufficiencies of using large language models (LLMs) to generate text; • understand the connection between writing/presenting style and cognitive load; • have experience collaborating with their peers on the linguistic analysis of writing; • be able to write clearly and succinctly; • understand the effectiveness of using images, graphics, lists, tables, etc., in scientific writing.
