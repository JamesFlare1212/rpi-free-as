## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The theory of computation currently stands as the most promising candidate for understanding the incredible abilities of the human mind. In parallel with the development of cognitive science, recent advances in artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) have brought about new computational theories of learning. This course will explore the growing convergence between computational theories of human and machine learning. In particular, the course will focus on major theoretical frameworks including deep learning, Bayesian inference, information theory, and reinforcement learning, utilizing recent journal articles from both machine learning and cognitive science literatures. These topics will be critically evaluated from the perspective of how principled computational theories can be instantiated in cognitive systems. Both successes, and limitations, of current computational theories will be considered. This course is a combined undergraduate / graduate seminar, and **all students are expected to participate actively in class discussions.**

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\) Decribe/explain the human mind as a computational system, and how it relates to state-of-the-art approaches in machine learning
- 2\) Gain proficiency in reading and critically evaluating peer-reviewed scientific literature
- 3\) Demonstrate the ability to engage in critical discussion related to scientific theories
- 4\) Develop skills for presenting complex topics to an audience of peers
- 5\) [COGS 6230 Only] Synthesize peer-reviewed literature relating to their own research interests, and present this information in an accessible manner

- **Written summary / discussion points:** For each class, there will be one or more assigned readings (such as journal articles, or book chapters). Students will complete a short writing assignment based on these readings for each class. The writing assignments should utilize the following template:
- — Brief summary of the reading (in a few sentences)
- — A bullet list of at least three discussion points or questions. These should demonstrate the ability to critically evaluate the reading. For example, students might identify strengths/weaknesses of a proposed theory, or suggest how the assigned reading relates to other topics that have been discussed in this class or elsewhere. These discussion points will serve as a scaffold for in-class discussions.
- — (Optional) a bullet list of clarification questions or confusions about the reading

The writing assignments are due at least 24 hours in advance of the class meeting so that the presenter can incorporate them into their presentation.

**Note**: On classes with two or more assigned readings, students can choose to focus on just one of the assigned readings for that class when completing their writing assignment.

- **Class presentations:** Each reading will have one student assigned to prepare a presentation (lasting ~30–45 minutes, not counting discussion time) that summarizes the reading. The presentation should serve as a mini-tutorial on the topic of the reading, illustrate the main points or results described, and potentially describe cited or related work. Each presentation will be followed by a critical discussion of the paper by the entire class. The presenting student will also serve as a discussion leader/facilitator for the discussion and will given access to the submitted weekly writing assignments. Depending on the number of students enrolled, each student can expect to prepare 2–3 presentations over the duration of the course. Students who are presenting on the same week on related topics are encouraged to coordinate their presentations. Additional details will be provided in the handout, “How to Present a Paper”.
- **[COGS 6230 Only] Final presentation:** The final class meeting dates are reserved for presentations by graduate students enrolled in the course. The topic of the presentations will be chosen by the student (while fitting more-or-less within the theme of the course, “human & machine learning”). They should relate to the student’s research area or interests. The presenter will be required to choose and distribute a paper to the class at least one week before their presentation date. The final presentations are in addition to serving as presenter during other weeks of the semester.
- **Discussion participation:** This course follows a “seminar” format. This means that students learn not by passively listening to lectures delivered by the instructor, but rather by actively discussing and engaging with the course material. This is a more challenging format than lecture-based courses. Consequently, participation in class discussions will serve as the largest single component of student assessment.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
