## Course Goals

### Goals:

The goals of this course are designed to apply techniques in experimental physics to acquisition and manipulation of large data sets and to perform sophisticated data analysis. These goals include:

1. Observation and collection of experimental data;
2. Practical experience in working with and manipulating common laboratory equipment;
3. Fundamentals of error and uncertainty in scientific measurements;
4. Computational skills for basic data analysis
5. Written and oral presentation of results

## Course Content

### Content Details:

This course consists of lectures, hands-on experience of physics experiments, and preparation and presentation of written and oral reports. This course is taught differently than most other courses at RPI in that it stresses independent work and self-motivation. The written instruction covers the minimum necessary instruction to conduct the experiments, and student-initiated gathering of further information is expected.

We value the voice of every student in the course. Our diversity as a class—in race, gender, sex, religion, language, ability, veteran status, place of origin—is an asset to our learning experience. As a result, we will design inclusive lessons and assignments that provide you with the opportunity to speak and be heard, explore your own understanding, and encounter each other.

A group of two will complete five experiments over the term. You must select lab partners before or during the first class meeting. You are expected to work together on the electronics lab, then complete all labs together in subsequent class meetings. Before you come into the classroom, you are expected to finish reading all pre-lab materials, including the lab instructions. There will be a pre-lab quiz before each experiment with 5 questions, which will count for 10% of your final grade.

The instructions on the lab notes are a minimum requirement of the labs. Exploring further steps of each experiment beyond the instructions in the notes is expected. **During the experiments, we will instruct more about “what” to do (e.g. principles), but much less about “how” to do it (e.g. which button to push and why).** It is an important problem solving training to find a way to take the desired data based on the information you have and through the logical thinking process when you are in the situation where you have never been before. A separate document that will accompany the Instructions Manual will describe the theory behind the experiment. This is a concise write up but has sufficient information to guide you to an understanding of the experiment.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Communicate clearly with your lab partners, prior to, during, and after each experiment. You cannot do these assignments on your own.
- Communicate your results to the outside world in three different ways: lab notebooks, formal lab reports and a final oral presentation
- Present effectively on scientific measurements

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The **grades** you will receive on the lab notebooks, formal lab reports, and final presentation **are the** **metrics for learning outcome 3 above**. You will receive continuous feedback on what you present that will teach you how to effectively present scientific measurements to peers.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are required to attend laboratory hours as scheduled (see attached course schedule). Students are required to start laboratory hours on time and advance the laboratory work until the end of the scheduled class period. With instructor permission students can leave class before the end of the scheduled period if all measurements have been completed and the collected data is sound. Students are encouraged to start data analysis during scheduled class hours to complete the Jupyter Notebooks on time.

**Class Absences**

***Medical Absences:*** Formal excuses are not required for medical absences for up to a week of missed classes. Students are required to contact course instructors directly to discuss completing missed assignments.

Students missing more than a week of classes due to illness are required to provide a doctor’s note or other supporting documentation to the Student Success Office. Your Class Dean will communicate the receipt of the note (with expected end date) to the relevant faculty.

***Prescheduled Absences:*** A note of excused absence issued by the Office of Student Success are required for prescheduled absences including but not limited to graduate school interviews and/or visits, family reunions, performances, weddings, fraternity/sorority trips, non-sanctioned club or organization functions, professional conference, research discipline symposium.

***Make-Up Policy:*** Students who miss a scheduled lab because of a medical absence or a prescheduled absence can make-up the missed lab with approval by the instructor.

Students who miss a scheduled lab without valid excuse cannot make-up the lab and will receive 0 points for this lab.

When the instructor finds a student’s attendance unsatisfactory, the instructor will submit a notice through the Electronic Warning System (EWS).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Working together on conducting the experiment, collecting data, and analyzing date is permitted and expected among lab partners. Working together means preparing properly, performing an equal share of the laboratory work, and discussing ideas openly. Students are permitted to discuss any aspect of the experiments with anyone.

Students are required to engage actively in the experiment within the limits of their abilities. ***Substitution***, i.e. utilizing a proxy or acting as a proxy when conducting the experiment ***is considered an academic integrity violation*** and will result in zero points for the assignment[i1] .

All assignments (homeworks, Jupyter notebooks, final report, oral report) are handed in individually. Lab partners must hand in separate and different write-ups. ***Copying*** your partner’s laboratory notes, data analysis and writing, or copying from the internet or an existing reference ***is considered an academic integrity violation*** and will result in zero points for the assignment.

***Fabrication*** of data and ***falsification*** of lab records are ***considered an academic integrity violation*** and will result in zero points for the assignment. However, in the unfortunate case where data was compromised as a result of blunt errors and the analysis proves fruitless, students are allowed to use data from another source with instructor permission.

***Collaboration*** on prelab quizzes, or copying prelab quiz results from another student, the internet or an existing reference are ***considered an academic integrity violation*** and will result in zero points for the quiz.

***Taking digital images of the quizzes*** with smartphones/notepads/cameras and supplying the digital images to other students or ***using the digital images of prelab quizzes taken by other students*** are considered as copying, ***are treated as an academic integrity violation***, and will result in zero points for the quiz.
