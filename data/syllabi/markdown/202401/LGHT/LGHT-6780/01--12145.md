## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The graduate students at the LRC receive the best technical foundation for pursuing a career in lighting. In addition to technical skills professional success depends upon an appreciation of organizations within which a lighting career is pursued and how change in lighting technologies, practice, and processes can be driven. Therefore, it is the collective view of faculty that our students should receive some formal assistance in making the transition from the LRC to their chosen profession.

To this end, students will get an introduction to business concepts and gain hands-on experience working individually and in teams.

The course is divided into three segments:  
*Introduction to business concepts*

- Students are asked to read about leadership, alignment, organization theory and systems, entrepreneurship, communication, innovation, emotional intelligence, teams, among other topics. In-class discussion and lectures will be the basis for this segment.

*Developing a business roadmap*

- Students should learn how to provide technical support for a new business venture. This section will cover methodology, tools, and communication needed to develop a business plan.

*Execution and deployment (class project)*

- Students, working as a team, will conduct a case study to practice concepts such as alignment, performance & goal setting and measurement, as well as the relevance of leadership, coaching, rewards and feedback on team performance.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The course objective is to give students some formal assistance in making the transition from the LRC to the lighting industry. To this end, students receive an introduction to business concepts and interact with LRC and RPI faculty and staff at a different level than before. Students also work in teams to develop case studies and business plans related to lighting products, services, or organizations to apply the concepts of the course and develop in-depth, practical analyses. At the completion of this course, it is expected that students will be able to:

- Understand important business concepts related to entrepreneurship, marketing, technology management, organization theory, and business strategy and use this knowledge to advance in their own careers
- Understand important attributes of leadership and be able to form their own personal leadership model
- Work effectively in teams to accomplish a stated goal or objective
- Conduct a comprehensive case study of a business or other organization including an assessment of marketing, business, and financial aspects of the organization
- Be able to communicate ideas clearly and effectively to a group or team

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

If there is reason to believe a student in this course may have been involved in academic dishonesty, the instructor will contact the student(s) and schedule a meeting to discuss the allegations. When meeting with the student, the instructor will review the circumstances and evidence related to the suspicion of academic dishonesty and allow him or her the opportunity to provide his or her perspective on the situation. If it is concluded that the student has violated the Institute academic dishonesty policy, the instructor will determine the appropriate academic (grade) penalty and will communicate this decision to the student in writing. In this course, penalties for academic dishonesty are as follows:

- For a first, minor violation – The student will be given a warning and reminded of the types of activities that are considered a violation of academic integrity.
- For a second offence – The student will be given a grade of “F” or zero on the assignment in question.
- For a third offence – The student will be given a failing grade for the course.

In cases where students are found to be in violation of the academic integrity policy, the written decision of the instructor will be copied to the Senior Judicial Administrator in the Dean of Students Office, along with a brief summary of the case facts and a copy of any supporting documentation (i.e., exams/assignments involving cheating, crib sheets, witness statements, etc.). A copy of the incident of academic dishonesty will be kept on file in the Dean of Students Office.
