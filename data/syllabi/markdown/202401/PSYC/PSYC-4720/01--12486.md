### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

**Course Description and Objectives:**

## The definition, history, major schools of thought, and models of the normal and abnormal personality are presented. Disorders are examined within the framework of D.S.M. and competing schools of thought. The description, etiology, treatment, including pharmacologic, and prevention of each of the disorders are considered. Illustrative cases are presented. Students write a paper on a topic, approved by the instructor, that focuses upon the impact of public policies on psychopathology.

## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Throughout the semester, you will be asked to: (1) identify, evaluate, and synthesize psychological research relevant to Abnormal Psychology; (2) develop hypotheses and arguments; (3) present your viewpoints to the rest of the class in the form of discussions, debates, and presentations; and (4) present your work in writing. You will gain familiarity with presenting your work in a format that is consistent with current standards in psychology, including the use of APA style. You will receive detailed feedback on your work.**

**Information Literacy Key Features**

This class involves readings from the primary research literature. We will use databases such as APAPsycArticles to conduct literature searches to obtain relevant literature. Topics such as the following will be discussed:

- The publishing process, including peer review
- Distinguishing primary research literature from secondary sources
- The meaning of a scientific journal – print and electronic characteristics
- Major databases for use in psychology
- Citation management software and conformity to APA style
- Ethics in conducting and publishing research

**Oral Discourse Key Features**

Group work and formal presentations are an important part of this course. You will learn to:

- Communicate ideas clearly in a frame of reference of the psychological sciences
- Present rationale for ideas and defend viewpoints
- Receive and provide feedback regarding the oral presentation of ideas

**Advanced Writing Key Features**

Writing assignments will emphasize:

- American Psychological Association (APA) style and rules
- Expression of concepts and methods in the psychological sciences
- Incorporation of critical feedback in writing and developing an understanding of the revision and rewriting process
- Articulation of strengths and weaknesses of written work
- Contrasting scientific writing with persuasive writing

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**COURSE OBJECTIVES:**

I love teaching this course, and my intentions are:

(a) To provide you with an overview of the field of abnormal psychology and major psychological problems;

(b) To familiarize you with the causes of psychopathology from several different theoretical perspectives;

(c) To illustrate an integrative view of human suffering and related research about abnormal behavior;

(d) To educate you about new trends and treatment strategies for psychological disorders

(e) And most importantly, to give you a perspective on human suffering that may help you live well when life is hard.

## Course Content

Lumen Learning Abnormal Psychology

Module 1: Understanding Abnormal Behavior

1.0 Module 1: Examine and Classify Abnormal Behavior

1.1 Define psychological disorders and explain how they are classified

1.1.1 Describe how psychological disorders are defined, as well as the inherent difficulties in doing so

1.1.2 Describe etiological theories (supernatural, somatogenic, and psychogenic) used to explain mental illness up through the Middle Ages

1.1.3 Explain modern views on mental illness, including the rationale and impact of deinstitutionalization

1.1.4 Describe the ways in which mental health services are delivered today, including the distinction between voluntary and involuntary treatment

1.2 Describe how mental disorders are identified and classified

1.2.1 Describe the basic features of the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5) and how it is used to classify disorders

1.2.2 Outline the major disorder categories of the DSM-5

1.2.3 Explain how the International Classification of Diseases is used to classify mental disorders

1.2.4 Discuss arguments and objections surrounding the DSM classification system

1.2.5 Describe problems associated with classification and labeling

1.2.6 Describe types of stigma associated with mental disorders and ways to challenge these stereotypes

1.3 Describe and discuss various psychological assessment techniques

1.3.1 Explain the steps and people involved in the diagnostic process, including the case formation

1.3.2 Describe the types and purposes of psychological assessment

1.3.3 Describe methods for clinical interviews and the mental status examination

1.3.4 Explain the purposes and types of intelligence testing (including the Wechsler and Stanford-Binet intelligence tests and the Kaufman Assessment Battery for Children)

1.3.5 Describe personality testing, including self-report inventories and projective personality tests

1.3.6 Describe how cognitive and behavioral assessments are used to inform psychotherapy

Module 2: Research and Ethics in Abnormal Psychology

2.0 Examine Research Methods in Abnormal Psychology

2.1 Examine how descriptive, correlational, and experimental research is used to study abnormal behavior

2.1.1 Explain how scientific research and the scientific method are used to address questions about behavior.

2.1.2 Explain the importance and uses of descriptive research, especially case studies, in studying abnormal behavior

2.1.3 Describe how archival, longitudinal, cross-sectional, and epidemiological research are valuable to abnormal psychology

2.1.4 Differentiate between prevalence and incidence

2.1.5 Explain correlational research, including what a correlation coefficient tells us about the relationship between variables

2.1.6 Describe the experimental process and its importance to abnormal psychology

2.1.7 Explain how reliability and validity are important in understanding research findings

2.2 Describe ethical standards and concerns in abnormal psychology

2.2.1 Explain how research involving humans or animals is regulated

2.2.2 Explain the main ethical principles as presented in the APA's Ethical Principles of Psychologists and Code of Conduct

2.2.3 Describe ethical standards and expectations for psychologists including competence, informed consent, confidentiality, relationships with clients and others, and record-keeping

2.2.4 Explain forensic issues in psychological treatment, such as the insanity defense, competency to stand trial, and the purpose of punishment

Module 3: Etiology and Treatment of Mental Disorders

3.0 Examine psychological models for understanding and treating mental disorders

3.1 Describe how multidimensional psychological models and perspectives aid in understanding the etiology of mental disorders

3.1.1 Explain why unidimensional explanations of mental disorders are insufficient

3.1.2 Explain the interaction between biological, psychological, and social factors in the development of psychopathology

3.1.3 Describe how the diathesis-stress model and gene-environment correlation models explain the etiology of mental disorders

3.1.4 Explain polygenic inheritance and genetic heritability in mental disorders

3.1.5 Explain neural communication and the role of neurotransmitters in neural transmission

3.1.6 Describe the functions of different major areas of the brain and how they are involved in mental disorders

3.1.7 Explain different forms of biomedical therapies and brain stimulation treatments

3.2 Describe the key concepts and treatment methods connected to the understanding of mental illness from psychodynamic and humanistic approaches

3.2.1 Describe Freud's concept of the unconscious and explain how the three basic elements of personality interact

3.2.2 Explain the psychoanalytic concepts of psychosexual development and defense mechanisms

3.2.3 Describe psychoanalysis and psychodynamic treatment methods

3.2.4 Describe the key concepts and applications of the humanistic approach to psychopathology

3.3 Explain the key principles of behaviorism, cognitive psychology, and how cognitive and behavioral methods are used together

3.3.1 Describe the key concepts and applications of the behavioral approach to psychopathology

3.3.2 Explain how behavioral principles such as exposure and systematic desensitization and others are used in treating psychological disorders

3.3.3 Describe the key concepts and applications of the cognitive approach to psychopathology, including rational-emotive-behavioral therapy and cognitive-behavioral therapy

3.3.4 Explain how cognitive-behavioral therapy is used to treat mental disorders

3.3.5 Describe major conclusions about the common factors that produce effective results with psychotherapy

3.3.6 Give examples of several relatively new or emerging forms of psychotherapy

3.4 Explain different types of treatments modalities used in treating psychopathology and the importance of cultural awareness and competence in working with clients from different backgrounds

3.4.1 Describe the benefits of different types of treatment modalities and how they are used to address specific problems and populations

3.4.2 Describe the impact of culture on key aspects of treatment availability and access, and explain the importance of cultural competence to ethical and effective clinical practice

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grades are based on the satisfactory completion of the following requirements:

- **Regular attendance and active** participation (10% of grade). Regular attendance is mandatory since much of the course is based on group discussion and in-class activities that cannot easily be made up. Three points will be deducted from your grade for each unexcused absence you miss beyond your second absence.
- **Module quizzes** (15% of grade)
- **Creative Class Discussion** (50 % of grade). To promote critical engagement with the readings, each student is required to lead a class discussion on one section of the required readings or one of the films once during the semester. Students will work in small group teams to develop material to lead their class discussion. Discussions should last approximately one hour to an hour and 15 minutes and should be geared towards promoting enriching dialogue focused on a synthesis of reading material assigned for the week. Please note that these discussions are designed to be creative. Students should work together to formulate engaging material and questions that inspire thought and fruitful discussion. Examples of creative material may include but is not limited to games, role plays, video clips, songs, spoken word, YouTube videos etc. GET CREATIVE! I’m excited to see what you all come up with. You are required to upload an outline of your discussion presentation the day before the class in which you present.
- **Final Project** ( 25% of grade) The description is at the end of this syllabus

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
