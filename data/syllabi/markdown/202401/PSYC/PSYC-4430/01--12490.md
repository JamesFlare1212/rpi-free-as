## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Throughout the day we tend to spend much of our time thinking about the past and the future. Mindfulness involves giving particular attention to our moment-to-moment experiences in a way that emphasizes observing, rather than judging or evaluating. Everyday activities such as driving a car or eating a meal provide us the opportunity to practice this focused, moment-to-moment attention. In addition, we can practice mindfulness through formal meditation. There is empirical evidence of the many health benefits of mindfulness meditation, including the potential to produce fundamental changes in brain structure. In this class, we will discuss the psychological foundations of mindfulness and empirical findings supporting its positive effects. Students will participate in structured exercises. This course is a very hands-on approach to mindfulness…you will be required to practice mindfulness and meditation and develop your mindful skills. It is also more of a discussion course, so be prepared to talk in class.

***Note about Communication Intensive Course***: This is a communication intensive course. Therefore, you need to be prepared to communicate in class frequently.

We will be employing the Koru Mindfulness program. This is a program specifically designed for college students. I am merging this program into our Psychology of Mindfulness course.

*The Journey: Big Panda and Tiny Dragon.*

Listening to the bamboo is a way to clear our minds of the thing we are worried about so we can think of a way to move forward.

*Please Take Note…*

It is likely that at some point a topic may arise that makes someone uncomfortable. If someone in class voices their discomfort, please respect their wish to move on to another topic. Know that I may not always be able to move on right away though. I want to make sure that I have discussed ways to work through the challenge. Having said that, it is also likely that topics arise in class that are important to talk about so that we learn how to work through challenges in life. For example, I will be talking about my experience with cancer. Talking about cancer may make some students uncomfortable. Talking about sensitive topics such as cancer is important so that we can learn how to think about those challenges and work through them. The first semester I shared with the students that I was a breast cancer survivor, a student came up after class and said, “Prof. Traver, I’m a cancer survivor too, and I want to learn how to think like you.” We both cried and hugged and laughed.

Bottom line: yes, we may discuss things that some may find uncomfortable. If we don’t discuss them, we are avoiding those things. When we avoid things, we don’t develop the skills to handle the emotions, and the situation could escalate. This class is to help prepare you for crises and challenges in life, so that you can deal with them, or even recover more quickly from them. With that being said, if a topic or discussion or question in class is too much for you at this point in time, step away and come back when you are able.

***One More Thing*** The very last day to submit any assignment (even a late assignment) will be April 5th. Work submitted after April 5th will not be graded.
