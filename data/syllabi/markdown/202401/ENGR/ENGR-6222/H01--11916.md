## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is focused on advances in Machine Learning, Machine Intelligence and Artificial Intelligence for the purpose of data-driven predictions, decision-making and business solutions across engineering and commercial use cases. Not a general management course; focuses explicitly on what is relevant for practitioners and managers of practitioners, engineers and scientists working in a commercial environment. For junior/senior undergraduate or graduate students.

This course is designed to be delivered in a blended format where approximately 90% of the course is delivered via the learning management system, and synchronous components make up approximately 10-20% of the course. Students are strongly encouraged to be present during synchronous sessions.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Evaluate the opportunity for using data in combination with machine learning and artificial intelligence to add value across a broad range of use cases
- Develop machine learning solutions for data-driven problem solving
- Calculate the accuracy of machine-generated predictions and classifications
- Evaluate the competitive and strategic position of the organization with respect to the use of data
- Recommend specific strategies to improve the organization’s performance using data
- Propose strategies for new opportunities, products and services using data and machine learning strategies to fulfill a market or corporate need.
- Evaluate data, machine learning products and solutions that may have potential for driving new value to their organizations
- Articulate how specific initiatives of the organization using data with machine learning or machine intelligence techniques may influence financial and strategic performance
- Interpret predictions and classifications from ML/MI systems.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

### Academic Integrity

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Dean of Students office website defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs). and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. Of course, learning with and collaborating with others in the class is encouraged – work submitted must be completely your own and original. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy may result in a penalty of failure of the assignment or of the course, depending on the severity. A grade of zero will be given on the first assignment where a violation of the policies described above is detected, and a referral to the Dean of Graduate Education. If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of “F” for the course and referral to the Dean. If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
