## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

An integrated development of modeling-and problem-solving techniques for particles and rigid bodies emphasizing the use of free-body diagrams, vector algebra, and computer simulation. Topics covered include the kinematics and kinetics of translational, rotational, and general plane motion, energy and momentum methods.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Kasdin, N. J., & Paley, D. A. (2011). *Engineering Dynamics: A Comprehensive Introduction*. Princeton University Press.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Analyze a diverse set of problems in particle and rigid body dynamics using 3D vector algebra.
- Apply the principles of Newtonian dynamics, momentum, and energy considerations, change of coordinates, and frame of reference.
- Derive mathematical equations that describe the behavior of particle and rigid body systems.
- Solve equations of motion or simulate them in Python and clearly present the results.
- Physically interpret solutions of equations of motion and sanity-check the results and their units.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Homework: At least eleven homework sets will be assigned on LMS. The homework is to be submitted to the TA in person **before class begins** or by email. No late homework assignment will be accepted. The maximum grade for each homework will be 10 points.

Makeup exams may be written or oral, up to the discretion of the instructor. If there is an exam conflict with another course, the student must submit a request to take a makeup exam no later than 1 week prior to the final examination (note that only 1000 level courses have priority over this course)..

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Final (transcript) letter grade will be assigned based on the *curved numerical grade*.

The *curved numerical grade* will be **at least as high as** the *raw numerical grade*. The raw numerical grade may be curved up **if necessary**. Students who do not take all the exams will not be included in the course curve calculation.

The *raw numerical grade* is the minimum of the sum of all homework grades and the sum of all exam grades:

100% exams grade, if exams grade is lower than homework grade

100% homework grade, if homework grade is lower than exams grade
