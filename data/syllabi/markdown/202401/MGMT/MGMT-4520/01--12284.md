## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The purpose of this course it to give students an in depth, conceptual understanding of entrepreneurship and the entrepreneurial process. Entrepreneurship fundamentally differs from traditional strategic planning processes employed in large established corporations. This course gives you a flavor of what it is like to start your own business and the multitude of challenges you will face, especially at early stages, as you nurture and scale it into a full-fledged company.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The course’s objective is to equip you with tools and frameworks that will enable you to make better decisions as an entrepreneur. We will be dealing with aspects such as:

- What is entrepreneurship?
- The entrepreneurial mindset and way of thinking
- Understanding what makes for a good and viable opportunity
- The role of business plans
- The Lean Startup approach
- The role of business models
- Issues involved in raising capital
- Marketing challenges in entrepreneurial ventures
- Challenges in scaling up a venture
- Harvesting the venture, and exit

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

You will be required to attend classes regularly, and to read the materials assigned for each class (Note-from time to time, ***additional readings will be assigned*** depending upon the subjects we cover). Based on your reading and in class discussions, you are then expected to make thoughtful and constructive contributions. Students may be called upon randomly to answer questions in class to ensure uniform participation. In addition I may also give you brief polls or in class exercises which you are expected to complete, and which will count towards your participation grade.

An attendance sheet will be circulated at the beginning of every class. It is your responsibility to make sure that you record your attendance. You are allowed a maximum of **two absences** (unexcused without documentation). Any additional absences will require clear and supporting documentation (e.g. a doctor’s note) giving reasons for why you should be excused from class. In general, unless circumstances are exigent, I would expect your total absences to be minimal. **Unexcused absences and excessive excused absences will reduce your class participation grade.** Three unexcused absences will reduce your class grade one-half a full letter (ex. B to a B-), after which two percentage points will be deducted for every additional absence.

Note: Attendance alone will not count toward your class participation grade, and the two will be graded separately.

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in (1) an academic (grade) penalty and (2) reporting to Lally’s Associate Dean of Academic Affairs and either the Dean of Students (for Undergraduates) or the Dean of Graduate Education (for Graduate students).

**In this course, the academic penalty for a first offense is 30% of the overall course grade*.* A second offense will result in failure of the course as noted in Lally’s Three Strikes Policy.** If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

**Chat GPT/ LLM Policy**

You are encouraged to use AI tools such as ChatGPT to supplement your learning in class. Chat GPT and other LLMs can be a powerful tool for researching entrepreneurial opportunities, industries, and technologies. However they can also be wrong, and in many instances they can be superficial and not in depth enough. Hence you should critically examine and question whatever information they produce.

If you are using ChatGPT or another LLM, it is your responsibility to cross check the information and ensure it is correct and sufficiently detailed to address the class assignment. LLMs may provide a useful starting point and a first draft, but you will invariably need to conduct additional detailed research to meet class requirements.

If you use an LLM such as ChatGPT, please provide a short paragraph at the end of the assignment stating how you used it, along with the prompts you used. Not doing so is a violation of academic integrity policies, and would count in the minimum as one strike.

However note that you are *explicitly prohibited* from using Chat GPT or any other LLM to solve case questions or obtain information pertaining to the cases assigned in class. Doing so, again, constitutes a violation of academy integrity policies, and would count in the minimum as a strike.

**Guidelines for Written Reports**

Written reports should be prepared in a professional manner, with attention to both content as well as stylistic aspects. Please submit type written reports, one sided, on 8 1/2 x 11- inch paper, with 1-inch margins all around. The report should be double spaced, and the font size should be 12. Your report should use headings/ sub headings to reflect what you are discussing in each section, and to guide the reader throughout.

All reports must have a cover page mentioning your name /your group members’ names and group number. Please include page numbers while adhering strictly to the specified page limits. ***E-mail me your report 10 mins. ahead of class time the day your case is to be presented/ your assignment is due***.

**Group Assignments and Peer Review**

50% of your grade is determined by group case assignments. At the end of the semester, your group members will be asked for input on whether your contribution to group activities was satisfactory or unsatisfactory. This system is designed to identify any ‘free riders’ in the group. If two or more group members rate your contribution as unsatisfactory, you will be awarded a substantially lower grade (50% or lower) on all group work. It is therefore essential that you do your fair share of group work.

Note- If any group member does not contribute satisfactorily to group activities, please let me know asap, preferably before the 1st deliverable for the VCP project. Additionally I may award a particular group member a lower grade if I have reason to believe that group member has not contributed adequately to a project (for instance if the group member is unable to answer questions on a group project satisfactorily).
