## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

# 1 COURSE DESCRIPTION:

This undergraduate course is an introduction to ethical and legal frameworks currently being applied in the business environment in the United States. The course looks to engage with moral, and legal challenges that businesses currently face and may face in the future, such as maintaining a safe workplace, respecting employee rights and responsibilities, addressing threats to data privacy and security, issues pertaining to climate justice and environmental sustainability, and issues arising due to employee surveillance mediated through digitization among others. In addition, students will have the opportunity to conduct a project to understand how financial considerations of companies and ethical decisions may intersect, and how to balance these considerations.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Understand the role of ethical and legal landscape within which businesses exist.
- 2\. Develop an understanding of the effects of law, government, society, technology and ethics on business creation and operation.
- 3\. Practice how to identify and resolve ethical tradeoffs in professional, and business investment decision making contexts.
- 4\. Developing oral, written and presentation communication skills via written assignments and group projects.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- There will be 6 quizzes in total. The quiz scores will be averaged together with the lowest score dropped. Quizzes will take place in person during regular class time and will last between 20-30 minutes.
- There will be short reflection exercises due before most lectures to help you reflect on the readings for the lecture and prepare you for in-class discussion. The reflection exercise will contain a few short answer-type questions to gauge your comprehension of the readings. A lot of the readings in this course will have moral and philosophical implications and simply reading them will not be sufficient to understand the nuances contained within them. I suggest reading the text well in time before lecture so you have time to reflect on the readings.
- There will be a final exam which will include multiple-choice questions and short-answer type questions, along with some case-based questions.
- There will be a course project where you must make investment decisions on behalf of a company and make the case to your shareholders for investing in these companies.
- **Course Project Details:**
- This semester we will use the Impact Investing Project as our way of practicing: 1) articulating values to different stakeholder groups, 2) defending those values in analytically rigorous and compelling ways, and 3) integrating those values into ongoing business decisions. As we will discuss at length in class, “impact investing” is a type of socially responsible finance that seeks to find investible opportunities that advance a social or environmental value.

- The project will have two stages, each stage requiring an in-class presentation by each of the groups. The two stages are as follows:

1. Articulation of your fund’s values and selection criteria
2. Pitch of two portfolio companies

### Other Course Policies:

# COURSE POLICIES

The structure of this course will be a mixture of lecture, in-class group work, and discussion. I expect you to arrive in class having carefully read that day’s readings and prepared to engage critically with them.

You are expected to treat your classmates and their opinions with courtesy. Failure to do this will result in a penalty in your participation grade.

**Electronic devices are not permitted in class**. This is to create an environment conducive to learning. While phones and upright laptops are not allowed, tablets flat on the table will be permitted *for note taking only*. If you are curious to read more about the scientific case for this policy, you may do so here: https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/students-are-better-off-without-a-laptop-in-the-classroom/

# ACADEMIC INTEGRITY

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities define various forms of Academic Dishonesty, and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work, including exams. **In this course, the academic penalty for a first offense will result in a penalty of a failing grade for that exam or paper (subject to review of circumstances) and will be reported to the Associate Dean for Academic Affairs. A second offense will result in failure of the course as noted in Lally’s Three Strikes Policy.** Be aware that it is your responsibility for the security of your own work.

If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

**AI POLICY**

Use of AI tools, including ChatGPT, is permitted in this course for specific assignments.

Use of AI tools is prohibited when attempting exams/quizzes. My recommendation is to use AI to extract information and generate ideas. To adhere to our scholarly values, students must cite any AI-generated material that informed their work (this includes in-text citations and/or use of quotations, and in your reference list; using different color font to highlight text generated by AI). Where applicable, you must also verify the content generated by AI is true. Do not assume that AI tools are providing factually correct information. Finally, using an AI tool to generate content without proper attribution qualifies as academic dishonesty.
