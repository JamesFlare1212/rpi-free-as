## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

We live and work in a true digital economy, fueled by advances in social, mobile, analytics, cloud, and IoT technologies. The Internet and associated technological innovations have helped to restructure the global economy into a networked economy, characterized by unprecedented levels of electronically mediated communication, collaboration, and commerce. It is not surprising, therefore, that the IT sector is now the single largest sector in the S&P 500.

As a forward-thinking manager and executive of tomorrow’s organizations, it is essential that you develop an understanding of cutting-edge technological trends and their potential organizational impact. Managers cannot be effective without knowing the role of IT and its impacts in terms of transformations on the organizational, process, market, and global enterprise levels. Insightful executives of leading companies across the world are increasingly leveraging IT to create value and obtain a competitive advantage.

This course brings together technical and managerial perspectives to discuss key issues involving IT. Understanding of technology-enabled changes in contemporary business environments, and how insightful executives leverage IT are key to creating value. The course will explore the business drivers of technology-related decisions in organizations, and stimulate ideas for new applications of technology in commerce, including new products, processes, and business models. Some of the topics include:

- Understanding how different business models necessitate different investment in IT.
- Exploring the dynamics of platform models that emphasize network effects.
- Understanding how IT coupled with Big Data and Analytics impacts different industries, including entertainment industry as well as bricks and mortar firms.
- Learning how IT can hasten and aid the growth of disruptive innovation upstarts, as well as how it can spell the demise of other incumbents.
- Analyzing how companies can use the wisdom of the crowd for innovation.
- Understanding how blockchain technologies can be leveraged to improve the organization’s supply chain.
- Learning about the possibilities and limitations of generative AI, and how this disruptive technology can be harnessed to create value.

Conceptually, this course is designed to make you knowledgeable of the fundamentals underlying the design, implementation, control, evaluation, and strategic use of modern information systems. While some of the effort will be devoted to hands-on work with some business software, the major emphasis will be on the understanding the managerial and strategic aspects of information technology. The course will be useful for managers, consultants, technology entrepreneurs, venture capitalists, and active investors, or anyone who is looking to lead an organization in the digital economy.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Terminology: You will be able to explain the meaning of terms used to describe common techniques and concepts in business information systems.
- Advanced Information Systems Concepts: You will be able to describe the ways in which computers are and will be used in business and management. These concepts include telecommunications, electronic commerce, business analytics, and future directions of information systems.
- Business Strategy and Systems Development: You will be involved in a small-scale IT project and experience how it is to work in a team setting. You will not need to do programming in this course but will evaluate a business lifecycle for your proposed product.
- Behavioral and Organizational Issues: You will be able to identify and suggest appropriate responses to managerial and organizational issues stemming from development, implementation, and use of computer-based information systems.
- Social and Ethical Issues: You will understand the major social and ethical issues involved in the development and use of information technology.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students' understanding and ability to explain terminology, information systems concepts, behavioral and organizational issues, international issues, and social and ethical issues will be measured through their exam responses and in the quality of their answers to my questions in class and in the quantity and quality of their participation in class discussion and activities.

Students' skill of building and manipulating an IT solution will be measured in the quality of the group project work and the individual assignments that they will submit. The quality of their contribution will be measured both by me, self-evaluation, and the other group mates in the project group. The average evaluation of the peers may affect project grade. This will ensure an objective measurement of students' work in this team effort.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

I expect students to attend all classes, to arrive on time and stay till the end of class. If they miss a class, it is their responsibility to obtain notes from a classmate. I follow RPI’s attendance policy; if students miss any class, especially that which had a pop quiz, any excused absences need to be processed via the Student Experience Office (SEO) (4th floor, Academic Hall, x8022, se@rpi.edu). In the spirit of fairness to the student's peers, I do not offer make-up quizzes for excuses that are not acceptable to the SEO.

### Other Course Policies:

Office hours are **not** meant for individual tutoring (for classes missed).

Students must follow, without exceptions, ***all*** institute safety and health protocols throughout the semester.

**Classroom conduct**: Students should avoid private conversations during class.

**Mobile devices:** Students should ensure that their mobile device(s) are turned off during class. Use of electronic gadgets, unless required for a class activity, is distracting and will adversely affect their class participation and overall grade.

**Respect the comments of your peers:** One aspect of learning is the exchange of ideas. There will be disagreements. Student should make an effort to respect the opinions of your peers (even if you disagree with them) by listening actively. Students should present their arguments objectively by sticking with the relevant facts, using reason, and keeping a cool head. Students should focus on the topic, not the individual (i.e., depersonalize the issue). The purpose of the discussion is to attain a higher level of learning by examining diverse opinions, not to achieve consensus. Students should contribute to the conversation by raising questions or comments that will encourage learning, rather than engendering defensiveness or conflict.

**24-hour communication blackout:** I will not answer any questions 24 hours before the mid-term exam or the due date of the team term project. Students should manage their time and schedule accordingly.

**Extra credit:** I do **not** offer any extra credit assignments.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
