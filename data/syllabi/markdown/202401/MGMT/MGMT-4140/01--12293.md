## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Information technologies (IT) are changing the business world. Managers cannot be effective without knowing the role of IT and its impacts in terms of transformations on the organizational, process, market, and global enterprise levels. Insightful executives of leading organizations across the globe are increasingly leveraging advanced IT to create value and gain competitive advantage.

Because IT is usually composed of heavily invested organizational resources, decisions on whether to adopt certain IT, as well as appropriate management practices and utilization of IT often have important consequences for the organization and its members. This course teaches how IT affects management practices and transforms organizational and industry structures.

This course is designed to help you gain knowledge of the fundamentals underlying the design, implementation, control, evaluation, and strategic use of modern IT. The emphasis will be on the understanding the managerial and strategic aspects of IT.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Terminology: To be able to explain the meaning of terms used to describe common techniques and concepts in business information systems.
- Advanced Information Systems Concepts: To be able to describe the ways in which computers are and will be used in business and management. These concepts include telecommunications, electronic commerce, business analytics, and future directions of information systems.
- Business Strategy and Systems Development: To experience how it is to work in a team setting on a small-scale IT project. You will not need to do programming in this course but will evaluate a business lifecycle for your proposed product.
- Behavioral and Organizational Issues: To be able to identify and suggest appropriate responses to managerial and organizational issues stemming from development, implementation, and use of computer-based information systems.
- Social and Ethical Issues: To understand the major social and ethical issues involved in the development and use of various technologies.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students' understanding and ability to explain terminology, information systems concepts, behavioral and organizational issues, international issues, and social and ethical issues will be measured through their exam responses and the quality of their answers to my questions in class, as well as in the quantity and quality of participation in class discussion and group activities.

Students' skill of designing, building and manipulating an IT solution will be measured in the quality of the group project work and the individual assignments that they will submit. The quality of their contribution will be measured both by me, self-evaluation, and the other group mates in their project group. The average evaluation of their peers may affect project grade. This is to ensure an objective measurement of their work in this team effort.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

I expect students to (1) attend all classes, (2) arrive on time, and (3) stay till the end of class. If you miss a class, it is your responsibility to obtain notes from a classmate. I follow RPI’s attendance policy; if students miss any class, especially that which had a pop quiz, any excused absences need to be processed via the Student Experience Office (SEO) (4th floor, Academic Hall, x8022, se@rpi.edu). In the spirit of fairness to your peers, I do not offer make-up quizzes for excuses that are not acceptable to the SEO.

### Other Course Policies:

Students should note that office hours are **not** for individual tutoring (for classes missed).

Students must follow, without exceptions, ***all*** institute safety and health protocols throughout the semester.

**Mobile devices:** Students must ensure that their mobile device(s) are turned off during class. Use of electronic gadgets, unless required for a class activity, is distracting and will adversely affect class participation and overall grade.

**Classroom conduct**: Students are asked to avoid private conversations during class.

**Respect the comments of your peers:** One aspect of learning is the exchange of ideas. Students should make an effort to respect the opinions of their peers (even if they disagree with them) by listening actively. Students should present their arguments objectively by sticking with the relevant facts, using reason, and keeping a cool head. Students are advised to focus on the topic, not the individual (i.e., to depersonalize the issue). The purpose of the discussion is to attain a higher level of learning by examining diverse justified opinions, not to achieve consensus. Students should try to contribute to the conversation by raising questions or comments that will encourage learning, rather than engendering defensiveness or conflict.

**24-hour communication blackout:** I will not answer questions 24 hours before the mid-term exam or the due date of the team term project. Students need to manage their time and schedule accordingly.

**Extra credit:** I do **not** offer any extra credit assignments.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own.
