### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

This course emphasizes the basic principles of managerial functions and processes using an interdisciplinary approach to goal-oriented situations of private and public organizations.

The course is designed to introduce students to the key decisions and processes inherent in business. We will gain an overview of decision making in the management field through the lenses of strategy, marketing, finance, HR, technology management, entrepreneurship, among other disciplines. Additionally the course will touch upon important phenomena relevant to management, such as disruption and the implications of AI for business.

For business majors, the class will serve as an introduction to these topics such that you can be familiar with them as you work through your curriculum at Lally. For non business majors, the course will give you familiarity with these concepts so that you can use them and have a basic understanding as you go to work in organizations as engineers, scientists, architects, etc.

We will use readings and lectures to provide content. Additionally we will use cases and/or exercises extensively in order to help you *apply* the ideas. In some deliverables you’ll apply concepts to specific questions, but in others you’ll be tasked with making decisions and recommendations that would impact an entire business. That is one of the main objectives of this class: to give you experience with the complex, multi-faceted decision making that is done by organizational leaders. Additionally you will also build team, interpersonal, and communication skills through various assignments and projects.

### Attendance Policy:

You will be required to attend classes regularly, and to read the materials assigned for each class. Depending upon how the class progresses, additional articles may be assigned from time to time. Based on your reading, you are then expected to make thoughtful and constructive contributions to the discussions. Students may be called upon randomly to answer questions in class to ensure uniform participation.

An attendance sheet will be circulated at the beginning of every class. It is your responsibility to make sure that you record your attendance. You are allowed a maximum of **two absences** (unexcused without documentation). Any additional absences will require clear and supporting documentation (e.g. a doctor’s note) giving reasons for why you should be excused from class. In general, unless circumstances are exigent, I would expect your total absences to be minimal. **Unexcused absences and excessive excused absences will reduce your class participation grade.** Three unexcused absences will reduce your class grade one-half a full letter (ex. B to a B-), after which two percentage points will be deducted for every additional absence.

Note: Attendance alone will not count toward your class participation grade, and the two will be graded separately.

You are encouraged to use AI tools such as ChatGPT to supplement your learning in class. Chat GPT and other LLMs can be a powerful tool for researching industries, firms, and technologies. However they can also be wrong, and in many instances they can be superficial and not in depth enough. Hence you should critically examine and question whatever information they produce.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

**QUIZ I & II (15% EACH)**

Dates for the two quizzes are indicated in the schedule (February 23rd, April 16th). These quizzes will cover all topics discussed in class up to that point in time, including cases. An important objective of this course is to help you develop an ability to critically evaluate the material covered. Hence straightforward questions requiring a commitment to memory will not be on the quizzes.

**CASE ANALYSIS- REPORT AND PRESENTATION (GROUP; 12.5%)**

Case analyses are a highly effective tool to understand the challenges managers confront in their decision making. For the purpose of this assignment and other group work, students will be earmarked into groups of about 3-4 members. Each team will be responsible for preparing a written report and making a formal presentation of their analysis and recommendations. The enclosed schedule indicates the case and the date it is to be presented. Broadly speaking, your presentation will cover the background of the case, the issues identified (see the discussion questions for each case below on page 6), recommendations for the future, and any other points that you think are relevant.

Each team will prepare a report that is no longer than 12 pages, excluding the cover page (please see the guidelines for written reports below). In addition, each team will make a presentation in class for a maximum of 20-25 minutes (guidelines are also provided below for presentations). Note that the report is due on the day the case is scheduled to be presented. Late submissions will not be accepted. Please email your report and slides on the day the case is to be presented, ahead of class time.

Note- The cases discussed in class are designed to illustrate specific concepts and decision scenarios. Please therefore do not look up any online material or ‘solutions’ for the case, including ChatGPT or other LLMs. Any attempts to do so will be treated as a serious ethical violation, and in the minimum equivalent to a first offense (see below, and also see ChatGPT/LLM policy).

**WORKSHOP (GROUP; 7.5%)**

The workshop assignment requires you to develop an insightful presentation on an assigned topic (e.g. the role of culture in companies, and application of AI to business). You will be given brief guidelines ahead of the assignment, and your task is to develop a presentation of about 20 mins as a group. The purpose is to not only get you thinking about the topic, but also to inform the rest of the class and enhance their understanding of the subject. It is essential that you develop an in depth analysis and come well prepared to present on the day that the workshop assignment is scheduled, since you will be playing the lead role in these discussions. Please email your workshop slides on the day your workshop is scheduled, ahead of class time

**COMPANY ANALYSIS REPORT AND PRESENTATION (GROUP; 15% EACH )**

The company analysis report and presentation has two components: mid term and final. For the mid term, this assignment requires you to select an established company and analyze its scope and operations in detail. For the final, you will be selecting a younger company and analyzing its entrepreneurial growth journey to date. Details of the assignment and the issues to be addressed will be provided in class.
