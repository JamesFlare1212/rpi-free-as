## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a required first course for management majors and minors. It emphasizes broad, basic principles of managerial functions and processes using an interdisciplinary approach to goal-oriented situations of private and public organizations. The course is designed to introduce students to the key managerial functions and processes used in businesses. We will cover finance, accounting, marketing, operations, entrepreneurship, business analytics, and other general management concepts—as noted in the textbook we’re using. The class will serve as an introduction to these topics, such that you can be familiar with them as you work through your curriculum at Lally and move on to the workforce.

We will use readings and materials to provide content, but then will use cases and/or exercises extensively in order to help you *apply* the ideas. In some deliverables you’ll apply concepts to specific questions but in others you’ll be tasked with making decisions and recommendations that would impact an entire business. That is one of the main objectives of this class: to get you experience in the complex, multi-faceted decision making that is done by organizational leaders. This culminates in your final project, wherein you’ll analyze a business and present your findings and recommendations in a professional presentation.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Class Participation (10%)**

You’ll find that complex business decisions often don’t have a ‘correct’ or ‘best’ answer. In some areas, such as accounting, there is a correct way to do things, of course. However, much of what we discuss will require informed judgment, points of view, and differing opinions. This makes participation very important – the class is not just about me telling you what to think. Therefore, your contribution to discussions is necessary. Your participation grade is based on your “presence,” as opposed to mere attendance, and your contribution to discussions, both in the larger class and in your groups.

Note that you will be asked to complete confidential peer reviews of your team members at the end of term. Thus, your team members will be assessing you as well. Your grade for participation, and on your group assignments, *may* be impacted by your teammate’s evaluation of your group participation and contributions to group deliverables.

We will take attendance in each class—with a class that’s big it’s difficult, especially early on, to get an accurate sense of everyone’s presence. So, the attendance sheet serves as a hardcopy portion of the final “class participation” grade. If you are going to miss class, please notify me in advance by email—this will help as I plan in-class activities around the content for class. An absence does not exempt you from the assignment due for that day. Excessive absences will absolutely reduce your class participation grade. There are 23 class meeting times throughout the entire semester (not including exam days). You start missing a few classes and that adds up in terms of percentage of class time missed. If you are struggling –with anything– that’s impacting your attendance and performance, please let me know and we can talk it through and find solutions.

Note: If you find yourself struggling with your mental or physical health this semester, please feel free to approach me. You may also contact the Troy Student Health Center (518-276-6287) which provides a variety of services for full-time/degree-seeking undergraduate and graduate students. The Counseling Center is also available to help students maximize their sense of well-being as well as their academic, personal, and social growth. Appointments are free and confidential, and in-person at the Counseling Center, located 4th Floor of Academy Hall. Appointments can be made by calling 518-276-6479 or email counseling@rpi.edu.

**Quizzes & Homework in Revel (25%)**

This course is designed to have you read the chapters and other materials prior to the class that those materials will be discussed. As such, you will be required to complete a chapter quiz and some other short homework assignments in the Pearson’s Revel Learning tool. These assignments, unless otherwise noted in class and on the LMS, are expected to be completed by 9:59 am on the day of the class they will be discussed. So, if the Course Schedule states that students are to Read Chapter 1 and complete the Revel assignments for the Friday of Week #1 (1/12), then the student will read that chapter and complete all of the Revel assignments by 9:59 am on 1/12. That will ensure that everyone is more familiar with the content we’ll be covering. The style of this course is “Socratic”—namely, the instructor serves as a facilitator for discussing the content for the day. Very rarely will classes involve traditional lectures with the Professor droning on and on about a topic. By completing the chapter quizzes and other assignments in Revel, we’ll be sure to have a healthy and fruitful dialogue each class.

**Individual Assignments Outside Revel (10%)**

There will be some assignments given throughout the semester that are not found in Revel. These assignments and expectations (i.e., grading rubrics) will be available on the LMS and discussed in class. The instructor retains the right to make these assignments throughout the semester. Just as it is working in an organization, not all assignments and deadlines are scheduled. It’s important to learn to manage “last minute” work. However, students will have at least one week to complete any of these types of short assignments. Late submissions will be penalized by 10% per week late.

**Section Exams: (25%) & Final Exam (10%)**

There will be five “mini-exams” throughout the semester covering each of the “Parts” noted in the textbook. They will primarily cover the topical content, (strategy, finance, accounting, marketing, operations, etc.) found in those Parts of our semester learning. It may reference any cases we also use during those Parts, but you will not be tested on detailed case knowledge. Each may consist of multiple-choice questions, True/False, fill in the blanks, and some calculations. There will also be a Final Exam that will cover the entire material covered in the course. A study guide for the final exam will be provided to students along with a review session. The goal for this course is that you leave with a set of skills and knowledge that can be carried with you in any job or career you might occupy. A final exam provides the incentive to remember, recall, and critically think about the topics covered.

**Group Company Project & Presentation (20%)**

This is a research project in which you and your team will analyze one company (from an approved list) in depth over the course of the semester. There will be one company chosen for this early in the term and all teams will analyze separate companies. The final presentation includes supporting documentation which illustrates the research, planning, and preparation done by your team. The overall purpose of the assignment is for you to identify challenges the company is facing, come up with recommendations to improve the business, and provide your best assessment of where this company will be in five years. Your recommendations should be achievable, well thought out, and capable of having a substantial impact on the company.

The project should build off the entire course. Think of it in a number of phases: (1) assess the situation / market / company (using tools from readings/lectures/milestones #1) and identify the biggest issues your firm is facing. From your analysis, convince me that you know what is going on (2) generate ideas for recommendations; (3) select recommendations and present them, and (4) present your assessment of their five-year outlook. We can discuss your ideas in individual team meetings throughout the semester.

Your team will be graded on the quality of your analysis, your recommendations, the quality and professionalism of your presentation, your use of concepts from the class, and your ability to provide a recommendation(s) with an accompanying detailed implementation plan and respond to challenges during a question-and-answer session.

Each team should include references/citations (APA format), and a spreadsheet that includes financials that incorporate investment needed and illustrate the growth projections, such that they cover a number of years.

In addition, the team will submit their presentation as a deliverable. A final written paper is not needed, so be sure the presentation is clear and well written. Any additional research, pro-forma financials, data gathered, supporting materials, etc. can be submitted appendices as a part of the PPT/presentation.

Although this is a team project, the instructor reserves the right to allocate grades individually, based on each individual member’s contributions to the presentation and to the question-and-answer session.

Again, note that you will complete confidential peer reviews of your team members at the end of the term. Your grade on this group assignments *may* be impacted by your teammate’s evaluation of your contributions.
