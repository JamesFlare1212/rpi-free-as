## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

An introduction to quantum physics principles and the Schrodinger equation. Solutions to the time independent Schrodinger equation in one, two and three dimensions, including one electron atoms.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Course Topics:**

Why Quantum Physics ? – Compelling Experiments

Math

Superposition of waves

Complex numbers

Distribution functions

Quantum state functions

Properties of wavefunctions

The Schrodinger Equation

Free particle solutions

Space and momentum functions

Probability current

Wavepackets

Expectation values

Operators

The Time Independent Schrodinger Equation – special one-dimensional cases

Particle in a box

Eigenvalues and Eigenfunctions

Superposition

Finite square well

Delta function

Simple Harmonic Oscillator

Double square well

Travelling waves and the step potential

Barrier tunneling

TISE – Three dimensional cases

**Homework:** Solving problems and explaining your solutions are significant learning tools in Physics. You should plan on spending at least four hours per week on self-paced assigned homework, practice, and material review. Written assignments will be due on Fridays unless otherwise stated in the assignment. When feasible, I will also supply practice problems that will not be graded.

Late homework is accepted, but with a deduction of 20% for homework submitted after but within 24 hours of the due time, and an additional 10% for each weekday late thereafter up to a maximum deduction of 50%.

**Attendance Policy:** Quizzes and in-class activities form a significant part of the grade as described in the grading rubric. Please let me know if you are not able to attend class synchronously so we can make arrangements.

Quizzes will usually cover the topics and reading from the previous week’s homework and class activities, but may include earlier material if it is relevant. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students are encouraged to discuss solutions to homework problems with one another before the class due date, but all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. Do not copy someone else’s homework and do not cheat on exams. If submission of any assignment is in violation of this policy, then you will be charged with academic dishonesty, and given a formal letter of reprimand which will be copied to the Dean of Science, Dean of Students, your advisor and the Learning Center. You will receive a non-droppable zero for that work. If submission of any exam is in violation of this policy, then you will receive an ´F´ for the course and you will receive a written warning to be copied to the Dean of Science, the Dean of Students, your advisor, and the Learning Center and it will become part of your permanent record.

Some examples of cheating:

- Copying another student’s exam, quiz, homework, or software.
- Giving or receiving help on exams or quizzes to or from anyone other than the instructor or proctor.

Some examples of “not cheating”:

- Discussing solution approaches and collaborating on homework and computational problems. (I strongly encourage you to collaborate and may even steer you into groups.)
- Collaborating on in-class activities.
