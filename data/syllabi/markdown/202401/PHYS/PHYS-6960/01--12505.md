## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will enable students to gather information about stars, the Milky Way galaxy in particular, and galaxies in general. After taking this class, students will be able to describe the structure, content, formation, and dynamics of galaxies; will be able to derive simple formulae that describe galaxies and use them to make estimates. Students will master the computational foundation that will allow them to make more complex calculations of the interactions of stars and gas in galaxies. ASTR 4960 is an upper level undergraduate course. PHYS 6960 is a graduate level class.

Course Themes: The structure and stellar content of galaxies, dynamics of stars in the Milky Way, stellar and Galactic evolution

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

**Required Text:** Galaxies in the Universe, by Sparke and Gallagher, second edition

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to describe the Milky Way galaxy and the matter of which it is composed.
- Students will be able to identify stellar types in spectra and photometry, and calculate the effects of reddening from dust.
- Students will be able to describe the stellar populations in the Milky Way, including the stellar densities and luminosity functions.
- Students will be able to calculate parallaxes and proper motion, create and interpret reduced proper motion diagrams.
- Students will be able to make simple calculation of the dynamics of stars in the Milky Way, including orbits, spiral density waves, bars/bulges, tidal disruption and virialization.
- Students will be able to describe the chemodynamical evolution of baryonic matter in the Milky Way.
- For PHYS 6960: Students will learn the rudiments of teaching astronomy

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

Homework will be due every two weeks, six assignments in total. The dates that homework is due are listed in the syllabus below. Students are encouraged to discuss homework assignments, but the work that is turned in must be the student’s original work. Submitted homework must clearly show the steps leading up to each answer. If you feel that you have received a grade in error, see the graduate TA first. The instructor will resolve grading disagreements only after an attempt has been made to resolve the issue with the TA.

The exams will test the students’ mastery of the homework and important derivations in the lectures and textbook. The goal is to ask typical Galactic structure questions that might be asked on a qualifying exam in astronomy graduate school. Students will be allowed to use a calculator (nothing in the memory), but no notes or books. Sample questions will be distributed ahead of the exams to facilitate studying. Exam questions will be selected from the list of sample questions. Grading questions on exams should be resolved with the instructor.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students who cheat will receive a failing grade for the course. Cheating includes: copying, or allowing another student to copy, a test or assignment; altering a graded test or assignment before requesting re-grading; copying submitted solutions from published solution manuals or previous years; using materials other than those that are allowed on an exam; misrepresenting the work of another group or individual as your own. It is considered cheating to help another student cheat. Because of the online format, it is unusually easy to cheat on exams. My expectation is that you will not consult each other or unallowed sources even though it might be easy for you to do just that.

The environment in which students operate has many sources for solutions to homework and projects, including AI chatbots, online information, Chegg, student-organized archives, classmates, and other people. It is okay for to consult any of these sources for ideas, information, and to check your work. However, the work you actually turn in must be 100% your own. You must show how and why each step in the homework solution is done, and you must be writing them from your head and not by copying from somewhere else. A good test for you to determine whether you are following the course rules is to ask yourself whether your actions promote learning, whether you are learning the information and skills that the course is designed to teach you, and whether everything that you turn in is your own work.
