## Course Goals

### Goals:

- Present students with state-of-the-art knowledge in the theory and practice of solving Network Optimization problems
- Build students' to perform rigorous analysis of network optimization algorithms
- Develop students' abilities and intuitions for algorithm development and analysis for network problems

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Core fundamentals of proof techniques, runtime analysis, and algorithm intution/design for network problems are present in every module of the course. Modules may fluctuate based on student interests in network problems. Typically topics will be:  
Network Preliminaries

Intro to Runtime Analysis

Topological Sorting and Search Algorithms

Path Finding, Including Heuristic Based Extensions (A*)

Max Flow and Min Cut

Minimum Spanning Trees

Approximation Algorithms (Constructions, Insertions, Improvements)

MetaHeuristics

Matchings

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Develop mathematical language skills necessary to read and write algorithms and algorithm analysis.
- Rigorously analyze and prove correctness for (network) algorithms.
- Develop intuition for algorithm development and algorithm analysis.
- Modify and implement heuristic techniques for new network problems.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Regular assignments (biweekly) and two exams will be used to monitor student progress on learning outcomes.

I care that every student has the opportunity to learn. To do well in this class, you should capitalize on that opportunity – participate during class time, re-watch videos, complete practice problems, review notes, attend office hours and ask questions. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Submission of any assignment in violation of this policy may result in the following:

• a 0 on the assignment/exam if you notify me of the issue.  
• an F in the course if you do not come forward on your own regarding the issue.

Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

What Constitutes an Academic Violation for This Class:

For exams, outside collaboration or any communication with any other student during the exam is forbidden.

For assignments, I freely allow, and encourage, you to collaborate with others so long as you provide proper citation. I’m not worried about proper MLA/Chicago style as long as you provide proper credit. Examples:

– Acceptable: You work with Phyllis from class on problem 3 and, for problem 3, you write “Phyllis [last name] and I collaborated on this problem”.

– Acceptable: You copy solutions from Chegg and write, “these solutions come from [insert url]”. Note: I do not recommend this because (1) it doesn’t help you learn and (2) many modeling solutions on Chegg are sadly incorrect.

– Unacceptable: You use part of Phyllis’s code or solution without giving her credit.

In general, be professional (and ethically safe) and provide credit to anyone that contributes to your work. Please do not photocopy someone’s assignment and write “I photocopied Phyllis’s assignment” (someone has done this in the past and, no, they were not reported for academic misconduct). This policy should not deter you from working with others or seeking outside help. While I have caught quite a few instances on academic dishonesty on exams in the past, I rarely have issues for assignments. Please help maintain this trend.
