## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • identify the kinds of questions scientists in stress research ask and the methods and research design that are employed to investigate these questions.
- • identify the three interacting components of the stress response (behavior, endocrine, and physiological).
- • explain differences between acute, adaptive stress responses and chronic, maladaptive stress responses.
- • analyze and interpret data collected in class and results reported in the primary literature as well as popular press about stress effects and communicate their perspectives.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Participation and attendance (20%):** Attendance and active engagement in class is expected. Students will receive an average grade for class participation and attendance.

**Assignments**

**Journal Article Critiques (10%):** Students will be responsible for reading and writing a critique of 5 journal articles. Each article will be discussed in class (see schedule). These critiques should be brief (1 page, single-spaced), include student’s thoughts about the work, and submitted on LMS.

**Group presentation (15%):** Working in groups, students will complete a research project that they will present as a poster at the end of the semester. Groups will be based upon similar topical interests and scheduled early in the semester.

**Reflection paper assignment (15%):** Students will be asked to complete one reflection paper. We will discuss details later this semester. Reflection papers should be 2-3 pages, double-spaced, with 12 point font.

**\*\* Any assignment that is worked on outside of class needs to be submitted via the LMS link by 11:59 pm on its due date to be considered on time. See lateness policy below for more details.\*\***

**Quizzes & Exams**

**Quizzes (10%):** Quizzes will be given most weeks on the dates indicated in schedule. These quizzes will be multiple-choice, true/false or fill-in-the-blank questions. Students will be expected to answer question associated with assigned readings and the lecture materials from the previous lecture(s). No make-up quizzes will be permitted. I will drop the lowest quiz grade for each student when calculating the average quiz grade.

**Exams (30%; 15% each):** There will be two exams during the semester consisting of multiple-choice, short answer, and true/false questions. Questions will be related to *all* materials (e.g. lectures, readings from text, journal articles, films watched) covered in the class up until when the exam is given. No make-up exams will be permitted.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is expected and will be taken. Any unexcused absences will be reflected by a lower attendance grade. Late attendance to class (more than 5 minutes) is disruptive to our community and you are likely to miss the important “sparks” we do at the beginning of most classes (every 2 days late = 1 absence). Certain situations do arise that preclude coming to class that are understandable, such as when you are feeling sick or you joined the course late; I will not count two such instances against your attendance grade. If you are missing class because of team obligations, please provide the memo from your coach. If you need to obtain an official excuse for an extended absence of five days or more, please contact the Office of Student Success success@rpi.edu. Important: you must attend all project presentation days, even if you are not presenting that day; if you are absent for a presentation day, you will also lose 10 points on your own grade for the presentation. Please note: If you are late, not fully participating, or leave early (without prior approval), points will be deducted from your attendance/participation grade.

### Other Course Policies:

***Lateness policy***

Late assignments will be accepted, but for each day an assignment is late, 3 points will be deducted from the grade. *I will only accept assignments up to two weeks late and they must be submitted by the hard deadline during the last week of classes.*

***Incomplete Grades*** In the case of an extended excused absence from class, and inability to complete coursework by the end of the semester, an Incomplete may be given. The student needs to contact me to work out the logistics and deadlines for the Incomplete before the last day of class. This is worked out on a case-by-case basis with me.

Students can earn one point to be added to their final averages for each assignment completed well. Students can turn in a maximum of 3 assignments. All extra credit assignments are due by the last day of class, but can be turned in at any point during the semester.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
