## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- describe the relationship between the mind and the brain.
- describe the basic functions of nerve cells, that neurons communicate by electrical and chemical means, and general psychopharmacology principles
- explain how the mammalian nervous system develops and changes throughout the lifespan
- identify brain targets and mechanisms for sensation/perception, movement, and other complex behaviors (ingestion, reproduction, memory, emotion)
- describe the current understanding of neurobiological underpinnings of brain health and well-being as well as neurodevelopmental, neurological, and neuropsychiatric disorders
- apply their understanding of Behavioral Neuroscience to analyze and interpret data collected in class or described in scholarly literature, and communicate their perspectives.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Participation and attendance (10%):** Attendance and active engagement in class is expected. Students will receive an average grade for class participation and attendance.

**Assignments**

**Group presentation (10%):** Students will be required to participate in one group presentation this semester. For this presentation, student groups will be given data on emotional or cognitive measures of human subjects to analyze and interpret and present to class.

**Journal Article Critiques (10%):** Students are expected to read and write critiques of 5 primary literature articles related to the topics discussed in class. Each critique is due on the day that the article will be discussed by 11:59 pm (see schedule). For class sessions indicated, students must write a critique or reflection on the journal article listed. These should be brief (1 page, single-spaced), explicitly include student’s thoughts about the work, and submitted on LMS.

**Graded assignment (15%):** Students will be asked to complete one graded assignment this semester: “Methods assignment.” In completing this assignment, the class material, source material listed below, and any other relevant information can be utilized, and should be cited appropriately. However, the work should be the student’s own and this is not to be done as a group project. This assignment includes a test on Methods slides and textbook reading (14 questions), completing a mock experiment on google forms, and a short reflection on completing the “mock experiment” (2-3 pages in length, double-spaced, 12-point font).

**\*\* Any assignment that is worked on outside of class needs to be submitted via the LMS link by 11:59 pm on its due date to be considered on time. See lateness policy below for more details.\*\***

**Quizzes & Exams**

**Quizzes (10%):** Quizzes, based upon the assigned reading, will be completed at the beginning of class, on the dates indicated in schedule (most Tuesdays). These quizzes will be multiple-choice question and closed-book. Students will be expected to answer question associated with assigned readings and the lecture materials from the previous lecture(s). No make-up quizzes will be permitted. I will drop the lowest quiz grade for each student when calculating the average quiz grade.

**Exams 1, 2, and 3 (45%; 15% each):** There will be three exams during the semester consisting of multiple-choice, short answer, and true or false questions. Questions will be related to materials (e.g. lectures, readings from text and journal articles, exercises) covered in the class up until when the exam is given. No make-up exams will be permitted.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is expected and will be taken. Any unexcused absences will be reflected by a lower attendance grade. Late attendance to class (more than 5 minutes) is disruptive to our community and you are likely to miss the important “sparks” we do at the beginning of most classes (every 2 days late = 1 absence). Certain situations do arise that preclude coming to class that are understandable, such as when you are feeling sick or you joined the course late; I will not count two such instances against your attendance grade. If you are missing class because of team obligations, please provide the memo from your coach. If you need to obtain an official excuse for an extended absence of five days or more, please contact the Office of Student Success success@rpi.edu. Important: you must attend all project presentation days, even if you are not presenting that day; if you are absent for a presentation day, you will also lose 10 points on your own grade for the presentation. Please note: If you are late, not fully participating, or leave early (without prior approval), points will be deducted from your attendance/participation grade.

**Professor Walf’s I.D.E.A. Statement** In this classroom, we will acknowledge and aim to respect **i**nclusivity, **d**iversity, **e**quity, and **a**wareness. For example, individual differences between human subjects in experiments were often minimized or considered confounds because of the added complexity they may have contributed to the analysis of the data. Our classroom will represent a diversity of individual beliefs, backgrounds, and experiences. We all must listen and show respect for every other member of this class even if we do not necessarily agree.
