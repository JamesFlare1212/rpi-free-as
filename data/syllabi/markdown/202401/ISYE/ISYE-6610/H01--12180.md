## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Overview:** As current or future systems engineers, industrial/project engineers or project managers and business operations leaders, many of you are or will be in roles requiring you to make informed decisions on complex problems related to the products, systems or processes you work on, based on quantifiable objectives and constraints. This course is a survey of decision science methodologies in the context of technical and economic decision problems. The course seeks to develop a conceptual understanding of these methods and basic implementation skills. Students will learn how to apply decision science methods from problem recognition, problem scenario representation via system models, and data development through mathematical formulation and computer solution. It is intended to give you an understanding of and appreciation for the quantitative aspects of modeling technical and economic systems for achieving specific operational performance objectives. It is meant to provide you with the knowledge of methods and tools and useful skills you can apply to your work in combination with the other courses you are taking as part of the Systems Engineering and Technology Management (SETM), Engineering Science or related certification programs.

We will begin with a brief exploration of the diversity of systems engineering problems you could encounter depending on your specific discipline and then relate them to the modeling formulations that help solve the associated decision problems, and then go into some of the applicable decision science methods. Possible application areas include production operations, supply chain management, event scheduling, statistical quality control, and risk analysis. Decision science methods include linear programming, integer programming, multi-criteria decision-making, and decision analysis. As we study some of these techniques, we will also focus on how using a systems-thinking and systems-modeling approach can help with problem formulation, as well as on computational tools that are available in software packages you can employ to solve your problems. We will travel this journey through a set of assignments or projects to help you become a hands-on professional in your systems engineering roles.

Before you get started, review this document to familiarize yourself with the course, the course structure, expectations, outcomes, and the flow of work.

The delivery of this course’s online content will be through a series of project-based learning activities. ## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Formulate, using systems thinking, real-world problems as decision science model-solving problems.
- Apply commonly used decision science methods to aid decision-making in a variety of contexts. This includes a conceptual understanding of these methods and how to use computer software to implement them to provide solutions and analysis.
- Assess the applicability and limitations of the decision science problem formulation and models to practical problems
