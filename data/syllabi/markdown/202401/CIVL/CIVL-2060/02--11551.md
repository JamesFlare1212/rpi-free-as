## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Water Resources Engineering provides a foundational treatment of the design of systems to control the quantity, timing, and distribution of water to meet the needs of human habitation and the environment within the Civil and Environmental Engineering curriculum at Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute. Topics covered include the hydrological cycle, design criteria for water distribution systems and water storage reservoirs, pump performance, applied pipe flow analysis, and pipe networks. Open channel hydraulics is also strongly featured, including the performance of hydraulic control structures such as weirs, bumps, and contractions. Theory is strongly complemented with experimental laboratory sessions. Contemporary and social and environmental issues are introduced at various points throughout the term including equity issues related to infrastructure, green infrastructure, and climate change.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand the major components and performance criteria of water distribution systems
- Appreciate non-technical perspectives on water and water use (social, economic, environmental, etc.)
- Model physical systems (pipes, pumps, pipe networks, channels, streams, hydraulic structures) by employing principles of conservation of mass, energy, and momentum to develop equations describing their behavior.
- An ability to identify, formulate, and solve complex engineering problems by applying principles of engineering, science, and mathematics.
- Solve conservation equations via concepts of specific energy to determine characteristics (flow, runoff) and compute a variety of flow and water surface elevation scenarios and observe these in laboratory experiments.
- Develop skills in computer modelling of water distribution systems and open channel flow systems using software
- An ability to recognize ethical and professional responsibilities in engineering situations and make informed judgments, which must consider the impact of engineering solutions in global, economic, environmental, and societal contexts.
- An ability to communicate effectively with a range of audiences
- An ability to function effectively on a team whose members together provide leadership, create a collaborative and inclusive environment, establish goals, plan tasks, and meet objectives.

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is strongly encouraged during all lectures, and each student is responsible for *all* materials presented and *all*announcements made in class. Note: there is a strong correlation between attending classes, doing in-class problems and homework assignments and one’s overall performance in the class.

### Other Course Policies:

Organization:

The course is structured into:

- **Lecture**: two-110-minute lectures per week. Each class will be divided into lecture sessions and in-class participation sessions by the students. Lectures will be conducted on a tablet and lecture notes will be posted on the Blackboard LMS.
- **Labs**: three laboratory experiments and a “lab”/tutorial session held on Wednesday as assigned by sections 1, 2 or 3. Typically labs run for 1 hour and are conducted with small teams of 4-6 classmates.
- **Seminar**: four guest seminar speakers discussing various aspects of lead service line issues in Flint MI and around the USA are scheduled throughout February. Students must also read “The Poisoned City - Flint's Water and the American Urban Tragedy, by Anna Clark”. Seminar is from 1 – 2pm in the CBIS Auditorium according to the schedule below.
- **Recitation/office hours**: the instructor and TAs host weekly recitations or office hours as listed above.

Labs:

There are 3 laboratory experiments and a “lab”/tutorial session for the software package EPANET. Attendance at the labs is required. The experimental method is described as observing, modelling, predicting, and evaluating fluid behavior. Typically, theoretical model will predict results for the experiment. Theory and measured results can then be compared and evaluated. Students will write a succinct report on the experiment and discuss the experimental observations in the context of theory. The labs for S23 are:

- EPANET (flow modelling software. Note: does not include a lab report and is not included in lab grade)
- Pumps
- Broad crested weir
- Spillway + hydraulic jump

Some extended answer problems will require the use of software. Students are encouraged to become familiar with MATLAB, however, most packages will suffice (i.e., MS Excel, Python, etc.). Homework assignments will be graded as follows:

- One question (selected by instructor or TA) graded for correctness (5/10 points);

You are encouraged to collaborate with other students on homework assignments, however, each student is expected to submit their own work. **Students found to have copied any part of their submission from a solution manual or from an online database will be considered in breach of academic honesty. Appropriate recourse will** **sort based on the circumstances.**

*Homework presentation:*

**1)** It is encouraged that students use “ruled” paper (physical or digital). Each problem should begin at the top of a new page with the problem # and text problem # shown.

**2)** The upper margin of the first page of your homework should show your name, the homework assignment number, the date the homework is turned in, and the page number. The upper margin of the first page of each problem should show the problem number and page number. Other pages should include the page number. The page number should be given in the upper right corner in the format A/B where A is the current page number and B is the total number of pages in your homework.

**3)** Computations should be arranged in logical order and each step recorded in full (other engineers should be able to easily follow your work). The order of presentation of work should be as shown below. Any work that does not substantially follow this order of presentation will receive no credit.

\- List given data and diagrams

\- List required results

------------------------------------- (use a line to separate information given to you from your work)

\- Calculations and diagrams

\- Final answer (boxed in)

**4)** Do not make short-cuts. Perform one operation at a time and make a complete record of it. Only experienced engineers can safely take short-cuts. An exception may be made for repetitive or tabular computations, in which case it is enough to provide a complete record of a sample calculation.

**5)** Final results should be clearly indicated by *boxing* them in.

**6)** The correct units should be provided for intermediate results and final answers. A result is of no value if units are not provided. Note that careful consideration of units will often prevent mistakes.
