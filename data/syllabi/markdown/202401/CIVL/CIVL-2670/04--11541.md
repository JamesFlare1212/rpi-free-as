## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Introduction to the elastic behavior of structural components. Analysis of statically determinate systems. Deflection calculations by virtual work and elastic load methods. Analysis of simple statically indeterminate structures. Influence lines. Interaction of structural components. Typical structural engineering loads.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The objective of this course is to develop a working knowledge of the basic principles of structural behavior, modeling, and analysis. Students will do this by building on the knowledge gained through statics (ENGR 1100) and strength of materials (ENGR 2530).

Students will gain a basic understanding of:

- structural elements and systems
- load paths and anticipated structural behavior
- typical structural loads
- commercial structural analysis tools

Upon successful completion of this course, students will have the necessary skills to take advanced structural engineering courses such as Matrix Structural Analysis (CIVL 4440), Steel Design (CIVL 4070), Concrete Design (CIVL 4080) and graduate courses such as Structural Dynamics (CIVL 6450), Wind Engineering (CIVL 6390) and Earthquake Engineering (CIVL 6490).  
The secondary objective of the course is to explore the role of the structural engineer. Students will be exposed to career options, hear from practicing engineers, and reflect on the relationship between structural engineering and equity in the built environment.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Sketch deformed shapes of determinate and indeterminate structures and compute or estimate internal force and moment distributions
- Compute (by hand and via software) external reactions, internal forces, and deflections for statically determinate structures
- Construct unit influence lines for statically determinate structures and utilize them to predict maximum load effects on structures
- Predict the response form of structural elements: axial (tension/compression) and/or shear and bending
- Explain the importance of and determine load paths through a structure
- Perform simple experiments using responsible data management practices
- Develop simple experimental procedures in a laboratory setting
- Critique the role of civil engineers in society

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students will have the opportunity to demonstrate understanding of the course material through a series of credentialing quizzes, participation in lectures and in-class activities, homework assignments, lab reports, two midterm exams, and an optional final exam.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Adequate participation can be demonstrated in many ways, such as asking and answering questions in class. Participation requires consistent attendance at lecture and lab at a minimum. Students are strongly encouraged to attend the instructor’s and/or TA’s office hours. Please silence all cell phones and do not use laptops during class unless requested.  
Unless directed otherwise, students are expected to arrive promptly in the Structures Laboratory (JEC1042F) during their assigned time every week. These sessions will be used for a combination of practice problems, credentialing, and experimentation. Students must wear closed-toed shoes and proper PPE while in the lab.  
All sophomore Civil Engineering students are expected to read *The Poisoned City: Flint's Water and the American Urban Tragedy* by February 7th. Students are required to attend the department seminars relating to this topic as laid out in the class schedule. One or more homework assignments will be based on this material.

### Other Course Policies:

MINIMUM CREDENTIALS  
Students are required to demonstrate a minimum understanding of course content. These skills will be assessed through a series of small, untimed, closed-note quizzes administered during the lab sessions as scheduled on the syllabus. Students are permitted to retake these assessments as many times as needed in order to obtain a satisfactory score.  
Students must attempt to achieve each credential as scheduled on the syllabus. Students may use unlimited retakes, waiting at least one week between each attempt. Each retake will typically occur in the next lab session; however, there will also be scheduled “retake” days to allow subsequent attempts or assessment of multiple previous credentials.  
Each quiz will contain five or ten multiple choice or fill in the blank questions. A score of 80% is required to obtain the credential. No partial credit will be given.  
Three prequalification credentials must be completed before any others are attempted. The other credentials are originally administered in the order of the class content, but do not depend on each other and may be acquired in any order.  
The tentative list of credentials is presented below, but is subject to change.  
Prequalifications: (1) Math Foundations (2) Statics 1 and (2) Statics 2  
Credentials: (1) Truss Analysis (2) Shear and Moment Diagrams (3) Determinacy and Stability (4) Structural Behavior (5) Deflections  
LAB POLICY  
Four sessions will be dedicated to laboratory experiments. Unless directed otherwise, all students are required to record their own data and submit individual lab reports. Copying or falsifying data will be considered a breach of academic integrity and subject to appropriate consequences. The reports are due at the start of the lab session on the week shown in the syllabus schedule, typically one week after the experiment.  
EXAM POLICIES  
A missed midterm exam will result in a grade of zero unless permission is granted before the scheduled exam time. Permission will only be granted for serious illness or emergency. Such situations must be documented through the Office of Student Success (4600 Academy Hall, x 8022, [Excused Absence Request Form](https://rpi.qualtrics.com/jfe/form/SV_1Gkkny395MUvIsl)). In such cases, either a makeup exam will be given or the weight of the other exams will be adjusted.  
The final exam is optional but is necessary to obtain an A in the class. Students will be asked to register for the exam approximately two weeks before the last day of classes.  
HOMEWORK POLICIES  
Submitted homework should be presented in a professional format (i.e., legible work that is presented in a logical order such that it can be readily followed). See detailed "Homework Requirements" at the end of the syllabus. If a problem is illegible or is not presented in a logical order that can be readily followed or does not substantially follow the guidelines in the "Homework Requirements", the problem will be regarded as not completed and will be assigned a grade of zero.  
Homework submissions are due at the beginning of class (12:00pm) on Fridays. Late homework will be penalized by 10% each day and will not be accepted after three days. The penalty applies to late portions only, so students should submit whatever work they have completed by the original due date/time. Students who experience any emergency preventing them from submitting the homework on time should contact the instructor.  
Homework assignments are intended for students to practice the skills necessary for successful understanding of the course content. On each homework assignment, some problems will be graded for accuracy while others will only be graded for completeness. The distinction between these problems will not be announced ahead of time. Students are strongly encouraged to complete all problems on each assignment.  
Students are *strongly* encouraged to solve the homework problems through collaboration with classmates (after trying the problems themselves); however, each student must turn in their own work.
