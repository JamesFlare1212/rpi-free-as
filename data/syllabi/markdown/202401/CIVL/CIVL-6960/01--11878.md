## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is graduate-level, covering theoretical foundations and practical applications of assessing reliability and risk of civil infrastructure systems subject to external events such as natural hazards. The goal is to provide insights and perspectives on the use of reliability analysis in engineering decision-making. Topics covered include:

- Probability theory (random variables, distributions, conditional probability, correlation, independence, moments and expectations, functions of random variables, etc.)
- Monte Carlo simulation and variance reduction techniques
- Reliability analysis of components and systems
- Time-dependent reliability
- Probability-based codified design
- Reliability updating

Real-world applications will be used to reinforce theoretical concepts with an emphasis on hands-on implementation of above topics using programming languages.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

- Enhance students' understanding of the principles and methodologies in structural reliability analysis
- Provide students with practical skills to apply statistical and probabilistic methods to assess and improve the reliability and safety of civil engineering structures and infrastructure
- Develop students' ability to conduct advanced stochastic simulations and use computational tools for solving real-world structural reliability problems

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand probability theory and statistical methods and their application in structural reliability analysis
- Apply computational tools and software for stochastic simulation and reliability analysis
- Compute reliability and failure probability of components and systems
- Explain the role of reliability in probabilistic codified design
- Analyze the updated reliability with additional data available

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students will have the opportunity to showcase their grasp of structural reliability concepts through active engagement in class discussions and activities, completing regular homework assignments, and through their performance in both a midterm and a final examination.

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

**Homework Policy:**

- Homework assignments are to be submitted at the beginning of the lecture period on the date due. Late homework will be penalized 10% per day late. Homework submitted after the solutions have been provided will not be accepted.
- Some homework assignments will require simple computer calculations. Matlab will likely be the easiest tool for performing these calculations. You are free to use another program if you prefer, as long as you clearly document what you have done.
- Clearly explaining what you have done to solve a homework problem is at least as important as obtaining a correct numerical result. Computer or calculator computations must be accompanied by appropriate documentation of how the computation was carried out. This might involve writing a few sentences of explanation, or attaching a printout of commented computer code.

**Exam Policy:**

- There are no make-up exams.
- The two exams (midterm and final) are designed as individual take home exams, so collaboration among the students will be strictly prohibited and any student involved will be assigned a grade of zero.
