## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Principles of Economics is a one semester survey course in micro- and macroeconomics which has two goals: (1) to introduce a wide variety of concepts and applications in the broad subject of economics, and (2) to function in its special Rensselaer designation as a HASS Inquiry and Data Intensive Course. Economics is the study of our choices. Traditionally, these choices have been framed as how best to employ scarce resources to produce goods and services and distribute them for consumption. To describe these choices we will introduce the concepts of opportunity cost, demand and supply theory, and market structures and consider the role of government in resource allocation choices. Also presented are macroeconomic topics such as gross domestic product, economic growth, inflation, unemployment, long-run trends and short-run fluctuations, money, and banking. As a HASS Inquiry course, a foremost objective will be to evaluate multiple diverse perspectives on contemporary and complex global issues and address their implications to social equity. Furthermore, the course is designated as a Data Intensive course and provides opportunity to practice analysis of economic data.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- to gain a factual knowledge of theories, data, and methods of analysis used by economists
- to evaluate multiple diverse perspectives on contemporary and complex global issues
- to analyze policies with regards to social, cultural, environmental, and economic outcomes
- to consider ethical responsibilities of protecting the public’s health, safety, and welfare
- to practice data analysis within the economic context

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

MyLab Assignments: Assignments using the Pearson MyLab online system will be due quarterly. All Homeworks and Quizzes are available from the start of the semester through their due date/time. Due dates are cut-off dates for the submission of work; due dates are not meant to be dates for you to begin or even finish work. It is expected that all students will complete work in a time efficient manner according to Course Calendar on the last page of this syllabus. This means that work should be completed on a weekly basis and in advance of the due date. Any student who saves work to complete on the due date must expect that technical difficulties will arise and prevent the finishing of said work by the cut-off time and credit will be lost. All assignments will be available for late completion until the end of the semester; a 10% late penalty will apply for assignments completed after the due date. The final “late” due date for all assignments is 4/25.

Tests: There will be four tests administered online via MyLab. The basis for tests will be all textbook readings, all lecture material, and all MyLab assignments. Each test will be made available for a period of seven days; refer to MyLab for specific availability dates. There is no late window for tests. Test format is take-at-home, open book/note, and multiple choice/numerical answer. Each student will receive a unique test. Tests must be completed in one session and will be timed at 110 minutes.

**MAKEUP TEST POLICY:** *Makeup tests will only be allowed at the discretion of professor. If allowed, the makeup test is scheduled during the week after the test.*

Optional Final Exam: An optional final exam will be offered as a way for students to attempt to improve their grade. The final exam will cover all course material. If you take the final exam, the score you obtain on the final exam will be compared with the average of your mid-semester tests. You will be awarded the higher of these scores as your grade for the test portion of the course. It cannot hurt you to take the optional final exam.

HASS Inquiry: This course is identified as a HASS (Humanities, Arts, and Social Sciences) Inquiry course. The purpose of a HAAS Inquiry course is to provide opportunity for the collective discourse of diverse and divergent ways of thinking about complex global issues. In particular, we strive to be thorough in understanding many perspectives with regards to important issues and the outcomes they create for society, culture, the environment, the economy, and many other realms. Additionally, we must evaluate the ethical responsibilities of protecting the public’s health, safety, and welfare. These are the specific objectives charged to a HASS Inquiry course. Instructions for the HASS Inquiry assignments are available on LMS and will be discussed in class. **If you miss the HASS Inquiry assignment(s), you may contact the TA for permission to complete late for half credit.** Written work is strictly monitored among current and former students; any student who copies work from another student is violating the academic integrity policy of this course and will be penalized accordingly – the see the Policy on Academic Integrity, below.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

By enrolling in this course, all students are committed to absolute agreement to and understanding of the course policy on academic integrity.

- \- With regards to MyLab assignments, teamwork is allowed. However, students are expected to put forth their own effort and not simply take answers from other students. By doing one’s own work on assignments, one gains deeper understanding and is better prepared to do well on tests.
- \- It is expected that students will do their own work to complete tests. Tests are open book/open note. Teamwork on tests is counterproductive because each student receives a unique and timed test.
- \- Plagiarism involves the intentional copy of sentences or paragraphs from published or online sources without citation or from work completed by a current or former student. Written work submitted will be monitored for plagiarism from any source, including present and former students. Plagiarism, either in entirety or in part, is a violation of the policy on academic integrity.

In general and to a great extent, there is a degree of “you get out of it what you put into it.” Faculty cannot possibly monitor all dishonest activity, nor can they force students to learn. It is your responsibility to get your money’s value out of this and any college class. The penalty for dishonest activity in any aspect of this course is failure of the course.
