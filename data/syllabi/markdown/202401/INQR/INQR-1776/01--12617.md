## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Utilize rhetorical and narrative analysis as a means to investigate the broad, flexible, and interdisciplinary nature of studies in the humanities. • Analyze a variety of works. • Perform comparative close readings of literary and cultural texts, past and present. • Demonstrate oral presentation skills. • Use interpersonal communication skills through exploration and discussion of complex social, cultural, and intercultural issues.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Dreaming in Fiction (Short Story):** You will conceive of the American Dream through creative writing. To prepare you for this, we will focus on poetry and short fiction as we work towards your short story wherein you can interpret a character’s struggle with, success and/or failure to achieve a version of the American Dream. We will study elements of Tension, Conflict and experiment with Point of View. (4-6 pages)

**Dreaming in Lit (Essay):** As we read both *The Great Gatsby* and *The Lathe of Heaven* consider the differing philosophies represented by the characters in each text. This essay will ask you to pick one of the novels and explore both the representation of that philosophy in the novel and its social relevance and currency. (4-5 pages)

**American Dream Project (Report/ Presentation):** In order to become more familiar with one another and the basic premises on which this course is built, you will write, polish and present what you have independently researched and learned regarding the Reality of the American Dream. Presentations should be around 7 minutes.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

*The* *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* and *The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement* define various forms of academic dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent your own work or designated group’s work. If you plagiarize you will receive an F on the assignment, may fail the entire course and could be reported to the *Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education* as appropriate. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask me for clarification. The basics: when using information from another source (including anonymous online sources) always cite the original text or URL and distinguish it from your own commentary. If you directly cut and paste another person’s language, you need to use it as a quotation. Any use of Chat GPT/AI generated text or material is considered plagiarism.
