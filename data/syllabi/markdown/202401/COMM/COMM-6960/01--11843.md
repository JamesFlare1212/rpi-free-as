## Course Content

### Content Details:

Course Description

This course explores theories and practices of adaptation across media formats and genres. We’ll consider what happens when we adapt a novel into a film, produce a TV show inspired by a video game, create a board game from a comic, and so on, focusing on what happens in the transmedial adaptation processes between ludic and narrative experiences. We’ll trace some of the historical trends in adaptation studies, and use various conceptual frameworks to closely examine adapted media and games in connection with their source materials. Throughout the class and in their final projects, students will have opportunities to both undertake critical analyses of adaptations and/or to produce their own adaptations in a medium of their choosing.

In other words…

We’re going to read about and discuss concepts from the field of ‘adaptations studies’ and apply them to examples of adaptation (and cases of intertextuality writ large). In the first half of the class, we’ll mostly be doing analytical work, using terms and ideas from the readings to better understand specific instances of adaptation. We’ll also consider how the broader concept of ‘adaptation’ (or related notions like ‘translation’) gives us insights into creative, analytical, and communicative processes. In the second half of the class, through your extended final project, you’ll have a chance to either do a critical analysis of an adaptation (or body of adaptations), or to undertake an adaptation process of your own. We’ll take a special look at games⇔stories adaptations, but you don’t necessarily have to focus on that if it’s not your interest.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Summarize and discuss works of adaptation in both narrative and ludic modes
- Identify, explain, and evaluate conceptual frameworks related to adaptations across media formats, with a particular emphasis on understanding how scholarly debate on adaptations has evolved over time
- Apply critical and theoretical lenses to specific narrative and ludic adaptations, and debate the value and functions of those critical strategies in evaluating them
- Work collaboratively with groups of peers to compose multi-faceted critiques of adaptations in various formats, and present those critiques to the larger class
- Produce an extended critical analysis of an example of adaptation OR produce a theoretically informed adaptation of an existing work in response to model adaptation texts
- Present a body of information to undergraduates as a way of demonstrating comprehensive knowledge of it AND engage those students in an activity that helps them to internalize that knowledge

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

ADDITIVE ASSESSMENT:

Rather than an exercise in knowledge absorption, this course should feel like a process of exploration, articulation, reflection, and re-articulation. You’ll be carefully considering concepts in connection with concrete examples, putting yourself in dialogue with past scholars and with each other through the media and games we’re examining, and discovering new perspectives as you create your own work (analyses or adaptations of your own devising). Assessment in this class will be an attempt to measure your depth of engagement with and understanding of the material.

We will use an ‘additive assessment’ strategy, where everyone starts with zero points and then accumulates points based on assignments and activities. You’ll be evaluated based on your depth of engagement with the material. You won’t be graded on whether or not you agree with my point of view on the materials; you’ll be graded on whether or not you’ve really thought it through. In general, if you follow the guidelines for each assignment and activity, and genuinely work towards thinking through and applying the concepts, you’ll get most or all of the points for the assignments.
