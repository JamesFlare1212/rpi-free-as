## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM) of materials with a strong hands-on lab component. Topics: principles of electron optics, imaging and contrast mechanisms, electron diffraction analysis, materials/device sample preparation, and practical TEM operation with lab assignments.

Completing this course with a passing grade will automatically allow user-level access to the Jeol TEM at Nanoscale Characterization Core, CMDIS. Training costs are included in tuition.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Main textbook: "Transmission Electron Microscopy: A Textbook for Materials Science", authors: David B. Williams, C. Barry Carter, 2nd edition, 2009, Springer.

Lecture notes will be available to students.

Non-mandatory additional textbook for more in-depth on certain topics: “Transmission Electron Microscopy and Diffractometry of Materials”, B. Fultz and J.M. Howe, 4th edition, 2013, Springer.

Background reading as needed.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

1. Hands-on knowledge of transmission electron microscopy analysis in materials research: imaging and diffraction.
2. TEM data analysis.
3. Application of TEM to various problems of materials characterization.
4. Methods of sample preparation.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

- Introduction. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM), imaging and electron diffraction, thin sample.
- TEM instrumentation, Vacuum system, detectors, holders. RPI’s Jeol 2011 TEM, new TEMs.
- Basics of TEM electron optics.
- Image and diffraction modes and practical applications.
- Diffraction: SADP, CBED
- Contrast mechanisms in TEM:
  - Mass-thickness contrast
  - Diffraction contrast
  - BF, DF. WBDF examples of dislocation studies
  - Introduction to phase contrast
- STEM
- Analytical TEM: EDS, EELS
- Sample preparation
- Biological samples, other application examples

Laboratory sections will be carried out on the Jeol 2011 TEM. In case of unforeseen equipment circumstances (e.g. breakage), we will use our Versa FIB-SEM and Zeiss Cross-Beam to learn the FIB-based lift-out TEM sample preparation. Time permitting, we may visit UAlbany’s aberration-corrected Titan TEM to compare modern capability with our TEM.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Hands-on knowledge of TEM operation and its use for materials and device analysis. Collecting different types of TEM data such as diffraction, BF, DF, and HR TEM imaging.
- 2\. Ability to analyze TEM data.
- 3\. Analyze published transmission electron microscopy articles.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Lab reports**: every 2-3 weeks based on lecture and lab materials, typically due 1-2 weeks after the assignment day.

**Mid-term**: Take-home assignment, a written review of 2-3 scientific papers on a TEM subject from the list (will be provided), 3-4 pages, and references as needed to support your statements. Due by Mar 1st. Be aware of plagiarism, refer to the Additional Course Academic Integrity policy section, below.

In-class or in-Lab **presentation of the mid-term paper** (above), 10-15 min + 5 min for question/discussion. Due by the weeks of Mar 18-25th.

**Final examination:** practical 1 h test on the microscope to demonstrate knowledge of basic operation and alignments. Times will be individually scheduled for the week of finals April 29 – May 3rd or could be scheduled earlier per the student’s request.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Lab reports:** In this class, teamwork is strongly encouraged during the labs and on the Lab reports with jointly collected data (when applicable). However, each student is expected to submit the Lab reports. When this is a teamwork, please, list all collaborators as you would list the co-authors on your papers.

Please, be aware of and avoid plagiarism, here are suggested guidelines: (1) reference any sources you use for the information. Use the primary source reference whenever available. (2) Use quotation marks when incorporating phrases or substantial parts of sentences from sources.

Breaching of academic integrity e.g. cheating, and plagiarism will result in zero points the first time, and an F final grade for the course the second time.
