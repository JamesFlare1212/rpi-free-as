### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

**Course Outline**

Dive into the fascinating world of materials science in this comprehensive introductory course. Our journey begins with an exploration of the intricate structures of materials, spanning the crystalline realms of metals to the unique non-crystalline landscapes of glass and polymers. You'll gain a deep understanding of different material structures, including crystal structures, and observe how they undergo transformative changes.

Key topics include:

- **Structural Changes and Transformation:** Delve into the dynamics of structural transformations in various materials.
- **Defects and Microstructure:** Understand the imperfections that influence material properties and the microstructural world that dictates a material's behavior.
- **Crystal Structural Determination:** Learn the principles of x-ray diffraction, a crucial tool for unraveling the mysteries of crystal structures.
- **Advanced Characterization Techniques:** Get hands-on experience with state-of-the-art techniques used at photon factories for material characterization.

This course is designed to provide a solid foundation in materials science, offering insights into both theoretical concepts and practical applications. Whether you're a budding engineer or a curious scientist, you'll find this course an essential stepping stone in your Scientific and Engineering journey.

## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

1. **Course Outline**

Structures of metals, ceramics, and polymers and methods of structural determination will be discussed. The structure includes crystal structure, structural changes (transformation), defects, and microstructure. The crystal structural determination x-ray diffraction will be covered. Local structure of non-crystalline materials, such as glasses and polymer by FT-IR spectroscopy will also be covered.

**Chapter I Basic Crystallography with Notation and Crystal Structures of various materials**

- (A) Crystal Structure

Metals- f.c.c, b.c.c, h.c.p. tetragonal

Ceramics including Semiconducting Materials - Rock Salt, Corundum, Perovskite, Zinc Blend, Fluorite, Anti-fluorite, Spinel, Diamond, and Silicates and Silicas.

Polymers- orthorhombic

(B) Non-Crystalline Structure

Radial distribution of liquids and glasses

(C) Changes in Structure

Phase transformations (Crystal transformation, Order and Disorder, Phase separation and Crystallization), Crystallinity, Glass transition

**Chapter II** **X-Ray Diffraction and Its Applications to Structural Determination of Materials**

- (A) Generation and Properties of X-ray
- (B) Absorption of X-ray
- (C) Diffraction of X-ray
- (D) Line Broadening
- (E) Structure Factors

**Chapter III**. Defects and their influence on selected properties

- (A) Point defects- Vacancy and Interstitial, Schottky and Frenkel defects and Diffusion
- (B) Non-stoichiometry - Transition metal oxides, Impurity with different valence.
- (C) Dislocations - edge and screw, mixed dislocations. Plastic deformation.
- (D) Surfaces and Interfaces, Properties of nano materials.

**Chapter IV**. Local structure determination of non-crystalline materials: IR Spectroscopy

**Chapter V.** Microstructure—Ternary Phase Diagram

**Course Goals**

1. **Understanding the Basics of Materials Science:**
   - To provide a foundational understanding of materials science, including the study of glasses, polymers, and other key materials.
2. **Exploring Crystalline and Non-Crystalline Structures:**
   - To delve into the nature of crystalline and non-crystalline structures, covering aspects such as symmetry, space concepts, and x-ray diffraction.
3. **Analyzing the Crystalline State:**
   - To study in-depth the properties and characteristics of 2D and 3D crystals, including their symmetry and point groups.
   - To understand the phenomena of dislocations, deformation, and the role of defects in crystalline structures.
4. **Comprehending Defects and Diffusion:**
   - To explore various types of defects, particularly in liquid crystals, and their implications for material properties.
   - To gain insights into diffusion processes and their significance in material science.
5. **Exploring Liquid Crystals and Topological Defects:**
   - To examine different forms of crystals, including quasi, plastic, and liquid crystals.
   - To understand the basics and complexities of point defects and their impact on material behavior.
6. **Mastering Advanced Material Characterization Techniques:**
   - To introduce and gain proficiency in advanced operando materials characterization techniques, particularly those used at photon factories.
   - To understand the use of advanced synchrotron probes in the study of materials.
7. **Applying Knowledge through Exams and Reviews:**
   - To apply and demonstrate understanding of course material through regular exams and review tutorials, ensuring a comprehensive grasp of the subjects taught.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Learning Outcomes for the Successful Student: Understanding of Material Structures: Be proficient in describing and analyzing the structure of engineering and biomedical materials. This includes the use of mathematical and crystallographic notation to articulate structural details. (ABET Criterion 3.1) Comprehension of Characterization Methods: Be able to clearly explain the physical principles underlying the characterization methods discussed in class. This also involves understanding the physical limitations of these methods, enabling a deeper appreciation of their applications and constraints. (ABET Criterion 3.6) Analysis of Experimental Data: Develop the skill to analyze experimental characterization data effectively. This includes the ability to correctly identify unknown engineering materials, considering the inherent uncertainty of the characterization techniques used. (ABET Criterion 3.6) Course Credit Allocation: Estimated category content in Math and Basic Sciences: 2 credit hours. Estimated category content in Engineering Topics: 2 credit hours.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Data acquisitions and analyses of two experiments are demonstrated. Students will learn how the data are acquired and then receive sets of the obtained data. Each student is required to analyze the data and submit a report on each topic. The report should follow the format of standard technical literature, having such sections as Introduction, Experimental Procedure, Results, Discussion and Conclusion. Referred literature should be cited at the end.

- (I) X-ray diffraction of crystalline materials.

Student will be shown the XRD equipment , Student will learn how the X-ray diffraction data are obtained. Student will receive x-ray diffraction data of an unknown samples. Students are asked to identify the crystalline material from various candidate materials.
