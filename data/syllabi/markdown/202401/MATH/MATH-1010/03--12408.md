## Course Goals

### Goals:

Learning Outcomes

Upon successfully completing the courses, students will be able to demonstrate:

- basic and advanced symbol manipulation skills,
- the ability to convert between calculus concepts and their graphical, numerical, and symbolic representations,
- the ability to apply calculus to selected problems in science and engineering
- the ability to apply certain fundamental theorems, basic concepts, and rules from calculus to solve symbolic and graphical problems.
- the ability to present problems related to the theory of calculus and its applications to science and engineering disciplines.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are responsible for all material covered in class so attendance of every lecture is strongly recommended and encouraged. If a student is absent, they should get the class notes posted each week on LMS and keep up with the homework assignments as assigned on Achieve and LMS as well as make sure to submit their Gradescope problem(s). The best way to stay on top of the course is to come to class and pay attention.

### Other Course Policies:

**Exams**: Exam problems will consist of problems for which the students must show all their work, as well as multiple-choice, multiple-select, true/false, and fill-in-the-blank questions. Exam problems for which the students must show all their work are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples. Answers to odd-numbered textbook problems can be found on Achieve at the end of the E-book. Missed exams may only be made up if a student has notified the instructor prior to the exam (in the rare case of an emergency, students may have until midnight of the exam day to notify the instructor). The exam may only be made up during the Wednesday test block that follows that exam or receive a grade of 0. The 4th and last exam will be given on the last day of class, therefore its makeup exam will be given during the scheduled final exam slot. There is no final exam for this course. Exams will be uploaded to Gradescope by the instructor, where students will be able to check their grades and feedback. It is the responsibility of the students to review their exams for any discrepancies between their work and points awarded as described by the rubric.

**Gradescope Problems**: Each class, I will assign some problem(s) for practice, mostly from the homework. Students can work on these in groups or individually, then submit them using Gradescope. Students can submit their work from the time we start working on them in class until midnight the following day. After that, submissions will not be accepted. Submissions will be graded as they would be on exams to give some feedback on how you present your work.

**Homework**: Achieve homework assignments will be due twice weekly on Mondays and Thursdays at 11:59 PM and will be graded automatically by the system. Students may submit these assignments late up to two days after the original deadline, but a penalty will be applied to the score. A 10% penalty will apply to assignments submitted the day after the deadline and a 20% penalty will apply to assignments submitted two days after the deadline. No other due date extensions will be granted for any reason. To further compensate for this, several homework assignment scores will be dropped at the end of the semester.

Additional problems will be assigned from the textbook and will be posted on LMS under each week. These are mandatory, but they will not be collected nor graded. Success in the course depends on successful completion of all homework, both Achieve homework and textbook homework. As stated under Exams, exam problems are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples. This means that you must be able to correctly solve and show all your work as well as fully understand your answer to each homework problem and lecture example to consider yourself well-prepared.

Change to the grading scheme may be made at the discretion of the instructor.

**Getting help**: don’t hesitate to ask and get help. You can ask math questions during class, office hours of the instructor, and office hours of the Teaching Assistant. You can also use email. When asking questions, it helps to be as precise as possible and to have a picture of the work you have tried. For example, if you ask a homework question by email, please attach a picture of your work so that I may check it to provide better and faster assistance. There is also tutoring and support offered by ALAC.

**Calculator Policy**

Calculators will not be allowed on exams. Problems will be designed so that this does not create an issue. Occasionally, a calculator may be required to complete a homework problem, but students should not rely on calculators for simple arithmetic or trigonometric calculations.

**Classroom Decorum**

Phones may not be used during class and should be silent. Computers and tablets may not be used for anything other than taking notes. Students who are unable to courteously comply with these policies may be asked to leave the room. If you are on your phone or laptop during the lecture, then you are probably missing most of the lecture, including practice questions, common errors or hints that may be given about graded assignments, quizzes, or exams.

**Health and Safety Protocols**

Students are required to follow the health and safety protocols of RPI, which are subject to change throughout the semester. Students who are not cleared for campus access are prohibited from attending class. Failure to comply with health and safety protocols will be met with a request to comply, a request to leave the class, or a dismissal of the entire class and referral of the non-compliant student to the Dean of Students.

**Visitors**

Visitors are prohibited during all class meetings. Only students who are registered for the class should be present in the classroom. Students may only attend class meetings at the time and place listed on their schedule on SIS.

**Grade Appeals**

If upon reviewing an exam a student feels that his or her work was graded incorrectly, then the student may submit a grading appeal to the instructor. The student should submit, in writing, an explanation of the grading issue along with the original exam. The student should not make any marks on the original exam. Students have one week after the exam is returned to submit an appeal.

**THERE ARE NO EXTRA CREDIT ASSIGNMENTS GIVEN AT THE END OF THE SEMESTER. All course credit comes from the assignments, quizzes, and exams assigned and administered regularly throughout the semester. The successful completion of the class indicates an in-depth understanding of the content AND the ability to correctly and precisely communicate this understanding by solving relevant problems using symbolic representation. If you cannot do the actual math, you cannot pass the course.**

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://rpi.app.box.com/s/d4ndpn3f7qdwxh9jaz5n1hkkr0mnd25e) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams must be the student’s own. A student may not change an answer on an exam before submitting it for an appeal. In addition, no calculators, computers, tablets, open books, open notes, phones, headphones, or smartwatches may be used during exams. Talking or communicating with other students in any way during exams is also prohibited. Students may discuss homework with other students, mentors, TAs, and instructors, but the work submitted must be the student’s own.

The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
