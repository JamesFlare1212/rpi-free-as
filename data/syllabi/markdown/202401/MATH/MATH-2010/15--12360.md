## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Directional derivatives, maxima and minima, double integrals, line integrals, div and curl, and Green’s Theorem; matrix algebra and systems of linear equations, vectors and linear transformations in R^n, eigenvectors and eigenvalues, applications in engineering and science.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Calculus: Early Transcendentals, 4th Edition (Rogawski, et al)  
Introduction toLinear Algebra, 5th Edition (Johnson, et al)

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will learn derivatives and integrals of functions of multiple variables
- Students will learn vector analysis and Green's Theorem
- Students will learn Gaussian elimination
- Students will learn to compute eigenvalues and eigenvectors of matrices

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

**Exams:** The material covered on each exam will be announced on LMS when the date of the exam is near. Exams will be given during Test Block. Exam 1 covers Multivariable Calculus content and Exam 2 covers Matrix Algebra content.  
If a student has a pre-scheduled absence (e.g. athletic competition, conference) on the day of an exam, then they must notify the instructor via email as soon as they learn of the absence and no later than one week before the regularly scheduled exam. Documentation excusing the student from class at the time of the regularly scheduled exam must be received by the instructor before the day of the exam. This documentation must be either an excused absence documented by the Student Success office or, if the absence is due to an athletic competition, documentation from a coach. Provided the above conditions are met, the student will then take a conflict exam at a different time.  
If a student misses an exam due to an unforeseen absence (e.g. illness on the day of the exam), then they may take a make-up exam provided they notify the instructor via email before the exam begins. The make-up exam may differ in format from the regularly scheduled exam and must be taken within one week of the regularly scheduled exam. It is the sole responsibility of the student to contact the instructor to make arrangements for a make-up exam when an exam is missed. Students who miss the exam and fail to notify the instructor before the exam starts may not take the make-up exam and will receive a score of 0 on the exam.  
The make-up exam policy above applies to Exams 1–2. There is no make-up for the Final Exam.  
**Optional Final exam:** Students taking the full course can decide to take the optional final exam. The final exam will be cumulative and will be administered during the final exam week (Monday, Apr 29 — Friday, May 3) determined by the Office of the Registrar. All students who choose to take the final exam, must take the final exam during the final exam block at the time scheduled by the Office of the Registrar. Exceptions will only be made if there is a scheduling conflict as defined here. The Student Success office will only issue an excuse for a missed final exam for extreme circumstances, such as hospitalization. Therefore, students must not make end-of-semester travel arrangements until the final exam schedule is posted, or should only schedule travel for the time period after the last day of final exams, which is Friday, May 3.  
**Quizzes** will be given most weeks during recitation. Material to be covered on each quiz will be posted on LMS. Students must take quizzes in the section for which they are registered to receive a grade on the quiz.  
No make-up quizzes will be given for any reason. To compensate for this, the two (2) lowest quiz scores for each student taking the full course will be dropped at the end of the semester (and one (1) lowest quiz score for students taking half courses). Students who miss a quiz due to a valid pre-scheduled reason (for example, athletic event or conference) may be exempt from that quiz if documentation is submitted to the instructor at least one week in advance.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams  
and quizzes must be the student’s own. In addition, no calculators, computers, tablets, open books, open notes, phones, headphones, or smartwatches may be used during exams or quizzes. All electronic devices must be powered off and stowed in a backpack during quizzes and exams. Talking or communicating with other students in any way during exams or quizzes is also prohibited. The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
