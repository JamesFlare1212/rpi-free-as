## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is devoted to advanced methods rather than theory. Content includes such topics as matched asymptotic expansions, multiple scales, WKB, and homogenization. Applications are made to ODEs, PDEs, difference equations, and integral equations. The methods are illustrated using currently interesting scientific and engineering problems that involve such phenomena as boundary or shock layers, nonlinear wave propagation, bifurcation and stability, and resonance.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

We will cover the basic methods involving perturbation approximations. In addition to getting a solid grounding in the fundamentals, it is anticipated that students will gain the following: (i) An ability to adapt the methods to a wide variety of problems, (ii) Improved skills at deriving analytical solutions of difficult mathematical problems, and (iii) An ability to determine the qualitative behavior for the solutions of nonlinear problems

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Tentative Outline**

1. Introduction [1.5 weeks]
2. a) asymptotic approximations
3. b) algebraic and transcendental equations
4. c) regular ODE and PDE expansions

1. Matched Asymptotic Expansions [2 weeks]
2. a) boundary layers
3. b) interior and corner layers

III. Multiple Scale Methods [2.5 weeks]

1. a) two-timing and derivative expansions
2. b) applications to nonlinear vibrations and dispersive waves

2. WKB Method [2.5 weeks]
3. a) application to ordinary and partial differential equations
4. b) turning points and transition regions
5. c) wave propagation
6. d) connection with energy methods

1. Homogenization Methods [2 weeks]
2. a) periodic and nonperiodic substructures
3. b) connection with multiple scales and averaging
4. c) derivation of macroscopic descriptions

1. Bifurcation Problems [3 weeks]
2. a) steady states, stability, and Lyapunov-Schmidt procedure
3. b) relaxation dynamics
4. c) periodic solutions
