## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

First-order differential equations, second-order linear equations, eigenvalues and eigenvectors of matrices, systems of first-order equations, stability and qualitative properties of nonlinear autonomous systems in the plane, Fourier series, separation of variables for partial differential equations. (from the [Rensselaer Catalog 2023-2024](http://catalog.rpi.edu/)).

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Mark H. Holmes, Introduction to Differential Equations, 3ed, XanEdu Publishers.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Mathematical models of practical problems in science and engineering often involve differential equations. The equations may be written as a single equation of a certain order or may be written as a system of first-order equations, and they may be linear or nonlinear. The goal of this course is to teach analytical techniques for the solution of elementary differential equations that arise often in areas of application.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Successful completion of this course should enable you to demonstrate an understanding of fundamental techniques for the solution of first-order and second-order ordinary differential equations.
- Successful completion of this course should enable you to demonstrate an understanding of Fourier series and separation of variables for certain partial differential equations.
- Successful completion of this course should enable you to demonstrate an understanding of solution techniques and the behavior of solutions of systems of first-order differential equations.
- Successful completion of this course should enable you to present written solutions to problems in a clear, concise and coherent fashion.

## Policies

### Other Course Policies:

**Exams:** The material covered on each exam will be announced on LMS when the date of the exam is near. Exams will be given during lecture (see **Meeting Times** above). The dates for Exams 1–3 are listed above in section **Grading**.

If a student has a pre-scheduled absence (e.g. an athletic competition, official excused absence to attend a conference) on the day of an exam, then they must notify the instructor via email as soon as they learn of the absence and no later than one week before the regularly scheduled exam. Documentation excusing the student from class at the time of the regularly scheduled exam must be received by the instructor before the day of the exam. This documentation must be either an [excused absence documented by the Student Success office](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/current-students/academic-and-personal-support/requesting-excused-absence) or, if the absence is due to an athletic competition, documentation from a coach. Provided the above conditions are met, the student will then take a conflict exam at a different time.

If a student misses an exam due to an unforeseen absence (e.g. illness on the day of the exam), then they may take a make-up exam provided they **notify the instructor via email before the exam begins**. The make-up exam may differ in format from the regularly scheduled exam and must be taken within one week of the regularly scheduled exam. It is the sole responsibility of the student to contact the instructor to make arrangements for a make-up exam when an exam is missed. **Students who miss the exam and fail to notify the instructor before the exam starts may not take the make-up exam and will receive a score of 0 on the exam.**

The make-up exam policy above applies to Exams 1–3. There is no make-up for the optional Final Exam.

**Optional Final Exam:** The optional final exam will be cumulative and will be administered during the final exam week (Monday, Apr 29 — Friday, May 3) determined by the [Office of the Registrar](https://info.rpi.edu/registrar). All students who choose to take the final exam, must take the final exam during the final exam block at the time scheduled by the Office of the Registrar. Exceptions will only be made if there is a scheduling conflict as defined [here](https://info.rpi.edu/registrar/grades/#FinalExamScheduleandConflicts). The Student Success office will only issue an excuse for a missed final exam for extreme circumstances, such as hospitalization. Therefore, students must not make end-of-semester travel arrangements until the final exam schedule is posted, or should only schedule travel for the time period after the last day of final exams, which is Friday, May 3.

**Homework/Quizzes:** Quizzes and graded Homework assignments are weighted equally. Graded homework assignments are typically assigned weekly. Homework will be submitted on Gradescope and will be due by 11:59PM on the due date. If you are unable to hand in your homework on time due to a valid reason (work in other classes is not a valid reason), then you must make arrangements with the instructor in advance and **not** after the due date. Late submissions will be subject to penalties as follows: less than 2 hours late 2%, 2–24 hours late 10%, 1–2 days late 30%. Late submissions will **not** be accepted after two days late.

Additional suggested problems will be assigned for each section from the textbook as we cover the material in class that will not be collected or graded. While these problems are not graded, regular attempts of these problems will generally correlate with greater success in the course. Quiz and Exam problems are frequently similar to graded homework assignments, suggested problems, and lecture examples.

A short quiz will be given most non-exam weeks during recitations. Dates and topics covered on each quiz will be posted on LMS. No make-up quizzes will be given for any reason. To compensate for this, the lowest two (2) scores in the Homework/Quizzes category will be dropped at the end of the semester. For example, a student could miss two quizzes OR a student could miss one quiz and not submit one homework. In either scenario, the student will receive a score of 0 on both missed assessments. These scores will be dropped as the lowest two Homework/Quizzes scores at the end of the semester. Students who miss a quiz due to a valid pre-scheduled reason (for example, athletic event or conference) may be exempt from that quiz if documentation is submitted to the instructor at least one week in advance.

**Lectures:** An unmarked version of the lecture notes will be made available the day before lecture. At some point after lecture, a marked up version of the notes will be posted to LMS. Students are encouraged to preview the textbook sections to be covered in class **before the class**. It is not necessary to have a thorough understanding of the material after a first reading, but knowing the basic facts and content should help in following the class discussion. This way we can spend more time on examples. Students are expected to read the relevant sections of the textbook to supplement the material covered in lecture.

**Recitations:** The teaching assistant (TA) will conduct weekly recitations. During these 50-minute sessions, the TA will solve problems and answer questions about the material, including homework problems. Short quizzes (1-2 problems) will be given most non-exam weeks during recitation. Material to be covered on each quiz will be posted on LMS. Students must take quizzes in the section for which they are registered to receive a grade on the quiz.

**Attendance:** Students are responsible for all material covered in lectures and recitations whether they are present or not. Attendance is expected in lectures, but will only be checked on exam days. Attendance is required in recitations whenever there is a quiz.

**Classroom Decorum:** Please adhere to the following during meetings for this course.

- Students must be on time for class and should refrain from leaving and reentering the classroom during class. If a student has a legitimate reason for arriving late to class or being excused early from class, then they should discuss this with the instructor or teaching assistant before class.
- Phones may not be used during class and should be silent.
- Computers and tablets may not be used for anything other than taking notes.
- Visitors are prohibited during all class meetings. Only students who are registered for the class should be present in the classroom.

Students who are unable to courteously comply with these policies may be asked to leave the room.

**Calculator policy:** Calculators will not be allowed on exams or quizzes. Problems will be designed so that this does not create an issue. Occasionally, a calculator may be required to complete a homework problem, but students should not rely on calculators for simple arithmetic or trigonometric calculations.

**Extra Credit and Rounding:** No extra credit opportunities will be available during the course, so be sure to give your best effort on exams and quizzes throughout the semester. The grade cut-offs in **Grading** above are the minimum overall course grade needed to guarantee the associated letter grade without rounding. Any requests for extra credit or inquiries about rounding will be ignored.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://info.rpi.edu/sites/default/files/Rensselaer%20Handbook%20of%20Student%20Rights%20&%20Responsibilities%20%C2%BB.pdf) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams, quizzes, and homework must be the student’s own. In addition, no calculators, computers, tablets, open books, open notes, phones, headphones, or smartwatches may be used during exams or quizzes. All electronic devices must be powered off and stowed in a backpack during quizzes and exams. Talking or communicating with other students in any way during exams or quizzes is also prohibited. Students may discuss homework with other students, the TA, and the instructor, but the work submitted must be the student’s own. Copying someone else’s solution and submitting it as your own is not allowed. Submissions with glaring similarities will violate the academic integrity policies.

The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
