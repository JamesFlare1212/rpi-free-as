### Additional Information for the Instructor Section:

A working knowledge of the basic facts in single-variable and multi-variable calculus and elementary linear algebra, through MATH 2010, is essential. Calculus of variations will also require facility to solve simple differential equations. Here is a list of topics for you to know, and if need be, review. Our review of these materials in class (Chapters 1 and 2 of the textbook) will be brief.

- Vectors in R2 and R3 : geometric and algebraic representations, basis vectors, parametric representation of curves, dot and cross products, projection, planes and distances.

- Polar, cylindrical and spherical coordinates.

- Functions of several variables: definition, visualization, limit and continuity. Partial differentia-

  tion, differentiability, linear approximation, chain rules, directional derivative, gradient.

- Elementary matrix operations. First-order linear and separable ODEs, constant-coefficient ODEs. This material will not be reviewed in class.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Learning Outcomes: Functions of several variables, or multivariate functions, arise naturally in a variety of fields in engineering and science such as motion and deformation of solids and fluids, thermodynamics, heat and mass transfer, and electromagnetics, and so on. A successful completion of the course should enable you to

1. demonstrate a conceptual and practical understanding of multivariate functions and their rates of change,

2. compute extrema of multivariate functions,

3. demonstrate a conceptual and practical understanding of integrals of multivariate functions along

   paths and over surfaces and volumes,

4. demonstrate a conceptual understanding of integral theorems and their practical applications,

5. compute extrema of functionals, and

6. demonstrate a conceptual and practical understanding of the notion of Cartesian tensors.

   The primary focus will be on understanding the principles of multivariable calculus and learning to apply them to practical situations.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

A course emphasizing advanced concepts and methods from calculus. Topics include: multivariable integral theorems, extrema of multivariable functions (including Lagrange multipliers), the calculus of variations (Euler-Lagrange equations, constraints, principle of least action) and others.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:** Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://rpi.app.box.com/s/d4ndpn3f7qdwxh9jaz5n1hkkr0mnd25e) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams must be the student’s own. Students may use their textbook and notes during exams, but the use of online resources outside of LMS is prohibited. Communicating with other people in any way during exams about the content of the exam is prohibited. Students may discuss homework with other students, TAs, and instructors, but the work submitted must be the student’s own.

The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
