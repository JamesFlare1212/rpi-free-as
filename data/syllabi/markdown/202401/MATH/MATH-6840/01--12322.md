## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate an understanding of: • the fundamental concepts in PDE discretization such as consistency, stability, and convergence • the behavior of hyperbolic, elliptic, and parabolic PDEs • the behavior of numerical methods for these PDEs and in particular their accuracy and cost
- Implement numerical methods for PDEs using clear and correct computer code
- Present written solutions to problems in a clear, concise, and coherent fashion

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Homework:  
Homework will be assigned regularly and constitutes a significant portion of your grade. Sub- missions should include any computer code you wrote, and any results that are generated. Note that as the semester proceeds your codes may become rather long, and it may be sensible to include them as an appendix. You are expected to submit a physical version of your typeset document (preferably using LATEX) either to me in class or to my mailbox by the close of business on the due date. To facilitate this, there will be an example LATEXtemplate. Also note that use of AI technologies, such as ChatGPT, is limited to LATEXformatting. However, note these tools are pre- dictive text generators, often yield incorrect logical statements, and have no checks for accuracy or truthfulness.

Late homework policy:  
No late homework will be accepted. Homework submissions will be due by the close of business on their due date. If you are unable to submit a homework assignment on time, you must make arrangements with me before the assignment is considered late. For example, if you are sick or have a family emergency please discuss with me. Please realize that excessive work load will not constitute a valid reason for an extension.

Exams:  
There will be two exams throughout the semester, a midterm and a final. Each exam will cover all topics discussed up to that point. In both cases the exams may be given as in-class, take-home, or a combination. In the case of take-home portions there would be a set time limit, and you will be required to sign an honor pledge stating that you have adhered to the rules as laid out for that exam.

Appealing grades:  
If you would like clarification on a grade or wish to appeal a grading decision simply stop by my office during office hours and we can discuss it. Such appeals must be performed within one week after receiving the graded homework or exam. After that time we can still discuss the issue but there will be no changes to the grade.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

The relationship between a student and instructor is built on mutual respect and trust. For example, the student should treat the instructor with common courtesy and respect, and trust that the instructor has made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the course. The instructor, on the other hand, should be courteous and respectful to every student, and trust that the students are working diligently and honestly on the assigned material. Acts which violate this mutual respect and trust undermine the education process.

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Aca- demic Dishonesty and you should familiarize yourself with these. In the case of homework assign- ments, collaboration between students is encouraged. However, each student must write their own solution and submit their own work. Copying assignments or computer codes is not permitted. Similarly, use of AI technologies such as ChatGPT is limited to LATEXformatting, and AI is not allowed for mathematical or logical deductions. If violations of this policy are suspected, then an explanation will be requested. If the explanation is not satisfactory, then a grade of zero will be given and a report made to the Dean of Students.

In the case of exams there is no collaboration of any kind. If cheating is suspected, then an explanation will be requested. If the explanation is not satisfactory, then an exam grade of zero will be given and a report made to the Dean of Students.
