## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Description:** Techniques and applications of integration, polar coordinates, parametric equations, infinite sequences and series, vector functions and curves in space, functions of several variables, and partial derivatives. Prerequisite: MATH 1010 (from the [Rensselaer Catalog](http://catalog.rpi.edu/))

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Learning Outcomes

Upon successfully completing the courses, students will be able to demonstrate:

- basic symbol manipulation skills pertaining to integration
- the ability to state and explain basic Calculus definitions and theorems and their applications
- the ability to describe curves parametrically and apply Calculus concepts and techniques to curves described in this way
- the ability to express curves via polar equations and to solve Calculus problems in the polar coordinate system
- the ability to apply calculus to selected problems in science, engineering and mathematics
- the ability to use convergence tests to analyze the behavior of infinite series
- the ability to represent functions using power series and solve problems using these representations

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are responsible for all material covered in class so attendance of every lecture is strongly recommended and encouraged. If a student is absent, they should get the class notes posted each week on LMS and keep up with the homework assignments as assigned on Achieve and LMS as well as make sure to submit their Gradescope problem(s). The best way to stay on top of the course is to come to class and pay attention.

### Other Course Policies:

**Exams**: Exam problems will consist of problems for which the students must show all their work, as well as multiple-choice, multiple-select, true/false, and fill-in-the-blank questions. Exam problems for which the students must show all their work are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples. Answers to odd-numbered textbook problems can be found on Achieve at the end of the E-book. Missed exams may only be made up if a student has notified the instructor prior to the exam (in the rare case of an emergency, students may have until midnight of the exam day to notify the instructor). The exam may only be made up during the Wednesday test block that follows that exam or receive a grade of 0. Exams will be uploaded to Gradescope by the instructor, where students will be able to check their grades and feedback. It is the responsibility of the students to review their exams for any discrepancies between their work and points awarded as described by the rubric.

**Gradescope Problems**: Each class, I will assign some problem(s) for practice, mostly from the homework. Students can work on these in groups or individually, then submit them using Gradescope. Students can submit their work from the time we start working on them in class until midnight the following day. After that, submissions will not be accepted. If you are unable to submit your work using Gradescope, please email it to me by the due date and time. Submissions will be graded as they would be on exams to give some feedback on how you present your work.

**Homework**: Achieve homework assignments will be due twice weekly on Mondays and Thursdays at 11:59 PM and will be graded automatically by the system. Students may submit these assignments late up to two days after the original deadline, but a penalty will be applied to the score. A 10% penalty will apply to assignments submitted the day after the deadline and a 20% penalty will apply to assignments submitted two days after the deadline. No other due date extensions will be granted for any reason. To further compensate for this, several homework assignment scores will be dropped at the end of the semester.

Additional problems will be assigned from the textbook and will be posted on LMS under each week. These are mandatory, but they will not be collected nor graded. Success in the course depends on successful completion of all homework, both Achieve homework and textbook homework. As stated under Exams, exam problems are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples. This means that you must be able to correctly solve and show all your work as well as fully understand your answer to each homework problem and lecture example to consider yourself well-prepared.

Change to the grading scheme may be made at the discretion of the instructor.

**Getting help**: don’t hesitate to ask and get help. You can ask math questions during class, office hours of the instructor, and office hours of the Teaching Assistant. You can also use email. When asking questions, it helps to be as precise as possible and to have a picture of the work you have tried. For example, if you ask a homework question by email, please attach a picture of your work so that I may check it to provide better and faster assistance. There is also tutoring and support offered by ALAC.

**Calculator Policy**

Calculators will not be allowed on exams. Problems will be designed so that this does not create an issue. Occasionally, a calculator may be required to complete a homework problem, but students should not rely on calculators for simple arithmetic or trigonometric calculations.

Phones may not be used during class and should be silent. Computers and tablets may not be used for anything other than taking notes. ### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:** Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://rpi.app.box.com/s/d4ndpn3f7qdwxh9jaz5n1hkkr0mnd25e) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams must be the student’s own. Students may use their textbook and notes during exams, but the use of online resources outside of LMS is prohibited. Communicating with other people in any way during exams about the content of the exam is prohibited. Students may discuss homework with other students, TAs, and instructors, but the work submitted must be the student’s own.

The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
