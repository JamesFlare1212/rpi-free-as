## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Description:** Techniques and applications of integration, polar coordinates, parametric equations, infinite sequences and series, vector functions and curves in space, functions of several variables, and partial derivatives. Prerequisite: MATH 1010 (from the [Rensselaer Catalog](http://catalog.rpi.edu/))

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Basic symbol manipulation skills pertaining to integration.
- Ability to state and explain basic Calculus definitions and theorems and their applications.
- Ability to describe curves parametrically and apply Calculus concepts and techniques to curves described in this way.
- Ability to express curves via polar equations and to solve Calculus problems in the polar coordinate system.
- Ability to apply Calculus to selected problems in Science, Engineering and Mathematics.
- Ability to use convergence tests to analyze the behavior of infinite series.
- Ability to represent functions using power series and solve problems using these representations.
- Ability to describe lines and planes in space in equation form and solve problems using these representations.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Exams:** Exams will be given during lecture with the exception of DSS students and excused absences as outline below. Attendance will be taken at all exams using sign in sheets. Details about each exam, including the material to be covered on the exam and time limit, will be announced on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/) when the date of the exam is near. The dates for Exams 1–3 are listed above in the section **Grading.** Details on exams for DSS students are given below in the **DSS** section.

A conflict exam will be given only to students who have made prior arrangements with the instructor. Students eligible to take a conflict exam are DSS students (prior arrangements made by providing DSS letter) and students with a pre-scheduled absence (e.g. official excused absence documented by Student Success to attend a conference, athletic competition) on the day of the exam who have notified the instructor of said absence as soon as they learn of it and no later than one class period prior to the regularly scheduled exam. Documentation excusing the student from class at the time of the regularly scheduled exam ([**excused absence documented by the Student Success office**](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/current-students/academic-and-personal-support/requesting-excused-absence) **or documentation from a coach with game departure time noted)** must be received by the instructor before the day of the regularly scheduled exam. Any student who “shows up” without making prior arrangements with the instructor, will not be allowed to take the conflict exam. If you know ahead of time that you are going to miss an exam, for any reason, you must discuss this with the instructor no later than one class period prior to the exam.

**In-semester** **make-up exams will not be given.** **The final exam is the make-up exam.** Thus, if a student misses an exam for any other reason (because they overslept, was ill on the day of the exam, didn’t know where the exam was, or didn’t know there was an exam, to give a few examples), the student will receive a score of 0 on the exam. Likewise, if a student misses a conflict exam, the student will receive a 0 on the exam. This score will be dropped as the lowest score at the end of the semester and be replaced by their score on the final exam. Thus remaining 2 exam scores plus the final exam score times two (added in as two identical exam scores) will count toward the student’s course grade. **Only one exam can be replaced by the final exam score, thus if** a student misses more than one exam during the semester, they **must email** **the instructor** within 24 hours of missing the additional exam(s) to make arrangements to discuss options, ***if any***. Likewise, if a student has an unforeseen absence, it is best practice to **email** **the instructor** within 24 hours of missing the exam to inform the instructor of the nature of their absence.

Note that the policy outlined here is for in-semester exams only. The policy for the final exam is outlined below.

**Final Exam:** The Final Exam will be cumulative and will be administered during the finals period April 29 - May 3. **All students must take the final exam at the time scheduled by the Office of the Registrar. Exceptions will only be made if there is a scheduling conflict as defined** [**here**](https://info.rpi.edu/registrar/grades/#FinalExamScheduleandConflicts)**, or if a student is excused by the Student Success office.** Please note that the Student Success office will only issue an excuse for final exams for extreme circumstances, such as hospitalization, and not for travel. (Read more here.) All students **must** take their exam at the assigned time for MATH 1020 Sections 1 - 8. You may NOT take a MATH 1020 final exam given by a different instructor. ***Do not make arrangements for travel at the end of the semester until the Final Exam Schedule from the Registrar’s Office is published or schedule your travel after the last day of final exams - May 3.***

**Homework:** Achieve homework assignments will be due once or twice weekly on Mondays and Thursdays at 11:59 PM and will be graded automatically by the system. Students may submit these assignments late up to two days after the original deadline, but a penalty will be applied to the score. A 10% penalty will apply to questions completed the day after the deadline and a 20% penalty will apply to questions completed two days after the deadline. Any questions completed before the deadline receive full credit. No other due date extensions will be granted for any reason. To further compensate for this, several homework assignment scores will be dropped at the end of the semester.

Additional problems will be assigned from the textbook and will be posted on [LMS](http://lms.rpi.edu/) in the **Homework: Achieve & Textbook** content area found in the left toolbar. Though these problems will not be collected nor graded, they should not be viewed as optional. Success in the course depends on successful completion of all homework, both Achieve homework and textbook homework. Exam problems are frequently chosen from homework assignments and lecture examples. Full solutions to odd-numbered textbook problems can be found on Achieve. When logged in to our Achieve course, view content by assignments and scroll to the bottom of the page. The student solutions manual for chapters 5-7 and 10-14 may be found below No Due Dates.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity:** Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://rpi.app.box.com/s/d4ndpn3f7qdwxh9jaz5n1hkkr0mnd25e) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams must be the student’s own. In addition, no calculators, computers, tablets, open books, open notes, phones, headphones, or smartwatches may be used during exams or quizzes. Talking or communicating with other students in any way during exams is also prohibited. Students may discuss homework with other students, mentors, TAs, and instructors, but the work submitted must be the student’s own.

The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
