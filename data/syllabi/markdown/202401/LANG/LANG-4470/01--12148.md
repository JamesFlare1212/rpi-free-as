## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a continuation of Chinese IV, a course for the standard modern Chinese language (Mandarin). Students learn more Chinese characters and words, reach a total of about 950 characters, 1700 words, and use more complicated grammatical structures, e.g., idiomatic expressions, expressions of comparison, concession, and subjection, etc. In sum, students learn more in all four aspects – listening, speaking, reading, and writing – presented in Chinese IV.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Throughout the semester, students will be actively involved in communicative tasks both inside and outside the classroom. Students will learn how to describe various topics such as weather, travel, and climate, as well as academics, including study abroad experiences, clubs, hobbies, and more. Furthermore, students will have the opportunity to discuss on- and off-campus housing, as well as online shopping. Writing skills in a formal context will receive particular emphasis, along with reading, speaking, and listening. In addition, students will be exposed to innovative learning environments incorporating new technologies and language learning theories. Throughout the semester, a wide range Chinese culture will also be introduced to students.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students who complete this course will acquire the following competencies and be able to perform communicative tasks at the Intermediate Mid-level of language proficiency, as described by the American Council on the Teaching of Foreign Languages: 1. Interpersonal Communication: Students will be able to (1) carry out and continue conversations involving personal views on familiar topics with many details and in uncomplicated settings (e.g., academic applications, clubs), (2) use conversation skills to join and participate in a spontaneous discussion on a variety of familiar topics (e.g., housing, online purchase and return), and (3) use the language to exchange information with people from the target culture about familiar topics and personal opinions in uncomplicated situations (e.g., describing weather and travel). 2. Presentational Communication: Students will be able to (1) use a series of connected sentences in presentations to describe experiences, events, and opinions, (2) use readily available technology tools and digital literacy skills to present academic information in the target language, and (3) use series-connected sentences to describe arts, sports, games, and media from the Chinese culture. 3. Interpretive Communication: Students will be able to (1) understand the main idea and many details of familiar topics in a series of connected sentences, conversations, presentations, and messages, and (2) understand the meaning of longer messages on familiar and unfamiliar topics displayed in the community created by communities of learners of the same target language. 4. Cultural Competence: Students will be able to (1) deconstruct written and spoken texts for cultural attitudes, viewpoints, and values, and (2) evaluate the traditions of Chinese culture and American culture (e.g., college admissions and store return policy).

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

***Participation & Performance***: Language learning requires using the vocabulary and grammar you learn each week to interact with others in a target language society. Active engagement is a must. Some assignments may involve recording your voice or participating in role-plays. Excised class absences will not affect your grade, and you can schedule make-up work for those absences. However, ***unexcused absences and lateness*** ***do not allow for make-up work***.

***Weekly Routine Tasks***: Daily practice is crucial for successful Chinese learning. *To promote proficiency-based language learning**, assignments for this course are spread out over the week,*** requiring 20-30 minutes of practice each day. Make sure to check each new week's tasks and due dates on the LMS. There are ***THREE (3) due times*** *for the weekly routine tasks*: (1) Before the classes on Tuesday and Friday, and before Sunday Midnight.

Students must carefully review the assignments and due dates for the week, which are posted early on the LMS. Late submissions will result in a deduction of 10% of the possible points per 24-hour period after the expected time. *If you submit **homework late**, please inform me when doing so.*

***Projects***: Throughout the semester, there will be several projects in this course that *require self-regulation in terms of planning and monitoring the completion*. You can find more details in the "Project Instruction and Rubric" folder in the LMS Course Documents.

***Tests & Exams***: This course includes two exams and several unit review tests. Each exam consists of two parts: written and oral parts. The unit tests serve as comprehensive practices for the course contents. To prepare for the exams, follow the exam study guides. **\*\*\* Note**: If you require **extended time on exams** or **any other accommodations**, please provide me with **a memo issued by Disability Services for Students at the beginning of the semester**.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

It is VERY EASY to spot cheating in a foreign language course. Students are not permitted to turn in work done by someone else. Getting assistance from a native speaker on assignments is OK, but your final product must be your own work as it is easy to identify the excessive structures beyond your level of proficiency. Besides, **using ChatGPT or a WEB TRANSLATE (e.g., Google Translate) for translation other than vocabulary or phrases is not permitted**.

Properly cite any external sources or tools used in your work, including ChatGPT or online translation tools.  
Plagiarism, whether intentional or unintentional, is a violation of academic integrity and will be subject to disciplinary actions.

***Consequences:***

Violations of this policy will result in appropriate actions being taken in accordance with the academic integrity guidelines established by the university. These actions may include, but are not limited to, assigning a failing grade for the particular assignment or the entire course.

In the event that I suspect cheating for the first time, I will issue a warning to the student and provide them with an explanation as to why I have suspicions (e.g., use of grammar or vocabulary or structures beyond the expected proficiency level for the course). Additionally, I will deduct 50% of the grade for the assignment. However, if the cheating is extremely evident, no warning will be given and a grade of zero will be assigned. For any subsequent violations, the student will be given an “F” grade for the course or reported to the Dean's Office.
