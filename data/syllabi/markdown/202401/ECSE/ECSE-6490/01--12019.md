## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course introduces methods that leverage the basic analysis techniques learned in Robotics I to develop numerical and algorithmic techniques needed to endow robots with the "intelligence" to devise strategies to solve problems they will encounter. Once these abilities are sufficiently well developed, robots will become safe and autonomous, thus paving the way for pervasive personal robots. Topics include: configuration space representation, cell decomposition, roadmap methods, rapidly-exploring random trees, simultaneous localization and mapping, contact modeling, grasping, and dexterous manipulation.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Students who complete this course satisfactorily will be able to understand and apply the following major topics:  
\- Localization  
\- Kalman Filter  
\- Mapping  
\- Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM)  
\- Motion Planning: Bug Algorithm, Potential Field, Sampling Approach  
\- Distance Calculation, Moving Obstacles  
\- Formation Control  
\- Closed Kinematic Chain  
\- Grasping, Force Control  
\- Point Contact with Friction, Friction cone

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- find a feasible motion plan for a robot manipulator or a mobile robot
- use sensors and landmarks for localization and mapping
- characterize the forward, inverse, and differential kinematics of robots in closed kinematic chains
- design compliance force controller for constrained robots
- criticize a research paper discussing topics related to grasping or mobile robotics

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Course deliverables will consist of written reports for four mini projects, a final project presentation, and quizzes delivered every 1-2 weeks. There will be a research paper summary assignment for 6000-level students

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Even though attendance sheets will not be formally collected, students should try to attend the lectures unless they have a good reason to not to. The lecture recordings are for the reference after the class and they should not be treated as a substitute for in-person attendance.

### Other Course Policies:

Lab Etiquette Lab access is necessary for satisfactory completion of the projects. Managing shared workspaces and resources is an important skill for all professionals. Students will be held to high standards with regard to cleanliness, professionalism, and consideration.  
Following are some of the rules that need to be followed by students.  
\- Card access will be given to JEC 5312 to work on your mini-projects.  
\- Be mindful that other teams must use your station. Coordinate with these teams accordingly. Blocking out large time ranges for multiple days in a row will not be allowed.  
\- Food and drink are not allowed in the lab space. The equipment is new and sensitive. Damage resulting from spilled food or drink will result in the revocation of lab privileges.  
\- If lab cleanliness overall becomes a problem, lab hours may be restricted or revoked for those deemed responsible.  
Any concerns regarding lab etiquette should be brought to the attention of the instructor as soon as possible.  
Late Submissions: Because there might be unexpected events (e.g., work or family commitments) there is 0% penalty for 48 hours (no questions asked) for late assignment submissions. After this period students would get 0 credits. Please note that this rule only applies to mini-projects and 6000-level supplemental assignments. You can not get bonus points if you have submit late. Quizzes and final presentations should be submitted on the posted due date, after which students would get 0 credits. If you get sick, communicate this to the instructor before the posted due date so the necessary extensions can be granted.  
Grade Appeals: Requests for regrade must be made (in writing) within 72 hours of the grade being communicated to the student. Any concerns regarding overall course performance must be communicated to the instructor as soon as possible. Final grades will not be modified once published in SIS unless there is a demonstrable error made by the instructor (i.e. a calculation error).
