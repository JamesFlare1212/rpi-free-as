## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course covers tools and methods for the analysis and synthesis of linear multivariable con- trol systems. The emphasis is on modern multi-input/multi-output control system design using state space and frequency methods, systematic consideration of robustness, disturbance rejection, and performance, model reduction, and system identification. Computational tools such as the MATLAB robust control toolbox and system identification toolbox will be used in the course.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Describe an LTI MIMO control system in state space and transfer function matrix forms.
- Describe conditions for stability, passivity, small gain, controllability, and observability, and apply the conditions to a given LTI MIMO system.
- Compute robust stability bounds using Nyquist, H∞, passivity, μ measure.
- Reduce the model order of an LTI system by using Hankel singular values.
- Construct an MIMO LTI model based on the input and output data.
- Construct a feedback stabilizing controller that optimize the closed-loop H2 or H∞ norms.
- Formulate H∞ and H2 optimization problems by using the linear matrix inequality (LMI) technique.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty. Familiarize yourself with these definitions. Three relevant academic integrity violations to empha- size include:

- Unallowed Collaboration: Collaboration is defined as deliberately facilitating an act of aca- demic dishonesty in any way or form; for example, allowing another student to observe an exam paper or allowing another student to ”recycle” one’s old term paper or using one an- other’s work in a paper or lab report without citing it as another’s work.

- Copying: Copying is defined as obtaining information pertaining to a graded exercise by deliberately observing the paper of another student; for example, noting which alternative a neighboring student has circled on a multiple-choice exam.

- Plagiarism: Plagiarism is defined as representing the work or words of another as one’s own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference. Examples include using sentences verbatim from a published source in a term paper without appropriate referencing, or pre- senting as one’s own the detailed argument of a published source, or presenting as one’s own electronically or digitally enhanced graphic representations from any form of media.

  Project report submission must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received or given (including by any digital tools, such as AI), a notation should be included to indi- cate the collaboration. Usage of materials (books, internet sites, etc.) outside the listed resources should be cited. Evidence of academic dishonesty will result in a zero for the associate report to the Dean of Students for possible additional actions.
