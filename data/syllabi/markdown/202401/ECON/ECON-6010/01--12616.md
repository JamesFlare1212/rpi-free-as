## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Microeconomics studies individual decision-making and its implications for various market phenomena. The intent of the course is to provide a rigorous development of the economic and analytical tools to study individual economic decisions (e.g., how consumers make choices under resource constraints with or without uncertainty, how firms make decisions subject to technology constraints, etc.), market behavior (e.g., competitive markets and imperfectly competitive markets), information economics (e.g. adverse selection and moral hazard), market design (e.g. auctions, matching, etc.). Policy applications include the economics of insurance, international trade, taxation, and other topics.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- After completing the course, the student will be able to: 1. Demonstrate knowledge of fundamental concepts in microeconomics. 2. Develop an ability to model economic behavior in a structured mathematical framework. 3. Apply microeconomic theory to evaluate public policy and translate theory into meaningful policy predictions and recommendations. 4. Evaluate the performance of classical microeconomic theory and understand its strengths and weaknesses.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Problem sets, in-class quizzes, two midterm exams, and a final exam.
