## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

## This course provides an introduction to experimental methods in economics. It teaches you how to test economic theories, as well as introducing you to the experimental methods that are used to investigate questions that may not be possible to study using existing data sets. Students will be introduced to state-of-the-art research methodology in experimental economics. Although not required, it is highly suggested that students take a statistics or econometrics course before taking this course.

***Leading Paper Discussion:***

- During each of the paper discussion days, we will discuss two papers in class. Each paper discussion will be led by 1-3 students (i.e. leaders) (depending on the class size). These papers will be chosen from a list of papers (provided by me) by discussion leaders.
- Discussion leaders will read their assigned paper very carefully (including the online appendices and experimental instructions, if available) and lead a discussion of the paper in class. They will come to class prepared with slides and a list of questions that you want to ask to class that will guide the discussion of the paper.
- In addition to their assigned paper, leaders are responsible to read the introduction of the other paper to be discussed and participate in the discussion.

***Grades:*** Your grade will be determined based on i) how well you understood the paper, and ii) your ability to foster an active and engaging discussion environment.

***Discussion Participation:***

- During each of the paper discussion days, we will discuss two papers in class. Half of the class will be assigned to one paper and the other half will be assigned to the other one. You will be responsible for reading your assigned paper very carefully. Additionally, you are also expected to read the introduction section of the other paper.
- Whether it is your assigned paper or not, all students (including leaders) are expected to participate in the discussion of both papers. Students are expected take an active role by asking leaders questions, sharing their thoughts, and making comments.

***Grades:*** Your grade will be determined by the quality of your participation during the paper discussions. I will give you a grade at the end of each discussion day using the following table. Your final grade will be the average of these grades.

| **95-100** | You participated substantially in the discussion of both papers and asked thoughtful questions in each discussion |

***Research Project (******ECON 6360 Students******):***

You will be put in a group with other graduate students (if available), and each group will be responsible for conducting one experiment. More detailed instructions to come, but here are the main parts.

1. Come up with potential research questions. Think about multiple research questions that you are interested in studying and think about potential experimental designs to study those questions.

**Follow this strategy:**

1. Each group member does their own research on the topics that they are interested in studying and writes 2 or 3 short research proposals. In these proposals, they introduce a research question of interest, provide a motivation of studying that question, provide a quick literature review of the topic, and discuss experimental design idea(s) to study that question. Each proposal should be about 1 page long.
2. Once all group members have prepared their proposals, come together as a group and discuss each of these. Decide whether the question is interesting and whether it is doable as an in-class experiment and choose 2-3 of these ideas as potential ideas.

2. *Submit your research ideas* (**due Feb 29th Noon**). Decide on two or three research question(s) that interest you and provide research proposal drafts for each of these ideas for my review. You will prepare a one-pager for each idea (you must use the template that I will provide) and email them to me by due date. I will provide written feedback. We can schedule a meeting to further discuss if needed.
3. *Submit your main research proposal* (**due March 13th Noon**). Once you decided which research question to study, you will motivate that question, review the literature, and provide a very detailed experimental design. In light of the feedback you will receive from me, you will move to the next step.
4. *Prepare Experimental Materials* (**due March 25th**). This means that you will have to prepare all the materials that you will use to run your experiment (e.g. google forms). You will add me as a collaborator on your google form and share the screenshots (from the participants’ perceptive not the experimenter’s) in a word document. I will go over your materials and provide feedback. You will revise your materials if needed. You will receive my feedback only twice, so make it count!
5. *Conduct the experiment*. Your group will conduct the entire experiment. You will explain the instructions to the class, run the experiment, keep records, collect the data etc.
6. *Organize your data and analyze it*. Organize your data and start analyzing it. You should start working on your final project write-up and presentation slides. Meet with your TA or me if you need assistance.
7. *Present your findings in class*. This includes presenting your motivation and research question, summary tables and graphs, calculating useful statistics, performing statistical tests, explaining any relevant theory, comparing the experiments to others we have done in class and discussing the conclusions you draw from the experiment. I will provide a template for this presentation.

*Evaluation*. At the end of your presentation, your classmates will evaluate your research. I will incorporate these evaluations to determine your grade.

8. *Final Project Write-up*. You will update and extend your research proposal, incorporate your findings, and submit it (along with your experimental materials and your raw data). Consider this write-up as an academic paper. This is the final product of your hard work!

*Evaluation*. We will have an in-group evaluation. This means that each group member will evaluate one another. I will incorporate these evaluations to determine your grade from the group project.

**Research Project Grades (ECON 6360):**

Students will earn numerical grades using the following weighting:

*Meeting deadlines and overall group preparedness (5%)*

You are expected to meet all deadlines. You will be penalized for each deadline you miss.

*Quality of Submitted Proposals (20%)*

The quality of your idea pitch and research proposals, how closely you follow instructions, as well as how well you revise your proposal based on the feedback you receive will help me determine your grade.

*Quality of Experimental Materials (20%)*

The quality of your experimental materials submitted during the first round and how closely you follow my instructions to revise them during the second round will help me determine your grade.

*Presentation (30%)*

The quality of your presentation and how closely you follow my instructions will help me determine your grade.

*Final Project Write up (30%)*

The overall quality of your write-up, data analysis and presentation, how well you follow my instructions etc. will help me determine your grade.
