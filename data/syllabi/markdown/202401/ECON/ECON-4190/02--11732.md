## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- proficiency in the fundamental economic concepts and theory used in international economics
- proficiency in the empirical methods and data analysis used in international economics
- the ability to use economic theory and empirical evidence to analyze the impact of international trade and globalization on output, incomes and welfare
- the ability to apply economic theory and empirical evidence to the critical evaluation of international institutions and economic policy related to international trade and globalization

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1. Problem sets:

   Problem sets will accompany our main topics. I will post solutions to each problem set after the due date. I encourage working through assignments in groups, but all solutions must be written and submitted individually. I expect submitted assignments to be legible and clear. This may require transcribing initial work into a final version to turn in. Typesetting solutions is encouraged, but not required.

2. In-class exercises: We will occasionally pause lecture to work on practice problems or discuss an issue in small groups. Students will submit their individual work at the end of the exercise. In-class exercises are graded on a completion basis only. All students drop their lowest exercise score (e.g. due to unexcused absence).

3. Short written assignments

   Over the course of the semester, I will assign a few short written reports on a pre-specified topic. For example, I may ask you to evaluate a specific news article, political platform, or opinion piece using the concepts we develop in class. These assignments must be completed individually and written in your own words.

4. Project

   Students will select a single event or policy related to international economics to explore in depth in a written paper. The paper will include a description of the chosen topic using the language of economics, a synthesis of existing discussion/debate, and your original critical evaluation of the topic.

5. Exams

   There will be three exams over the course of the semester, each covering roughly one third of the course. Exams will be written, closed book exams. I will give at least two weeks notice prior to an exam date.

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
