## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

An exploration of models of decision-making under alternative assumptions, expanding on “rational” models of introductory economics. The course will discuss the assumptions of these standard models and their shortcomings. The shortcomings will be addressed through the exploration of limited attention and considerations of fairness, uncertainty, and strategic interaction. Applications in areas such as consumer and household finance, natural resource management, and public policy will be emphasized throughout.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

My grading philosophy is that your letter (and percentage) grade should reflect your *mastery of knowledge in economics*. Note that formative work (problem sets, in-class exercises, general study) doesn’t necessarily reflect your mastery of these topics. That being said, I recognize that you probably need *some* incentive to do the interim work. So your grade will be 20% *formative* (stuff you do to learn) and 80% *summative* (stuff you do to prove what you’ve learned).

Formative:

- Participation, misc. 15%
  - o : random tasks throughout the semester that I feel need incentivization. These will include online submissions of survey responses, in-class exercises, reference searches, etc. These tasks will be announced in advance and it will be explicit that they are included in this category for grading.
- Problem Sets 20%
  - o Drop 2 lowest scores

Summative:

- Paper Presentation 20%
- Modeling Project 45%

**Appeals**: you are of course permitted to advocate for yourself in your grading if you feel that something has been graded incorrectly. However, in order to contain the madness, you will have *one week* to submit an appeal to me, in writing. In order to do so, you must:

1. Send an email to me with the subject line “Models Grade Appeal: [Assignment]”
   1. Emails without this subject line will be disregarded.
2. In the email, please include:
   1. The assignment in question
   2. What your response was (include as an attachment, photo, etc.)
   3. The grade you were assigned

Your *precise justification* for why you think you deserve more points.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

I will not be taking attendance. That being said, problem sets will be assigned (typically) each week and are on material covered in lecture. There will also be little time for you to come to office hours between when problem sets are assigned and when they are due. So this effectively works as a means to make sure you are i) here and ii) paying attention. That being said, I don’t intrinsically care about whether you are physically in class on any given day.

If you miss any in-class graded task (paper presentation, modelling project component, etc.), you can make it up *only if* you have a valid, documented excuse as per RPI policy. You have *one week* from the date the missing grade is posted to LMS to make it up. You cannot make up in-class quizzes – they are simply excluded from your grade if you present a valid documented excuse. If you miss any in-class quiz and do not present a valid documented excuse within 10 days from the date the activity was held, your grade for such a quiz will be equal to zero.

### Other Course Policies:

## Course Assessment Measures

You will be assessed on the above learning outcomes using a combination of quizzes, problem sets, examinations, written exercises, and a research project. Specifically, you’re going to be asked to complete the following:

- Participation: come to class prepared to participate in discussion of topics. Ask questions about the lecture material. Participate meaningfully in small group exercises in class.
- Problem Sets: these problem sets will ask you to solve models introduced in class and interpret your results. Typically, these would happen each week and will consist of one problem each.
- Paper Presentations: in small groups, you will present one applied paper on a topic in this course. We will discuss these presentations in more detail in class.
- Modeling Project: to solidify your understanding of economic models, you will complete a project intended to deepen your understanding of economic modeling
  - If you are enrolled in the 4000-level class, you will work in pairs of two students to research a model **not** discussed elsewhere in the course and present it to the class
  - If you are enrolled in the 6000-level class, you will work to develop **your own** model, write up your findings, and present it to the class
  - More details about this project will be shared in class, separate from this syllabus.
