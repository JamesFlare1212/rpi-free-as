## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is designed to help students build a solid ground in data analytics and policy evaluation. Particular attention is given to the analysis of ‘panel’ or ‘longitudinal’ data sets. Topics to be studied include fixed effects and random effects models and their estimation and inference in the linear panel data framework. With a range of applications from the literature, we will also discuss some recently developed econometric techniques for policy evaluation. In particular, quantile regression will be introduced to investigate the heterogeneous treatment effects. Some other recent advances in data analytics including LASSO and adaptive LASSO will be briefly introduced.

### Content Details:

1. Introduction to Panel Data
2. Fixed Effects Models
3. Random Effects Models
4. Dynamic Panel Data Models
5. Test of Hypothesis with Panel Data
6. Quantile Regression and Its Applications
7. Shrinkage Methods with Many Regressors
8. Statistical Inference Using the Bootstrap
9. Other topics may include:
   - Partial Least Squares
   - Double Machine Learning

**Guidance for Writing a Reading Report:**

1. **Introduction**

- **Paper Information:** Start by providing the full citation of the research paper, including the title, authors, journal or source, publication date, and any relevant publication information.
- **Purpose:** Explain why you chose this particular paper. What specific aspects or econometric techniques does it address that are relevant to the course?

2. **Summary**

- **Overview:** Summarize the paper's main objectives, research questions, and findings.
- **Methodology:** Describe the econometric methods used in the paper. Include information about the model specifications, data sources, and estimation techniques.
- **Key Results:** Highlight the key empirical results and their implications. Mention any hypothesis testing or statistical inference conducted by the authors.

3. **Critical Analysis**

- **Modeling and Data Analysis**: Analyze the econometric model(s) used in the paper. Discuss the appropriateness of the models and their relevance to the research questions.
- **Data Interpretation:** Evaluate the quality and relevance of the data used. Discuss any data issues, such as missing data, outliers, or potential sources of bias.
- **Statistical Techniques:** Assess the statistical techniques employed by the authors. Were they used correctly? Did the authors address potential endogeneity, multicollinearity, or heteroscedasticity?
- **Interpretation:** Offer your interpretation of the results. What do the findings mean in the context of the paper's research question, and how do they contribute to the field of applied econometrics?

4. **Application**

- **Real-World Application:** Discuss how the econometric techniques and findings from the paper could be applied to real-world economic or policy issues.
- **Relevance to Course Concepts:** Explain how the concepts, models, or techniques presented in the paper relate to what you've learned in the course so far.

5. **Conclusion**

- **Summary and Recommendation:** Summarize your report's main points and whether you would recommend this paper to your classmates for its contribution to understanding applied econometrics. Explain why or why not.

**Grading Criteria for the Reading Report:**

- Comprehension (20%): How well did the student understand the paper's objectives, methods, and findings?
- Analysis (40%): To what extent did the student critically evaluate the econometric modeling, data analysis, and interpretation in the paper?
- Application (20%): Did the student effectively apply the concepts from the paper to real-world situations and relate them to the course material?
- Clarity and Organization (10%): How well was the report structured and presented? Were ideas clear and logically arranged?
- Engagement (5%): Did the student actively engage with the material, demonstrating a thoughtful and in-depth examination?
- Grammar and Style (5%): How well did the student write and proofread the report in terms of grammar, spelling, and overall writing style?
