## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Course Objectives By the end of this course, students will demonstrate that they: • Understand literary texts, their conventions and techniques. • Understand the interplay of form and content. • Have improved their oral and written communication skills • Can perform critical thinking. These outcomes will be assessed through student performance in class discussion and in formal essays.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Essays (4-5 pages)

There will be prompts for 6 texts and you will be asked to write 3 essays in total.

Resubmission: Any grade below an A- on Essay#1 will require a Revision and resubmission.

**Class Participation**

Not surprisingly, the participation grade is based on your contribution to class discussion. To do well, make sure you have completed the assigned reading and have thoughts or questions ready to share.

Periodically, there will be short debates or other exercises that will require minimal prep outside class.

**Class Presentation**

Each of you will be asked to initiate the discussion once during the semester by giving a focused

**7-10 minute** **PowerPoint** (or similar) **presentation**. If there is a particular time you would like to make this report, let me know in advance and I will try to accommodate you.

*Tip: A good presentation will avoid mere plot summary and will focus on the assigned topic. It will demonstrate that you are familiar with the text, that you have given the topic some thought, and that you can articulate your ideas clearly.*

## Policies

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

*The* *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* and *The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement* define various forms of academic dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent your own work or designated group’s work. Note: only the project is collaborative. **If you plagiarize once,** you will receive an **F on the assignment.** If you plagiarize again, you will **fail the entire course** and may be reported to the *Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education* as appropriate. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask me for clarification. The basics: when using information from another source **(including anonymous online sources**) always cite the original text or URL and distinguish it from your own commentary. If you directly cut and paste another person’s language, you need to use it as a **quotation**.
