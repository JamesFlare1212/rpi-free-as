## Course Goals

### Goals:

- Explore HCI testing and evaluation processes from both a scientific and heuristic perspective using real world case studies.
- Understand key issues in human and computer interactions.
- Communicate with a variety of *real audiences* in different written and spoken generic forms.
- Achieve a *variety of purposes* – provide perspective on an experience, showcase your experiences and skills, and propose a creative, innovative solution to positively impact the world.
- Practice creating and making effective oral presentations and being attentive and aware designers.
- Become active and close readers of challenging texts within the field of HCI.
- Become more aware of the choices you and others are making with technology.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

This course will utilize a series of curated readings and in class discussions to explore theories about HCI and its peripherals. We will be discussing and working through the primary question: ***where are we (location) and where are we going (speculation) in this technological moment?*** As a project-based course you will also be better able to complete group tasks and gain valuable experience working with real life technological case studies. This semester we will be working on the theme of environmental impact. Projects, readings, and topics will explore how we can think about our work as contributing to positively impacting our current ecological crisis. We have chosen this theme as technology has and continues to play a double role in providing solutions and problems for our planet. The hope is that you leave this class with a better understanding of how you can personally positively impact the current global crisis. We will also be addressing a diversity of subordinate topics such as aesthetics, semiotics, iterative design, social praxis, storytelling, bio-art, post-humanism and cyborg theory, artificial intelligence, computational ethics, and others.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Analyze technological artifacts. Identify specific ways technological mediation impacts knowledge, needs, emotions, values, and experiences that are both individual and social-cultural in origin. • Engage clients. Learn to more deeply see and listen to your future client’s needs more deeply while guiding them to more successful outcomes. • Explain your ideas in ways that reflect a thorough understanding of the subject and your audience’s needs, knowledge, values, etc. by anticipating and answering the audience’s likely questions and elaborating appropriately (i.e. to consider things your audience takes as “givens”). • Explain appropriate technology, not only in your design processes but in the kinds of questions you answer or avoid, and the kinds of information you choose to include and exclude. • Practice and hone your speaking and listening communication skills in both one-on-one, small group, and large group settings. • Reflect on how you want technology to function. • Begin to establish a toolkit of “practices” that you can take with you for designing your own content and analyzing the work of others.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**ASSESSMENTS**

The deliverables of this class are as follows.

1. **Short Paper: Accessing Hardware Life Cycle (10%) total points 1-10.**

Recently technological scholarship has suggested that the materials we use to make our technology are both extracted and disposed of in unecological ways. Through “planned obsolescence” the waste created by our devices is being dumped into our oceans and on the shores of developing nations. This short paper requires you explore the life cycle of a common product identifying how hardware negatively impacts our ecosystems. In this short paper you will identify a specific product, where the waste ends up, and offer suggestions of how these products could be designed in a more ecologically sensitive way. Product examples,

iPhone

Microchips

Screens

TVS

HDMI cables

Silicone

Laptop

1. This short paper will be 500 words.

2. **Midterm: Midterm will be multiple choice engaging the engaging and information from class (20%) total points 1-40 weighted.** The information will be pulled from a general understanding of the arguments found within the readings. There is no way to remember “everything” so we will be adding in hints and moments of attention throughout the semester. Paying attention to these hints will save you time and frustration when you sit down to study. This course builds upon itself so you will need to keep up with your readings as it is difficult to do it any other way.

3. **In class group reading reflections (15%) total Points 1-15.** We will have multiple group-based reading responses to keep us all engaged and on our toes. *Questions will be pulled from readings for that day*. We will teach you what a good reading reflection should look like.

4. **Group Project One: Experimental Design: Speculative Design (20%) total points 1-20.**
   1. Building from your readings, this project asks that your group proposes a speculative design for the betterment of the environment using any imagined human computer interaction. This design could be web based, robotic, biomimetic, AI based, or cybernetic. These designs do not need to be built, only proposed. You will do an in-semester group presentation that will be 3M with an image/diagram of your design. This image/diagram can be computer or hand rendered but cannot be an already existing image from the internet. There will be no written component to this project. You will be graded on your ability to articulate the design and the strength of your proposal by the instructional team. You will also be required to present your design to the class as part of your critique to practice your presentation skills.

5. **Final Project Group Project: User Experience Study (20%) total points 1-20.** As a group you will pick one of the case studies offered by the instructional staff to run a complete UX study using one of the many methods learned in class.
   1. This semester we are going to focus on local business and organizations that are helping contribute to solving climate change. Particular attention was given to projects in need that can benefit from our work. The goal of this project is to offer a concrete and useful offering to these projects. At the end of the semester, you will be sending your reports to these clients. The goal of this project is to overview issues and opportunities that can enhance the success of these clients’ projects.
   2. This project will use APA format: It will require an abstract, introduction, literature review (articles from class can be used), results sections, and a discussion. For more details on APA formatting see Purdue owl (APA).
   3. These papers will be group based and thus will be 20 pages in total in length including a reference page and cover page/letter.
   4. The aim of the project is to help these clients and support their mission through UX.

**GRADING**

To help you achieve the goals and outcomes for this course, I will introduce you to research and theory about how technology is thought about and used. Based on the understanding of the theoretical background that supports this course, you’ll learn strategies you can use to communicate the concepts effectively with your audience for any communication practice—oral, written, or visual—in any situation, both in classrooms and in your career. These strategies are not algorithms or recipes that can be followed blindly: They are actions and choices people routinely take and make—sometimes consciously, habitually, and/or intuitively—in communicating effectively. In your assignments, therefore, you’re expected to:

- Demonstrate critical thinking concerning technology
- Organize communication about your theories and ideas coherently and cohesively
- Produce grammatically and syntactically appropriate written and oral communication (including using Standard Academic English when appropriate and warranted)
- Appraise the communication of peers
- Revise texts as part of the writing process and in response to peer and instructor feedback
- Reflect on your own work in terms of the learning outcomes

In grading all of your work, I’ll ask four basic questions:

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

*The* *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* and *The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement* define various forms of academic dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade *must represent your own work*. **If you plagiarize once,** you will receive an **F on the assignment.** If you plagiarize again, you will **fail the entire course** and may be reported to the *Dean of Students* as appropriate. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask me for clarification. The basics: when using information from another source **(including anonymous online sources**) always cite the original text or URL and distinguish it from your own commentary. If you directly cut and paste another person’s language, you need to use it as a **quotation**. *We will talk specifically about citation styles and how to make sure that you are giving “credit” when it is required of you in spirit of scholarly collaboration.* In regard to using AI (e.g. ChatGPT or other Open AI resources): While it may be tempting to use this resource, please note that there is no “check” to ensure the information that you are receiving is correct. Researchers are finding that ChatGPT can provide misinformation. Using this resource is NOT considered *your own work* and **will** be deemed as an instance of academic dishonesty
