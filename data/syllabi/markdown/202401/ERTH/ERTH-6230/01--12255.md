## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Climate change is driving an increase in extreme weather events (storms, drought, wildfire). These events manifest as episodic disturbances to affected ecosystems that can alter carbon and nutrient fluxes and reorganize biogeochemical cycles. This course is an in-depth study of current peer-reviewed literature addressing biogeochemical responses to environmental disturbance and extreme events across varied spatial and temporal scales.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Determine what constitutes an “extreme event” and differentiate disturbance from baseline conditions
- Predict how environmental disturbances propagate along river networks and the land-ocean continuum
- Synthesize and present current primary literature on how extreme events alter carbon and nutrient fluxes
- Explain and appraise the logistical and analytical challenges inherent to measuring and interpreting the immediate and legacy impacts of extreme events on biogeochemical processes
- Design and critique an environmental monitoring system for quantifying biogeochemical responses to extreme events

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

This graduate level course is designed to enhance students’ ability to digest and synthesize scientific literature and to think critically about relevant scientific research topics. In addition, familiarization with a mechanistic, process-based approach to understanding how environmental disturbance and extreme events alter biogeochemical responses more broadly will aid in formulating testable hypotheses that can be applied to constraining the modern carbon cycle, tackling engineering challenges, and evaluating water quality and resource management. In doing so, students will be empowered to design and conduct their own independent research. Student progress will be evaluated through performance on weekly discussion leads, discussion participation, designing an ideal monitoring system for quantifying extreme event biogeochemistry, and a final presentation and critique of the monitoring system designed. No final exam will be given.
