### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

This course will cover the exploration of hydrothermal experimental methods as they are considered in different fields such as geology, chemistry, materials sciences, biofuels, sustainability, and more. Coursework will consist of lectures covering background material related to hydrothermal chemistry and processes alternating with class discussions of student-led literature review focused on individualized student interests. Assignments related to the literature review and class participation in these discussions comprise the major components of class assessment.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

*Course Assessment Measures (*CHEM 4965 & ERTH 4965*)*

This course is designed to be a combination of lecture, literature review, and discussion in which students develop the skills to find, read, understand, and discuss the scientific literature related to the field of hydrothermal methods. As such, student participation is a critical part of achieving these outcomes. Students are expected to read relevant articles resulting from literature search prior to every other class meeting to discuss and compare their findings to results found by the rest of the class. Therefore, active participation and attendance comprise half of the student’s final grade in the course.

| • In-class participation (40%) | • Written assignments (50%) |
| --- | --- |
| • Attendance (10%) |  |

*Course Assessment Measures (*CHEM 6965 & ERTH 6965*)*

This graduate level course is designed not only to enhance students’ comprehension and

presentation skills, but also to help students develop the skills to lead scientific discussions, think critically about scientific research, and integrate new scientific results into their knowledgebase of methods relevant to their own research. Furthermore, the structure of the course will give students the chance to collect relevant literature and then synthesize that information into a final project combining what they have learned into either a summary of a specific technique, or a proposal for how they would design a new technique for use in their research.

| • In-class participation (30%) | • Written assignments (40%) |
| --- | --- |
| • Final project presentation (20%) | • Attendance (10%) |

All students **(4965/6965-level)** will be responsible for a weekly literature search followed by in class group discussion.

The written assignment – due on the day of discussion – must contain the following filled in sections (1) Title of article, (2) First author, (3) Year published, (4) Journal, (5) DOI url (if available), (6) Research area, (7) Summary of article and key points.

A template for the written component of the assignment will be provided.

The final project presentation is required for **6965-level** students and will consist of a 10 - 15 minute presentation synthesizing papers explored by that individual student during the course. For the presentation, the student will either (1) establish that the current techniques needed for their research already exist, how those techniques are currently used, and how to build or purchase the needed reactor system or (2) how the reactor necessary does not yet exist and a proposal for how to combine techniques and methods from different pools of literature to design a reactor for their needs. Class discussion will immediately follow the presentations, including suggestions for possible ways to improve the design and future directions of the work. Literature review beyond what which was covered for class assignments is in fact encouraged in both types of presentation.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

***A special note on plagiarism.*** Please see the definition of plagiarism given below, as outlined in The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights. To be clear, any time material that is not the students’ own is used, it should be in quotes and contain a citation. If a student paraphrases work from elsewhere, it also requires a citation. Furthermore, even when citations are used, others’ work cannot be used as a student response on an assignment. For example, in this class, if the main conclusions are given in an article, they cannot be used verbatim as a response in the post-discussion evaluation, even if a citation is present.

*“**Plagiarism:** Representing the work or words of another as one’s own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference. For example, using sentences verbatim from a published source in a term paper without appropriate referencing, or presenting as one’s own the detailed argument of a published source, or presenting as one’s own electronically or digitally enhanced graphic representations from any form of media.”* – ACADEMIC INTEGRITY (2014-2016 Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights & Responsibilities, August 2014)
