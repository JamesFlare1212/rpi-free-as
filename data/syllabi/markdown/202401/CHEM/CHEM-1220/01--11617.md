## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate a proficient knowledge in the core areas of chemistry (i.e., analytical, inorganic, organic, and physical) in order to prepare themselves for advanced courses in the various subject areas of science and engineering.
- Solve quantitative problems in chemistry and chemical systems using fundamental concepts of chemistry and mathematics.
- Effectively communicate ideas and inquiries related to topics in chemistry to professional chemists in academia and in industry.
- Identify and describe mechanisms for simple elementary organic reactions. Substitution, and elimination reactions (SN1, SN2, E1, and E2) for alkyl halides.
- Demonstrate safe and proper chemistry laboratory techniques relating to mass measurements, volume measurements, solution preparation, dilution, titration, elementary separation, and synthesis methods.
- Collect, analyze experimental data, and determine experimental errors using various scientific methods.
- Identify and use scientific literature and other reputable sources as references for experimentation and research.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Academic Integrity:**

The relationship between students and faculty is based upon trust and the continued maintenance of this trust is necessary for education to be successful. Students need to trust faculty to make appropriate judgments about the content and structure of the course. Faculty members need to trust that the work turned in by students represents their own effort. Violation of this trust undermines the educational process. For example, as professionals, you may be responsible in designing products (aircraft, the computers that run them, or the runways on which they land) where lives are at stake. As a result, there is no tolerance for any breach of academic integrity such as cheating, plagiarizing, or inappropriate sharing of laboratories or quizzes. (e.g., Using someone else’s REEF code is considered cheating.)

Cheating can include sharing answers, as well as stealing answers and looking up answers online during exams. Plagiarism means copying words from someone’s work, even if you “change the sentence a bit.” If you share your laboratory report, you are as guilty as the person copying it. This includes copying results and answers from previous years’ lab reports. If you do use material from an appropriate source, make sure you reference it properly in your reports. The Rensselaer Student Handbook and the Dean of Students Office Webpage (http://doso.rpi.edu/update.do?artcenterkey=676) defines various forms of academic dishonesty. Please familiarize yourself with these and ask us if you have questions about what constitutes academic dishonesty.

You will immediately receive a grade of an “F” if you are caught cheating during an exam. One strike and you are OUT!

**Lectures:**

There is an 80-minute lecture delivered by your course professor on Mondays and Thursdays. The material presented in the lecture will cover and expand on the material in the required reading assignments. The pace of the lectures will be very fast. Reading the textbook and doing the prelecture assignments prior to the start of each chapter are paramount in doing well in the class. In addition, you MUST attend lectures “in-person” by answering clicker questions in order to receive credits for lecture participation. All lectures will be streamed “live” and “recorded”. All links are located on the LMS.

**Discussion/Recitation Participation (CHEM1200/1220 only):** During these sessions, your discussion faculty member will go through several problems related to the current lecture topics. At the beginning of each discussion session, you will need to participate in discussion clicker questions in order to receive credits for discussion. Make sure to logon to i>clicker REEF with your laptop or a smart device to answer these questions. Discussions will last approximately one hour and will be held “in-person” during the first hour of each lab period. If you missed the questions, you must contact the discussion faculty member to schedule a makeup.

For CHEM1210 students, discussion sessions are HIGHLY recommended!

**Laboratory (CHEM1200/1220 only):** Chemistry is an experimental science. Labs will be performed “in-person” in Walker Laboratory. All pre-labs are handed in prior to each lab “in-person”. The lab simulations found on *Achieve* will focus primarily on applying your lecture gained knowledge to a hands-on but virtual laboratory experience. After completing the experiment, you will analyze the data, perform any necessary calculations, and answer all post-lab questions. Experimental data is not to be shared – the data you record for your experiment is the data that you will be using for your laboratory report. You will need a lab notebook to record the data and write down any observations, and perform your calculations. All excused laboratory absences must be cleared and rescheduled through Dr. Morrissey. (NOT your lab instructor).

Three-Strike Policy for UNEXCUSED Absences:

1. One absence with no makeup will result in a grade of “zero” for the corresponding lab session.
2. Two absences with no makeup will result in a grade of “zero” for the entire lab portion of the final grade (25%).
3. Three absences with no makeup will result in an “F” for the entire course.

Students in CHEM1200 expected to collaborate with your lab partner(s) to perform the experiment and collect the data. However, each lab partner is expected to analyze the results and work on the post lab questions independently. Students in CHEM1220 will be performed their experiments individually.

Make sure your post lab assignments are in your own words. Anyone who is caught copying from someone else’s work or work from past years will be subjected to academic disciplinary actions as follows:

Three-Strike Policy for PLAGARISM:

1. The first infraction will result in a grade of “zero” for the corresponding prelab and/or post lab with a verbal and a written warning.
2. The second infraction will result in a grade of “zero” for the entire lab portion of the final grade (25%). A second written warning will also be issued.
3. The third infraction will result in an “F” for the entire course.

If you missed or need to miss an experiment due to an excused absence, please contact Dr. Morrissey to reschedule either to another lab section and/or a separate time. All experiment with legitimate excuses must be made up.
