## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to interpret IR, MS, UV, NMR spectra and determine molecular structures from spectra and other information together. More details from the instructor's knowledge of spectroscopy that are not included in the text will also be covered.
- Students will demonstrate knowledge of the reactions of carboxylic acid derivatives and the mechanisms of those reactions. Students will demonstrate knowledge of the reactions of aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acid derivatives (either saturated or unsaturated). Students will demonstrate knowledge of the reactivity of carbonyl alpha carbons, and be able to demonstrate the use of enol/enolate chemistry in synthesis.
- Students will be able to explain why benzene has different reactivity patterns than non-aromatic olefins, and write mechanisms for a variety of known reactions and conditions. Students will be able to predict the reactivity pattern (and necessary conditions) to add or remove functional groups from substituted benzene. Students will demonstrate knowledge of the reactivity and selectivity patterns of aromatic compounds.
- Students will demonstrate knowledge of the characteristics, reactivities and synthesis of amines and heterocycles.
- Students will demonstrate knowledge of catalysis in organic reactions and different types of catalysis.
- Students will demonstrate knowledge of synthetic polymers, including their synthesis, characterization and specialized properties.
- Students will demonstrate knowledge about the nomenclature, structures and reactivities of common carbohydrates.
- Over the course of this second semester of Organic Chemistry, students will be able to review and better understand the topics covered in Organic Chemistry I.

**Recitation Activities**

These activities will be distributed, completed, and collected during the recitation period. Therefore, you will be graded on completion, not correctness. If you complete every question with a relevant, thoughtful answer you will receive full credit. If most students do not have enough time to finish the activity, grades will be adjusted to reflect that. Some of the recitation activities may require the use of a laptop.

**Homework**

Homework will be assigned for each chapter we complete and will be turned in using Achieve. Late assignments will be penalized at 10% per day late. It is well established that students learn much more through active learning than through passive learning. Attending lectures and taking notes in lecture, and reading the textbook are passive learning, while working homework problems is active learning. Therefore, most of your learning will take place while you work on homework problems. Some students may find that the assigned Achieve homework is sufficient for them to master the concepts we study. Others will need more practice before they achieve full mastery. I strongly recommend that you try textbook exercises to supplement the Achieve assignments since the answers are available to you in the solution manual. I will not assign specific problems from the textbook, and they will not be graded. It is up to you to assess your own understanding, and to work enough exercises to achieve your desired level of mastery.

**Quizzes**

Each quiz will be semi-cumulative. Approximately half (or more) of the points on each quiz will be for questions involving material that has not yet been quizzed. The other half of the points will be distributed amongst previous topics. The reason for this is to discourage cramming and ensure that you achieve long-term learning of Organic Chemistry. No subject can be retained long-term if you cram the material. To do well on quizzes, you will therefore need to briefly review as much as a month’s worth of material. Hopefully, this will substantially decrease your final exam studying needs, since you will have been reviewing and reinforcing knowledge all semester.

One consequence of this setup is that early in the semester, there will be no older material to cover, so the new material will account for the entirety of the quiz. Late in the semester, some topics will only be quizzed once or twice because we will run out of time. This means that early chapters will be somewhat over-assessed and later chapters will be somewhat under-assessed.

**Final Exam**

The final exam will be cumulative and will occur sometime between **April 29 and May 3** (inclusive). The exact date and time will be set by the Registrar later this semester. Attendance at the final exam is mandatory. A grade of 0% will be given for any unexcused absence and make-ups will not be allowed. Absence from the final exam due to travel is always unexcused.

**Semester Exams**

Each exam will be cumulative, with emphasis on material that has not yet appeared on an exam. The distribution of points will be more variable than on quizzes since a single exam question can be more complicated than a quiz question and incorporate ideas from several chapters.

As with quizzes, this will lead to material being covered early in the semester being assessed more often than material late in the semester. To provide some compensation for this over-emphasis on early material, the contribution of each exam to your final grade increases throughout semester, so that the last exam is worth twice as much as the first.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is mandatory at all class meetings.  
**Attendance and Make-up Opportunities**

Attendance is required at every lecture and recitation. Since it is true that active learning is generally preferable to passive learning, why is attendance important?

The instructors cannot cover all material completely during the lecture time we have. Therefore, they will naturally focus on and emphasize those aspects that are most crucial. It is not always clear which ideas or details are most important from reading a text, where almost all information is given equally thorough treatment. By coming to class, you will learn which ideas are most important, and then you can manage your study and active learning time accordingly.

- Some students will learn easily from reading the text. Others will not immediately connect with the text and will benefit from hearing an alternative treatment of the same material with different worked examples. Most students will find themselves falling in both categories at different times throughout the semester. Coming to class will help you clarify and reinforce the understanding you developed by reading.

**If you are absent for a quiz, recitation activity, or semester exam:**

- If your absence is not excused, you will receive a grade of zero.
- The first time you miss a quiz and provide a valid excuse, no make-up will be given, and your other quizzes will hold more weight toward your final grade. In other words, the missed quiz will not be used toward the quiz portion of your final grade. For second and subsequent missed quizzes with valid excuses, a make-up must be scheduled not more than one week after your absence. In such a case, those students should bring an official document that proves one's legitimate excuse of absence to the Dean of Students who requests a recommendation to the instructor. After discussing with the Dean of Students, the instructors will decide whether a make-up should be given or not. In case a make-up is given, it must be scheduled not more than one week after your absence.
- Activities cannot be made up. If a valid excuse is provided, the corresponding percentage of your final grade will be transferred to quizzes.
- Exams missed with a valid excuse for the date of the exam must be made up not more than one week after your absence.
- If your absence is due to a foreseeable circumstance (such as an athletic event) on the day of the quiz/activity/exam, you must receive permission to be absent at least 48 hours in advance.
- If your absence is due to an unforeseeable event (such as illness or family emergency) on the day of quiz/exam, you must provide proof of the event within one week of returning to classes.
- All excuses will be handled by the Dean of Students. Take documentation of the reason of your absence to the Dean’s office, 4600 Academy Hall, not to me. The Dean’s Office will then contact me.

**If you miss a homework assignment:**

Homework will be penalized 10% for each day it late. Since you have many days to complete each assignment, no excuses will be accepted for late homework apart from excuses which would also result in an incomplete grade for the course (e.g., prolonged illness).

If you experience technical difficulties and cannot log-in or submit your assignment, please contact customer service. They will be much better able to help solve your problem than I will be, and they will contact me with a recommendation to accept late work from you without penalty.

## Other Course-Specific Information

### Additional Course Information:

Organic Chemistry is a difficult course covering an enormous breadth of material at blinding speed. In this second semester of Organic Chemistry, we have 15 weeks to cover 12 chapters of the textbook. Due to holidays and breaks, these 15 weeks contain less than 14 weeks’ worth of class meetings. Our pace through the textbook will be correspondingly rapid. Since much material builds upon the preceding material, it is imperative that you do not fall behind. Disciplined, consistent study habits are necessary for success in this course. We will not generally begin a new topic/chapter on any day of the week. It may therefore help you to structure study efforts based on progress through a chapter instead of a rigid weekly schedule.
