## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Scientific machine learning (SciML) is a core component of artificial intelligence (AI) and a computational technology that can be trained, with scientific data, to augment or automate human skills. This course will introduce students to the theory and tools in SciML that are being used to interpret, analyze and modeling complex systems in science and engineering from the most general viewpoint of dynamical systems, i.e., nonlinear ordinary differential equations (ODE)/ partial differential equations (PDE) systems. Topics include review of linear algebra, singular value decomposition, SINDy, Koopman operator, physics-informed neural network, etc. Students will be expected to work on projects that requires coding experiences in Python and write documents in LATEX.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Goal 1:** Students will achieve a deep understanding of the core principles and techniques in Scientific Machine Learning, including the theory and practical application of methods like singular value decomposition, Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamical Systems (SINDy), Koopman operators, and physics-informed neural networks, as they pertain to analyzing and modeling complex systems through ODEs and PDEs.  
**Goal 2:** Students will become proficient in utilizing advanced computational tools and environments crucial to SciML. They will gain hands-on experience in coding with Python for scientific computing and learn to effectively document and present their work using LaTeX, enhancing their skills in both computational problem-solving and technical communication.  
**Goal 3:** Students will be prepared to apply their knowledge of SciML to real-world problems in science and engineering, demonstrating the ability to innovate and contribute to the field. They will engage in projects that require not just the application of existing methods, but also the adaptation and development of novel solutions to complex dynamical systems challenges.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- After completing the course, students will be able to explain the fundamental principles of Scientific Machine Learning, including its role and application in interpreting and modeling dynamic systems through nonlinear ordinary and partial differential equations.
- Students will be able to implement and critically assess various dimensionality reduction techniques such as Principal Component Analysis (PCA), Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA), and t-SNE, understanding their applications and limitations in real-world datasets.
- Students will gain proficiency in implementing Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamical Systems (SINDy) for discovering governing equations from data, evaluating the effectiveness and accuracy of the identified models.
- Upon completion, students will be able to apply Koopman Operator Theory to complex dynamical systems, interpreting and utilizing the operator to understand high-dimensional nonlinear system behavior.
- Students will be capable of designing, training, and deploying Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) to solve and understand complex engineering problems, demonstrating an ability to integrate physical laws into machine learning frameworks.
- Students will develop strong coding skills in Python specifically for scientific computing, including the use of relevant SciML libraries, and will demonstrate the ability to implement algorithms and solve problems related to the course content.
- Students will learn to effectively communicate complex scientific findings and the outcomes of machine learning models, demonstrating proficiency in writing detailed reports and documentation using LaTeX.
- Students will complete a capstone project that applies SciML techniques to a real-world problem, demonstrating the ability to handle data, develop models, and interpret results in a meaningful way.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Trust and Responsibility:**  
In our SciML course, trust and academic integrity are fundamental. You're expected to be familiar with and adhere to the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities regarding academic honesty.

**Coursework and Collaboration:**

- All submitted work must predominantly reflect your individual effort. While collaboration in the form of pair programming or discussion is encouraged for learning, the work you submit must be your own, clearly noting any collaboration.
- For assignments and projects, code should be individually written and reflect your unique approach. Collaboration is about learning together, not submitting identical work.

**Violations and Consequences:**

- Submissions not adhering to these standards, including identical assignments or unauthorized assistance in exams, will result in severe penalties.
- The first offense for an assignment violation may lead to a zero score on the work; subsequent violations can lead to failure of the course.
- Any exam violation might result in course failure.
