### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

Course Scope and Purpose - The primary purpose of Manufacturing Processes and Systems (MPS) II is to teach, by experience, how to build, and execute an efficient cost-effective manufacturing system that produces a useful, high quality, and economical product. MPS II allows students the opportunity to practice manufacturing lessons from MPS I through the implementation of technical data packages produced in MPS I. MPS I is a suggested prerequisite for MPS II. This course sequence develops technical communication skills through report writing (e.g., Technical Data Package) and oral presentations. An additional goal of this course sequence is for students to experience being on a team that takes existing product and manufacturing design specifications (Technical Data Package information) and delivers a product in small lot manufacturing quantities in a 14-week period. In the process, you will learn to plan your work, carry out your plan, integrate tasks, and to effectively communicate with fellow team members and suppliers. MPS II teaches an integrated project approach to planning and producing a product using the skills developed in MPS I. While in MPS I you learned to plan your work, now it is time to execute your plan, integrate tasks, and communicate.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Course Learning Outcomes -Upon successful completion of this course, students are expected to achieve the following three learning outcomes.  
1\. Students will demonstrate the ability to organize a team, plan work, execute plans, integrate tasks, and to effectively communicate with team members and staff. Assessment of this learning outcome includes milestone 1 and midterm/final peer evaluations, and two project update individual questions. • Milestone #1 – Establish updated project tasks and schedule, assign team member responsibilities including officers.  
2\. Students will demonstrate the ability to efficiently execute manufacturing plans from MPS I. Assessment of this learning outcome includes class milestones 2, 3, & 4 • Milestone #2 – Completion of tooling and mfg. fixtures (e.g., plastic injection mold tooling, thermoforming tooling, and sheet metal progressive dies are machined and ready for use). This milestone also includes drafts of quality assurance plans and standard operating procedures. • Milestone #3 – Completion of special purpose machines, automation equipment, end effectors, pallets, feeders, and packaging. This milestone also includes a preliminary demonstration of assembly system in the MILL including Octopuz robotic simulations. • Milestone #4 – Functional assembly system demonstration with production parts.  
3\. Students will demonstrate the ability to build, implement, document (both with written and digital simulations and documents including VKS and Octopuz), and demonstrate a complete semi-automated manufacturing system (component manufacturing and product assembly) for producing agreed upon production numbers of the team’s product to specifications. Assessment of this learning outcome includes team-based midterm and final evaluations by staff and industrial partners of presentations and technical data packages.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
