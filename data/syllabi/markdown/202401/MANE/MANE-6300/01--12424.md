## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is a graduate-level course, focused on solving the neutron diffusion/transport equations, thermal-hydraulic mass/momentum/energy conservation equations, and the relevant multiphysics coupling approaches to support reactor design and safety analysis. The course covers a diverse of topics from numerical computing fundamentals for multiphysics and multiscale computation, discretization schemes in converting continuous equations to discrete system of equations, numerical computing strategies to solve discrete equations involved with multiphysics solvers, to state-of-art techniques in speeding up numerical methods and convergence analysis.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The broad goal of this course is to introduce the theoretical foundations and pragmatic implementations of numerical methods used in nuclear reactor analysis. A practical goal of the course is to provide students with a working knowledge of numerical computing methods in performing reactor analysis and receive the necessary background for future research in applying numerical methods to solving nuclear reactor analysis problems.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

# I. Multiphysics Computation in Reactor Analysis

I.A. Overview of multiphysics phenomena in reactor analysis and their coupling relations

- Reactor physics: power distribution, radioactive isotope distribution and concentration – via static (criticality/eigenvalue problem) and kinetics/dynamics (fast transient, long-term burnup) analysis
- Thermal-hydraulics: temperature/velocity/pressure distributions,

I.B. Computational coupling methods

I.B.1. Multiphysics coupling between different physics models (equations)

I.B.2. Multiscale coupling between models (equations) at different scales within the context of single physics

I.C. Current tools in nuclear community

# II. Neutronics Analysis

II.A. Statics Analysis Methods  
II.A.1. Diffusion Nodal Methods

II.A.2. Transport SN and MOC Methods

1. 2-D Sn Equations and SI method
2. Integral transport equation and MOC methods

iii. Fourier Analysis of SI method

II.A.3. Acceleration Methods

1. DSA
2. CMFD

II.B. Dynamics Analysis Methods

II.B.1. Point kinetics model

II.B.2. Space-energy dependent dynamics model

1. adiabatic model
2. quasi-static model
3. improved quasi-static model
4. direct discretization model

II.B.3. Applications

II.C. Current tools

# III. Thermal-Hydraulics Analysis

III.A. High-fidelity modeling methods (CFD)

III.B. Sub-Channel modeling methods

III.C. System modeling methods

III.D. Current tools

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Identify and choose the correct numerical approaches used to analyze behaviors of neutronics, thermal-hydraulics, fuel performance and accidents in nuclear reactor systems. Understand the basic characteristics of coupling approaches to numerical solution of multiscale and multiphysics problems.
- Understand, with examples drawn mostly from simplified reactor systems, the advantages and disadvantages of various discretization schemes and convergence criteria, and their influence on the accuracy of particular numerical methodology.
- Gain the knowledge of specific features of production level reactor analysis codes for simulating reactor physics, heat transfer, fuel behavior and accident events in complex conditions, and the use of these codes in various areas of nuclear engineering.
