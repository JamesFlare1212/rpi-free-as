### Goals:

# Course Objectives

This course is an introduction to heat transfer. The course will discuss the three modes of heat transfer - Conduction, Convection, and Radiation. Apart from learning the fundamental aspects of heat transfer, the course also emphasizes applying this knowledge to solve various engineering problems.

# Exams, Project, and Schedule

**Exams:**

**Project:**

Students will be assigned a course project involving a design problem, requiring utilizing heat transfer knowledge gained during the semester. The report for the project will be due on the last day of instruction for the semester. Each student must turn in the class project.

The project consists of **designing a finned heat sink** able to decrease the temperature of a small heat-producing object with a (top) surface area of **25 mm x 50 mm** from **45****°C to 35****°C** under natural convection (no fans). In other words, if the heat sink were not used, the object’s surface would be at 45 C. With the addition of a heat sink, the surface temperature can be reduced to 35 C. The heat sink can be designed in Solidworks, which can be copy-pasted into the report.

**A team of two students** can work on this project. Examples of finned heat sink designs are shown in the images below. The heat sink design should be appropriate to meet the specifications (i.e., it is not overdesigned nor under-designed), the design approach should be correct, and the project should be completed and turned in by the due date provided in the course calendar.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

# Academic Integrity

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of academic dishonesty, and you should familiarize yourself with these rights and responsibilities.

In this class, all documents turned in for a grade must represent the student's own work. In cases where help was received or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate that. You may discuss approaches to problems with other people for doing homework, but I expect no copying of other people's efforts. I expect the solutions to quizzes, homework, and exams to be your own individual work. Exams are closed-book and closed-notes and must reflect each student's individual knowledge. No teamwork is allowed for exams. Any cheating that is detected will result in a lowered test and/or course grade and will be reported to the Dean of Students Office.

A violation of Academic Integrity policy is, by definition, considered a flagrant offense to the educational process. It is taken seriously by students, faculty, and Rensselaer and will be addressed in an effective manner. If found responsible for committing academic dishonesty, a student may be subject to one or both types of penalties: an academic (grade) penalty administered by the professor and/or disciplinary action through the Rensselaer judicial process described in the handbook. Three relevant academic integrity violations to emphasize include:

**Collaboration**: Collaboration is defined as deliberately facilitating an act of academic dishonesty in any way or form, for example, allowing another student to observe an exam paper or allowing another student to "recycle" one's work.

**Copying**: Copying is defined as obtaining information pertaining to a graded exercise by deliberately observing the paper of another student; for example, noting which alternative another student has circled on a multiple-choice exam.

**Plagiarism**: Plagiarism is defined as representing the work or words of another as one's own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference. Examples include using sentences verbatim from a published source without appropriate referencing, presenting as one's own the detailed argument of a published source, or presenting as one's own electronically or digitally enhanced graphic representations from any form of media. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines the full list of forms of Academic Dishonesty, and you should familiarize yourself with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student's own work. In cases where help was received or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. I expect that you'll take exams without speaking to other students via another communication channel. You cannot use any online/internet assignment-sharing system for graded assignments. Interaction with it results in an automatic failure for the entire course. A result of the violation using online resources to assist in graded assignments or exams will result in a formal report. As a warning, there are ways to detect your use of these online resources that compromise academic integrity.
