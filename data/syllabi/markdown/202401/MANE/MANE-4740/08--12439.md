## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

**Textbooks and Materials**

There are no new required texts for this class. However, students are expected to possess textbooks in the areas of thermodynamics, fluid mechanics, and heat transfer for the duration of the course. Students not owning textbooks should borrow books from the library or purchase used books (any edition will do). Students should be aware that “free” online versions of the textbooks listed below may actually be infringing on copyrights. Students using such resources do so at their own risk. Legal advice regarding copyright infringement should come from a lawyer. Nothing in this syllabus should be construed as legal advice.

- *Introduction to Thermal and Fluids Engineering* by Kaminski and Jensen is the most recommended reference. Other older books include:
  - o *Heat Transfer, J.P Holman*
  - o *Introduction to Fluid Mechanics, John & Haberman*
  - o *Engineering Fluid Mechanics, Mironer*
  - o *Fundamentals of Thermodynamics*, VanWylen & Sonntag
  - o *Introduction to Fluid Mechanics*, Fox & McDonald
  - o *Experimental Methods for Engineers*, J.P. Holman, is an older textbook that you may find useful. Older books should be available at the library. Used copies can be found at many bookseller websites, with price a function of condition and edition number.
- The Folsom Library also contains the following books:
  - o *Fundamentals of Thermal-Fluid Sciences*, Çengel and Turner
  - o *Brief Introduction to Fluid Mechanics*, Young/Munson/ Okiishi
  - o *Fundamentals of Engineering Thermodynamics*, Moran and Shapiro
  - o *Fundamentals of Heat and Mass Transfer*, Incropera and DeWitt
  - o *Fluid Mechanics* by White
  - o In addition, you may find the book by Figliola & Beasley (*Theory and Design for Mechanical Measurements* 2nd Edition, 1995, Wiley) to be a useful reference.
- Materials specific to the four experiments performed in this class will be provided through handouts posted on LMS. Extensive handouts have been prepared on the first three experiments. These handouts include an introduction to the project, theory, specific objectives, equipment description and operating instructions, and lab report requirements. Another handout has been prepared to provide information on uncertainties in experimental data. It should be studied as carefully as the ones on the individual experiments.

Students should purchase an engineering computation pad for doing homework.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Course Objectives**

The topics to be covered will be a combination of new material specific to this lab course and material covered in previous courses.

The specific objectives of the course are:

1. To demonstrate thermodynamic, heat transfer, and fluid flow principles;
2. To integrate theory and practice through the operation of experiments and through the acquisition and analysis of experimental data;
3. To familiarize students with complex, realistic systems and the interactions among thermodynamics, heat transfer, and fluid flow;
4. To introduce some new concepts and techniques in the three topic areas;
5. To develop abilities to understand, experiment with, and analyze thermal and fluids systems, including analysis and decision making in the face of experimental uncertainty;
6. To exercise technical communication skills in a variety of modes.

**\*\*Online Quizzes**

Throughout the semester (see course schedule), there will be a series of quizzes administered via LMS on topics covered during lectures and in the laboratory. The primary intent of the quiz is to draw attention to details in both the lab manual and the laboratory procedures manual that may be important during the lab, and to be sure students understood the underlying physical principles of the experiments. ## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance:** Attendance is expected for the weekly lecture. Attendance is mandatory for the lab sessions. Attendance in the studio discussions is expected for all group members. You must attend the lab scheduled for **your** section and **your** group. Alternative arrangements should be extremely rare and only with the approval of your section instructor. Absence from the lab session without an instructor-approved excuse will result in a grade of zero for the report. Please communicate with your section instructor if you need an accommodation.

In the event of an illness that prevents a student from participating in a lab session or discussion session, the student should inform the section instructor right away. If the student cannot participate in a lab session, the student’s group is encouraged to use Webex so that the student can experience the lab remotely.

Students are expected to pay attention and take notes in the lectures. Use of laptops and cellphones is not permitted in the lectures. Photography is not permitted in the lectures. Students should not rely solely on the instructor’s lecture notes as lecture notes may not be posted to LMS.

### Other Course Policies:

**Team Participation**

All students are expected to participate fully in each laboratory experience and in the preparation of all group assignments.

**Lab Material Reading**

Much more can be gained from an experiment if, before attempting to perform the experiment, the student has the background knowledge about the objectives and concepts (theory) governing the processes to be studied. It is also critical to understand the objectives and the plan of work for each lab before coming to the lab. This information is provided in the extended handouts for each experiment. **Please read carefully all of the handouts.** Some supplemental materials may also be provided on LMS for those wishing a deeper understanding of the theory, instrumentation, and/or standards related to the experiments.

**Final Exam:** There is no final exam. (A timeslot for this course might appear on the finals schedule, but that is just a placeholder.)

**Late Policy:** Without a formal excuse, an assignment will receive a 10% penalty if handed in within 24 hours of the deadline, a 30% penalty if handed in within 1 week of the deadline, and a 100% penalty (i.e. no credit) otherwise.

Students are expected to be on time for their labs. At the discretion of the instructor and TA, a penalty of 1 point per minute late may be applied to the lab report grades of tardy students.

**Grade Curving:** The difficulty of the material may require curving the class grade, but this is seldom needed given that students have a high degree of control of their level of performance on assignments. Equalization of the sections will be performed to account for differences in report grading between instructors.

**Grade Modifiers:** Grade modifiers (+, −) will be used in assigning the grade in this class.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Rensselaer’s policy on academic dishonesty is explained in the *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities*. You are expected to review and understand that information. In addition, the following policies apply specifically to this course.

- Committing, or attempting to commit an act of academic dishonesty, or assisting in the commission or attempt of such an act, is a flagrant offense to the process and culture of education; expect our reaction to it to be harsh.
- You are expected to refrain from any academically dishonest act, and to be vigilant in protecting your work from others who might attempt to commit an act of academic dishonesty.

In this course, academic dishonesty – *cheating* – can take the following forms.

- Submission of a lab report that, except for short phrases or properly referenced material (e.g., quotes and figures from the lab manual, etc.), consists of any content not created by and original to the report authors listed. Plagiarism may be penalized according to the severity and deliberateness of the act, from reduction of report grade to failure of the course and referral to the Dean of Students for dismissal from Rensselaer.
- Taking a quiz with any assistance from another person, regardless of who they are, or the medium of communication for assistance (talk, phone, texting, etc.).
- Using any materials (such as a cell phone, camera) that are not explicitly permitted during a quiz. (Quizzes are open book, open notes, open lab reports, open laptop, open internet searches, but closed communication.)

Mobile Devices: During a quiz all mobile devices that are not your laptop (e.g., cell/smart phones, pagers, cameras, etc.) must be stored securely away and are not to be used unless specifically directed otherwise by the instructor. The use of, or any interaction with, a mobile device during a quiz without explicit permission of the instructor will be interpreted as the illicit transfer of quiz data, which will be considered an act of cheating and treated as such.

The penalty for violating these policies is at the discretion of any or all members of the instructional team. In general, the penalty for any violation is failure of the course and referral to the Dean of Students

**Collaboration**

You are expected to collaborate with the members of your laboratory group with respect to the conduct of your experiments and the preparation of your reports. However, as stated above, your report, including the experimental data and observations described therein, must be those of your own group. In rare cases (e.g., lab equipment failure) your instructor may provide you with experimental data to use as if you had obtained it yourselves; this will be considered “your data”, regardless of the actual source.

For homework assignments, you must submit your own work individually. Collaboration means discussion to understand the assignment and how to complete it. It can mean comparing results and/or assumptions (but not calculations) to check for errors. It does not mean copying one another's work. It does not mean dividing up the work. It does not mean submitting someone else's work, in full or in part.
