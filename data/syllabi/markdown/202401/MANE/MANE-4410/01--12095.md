## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides an undergraduate- and graduate level review of atomic and nuclear physics and quantum mechanics; application to atomic, molecular, and nuclear systems; particle and photon emissions; photon/particle interactions; quantum statistics; applications of nuclear physics to nuclear data, medical physics, accelerators, fusion systems, nuclear reactors; key measurements and databases; basic level of technical writing and presentation for undergraduate-level and advanced level of technical writing and presentation for graduate-level.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The goal of this course is to enable the student to analyze various nuclear and quantum theoretical frameworks in the context of practical applications. The student shall be able to deconstruct theoretical frameworks, validate legacy interpretations of framework sub-components by comparison with modern observations, and apply validated theoretical methodologies to existing technology embodiments or scientific interpretations of natural phenomena to serve as the basis for future research and development activities. The student shall learn to present problem statements and proposed solutions related to atomic and nuclear physics in a well-organized, neat, and professional manner.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Evaluate legacy interpretations of natural phenomena selected by the instructor and detect at least one inconsistency with modern atomic, nuclear, and quantum theoretical frameworks. Student at the 6000 level will detect at least 3 inconsistencies.
- Students will be able to analyze inconsistencies identified by the course instructor and determine relevant portions of atomic, nuclear, and quantum theory that may be useful in dissecting these inconsistencies.
- Students will be able to apply relevant portions of atomic, nuclear, and quantum theory to problems identified by the instructor to construct novel theoretical explanations of natural phenomena.
- Students will be able to create a unique hypothesis and write a report documenting the predicted outcome.
- For 6000: Students will create and defend a unique hypothesis and document predicted outcomes.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students assessments measures will consist of homework and exams. For 6000 level students: Addtional Homework and Exam questions may be assigned, in addition to increased complexity of common questions as documented in assignments.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Due to the dynamic nature of the class material that will be presented, lecture notes and in-class discussions will be the primary resource of learning for students. While attendance is optional and no formal penalties will be assigned for non-attendance, the course instructor will typically not make accommodations to revisit material covered or extend the deadline for assignments completed in class for students that do not attend lectures. On a case basis, with advanced notice of a planned absence, certain arrangements may be made such as recording lectures or providing copies of lecture notes.

### Other Course Policies:

**Collaboration** on in-class assignments and *homeworks* is permitted and encouraged. Seeking help from other sources (e.g., TAs, ALAC, etc.) is also encouraged. However, the work you submit must be your own and simply transcribing another person’s homework does not qualify as collaborative work, is not acceptable and will result in a zero grade for the homework assignment in question.
