## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

## Course Description

This is an introductory course in Biology. It is intended for undergraduates, both non-majors and majors. It serves as the first course for life science majors and for students interested in the health professions.

Laboratory activities: Students generate hypotheses, and share their ideas on how to test their hypotheses, within interactive in-person mini-groups. Hands-on wet and dry labs include basic cell and molecular biological skills. Activities include introduction to scientific computing in interactive virtual environments using Python and R.

The goal is for students to gain biological literacy by applying the scientific method. Students will collect various types of biological data, such as whole organismal or cellular or molecular data. Students use quantitative tools, and simulations. Students develop data dexterity, and analytical thinking skills while learning basic fundamental laboratory skills. This is a D1 Data Intensive Course.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

## Course Content

The course content is broadly divided into five modules:

1. Biostatistics and Quantitative Biology - Ecology
2. Bioinformatics and Cladistics - Evolution
3. Cell Biology
4. Genetics and Medicine
5. Molecular Biology

**The Scientific Method** is applied to understand fundamental concepts, including the following introductory topics, which are studied in silico, in vitro or in vivo:

- Cell Biology - Mitosis, Meiosis, and the Cell Cycle.
- Molecular Biology - DNA replication, Protein Synthesis, and Regulation; DNA extraction, PCR, Gel Electrophoresis.
- Microbiology - Prokaryotic (versus Eukaryotic) cell size, shape & morphology.
- Virology - SarsCov2 genome & spike protein data visualization using PDB.
- Biochemistry - Structures of amino acids, nucleotides, atoms and bonds, polymers.
- Genetics - Mendelian Patterns, probabilities, ratios, Chi Square Analysis using corn purple/yellow plump/wrinkled mutant kernels or Fruit Flies.
- Ecology, Biodiversity and Conservation - Aquatic Ecosystems with EPT, H, J indices and Student's TTEST, Biodiversity, Conservation, Epidemiology, eg., R0, vaccine critical threshold, Hardy Weinberg Allele frequency and other calculations.
- Evolution, Phylogeny - Cladistics, Bioinformatics, Using Public Databases and Bioinformatics Software.
- Medicine - Drug/vaccine design and genetic engineering using Simbio’s simulations.
- Computational Biology: Excel, Introduction to Jupyter Notebooks with R Statistical Language and Python using Supercomputer access to RPI students.
