## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is an introductory course in biological systems focusing on major topics in evolution, human genetics & medicine, and ecology. It is a course that is intended for non-majors and majors and covers a variety of topics that impact our daily lives and the grand challenges of this decade and your generation. The goal is to provide an understanding of fundamental principles, to develop analytical thinking skills in the context of modern biology, and to develop data dexterity for understanding and solving grand challenges in the life sciences. Note that this is an approved DI-1 course.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The Course Goal is to provide an understanding of fundamental principles, to develop analytical thinking skills in the context of modern biology, and to develop data dexterity for understanding and solving grand challenges in the life sciences.

1. Demonstrate mastery of the foundational knowledge and core concepts of evolution, genetics & medicine, and ecology.
2. Apply foundational knowledge and core concepts in these areas to solve novel problems, analyze realistic scenarios, and make predictions.
3. Apply reasoning and methods to the analysis and solution of biological problems.
4. Utilize information and data from a wide range of sources to construct knowledge and communicate that knowledge effectively.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

1. Evaluation: Answer multiple choice, short answer, and true/false questions through quizzes and exams.
2. Evaluation: In-class discussion, homework, reflection assignments, and exams.
3. Evaluation: Homework, exams, and a class presentation.
4. Evaluation: Select a current topic in biology, gather information from research articles, texts, the web, and other relevant sources. Analyze, organize, distill, and synthesize this information and present a summary in your class group presentation.

1. Discussions: 4 In-Class team Discussions/Presentations, each worth 5 points, for a total of 20 points per unit, adding up to a total of 60 points this semester. Submit on LMS prior to the start of class.
2. Quizzes: 4 End-Class Quizzes, each worth 5 points, for a total of 20 points per unit, adding up to a total of 60 points this semester. Submit on LMS before the end of class.
3. Presentations: Pre-Class Homework Readings are necessary for you to summarize your learning in-class per unit. 3 Summaries are each worth 5 points. Summaries are 300 words or more. Summaries that are less than 300 words will be given zero points. Combine with Final Presentation, which is worth 5 points, for a total of 20 points this semester. Submit on LMS.
4. Exams. 3 Unit Exams, each worth 20 points per unit, and a total of 60 points this semester.
5. Final Exam or the 4th Exam is Optional. It is comprehensive, in that it is inclusive of the entire course content. This grade will replace your lowest scoring Unit Exam. DO NOT take the Final Exam if you are reasonably doing well in the course. Otherwise, your low Final Exam score will replace your former reasonably good score. Submit on LMS.

NOTES on Quizzes and Exams: These will be in-class, with a combination of multiple choice, true false, and possibly other forms of short answer questions. Questions? Email Dr. Ramanath.

NOTES on Self-Grading and Peer Comments: For each of your answers to Discussion Questions, as soon as you submit your URL on LMS, you will immediately get 5 points on LMS. However, these points will be taken away if your submission is non-existent, plagiarized or lacks citation/s, or is less than a 100 words in length. Incorrect URLs make it difficult for the TA to find your submission, and such submissions will be given zero points. Late submissions will be given zero points. Follow the class Honor Code: You must write in your own words. Your opinions are what matter. If you refer to data or hypotheses from other sources, to support your opinions, cite your sources by including their URL. You do not repeat what your textbook/sources say. You interpret, analyze and comment on your sources' claims. Discussions are in open book format, but quizzes are not - unless otherwise announced in class. Cheating in quizzes and exams are forbidden, and such submissions will be given zero points. Add your peer comments to a peer’s slide, and place their slide URL on LMS for credit, 1 point, as announced in class. Your comment must be scientifically substantive with citation.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Electronics in Class: There is a tendency in today’s world of texting, electronic chatting, and surfing the net, to forget that there is a time and place for everything. We assume that you will be respectful to each other and to us, refraining from disruptive behavior during our class sessions. And we expect you to be highly engaged in the class material and be a part of the day’s discussion.

Academic Integrity: Relationships within the academic community are built on trust. For example, students must trust that their instructors have made the appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and the faculty must trust that the assignments that students submit are their own. Acts which violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty, and as a Rensselaer student, it is your responsibility to read and understand these. See https://rpi.app.box.com/s/bfzzwdsrqxzrm3jikr2uv6gn7zu6dhlt

In addition to what is stated in the Handbook, we provide the following guidelines for BIOL-1010:

Class Activities: We encourage students to discuss all activities in pre- and in-class sessions with fellow students. Learning is interactive, and we encourage this. However, when you are writing pre-class or in-class assignments, you must write your answer in your own words.

Quizzes & Exams: You may not give or receive assistance by any means before, during, or after an exam. Note that using google and/or notes on your laptop during a quiz/exam is an academic violation.

If you are caught cheating, you will receive a 0 (zero points) on the quiz or exam and will not be permitted to take the optional 4th exam. A second violation of academic integrity will result in immediate failure of the course.

Team Presentations: For team projects, you must work as a team. The expectation is that each person will contribute equally.

NOTE: Because of the ability to use the internet as a research tool, the greatest academic integrity problems arise because of copying and pasting electronic words without thinking. It is critical that you learn early to use these tools and then to put the ideas in your words and provide a reference of the source of the information. In addition, while Wikipedia is a wonderful tool, we do expect you to read the literature we assign and get beyond “fast facts”, some of which may be incorrect.

The second most common academic integrity issue at Rensselaer is taking advantage of previous students’ papers, essays, problem sets, posters, etc. and submitting them as your own or submitting recycled work of your own. While some may view this as a game and part of the competitive process, your faculty and the Institute consider these as other academic integrity violations. Think carefully about your moral compass and the person you want to be. It is your choice.

If you have any doubts or concerns of whether an action is academic dishonesty, please feel free to discuss it with your instructor or TA.
