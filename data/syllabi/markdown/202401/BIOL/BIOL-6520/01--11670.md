## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is a two-semester course for first year graduate students in the Graduate Program in Biology and the Biochemistry/Biophysics Graduate Program. There are four parts to the course:

1. Introduction to the research being done in the department of Biological Sciences and by faculty in the

   Biochemistry/Biophysics Program.

2. Practice and experience in reading/evaluating/discussing scientific literature.

3. Introduction to various skills and topics needed to succeed in graduate school and science.

4. Introduction to scientific writing.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Text: no textbook, but *At The Bench: A Laboratory Navigator*, Kathy Barker, Cold Spring Harbor Press, 2005 and *A Short Guide to Writing about Biology*, Jan A. Pechenik, Pearson, 2016 are highly recommended. Additional readings (mostly current scientific journal articles) will be assigned and will be posted in LMS. All readings will be given to you at least 2 days before the class in which they will be discussed.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate knowledge of biological research conducted at Rensselaer, and integrate this knowledge into a broader understanding of the field of biological research.
- Critically evaluate and analyze primary scientific literature in multiple fields of biology, and be able to interpret, explain and communicate scientific literature to peers.
- Demonstrate knowledge of experimental procedures used to generate scientific data, identify the advantages and limitations of these techniques, and interpret data derived from experiments.
- Integrate knowledge of effective scientific communication in order to write a grant proposal using appropriate style and format.
- Integrate an understanding of the funding of science and evaluation of proposals in order to evaluate proposals, write constructive critiques, and defend critiques in a mock review panel.
- Identify and evaluate issues related to responsible use of statistics in science.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

These objectives will be assessed by participation in class discussions and interactive activities (all faculty presenting on their research and/or professional development topics will give each student a S/U grade based on their participation), and by a written paper that will be assigned a letter grade by the primary course instructor (Dr. Ligon for BIOL, Dr. Royer for BCBP).

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

The final grade will consist of the letter grade given on the Proposal (80%) and Proposal reviews (20%) (see below), but this grade will be decreased by U grades given by faculty lecturers. Each U grade will decrease the overall course grade by a half grade (i.e. from A to A- or from A- to B+).

| Grading Criteria for Proposal |  |
| --- | --- |
| To receive an A: | You must first choose a topic of the appropriate scope. Your proposal should have 2 specific aims or project goals (and no more than 3), one of which can come from work you are doing in one of your rotation labs, but one must be your own idea that expands on what the lab is doing. The A proposal will have a thorough introduction and background section that shows the significance of the work proposed. The specific aims or goals will be focused and answerable. The research plan will give sufficient detail on the methods (but will not be a protocol), and will consider how the data will be analyzed and interpreted, what the possible pitfalls might be, etc... An A proposal will be compelling, well-argued and logical. The A paper will also be virtually free of grammatical and or copywriting errors. |
| A- | The A- paper will fall a little short on the description above in some, but not all elements. Perhaps the argument has some logical flaws, perhaps there are grammatical errors, etc... |
| B+ | The B+ paper will have flaws in some areas, but have some redeeming features as well. |
| B | The B paper has significant flaws in multiple areas. Perhaps the topic was poorly chosen and is either too broad or too narrow, perhaps the writing is filled with grammatical and/or spelling errors, perhaps the argument is not logical or convincing, etc... |
| B- | The B- paper is slightly above the bare minimum. |
| C | The C proposal is just passing. The proposal either has major logical or organizational flaws. |
| F | Anything less than the description for the C paper will receive a failing grade. |

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

This is a graduate level course that represents your first professional obligation as a scientist. Attendance is expected at each class. As there is no textbook, all the relevant information will be covered in class. Students are expected to read the assigned papers before class and be prepared to discuss them in detail.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
