## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This second course in the Digital Constructs sequence will focus on refining descriptive and speculative drawing skills. Drawing and visualization will continue to be discussed as the medium through which architects generate and express forms and concepts. Work will be done in two and three dimensions, in vector and raster formats, with a range of software packages. The semester will begin by documenting an architectural stair, which will be subsequently built into intricate, complex structures with particular aesthetic characteristics. These structures will then be visualized to gauge their potential architectural affect. Class meetings will incorporate critique, tutorials and working sessions. The semester is organized into three phases that will build on each other.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Project 1** – Documentation and Advanced Modeling

Project 1 will build on two and three-dimensional drawing skills acquired in Digital Constructs 1. It will start by documenting an architectural stair. The stair will be drawn to scale in a related series of orthographic drawings. The drawings will then be used to construct a detailed three-dimensional model. Every detail of the stair, down to the smallest bolt, must be drawn and modeled. The three-dimensional model will be represented in an exploded axonometric drawing.

**Project 2** – Associative Modeling and Speculative Structures

Project 2 will introduce associative modeling and computational drawing through Grasshopper, a visual programing plug-in for Rhino. Students will learn to associate elements of geometry, operate on them parametrically, and then through multiplication, transformation and other technical opportunities presented by Grasshopper, develop a series of speculative drawings based on documentary drawings done in Project 1. By customizing Grasshopper templates presented in tutorials, students will begin to author their own drawing procedures. The final drawing for Project 2 will be a multi-view composite that must demonstrate mastery of the range of techniques introduced during the semester. Moving between two and three dimensions will be discussed as a fundamental problem/opportunity within the discipline and discussed from a technical standpoint through workflows between Rhino, Illustrator and Photoshop.

**Project 3** – Portfolio

Project 3 will be to develop a portfolio of Digital Constructs 1 and 2 work. The portfolio will be discussed as a vehicle through which one’s work can be framed, focused, and refined. The final multi-view drawing from Project 2 will continue to be refined in conjunction with portfolio development. The final deliverable for the course will a digital portfolio including all work done in Digital Constructs 1 and 2.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Ability to read and understand architectural drawings of an existing stair, how components of the stair come together and how the stair meets ergonomic and safety requirements. Ability to make accurate, detailed orthographic drawings of an existing architectural stair, and output the drawings at different architectural scales using layouts in Rhino. Ability to make a detailed and accurate three-dimensional digital model of an existing architectural stair, and output three-dimensional drawings and images using the model. (Demonstrated by assessment measure #1).
- Ability to create an exploded analytical drawing of a stair that shows how all components of the stair relate to each other in terms of structure and assembly sequence. (Demonstrated by assessment measure #1).
- Ability to create a simple parametric model in Grasshopper that can be updated through associates of geometry and data. Ability to think computationally by using and adapting a given Grasshopper template to author unique speculative drawings of the stair. (Demonstrated by assessment measures #2).
- Ability to bring diverse representational elements together in a composite drawing, including descriptive drawings produced explicitly (though typical non-associative drawing and modeling), and speculative drawings and images produced associatively (though parametric modeling in Grasshopper). Ability to use graphic techniques to combine and relate these diverse elements into a legible whole. (Demonstrated by assessment measure #2).
- Ability to layout drawings, images, and text in a simple portfolio containing all Digital Constructs 1 and 2 work. Ability to use a grid to structure a multi-page document that presents two projects in a consistent manner, and also creates clear separation between them. Ability to create visual hierarchies to emphasize particular aspects of the work. Ability to incorporate negative space into the layout. (Demonstrated by assessment measure #3).

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance and Timely Completion of Work**

Attendance is required at all class meetings during the semester. **Two (2) unexcused absences from class will result in the lowering of the grade by one (1) letter. Four (4) unexcused absences from class during the term result in an automatic F.** Whether an absence (health, immediate family death, officially approved field / sport trip, etc.) is excused or unexcused is determined by the Students Experience Office (4th Floor Academy Hall, x8022, se@rpi.edu), upon presentation of required documentation, according to established policies and procedures (see http:// http://se.rpi.edu/policies/ea/). Failure to work in class with undivided attention and disruptive behavior (tardiness, leaving early, lack of participation, general socializing, etc.), as well working on assignments from other classes during class, will count as absences.

All work must be submitted for review and discussion on the dates noted under each assessment measure. Work includes drawing design and development, learning software, pinups, presentations, and discussions.

For students with an ADHD diagnosis, extra time applies only to exams, and quizzes completed in class. It does not apply to projects or labs where the students are able to plan more time outside of class to complete assignments. Note that all work for this course is project-based work. Students are encouraged to attend faculty office hours and tutoring sessions to help plan the completion of studio, lab, or project-based assignments.

**Archiving**

At the conclusion of the course, all students will be required to submit all final digital works to Publications. Publications will assign a liaison to the class; all students will be responsible for submitting work to the liaison. In addition, selected physical material may be collected and stored between semesters so that it may be photographed or scanned by Publications. Grades for the semester will not be released until this material is received.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic Integrity**

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own performance. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

**Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism**

Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of this course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student. Any miss-representation of work shall be evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student will result in a failing grade for the course.
