## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Learning Outcome #1: Parti – Ability to create a clear, organized design concept and building massing, informed by a well-defined design language, which responds to the physical, social, and environmental context of an urban building site, as well as zoning regulations controlling bulk of the building on site.
- Learning Outcome #2: Environmental – Ability to diagram yearly/hourly sun path on a given site and orient a building in relation to the sun. Ability to show how apertures are used for daylighting, and how sun shading devices are used on different building exposures of the building. Graduate Learning Outcome #2 (Additional): Environmental – Demonstrate how building massing and shading devices are developed iteratively in response to solar performance. Demonstrate, through independent research, how selected buildings perform in relation to solar exposure.
- Learning Outcome #3: Program – Ability to generate and organize an architectural program that responds to the needs of diverse stakeholders within the community of downtown Troy. Ability to calculate gross square footage, net square footage, and floor area ratio (FAR) of a building. Ability to design and organize different types of housing units, and public and private space in a mixed-use housing building. Graduate Learning Outcome #3 (Additional): Program – Demonstrate how the program is developed in relation to the needs of diverse stakeholders.
- Learning Outcome #4: Technical – Ability to scale and dimension spaces (such as bedrooms, bathrooms, stairs, corridors, etc.) and architectural assemblies (thicknesses of walls, floors, and roofs and elements that make them up) accurately. Ability to create an accurate zoning diagram for a building site based on reading and understanding a municipal zoning code.
- Learning Outcome #5: Systems – Ability to organize building systems including circulation, services (routes for plumbing and electrical), structure, and daylight in relation to each other and programmed spaces.
- Learning Outcome #6: Synthesis – Ability to demonstrate how components of an architectural project relate and are synthesized across scales. This includes showing, how the scale of a building relates to the scale of the city, how the scale of a housing unit relates to the scale of the building, and how the scale of a building components (column, beam, stair, etc.) relate to the scale of the building, as well as how environmental factors, including daylighting and solar shading, are incorporated into the design language of the project. Graduate Learning Outcome #6 (Additional): Synthesis – Ability to critically research socio-cultural and disciplinary aspects of housing design and incorprate this knowledge into writing about the project.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic Integrity  
The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own performance. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.  
Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism  
Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of this course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student. Any miss-representation of work shall be evaluated and could result in a failing grade for the assignment. A subsequent infraction by the student will result in a failing grade for the course.
