## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Overview  
Urban Data II will examine the role of data representation and the use of web-based graphics and in contemporary design. Students will understand the power of combining the thought process and methods of an architect with increasingly sophisticated representational technique that broaden the function of a traditional drawing.  
Drawing from areas of their own interest, students will create unique data sets and design simple games that both educate and challenge the user. Urban Data II brings together a multitude of skills including research, design, game logic, and web creation. The ability to effectively combine the ideas and techniques taught in Urban Data II offers students uniquely relevant skills in an era where architects are constantly adapting to ever-changing technology.  
Topics of Interest  
Data Collection  
Students will learn the basics of data collection, primarily from open-source resources. Students will learn the advantages and disadvantages of different file types, and how to harness the power of geolocated data.  
Students will also learn to create their own data sets from the research they perform in Urban Data II. They will learn to format their work so it becomes usable in multiple data visualization softwares.  
Online Visualization and Interactivity  
A key to this seminar is that final work is visible online and has interactive elements. Students will learn to visualize their data online using javascript libraries specifically designed for this purpose. Student work will be visible and sharable on a publicly visible website, enabling it to be easily sharable.  
In addition to existing online, student work will include interactive elements that allow users to reveal further information about a project. Students will learn the importance of visual and informational hierarchy to ensure they are conveying their work in a clear manner. They will learn to leverage static and dynamic elements as it relates to interactivity, and how to draw in and keep a user’s attention.  
Game Logic  
Students will develop their final project using game logic that challenges users to complete a task while uncovering layers of information. Students will understand the basic idea of goals and resistance in completing a task, and implement these ideas in their work. Key to completing this work will be the ability to store information and the use of global and local variables. Students will learn the importance of information management in their coding work to ensure their visualizations appear appropriately.  
Coding  
Urban Data II will require students to learn some amount of computer coding. Students will learn to write javascript code and a smaller amount of html. They will use the D3 javascript library to assist in their visualization work.  
The primary focus of this course is not to learn computer coding, but to understand how a small amount of coding knowledge can significantly expand the effectiveness of architectural representation.  
Seminar Sequence  
The seminar will largely contain two phases, a personal map and a larger research project, to be undertaken in teams.  
Personal Map  
The first phase of the seminar will teach students a basic introduction to data collection and representation. During this time students will develop a map of a location where they would like to travel. Students will develop a simple dashboard with the map, and several interactive elements. Inputs and mutable variables will be introdused. From a technical standpoint, this phase of the course will introduce the major techniques to be used during the course. Design and content critique will be less present during this phase, with an emphasis on technical understanding.  
Urban Game  
The second phase of the seminar will include the development of a web-based game. The game topic and location will be the choice of each group. Groups will gather base map data as well as identify a specific topic within their urban environment around which they will focus their game. Students will implement a game logic of goals and resistance that challenge the user to complete tasks. As the game progresses, users will be asked to make spatial decisions or answer questions offered by a map view.  
Students will be critiqued on the design and logic of their game. The games will contain one main interface that includes a map and supporting information. Stupporting information will include inputs, text, graphics, interactives, and other materials. Game objectives should be clearly stated and users should have a clear idea of the goals they are trying to accomplish. Successful projects will include large amounts of detail that is carefully revealed at appropriate times.  
Additional Considerations  
Group Work  
We will work in groups during the second phase of this seminar. Group work is essential to your development as a student and as a professional. If you have concerns about group work, please discuss with me directly.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Outcome 1 Data Collection: (B.Arch+M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to collect open-source data and manipulate data via Geospatial Information Systems software. Demonstrate the ability to represent relationships between different open-source data sets. (M.Arch) Ability to converge multiple data sets sourced from different organizations.
- Outcome 2 Data Representation: (B.Arch+M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to represent data sets and communicate importance via web-based software. (M.Arch) Ability to create 2d drawings using methods standard to the practice of architecture, and import them into a web-based platform.
- Outcome 3 Research: (B.Arch+M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to use data to quantifiably analyze, assess, and project outcomes of an urban site that is undergoing change over time. Ability to identify and collect bespoke data sets as a result of individual and team research.
- Outcome 4 Interface Design: (B.Arch+M.Arch) Ability to create a web-based interface that allows for the detailed viewing of an urban context. Ability to create an interface that offers user functionality. Ability to create an interface that allows users to make decisions based upon data presented, in a game-like scenario
- Outcome 5 Communication: (B.Arch+M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to communicate existing site conditions and overlay of game conditions on this site, via web-based graphics that combine data sets and directly designed geometries. Demonstrate the ability to design and execute a data graphic that contains multiple layers and information to be revealed as a user investigates the work. (M.Arch) Demonstrate the ability to create both spatial and non-spatial graphics

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism:  
Interdisciplinary collaboration is a critical component of this course. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must accurately represent the student’s own work, and properly cite authorship of work that was not solely completed by the student.
