### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

Building Systems and Environment (BSE) is the third class, following Energy, Comfort & Ecology and Environmental and Ecological Systems in the environmental technology series and focuses on the components and processes that provide thermal comfort, lighting, electricity, water, and waste removal in buildings. Environmental and Ecological Systems is a prerequisite to BSE, so it is assumed that the student has a baseline understanding of climatic concerns for buildings, human comfort, basic energy theory, and an understanding of basic passive approaches to environmental control. It will examine the way active systems interface with passive systems within the context of architectural design to provide integrated building designs.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The goal of this course is to familiarize Architects with the roles carried out by various sub-consultants and engineers throughout an integrated design process. The intent is not to teach students how to complete the work of the various sub-consultants, but rather to empower Architects to coordinate, guide and actively engage with the entire design team. This includes, but is not limited to:

- Mechanical engineers
- Lighting designers and Electrical engineers
- Acoustic consultants
- Building energy consultants

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will demonstrate the ability to gather relevant climate information and calculate the impact of climatic conditions on a building (lab 01, lab 02, quiz 01)
- Students will demonstrate the ability to read mechanical drawings, trace the distribution of conditioning systems in a building and identify critical elements of each individual system (lab 04, quiz 02)
- Students will demonstrate an understanding of basic lighting design principles by designing a code-compliant electrical lighting plan and measuring natural daylighting conditions in a case study building (lab 05, lab 06, quiz 03)
- Students will demonstrate an understanding of electrical consumption and its relationship to renewable energy production in a building by reading post-occupancy data from a case study building and designing a corresponding photovoltaic array to achieve net-zero energy consumption (lab 07, quiz 03)

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process. Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust will merit a failing grade in this course and the violator(s) will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.
