## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

COURSE ABSTRACT

Architects and engineers are increasingly pushing climate analysis and engineering to new territories and in recent years we are beginning to see this implemented across multiple typologies of advanced building skins. Simultaneously, the building industry has consistently been breaking new ground in research and innovation and material technologies such as Engineered wood products (EWP) are frequently being announced. A contemporary high performance building relies on the ability to leverage design to control a number of interrelated systems including a) interior and exterior environmental comfort b) performative envelopes that decrease reliance on mechanical systems for thermal comfort c) energy production such as the integration of solar panels in the design d) calibration of building layout with environmental site conditions and e) built-in resilience through the measurement of material exposure and durability.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

PROJECT PROPOSAL

This independent study aims to provide a foundation in environmental analysis and design. Through a series of case studies or a research-by-design approach, students will familiarize themselves with the feedback looping workflow between analysis and design that is required for the production of climate engineering in construction. Students will employ CAD systems and visual scripting exploring the integration of design expression and environmental systems to produce a design study or analysis.

GRADING CRITERIA

The final grade reflects progress over the course of the semester as well as the quality and quantity of the work presented at the final review and booklet submission. Students will be provided with assessments after progress reviews In each case, the evaluation of the work will consider the levels of inventiveness, preparation, resolution, effort and engagement, as well as graphic and verbal presentations. The following grading system of letter grades will be applied and given at the end of semester:

- A (EXCELLENT) Exceptional performance strongly exceeding the requirements of the course, showing strong academic initiative and independent resourcefulness
- B (GOOD) Performance above the norm; accurate and complete; beyond the minimum requirements of the course; work demonstrates marked progress and initiative
- C (AVERAGE) Satisfactory work that adequately meets minimum requirements and demonstrates satisfactory comprehension, communication skills, and effort; demonstrates little initiative to investigate the problem without substantial input from the instructor; work shows little improvement
- D (INFERIOR) Unsatisfactorily meets minimum requirements; demonstrates minimum comprehension, communication skills, and effort at an inferior level; initiative lacking; improvement not noticeable

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

ACADEMIC INTEGRITY

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process. Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust will merit a failing grade in this course and the violator(s) will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.
