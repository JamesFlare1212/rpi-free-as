### Prerequisites or Other Requirements:

Prerequisites or Other Requirements

Corequisite: Students in ARCH 4820 Comprehensive Design Studio 1 and some in CDS2/Integrated Design Development (IDD) are required to co-register for this class, ARCH 4540. In Spring semesters, this will include the graduate level course co-requisite, ARCH 6620 paired with ARCH 5380.

PP1 is a co-requisite for students taking ARCH-4820 Comprehensive Design Studio 1 (CDS1) and Integrated Design Development (IDD) and Graduate students taking the graduate-level related studio course. The CDS1 Studio begins the process that continues in CDS2/IDD Studio, where students attempt to realize the design process through the design development phase and into construction documents, integrating all aspects that lead to actual construction. Students must be concerned with building systems including structures, material assemblies and services, and how these systems inform design decisions. As additional design disciplines, students design a code-compliant building and develop construction documents for all or major portions of the work.

Because students are expected to take the material from the PP1 course and use it to inform studio project development, ARCH-4820 and ARCH-4540 are conceived as complementary to each other, and ARCH-4830 students will continue the benefit of this instruction. Students who are not Architecture majors may take PP1 without also taking the CDS1/2 Studio. While they may not benefit from immediate application of the course material in a design setting, they will acquire a general and logical understanding of how codes, sustainability, costs and conduct inform the design process.

## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

An introduction to architectural practice as related to accomplishing design projects. An overview of professional obligations, registration and conduct, architects’ roles in project delivery, and office organization and management for delivering professional services. In-depth examination of architects’ responsibilities for health, safety, and welfare in design; building code requirements for fire protection, life safety, and accessibility; economics of building systems and assemblies; design and construction contracts; and design documentation.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\. Regulatory Context (NAAB SC.3): students will understand the fundamental principles of life safety, land use, and current laws and regulations that apply to buildings and sites in the United States, and the evaluative process architects use to comply with those laws and regulations as part of a project.
- 2\. Technical Knowledge (NAAB SC.4): students will understand the established and emerging systems, technologies, and assemblies of building construction, and the methods and criteria architects use to assess those technologies against the design, economics, and performance objectives of projects.
- 3\. Design Synthesis (NAAB SC.5): students will develop the ability to make design decisions within architectural projects while demonstrating synthesis of user requirements, regulatory requirements, site conditions, and accessible design, and consideration of the measurable environmental impacts of their design decisions.
- 4\. Building Integration (NAAB SC.6): students will develop the ability to make design decisions within architectural projects while demonstrating integration of building envelope systems and assemblies, structural systems, environmental control systems, life safety systems, and the measurable outcomes of building performance.
- 5\. Health, Safety and Welfare in the Built Environment (NAAB SC.1): students will understand the impact of the built environment on human health, safety, and welfare at multiple scales, from buildings to cities.
- 6\. Professional Practice (NAAB SC.2): students will understand professional ethics, the regulatory requirements, the fundamental business processes relevant to architecture practice in the United States, and the forces influencing change in these subjects.
