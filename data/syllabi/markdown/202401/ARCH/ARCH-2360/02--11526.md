## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The life cycle of a building – from the mining and processing of its materials, to the energy it consumes over its lifetime and its eventual degradation – has always been part of changing, interconnected and broader environmental and ecological systems. Despite the modern perceived independence of humans and their habitats from this broader context, the scale and nature of today’s building sector on the global environment has placed immense pressure on the architectural profession to design buildings that, through their material selection, form, orientation and climate-specific strategies, mitigate their impacts on the climate and the ecosystem. In this class, an overview understanding of the relationship between the built environment and its context will be provided through an introduction to key environmental theory and principles, climate/microclimate types, thermal comfort and thermal delight theories, as well as an introduction to the fundamentals of building physics as it pertains to building thermodynamics, heat and mass transfer, and airflow. The assignment component of the course will introduce analysis and simulation tools which put the environmental principles covered in course lectures into practice. Projects will run in coordination with the 2nd-year design studio and will focus on site-specific climate analysis and the design of environmental strategies at multiple scales. Projects will serve as an opportunity for the application and design synthesis of student’s understanding of course material.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Environmental Principles in Architecture: Students will demonstrate broad knowledge of the principles, methodologies, and terminology of ecology and sustainability as they apply to architectural design.
- Heat Transfer in Buildings: Students will demonstrate understanding of the fundamental principles of thermodynamics, heat transfer, and airflow and how they affect architectural design and building performance.
- Comfort and Architectural Space: Students will be able to perform a thorough analysis of thermal comfort factors in a built or proposed architectural space, including considerations of temperature, humidity, ventilation, air quality, materiality, and lighting.
- Site Analysis: Students will be able to perform a thorough climate analysis of a given site, including gathering data and performing analysis of air/ground temperatures, solar exposure, prevailing winds, and micro-climate factors. Additional CLOs for Graduate students: In addition to the undergraduate outcomes noted above, graduate students will be able to demonstrate comprehension of course materials through the deep analysis of site design and environmental performance in an architectural case-study.
- Design Application and Representation: Students will be able to integrate environmental principles and passive systems introduced in course lecture materials and assignments into their studio design work through text, schematic drawings/diagrams, and other media. Additional CLOs for Graduate students: In addition to the undergraduate outcomes noted above, graduate students will be able to simulate and apply passive ventilation strategies and shading strategies to their design projects.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

In-person attendance for each class is mandatory. An excused absence is defined as one that was discussed with and approved by the professor at least 24 hours prior to the date of absence, or a family or medical emergency that is confirmed by your physician or an institute official. Two or more unexcused absences will result in a half-letter reduction (5 pts) of your final grade. Students who miss four or more class sessions, may be asked to drop the subject or receive a failing grade.

### Other Course Policies:

**Format:**

The course will be taught and student work will be evaluated by both course faculty. Monday class meetings are typically reserved for lectures and/or quizzes. Thursday class meetings are typically reserved for design exercises, software demonstrations, and workshops. Thursday sessions will typically demonstrate a particular design tool or simulation workflow and launch an associated homework assignment.

Brief quizzes will cover the past reading assignments and materials covered in course lectures. Students are expected to keep a sketchbook or notebook dedicated to this course. This sketchbook will be the ONLY resource permitted to be used on the course quiz (no laptops, phones, or printed versions of lectures and slides readings will not be permitted). Exceptions for taking the quiz after the date listed in the course schedule will be made on a case-by-case basis and should be arranged in-advance with course faculty.

Select course assignments and projects will align with the student groups, site and design brief given in Architectural Design Studio 4. Students not currently enrolled in AD4 will be given adapted assignments and projects.

**Course Announcements & Communication:**

Course announcements will be made at the beginning of each class. The class will make use of a class-wide WebEx space for correspondence, file-sharing, announcements and questions. Lecture slides and supplemental materials will be uploaded to both LMS and the WebEx space. For personal matters or excused absences, email BOTH course faculty (pekdee@rpi.edu + stradm3@rpi.edu).
