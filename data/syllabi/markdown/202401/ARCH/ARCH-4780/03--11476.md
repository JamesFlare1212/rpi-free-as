## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- OUTCOME 1 Research: 1a. abilty to use / develop specialized knowledge - Students will have the ability to work with Computer Numerical Control (CNC) fabrication of concrete formwork for armoring units and sea wall panels in marine habitats; 1b. abilty to use / develop broad and integrative knowledge - Students will have the ability to derive performance criteria from an understanding of local site and ecology. Students will have the ability to identify and analyze waterfront resiliency tactics used on selected precedent projects.
- OUTCOME 2 Intellectual Design Skills: 2a. ability to design - Students will have the ability to develop designs in response to perfromance criteria, structural requirements, fabrication tolerances, and aggregation strategies through a direct engagement with the fabrication processes; 2b. ability to design using information resources - Students will have the ability to develop designs utilizing site survey information and publicly available resources from the NYC website (3d info, GIS, elevation points, etc.); 2c. ability to design engaging diverse perspectives - Students will have the ability to develop public-facing proposals and develop written and visual language to explain the proposal to the general public which will consist of diverse stakeholders; 2d. ability to design with ethical reasoning - Students will have the ability to develop proposals that engage both human and non-human species as part of a shared habitat; 2e. ability to design with quantitative fluency - Students will have the ability to develop their own prescriptive requirements based on readings, site observation, precedent analysis, and study of NYC waterfront ecology; 2f. ability to design with communicative fluency - Students will have the ability to articulate research and design proposals in written and verbal formats.
- OUTCOME 3 Collaboration: 3a ability to collaborate with team - Students will have the ability to work in a professional and respectful manner within teams of two. Students will have the ability to plan work and assign tasks in an equitable manner. Students will have the ability to exchange design ideas and coordinate efforts for a successful design synthesis; 3b ability to collaborate with a community partner - Students will have the ability to work in a professional and respectful manner with civic and professional partners.
- OUTCOME 4 Civic and Global Engagement: 4a. ability to engage the civic realm - Students will have the ability to engage in meaningful and respectful dialogue with a civic partner and to work with local habitats; 4b. ability to engage the global realm - Students will have the ability to engage in meaningful proposals for shoreline restoration and urban waterfront resilience with respect to storm surge and sea level rise.
- OUTCOME 5 Design Execution: 5a. Midterm Review - Students will have the ability to present analysis and design proposals to guests verbally and with visual documentation; 5b. Final Review - Students will have the ability to present analysis and design proposals to guests verbally and with visual documentation. Studens will have the ability to prove efficacy of fabrication through realized full-scale prototypes.

Grades will be established according to the following policy:

A (EXCELLENT) The design, analysis and fabrication work is exceptional in every way. It has been undertaken with uncommon insight and energy and the ideas have been pursued with criticality and creativity. The project shown a strong ability to integrate systems and exceed the functional requirements. The results demonstrate the ability to innovate.

B (GOOD) Project is a creative and energetic response to the program, the work is timely, complete and well presented. Performance above the norm; accurate and complete; beyond the minimum requirements of the course; work demonstrates marked progress and initiative.

C (AVERAGE) Satisfactory work that meets minimum requirements and demonstrates satisfactory comprehension, communication skills, and effort; demonstrates little initiative to investigate the problem without substantial input from the instructor; work shows little improvement.

D (INFERIOR) Unsatisfactorily meets minimum requirements; demonstrates minimum comprehension, communication skills, and effort at an inferior level; initiative lacking; improvement not noticeable. The work is not produced and submitted on time, or is incomplete.

F (FAIL) Does not meet minimum requirements; fails to adequately demonstrate comprehension, communication skills, and effort. Major project presentations may be missed or have inadequate or missing components or the work does not demonstrate the care and thoroughness befitting a professional education.

Attendance and Participation are assumed and expected and can only count to reduce your grade if lack of either becomes a concern. As part of our ongoing assessment of the learning outcomes, students will be required to complete a Student Self-Assessment Survey prior to receiving a grade for the semester.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**ACADEMIC INTEGRITY**

The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Academic dishonesty violates the trust between students and teachers. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach. Teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own performance. Violations of this trust undermines the educational process. Cheating, plagiarism (defined as the lack of attribution or citation of the work of others), willful damage or theft of another person’s work violate academic integrity. The first instance of the breach of this trust will merit a failing grade in this course and the violator(s) will be reported to the Dean of Students for disciplinary action.
