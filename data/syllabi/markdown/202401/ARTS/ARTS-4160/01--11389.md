## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course builds on the skill and knowled in computer music programming, applications, and performance developed in ARTS 2020 Music & Technology I. It is directed to undergraduate students as an upper-level seminar guiding their progress through the composition of a significant musical work or the design and development of a significant research project. The class is divided between a group seminar, focusing on aesthetic, theoretical, and technical issues, and a workshop/lab in musical systems design, performance, computer music applications, and composition.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

The course will focus on the Max software environment.

- **Max:** Formerly known as Max/MSP, a popular and flexible visual programming language for interactive sound and media. There are a large number of highly developed externals, some created by institutions such as IRCAM and CNMAT, and can interface with other programming languages. Max has a free 30-day evaluation period, aftwards it must be purchased. There are two purchase models:
  - $250 - full license

  - $59 - annual license

  - [https://cycling74.com/shop](https://cycling74.com/shop) - be sure to go to “Students & Teachers” tab.

  - There is also a monthly license available for $9.99/mo.

  - Linux Users: Max is not available for Linux operating systems, so students may use PureData with the Cyclone externals installed. Note that PureData does not have the same tutorials and packages available as Max, and students will need to be flexible and adapat as needed. (Ideally, Linux users are used to this - that why you use Linux!)

    **Laptop Computer**  
    Students are required to bring their laptop to every class. Windows or Mac operating systems will work with all the software available, and are recommended. A wide range of laptops are possible, but the machine must be powerful enough to do basic audio processing: most recent models will be fine. Linux users will need to use alternate software.

    **Headphones**  
    Students are required to bring a set of headphones to class in order to be able to work on in-class audio projects without disturbing each other. It is highly recommended that you purchase a decent pair of headphones if you do not already have some. See the handout “Listening to Recorded Music” for a discussion on the topic and some recommendations.

    Being able to hear the depth and detail in the sound is crucial to success in the class:
    - Do NOT use the free earbuds that came with your computer/phone/etc.
    - Computer speakers are notoriously poor at audio reproduction, so do not do compositional/audio processing work using only computer speakers.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate intermediate-level skill in using Max software.
- Utilize MIDI and OSC as communication protocols for musical applications.
- Demonstrate intermediate-level facility with electronic-synthesis and sound design.
- Demonstrate and awareness of current applications of technology in musical and sound art contexts.
- Demonstrate skills in networked music.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Participation**  
This is a hands-on class. This includes working with the relevant software in class during lecture/demonstrations. There will be several in-class projects to be worked on individually or in groups as well. Participation also includes discussion and analysis of listening sessions and other projects. Your computers will be open during the session: you are expected to pay full attention during the entire session. Multitasking impedes your ability to learn the material. Please resist the temptation to check email, Instagram, surf the web, do other homework, etc. Students who are consistently caught doing outside activities will be asked to leave the class session and will receive a grade reduction. Let’s not make this a “thing”. . .

**Events/Writing assignments**  
Students are required to attend two events (concerts, films, etc) which can be at EMPAC or other local venues. A list of events will be posted in LMS. Short writing assignments based on these events are required.

**Final Project**  
The focus of the course is to develop and execute a significant project using the computer as a creative agent. This project will require additional investigation into an areas (such as software, Max objects, etc) beyond what has been directly covered in class. In-class time will be given throughout the semester to work on the project and get feedback from the professor and your peers. There will additionally be a series of deadlines through the semester to ensure progress goals are being met. Projects will be presented in the last few days of class.
