## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

### ![](https://learn.content.blackboardcdn.com/3900.82.0-rel.45+82d6e90/images/ci/icons/generic_updown.gif) Course Description

[![Course Description item options](https://learn.content.blackboardcdn.com/3900.82.0-rel.45+82d6e90/images/ci/icons/cmlink_generic.gif)](https://lms.rpi.edu/webapps/blackboard/content/listContentEditable.jsp?content_id=_673450_1&course_id=_18873_1#contextMenu) [![Hide Details](https://learn.content.blackboardcdn.com/3900.82.0-rel.45+82d6e90/images/ci/ng/portlet_contract.gif)](https://lms.rpi.edu/webapps/blackboard/content/listContentEditable.jsp?content_id=_673450_1&course_id=_18873_1#)

The current popularity of 3D audio is the latest development in the long history of spatializing sound that reaches back into antiquity. This seminar provides an overview of the history, praxis, and aesthetics of locational space as a compositional element in music and sonic art. Following a brief introduction to the psychoacoustics of sound localization, the course will survey works ranging from Renaissance polychoral masses and multi-ensemble compositions to contemporary electronic works, sound installations, and multilocation pieces. In coordination with EMPAC, the course will provide an overview of current technologies and formats for 3D sound simulation including surround sound, live diffusion, ambisonics, wavefield synthesis, binaural audio, and virtual environments. Students will also have access to an extensive repertoire of historic and contemporary spatial works.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Unit I: Spatial Listening

- Considerations of Sound and Space
- Introduction to Acoustics and Psychoacoustics

Unit II: Acoustic Spatial Music

- Europe: Renaissance and Baroque
- Europe and America: Claissacl, Romantic, and Early Modernism
- The 20th Century

Unit II: Electronic Media Works

- Consumer Formats of the 70s and early 2000s
- Quadrophonic Jazz and Funk
- Multichannel Electronic Works
- Binaural Composition
- Multilocationality and Sound Installation

Unit IV: Contemporary Formats and Creative Processes

- Sound Spatialization Formats
- Spatial Techniques
- Final Projects

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Ability to identify historical periods of spatial music and their associated practices.
- Be able to analyze spatial works in the context of acoustic and psychoacoustic considerations.
- Ability to articulate the relationship between technological developments and spatial music practices.
- Ability to identify and evaluate contemporary sound spatialization techniques.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

In class discussions are essential to the success of the class. Students are expected participate in all class discussions. In addition, the following assignments will take place over the course of the semester.

- Presentation on historical composer or work of spatial music prior to 1900.
- Presentation on 20th or 21st century composer or work of spatial music.
- Final project. The Final Project will be developed in consultation with the instructor. It can take one of two forms:
  - Research paper
  - Creative project (composition, piece of software, or other type of creative endeavor)
