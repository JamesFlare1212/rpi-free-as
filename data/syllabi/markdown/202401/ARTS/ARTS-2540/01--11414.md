## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Contributions to class discussions, summaries of readings, course communication skills: (10% of final grade):**

Communication and correspondence: This is a “Communication Intensive” course and you should communicate/correspond via email according to academic standards, see “Communication with each other” “email protocol” section, below.

Class discussions: The class discussions will be founded on the lectures, required readings, presentations of artworks, films/videos, and media, and reading assignments. You will be graded on how well you participate in course discussions and if you are prepared with summaries of the readings.

Required readings: The required readings are a key element of the class discussion. You are required to have downloaded or printed electronic texts for each week's readings that are marked with an "*".

Readings with an "*" indicate that there is a thesis/theses or key idea/s presented in this text (as opposed to primarily factual knowledge, definitions, dates, etc.).

Short summaries, thesis statements, or questions regarding each of the required readings that are marked with an asterisk (*) should be prepared for class and you may be asked to read these summaries or describe them in class discussion. These summaries will be your “study guide” for the sections of the in-class midterm and final essays. If, for some reason, you cannot prepare your summaries for class, please email me before class.

One common and helpful way to prepare reading assignments for class discussion is to select important quotes, with page numbers, so you can reference and read these in our class discussion.

**Laptops/Electronic Devices:** The use of laptops, phones, electronic devices for non-class related activity will result in a lower final grade for the course. Three instances of such mis-use during class-time will result in a reduction of your final grade.

**Notes:** It is expected that students will take notes during class, and your attention to this matter may affect your class participation grade. If you have a learning issue and note taking is a distraction, or, for some other reason, taking notes is a hindrance to your learning, please contact me about this.

**Summary of overall class participation guidelines**: If you do not contribute to class discuss or go online regularly using electronic devices for non-class activity, or never take notes: this is an D or F grade for the class participation portion of your grade; very occasional participation or some non-class online engagement with electronic devices or very occasional note taking is a C, C, or C+; some participation with contributions to discussion, with some notes, and no online mis-use, is a B-, B, or B+; and full participation with regular contributions to discussions and notes, and no on-line mis-use, an A-, A, or A+.

**Microsoft Word:** All assignments must be submitted in Microsoft word, which is available for free via RPI 360, see this link to access: https://itssc.rpi.edu/hc/en-us/articles/360009791772-Microsoft-Office-Suite-Application-Installation-instructions

**Two Presentations/Papers: Two 4-minute presentations and two minimum-2-page papers, with additional endnotes and bibliography of your sources (17.5% each of final grade):**

There will be two presentations/papers based on a work of art you will select from a list of topics related to the course content. The presentations should be 4 minutes and the summary/essays should be at a minimum 2 pages, with additional pages of supplementary Chicago Manual of Style humanities “notes-and-bibliography-style” endnotes (not footnotes) and bibliography. The essays should be in Microsoft word, double-spaced with a standard-sized font, such as New Times Roman or Calibri, in 12-point font. The endnotes can be single-spaced. The first will be due near the middle of the semester, the second near the end of the semester. See below for more detailed paper “Research/writing standards and formatting” requirements.

**Presentation/paper Topics**: All topics must be approved by Professor Staniszewski. The presentations will be an opportunity to integrate students’ particular interests into the course.

**Sources:** You must consult and cite a minimum of two books, or one book and two scholarly articles, or four scholarly articles for these papers. Do not use Wikipedia or blogs as your sources. If there are not sufficient scholarly sources, this will lower the grade by an entire grade. For example, rather than an A, the paper will be a B.

**Mid-term and Final Course-Summary Essays (midterm 25% and final 30% of final grade):**

There will be a take-home midterm and a final essays based on the course topics and art works presented in the class lectures, and the required readings for the course. The essays should be a minimum of 6 pages, with additional pages of supplementary Chicago Manual of Style “notes-and-bibliography-style” endnotes (not footnotes) and bibliography. The essays should be in Microsoft word, double-spaced with a standard-sized font, such as New Times Roman or Calibri, in 12-point font. The endnotes can be single-spaced. The final essays will be cumulative. These essays will be structured as “take home” essays. See below for more detailed essays “Research/writing standards and formatting” requirements.

**Attendance at two Arts Department or EMPAC exhibitions or performances:**

Attend at two arts events at EMPAC or an Arts Department [iEAR Presents] event, or an event at Troy’s Sanctuary for Independent Media is required. A visit to a modern or contemporary art museum or gallery can be substitute for these events, but this substitution must be approved by Prof. Staniszewski. A summary of, or your comments about, your visit to these two events will be an element of your midterm and final course essays. See info/links at “Arts Events @ Rensselaer/Troy” section of syllabus.

**Research/writing standards and formatting requirements for Essays:**

**Microsoft word:** All assignments must be submitted in Microsoft word, which is available for free via RPI 360, see this link to access: https://itssc.rpi.edu/hc/en-us/articles/360009791772-Microsoft-Office-Suite-Application-Installation-instructions

**Notes and Bibliography:** Use Chicago Manual of Style humanities “Notes and Bibliography” style endnotes (end of document) not footnotes (bottom of page). Please note: “footnote” is a generic term for footnotes and endnotes; however, footnotes and endnotes are formatted similarly, but are placed in different areas of a text. Endnotes, not footnotes, are required and do not use in-text citations (“Author-Date” style citations), the latter are not “footnotes/endnotes.” If there are no endnotes and bibliography for your essays, your grade will be lowered by at least one grade, for example, rather than A, the paper will be a B. If your overall endnote and bibliographic style is incorrect, the grade will be lower by half a grade for the first paper, and a whole grade for the second.

See sources at the end of the syllabus, for example, the Chicago quick guide: https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/tools_citationguide/citation-guide-1.html and other related guidelines at the end of the syllabus and on the Assignment Checklist.

RPI’s writing center, “The Center for Communication and Design,” can help you with the writing and formatting of your assignments.

**Checklist:** Assignment guidelines in the form of a “Checklist” should be submitted with each assignment. The checklist is available on LMS.

**Sample papers:** Examples of papers with Chicago Style endnotes and bibliography is available on LMS.

**Late assignments:** are not accepted, or this lateness will affect the grade, depending on circumstances as determined by Prof. Staniszewski. However, a request for an extension can be made before the due date, and this may be approved by Staniszewski, depending on circumstances as determined by Staniszewski. This lateness may not affect the grade, as determined by Staniszewski.

**Extra Credit Assignment:** If you are in between grades, this extra credit assignments can help you attain the higher grade, but your grade will depend on all the other grading factors as well.

**Timeline:**

It is strongly recommended that you create a timeline of the course, which you should start at the beginning of the course. This is a study tool, and can be handed in at the end of the course as an “extra credit” assignment. How to create a timeline will be reviewed in class.

**LAPTOP/ELECTRONIC DEVICES:** The use of laptops, phones, and electronic devices for non-class related activity will result in a lower final grade for the course. Three instances of such mis-use during class-time will result in reduction by half of a grade of your final grade.

**GRADING/EVALUATION:**

A: Excellent presentations or essays, distinguished by originality of topic, and depth of research. Essays have good grammar, are spell-checked, and formatting requirements are met. Active and consistent class participation, no issues regarding being distracted for long periods on computer.

B: Good quality presentations or essays, with solid research. Essays have good grammar, are spell-checked, and formatting requirements met. Some participation in class, no issues regarding being distracted for long periods on computer

C: Minimal work for presentations or essays. Essays have satisfactory grammar, may have some issues with bibliography, citations, spelling, and formatting requirements. Very occasional participation in class, possible issues regarding being distracted on computer.

D: Poor quality work for presentations and essays with the latter having incorrect content, insufficient length, sloppy bibliographies and citations, and incorrect formatting, spelling and grammar. Consistently distracted for long periods on computer. Two unexcused absences.

F: Extremely inadequate work for presentations or essays, or incomplete assignments. Essays with an incorrect content, insufficient length, incorrect bibliographies, no citations when necessary, incorrect formatting, spelling and grammar. Consistently distracted for long periods on computer. Three or more unexcused absences.

Students are not permitted to re-do or re-submit presentations and essays. However, if improvements are made during the semester, there will be a very strong emphasis on this improved work when determining the final grade.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**ACADEMIC INTEGRITY:**

Academic integrity is described in the *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities Handbook* (May 22, 2023) as follows:

“Intellectual integrity and credibility are the foundation of all academic work. A violation of Academic Integrity policy is, by definition, considered a flagrant offense to the educational process. It is taken seriously by students, faculty, and Rensselaer and will be addressed in an effective manner."

If found responsible for committing academic dishonesty, a student may be subject to one or both types of penalties: an academic (grade) penalty administered by the professor and/or disciplinary action through the Rensselaer judicial process described in this handbook.

Academic dishonesty is a violation of the Grounds for Disciplinary Action as described in this handbook. A student may be subject to any of the following types of disciplinary action should disciplinary action be pursued by the instructor: disciplinary warning; disciplinary probation; disciplinary suspension, expulsion and/or alternative actions as agreed on by the student and hearing officer.

It should be noted that no student who allegedly commits academic dishonesty will be able to drop or change the grade option for the course in question and is not eligible to request an F for the course….” See Handbook, page 16, https://rpi.app.box.com/s/itdt9diqmjkfj2luqhe8irxf99f9pntu

See Handbook for types of academic dishonesty, such as plagiarism, and for more information, see Handbook, pages 16-18.

Plagiarism, for example, is quoting or very closely paraphrasing a text without citing the source. All quotes, citations of data, and close paraphrases should be footnoted with a citation to the original source. If you are uncertain about how to properly source material in your assignments for this course, check with the Professor or TA about the proper procedures. Guidelines for proper sources will be reviewed in class and are listed at the end of the syllabus.
