## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- --Graduate students will analyze and demonstrate knowledge of the history and theory modern and contemporary art practices with an emphasis on “criticality,” interdisciplinarity, intersectionality, and technological innovation. --Graduate students will analyze and demonstrate knowledge of key works, practices, and theories and will write analyses related to course topics.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**REQUIREMENTS, which are course assessment measures and include grading criteria:**

**General contribution to class discussions based on presentations, class topics, and required readings (20%):** The course discussion will be based on lectures, discussion of class topics, and required readings. This may also be augmented by grad student brief presentations. Required readings will be available online or at LMS website: https://lms.rpi.edu/webapps/login/

When discussing texts, students are required to have access to the required readings for the class sessions. It is suggested that a short summary (featuring the thesis or key ideas of the text) or questions be written up as preparation for class discussion and this will be the basis of your annotated bibliography, see requirement #3 below. One common and helpful way to prepare readings for class discussion is to select important quotes, with page numbers, so you can reference and read these in our discussion. For most class sessions, a grad student will be selected to begin class discussion on a required text.

In some instances, grad students may also want to present contributions to class discussion due to attendance at an event or a specific interest in a course-related issue, work, or reading.

It is recommended to begin to think of your research and readings in the Arts PhD program in terms of historicized and theoretically-informed notes and annotated bibliographies. See requirement #3 below.

**Two summary-assessment-essays, based on the course content. (25% for mid-term assessment, 30% for the final summary assessment.) The key texts and works are bolded and noted with an *.**

It is recommended that you create an annotated timeline of creative works and annotated bibliography.

**One research essay based on course topics, lecture presentations, discussions, or readings (25% of grade).** The length of the essay should be at a minimum 7 double-spaced pages, not counting notes or bibliography. The essay may be submitted anytime during the semester, but no later than the last day of course.

Research and Formatting Requirements for Written Assignments:

**Formatting: *Chicago Manual of Style* humanities-style** (“notes and bibliography”) endnotes (not footnotes) and bibliography are required for the papers. See last pages of syllabus for more *Chicago Manual*guidelines and sources. Please note: Endnotes not footnotes are required: No in-text citations (author date), the latter are not “footnotes/endnotes.”

**Microsoft word:** All assignments must be submitted in Microsoft word, double-spaced with a standard-sized font, such as New Times Roman or Calibri, in 12-point font.

Microsoft word is available for free via RPI 360, see this link to access: https://itssc.rpi.edu/hc/en-us/articles/360009791772-Microsoft-Office-Suite-Application-Installation-instructions

**Checklist:** Guidelines in the form of a checklist, will be distributed and is on LMS, and should be submitted with the paper.

Sample paper: An example of a paper with Chicago Style endnotes and bibliography is available on LMS.

**Extensions:** If, for some extremely good reasons, an assignment cannot be completed on time, a request can be made for an extension. This must be done before the due date, and given the circumstances, this may be approved by M.A. Staniszewski. But approval must be obtained to avoid a possible reduction in your grade.

**Revisions and re-doing assignments:** There are no make-up assignments; however, the final grade depends on cumulative performance and the degree of attainment of the goals of the course outcomes by the end of the semester.

**Annotated timeline bibliography and study guide (recommended):** As a study guide, it is recommended that graduate students develop an annotated timeline and an annotated bibliography of art practices, texts, and theories set within aesthetic-cultural-socio-political frameworks related to the subject matter of the course and their creative work and research. These can serve as foundations/templates for their doctoral exams and dissertation requirements.

**GRADING EVALUATION/CRITERIA:**

A: Excellent assessments or essays, distinguished by originality of topic, and depth of research, evidence of broad historical/conceptual frameworks bolstered by specificity of references. Assessments or essays have good grammar, are spell-checked, and formatting requirements are met. Active and consistent class participation, no issues regarding being distracted for long periods on computer.

B: Good assessments or essays, with solid research with sufficient sense of historical/conceptual frameworks and references. Assessments or essays have good grammar, are spell-checked, and formatting requirements met. Some participation in class, no issues regarding being distracted for long periods on computer

C: Minimal work on assessments or essays. Assessments or essays have somewhat satisfactory grammar, may have some issues with bibliography, citations, spelling, and formatting requirements. Very occasional participation in class, possible issues regarding being distracted on computer.

D: Poor quality work for assessments or essays with the latter having incorrect content, insufficient length, sloppy bibliographies and citations, and incorrect formatting, spelling and grammar. Two unexcused absences. Consistently distracted for long periods on computer.

F: Extremely inadequate work for assessments or essays, or incomplete assignments. Essays with incorrect content, insufficient length, incorrect bibliographies, no citations when necessary, incorrect formatting, spelling and grammar. Three or more unexcused absences. Consistently distracted for long periods on computer.

**GRADE APPEALS:**

**Internal to the course for assignments:**

Grading in the course is based on the process of learning and improving. There will not be chances for re-doing papers or presentations. However, if improvements are made during the semester, there will be a strong emphasis on this improved work when determining a grade.

**External to the course for final grades:**

Please see the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities for policies on gradings and students’ rights and responsibilities, particularly pages 16 to 18.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**ACADEMIC INTEGRITY:**

Academic integrity is described in the *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities Handbook* (May 22, 2023) as follows:

“Intellectual integrity and credibility are the foundation of all academic work. A violation of Academic Integrity policy is, by definition, considered a flagrant offense to the educational process. It is taken seriously by students, faculty, and Rensselaer and will be addressed in an effective manner.

If found responsible for committing academic dishonesty, a student may be subject to one or both types of penalties: an academic (grade) penalty administered by the professor and/or disciplinary action through the Rensselaer judicial process described in this handbook.

Academic dishonesty is a violation of the Grounds for Disciplinary Action as described in this handbook. A student may be subject to any of the following types of disciplinary action should disciplinary action be pursued by the instructor: disciplinary warning; disciplinary probation; disciplinary suspension, expulsion and/or alternative actions as agreed on by the student and hearing officer.

It should be noted that no student who allegedly commits academic dishonesty will be able to drop or change the grade option for the course in question and is not eligible to request an F for the course….” See Handbook, page 16, https://rpi.app.box.com/s/itdt9diqmjkfj2luqhe8irxf99f9pntu

See Handbook for types of academic dishonesty, such as plagiarism, and for more information, see Handbook, pages 16-18.

Plagiarism, for example, is quoting or very closely paraphrasing a text without citing the source. All quotes, citations of data, and close paraphrases should be footnoted with a citation to the original source. If you are uncertain about how to properly source material in your assignments for this course, check with the Professor or TA about the proper procedures. Guidelines for proper sources will be reviewed in class and are listed at the end of the syllabus.

**Academic Integrity Statement for all 4000 level plus syllabi:**

*The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* and *The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement to the Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities 2014-2016* define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own performance. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

*The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* and *The Rensselaer Graduate Student Supplement* define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.[1] In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty. If found in violation of the academic honesty policy, students may be subject to two types of penalty. The instructor administers an academic [grade] penalty and the student is reported to the Dean of Students or the Dean of Graduate Education as appropriate. The first violation results in 0 grade for that assignment. The second violation results in failure of the course. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
