### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**LEARNING OUTCOMES**

*Students who complete this course will be able to:*

- Learn how to use art and creative practice to support community animation and development.
- Identify problems presented in a professional context and devise solutions.
- Communicate verbally, visually, and with written words as required in a professional environment.
- Apply creative skills and knowledge of technology to practical applications.
- Integrate into a professional context to execute effective work, seeing the project from idea to production to realization.
- Develop and manage projects in a non-academic context.
- Develop your professional portfolio and resume with real-world applications, uniquely positioning for the job market upon graduation.
- Theorize and reflect through research and experience with host institution in relation to contemporary trends in art and social practice particularly community building using technology.

This course provides students with experience in the practical application of arts practices in the community. It is good for professional development across disciplines, as well as strengthening relationships between RPI and the community. *On a graduate level, the course provides needed experience for those whose research involves social practice and community engagement.*

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**POLICY on ACADEMIC INTEGRITY**

**Student-teacher relationships are built on trust.** For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

***ONLINE CODE OF CONDUCT***

Participants are responsible for knowing and abiding by these rules.

- Participate in an authentic and active way. In doing so, you contribute to the health and longevity of our online classroom experience.
- Exercise consideration and respect in your speech and actions.
- Attempt collaboration before conflict.
- No harassment, including offensive comments, negative behavior and sustained disruption of discussion

We're a mix of many people with many backgrounds and levels of experience. To maintain a safe and productive working and learning space for student and teacher relationships:

- Be welcoming, friendly and patient
- Be considerate and respectful
- Be careful in the words that we choose
- Be a supportive community builder

Guidelines for communications

- Keep muted unless you are talking
- Keep video on (unless technical challenges arise)
- Avoid distortion from back and forth conversation as much as possible

The RENSSELAER HANDBOOK defines various forms of Academic Dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. All forms are violations of the trust between students and teachers. Students should familiarize themselves with this portion of the RENSSELAER HANDBOOK and should note that the penalties for plagiarism and other forms of cheating can be quite harsh. A grade of zero will be given on the first assignment where a violation is detected. If there is a subsequent infraction the student will receive a grade of F for the course. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.

At the same time, often creative and communicative activities depend on collaboration. I do not expect you to work in total isolation; that's not the way that the best projects are done. I encourage you to bounce ideas off each other, offer each other suggestions on how to improve assignments, designs, etc. When you draw upon other sources or collaborate with others, give credit where it is due. Ask me if you have any questions or concerns about particular situations. Please consider, in developing any project you plan to publish (putting it on a portfolio website, for instance), to do so in accord with US Copyright Law. For example, all text, graphics, and other materials posted on a website/portfolio must be your original work, or posted with permission of the copyright owner, or using Creative Commons copyright indicators (http://creativecommons.org/license/).

Students are expected to use gender-neutral language in their writing and discussion.
