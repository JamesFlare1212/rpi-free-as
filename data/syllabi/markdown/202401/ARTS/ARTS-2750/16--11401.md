### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

Arts 2750-16: Private Music Lessons: Percussion

**All percussion students are required to have the following:**

- \- A key for West Hall Room 321 – request this from the Arts Office on the first floor of West Hall. 321 is where we will meet for all lessons, and where you can practice as well.
- \- Pencils for every lesson and rehearsal
- \- a metronome or metronome app
- \- a drum key
- \- a practice pad (Vic Firth 12” or equivalent gum-rubber style pad)
- \- Stick and mallet requirements will vary depending on the repertoire being studied, but you must bring the necessary equipment to lessons.

The 'Course Text' (see below) includes the basic materials we will be using during lessons, not including solo repertory or additional exercises. You should purchase these on your own, rather than using copies; building your library of repertory and teaching materials is essential for your own teaching and performing in the future. You need not purchase all of these right away – I’ll let you know what’s needed as we progress through the semester.

Additionally, of course you need sticks, but there might be additional items as well. The school does have a supply of mallets, but additional personal purchases are strongly encouraged as appropriate.

**Suggested Sticks/Brushes (to be purchased according to lesson needs):**

2 pair Vic Firth drum set sticks (5A, AJ1, Erskine “Big Band” or comparable)

1 pair Vic Firth HB Heritage Brushes or Regal 583 (classic) or 596 (Jeff Hamilton)  
2 pair medium marimba (or vibraphone) mallets- Dragonfly, MarimbaOne, Innovative, etc.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The goals of primary and secondary percussion studies in the RPI percussion studio are as follows:  
1\) Maximize the growth of students’ musicianship  
2\) Explore percussion techniques for application to any musical situation  
3\) Provide students with an opportunity to explore the variety of instruments that percussion offers

Specific semester-by-semester goals will vary from student to student. The skills we work on in lessons are meant to be applied to all the playing you do both in school and out and are geared toward a lifelong pursuit of improving your musicality.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Each lesson normally has at least one assignment that is a 'performance' (etude, play along song, etc.). Additionally, each lesson includes material that builds a foundation in which to perform on that percussion instrument (whether it is snare, drum set, mallets, timpani, etc.).  
A successful performance along with understanding and demonstration of the needed concepts and techniques is the intended outcome.  
However, lessons should not be thought of as primarily performance oriented/auditions, etc. The primary 'outcome' is that learning is taking place. Even mistakes are an important part of that. The assessment is totally based on the learning gained through time and effort shown by the student. RPI is not a conservatory, and a perfect performance is never a requirement for an A for the week or the semester. All aspects of insight and furthering of technique on the part of the student will be considered as a primary component of the outcome and assessment.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Primary Lessons**: The final grade for this course is a balance between the studio grade (weekly preparation for lessons), performances, if applicable, and the final lesson assignment.  
75% (lessons, or 50% lesson and 25% performances if applicable)  
25% (final assignment)

**Secondary Lessons**: The final grade for this course will depend on the successful completion of goals established by Mr. Foster and the student over the course of the semester.  
Students are always encouraged to discuss their progress and the instructor is open to any process to improve the student's final grade (e.g.-through additional remedial material or videos submitted to improve previous lesson assignments, etc.)

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
