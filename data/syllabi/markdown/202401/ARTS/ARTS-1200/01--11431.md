## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- The student will acquire and / or improve upon their understanding of and their facility in manipulating the following concepts: 1. Observational measuring and space mapping 2. Line quality, sensitivity, and variance 3. Proportional accuracy and spatial description 4. Linear and atmospheric perspective systems 5. Structural systems of various simple forms 6. Development of surface and texture 7. Translation of light information through the manipulation of value 8. Control of canonical 2-dimensional design paradigms

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Basic Drawing is an introductory course in free-hand drawing, where practicing and developing an appropriate set of mark-making skills is utilized in the service of producing a believable image. The primary emphasis of this course is the close observation and subsequent understanding of the structure of forms, the physical relationship they have to one another, and to the space they inhabit relative to the viewer.

Studio work is performed from direct observation. Projects involve still life, live model, architectural and landscape sources. The course is a “hands-on” studio experience, supported by studies in the history of art, using examples from the Masters and contemporary artists.

Critical abilities are developed through group discussions and critiques. Homework assignments when given are related directly to the production of work done in the studio for graded projects.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work.
