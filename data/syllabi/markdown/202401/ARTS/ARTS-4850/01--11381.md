## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- After successfully completing this course, students will be able to: • Interpret, compare and critique a variety of forms of contemporary art • Demonstrate a daily artistic process which is optimized to short or long-term goals • Articulate a personal philosophy toward contemporary art and artmaking • Demonstrate practical skills of the working artist • Work with a team to produce a professionally run visual arts Public Exhibition • Document work for accessibility for future research

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- Finalize concepts and ideas for the Project
- Produce a model (physical or digital) of the space in the gallery reflecting how finished work will be seen at the exhibition opening.
- Produce all Project works appropriately prepared and ready for the public Exhibition
- Complete a final version of their Written Thesis
- Work together as a class to plan for and set-up complete Exhibition and Opening public Reception at ACCR in downtown Troy
- Work in teams to provide promotional materials and appropriate hospitality for the exhibition and opening.
- Document all work and tear down Exhibition and clean-up gallery space at ACCR.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Grading**

Grading will be composed of three major components: Did you meet your personal goals? Did you assist in the success of the class accomplishing its collective goals? Did you actively and consistently participate in group discussion relevant to the project?

**Personal Goals**:

- Set weekly benchmarks for yourself with the help of the instructor on Mondays. You will want to set benchmarks that are both achievable and challenging/ambitious. Benchmark progress will be reviewed Thursdays.
- Some weeks your benchmark will be set specifically by the instructor. Some weeks you may spend most of your time in the studio working on your project or as a group planning for the exhibition. There should be no week where you aren’t making art or associated deliverables.

**Collective Goals:**

- You will have regular class discussions to familiarize the pace of your personal progress relative to the group. In these conversations any avenue of assistance will be discussed which will help the group to succeed as a unit.
- The discussions will also cover individual and group responsibilities in relation to the successful scheduling of the exhibition and its various component requirements.

**Discussion:**

- Are you able to reflect on relevant topics in class discussion? Are you putting in the work to constructively critique work produced by classmates to help them make better work? Are you constructively contributing to the planning and actualization of the exhibition and reception.

Based on these three components, you will be assigned weekly points for 15 weeks, with a maximum of 6 points per week of class, for a total of 90 points for the semester. Spring break week is not included in this calculation.

10 extra points will be added after the exhibition for significant contribution to its successful completion.

**Weekly point rubric:**

- 6 points: I made all my goals, made some surprising discoveries, and acted upon them. I actively helped with the success of the group and its collective goals, exceeded expectations, and constructively participated in all relevant class discussions.
- 5.5 points: I made a lot of progress, though I didn’t make every goal. I helped the class in achieving its collective goals and actively participated in most class discussions.
- 5 points: I made some decent progress. I helped others if asked and was available for the group if necessary, and I talked a bit in class discussions.
- 3 points: I made some stuff. I said some things. I hovered like a ghost.
- 0 points: I made no real progress, no contribution to discussions, and offered no help.

Any grade appeal must be brought to the instructor during office hours or at a scheduled time convenient to both parties. Any appeal has the potential to raise or lower your grade. Weekly assessments are available upon request and will be discussed privately. They will not be published online or elsewhere. If you really need to know just ask.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

*The Rensselaer Handbook* defines various forms of academic dishonesty and procedures for responding to them. Students are responsible for familiarizing themselves with these policies. Students should note that the penalty for plagiarism and other forms of cheating is a grade of “F” for the course.

In this class “cheating” consists of plagiarism or representing the work of others as your own. This can include the deliberate or unintentional omission of acknowledgments or references. Cheating also consists of copying images, audio, video, or any creative assets generated by others and submitting that work as your own.
