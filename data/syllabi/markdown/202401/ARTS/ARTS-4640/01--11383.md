## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Outline**

Science Fictions is advanced narrative video production and theory course. The class looks at thematic areas of science fiction, utopia and dystopia, cli fi, ‘slipstream’ fiction (Sterling, Bruce), paranormal and speculative fiction. ‘Speculative fiction’ is a genre that looks at the real world and extends what we know about it, building on the ‘real.’ The class has two threads: to study narrative structure and create a series of sci-fi videos; discuss and analyze mainstream and avant-garde science fictions films and themes from early 1900s-present.

ARTS 4640 is 4 credits.

**Class Description:** Classes will be broken down into lectures, sci-fi film screenings, viewing student video works accompanied with in-class critiques, and presentations of related materials. Experimentation and risk taking is encouraged, including developing hybrid methods of telling a story, and non-traditional approaches to reconsider histories and futures. Pre-figurative ideas, in other words ideas that imagine and embody a utopian society are encouraged. Students will produce videos relating to their own particular area of interest falling under the umbrella of ‘science/speculative fiction.’ There will be readings including science fiction short stories, articles about science fiction history, theory and the production/construction of short film narratives.

**Questions to consider:**

- What does speculative fiction really mean?
- What are socially effective ways of making works of fiction?
- Why is science fiction such a popular genre for contemporary Western culture?

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Assignments:**

**Attendance and Readings and Class Participation****: (20% of grade)**

- **READINGS -** There will be articles assigned to read that you will be asked to discuss in class. Readings will be provided digitally as a PDF. Your knowledge of these readings will be reflected in your class participation grade. **(10% of grade)**
- **CLASS PARTICIPATION -** You will be expected participate in discussion about the readings and the films screened in class. **(10% of grade)**

**Sci Fi Research INTERVIEW Assignment:** **(10% of grade) -** There will be one interview assignment where you will be asked to interview an “expert” in a field of your choice that relates to a sci fi topic of interest for you. 4000 level students: submit 1-2 page report on interview. 6000 level students submit 2 page report.

**Description:**

assignment is still to get you out of the classroom and have some real world encounters to inform The goal of this your work. Procedure:

1. Identify an area that you would like to research – preferably a science-based research area.
2. Find a person, organization, etc. to either visit and or interview. Contact them to ask for a visit or an interview (could be conducted via email or skype). Don’t forget the ‘suck up’ factor (“I so admire your contributions to the field…yada yada”)
3. Make a list of questions about the topic you are interested in investigating. Do some research about the topic and see how you want to approach it (do you want to talk about the history of something, the future, the design, a specific scientific approach, etc.?)
4. Conduct the visit / interview and record it somehow for your own purposes – this can be done with videotape, or audio recording. Remember to get a video /audio release form signed from your ‘subjects’ in case you might want to use this material at a later date in a project.

**Submission:** Write a summary of the interview and what you learned from it. Comment on how this interview/visit inspired and informed the beginnings of the story of your final project – and what story might be (described broadly). Minimum 1200 words – about two pages. Can be longer.

(Note: If the research does not relate to your final project that’s okay. But the idea is to try and trigger some ideas in your final!)

Examples brought up in class included: a visit to a nuclear power plant to view the mechanical design; an interview with an immunologist to discuss the future of allergies; an interview with a virtual reality designer to discuss envisioned futures of VR uses; visiting an animal sanctuary to observe the care of wild animals; interview with a pyrotechnics expert to inform on special effects!

You are welcome to email me to assist you with brainstorming ideas, making suggestions as to how to proceed, contact people, etc. DO NOT wait until the last minute to work on this assignment – you will crash and burn if you do.

You should put at least 6 hours into this assignment – since you are being given two classes of free time to work on this plus homework time!

**Hands-on exercises & projects: (see breakdown below)**

There are three video projects aimed at giving you a chance to use what we're learning in class. Throughout the semester, you will design and work on your own productions which may be a narrative or experimental in form. You will be asked to present and screen film materials to the class throughout the semester, as well as comment on other student’s works. If an assignment is graded poorly, the student may do it over and resubmit to Professor High.

- **Production Exercise #1: (15% of grade)**

Make a film about a future time. But do not be reliant on spoken language to tell your story visually with no spoken word, no voice over or narrator to develop the story. There can only be limited speaking characters in the film. Try to make a story using visual shots and sounds that create a mood. The project is more like a visual/aural poem. Minimum length is 1-3 minutes.

- **Production Exercise #2: (20% of grade)**

Interpret one of the sci fi short stories we have read in class. Open interpretation. Minimum length is 3 minutes. Collaborations are permitted if given prior approval by Professor High.

- **Sci Fi Production Exercise #3 FINAL: (20% of grade)**

Open assignment – anything science fiction! The final assignment can be filmed on any type of recording device – including cell phones. I also will accept audio only projects, films made using still images, animations, and scripts with accompanying storyboards. TBD with Professor.

Films or time-based projects must be at least 3 minutes in length minimum. Collaborations are permitted if given prior approval by Professor High.

THIS PROJECT IS TO BE CONDUCTED IN TANDEM WITH THE BELOW “WRITING ASSIGNMENT.”

**ABSTRACT** must be submitted describing overall project (1-2 paragraphs)

**ROUGH FOOTAGE** for final film must be reviewed with Professor

**FINAL FILM ASSIGNMENT** to be screened in our last classes!!

- **Sci Fi research writing assignment: (15% of grade)[1]**

A paper is required by the end of the semester (2,000 word minimum for 4000 level students. to accompany your final media project relating to the science fiction topics discussed in class and also to the student’s own interests.

The paper should give the “back story” for your final science media project and research. For example, if you are making a film about the coronavirus you probably will have lots of information and articles that you have researched to give you a basis for your project. List these and address in the paper what you read and researched.

The research for this paper could also relate to the “expert” interview conducted earlier in the semester if it relates to this final project.

Most likely you will develop a speculative story from that research. For example, you might make a video about the future of our lives in 10 years after living with the coronavirus. Please write up your rationale and how you considered making the speculative “leap” from the research you found to your sic fi media project.
