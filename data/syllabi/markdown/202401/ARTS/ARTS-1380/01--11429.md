## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is the first part of a three-semester sequence aimed at cultivating theory, analysis, and musicianship skills that will enhance students’ ability to understand and create music in a diverse range of styles and media. Each unit is focused around a general technical principle which is explicated and explored through written work, analysis, dictation, improvisation, prepared performance, and creative practice. Topics include the physics of sound, pitch and rhythmic notation, meters, scales, key signatures, movable DO solfege, intervals, and chords. Students will also engage in expanded listening exercises, helping them to develop and articulate their own approaches and responses to a variety of musical and sonic experiences drawn from contemporary music and sound art.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will gain a fluency in musical notation.
- Students will be able to recognize and identify (both on paper and by ear) the fundamental building blocks of music theory, including pitches, intervals, rhythms, scales, meters, and chords.
- Students will be able to sing in movable-DO solfege.
- Students will begin to develop an individual creative practice.
- Students will develop the foundational skills of critical listening and acquire a vocabulary for articulating what they hear.
- Students will acquire the skills necessary to advance to Music and Sound I.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Class Participation:** This course depends on engaged and open-minded participation in all class activities, including presentations, discussions, workshops, performances, and listening exercises.

**Listening Responses**: Throughout the semester, there will be seven listening exercises, each of which will be accompanied by a written response. Short responses are worth 1 percentage point while the longer responses are worth 2 percentage points.

**In-Class Written Exams**: Each unit, students will take an in-class exam to demonstrate their grasp of the unit’s basic theoretical concepts.

**Analysis Projects**: Students will complete an analysis project for each unit in which they will apply their knowledge of the unit’s material to a specific musical work. These projects are graded on grasp of the theoretical concepts and the accuracy of their application.

**Creative Projects:** Creative work provides students the opportunity to apply theoretical concepts and skills to their own creative practice. Depending on the unit, students are invited to strike a balance between the limitations of the assigned topic and their own creative preferences.

**Performance Projects:** For each unit, there will be an assigned performance project which will provide students with an opportunity to apply the relevant topic to their ongoing practice of musical performance.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
