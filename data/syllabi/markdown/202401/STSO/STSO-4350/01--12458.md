## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

In this class you will study the relationship of the design of useful artifacts to the quality of social and political life. Our inquiries are interdisciplinary, blending materials and approaches from social science, philosophy, engineering, industrial design, architecture, urban planning, and information systems.

We begin with the recognition that the world in which we live is, to a large extent, the product of deliberate planning and construction. The built forms that surround us strongly condition our actions, experiences, and sense of personal and collective possibilities. Relevant forms include material things: buildings, towns and cities, tools, instruments, technological systems, and other furnishings of everyday life. But other significant forms include such intangible "things" as patterns in social rules, roles, relationships, institutions and political constitutions. For our purposes the concept of "design" includes the deliberately chosen enduring forms of both material and intangible entities that affect human relations.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Our purpose is to study (1) social, cultural and political origins of the designs we find around us; (2) ways in which political *ideas* are represented in design; (3) how design affects people's orientation toward power, authority, community, and freedom; (4) how an understanding of the significance of design might influence future attempts (especially your own) to build humane, sustainable, democratic structures to aid human activity.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- (1) Understand the social, cultural and political origins of the designs we find around us;
- (2) Interpret ways in which political ideas are represented in design;
- (3) Discuss how design affects people's orientation toward power, authority, community, and freedom;
- (4) Learn how an understanding of the significance of design might influence future attempts to build humane, sustainable, democratic structures to aid human activity;

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Meetings**:

We will meet twice a week for a seminar style discussion. Attendance will be taken. Everyone should come to class fully prepared to talk about the readings assigned for that week and their own ideas about them.

**Communication Intensive:**

Regular, *active participation is required* and is an important part of your grade. I will call on people directly, more or less at random. As a CI course you will develop and practice key communication skills to carry into the future. The modes of communication in this class will be expressed through writing, speaking, visually and technologically.

**Daily comments and observations:**

For every reading session of the class, I ask that you prepare (1) a brief comment of a sentence or two and (2) a question. I will call on people at random to state their observation or question. Save your comments and questions in one document in chronological order. This document will be submitted to me with your midterm.

**Midterm paper:** There will be one midtermpaperoftwo to five double spaced pages. Drawing from the weekly readings and class discussions select a topic that resonates with you and directly affects your life and academic pursuits. See schedule below for submission date.There is no “right answer” for the midterm essay. The purpose is to demonstrate your grasp of readings and other materials in the course along with your ability to address creatively key questions and concepts.

**Note: The subject of all email sent to me regarding work for this course (including midterm essays) shouldbe POLITICS OF DESIGN.**

**Term project**:

The class will be broken down into teams, ideally 3 students per team, to research and write a paper, about 10 pages double-spaced(with drawings and photos, if appropriate), on a term project covering an aspect of the *social and political dimensions of design.* You’ll propose a topic of special interest to you for study, conduct the research and present your results at the end. Prepare a 10 – 15 minute in class presentation of your term project (I suggest powerpoint or similar). Papers to be submitted the last day of class. All team members will receive the *same grade* for the project. [Note: We may need to schedule an extra session for the oral presentations.]

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Your grade will be determined by: Class participation, midterm essay, group term paper and oral presentation. No late work of any kind will be accepted (unless you have a legitimate excuse – illness, job interview, or family emergency). *You may miss any two daily sessions* without penalty; if you miss more than that, your grade will suffer. All papers, exams and presentations will be graded as follows, using pluses and minuses as needed: Excellent: A; Good: B; Average: C; Very poor: D; Failing: F.

General grading weights:

- Attendance and class participation: 20%
- Midterm paper and Daily comments/questions: 30%
- Final paper and presentation: 50%

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic Honesty: Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. Students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the course, and teachers must trust that the assignments students turn in are their own. Any act that violates this trust undermines the educational enterprise. They contradict our very reason for being at Rensselaer. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities defines various forms of academic dishonesty and the procedures for responding to them. You are expected to have reviewed and understand the Handbook. The following additional policies apply to this course:  
For this course, penalties for academic dishonesty, especially in cases involving false representation of work, may well result in your failing the course, with a citation placed in your academic record. While the process for assessing the situation is as outlined in the Rensselaer Handbook, the initial judgment about a violation, and the penalty if any, will be determined at the discretion of the instructors and what they believe to have occurred. If you have any doubts about a situation, especially regarding plagiarism, please check with us first.
