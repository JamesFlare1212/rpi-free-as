## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This class explores the relationship between human communities and the physical world they find themselves in. We will do so both by taking an environmental perspective on human activity and by taking a humanistic perspective on environmental topics. Through this, we will examine four particular avenues through which this relationship emerges. First, we will consider what it means to treat “the environment” and its closely related terms like “nature” as objects of human study. In other words, what are we talking about when we talk about “environments?” Second, we will use examine the politics of nature and environmental governance. Third, we will look at how the recognition of non-human animals in our daily lives shifts classic social scientific understandings of humanity. Finally, we will examine how the climate crisis poses new challenges for the understandings developed in this course.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Understand the core concepts of the social study of environmental concerns.
- Incorporate a social/humanistic perspective in environmental conversations and an environmental perspective in social/humanistic conversations.
- Compellingly communicate these perspectives in writing and presentations.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students will have in-class presentations, discussion questions, and a semester-long research project leading towards a paper.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).
