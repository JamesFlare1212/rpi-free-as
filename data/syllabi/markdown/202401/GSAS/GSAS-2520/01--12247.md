## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Catalog: This course is designed to provide an overview of the art of effective story development. Students will come away with a heightened awareness of the structures and principles that master storytellers apply to their craft in games and other popular media. It is intended for all GSAS majors and other interested students, as well as the prerequisite for the GSAS concentration in Writing  
Communication Intensive (QuACS)

## Course Goals

### Goals:

- Learn how to identify, analyze, and incorporate elements of story development:
  - Plot Structure
  - Theme
  - Character
    - Motivation
    - Dialogue
  - Worldbuilding
- Create Original Work
  - An Articy Draft project for a semester long story development
  - Daily Writing warm ups
  - Multiple versions/drafts of writings/analyses
- Review Other’s Work
  - Learn how to give constructive (positive and negative) feedback helpfully and respectfully.
- Polish their own writing
  - Use correct structure, grammar, and punctuation.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, AI was used, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. You should consult with others on your work, but you should not hand in work that is a copy of someone else’s. You should never use AI generated work or text/media directly from the internet. Citations are necessary when paraphrasing or directly quoting someone else’s work or your own prior work. Use the APAStyle website for more information: https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines

Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy may result in a penalty of 0 on that assignment. After discussion between the instructor and the student, further violation will result in the failure of the course and a formal report to the appropriate Dean of Students.

If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
