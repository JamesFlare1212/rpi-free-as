## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is designed to provide an overview of the art of effective story development. Students will come away with a heightened awareness of the structures and principles that master storytellers apply to their craft in games and other popular media. It is intended for all GSAS majors and other interested students, as well as the prerequisite for the GSAS concentration in Writing.  
Students will be expected to identify/employ the basic elements of storytelling and apply them to both game-specific projects and writing in general.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The goal is to relay the basic elements and forms of storytelling and then apply them, specifically, to the creation of video games. Students will learn to identify the ways storytelling in video games both overlaps with, and diverges from, traditional narratives.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Short stories; Game history; perspectives; plots; structure; characters; exposition, foreshadowing, planting; Kishotenketsu (four-act story structure); game narrative review; and archetypes/tropes.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to identify traditional storytelling techniques such as plot, characterization, theme, character arcs, and structures (three-act, five-act, etc.) and outline the ways in which they align with, and diverge from, the storytelling presented in video games.
- It is expected students will be able to produce content specifically designed for inclusion in video games including scripts, barks, and character biographies.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

I. Short story -- create original short story utilizing concepts/techniques outlined in lectures.  
Rewrite short story -- Working from critical feedback, rework story.  
Grading criteria: Story content, composition, grasp of fundamentals, ability to work with critical feedback.  
II. Game plots assignment -- Create basic game plot, mechanics, and story structure.  
III. Environmental storytelling assignment -- Recognize the unique aspects of environmental storytelling in video games. Compose an original setting and tie it to a series of game mechanics.  
IV. Game Narrative Review and Presentation -- Compose an in-depth analysis of a recent video game focusing on its story structure, narrative delivery mechanics, and other narrative elements.  
Presentation will be graded on insight and ability to relay information to the class.  
V. Test -- In-class examination, essay-based, to gauge a student's knowledge of the concepts presented over the course of the class.  
VI. Attendance and Participation -- Student will have demonstrated a reasonable attendance record and willingness to engage with the class.
