## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course follows Game Development I. Students work in interdisciplinary teams to create one large-scale 3D game, working from concept to public release. Projects may include games, educational games, serious games and simulations, and interactive artworks, and will focus on creative design, technical execution, and use of game design principles. The course builds on skills and knowledge in previous courses in the GSAS core, including game design, game mechanics, game programming, art, and narrative.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

All students should have an active subscription to Adobe Creative Cloud. Student discount pricing is available through the School of HASS. Teams may choose their own game engine and tools such as Unity, Unreal, Maya, Substance Painter, Photoshop. We will use Perforce for version control, Basecamp for project management, and Discord for general class communication.  
Students are responsible for bringing their laptop and charger to class.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

By successfully completing the course, students will:

- Demonstrate effective team collaboration
- Apply game development techniques
- Demonstrate project and time management skills to build a complete game
- Demonstrate effective written, spoken, and electronic communication
- Analyze and critique peer work

## Course Content

### Content Details:

This course will use an Iterative development model, introducing project management and production processes and Agile software development methods.  
Lectures and tutorials will be adjusted based on the course projects, and may include topics such as 3D art asset pipeline; texturing, rigging, and animation; camera control paradigms; networked multiplayer; navmesh and AI agents; level design; sound design; virtual reality and immersive environments; and other relevant topics.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Collaboration: As game development is a fundamentally collaborative practice, work is assessed as a combination of the team effort and the student’s individual effort.  
Use of third-party assets: All code and assets must represent the student’s own work. Engine standard assets are allowed. Additional third-party assets (models, textures, animations, code frameworks, audio, or other assets) may be used only with permission of the instructor and must be clearly designated. Code examples may be used as reference, but any substantial code not written by the student must be clearly designated.  
Derivative work: work that derives from existing works such as collage, remix, or derivation must demonstrate significant transformation on the part of the student to receive credit as original work.  
Use of AI content generators: the use of AI and machine learning content generators such as ChatGPT, Dall-E, Stable Diffusion, etc for any game assets should be considered under the same policy as third-party assets and derivative works. Image generators are allowable during the initial concept phase for producing inspiration images and moodboards.  
Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy may result in a penalty of a grade of zero for the assignment.  
Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of  
Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate  
students, respectively).
