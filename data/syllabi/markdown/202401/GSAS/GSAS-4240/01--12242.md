## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is the second course in the central track of the Writing concentration for GSAS students. In this project-intensive course, students will learn and practice basic, in-the-trenches writing and narrative development for games, including story outlines, cutscenes, scripted dialogue, systemic dialogue, and mission writing and design, across multiple game types and narrative genres. Prerequisites: GSAS 2520 or WRIT 2330 , or permission of instructor. When Offered: Spring term annually. Credit Hours: 4

## Course Goals

### Goals:

The course is designed to provoke analytical consideration of narrative elements in games, and to expose students to the practice of creating narrative elements within a framework of guidance and industry experience.

Students are expected to work both individually and collaboratively on assignments; to learn to critically assess writing and provide clear editing direction, and to rewrite based on feedback.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

- \- Adaptation
- \- Character Creation
- \- In-game dialogue and editing
- \- Writing a premise and synopsis
- \- Creating a beat sheet
- \- Writing screenplays
- \- How to take industry tests
- \- Software commonly used in game development

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will write, and rewrite, video game-specific elements including scripts, lore entries, and barks.
- Students will take a game-specific writing test

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Original screenplay and rewrite: 30 percent

- Create-a-Game: 30 percent
- Industry test #1: 10 percent
- Group Project 1: Editing and dialogue 10 percent
- Group Project 2: IPs or turning other things into games 10 percent
- Attendance and participation 10 percent

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grading percentages as follows:  
10 percent: Attendance and participation  
10 percent: In-class writing and editing test (Industry test)  
20 percent: Two in-class group projects (Editing and dialogue / IPs or turning other things into games) . Each project will count for 10 percent of final grade.  
30 percent: Create-a-Game  
30 percent: Original screenplay and rewrite. Instructor will discard lower score.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Students are expected to attend classes to the best of their ability. No penalties for absences will be levied.

### Other Course Policies:

It is expected students will participate in lectures and discussions, will be able to accept feedback and use it to improve their writing.  
Some assignments will require collaborative effort.  
It is expected all students will complete assignments in a timely fashion. Game writing often requires strict adherence to deadlines.  
Makeup assignments will be assigned to students. Penalties may be assessed for late submissions.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own.
