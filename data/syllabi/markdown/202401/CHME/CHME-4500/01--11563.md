## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Principles of kinetics, reactor design, and analysis for both homogeneous and heterogeneous (catalytic) systems. Topics include design for multiple reaction networks (optimum selectivity), analysis of simple reactor combinations, and design of isothermal, adiabatic, and optimum temperature profile reactor.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

Rawlings, J.B. and J.G. Ekerdt, Chemical Reactor Analysis and Design Fundamentals, 2nd ed., Nob Hill Publishing (2014), ISBN-13: 978-0975937723 --- available through Amazon. Course notes will be uploaded to LMS after each lecture.

#### Expected Knowledge & Skills

Students enrolled in the course are expected to be proficient in calculus, mass and energy balances, thermodynamics, and transport phenomena; exam problems and homework assignments may require students to apply knowledge and skills learned in previous courses.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

The purpose of this course is to introduce undergraduate students to the theory and practice of chemical reactor analysis and design.

**Topics Covered**

Reaction Stoichiometry for single and multiple reactions

Reaction Equilibria

Material balances on major chemical reactor types (batch, CSTR, and PFR)

Energy balances on major chemical reactor types (batch, CSTR, and PFR)

Numerical solution of ordinary differential equations for chemical reactors using MATLAB/Simulink

Simulation of major chemical reactor types (batch, CSTR, and PFR) in Aspen Plus

Extraction of kinetic parameters from experimental reaction rate data

Design of packed bed reactors

Reactor safety and control systems

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate an understanding of the modeling, design and operation of batch stirred tank reactors, continuous stirred tank reactors (CSTR), and plug-flow reactors (PFR), and compare the performance characteristics of the different reactor types.
- Analyze rate data using integral and differential methods to find rate constants and reaction order.
- Evaluate kinetic rate constants or thermodynamic parameters by fitting of experimental reaction data to kinetic or thermodynamic models
- Demonstrate an understanding of multiple steady-states, reactor stability, and runaway reactions.
- Demonstrate an understanding of the safety implications and control strategies for reactor design.
- Use computer packages (such as MATLAB and Simulink) to solve reactor models, and be able to model and optimize chemical reactors in Aspen Plus software.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Homework Assignments** will include both conventional hand-written problem solutions and some assignments that are programming-intensive. Homework is due at the beginning of class, not at the end. Printed/paper copies of assignments will be collected on a week-by-week basis. The teaching assistants may only grade selected parts of homework assignments or check only for completeness of problems at their discretion.

**Active Learning Exercises** will be distributed during class time, and students are expected to complete them during class (participation/attendance grade). Class attendance and participation will therefore be measured by completion of ALE, many of which will involve MATLAB or Aspen programming. Online assignment submissions (though LMS) will be required by the end of class time. Electronic sharing of ALE solutions with students who are not actually working on the assignment is cheating, and will result in grades of zero for everyone involved.

Two **midterm exams** will be given during normal class time. Exams will involve both hand-written problem solving exercises and software-based problems in MATLAB, Simulink, and Aspen. If a laptop is required for an exam, the instructors will notify students beforehand.  
A **final examination** will be given at the end of the semester.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Course grades will be curved. Instructors will periodically provide updates on students' standing in the course. The course grade will be determined using the following components:

Homework Assignments (6-8) 20 %

Active Learning Exercises (12-20) 15 %

Midterm Exams (2) 40 %

Final Exam (1) 25 %

Total 100 %

Course grades will be curved. Approximate letter grade ranges for each exam will be announced after the exams are graded and returned.

Guideline #1: an exam score of 90% or higher indicates a letter grade of at least A-

Guideline #2: an exam score of < 50 % indicates unsatisfactory (failing) performance

1\) Exams will be given in-person in the normal classroom.

2\) Students missing exams without prior approval of the instructor will not be granted a make-up.

3\) There will be no make-up exams given without an excuse letter/email from Student Success.

4\) Exams grades will be posted on LMS, and solution keys will be provided.

This policy will not be relaxed. Fair warning! Conversely, an exam average > 50 % does not guarantee a course grade of C.

### Other Course Policies:

Required Computer & Software

MATLAB, Simulink, and Aspen Plus v12 software will be used extensively, and many of the assignments will require computer-based simulations (both during class time and in homework assignments). Students should install the required software and plan on bringing their laptop computers to each class. Versions of MATLAB/Simulink older than 2022B will not be supported.

Courses at RPI are generally expected to require 2-3 hours of outside work for each credit hour. You should expect, therefore, to commit at least 6 hours/week outside the classroom for this course.

ALE Grading Policy:

1\) No make-up ALEs will be given for any reason.

2\) Students missing an ALE with an excused absence from Student Success will be given a grade of "X", which means the ALE does not count.

3\) Each student gets to drop (1) ALE at the end of the semester, which may be a missed class due to illness or a job interview. Otherwise, you may drop your lowest ALE score.

4\) No extra time accommodations are offered on ALEs.

5\) Some ALEs may be graded like quizzes; others may be "participation" exercises in which everyone is given a score of 100% for completing the assignment.

A student may appeal a grade by attaching a memo (to the instructors) to the specific exam or homework assignment that is being appealed. This memo must state the specific reason(s) for the re-grade. The student has one week after the grades have been assigned to contest the grade; after that time period, no more re-grade requests will be considered.
