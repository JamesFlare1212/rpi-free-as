## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Introduction:** provides a high-level introduction to the topic of biological imaging and introduces a core set of image analysis methods that are relevant for the course.

**Imaging Methods:** discusses the different methods for imaging cells and tissues. Rather than reviewing the technical details of the various imaging methods, we will discuss why certain imaging methods provide challenges for automated image analysis. The unit makes reference to the standard imaging methods for cells and tissues such as automated microscopy.

**Image Preprocessing**: discusses the acquisition and representation of digital images. In particular, we will focus on acquiring multi-spectral data sets. Algorithms that are used to remove image noise and unmix multi-spectral signals will be presented.

**Detection Methods:** provides an overview of basic detection methods. Applications to common biological signal detection problems will be discussed.

**Image Registration:** reviews the most commonly used image registration algorithms. Image registration can play an important part in the image formation process.

**Image Segmentation:** provides an overview of different segmentation strategies. Automated image segmentation techniques play a central role in the acquisition and analysis of the probe signal.

**Algorithm Validation:** gives a general overview of validation methods. Algorithm validation plays a major role in medical and biomedical imaging applications. Only if the users have established the validity of an algorithm can it be used for experimental studies. When ground truth is available, it is possible to use established approaches to validate a new algorithm. However, many biological applications pose the challenge that establishing the ground truth is either very difficult or even impossible. The inherent variation of the biological samples posed additional challenges. This unit will highlight general principles and requirements.

**Morphological Assessment** **of Cells:** introduces classification methods to identify different cell populations. Changes in cell morphology and tissue structure play an important role in the diagnosis of cancer. This unit will introduce methods for characterizing cellular shape by measuring certain geometrical aspects of cells.

**Tubular Structures:** introduces some of the specific algorithms that are necessary for extracting tubular structures and assessing their characteristics. In addition to the detection of nuclei, the segmentation of microvasculature (or generalized tubular structures) is highly relevant.

**Algorithm Implementation:** provides training and opportunities for students to implement image analysis algorithms in high-level efficient computer programming languages like Python on top of the commonly adopted image processing/analysis libraries.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Grading – 95% (for both 4000 and 6000 levels):**

The final grade in this course will be based on the student’s total score on all components of the course. The total score is broken down into the following components:

1. Homework assignments (35% of grade): 7 homework assignments (due as assigned) that will include coding activities in Python.
2. Test I (20% of grade): There is no makeup for Test 1. If a student is unable to participate in this test due to a valid and verified excuse, the percentage of Test 2 will substitute the grade for Test 1. Without a valid excuse, the missed test will be graded as zero.
3. Test II (20% of grade): There is no makeup for Test 2. If a student is unable to participate in this test due to a valid and verified excuse, the percentage of Test 1 will substitute the grade for Test 2. Without a valid excuse, the missed test will be graded as zero.
4. Group project (20% of grade): focuses on the application of image analysis methods to an actual challenge problem. Students will be working in small groups, and the project will be organized into various phases. The grade will be based on the progress and results in a series of presentations.

**Grading – 5% (6000 level):**

To attain the remaining 5% score, 6000 level students will prepare and deliver an in-depth technical presentation on selected research topics (30 minutes presentation with 10 minutes Q&A). Dr. Yan will help guide the development of these presentations. A list of topics will be provided to the students.

**Grading – 5% (4000 level):**

To attain the remaining 5% score, 4000 level students will study the selected research topics and actively ask questions at the presentation.
