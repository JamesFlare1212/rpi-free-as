## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Introduction of number systems – decimal, binary and hexadecimal –; variable types including

Boolean, integer, float, character and string; general overview of computer architecture and

software, programming in “C/C++” including input/output, control statements, operators, functions, arrays, strings, classes, objects, inheritance, pointers, references, file processing, exceptions and operator overloading; brief introduction to other programming languages including Matlab and Python; design of algorithms for applications that are relevant for biomedical engineering applications.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- • Have a basic understanding of computer hardware and architecture.
- • Understand how a computer stores and processes integer and floating point numbers as well as char and string variables.
- • Understand binary and hexadecimal number systems.
- • Comprehend and apply different programming statements, including if, for, while, else, switch.
- • Deal with string operations and basic handling of database operations.
- • Solve mathematical problem by translating them into computer codes.
- • Write simple programs to solve BME related problems.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Homework Assignments: Counts 20% towards the final grade calculation**

There are **11 homework assignments**, typically one every week, which contain programming

tasks and understanding type-questions based on the material that has been covered in class up to

that point. ***Each homework submission needs to be uploaded on LMS under homework assignments on or before the due date (which is typically one week after the homework assignment was made available). Late submissions will not be accepted and will receive a grade of zero*.** One lowest homework grade will be dropped.

**Quizzes: Counts 20% towards the final grade calculation**

There will be paper-based ‘surprise’ quizzes administered at random times in class during the semester. The questions will test your knowledge on material covered in class and homeworks up to that point. One lowest quiz grade will be dropped. No make-up quizzes will be offered for any absences excused or unexcused.

**Midterm Examination: Counts 30% towards the final grade calculation**

There is **one midterm examination** that includes the design of simple programs and understanding- type questions based on material that has been covered in class up to that point. The midterm examination is conducted during lecture hour (see calendar for more details)***. If a student is unable to participate in the midterm exam due to a valid and verified excuse, the percentage of the final will substitute the grade for the midterm exam. If no valid excuse can be produced, the missed midterm exam will be graded as zero. There is no makeup for the midterm exam.***

**Final Examination: Counts 30% towards the final grade calculation**

There is **one final examination** that comprehensively assesses the course material. The exam is

cumulative and contains simple programming tasks and understanding-type questions about the

course material. The final examination is conducted during the final’s week announced by the institute registrar. Refer to registrar website for details. ***If a student is unable to participate in the final exam due to a valid and verified excuse, the percentage of the midterm will substitute the grade for the final exam. If no valid excuse can be produced, the missed exam will be graded as zero. There is no makeup for the final exam.***

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

Attendance is highly recommended. The class will be conducted in person only. The lectures will not be recorded. For a successful completion of this course, it is essential to engage with the course material, by attending in-person lectures. It is also essential to review the material after each class and practice the simple problems that we discuss during class.

### Other Course Policies:

**Grading concerns:**

All graded homework submissions and examinations are graded on LMS (feedback will be provided). If there are any questions or concerns, ***there is a one-week window after each homework assignment or the midterm examination has been graded to seek clarification during the office hours. It is important to note that any issue that has not been brought to my attention one week after a homework assignment or the midterm exam was graded is not going to be considered.*** The grades for each homework assignment and the grades for the midterm and final examination will be uploaded on LMS.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work.
