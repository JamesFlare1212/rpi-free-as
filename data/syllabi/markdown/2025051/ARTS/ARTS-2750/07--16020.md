### Prerequisites or Other Requirements:

Repertoire across the vocal spectrum is welcome. Students new to singing are welcome, as previous singing experience is not a requirement, though previous music or ensemble experience is encouraged. Participation in Private Voice Lessons (ARTS 2750) and Concert Choir (ARTS 2310) is welcome but not a prerequisite.

### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

Private applied music lessons are offered to provide a fine arts experience for credit and are focused on vocal performance and assessment in both private and group settings.

This course creates an explorative environment for students to develop their voice and vocal technique with the student’s vocal identity at the center. Introductory ideas to voice science/vocal pedagogy, breathing and singing technique, languages/diction, and phonation, with the aim of making a healthy, efficient acoustic sound across a variety of vocal genres in an energizing, encouraging experience. This is an exploration of student’s unique vocal identity, guided by technique and the instructor’s insight as tools for individualized progress.

In this course, students will gain the skills to give and receive technical feedback, gain performance experience, access information on voice resources, workshops, and vocal connections across campus, and engage in periodic activities throughout the semester, including shared assessment in other singers’ lessons. Certain dates are TBD.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students in the private lesson program will study to improve their sense of vocal singing identity and to musically interpret a variety of musical works and styles/genres.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Students in the private lesson program will study to improve their sense of vocal singing identity and to musically interpret a variety of musical works and styles/genres:

- Students will commit to their own daily technical singing work to create space for greater individual expression and vocal exploration, in pedagogic solo, chamber, choral and various musical contexts.
- Students will explore application of learnings in group voice / performance settings.
- Students will improve listening skills, as well as mature in their ability to work at once independently on appropriate warm-ups, ideas, and music. as assigned and discussed in class, as well as guided self-direction.
- Students will be encouraged to question the musical canon, and structures and systems of organization in vocal/musical history, and to explore elements of social justice in their repertoire and application of knowledge.
- Students track progress with note-taking and weekly updates via a shared Google Doc.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Grading and Assessment**  
Grades and assessment of student progress is made based on individual pace, effort, and improvement over the course of the semester.

- In-class singing/feedback application 70%
- Attendance at group events/lessons 10%
- Self-assessment/journal 20%

Regular in-person assessments and feedback will be provided during workshops, along with a warmup routine and repertoire selections.

Note on vocal physicality

Students may expect to do in-class breathing and movement exercises as part of class. These facilitate singers’ awareness that the unified singing mind-body can be inhabited, explored, and understood through the physical embodied act of singing. These exercises will always be catered to the individual, so open communication about any accommodations, vocal needs, and preferences is strongly encouraged and welcome.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
