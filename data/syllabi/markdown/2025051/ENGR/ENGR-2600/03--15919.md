## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

Data summary and presentations; probability; discrete and continuous probability distributions; descriptive statistics; confidence intervals; hypothesis testing; regression analysis and introduction into machine learning; use of computer applications in statistical analysis. The examples and exercises strongly emphasize engineering applications and the use of R language.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course teaches students

- An appreciation for uncertainty in the application of engineering models;
- The difference between probability and statistics;
- Discrete and continuous distribution functions;
- Marginal and conditional distributions;
- Descriptive statistics;
- Sample distributions and point estimates;
- Confidence intervals;
- Hypothesis testing.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Students will be able to:

- Solve for the probability and conditional probability of events in various scenarios;
- Obtain and graph information about random variables and variable sets;
- Identify variable distributions correctly and apply various discrete and continuous distribution functions;
- Construct and interpret one- sample and two- sample confidence intervals;
- Formulate hypotheses tests and perform the tests using the method of critical region, p-value, and confidence interval.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

- **Tests and final exam (80%):** There are three 1.5-hour long tests and a 2-hour long final exam. All tests are cumulative but with emphasis on the material since the last test. The lowest test grade can be replaced by the final exam grade if you have at least two tests taken before the final exam. A zero-grade due to an unexcused absence cannot be replaced. The three tests count for 15%, 20%, and 20% respectively. The final exam includes the entire semester and counts for 25%. They are all close-book and in-person. Relevant formulas and statistical tables will be provided.

One of the essential skills for an engineer is being able to clearly communicate their ideas and process in problem solving. Therefore, the majority of points are allotted to proper showing of work on tests and projects. Examples of sufficient, detailed work shown can be found on LINUS LMS past test solutions.

- **Projects (10%):** There are two projects based on R, each counting for 5% of overall grade. Late submissions receive a reduction of 25% per day from points earned.
- **Quizzes (10%):** There are quizzes taken remotely via LINUS LMS (https://mau.linuslearning.net/) on Thursday evenings. If you prefer taking the quizzes in person, they will be in the classroom immediately after Thursday’s lecture hours. Missing quizzes with valid excuses will be substituted by the final exam grade. The lowest quiz grade will be dropped.
