## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

A survey, application-oriented course for engineering and science majors. Transducers and measurement devices. DC and AC analog circuits including impedance, power, frequency response, and resonance. Diodes, transistors and operational amplifiers. Signal conditioning, noise, and shielding. Digital electronics, A/D and D/A conversation. Power supplies, rectifiers, and electromagnetic devices.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will be able to analyze simple DC circuits and will understand AC steady-state responses of resistance, inductance and capacitance in terms of impedance and be able to analyze simple AC circuit
- Students will be familiar with basic properties of operational amplifiers and the analysis of simple operational amplifier circuits.
- Students will understand functions and characteristics of diodes, transistors, and transformers
- Students will understand the concept of frequency response and the transient responses of capacitors and inductors.
- Students will be able to draw accurate schematics and use them to perform electrical measurements, construct circuits on breadboards and model the circuit response using simulation software
- Students will be able to interface between electrical systems and other engineering systems (e.g. biological, thermal, mechanical, photonic ...) through the use of sensors and actuators and the application of basic engineering design principles.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Assessment of student progress in the course objectives will be performed through a variety of mechanisms.

- 8 Homeworks will be assigned for each major topic in the class.
- 8 Hands-on experiments will be assigned to guide student learning in each topic
- 3 Projects to serve as a culmination of sets of topics
- 3 Exams will also be used to gauge understanding. There is no final exam for this course.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

COLLABORATION AND ACADEMIC DISHONESTY:

Intellectual integrity and credibility are the foundation of all academic work. A violation of Academic Integrity policy is, by definition, considered a flagrant offense to the educational process. It is taken seriously by students, faculty, and Rensselaer and will be addressed in an effective manner. If found responsible for committing academic dishonesty, a student may be subject to one or both types of penalties: an academic (grade) penalty administered by the professor and/or disciplinary action through the Rensselaer judicial process described in the student handbook. Three relevant academic integrity violations to emphasize include:

**Collaboration:** Collaboration is defined as deliberately facilitating an act of academic dishonesty in any way or form; for example, allowing another student to observe an exam paper or allowing another student to "recycle" one's old term paper or using one another's work in a paper or lab report without citing it as another's work.

**Copying:** Copying is defined as obtaining information pertaining to a graded exercise by deliberately observing the paper of another student; for example, noting which alternative a neighboring student has circled on a multiple-choice exam.

**Plagiarism:** Plagiarism is defined as representing the work or words of another as one's own through the omission of acknowledgment or reference. Examples include using sentences verbatim from a published source in a term paper without appropriate referencing, or presenting as one's own the detailed argument of a published source, or presenting as one's own electronically or digitally enhanced graphic representations from any form of media.  
Any instance of an academic integrity violation will result in a student being assigned a 0 for the assignment in question. A repeated offense will result in immediate failure of the course. All violations will be reported to the Dean of Students.
