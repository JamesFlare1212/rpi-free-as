Faculty Bio :

With over a decade working as an artist & designer, Elise has created unique product collections and production systems that result in furniture , home goods, clothing and accessories under the design studio LikeMindedObjects. This work often incorporates traditional upholstery and soft goods techniques with unique waste stream driven material experimentation. As an artist, Elise has created sculpture, performance and collaborative public installations within street fairs , newsstands, galleries and conferences . Elise sees each object as telling a story of social and material culture from natural material harvesting to disposal, and feels it's our job as designers in America, the largest consuming and wasting nation in the world, to utilize scaled upcycling of material otherwise treated as waste post use before it becomes a global pollutant. McMahon’s knowledge around craft and design history came from training in Furniture Design @ Rhode Island School of Design for her BFA & Industrial Design with a focus on Waste & Sustainability @ Parsons School of Design for her MFA. Through LikeMindedObjects and other art and education projects, she experiments fluidly towards the seamless combination of art and industry. Her work in Art, Design, Research and Education has resulted in international projects (@ such places as Night Gallery LA, LA Philarmonic, NADA New York , Material Art Fair Mexico City , etc) and diverse press coverage ( New York Times, Vogue , Architectural Digest, Sight Unseen, Art News etc) .

Course Description: DIS Studio A introduces students to the principles and practices of design for environmental sustainability. It is open to majors in Design, Innovation, and Society (DIS), Sustainability Studies, and Science, Technology, and Society (STS). ## Course Goals

### Goals:

Course Purpose Overview

With the majority of the world's social and environmental injustices traced back to poor judgment in the development of scaled designed systems , it is our job as designers of today and tomorrow to rethink the future of design . A product can no longer be considered successful merely based on its merits as a functional & beautiful object , but now must be judged with a holistic view of its surrounding systems like virgin material extraction, delivery methods , labor practices , production , health implications and disposal/reuse methods, in short, its full Life Cycle . The United Nations has prompted countries to prioritize the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) , global cities are beginning to apply regulatory policies such as single use plastic bans and extended producer responsibility (EPR), this reflects that the world is finally acknowledging that across industries we all must move towards better practices. We hope our students will be enthusiastic participants and leaders in embracing these appropriate and necessary strategies moving forward. Over the next 12 weeks we will begin to explore Resourcefulness in Design, Material Health, Waste Driven approaches, Life Cycle Analysis , Sustainable Design Strategies, Market Surveys and Craft Experimentation to establish a baseline of thoughtful sustainable, equitable and regenerative approaches for your future in product design.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these.
