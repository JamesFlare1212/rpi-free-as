## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course covers structure, processing, and property relationships for industrially important plastic materials. Fundamentals of fluid flow, rheology, heat transfer, and transport phenomena are applied to analyze polymer processing operations including extrusion, multi-phase mixing, calendering, blow molding, thermoforming, fiber spinning, compression molding, injection molding, and recycling.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

The course packet serves as the textbook for CHME 4610. Complete lecture notes (course packet) will be distributed electronically on the first day of class.

**References** 1. *Materials Science of Polymers for Engineers*, 2nd Edition, Osswald and Menges. Hanser Publishers, Munich.  
*2. Principles of Polymer Processing*, Tadmor and Gogos.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Introduction to polymers & processing, glassy and semicrystalline polymers; single-screw extrusion, twin screw extrusion, thermoforming, polymer melt rheology, mixing operations, molding processes, secondary shaping processes, plastics recycling, micro/nano-lithography.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students can apply mathematical methods to solve problems related to the theory of polymer processing operations.
- Given polymer products like pipes, bottles, automobile dashboards, etc., students will be able to identify techniques, skills, and modern engineering tools necessary to manufacture them.
- Students will demonstrate a rational approach to solving engineering problems related to polymer processing operations.
- Students will be able to understand contemporary technical, economic, social, and political aspects of the global plastics recycling industry.
- Students will appreciate the dynamic nature of the plastics industry by understanding emerging trends in materials (e.g. bioplastics) and methods (e.g. 3D printing), and thereby recognize the need for life-long learning.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Homework**. Approximately 6 homework problem sets will be assigned and collected for grading during the semester.

**Exams**. Two midterm exams will be given during class time. It is the student's responsibility to attend or arrange for a make-up with the instructor prior to the exam date in case of illness or emergency. Exams typically contain a mixture of essays, multiple choice questions, short answer questions, and numerical problems.

**Design Project.** Students will use Autodesk Moldflow Plastics Insight to simulate a plastics injection molding process and optimize processing conditions for a given set of material properties. Students will submit their final projects and a brief term paper describing their findings.
