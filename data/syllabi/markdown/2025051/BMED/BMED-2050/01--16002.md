## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students will be able to:

- Have a basic understanding of computer hardware and architecture.
- Understand how a computer stores and processes integer and floating point numbers as well as char and string variables.
- Understand binary and hexadecimal number systems.
- Comprehend and apply different programming statements, including if, for, while, else, switch.
- Deal with string operations and basic handling of database operations.
- Solve mathematical problem by translating them into computer codes.
- Write simple programs to solve BME related problems.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

This course teaches students:

- Computer architecture
- Algorithms
- Overview of C/C++
- Constants, Variables, Data Types
- Operators and Expressions
- Control Structures, Loops and Arrays
- Modules and Functions
- Pointers and references
- Classes and objects
- Friendship and inheritance
- Outlook to other programming languages – Matlab and Python

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- **Homework Assignments (20%):** Homework assignments will include programming tasks, implementation of numerical methods and problem solving. Homework will generally be assigned weekly.
- **Attendance (5%):** Attendance is mandatory. *Missing more than 20% of the classes in the semester will result in failing the course.*
- **Quizzes (15%):** There will be scheduled quizzes that will take place at the beginning of class. The quiz content will cover material that has been discussed in recent lectures as well as general programming logic and syntax.
- **Midterm Examinations (30%):** There will be a midterm exam that includes designing simple programs and understanding questions based on material that has been covered up including the lecture before the day of Midterm.
- **Final Examination (30%):** Cumulative exam that includes designing simple programs

and understanding-type questions based on the material covered up (including) the final lecture.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).
