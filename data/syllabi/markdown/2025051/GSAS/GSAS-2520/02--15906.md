## Course Goals

### Goals:

Learn the basic principles, techniques, and methods of game storytelling, including story structure, worldbuilding, environmental design, branching narratives, and more.  
Develop short narrative games using beginner-friendly tools that do not require prior programming experience.  
Analyze and critique narrative strategies used in video games, with the ability to apply these techniques to your own projects.  
Gain insight into the role of a narrative designer in game development, including how writers, artists, and programmers collaborate to create narrative-driven games.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Develop narrative games using tools like Twine and Unity.
- Understand plot models and write short stories with these models.
- Understand and implement narrative techniques and methods in game design.
- Explore storytelling techniques from other media and adapt them for games.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Short Story  
Due: 5/29  
Choose a well-known short story, fable, or fairytale (e.g., Little Red Riding Hood, The Little Mermaid, The Hare and The Tortoise, Rapunzel). Begin by analyzing the story's original structure (e.g., Three-act, Five-act, Kishōtenketsu, Nested, Parallel, etc). Then, reconstruct it to fit a different structure of your choice.  
Twine Game  
Due: 6/16  
Watch the full film Princes et Princesses: https://youtu.be/nLkXJPr_FOw (French with English subtitles). Choose one episode out of six and adapt it into a Twine game. Your game must contain at least two important characters from the chosen episode, with the option to create additional original characters. This assignment emphasizes learning and applying character development and interactive choices.  
Visual Novel  
Due: 8/14  
Create a visual novel with completed storylines and characters using Unity and Fungus. You are NOT expected to create original game assets (e.g., art, sound, music). However, please cite any open-source art, royalty-free music, or licensed work you have used in your game.  
Participation  
Students will be graded on their contributions to class discussions and in-class exercises. Failing to complete the assigned readings and arriving unprepared to discuss them will negatively impact the participation grade. While the frequency of participation is important, the quality of contributions is equally critical.
