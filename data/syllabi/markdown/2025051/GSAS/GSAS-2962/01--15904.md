**GSAS/ARTS 2505** : Concept Art

Overview

This course covers the concept art phase in most digital media production pipelines such as Film, Television, and Games. Concept art is a preproduction technique used in order to quickly visualize content that will need to be generated through out a pipeline.

Characters, costumes, environments, props, lighting schemes, user interfaces are all possible purviews of a concept artist in this early phase of production.

Concept artists work quickly, iterate heavily, and follow under the direction of the designers, directors, writers, and other creatives. These artists must be able generate various types of imagery in efficient and appealing ways.

Concept artists help these creatives by translating their ideas into hard visual representation. Concept artists need to have worldly views on a wide array of minutia in order to be successful. Does that character’s belt have rivets or punch holes? Is their scarf stitching a double over or a cross stitch? Inundating students into this detailed decision-making process is how this course builds better concept artists.

This course is designed to immerse students in the rigors of being a concept artist by combining education of new techniques with a persistent output of conceptual work.

Students can expect to have the following skills upon completion of the course:

- Concept drafting
- Digital Painting using Photoshop and Procreate
- Clothing and Fashion study
- Anatomy
- Surface rendering
- Lighting and Color scheme
- Kit bashing
- Compositing

Submission Guidelines

- - All model images should be submitted as JPGs at 1920x1080+.

    - Example: Rush Swope Milestone 1 would be

      - **swoper_Milestone3_02.jpg**
  - All images should have student’s name, class, and semester with year somewhere in the file name.

Grading

Grading standards for this class are high. This is one of the upper levels game electives, not to mention a course in one of the most competitive fields in the current job market.

A’s will denote work that is currently representative of industry level practices and quality.

B’s mean the student is rendering work just on the cusp of transitioning into professional quality, but still has visible denotations of student work.

C’s translate to student work that is clearly rendered by a student hand and eye. The effort and time is present, but the quality level is not on the cusp of being valuable to the game industry.

D’s & F’s when students are not only delivering work that is not industry level, but also lacks the effort of a committed student, and has failed to include the required amount of assets, techniques, and expectations of each milestone.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Policies

- Late Work
  - Late submissions will be met with a 10 point deduction per day that they are late.
  - Better to submit something 90% complete and finish it later for your portfolio than it completely done a few days late.
- Attendance
  - Attendance accounts for 10% of your grade.
  - Class lectures will be very important for completing your model on time and will save you hours/days of research later.
- If any of these policies conflict with your schedule/needs please contact me.
- AI - Usage of AI in any images of ideation will be equivalent to plagiarism.
- Purposeful Ambiguity - In accorance with working with Creative Directors, clients, and other less technically inclined colleagues, sometimes feedback will be left ambiguous to allow your work to grow organically. This can be frustrating at times, but extremely fruitful to help cultivate your own personal style, workflow, and creative process.

Course Procedures

- - Each class in between Milestones will also have an optional critique for students looking for feedback on their models.
  - Classes will consist of the professor walking through the next part of character development on a large screen so students can follow the techniques, concepts, and softwares used for concept art.
  - Each class will have an outline of important techniques and topics covered so students can review them later on documentation sites, with the professor, or the optional readings.
  - Assignments will be distributed and turned in via Blackboard.
  - Advanced Submissions - To try and compensate for different goals, skill levels, and career paths each assignment has an optional advanced submission that does not affect student grades.

Final Project

The final project will be worked on by the students throughout the semester. Students’ progress on this project will be tracked via Milestones listed above. The final submission will be a collection of images defined in the proposal section of the final project. The final project will then consist of milestones that should be met by the student to ultimately deliver a final collection of images at the end of the semester. Students will be expected to explore alternative color palettes, lighting schemes, compositions, layouts, and anything else prudent to their designs.
