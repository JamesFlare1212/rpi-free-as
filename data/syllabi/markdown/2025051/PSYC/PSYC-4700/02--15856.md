## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will examine hormone and brain/behavior relationships across the lifespan. Hormones are molecules that are secreted by glands (the majority of which are located outside the brain) and have distal effects on their targets, such as the brain, throughout development. A focus will be on hormones’ effects, mechanisms, and brain regions of interest for behavior and cognitive processes, such as perception, learning/memory, social cognition, motivation, and emotion.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students who successfully complete this course will be able to describe the basics of behavioral neuroendocrinology: classes of hormones, secretion from glands, and actions in target tissues, including the brain.
- Students who successfully complete this course will be able to integrate this information about hormone action into their existing knowledge base of the brain, behavior, cognition, and plasticity and expand their understanding to be able to compare and contrast the effects of different hormones for these.
- Students who successfully complete this course will be able to develop a skill set that enables them to understand and critically evaluate new information in this rapidly progressing field, including review of others’ work and reading the primary literature.
- Students who successfully complete this course will be able to effectively communicate their understanding of hormone, brain, and behavior relationships in class discussions, group/pair work, and writing.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Grading is not competitive and students are encouraged to study and discuss materials together. However, unless explicitly stated otherwise (e.g. group project), any work turned in must be the student’s own original work completed for this class. Students’ final course grades will be based on their performance on the assessments in this course (described below). Grades will be posted in the Blackboard LMS gradebook regularly and students will be able to assess their standing in class throughout the semester by checking this gradebook. If you ever have any questions regarding your grades, please ask me. Please note that grades on assessments and final course grades are non-negotiable and will be based upon the same metrics described in the syllabus for each and every student. Any appeals about final grades (e.g. due to mistake in input/calculations, deviation from syllabus) must be brought to my attention through email before the last day of finals.

This course uses an additive grading system where there are several opportunities throughout the semester for you to earn points. The points earned translate to your final course grade. This grading system is an example of what is called a gameful approach in teaching. Like a game, there are goals and different challenges that you can complete to reach those goals. One goal you may have is to earn a certain letter grade in the course. Goals for all of us are the learning outcomes described above. You will demonstrate that you have achieved these learning outcome (LO) goals by completing assessments associated with them. Your performance on these assessments, as well as points earned for attending and participating in class, will translate to points earned on your journey towards these goals.

Everyone begins with 0 points. There will be 1445 total points that can be earned this semester. As you accomplish assessments in the class, you build up points towards your final grade. See table below for the grading scale. There are two required assessments\*\* and readings you are expected to do to contribute to class discussions and activities, but there are many different assessments that you can choose to complete. Some are collaborative, some are individual, some are completed in class, and some are completed outside of class. It is up to you to figure out which of the available assessments you will take on in order to build up enough points to get the grade you want by the end of class.

You can earn attendance and participation points by being here and engaged for the entire class and completing participation challenges during class time. If you are not in class or do not complete the participation challenges, then you cannot earn these points. This is a seminar course; unlike a typical lecture-based course, it is expected that you will be discussing readings and other media in each class meeting. Attendance and participation, and reading responses, are associated with approximately 45% of the points available for you to earn this semester.

There will also be required assignments: an individual project and a group project. For the group project, you will work in a pair or small group to also lead an activity for the rest of the class. In addition to this group project, you will complete an individual project this semester. For both of these, you will propose your idea and get feedback from Prof Walf and start working on it once she approves it. Note: approximately 30% of the points available for you to earn this semester come from the group and individual projects.

Our course is organized into four modules. For some assessments, including the required ones, there are specific due dates. Most other assessments have due dates at the end of each modules, which serve as check points in our course this semester. Assessments must be turned in on time. No due date extensions are given, but you will be able to choose the pace at which you complete many of these (see table on next page).

**Why are we doing it this way?** I have studied the art and science of teaching for many years, including several trainings in the last two years on gameful learning approaches. I want to foster engagement in our class for individuals working on their own and in groups to support students’ learning of the material and reaching their goals. I want you to take an active part in this. I know it can be stressful to juggle many classes and other activities. I think this approach will allow the needed flexibility, agency, and choice while maintaining high standards and cultivating intrinsic motivation, which has stronger effects for learning than relying solely on extrinsic motivation.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Policy on collaboration and cheating/plagiarism**

All exams, quizzes, and assignments (except the group project) that are turned in for a grade must be the student’s own original work for this class only. In cases where help was received (does not apply to quizzes and exams), a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration (e.g. if someone else helped with editing or provided feedback). Work composed in whole or in part by artificial intelligence (e.g. ChatGPT) will be considered cheating. Plagiarizing, as defined by Webster’s, is “to steal and pass off (the ideas or words of another) as one’s own” or use of another’s material without crediting the source. Submission of any assignment, quiz, or exam that is in violation of this policy will result in penalties as described above. If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
