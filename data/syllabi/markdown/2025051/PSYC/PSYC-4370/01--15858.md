### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

What is cognition? How does the mind work? How does our cognitive system enable us to solve our problems and survive? Cognitive psychology is the scientific discipline that attempts to answer these questions through behavioral, computational, and biological experimentation. The course will focus on basic theories and experimental studies in cognitive psychology on fundamental phenomena such as sensation and perception, attention, memory, retrieval and coding, reasoning, decision making and problem solving, and language. We will discuss the classic as well as the contemporary experimental literature on these phenomena from the perspectives of central theories of cognitive psychology.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Define Cognition and Cognitive Psychology
- Identify techniques and methods used to study cognition
- Demonstrate an informed understanding of the basic mechanisms of cognition
- Combine cognitive aspects of every-day life with laboratory findings
- Demonstrate an informed understanding of higher-order processes of cognition

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Course Assessment Measures:**

***Assignments***

Group presentation:

Students will be required to participate in a group presentation of a research article in cognitive psychology. Groups of 2 or 3 students will be formed early in the semester based upon similar interests. Group members will choose an article published in a peer-reviewed journal in which the authors report and analyze their own experimental findings. *The article to be presented must be approved by the professor before the presentation.*

***Quizzes & Exams***

Three Exams: There will be three non-cumulative exams during the semester covering materials discussed in class.

LMS Quizzes: The LMS quizzes will be multiple-choice questions covering materials discussed in class. They will be on Blackboard for 24 hours and you will have three attempts and the attempt with the higher grade will be kept in the gradebook. ***No make-up quizzes will be permitted.*** The instructor will drop the lowest quiz grade for each student at the end of the semester.

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own.
