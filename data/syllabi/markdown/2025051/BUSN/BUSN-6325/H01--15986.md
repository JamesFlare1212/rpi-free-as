## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

In this course, students create an executable and fundable plan that optimizes people, platforms, products, projects, and resources for a specific organization in a specific market, in either an entrepreneurial or intrapreneurial context. Students develop a value proposition, a market strategy plan, a financial plan and three-year revenue/cost forecast, an operations plan, and a staffing plan. The final plan is presented to a panel of evaluators for viability and fundability. Students are individually mentored through their plan development process. This course serves as a capstone to the MBA program and requires that all other core courses be completed prior.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Envision a business, product, or process improvement that creates value to an organization.
- Plan and substantiate a plan to implement a market strategy for the business, product, or process.
- Develop financial estimates and forecasts for revenue, cost, and projected net operating results, with a corresponding investment plan for implementation.
- Articulate execution plans required to implement the business/product/process.
- Present plans in their entirety to a panel of stakeholders.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

This course uses a series of projects that serve as the context in which students learn and then demonstrate that learning. Specifically, this course has seven projects:

1. **Project 1:** **Developing the Idea**: Students develop a full and executable plan for a new product, a process improvement, or a product revision – either in the context of their employer or for their own execution. The plan must: create value for stakeholders, must be an original undertaking (no franchises or multi-level marketing plans), and must be technically and operationally feasible. The Plan consists of four overarching parts:
2. **Project 2: Your Idea and the Market Need:** Students articulate the scope of their product/process and substantiate that their plan provides value to the intended customer. The Value Proposition uses original research where needed to clarify the value proposition relative to other competing functional/emotional options available to customers.
3. **Project 3: Executing Your Idea:** In this project, students segment the market and develop strategies to reach their intended segments with the right message, at the right time, through the right channel. Students make decisions about place, promotion, price, and product attributes to best achieve intended results. Students develop sales forecasts over three years.
4. **Project 4: Budgeting Your Idea**: Using the Market Strategy sales forecasts and the Operations and Staffing plan costs, students develop a 3-year net operating results forecast, including any pre-operations investment requirements. Students determine needed investment and a return-on-investment forecast for the funding.
5. **Project 5:** **The Executive Summary**: In this project, you’ll develop a compelling executive summary that lays out your value proposition – and how you will execute to address market need with your value proposition.
6. **Project 6: The Final Presentation:** Students present their final and full plan to a panel of Faculty Mentors for viability and fundability. The presentation serves as the final assessment of learning in the MBA program.
7. **Project 7:** **A Final View of My Ethics as a Leader**: Students evaluate several scenarios addressing how they would handle the situation as the Leader of a large organization.
