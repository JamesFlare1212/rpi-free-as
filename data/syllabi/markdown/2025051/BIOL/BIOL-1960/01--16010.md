## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course examines how biology and biotechnology are represented in popular works of fiction. Explored themes include genome modification, eugenics, restorative medicine, brain-computer interfaces, xenografts/synthetic organ transplants, and pandemic disease. Through class discussions and projects, students will learn about the latest advances in biology, assess the plausibility of fictional depictions of biology and biotechnology, and explore the future applications of biotechnology and the bioethical implications of such technologies. To this end, students will learn how to formulate hypotheses, find and analyze peer-reviewed scientific articles, and become familiar with other means of communication used by scientists.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

At the end of this course, students should be able to:

1. The student will be able to identify concepts in biology found in works of fiction and evaluate the scientific validity of these concepts as they are presented in the work.
2. The student will be able to analyze bioethical dilemmas in works of fiction and contrast these with real world problems involving bioethics.
3. The student will be able to evaluate scientific hypotheses pertaining to biotechnology’s future applications and its impact on the environment, health, and society.
4. The student will be able to identify, analyze, and properly cite scientific literature.
5. The student will be able to demonstrate effective communication through the written and oral presentation of their work.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

- **In-Class Activities**
  - ­ A class activity will be held each class session. You will be graded based participation and effort.
  - ­ Activities might entail individual work, but all activities will have some **group work** You are expected to contribute **equally** to group work; failure to do so will result in losing credit for the activity.
  - ­ All work that makes use of outside sources must have appropriate citations; **plagiarism** and copying of other students' work will not be tolerated.
  - ­ You must be in attendance to participate in these activities; absent students who do not submit appropriate documentation for an excusable absence will not receive credit for that day's activity in addition to the penalty listed in the *Attendance Policies* **If your absence is excused, you are still expected to contribute to that day's activity** – contact Dr. Klein to arrange this.
  - ­ If you are in quarantine or in a similar situation where you are not able to be physically present for class but have a documentable excuse (e.g., a RPI athletics-related absence), **you will be allowed to attend class via WebEx**. Be sure to submit your documentation to the *Excused Absences* section on LMS as soon as possible.
- **Participation Credit**
  - ­ This is related to the In-Class Activities mentioned above. In each of these activities, you will be working as a group and submitting your work on LMS as a group. To do this, your group will need a coordinator who will type up the group’s work or collect work from others in the group and combine the work into a single submission. You are **required to act as a group coordinator at least once** this semester. You can earn **extra credit** if you server as a group coordinator more than once, but all members of your group must have served as a coordinator at least once before extra credit can be earned.
- **Homework Assignments**
  - ­ Homework assignments will be posted on LMS and must be **submitted by the start of class** on the days indicated on the *Schedule* at the end of the syllabus. Any changes to due dates will be indicated by an announcement from Dr. Klein.
  - ­ Most assignments are meant to be completed by students individually, but some are meant to be group efforts. The expectations for each assignment will be indicated in the assignment itself.
  - ­ Assignments are meant to be completed in full; partially completed assignments or submissions that demonstrate low effort will receive significant penalties or possibly **no credit**.
- **Group Presentation Project**
  - ­ Students will assemble into groups or be assigned to groups to work on a presentation that covers a particular theme related to biology in fiction. These groups are not necessarily the same as the groups you work in for In-Class Activities.
  - ­ The themes you can choose from will be posted through a link on LMS; if you fail to sign up for a theme, you will not be assigned to a group and will receive a 0 for the presentation project. Dr. Klein may reassign students to other themes to balance group sizes; each group size should be 3-4 students.
  - ­ For the presentation, your group is expected to select works of fiction that depict biology or biotechnology related to your group's theme, analyze these works to identify the technologies or biological principles that are being applied, and propose methods or strategies that must be developed in order to bring these fictional representations of biology into the realm of reality. Details on how the presentation should be structured and grading criteria will be presented in class and posted on LMS.
  - ­ In addition to the presentation itself, your group is expected to **lead a class activity** based on the theme covered in your presentation. The activity should challenge the class in a meaningful way. Dr. Klein can assist you in developing this activity.
  - ­ Each member of the group is expected to **contribute equally** to the project. If one or more members of the group are not contributing equally or are not collaborating in a constructive manner, it is the **responsibility of the other group members to contact Dr. Klein as soon as the issue arises** – be sure to email Dr. Klein about the issue and CC all members of the group on this email. Dr. Klein will attempt to rectify the issue so the project can be completed successfully by the whole group. Students who do not sufficiently participate in group work will receive a grade that reflects their contribution to the project.
  - ­ **IMPORTANTLY, it is expected that the project is completed in full** regardless of the lack of contribution by one or more group members. E.g., if you are in a group where all other members fail to contribute, you are still expected to submit a complete presentation project. This means it is important the group members regularly update each other about their progress so if one or more members are not contributing, you will be able to contact Dr. Klein before the situation worsens and we can work towards a complete presentation project.
  - ­ A **draft of your presentation** must be submitted **before the start of class on the Wednesday before you present** so that Dr. Klein can give you feedback. If you do not submit a draft, you will **lose 10%** from your presentation grade. Note that the draft you submit should be a **complete presentation** with all required parts present, proper formatting, references, etc. Think of this as a "beta test" – your draft presentation should be ready for the public, but you still have a chance to make changes if necessary.
