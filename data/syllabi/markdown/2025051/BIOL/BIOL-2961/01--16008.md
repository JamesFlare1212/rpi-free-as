## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course provides students with an introduction to the biology and ecology of aquatic habitats found in upstate New York, with a focus on field sampling techniques and data analysis. Topics covered may include lakes, rivers, wetlands, and other inland water bodies, as well as the unique flora and fauna that inhabit these systems. Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute’s Troy, NY campus is uniquely situated near the Huron Valley, an aquatic ecosystem with both urban and rural land use. This location can provide excellent research opportunities for students who are interested in biotic responses to environmental stressors.  
Class sessions will be a mix of field trips to Grafton Lakes State Park and the Troy Country Club for sampling along with laboratory experiments. Peer reviewed readings will be provided to add additional context for topics studied. See detailed schedule below for further information.  
While out in the field safety is the number one priority. We will be working in and around water, so extra caution is needed to limit slips / falls. We will be outside without access to washrooms. Please bring water and snacks. Sunscreen and or bug repellent may also be necessary. Ticks and Lyme Disease are common in New York State, and you should check yourself for ticks after each field session.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

Students will learn about fundamental topics in aquatic ecology. This course focuses on learning by doing and will include field trips to local ecosystems where students will gain hands on experience sampling lakes and rivers. Additional experiences will include laboratory experiments and exercise in reading and discussing peer reviewed literature.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

Topics covered in this class include:

1: An introduction to aquatic ecosystems.

2: Fundamental stream sampling techniques including the sampling of both physical and biological features.

3: An overview of watershed functions and features.

4: Fundamental water quality sampling includes processing of water samples in the field using filtering apparatuses.

5: A field trip to Lake George and the Darrin Fresh Water Institute.

6: Physical sampling of lake ecosystems including measuring lake temperature profiles and estimating water clarity.

7: Conducting a thermal stratification lab experiment.

8: An introduction to peer reviewed literature including where to find articles along with how to read, summarize, and discuss the major findings of articles.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate a basic understanding of aquatic ecological processes.
- Collect, manage, and process ecological data.
- Become proficient with common field sampling techniques and tools used in both lakes and rivers.
- Demonstrate the ability to identify, read, critique, and present results discussed within the peer reviewed literature.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:
