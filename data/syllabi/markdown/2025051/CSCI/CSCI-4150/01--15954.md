## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course introduces the fundamental concepts and techniques of Artificial Intelligence (AI), focusing on problem-solving, search algorithms, knowledge representation, reasoning, machine learning, and ethics in AI. The course will equip students with both the theoretical foundations and practical skills required to build intelligent systems that can solve real-world problems.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

There is no required textbook for this course. All necessary reading materials will be made available via Submitty. However, for those seeking a deeper understanding of the material, the following textbook is recommended: Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach (4th edition) by Stuart Russell and Peter Norvig, Prentice Hall, 2020

## Course Goals

### Goals:

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- Understand core AI concepts and techniques.
- Identify and apply search algorithms to solve AI problems.
- Represent knowledge using logical and probabilistic methods.
- Apply machine learning techniques to solve real-world problems.
- Critically assess the ethical implications of AI technologies.

## Course Content

### Content Details:

- Week 1: Introduction to AI, Search
  - Course introduction, syllabus
  - AI overview and applications
  - Search problems and algorithms: BFS, DFS, Greedy, A*
- Week 2: Problem-solving and optimization
  - Optimization fundamentals
  - Local search: hill climbing, simulated annealing
  - Genetic algorithm
- Week 3: Logic and Probability
  - Logic fundamentals: Propositional logic, first-order logic
  - Conditional Probability and Bayes’ Theorem
  - Naive Bayes classifiers
- Week 4: Bayesian Networks and Decision-Making Under Uncertainty
  - Bayesian networks and inference
  - Conditional independence and D-separation
  - Markov decision processes
- Week 5: Reinforcement Learning and Traditional ML
  - RL fundamentals
  - RL algorithms: Q-learning, SARSA
  - Supervised learning: KNN
- Week 6: Unsupervised Learning
  - Unsupervised learning fundamentals
  - Clustering: k-means
  - Mid-term Exam
- Week 7: Introduction to Neural Nets
  - Neural net fundamentals: weights and biases, activation functions
  - Backpropagation
  - Multi-layer networks and dimension projection
- Week 8: Data: Embedding and Vectorization
  - Word embeddings
  - Embedding multidimensional data
  - CNNs
- Week 9: Advanced deep learning architectures
  - Memory: RNNs, LSTMs
  - Encoder/Decoder architectures
  - NN Transformers
- Week 10: Modern AI practice
  - Large language models
  - Fine-tuning
  - Toolchains: RAG, multimodal AI
- Week 11: Pitfalls and Ethical AI
  - Hallucination
  - Ethics and bias
  - Transparency and explanation
- Week 12: Future of AI
  - Current research directions: agentic AI, emergent phenomena
  - Final exam

### Other Course Policies:

Late Policy  
Late submissions will incur a penalty of 10\\% per day. After 3 days, assignments will not be accepted without prior arrangement.  
Cheating Policy  
In this course, trust plays a central role in maintaining academic integrity. All work submitted for grading must reflect your individual effort. While discussing concepts and collaborating for understanding is encouraged, all assignments must be completed independently unless specified otherwise. Any attempt to misrepresent another person’s work as your own, including copying, plagiarism, or unauthorized collaboration, will be treated as academic dishonesty.  
Any assignment found to be in violation of this policy will result in:  
\begin{enumerate  
\item A \textbf{zero grade for the portion of the assignment in question.  
\item Notification of the violation to the appropriate academic authorities, which may include the Associate Dean of Academic Affairs, the Dean of Students, or the Dean of Graduate Education.  
\end{enumerate  
A second violation will lead to a \textbf{non-droppable failure in the course. If you have any questions about what constitutes academic dishonesty or need clarification on acceptable collaboration, please ask for guidance before submitting your work.  
AI Usage Policy  
In this course, the use of AI tools such as ChatGPT, code generators, or other generative AI tools is strongly discouraged for completing assignments. These tools should not be used to generate, assist with, or manipulate any content that will be submitted for grading.  
If a student decides to use any AI tool in any capacity for an assignment, it is required that the submission include an appendix detailing the exact prompts used and the AI-generated responses from what tool. This appendix should clearly explain how AI tools were involved in the creation of the work including the reason for using the generated results of these tools. Failure to include this information will be considered a violation of academic integrity.  
AI tools should be used solely for understanding and clarifying concepts, not as a substitute for independent work. All assignments must reflect the student’s own understanding and effort. Any use of AI tools that is not fully disclosed as described above will result in academic penalties as outlined in the course's Cheating Policy.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Collaboration:  
Students are encouraged to collaborate on assignments, but must complete work individually. Students may help one another with assignments; in such cases, they should explicitly make that clear by listing students they collaborated with in the Collaborators section of the assignment. Students should not directly copy code from one another; this constitutes plagiarism, not collaboration.  
Policy violations:  
Any student or submitted assignment, test, etc. found in violation of the Course Policies will receive a grade of zero. In addition, repeat offenses will result in a grade of F for the course, and the student will be reported to the appropriate Institute personnel.  
The instructors reserve the right to apply discretion in application of consequences for policies, such as in ambiguous or borderline cases. However, the instructors reserve the right to apply the full consequences in all cases that could be construed as a violation of these policies.
