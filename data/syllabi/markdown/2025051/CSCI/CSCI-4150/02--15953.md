## Course Goals

### Goals:

By the end of this course, students will be able to:  
• Understand core AI concepts and techniques.  
• Identify and apply search algorithms to solve AI problems.  
• Represent knowledge using logical and probabilistic methods.  
• Apply machine learning techniques to solve real-world problems.  
• Critically assess the ethical and environmental implications of AI technologies.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Explain core principles and methodologies in artificial intelligence, including search, knowledge representation, reasoning, machine learning, and decision-making under uncertainty.
- Formulate real-world problems in terms of appropriate AI paradigms and select suitable solution strategies.
- Implement fundamental AI algorithms using python and demonstrate proficiency in applying them to structured problem domains and aforementioned real-world problems.
- Assess the societal, ethical, environmental, and economic implications of AI technologies, including issues of bias, accountability, transparency, resource consumption, and responsible deployment.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Quizzes, exams and programming assignments.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

In this course, trust plays a central role in maintaining academic integrity. All work submitted  
for grading must reflect your individual effort. While discussing concepts and collaborating for  
understanding is encouraged, all assignments must be completed independently unless specified  
otherwise. Any attempt to misrepresent another person’s work as your own, including copying,  
plagiarism, or unauthorized collaboration, will be treated as academic dishonesty.  
Any assignment found to be in violation of this policy will result in:  
1\. A zero grade for the portion of the assignment in question.  
2\. Notification of the violation to the appropriate academic authorities, which may include  
the Associate Dean of Academic Affairs, the Dean of Students, or the Dean of Graduate  
Education.  
A second violation will lead to a non-droppable failure in the course. If you have any  
questions about what constitutes academic dishonesty or need clarification on acceptable collaboration, please ask for guidance before submitting your work.
