## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course Description:**

This foundational course is required for all management majors and minors and introduces broad principles of managerial functions and business processes. It uses an interdisciplinary, goal-oriented approach applicable to both private and public organizations. This course covers core areas of business, including finance, accounting, marketing, operations, entrepreneurship, business analytics, and other fundamental management concepts.

The course is designed to benefit both business and non-business students by providing a fundamental understanding of how organizations operate and how decisions are made. Teaching methods include readings, lectures, case studies, and exercises to promote practical application of concepts.

Students will develop critical thinking and decision-making skills by tackling business problems and delivering recommendations, culminating in a final project. The course also emphasizes the development of teamwork, communication, and interpersonal skills.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Assignments:**

The most effective way to learn is through consistent practice. The assignments in this course are designed to engage you in hands-on learning. You are expected to complete all chapters and related assignments according to the Schedule of Assignments. **All assignments are due by 11:59 PM ET on their respective due dates.** Submissions made after this time will be considered late and will not be accepted.

**Homework:** Homework assignments are completed online and are not timed. You are allowed up to three attempts, with the highest score counting toward your final grade. Each attempt will offer a new set of problems to provide essential practice in mastering key business concepts. To maximize your success in this course, I strongly recommend the following approach for each homework submission:

- Review the feedback provided after each submission to identify any mistakes.
- For problems answered incorrectly, revisit the corresponding sections of the chapter to strengthen your understanding.
- Once you have clarified the concept or technique, reattempt the same problem and ensure that you can arrive at the correct answer on your own.
- If the concept remains unclear, seek assistance from your instructor without delay.

**Case Studies:** All projects must be submitted in either **PDF** or **Excel format** (depending on assignment specifications). No other file formats will be accepted. After submitting your file, ensure that it is readable by downloading and reviewing it. If there are any issues, make the necessary changes and resubmit the file. Strict adherence to project instructions is crucial; failure to follow them may result in a score of zero. These case studies are designed to apply key course concepts to real-world business scenarios while testing your research and critical thinking skills. When using Excel, a substantial portion of your project grade will be based on the accurate application of Excel commands, formulas, and functions. Case Studies will often be assigned as a group project, however, your final score on the Case Study will be impacted by confidential peer reviews of your team members for each group project submitted.

**In-Class Assessments:** Many chapters will feature in-class assessments such as Attendance Quizzes and Team Case Debates. These assignments are aimed at assessing your comprehension of the material, being able to apply your knowledge in practical terms, defend your own analysis, and also for recording class attendance. In-class assessments can only be attempted by students physically present in class. Submitting an in-class assessment without attending will receive -20 points for that assignment, and it will be considered academic dishonesty. In the case of a missed assessment, a documented excuse (e.g., a doctor’s note or institutional documentation) is required to arrange a make-up.

**Exams:** The Exams are timed assessments administered in class. Exams will be based on the topics covered in class. Any form of collaboration or cheating during the exams is strictly prohibited and will be considered academic dishonesty. Sharing or discussing exam content or answers, whether directly or indirectly, with students who have not yet taken the exam also constitutes academic dishonesty. In the event of a missed exam, a documented excuse (e.g., a doctor’s note or institutional documentation) is required to arrange a make-up. Please note that make-up exams will differ from the original and will be significantly more challenging.

**Final Consultation Project:** Your final is a team project consisting of a written report and a class presentation on a company your team has researched during the semester. You’ll identify major challenges the company is facing and present clear, actionable recommendations to improve the business. Supporting documentation (research, planning, data, etc.) should be included.

**Project Structure**

The project should follow four phases:

- Assess the company, market, and situation
- Generate potential solutions
- Select and justify your top recommendations
- Develop an implementation plan

**Required Analysis**

Your analysis must draw from at least five major business disciplines covered in this course:

- Management
- Marketing
- Operations
- Finance
- Accounting

**Grading Criteria**

You will be evaluated on:

- Depth and clarity of your analysis, especially in your own field of study.
- Quality and feasibility of your recommendations
- Use of course concepts from all four disciplines
- Completeness and clarity of the written report
- Professionalism and effectiveness of your presentation
- Ability to respond to questions during the Q&A

**Submission Requirements**

- Submit a written report and presentation slides to the LMS
- Peer Evaluation

**Final Consultation Project grades may be adjusted individually based on each member’s contributions and confidential peer reviews completed at the end of the term.**

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work.
