## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- 1\) Apply course concepts with increased behavioral skills in relation to self- awareness, interpersonal and group interactions, and organizational effectiveness.

- 3\) Communicate more effectively in written and oral presentations.
- 4\) Work in a more informed manner with differences based on diversity and culture.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Weekly Quizzes and Homework via MyLab – “Chapter Assignments” (25%)** There will be weekly chapter quizzes and homework assignment completed through Pearson’s MyLab for this course. Unless otherwise noted in Blackboard/MyLab, quizzes and assignments are due before the chapter/topic is discussed in class (i.e., 9:59 am for our 10:00 am class each Tuesday/Friday). This helps ensure you’ve read and digested the material for that chapter before we meet on it. All due dates and expectations are found in Blackboard/MyLab and the schedule in this syllabus. Since you will have at least a week to complete them, missed assignments cannot be made up. However, the lowest two quiz/HW scores will be dropped from the final grade.

Section Exams (25%)

There will be an exam given in class for each of the textbook sections/parts (at 5% each). They will consist of multiple choice, True/False, or short answer questions. See the course schedule at the end of this syllabus for due dates.

Group Project – Company Research (20%)

Student teams will be developed and will be responsible for working together on a research problem (to be provided in class). We will use these group interactions throughout the semester also as a learning tool itself in relation to the content of this course. A final written report (i.e., PPT) and a verbal presentation based on that research will be the deliverables at the end of the semester (at 10% each). Information on both will be provided early in the semester as will grading criteria for the final project. See the course schedule for the due dates.

Individual Assignments Outside MyLab (10%)

At a minimum a Peer Review Article Presentation assignment will be required as well as a Final Self Reflection assignment (see LMS). There may be some other assignments given throughout the semester that are not found on MyLab. The instructor retains the right to make these assignments throughout the semester. Just as it is working in an organization, not all assignments and deadlines are scheduled. It’s important to learn to manage “last minute” work. However, students will have at least one week to complete any of these types of short assignments. Late submissions will be penalized by 10% per week late.

Contributions to the Class (10%)

Class participation is an integral aspect of this course. Please read and be prepared to discuss the assigned readings, videos or other materials listed on the syllabus for each session. This portion of the grade is centered on your non-group participation in class discussions and activities. Quantity (class presence) and quality (class involvement) will be considered in evaluating participation. If you are interested in participating but have difficulty initiating it, please let me know so that we can try to ease the process. *Note: Participation with your group during in-class activities and for the group presentation will be assessed as a part of the Group Project area of grading.*

Final Exam (10%)

There will be an optional final exam which will assess your cumulative knowledge from the entire semester. The format and additional study guidance will be provided prior to final exams. The date of the final exam is TBD. Students may elect to use the average of the five “Section” exams to serve as their final exam grade.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process.

The *Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities* and the Graduate Student Supplement (for 6000 level and above courses) define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in (1) an academic (grade) penalty and (2) reporting to Lally’s Associate Dean of Academic Affairs and either the Dean of Students (for Undergraduates) or the Dean of Graduate Education (for Graduate students).

In this course, the academic penalty for a first offense is a zero for the quiz, assignment, or test in question*.* A second offense will result in failure of the course as noted in Lally’s Three Strikes Policy.

If you have any questions concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
