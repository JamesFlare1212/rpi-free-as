## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

The study of cosmology is currently at the forefront of modern astrophys-

ical research. As technology continues to advance, we continue to gain insights into questions about

the Universe long thought unanswerable. At its roots though, Cosmology began as a philosophical

quest towards explaining some of the most fundamental questions of nature. How did the Universe

begin? Did it have a beginning in the first place? Is there an end? What is the nature of the

Universe? Do we, as humans, play any role in it? Over the course of this semester we will pon-

der these questions ourselves, explore the musings and observations of the early astronomers, and

discuss the modern quests towards finding some answers to those fundamental questions. We will

also explore some of the more exotic aspects of modern physics and astronomy such as: special and

general relativity, black holes, the nature of space and time, and the Big Bang. We’ll take a more

conceptual, rather than mathematical, approach to these topics and so this course will be suitable

for majors and non-majors but knowledge of algebra and an understanding scientific graphs, tables,

and equations is required.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- An ability to apply the scientific method, learned through the lens of its applications in cosmology.
- Knowledge of the history of cosmological thought that led to today’s understanding of the Universe.
- Gain an appreciation of the content and implications of Einstein’s theories of special and general relativity
- Knowledge of the major elements of the Big Bang theory of the Universe.
- An ability to connect foundational knowledge of cosmology to give context to modern unsolved problems.
- An awareness of the modern tools being used to address cosmological inquiries.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Exams, Homework, Quizzes

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Reading Assignments:

There will be a reading assignment in advance of every class period. (Please see the class calendar.)

In class I will be covering only the highlights of each chapter, assuming that you have already read

the chapter and are familiar with the material. This is a lot of material, so don’t get behind! Get

in a routine of reading the before the lecture. That way you will have a basic understanding of the

concepts and good questions to ask. Reading the book is the most important part of this

course! When you are done with the reading assignment, try to answer the review questions at

the end of each chapter. You do not have to hand in any solutions, but this is a good check for

yourself if you comprehend the material.

One learns physics by repetition. Read quickly, but multiple times. With each time, it makes more

sense. Don’t worry if you don’t understand things right away – just keep on reading.

Homework:

There will be a homework assignment every week. Homework solutions are to be submitted in

whatever hard copy format you prefer as long as it is legible. Late homework will be marked zero.

The homework will account for 25% of the final grade.

The lowest homework score will be dropped to compute your final grade in the course. This means

you miss one homework assignment without penalty.

The homework assignments are crucial in mastering the course material. Therefore, all homework

assignments should be prepared by the student. AI-generated submissions are not permitted and

will be treated as plagiarism. Ethics still matter and plagiarism is a serious academic integrity

oﬀense. The university’s policy on plagiarism still applies to any uncited or improperly cited use

of work by other human beings, or submission of work by other human beings as your own.

Quizzes:

In order to give you credit for doing the reading and attending, there will be quizzes given randomly

throughout the semester during the class meeting. The quizzes will be in form of multiple choice

questions and/or short answer questions. The quizzes may be given at the beginning of class on

previous material or at the end of class on material of that lecture. In the latter case, you may use

the notes you took during the lecture.

The lowest two quiz scores will be dropped in calculating the average score. The quiz average will

account for 25% of the final grade. No makeup quizzes will be given.

Exams:

There will be two exams during the semester to test your progress and mastering of the material.

2The exams will be a mix of multiple choice and short answer questions. The exams will be given

during class time on the following dates: Jun 26 and Aug 14. In calculating the exam average,

the exam with the higher scores will count as two exams, while the exam with the lower score will

count as a single exam. So that the exam average will be calculated from a total of 3 scores. The

exams account for 50% of your final grade.
