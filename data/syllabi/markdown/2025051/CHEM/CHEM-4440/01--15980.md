## Course Content

### Content Details:

**Topics to be covered:**

Gas Laws, Laws of Thermodynamics (0th, 1st, 2nd, 3rd), Thermodynamics of Single and Multicomponent Systems, Chemical Equilibrium including Electrochemistry, Transport Properties of Gases and Liquids, Chemical Kinetics, Transport Properties.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will learn to qualitatively and quantitatively work with the properties of perfect and real gases and the Three Laws of Thermodynamics.
- Students will learn how to apply the Three Laws of Thermodynamics to physical and chemical change in single, multicomponent systems, phase diagrams, chemical equilibrium and electrochemistry.
- Students will learn how to quantitatively characterize transport of properties by molecules, basic rates and mechanisms of chemical reactions.
- Students will learn to combine their knowledge of thermodynamics and transport an apply it to evaluate physical chemical processes.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

The student grade will be determined by 75% from the 4 Exams given along with 4410 and 25% from 4440 exam.

1. Students will be able to apply perfect and real gas equations of state to predict and quantify gas properties and apply gas transport theories to predict and evaluate transport properties.
2. Students will be able to qualitatively and quantitatively apply the Three Laws of Thermodynamics analyze and evaluate physical and chemical change in single and multicomponent chemical systems,
3. Students will be able to quantitatively apply transport properties of molecules, basic rates and mechanisms of chemical reactions and analyze these processes.
4. Students will be able to analyze and evaluate physical chemical events using thermodynamics and transport theories to create an explanation of the processes.

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

The relationship between students and faculty is based upon trust and maintenance of this trust is necessary for education to be successful.

Students need to trust faculty to make appropriate judgments about the content and structure of the course. Faculty members need to trust that the work turned in by students represents their own effort. Violation of this trust undermines the educational process. For example, as professionals, you may be responsible in designing products where lives are at stake. As a result, there is no tolerance for any breach of academic integrity such as cheating or plagiarizing. Anyone caught cheating or plagiarizing will receive an “F”in the course. Cheating can include sharing answers, unless otherwise stated, as well as stealing answers. Plagiarism means copying words or solutions from someone’s work, even if you change a sentence or solution a little. This includes copying results and answers from previous year’s assignments. If you do use material from an appropriate source, make sure you reference it properly in your reports. The Rensselaer Student Handbook and the Dean of Students Office Webpage (http://doso.rpi.edu/update.do?artcenterkey=676) defines various forms of academic dishonesty. Please familiarize yourself with these and ask us if you have questions about what constitutes academic dishonesty.
