## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Students will develop a broad catalog of important problems and solutions in graph theory and will be able to recognize situations in which those solutions can be applied to research and real-world problems.
- Students will prove graph theoretical results using a variety of techniques (including contradiction, strong induction, extremal cases, and structural arguments) with an eye towards applying those techniques to novel problems.
- Students will gain an appreciation for the pervasiveness of graphs in modern life, and particularly how the methods of graph theory can be used to obtain information about social and other large-scale networks.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

We are scheduled for 24 class meetings during this term. You are expected to attend each class period; however, we all have things that are higher priority than this course. To account for this, you are permitted to miss two **non-exam** classes with no impact on your course grade; after this, each absence reduces your overall course score by 1%. A quick e-mail informing me of your absence is courteous but is not required. In the event that external factors causes a more lengthy absence, this policy may be waived **so long as the student promptly informs me** so that we can come to a resonable alternative arrangement.

The beginning and end of a class period are times when attention is highest; disrupting these is detrimental to your classmates. Therefore, minor late arrivals and early departures are treated as half an absence. Arriving more than 20 minutes after the start of class or leaving more than 20 minutes before the end of class will be counted as a full absence. This may also be waived on a case by case basis **with prior instructor approval**.

Students may not use their “free” absences on exam days. In the event of an emergency absence, a makeup exam will be given. Other exam absences may, at my sole discrection, result in a zero for that exam or in a makeup exam with a grade penalty of up to 25%.

### Additional Course Information:

# Pedagogical philosophies

There are two key principles that have shaped the policies in this document:

- **Attention is a limited, and surprisingly communal, resource.**
- **Correct answers are not the goal.**

The first is fairly straightforward. Attention is not something easy for humans to maintain – it requires intentional effort. Additionally, research shows that it is easier to maintain when those around you are also being attentive… and harder when they are being inattentive. We will work together to ensure that our classroom is a place that allows us to cultivate collaborative attention.

The second point is counterintuitive for most students, and this is natural: We want to get things right! But the problem is Goodhart’s Law: “When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure.” This misalignment comes up often in AI: rather than play a game to reach a high score, the system discovers it can just rewrite the high-score table.

The goal (or at least, **my** goal for you) is that you comprehend the course material in such a way that it provides you with a new way of thinking, so that you can use that knowledge and understanding to solve problems outside of this classroom. A fundamental challenge of education that we have no direct way to assess that sort of knowledge and understanding – all we have are the responses that students give to various questions and prompts. And so, I urge each of you to keep the goal in mind. Correct answers and the corresponding good grades are great, but only when they indicate underlying understanding.

# A final word

***Take care of yourselves and each other.***

This is a challenging course at a challenging university. It is very likely to cause you stress at times during the term, and if it doesn’t, some other course probably will. Some stress is fine – it can lead us to perform better than we might have otherwise, sometimes better than we knew we could.

**But it doesn’t work unless you are in a position to handle it.** This means you need to eat, you need to sleep, you need to exercise, you need to shower, and you need to take time to be social and have fun. ***No college course is worth sacrificing your health over.***

If you start to feel overwhelmed, there are many resources available to help you on campus: the Health Center, the Counseling Center, your friends and classmates, and your instructor. **Don’t suffer in silence.**

If you notice that another student appears to be struggling, reach out! Sometimes all it takes is someone noticing.

Finally, if you have some issue (on or off campus) that is affecting your class performance, please let me know – I can be very flexible as long as you’re communicating with me. I want you to be both successful and happy, and I will do what I can to facilitate that.
