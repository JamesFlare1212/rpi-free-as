## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

**Course description:** The course provides an opportunity for the development of theorem-proving skills in the field of mathematical analysis. Expansion of a knowledge base comes as a by-product of energy expended in theorem proving and subsequent exposition. Analysis topics included are: sets, functions, the real numbers, cardinality, induction, decimal representations of real numbers, Euclidean spaces, ab-stract vector spaces, and metric spaces. This is a communication-intensive course. **Prerequisites:** math-ematics major and MATH 2010. (from the Rensselaer Catalog)

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

**Textbook:** Analysis With and Introduction to Proof, Sixth ed., by Steven R. Lay, Pearson, 2023. Access to the textbook — physical or electronic — is **required.**

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Student Learning Outcomes:** Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to demonstrate

- read mathematical proofs and verbally communicate how each sentence contributes to deducing the assertion,
- solve problems in analysis,
- write high quality mathematical proofs, and
- write LaTeX syntax for mathematics and mathematical equations.

### Other Course Policies:

**Exams:** The material covered on Exams will be announced on LMS when the date of the exam is near. Exams will be given during lecture. Exams will be ***closed book and closed notes*** and will be timed. Exams will include some combination of definitions, true/false, multiple choice, short answer problems and proofs.

Make-up exams will only be given to students with an [excused absence documented by the Student Success office](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/student-success/excused-absence). **Missing an exam should not be taken lightly.** Thus, students missing an exam due to an excused absence should notify the instructor to make arrangements for a make-up exam ***before the exam begins***. If a student with an excused absence fails to inform the instructor within 12 hours of the exam, it is at the discretion of the instructor to allow the student to take a make-up exam with possibility of a penalty applied. If a student misses an exam due to an unexcused absence (e.g., did not know there was an exam, overslept, etc.), then the student must inform the instructor within 12 hours of the exam. The student may elect to take a make-up exam at a time arranged by the instructor and a 30% penalty will be applied to the score. All make-up exams will be given on the Friday immediately following the scheduled exam.

**Homework:** There will be 10 homework assignments due on Thursday nights. Each homework consist of 2-4 problems that must be done in LaTeX (see below). Homework assignments will be submitted on Gradescope as a compiled pdf file and for some submissions the LaTeX code will be submitted on LMS. The rules for collaborating on homework problems with others are as follows:

Before working with anyone, each student must read the **entire** homework problem set and (minimally) do the following

- Look and memorize all relevant definitions and Theorems.
- Make an outline of what needs to be shown.
- Make and attempt at writing the proof.

Once students have satisfied the above criteria, students may seek help and collaborate with others (inside and outside of office hours) on figuring out how to write, refine and improve proofs using the following guidelines:

- Students may get help on problems from an instructor, TA or another student currently taking the course.
- Students may not get help on problems from anyone outside of the class without explicit permission from the instructor.
- Students are responsible to independently write their submission from their own notes taken during any above interactions.
- Students must include a list of anyone with whom they collaborated (outside of office hours) at the end of each homework assignment.
- LaTeX code and LaTeX files **may not** be copied or shared with other students.
- Submissions with glaring similarities will violate academic integrity policies. The penalties for violating academic integrity policies are shown in a subsequent section on this syllabus.
- Students are responsible for protecting their work and preventing it from being copied by others. In the event of an academic integrity policy violation, all students involved will be held accountable and penalized.
- In order to receive full credit on homework assignments, proofs must be constructed based on the definitions, properties and theorems presented in the course (book and lecture). Proofs constructed in alternate ways or using alternate notations, even if correct, will receive no credit.

Homework is due on the days and times posted on LMS. Homework assignments are between 35 - 50 points. Late homework will receive a 2 point deduction if submitted less than 24 hours late, a 5 point deduction if submitted 24-48 hours late and a 12 point deduction if submitted 48-72 hours late. Homework will not be accepted after 3 days late (72 hours). An excused absence, will not automatically result in an adjustment to the late penalty as these are out of class assignments with 1 week allowed for completion. Late penalties on homework will only be reduced or waived *at the discretion of the instructor*. This is generally reserved for extended illness and when notification of the absence/illness was done in a timely manner.

At the end of the semester, a student’s homework grade will be calculated out a smaller number than the total number of homework points available. For example, if there are 320 homework points at the end of the semester, the homework grade might be calculated out of 300 points with a cap of 300 so that the max homework grade is 100%.

**Presentations:** There will be two problem presentation assignments made during the semester. Presentations will be due at the beginning of week 6 and week 12 of the semester. Details regarding problem presentation assignments will be posted on LMS when the presentation dates draw nearer.

**Lecture Worksheets:** Worksheets will be distributed during lecture on Mondays with an online version available on Gradescope. These worksheets consist of definitions, short problems and proofs, which will be discussed and filled out during lecture. A student’s credit for lecture worksheet discussions will be a combination of participation/attendance in lecture and uploads of (partially)completed worksheets to Gradescope. Students are encouraged to upload worksheets directly after lecture. Worksheets will be accepted until 11:59 PM on the day they are assigned. A student who does not attend lecture may upload a worksheet by the deadline and receive up to half credit for the worksheet. No make-up worksheets will be allowed. To compensate for this, two worksheet scores for each student will be dropped at the end of the semester.

**LaTeX:** Learning LaTeX is part of the course. Students must use LaTeX in writing homework. Students will be required to submit the original LaTeX code on LMS for some homework sets. This is to help provide feedback to students about the appropriate ways of writing LaTeX to make it a useful system. In addition, there may be questions about LaTeX syntax on exams. Students should review log files from the LaTeX compiler to make certain that the LaTeX syntax is correct. (LaTeX compilers sometimes overlook incorrect syntax which may produce correct looking output but may eventually lead to intractable difficulties as the document grows.) Sample LaTeX files for homework will be provided for download and usage.

LaTeX is free to download. While the download and installation is time consuming, the advantage of downloading LaTeX is that you can edit files locally on your own machine without connection to the Internet and you can easily control your own source files. Most downloads come with a LaTeX editor, but LaTeX may be edited with any text editor. There is a choice of downloads with two major ones being TexLive and MikTex. Either will suffice for this class.

Alternatively, students may use an online LaTeX editor such as “Overleaf”. While this makes it a little easier to get started, it requires Internet connectivity and it controls your files. Note that the homework rules above explicitly forbid sharing LaTeX files in any fashion and thus prohibit using any of the sharing facilities of online programs such as “Overleaf”.

**Gradescope:** Gradescope will be used for exams, quizzes, worksheets and presentations for this course. The easiest way for students to upload content to Gradescope is to use the **Gradescope App**. A link to instructions on how to download and use this app on both iOS and android devices is provided [**here.**](https://help.gradescope.com/article/alyonjbud4-mobile-app) Students choosing to use Gradescope via the gradescope webpage will need to be able to either convert jpgs of their worksheets to pdfs or scan their worksheets directly to pdfs for upload. The mobile app will handle this for you. Quizzes and exams will be scanned by the instructor to Gradescope and once released, you may view the comments and rubric deductions that were applied to your work directly on Gradescope.

**Quizzes:** Quizzes will be 20 minutes in length and will be given on Thursdays during lecture. Quizzes will be over material covered the prior week in lecture. They will typically consist of 3-4 questions including definitions, multiple choice/multiple select, short answer and one proof. No make-up quizzes will be given for any reason. Students who miss a quiz due to an [excused absence documented by the Student Success office](https://success.studentlife.rpi.edu/student-success/excused-absence) will be excused from a quiz. Additionally, if a student has zero (0) or one (1) excused absence from quizzes, then one (1) quiz will be dropped when calculating the overall quiz grade at the end of the semester.

**Disability Services for Students (DSS):** Students with a memorandum from the [Disability Services for Students (DSS)](http://studenthealth.rpi.edu/disabilityservices) office identifying accommodations for quizzes and exams must present the memorandum to the instructor, ideally by Thursday, May 22. Providing the accommodations often requires advance planning. Students will not be granted accommodations unless a memorandum from DSS has been received.

**Grade appeals:** Changes to exam, quiz or homework grades will be made only if something has been overlooked. Grade appeals will be made on Gradescope. All appeals must be made **during the grade appeal window** specified on Gradescope for the exam, quiz or homework once the grade has been published on Gradescope. Before submitting an appeal please check Gradescope comments on graded assignments.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

**Academic integrity:** Students should familiarize themselves with the section on academic integrity in the [Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities](https://rpi.app.box.com/s/3f1cl3w1lt1mq8gqla68v6uk3vh1c19f) (pp. 16–18). All work submitted on exams and quizzes must be the student’s own. A student may not change an answer on an exam or quiz before submitting it for an appeal. Additionally you will be asked to “sign” an honor code that indicates your work is your own and you have not given or received any help on exams and quizzes. The minimum academic sanction applied to a student found in violation of the above policies will be a grade of 0 on the assignment. A stronger sanction may be applied if warranted. A report regarding the violation will also be submitted to the Dean of Students Office and will remain on file until graduation. Repeat offenders will fail the course and will be subject to judicial action.
