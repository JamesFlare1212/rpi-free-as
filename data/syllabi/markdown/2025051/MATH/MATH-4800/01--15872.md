## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Demonstrate an understanding of (i) fundamental ideas of numerical analysis: conditioning, stability and convergence, and (ii) the behavior of numerical algorithms: their strengths and limitations.
- Be able to implement basic numerical methods using clear and correct computer code.
- Be able to present written solutions to problems in a clear, concise and coherent fashion.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Course assessment will be based on student performance for assigned homework and exams. In particular, homework will be assigned regularly, collected, and graded. There will be two exams held in class on Thursdays, June 29 and August 17, i.e. the end of the two summer half-semesters.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

There is no late homework penalty if a homework is handed in by the due date posted on LMS. If you are unable to hand in your homework on time, then you must make arrangements with one of us (DWS for Part I and JWB for Part II) in advance and not after the due date. For example, if you are sick and unable to hand in your work on time, then you should let the active instructor know in advance that this is the situation. On the other hand, if you have work in other classes and are not able to hand in your work on time, then this is not a valid reason for an extension. Homework handed in up to 24 hours late will be penalized 20%. Homework handed in between 24 and 48 hours late will be penalized 50%. No homework will be accepted more than 48 hours late (unless a prior arrangement has been made).
