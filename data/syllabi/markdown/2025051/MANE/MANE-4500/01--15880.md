## Course Goals

### Goals:

This course provides the basic tools for MANE students for modeling dynamic systems and designing feedback controllers that will process sensor measurements and cause a single-input and single-output dynamic system to meet tracking accuracy and disturbance rejection specifications. The key course objectives are:

1. To develop an understanding of lumped parameter modeling and system specifications for electrical, mechanical, and electromechanical systems.
2. To develop an understanding of Laplace transforms to solve differential equations and derive transfer functions for systems (such as those described in 1.)
3. To understand the frequency response of a system and its utility in analyzing and designing systems.
4. To understand the notion of “stability” of a system and its relevance to design and analysis of control systems, through tools such as Root Locus and Routh-Hurwitz stability tests.
5. To be able to design feedback compensators for satisfying specifications such as those described in 2-4.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- Ability to model mechanical, electrical, and electromechanical systems
- Understanding of system specification, in the time and frequency domain
- Understanding of "stability" and methods of evaluating stability of a dynamic system
- Understanding of the "frequency response" of a system and application to design and analysis of feedback controllers
- Knowledge of basic design processes for controllers on single-input, single-output (SISO) systems

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

Homeworks will collectively represent 25% of the final grade.  
The project will represent another 25% of the final grade.  
The exams will collectively represent the final 50% of the grade.  
Grade appeals may be made on specific problems via email. You must point out, specifically, where you believe you should not have lost points. If your appeal is not specific enough, or not made through the proper channel, it will be denied. Appeals must be made within three days of you receiving an assignment back from me.  
Late homework assignments will be accepted up to three days after the due date listed in LMS. Late assignments will receive a 50% penalty in their final scores (i.e. a 70 becomes a 35). Grade appeals will not be accepted for late work. Exams may not be returned late, except for excused absences.
