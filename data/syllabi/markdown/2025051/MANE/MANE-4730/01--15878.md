## Course Goals

### Goals:

Introduction to heat transfer including conduction, external and internal convection, and radiation. Emphasis is on applications of heat transfer to a wide variety of engineering systems including HVAC, automotive design, materials processing etc.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- On successful completion of this course students will be able to: • to select simplifying assumptions in analyzing a complex thermal system. •
- • to analyze heat transfer in thermal systems by identifying heat transfer modes–convection (forced/natural, internal/external), conduction (transient and steady-state) and radiation using the appropriate equations to evaluate them.
- to analyze thermal systems by applying conservation of mass and energy.
- • to analyze the performance of heat exchangers and design heat exchangers given a set of specifications.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

**Attendance**

Attendance in lectures is not mandatory but is very strongly recommended. Each student is responsible for all materials presented all submissions due and all announcements made in class.

All missed in-class assignments (ICA) will receive a zero. Many ICAs are to be completed and submitted online by the end of the day.

Students who missed deadlines due to valid excuses may be allowed to submit assignments, upon request.

**TEST ATTENDANCE POLICY**

Any missed tests must be taken during the next available testing window, per the schedule outlined at the end of the syllabus. The first make-up opportunity is the next re-test. There are no make-ups or retests for Aug. 15 as the semester ends on that day.

### Other Course Policies:

**Class organization:** We will meet twice weekly for 125 minutes each time. The class follows a studio format and consists of small segments of lecture time intercalated with in-class exercises/assignments.

**Homework** will be assigned on Wileyplus and will be graded automatically. The homework is due on Wednesdays at 9 PM.

**In-class assignments** will be collected/submitted typically in class or at the end of the day.

**The module tests** are closed book. One page crib sheet will be allowed. The tests are one to two hours standard time and consist of one or more problems. Test 1, 2 and 3 can be re-taken. Test 4 cannot be retaken. For the test schedule please see the table at the end of this syllabus.

**Students with accommodations** must inform the instructor within the 1st week of the semester and schedule all tests and retests (if needed) with the TEST CENTER.

**Study Time**

Learning the material will require effort in class and outside of class. The rule-of-thumb for the needed amount of study time for college level classes is approximately *two-three hours outside for each contact hour. Thus, for this 3 credit class, you should set aside up to 12 working hours per week including class time*. Note that some courses require less time; others require more. Likewise, some individuals need more or less time depending on their background, ability, study habits, etc. Several steps can be taken to improve your learning of the material. Studying on a regular basis is more effective than cramming. Reading and/or thinking about the topics to be covered in lecture before attending the lecture can result in better understanding; this approach is particularly effective since new material introduced in lecture then is not so overwhelming.
