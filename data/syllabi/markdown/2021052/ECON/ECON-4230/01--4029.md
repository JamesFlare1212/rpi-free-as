### Additional Information for Course Information Section:

Environmental Economics strives to put into practice an objective data driven framework for understanding and solving problems of the environment often caused by human actions of production and consumption. This course strives to highlight important aspects of managing and mitigating damage in the environment and the analytical tools of economics for modeling environmental problems and solutions. A wide variety of environmental issues will be surveyed as we apply economics to major cases, such as air, water, solid wastes/ toxic substances, and global environmental management. This course is a studio (not lecture) course, meaning that the course depends heavily on student interaction and participation. This course also will satisfy requirements for Professional Development II and Communication Intensive criteria. Students will be responsible for textbook readings, interactive assignments, submission of a portfolio of written work, and a verbal presentation. Environmental Economics is a four credit course which spans a six week condensed summer term and the workload should be expected accordingly. It is critical that students keep on top of work (readings, assignments, lessons, and writing progress) and not get behind.

The format of the course is ‘hybrid’ and will accommodate both in-person and remote students. Assignments and activities will be the same or similar for each group of students, but the means for accomplishing these assignments and activities will be different. In-person students will engage with one another in the physical classroom during the scheduled class meeting times, while remote students will engage with one another online and apart from the regular class meeting times. In some instances, in-person and remote students will interact as one group. It is each student’s choice as to whether to complete the course as in-person or remote. You are asked to commit to one modality, either in-person or remote, for the entire 6-week course. If a need arises for you to switch modalities, you will need to speak with professor. The Course Calendar in this syllabus will describe the schedule for students in each group.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

Grade Component A: WEEKLY ACTIVITY GRADE (40%): A weekly activity grade will be given based on participation in a variety of interactive assignments. Details specific for each group of students is as follows:

1. In-person Students: Interactive assignments will be completed during each class meeting. These involve group and individual work and will be accomplished during the class. In addition, progress will be made toward meeting the writing requirements for this CI course during each class meeting. Students will be assessed each week with regards to their participation in assignments conducted in class. Student activity will require attendance, completing in-class assignments, participating in activities, and being generally engaged with the class. If an in-person student misses class, the weekly activity grade will be affected, unless this student speaks with professor and either makes up the work or justifies a switch to the remote track; see section on attendance policy, above. Please bring your computer to class each session.

1. Remote Students: Interactive Assignments will be given every Monday of our six week term and must be completed by the Saturday of the week they are assigned, 11:59 PM. Most of these assignments will require collaboration with other remote students. An activity grade will be given each week for participation in these interactive assignments. If you must complete an assignment late, you must contact professor and a 10% late penalty will apply.

Grade Component B: PORTFOLIO (40%): During the last week of the course, a portfolio of written work will be due and will include an Environmental Issue Report (EIR) on a topic of your choice. Components of the EIR are slightly different between in-person and remote students. In-person students will have opportunities during each class to write a part of the portfolio. Mid-way through the course a draft will be submitted for an editing workshop in which all students, in-person and remote, will participate; the workshop will take place in class for in-person students. The finished portfolio must be 15 pages minimum in length (double-spaced, 11 pt. font) in order to satisfy the communication intensive criteria. However, it is very likely that, by following the instructions for the Portfolio, the length will exceed the 15 page minimum and you are encouraged to not skimp on writing in order to keep to the 15 page minimum. It is imperative that students begin writing their portfolio at the start of the semester; this is not an assignment that can be pulled together during the last week. It is also expected that you will revise and edit your writing as needed so as to submit a polished and professional-looking final document. Credit will be deducted for spelling and grammar mistakes, awkward wording, and for the use of language translation software. If you are at all concerned about your writing skills, you are urged to contact the Rensselaer Center for Communication and Design; see the section titled, ‘Communication Intensive Criteria’ on page 4.

Grade Component C: PRESENTATION (20%): Students will present their Environmental Issue Report at the end of the term. Each presentation should be five minutes in length and highlight the parts of the portfolio you find most interesting. In-person students will present in the classroom. Remote students will create a video presentation to be posted to a Box Shared Space, viewable to the entire class.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

: For students who choose the in-person option, attendance is required. One absence will be allowed during weeks 1-5 of the term if accompanied by an excuse from the Student Health Center or Dean of Students Office AND this absence must be made up as determined by professor. Unexcused absences will result in a poor weekly activity grade. If you feel that you may have difficulty attending the in-person class, please talk with professor and you may choose to switch to the remote track. Attendance in class for student presentations is worth 20% of the student final presentation grade. Although attendance is required for the in-person track, keep in mind that work will be accomplished in a collaborative manner which reduces out-of-class burden. In addition, a mix of structured and independent work will allow us to meet in the classroom two days per week, while the other two days are spent out of class.

For remote students, there is no attendance requirement. Remote students are expected to accomplish assigned readings and other assignments during the week these items are assigned. Remember, you are given a weekly activity grade based on your participation in interactive assignments.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).

### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Academic Integrity: All work submitted for a grade must be each student’s own work. Violation of the policy on academic integrity involves activities such as copying from another student’s work or some other external source (plagiarism), submitting written work that has been written for another class or by a former student during a prior run of this course, and any activity that is meant to undermine the teaching and learning process. There is a zero-tolerance policy towards violations of academic integrity. If the professor discovers any incident of violation, the involved student(s) will receive a failing grade for the course.
