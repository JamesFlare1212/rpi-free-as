## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

You may have noticed that the community of research scientists within STEM is not reflective of the composition of the U.S. (and other countries) population with respect to race, gender, sexual identity, disability, socioeconomic status, etc. In fact, if you ask someone to envision a STEM researcher it is very likely the image conjured is of a heterosexual elderly male, possibly, bearded. What sorts of bias and exclusion occur systemically in STEM such that we see such appalling disparities? What is diversity? Why does it matter in STEM? In this course we will explore the systemic nature of barriers to diversity and inclusion as well as specific cases. We will also explore solutions to these barriers that will help to establish inclusive, diverse, and accessible research communities.

## Learning Outcomes

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes (CLOs):

- describe and analyze different elements of bias and how they may influence the practice of scientific research and education
- analyze many aspects of the complex relationship between science and society
- engage in the analysis of policies or practices that impact social, cultural, environmental, and economic outcomes as they relate to science
- analyze and consider the ethical responsibilities of eliminating bias and making science accessible to all

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

1. Reading (or video, etc.) Responses (30%) -- 3 required (must be on readings from 3 different classes). ### Other Course Policies:

Valuing Diversity

This course is intended to reach students from diverse backgrounds and perspectives - all are welcome here. The diversity students bring to class, across gender, sexuality, age, disability, socio-economic status, ethnicity, race, religion, and learning needs is valued as an important asset and strength in the learning process.

We will have many opportunities in this class to explore challenging issues and increase our understanding of differing perspectives. Our conversations and structured dialogues may not always be easy, and we will need patience and compassion, and a foundation grounded in respect for others. Therefore, an additional aim of this course is to increase our capacity for these kinds of conversations, as this is a valuable skill in both scholarship and in life. We are committed to providing an atmosphere for learning that respects diversity. While working together to build this community, we ask all members to:  
Share their unique experiences, values, and beliefs.  
Be open to the views of others.  
Honor the uniqueness of their colleagues.  
Appreciate the opportunity that we have to learn from each other in this community.  
Value each other’s opinions and communicate in a respectful manner.  
Keep confidential discussions that the community has of a personal (or professional) nature.  
Stories stay, lessons leave  
Use “I” statements  
Take Space, Make Space  
Accept that things may remain unresolved  
Embrace discomfort, but take a moment if you need it  
If you feel yourself getting angry or defensive, ask yourself why.  
You will make mistakes and apologize if you do (it is not about your intent it is about your impact)  
Take ownership of your words and actions. This is a good way to act with more intention and consideration of others in the classroom.  
Use this opportunity together to discuss ways in which we can create an inclusive environment in this course and across the Rensselaer community.

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
