## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course is intended to provide students with an introduction to the concepts and principles of Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA). ABA applies empirically derived psychological principles of learning theory in a systematic way to achieve meaningful (socially significant), beneficial behavior changes in humans and other animals. ABA emphasizes direct observation and measurement of behavior (what people say or do) and systematic evaluation of behavioral interventions to be sure they are producing the desired behavior change outcomes.

## Learning Outcomes

- Students will be able to identify, explain, and apply basic behavior analytic principles.
- Students will be able to describe behavior change procedures and their relationship to basic behavioral principles.
- Students will be able to describe the concepts and principles underlying the functional model of intervention selection and application.

## Grading Criteria

### Criteria Details:

**Exams**. There will be three online multiple-choice exams (about 50 questions per exam). The exams will be available at the LMS site over a three-day period to accommodate differences in students’ schedules. During the exam, you may use the course textbook and course notes, but you may not consult/collaborate with another person. You will have 2 hours to finish the exams. Once started, you must complete the exams (no stopping and-re-starting).

**Project**. The purpose of the project requirement is to allow students to demonstrate their understanding of the principles and methods of applied behavior analysis. The project can take the following forms, including:

(1) carry out a detailed review of a technique or application area within applied behavior analysis;

(2) carry out an actual behavior-change project (if you select this option you must get advance approval from the instructor and start by the end of Week 2);

(3) a critical review of an ABA book or a set of research articles on an ABA topic of interest to you; or

(4) propose your own project (this option requires approval from the instructor).

The project write-up must be 6-10 pages in length plus references and other appendices. The paper must be type-written, double-spaced, 12-pt Times New Roman, with 1-inch margins and formatted according to American Psychological Association (APA) guidelines.

*Note*: see https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/apa_style/apa_style_introduction.html).

### Academic Policies:

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. Acts that violate this trust undermine the educational process. The Rensselaer Handbook of Student Rights and Responsibilities and The Graduate Student Supplement define various forms of Academic Dishonesty and you should make yourself familiar with these. In this class, all assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration.

  Violations of academic integrity may also be reported to the appropriate Dean (Dean of Students for undergraduate students or the Dean of Graduate Education for graduate students, respectively).

  If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification. In addition, you can visit the following site for more information on our Academic Integrity Policy: [Students Rights, Responsibilities, and Judicial Affairs](https://info.rpi.edu/dean-students/student-rights-responsibilities-and-judicial-affairs).
