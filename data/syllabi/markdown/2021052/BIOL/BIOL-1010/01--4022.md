## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This is an introductory course in biological systems focusing on major topics in evolution, human genetics & medicine, and ecology. It is an undergraduate level course. It is intended for non-majors and majors and covers a variety of topics that impact our daily lives and the grand challenges of this decade and your generation. Activities, such as student presentations and written responses to lecture questions enable students to interact, and discuss these topics. The goal is to provide an understanding of fundamental principles, to develop analytical thinking skills in the context of modern biology, and to develop data dexterity for understanding and solving grand challenges in the life sciences.

FORMAT: Your presentation must contain a minimum of 5 slides. You must enter your talk in the Speaker Notes beneath each and every slide contained within your presentation. Approx. 300 words or more per slide. Therefore, 1500 words in total for your presentation must be entered in the Speaker Notes. However, each slide may contain more or less than 300 words, as long as the total is 1500 words for your entire presentation.

RUBRIC: Your presentation must contain:

1. A slide or more on introduction - explaining the background of the topic and core biological concept. For example, coronavirus - introduce the disease, and why you are presenting. Is it it’s origin and phylogeny that you wish to talk about? Or, is it drug design? Or, multiple biological facets? Or, interdisciplinary impacts, such as social fallout or economic costs borne by society? 5 POINTS.
2. A slide or more explaining the problem or the current status of our knowledge - for example, do we have drugs or therapeutics or vaccination? What are the issues?....etc. 5 POINTS.
3. A slide or more containing goals/solutions - for example, research labs are working on different types of solutions, drugs, vaccines - what are the various types of solutions possible or available? Is one better than the other? Why? 5 POINTS.

ACADEMIC TIPS: How to Study: In studying a subject like biology, which contains an inexhaustible amount of material, it is important to study actively, sorting the material into at least two categories: familiar, understood concepts and facts on the one hand, and new or difficult concepts or facts on the other. Plainly, once you do this, you know what it is you have to concentrate on in order to increase your grasp of the subject.
