## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course functions like a professional book review club and provides students with interdisciplinary learning opportunities in examining popular texts from the humanities, arts, and social sciences. Students will read, discuss, and evaluate potential books for next year's campus-wide community read selection in alignment with the campus Theme of the Year. Students will report on their assessments, including how likely the texts will succeed for a Community Read targeted primarily to first-year students. Students should note that active participation and peer-to-peer learning is an essential component of the course.

### Course (Student) Learning Outcomes Assessment Measures:

**Book Reviews (4):** The point of a scholarly book review is not to summarize the content of the book, but to situate the historical and cultural merit of the book in a specific context and to evaluate critically the author's purpose, thesis, contentions, and methods of analysis. Hence, the bulk of the body of one's review essay will be an evaluation of how convincing and clear was the author's presentation of their thesis, and a commentary on the book's suitability for an audience of first-year students at RPI. Book reviews will run approximately 700-1,000 words in length (3-4 pages double-spaced) adding up to 3,500-4000 words (14-16 pages double-spaced) among the four reviews. Students will develop drafts through a guided, feedback-driven process and revise two reviews at the end of the session.

**Presentation, Discussion Questions, Annotated Bibliography:** At the start of the session, students will sign up to present and lead a discussion on one of the required texts. To prepare for their presentation, students should read several reviews and/or scholarship to help understand their texts, prepare an annotated bibliography of works consulted, and submit a list of discussion questions to spark conversation during the presentation. Presenters will assign one secondary source to the class in advance of the presentation, such as professional book reviews, scholarship on first-year experience generally and/or common read programs specifically, relevant scholarship or public discourse on the topic of the assigned text. Presenters are encouraged to include an educational activity to engage their peers as part of their presentation.

**Final Portfolio.** Your final portfolio will showcase your in-class work, your best book reviews (newly polished), and your critical reading. Specifically, you will include 1) A letter of recommendation, addressed to President Jackson and Dean Simoni, indicating your first and second choice for the Common Read book, its relevance to the Theme of the Year, and additional rationales 2) two revised book reviews for your top choices, and 3) a “cache” of discussion questions and activities to be used in the teaching of these two potential Common Read texts.

**Attendance and Participation****.** The participation portion of this evaluation is determined holistically and in consultation with individual students as necessary. Generally, substantial participation can be demonstrated in two ways 1) demonstrating engagement in class discussions and activities (synchronous and asynchronous) 2) demonstrating engagement with the texts through the reading log. Students will submit reading logs on Week Three and on Week Six. Reading logs will be evaluated as follows: Complete = 100%; Majority Complete = 70%; Majority Incomplete = 0%-10%. Attendance is a prerequisite for participation, but attendance in itself is not participation.

## Policies

### Attendance Policy:

This is a small seminar class, the success of which is entirely dependent on active student participation. Therefore, I expect all students to attend every class and participate to the extent of their abilities. Students unable to attend or participate due to illness, instability, or irresolvable conflict should reach out to me as soon as possible so we can problem-solve together. Generally, every student can miss one class for whatever reason, no questions asked. Two further unexcused absences will deduct ten points each from the student’s final grade**. A student who exceeds three unexcused absences will automatically fail the course even if they are otherwise passing.** Excused absences should be processed through the Office of Student Success. The expectation for synchronous Zoom classes is that students will have video turned on so that we can better engage with one another as a community. Exemptions to the video policy will be made for students that require accommodations. For accommodations to this policy, please see me within a week of joining the class. You must both attend *and* participate (see student-centered learning above) in order to earn your participation points for the session.

- ### Academic Integrity

  Student-teacher relationships are built on trust. For example, students must trust that teachers have made appropriate decisions about the structure and content of the courses they teach, and teachers must trust that the assignments that students turn in are their own. ### Additional Academic Integrity Course Policy and Penalty Information:

Some examples of plagiarism include copying, paraphrasing, or summarizing without acknowledgment, submission of another student's work as one's own, the purchase/use of prepared research or completed papers or projects, and the unacknowledged use of research sources gathered by someone else. Failure to indicate accurately the extent and precise nature of one's reliance on other sources is also a form of plagiarism. Students are responsible for understanding legitimate use of sources, the appropriate ways of acknowledging academic, scholarly, or creative indebtedness. All assignments that are turned in for a grade must represent the student’s own work completed for this class in particular. In cases where help was received, or teamwork was allowed, a notation on the assignment should indicate your collaboration. If you have a compelling reason for wanting to use previous work of yours for the class, it must be substantially and fundamentally revised, and you must get approval from the instructor first.

Submission of any assignment that is in violation of this policy will result in a penalty of a zero for the assignment and the potential to fail the course. Second offenses will result in automatic failure of the course and the potential for disciplinary action by the institution.

If you have any question concerning this policy before submitting an assignment, please ask for clarification.
