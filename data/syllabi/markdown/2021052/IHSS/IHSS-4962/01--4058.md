## Course Description

### Additional Course Description :

This course will explore topics in intellectual property law, with special focus on how to patent an invention and how copyright law applies on the Internet. The course may be of particular interest to any student interested in attending law school, to STEM students interested in patent prosecution, and to anyone who wants to understand how the law shapes their online experience.

## Course Text(s)

### Text Details:

The primary texts for this course are Lydia Pallas Loren & Joseph Scott Miller, Intellectual Property Law: Cases & Materials, 6th Ed. 2018, and James Grimmelmann, Internet Law, Version 10.0, 2020. These texts are available at the publisher’s website, http://www.semaphorepress.com. Semaphore Press uses a model different from traditional casebook publishers, publishing books digitally as pdfs, distributed on the web. This books have a suggested price of $30, and Semaphore Press encourages you to pay the suggested price, which helps to make lower-cost books more widely available. Because we are only using a portion of the book, we ask that you pay $5 each for the Patent chapter in Intellectual Property Law and the Copyright chapter in Internet Law. Other materials for the course will be distributed in class or are available online.

## Course Goals

### Goals:

**Learning Outcomes:**

- Develop a broad understanding of intellectual property law topics.
- Understand and evaluate the philosophical justifications for intellectual property law.
- Work effectively to engage in and assist others in legal problem solving, including resolving uncertain, indeterminate, and unstructured situations.
- Critically analyze the technological, social, and moral context in which intellectual property issues arise.
- Present legal and policy analysis orally and in writing.
- Identify postgraduate opportunities in patent prosecution and intellectual property law practice.

Grades will be 20% based on class participation (including in-class exercises where student groups discuss and report on hypothetical legal problems), 30% on a first paper due at 5:00PM Tuesday, June 8 (analyzing the policy reasons why intellectual property laws might be justified in particular situations), and 50% on a second written assignment due at 5:00PM Monday, June 28 (analyzing legal problems faced by parties impacted by intellectual property law).
